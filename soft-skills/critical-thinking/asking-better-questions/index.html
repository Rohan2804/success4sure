Asking Better Questions

Ask clear, specific questions that get useful answers from managers, clients and teammates. You will learn when to use open and closed questions, how to clarify a task before starting and how to ask for help well.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Why the question shapes the answer

The quality of an answer depends heavily on the question. Ask your senior “Is my report okay?” and you will probably hear “Yes, fine.” Ask “Which section of my report would you change first?” and you get something you can actually use.

Many of us grew up in classrooms where questions were for exams, not for learning, and asking felt like admitting you did not know. At work, the opposite is usually true. People who ask sharp questions early save hours of rework, and managers tend to notice them for the right reasons.

A good question is specific, has a purpose and is easy to answer. Before you ask, take five seconds to decide what you actually want to know and why.

Do
  • Decide what you want to learn before you ask
Don't
  • Ask “Any feedback?” and expect a useful reply

In the full lesson

  • Open and closed questions, used on purpose
  • Clarify a task before you start
  • Ask for help the smart way

Key takeaways

  • Decide what you want to know before you ask.
  • Use open questions to explore and closed questions to confirm.
  • Avoid leading questions when you need honest input.
  • Clarify goal, audience, format, deadline and priority before starting a task.
  • When asking for help, share what you tried and where you are stuck.

Quick check

You want useful feedback on your draft report. Which question works best?

  1. “Is the report okay?”
  2. “Any feedback on this?”
  3. “Which section would you change first, and why?”
  4. “You liked the report overall, didn't you? I worked hard on it.”
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. “Which section would you change first, and why?”

A specific question invites a specific, usable answer.
