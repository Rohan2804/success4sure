Preparing to Negotiate

Prepare for any negotiation by fixing a clear goal, a walk-away point and a Plan B. You will also learn to map what the other side really needs before you say a word.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Decide what you actually want

Most negotiations go wrong before they start, because people walk in with a feeling instead of a goal. "I want a better deal" is a feeling. "I want the security deposit reduced from three months' rent to two" is a goal you can work towards and measure.

Write down two numbers or outcomes: your ideal result and a realistic target. Say you and two friends are renting a 3BHK in Pune at ₹36,000 a month. Your ideal might be ₹33,000 with the deposit at two months; your realistic target might be ₹34,500 with the same deposit.

Also list the things that matter to you besides money: a move-in date that matches your joining date, permission to have guests, or the landlord fixing the geyser before you shift. Listing them now gives you more to work with later.

Do
  • Write your ideal and realistic outcomes in specific terms
  • List non-money items that matter to you
Don't
  • Go in with only a vague wish like "a good deal"

Know your walk-away point and your Plan B

Your walk-away point is the least you will accept. Below it, you politely say no. Fixing it in advance protects you from agreeing to something in the moment that you regret on the way home.

Your walk-away point should come from your Plan B: what you will do if this deal does not happen. If another flat two kilometres away is available at ₹35,000 with a two-month deposit, then agreeing to ₹37,000 here makes little sense. If you have no alternative and must move in ten days, your position is weaker, and it is better to know that honestly.

Before any important negotiation, spend an hour improving your Plan B. Visit one more flat, get one more vendor quote, or ask one more colleague who could help. A stronger Plan B lets you stay calm and polite because you are not desperate.

Do
  • Fix your walk-away point before the conversation
  • Strengthen your Plan B in advance
Don't
  • Decide your limit while under pressure in the meeting
  • Reveal your walk-away point early

Map the other side's interests

A position is what someone says they want; an interest is why they want it. The landlord's position may be "Rent is ₹36,000, final." Their interests may be a steady tenant for at least a year, no damage to the flat, and rent that arrives on time.

Guess these interests before you meet, then test your guesses with questions. "What has been your experience with past tenants?" or "What matters most to you in choosing tenants?" often reveal more than any argument.

Once you know their interests, you can offer things that cost you little but matter to them: an eleven-month agreement instead of six, post-dated rent transfers set up on UPI autopay, or a written promise to repaint one room before leaving. Preparation is not only about your side; it is about understanding theirs.

Do
  • Separate their stated position from the reasons behind it
  • Prepare open questions to test your guesses
Don't
  • Assume the other side only cares about money

Build a one-page prep sheet

Put your preparation on one page, or one note on your phone. It should have five lines: my goal, my realistic target, my walk-away point, my Plan B, and their likely interests. Add two or three questions you want to ask and one or two things you can offer.

Plan your opening line too. For example: "We really like the flat and can sign for eleven months. Could we discuss the rent and deposit so it works for both of us?" This sets a friendly, problem-solving tone from the first sentence.

Finally, check facts. Look at rents for similar flats in the area on two or three listing sites, and note the range. Facts make your requests sound reasonable rather than random, and they help you spot an unrealistic offer quickly.

Do
  • Write a five-line prep sheet before every important negotiation
  • Research the going rate from more than one source
Don't
  • Rely on memory when numbers are involved

Key takeaways

  • Turn a vague wish into a specific goal and a realistic target.
  • Fix your walk-away point before you start talking.
  • A stronger Plan B lets you negotiate calmly.
  • Look for the interests behind the other side's position.
  • Put your preparation on one page and check the facts.

Quick check

Which of these is a well-prepared negotiation goal?

  1. "Get the landlord to be more reasonable about the rent and deposit"
  2. "Reduce the deposit from three months' rent to two"
  3. "Win the negotiation"
  4. "Pay as little as possible"
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. "Reduce the deposit from three months' rent to two"

It is specific and measurable, so you know exactly what you are working towards.
