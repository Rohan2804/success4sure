About this Negotiation & Persuasion course

Whether you are discussing a deadline with your manager or terms with a client, negotiation is an everyday work skill. This negotiation skills course online teaches you to prepare with a clear goal, a walk-away point and a Plan B, then build win–win offers by trading rather than conceding. You will practise persuasion skills that respect the other person's right to say no, and learn calm replies to refusals and tough counter-offers. Negotiation skills for freshers cover workload and deadlines, while finance professionals learn to discuss fees and terms ethically.

What you will learn

  • Prepare a one-page plan with your goal, walk-away point and Plan B
  • Build win–win offers by putting several issues on the table and trading
  • Respond calmly to a no or tough counter-offer using fair criteria
  • Persuade people by linking ideas to their goals without manipulative tactics
  • Negotiate deadlines and workload with your manager early instead of burning out
  • Discuss fees and terms with clients openly without compromising suitability

Who this course is for

  • Freshers juggling tight deadlines and new tasks
  • Early-career professionals who avoid difficult conversations
  • Team members who need to persuade colleagues without authority
  • Distributors, advisers and RMs discussing fees and terms with clients

FAQs

Who is the Negotiation & Persuasion course for?

How do I prepare for a negotiation?

Decide exactly what you want, the point at which you would walk away and your Plan B if there is no deal. Then think about what the other side needs and fears. Put it all on a one-page prep sheet so you stay clear and calm during the conversation.

What is a win–win negotiation?

A win–win negotiation gives both sides more of what they value instead of splitting one number. You bring several issues to the table, such as timing, scope and support, and trade the ones you value less for the ones you value more. Offering two or three options and writing down the agreement helps.

How do I say no to extra work as a fresher without sounding rude?

First clarify the request: what is needed, by when and why. If you are already full, ask your manager to help prioritise by showing your current tasks and asking what should move. Propose a realistic option early rather than missing a deadline later, and keep your word on what you agree.

What is the difference between persuasion and manipulation?

Persuasion links your idea to the other person's goals, shows honest evidence and leaves them free to say no. Manipulation relies on pressure, false urgency, guilt or hiding information. A simple test is whether you would be comfortable if the other person saw exactly how and why you made your case.
