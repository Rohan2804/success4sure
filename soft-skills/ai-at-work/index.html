About this Working with AI course

This AI skills course for professionals shows you how to use AI at work in a practical, responsible way. You will learn prompt writing for beginners using a simple task, context, audience and format structure, then check AI output for made-up facts and wrong figures before anyone else sees it. Lessons cover what you should never paste into a public AI tool, drafting emails and summaries faster, and using AI honestly in your job search. Finance professionals get a dedicated lesson on client data, suitability and compliance records.

What you will learn

  • Write prompts with task, context, audience and format to get useful answers
  • Spot made-up facts, wrong figures and bias in AI output before using it
  • Remove personal and confidential data from prompts and follow your company's AI policy
  • Use AI to draft emails, summarise long documents and start research faster
  • Tailor resumes and practise interviews with AI without inventing anything about yourself
  • Keep client data private and check every AI-generated number in finance work

Who this course is for

  • Freshers and students starting to use AI chat tools
  • Office professionals who want faster drafting, summaries and research
  • Job seekers using AI for resumes, cover letters and practice
  • Mutual fund distributors, advisers and relationship managers handling client data

FAQs

Who is the Working with AI course for?

Freshers preparing for or starting their first job, and finance professionals such as mutual fund distributors, investment advisers and relationship managers. Core lessons suit everyone; one lesson is written for each of the two groups.

Is this course free?

Yes. All 6 lessons, 60 quiz questions with explanations and 18 practice scenarios are free. You can start without an account; log in with Google to save your progress.

How can I use AI at work without making mistakes?

Give the AI a clear brief with the task, context and format you need, then treat its answer as a first draft. Verify facts, figures and sources before using them, match your checking to the stakes, and never paste confidential or personal data into public tools. Your judgement stays in charge.

How do I write a good prompt for ChatGPT or other AI tools?

Say what you want done, give the background, name the audience and describe the format, such as five bullet points or a 100-word email. Add an example or limits if useful. If the first answer is off, refine it in the same conversation instead of starting over.

Is it safe to use AI tools at work?

It can be, if you follow your company's AI policy and treat every prompt as sharing information outside the organisation. Never enter client details, passwords, salary data or unreleased business information into public AI tools. Remove names and identifiers, use approved tools where available and be open about when you have used AI.

Can I use AI to prepare for job interviews?

Yes. AI can generate likely questions, give feedback on your practice answers and help tailor your resume to a job description. Keep everything truthful and in your own voice. Do not use AI during online tests or live interviews unless clearly allowed, because a shortcut there can cost you the offer.
