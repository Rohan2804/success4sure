Using AI Safely: Privacy, Confidentiality and Ethics

Decide what you can and cannot share with AI tools and follow your organisation's rules. You will learn to remove personal and confidential data from prompts, and to be honest about when you have used AI.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Treat every prompt as sharing outside the company

When you type or paste something into an AI chat tool, it leaves your computer and goes to the tool provider's servers. Depending on the tool, your plan and your settings, conversations may be stored, reviewed or used to improve the service. Once it is sent, you cannot take it back.

The safest habit is simple: treat anything you put into a public AI tool as if you were emailing it to someone outside your organisation. If you would not send it to a stranger, do not paste it.

Many companies provide approved AI tools with stronger data protections under a business agreement. These are different from the free personal account on your phone. Know which one you are using before you paste anything.

What should never go into a public AI tool

Some information should stay out of public AI tools completely. Personal data: names linked to phone numbers, addresses, Aadhaar or PAN numbers, bank account details, health information or salary details. Confidential business information: unreleased financial results, pricing, client lists, deal terms, internal strategy, legal matters and HR cases. Security details: passwords, access codes and internal system information.

India's data protection law places duties on organisations that handle people's personal data, and your employer's contracts with clients usually promise confidentiality too. A careless paste can break both.

You can often still get help by removing the sensitive parts. Replace real details with placeholders: "Client A, a 45-year-old business owner in Nagpur" instead of a name and phone number; "[Amount]" instead of the real figure. The AI can help with the structure and wording without ever seeing the real data.

Do
  • Replace names, numbers and IDs with placeholders
  • Use your company's approved tool for work data
Don't
  • Paste Aadhaar, PAN, account numbers or passwords
  • Upload client files or internal reports to personal AI accounts

Know and follow your company's AI policy

Many organisations now have an AI policy that lists approved tools, what data may be used with them and which tasks need human review. Read it, and if your company has none, ask your manager or IT team before using AI for work data. No policy does not mean anything goes.

If you are unsure whether something is allowed, ask first. "Can I use the approved AI tool to summarise this vendor contract, or should legal review it directly?" is a sensible question, not a sign of weakness.

If you realise you have pasted something sensitive by mistake, tell your manager or IT team promptly. Early reporting lets them limit any damage; hiding it usually makes things worse.

Do
  • Read your organisation's AI policy
  • Report accidental sharing quickly
Don't
  • Assume a tool is approved because colleagues use it

Be honest about AI help

Using AI is not cheating in itself, but hiding it where honesty is expected can be. In colleges and many exams, rules say whether AI may be used for assignments; breaking them can count as academic misconduct. At work, some reports, client documents or published articles may need you to disclose AI assistance, or may not allow it at all.

AI text can also closely resemble existing writing, and AI-generated images may mimic other people's work. Check that your final work is genuinely your own wording and that you credit the sources of facts and ideas. Running AI output through a rewording tool to hide where it came from is still plagiarism.

Finally, you are responsible for how you use these tools. Never use AI to create fake reviews, impersonate real people, produce misleading images of someone, or pressure customers. If it would be wrong without AI, it is still wrong with AI.

Do
  • Follow your college or employer's disclosure rules
  • Credit the real sources of facts and ideas
Don't
  • Present AI work as entirely your own where rules require disclosure
  • Use AI to create fake reviews or impersonate people

Key takeaways

  • Treat a public AI tool like sending data outside the company.
  • Never paste personal data, client details, passwords or confidential results.
  • Use placeholders so AI can help without seeing real data.
  • Follow your company's AI policy and ask when unsure.
  • Report accidental sharing quickly.
  • Disclose AI help where rules expect it, and never use AI to deceive.

Quick check

You want help wording a reply to a customer complaint. Which prompt is safest?

  1. Paste the full complaint, including the customer's name, phone number and account number, so nothing is lost
  2. Paste the complaint after replacing personal details with placeholders like [Customer] and [Account]
  3. Paste only the customer's phone number so the AI can understand the context
  4. Upload a screenshot of the customer's profile from the CRM
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Paste the complaint after replacing personal details with placeholders like [Customer] and [Account]

Placeholders let the AI help with wording without exposing personal data.
