Building Trust and Credibility with Clients

Earn lasting client trust through reliability, plain disclosure of fees and risks, suitable recommendations and consistent follow-through, in line with SEBI and AMFI conduct expectations.

Last updated 5 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Trust is built from small promises kept

Clients rarely judge a distributor, adviser or relationship manager by a single brilliant recommendation. They judge by many small moments: did you call back when you said you would, send the statement you promised, and remember what they told you last time?

Make fewer, more specific promises and keep all of them. "I'll send you the comparison by Wednesday noon" is better than "I'll get back to you soon." Keep a simple follow-up tracker, in a CRM or even a spreadsheet, listing each commitment and its date.

When you cannot keep a promise, inform the client before the deadline with a new time. Reliability in small things gives clients the confidence to trust you with bigger decisions.

Do
  • Give specific times for follow-ups
  • Log every commitment in a tracker
Don't
  • Say "soon" or "I'll see" when you mean a date
  • Let a missed promise pass without a message

In the full lesson

  • Be transparent about fees, costs and risks
  • Suitability first, even if you lose the sale
  • Communicate proactively, especially in bad times
  • Credibility and ethical boundaries

Key takeaways

  • Make specific promises and keep every one.
  • Disclose your role, fees and costs before you are asked.
  • Explain risk honestly; never call market-linked products guaranteed.
  • Recommend only what suits the client, even if you lose the sale.
  • Call clients first when markets fall.
  • Never handle client money, OTPs or blank forms.

Quick check

A client asks how you earn money from the mutual funds you recommend. As a distributor, what is the best response?

  1. Say the service is free for the client
  2. Change the subject to the fund's strong returns over the past five years
  3. Say plainly that the fund house pays you commission on regular plans
  4. Say it varies and isn't important
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Say plainly that the fund house pays you commission on regular plans

Clear disclosure of how you are paid builds trust and is expected of distributors.
