Spot burnout early, set boundaries politely and build routines that help you recover.

About this Stress, Burnout & Work–Life Balance course

Busy weeks are normal, but constant pressure wears anyone down. This stress management course online offers practical, everyday habits rather than quick fixes. You will learn to notice the signs of burnout at work early with a five-minute weekly check, set polite boundaries around late-night messages and weekend work, and say no without guilt. Simple work life balance tips cover sleep timing, real breaks and switching off. Freshers learn to handle first-job pressure and imposter feelings, and sales and finance professionals learn to manage target pressure ethically. This is general wellbeing guidance, not medical advice.

What you will learn

  • Tell everyday stress from burnout and run a five-minute weekly self-check
  • Set polite boundaries for late-night messages, weekend work and heavy workload
  • Say no using a simple three-part structure and handle pushback calmly
  • Build a daily recovery routine with steady sleep, real breaks and switching off
  • Steady yourself in a first job by asking for help and tracking progress
  • Turn sales targets into daily actions while keeping ethics ahead of numbers

Who this course is for

  • Freshers feeling overwhelmed in their first job
  • Early-career professionals who struggle to switch off
  • People who find it hard to say no at work
  • Sales, banking and advisory staff under target pressure

FAQs

Who is the Stress, Burnout & Work–Life Balance course for?

Freshers preparing for or starting their first job, and finance professionals such as mutual fund distributors, investment advisers and relationship managers. Core lessons suit everyone; one lesson is written for each of the two groups.

What are the early signs of burnout at work?

Common warning signs include feeling drained even after rest, becoming cynical or detached about work and feeling that your effort no longer makes a difference. A short weekly self-check helps you notice these early. If low mood or anxiety lasts for weeks or affects daily life, please speak to a doctor or qualified counsellor.

How do I set boundaries with my manager politely?

Raise it calmly in a planned conversation rather than in the moment. Explain how you will stay reachable for genuine emergencies, agree response times for late-night messages and make workload trade-offs visible by asking which task should move. Framing boundaries as a way to do better work makes the conversation easier.

How can I say no at work without feeling guilty?

Use a simple three-part no: acknowledge the request, give a short honest reason and decline clearly. Offer a real alternative only if you can deliver it. Some guilt afterwards is normal, especially early in your career, and it fades as you see that clear limits help you keep your commitments.

How do I deal with imposter feelings in my first job?

Imposter feelings are common among freshers because everything is new. Ask for help early, keep a simple record of what you learn and finish each week, and talk to yourself as fairly as you would to a friend. If the feelings persist or affect your daily life, consider talking to a counsellor.
