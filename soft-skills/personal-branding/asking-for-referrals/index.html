Asking for Referrals and Introductions

Ask for job referrals and introductions in a way that is specific, easy to say yes to and easy to decline. You will write a forwardable request and learn how to follow up and say thank you.

Last updated 5 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Referrals and introductions are borrowed trust

A referral is when someone inside a company recommends you for a specific role. An introduction is when someone connects you with a person they know, such as a hiring manager, a potential client or a mentor. In both cases, they are lending you their reputation. If you turn out to be unprepared or unprofessional, it reflects on them.

That is why the best requests make the other person's job easy and safe. They know exactly what you want, why you are a reasonable fit, and what to forward. They can also say no without awkwardness.

Remember that a referral usually gets your application looked at; it rarely guarantees an interview, and never guarantees an offer. Treat it as a door opened, not a job secured.

Do
  • Treat every referral as someone's reputation on the line
  • Prepare as seriously as you would for an interview
Don't
  • Assume a referral guarantees a job
  • Ask without understanding what you are asking for

In the full lesson

  • Who to ask, and how well you need to know them
  • Make the request specific and forwardable
  • Asking for an introduction
  • Follow up, report back and return the favour

Key takeaways

  • A referral lends you someone's reputation, so make it easy and safe for them.
  • Ask people who know your work first; with weaker ties, ask for information before a referral.
  • Give the exact role, job ID, why you fit, your resume and a forwardable note.
  • Always offer an easy way to say no.
  • Follow up once, report the outcome and thank them whatever happens.

Quick check

Which referral request is most likely to be acted on?

  1. "Any openings in your company? Please refer."
  2. A long message describing your entire life story
  3. Your resume with no message
  4. Role, job ID, two lines on fit, resume and a forwardable note
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Role, job ID, two lines on fit, resume and a forwardable note

Specific, ready-to-forward information makes it easy for the referrer to help.
