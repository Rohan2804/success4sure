Posting on LinkedIn Without the Cringe

Write short, useful LinkedIn posts and comments that sound like you, not like a template. You will learn simple post formats, a four-line structure and the common habits that make posts feel fake.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Why post at all

Many people avoid posting because they fear sounding boastful, or because their feed is full of posts that make them wince. That is understandable. But a few thoughtful posts do something a profile cannot: they show how you think. A recruiter or client who has read your clear explanation of a topic already trusts you a little before you meet.

The goal is not to become an influencer or go viral. The goal is to be useful to a small group of people who care about your field. Ten relevant readers who remember you are worth more than a thousand strangers who scrolled past.

Start small. One post every week or two, plus a few thoughtful comments, is enough to be visible. You do not need to post daily to build a reputation.

Do
  • Aim to be useful to a small, relevant audience
  • Start with one post every week or two
Don't
  • Measure success only by likes
  • Post daily just to stay "active" if you have nothing to say

In the full lesson

  • Three formats that are easy to write
  • A simple four-line structure
  • The habits that feel cringe
  • Comments, confidentiality and criticism

Key takeaways

  • Post to be useful to a small, relevant audience, not to go viral.
  • Use simple formats: one lesson learned, a plain-language explainer or an honest recap.
  • Structure posts as a clear first line, the point, one example and a closing line.
  • Avoid humblebrags, invented stories, engagement bait and mass tagging.
  • Thoughtful comments count, and confidential work information never belongs in a post.

Quick check

Which opening line is most likely to make a reader keep going?

  1. "Excited and humbled to share my thoughts on this journey!"
  2. "Monday motivation for all my connections out there"
  3. "Three things my first month in a bank branch taught me about complaints:"
  4. "Not sure if anyone will read this, but here goes."
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. "Three things my first month in a bank branch taught me about complaints:"

It tells the reader exactly what they will get from the post.
