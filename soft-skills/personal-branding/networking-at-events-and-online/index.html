Networking at Events and Online

Start conversations at events, send connection requests people accept, and follow up so that a single meeting becomes a lasting professional relationship. You will practise openers, graceful exits and follow-up messages.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Networking is relationships, not collecting cards

Many people picture networking as handing out visiting cards and asking strangers for jobs. No wonder they dread it. A better way to think about it: networking is simply getting to know people in your field and staying in touch, so that you can help each other over the years.

The most useful mindset is to be curious and to give first. Ask people about their work because you are genuinely interested. Share an article, a contact or a tip that might help them. When you later need advice or an introduction, you are asking someone who already knows and likes you.

You do not need hundreds of contacts. Ten to twenty people you are in real touch with, across different companies and levels, can open more doors than a thousand silent connections.

Do
  • Go in curious about other people's work
  • Look for small ways to help first
Don't
  • Open a conversation by asking for a job
  • Judge an event by the number of cards you collected

In the full lesson

  • Before and during an event
  • Exiting a conversation gracefully
  • Connection requests and follow-ups that work
  • Staying in touch without being pushy

Key takeaways

  • Networking means building real relationships by being curious and helping first.
  • Prepare a one-line introduction and aim for a few good conversations, not many cards.
  • Leave conversations warmly with a thank-you and a next step.
  • Send personal connection notes and follow up within two days.
  • Stay in light, genuine touch every few months, not only when you need something.

Quick check

Which opener works best with a stranger at a banking conference tea break?

  1. "What did you think of the panel on UPI fraud?"
  2. "Do you have any openings in your team?"
  3. "Can I give you my resume?"
  4. "What is your designation and salary band?"
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. "What did you think of the panel on UPI fraud?"

A shared-context question starts a natural conversation without pressure.
