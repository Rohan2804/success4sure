A LinkedIn Profile That Gets Found

Write a LinkedIn headline, About section and skills list that use the words recruiters and peers actually search for. You will turn a generic profile into a specific, honest one that people can find and trust.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

How people actually find you

Recruiters, hiring managers and future clients rarely scroll through profiles at random. They type words into a search box: a job title such as "credit analyst", a skill such as "financial modelling", a city such as "Pune". If those words are not on your profile, you simply do not appear, however talented you are.

So the first job of your profile is to be specific. Ask yourself: what would someone type if they were looking for a person like me? Write those exact words down. A fresher aiming for banking operations might list "KYC", "customer service", "branch operations" and "MS Excel". A mutual fund distributor might list "mutual fund distribution", "SIP" and "financial planning support" in their city.

There is no secret algorithm trick that replaces this. Profiles stuffed with fifty keywords, fake titles or hidden text look odd to the humans who eventually read them. Use the right words naturally, in the places people look first.

Do
  • List the job titles, skills and city people would search for
  • Use those words naturally in your headline, About and experience
Don't
  • Stuff your profile with repeated keywords
  • Chase "algorithm hacks" shared in viral posts

A headline that says what you do

Your headline appears next to your name everywhere: in search results, comments and connection requests. Many people waste it on "Seeking new opportunities" or "Hardworking | Passionate | Dedicated". Nobody searches for "passionate".

A strong headline follows a simple pattern: role or target role, then your focus area, then one credential or proof point. Before: "MBA | Looking for job". After: "Aspiring Credit Analyst | SME lending and financial statement analysis | MBA Finance, Pune". Before: "Relationship Manager at a bank". After: "Relationship Manager | Retail banking and wealth products | Serving clients in Nagpur".

If you are between jobs, it is fine to say what you are aiming for, as long as you use the word "aspiring" or "seeking" honestly rather than claiming a title you do not hold.

Do
  • Use role + focus + one proof point
  • Keep the most important words at the start
Don't
  • Fill the headline with adjectives like "passionate" or "dynamic"
  • Claim a job title you have not held

An About section people finish reading

The About section is where a visitor decides whether to message you. Write it in the first person, in three short paragraphs. First: what you do and for whom. Second: evidence, such as a project, a result or a skill you have used for real. Third: what you are looking for and how to reach you.

Example: "I help small businesses in Indore understand their loan options. Over two years at a cooperative bank, I processed more than 300 MSME loan files and cut document rework by setting up a simple checklist. I'm now looking for credit analyst roles in Indore or remotely. Message me here or at the email in my contact info."

Avoid long life stories, quotes from famous people and lists of qualities. Two or three concrete facts are more convincing than ten adjectives.

Do
  • Write in the first person, in short paragraphs
  • Include one concrete example or result
Don't
  • Open with a motivational quote
  • List qualities without evidence

Skills, experience and a photo that builds trust

Add skills you can demonstrate in an interview or a client meeting. If you list "Python", be ready to explain a script you wrote. Pin the three or four skills most relevant to the work you want so they show first.

Under each role, write two to four lines about outcomes rather than duties, the same way you would on a strong resume. Keep titles, employers and dates consistent with your resume; mismatches make recruiters wonder which version is true.

Your photo should be recent, clearly show your face and have a plain background. Formal or smart-casual clothes suit most finance and corporate roles. A neat, simple banner image, such as your city skyline or a plain colour, is better than a cluttered one. These details are not about looking glamorous; they signal that a real, reachable person is behind the profile.

Do
  • Pin the skills that match your target role
  • Use a recent, well-lit photo where your face is clearly visible
Don't
  • List skills you could not discuss in an interview
  • Use a group photo, a wedding photo or a heavily filtered selfie

Keep it honest and current

A profile is a public claim about yourself, and people do check. Former colleagues can see your titles; recruiters verify degrees and certifications. Write "NISM Series V-A: preparing" rather than "certified" until you have passed, and never inflate team sizes or results.

Set a reminder every three months to update your profile: add a new project, a course you finished or a result you achieved. A profile last touched three years ago suggests you are not active, even if you are.

Finally, check your contact details and your custom profile URL. A clean link such as one ending in your name looks better on a resume or visiting card than a string of random numbers.

Do
  • Mark certifications as "in progress" until you have them
  • Review your profile every three months
Don't
  • Inflate titles, numbers or team sizes

Key takeaways

  • People find you by searching for titles, skills and cities, so use those exact words.
  • Write your headline as role + focus + one proof point.
  • Keep your About section to three short first-person paragraphs with real evidence.
  • List only skills you can demonstrate, and use a clear, recent photo.
  • Keep everything honest, consistent with your resume and updated every few months.

Quick check

A recruiter in Pune is looking for a credit analyst. Which headline is most likely to show up in her search?

  1. "Hardworking | Passionate | Dedicated | Team player"
  2. "Aspiring Credit Analyst | SME lending | MBA Finance, Pune"
  3. "Seeking new opportunities"
  4. "MBA graduate who loves challenges"
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. "Aspiring Credit Analyst | SME lending | MBA Finance, Pune"

It contains the title, focus area and city the recruiter is likely to type.
