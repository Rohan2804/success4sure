Writing Professional Emails That Get Replies

Write work emails with clear subject lines, a direct opening request and an easy-to-scan body. You will learn to rewrite vague emails so that recipients know exactly what to do and by when.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Subject lines that say what is inside

The subject line decides whether your email is opened now, later or never. A good subject tells the reader the topic and, if needed, the action and deadline. Compare "Query" or "Regarding the matter" with "Approval needed: Q3 travel budget by Thu 14 Nov".

Useful prefixes include "Action needed", "FYI" (for information only) and "Decision". For example, "FYI: revised branch timings from 1 Dec" tells the reader no reply is expected, while "Decision: choose venue for Q4 offsite by Mon" signals that a choice is needed. Keep subjects under about ten words so they do not get cut off on mobile.

When a long email thread changes topic, update the subject line. Otherwise people searching their inbox for the new topic will not find it.

Do
  • Include the topic, action and date in the subject
  • Update the subject when the topic changes
Don't
  • Use subjects like "Hi", "Urgent" or "Query" alone
  • Write the whole message in the subject line

In the full lesson

  • Put the request in the first two lines
  • Make the body easy to scan
  • Tone, CC and the final check

Key takeaways

  • Write subject lines that name the topic, action and deadline.
  • Put your request in the first two lines.
  • Use short paragraphs, numbered questions and clear attachments.
  • Keep one email to one topic.
  • Check recipients, attachments, figures and tone before sending.

Quick check

Which subject line is most likely to get a timely response?

  1. "Approval needed: client visit to Indore, by Wed 13 Nov"
  2. "Urgent!!!"
  3. "Regarding the matter discussed"
  4. "Hi Sir"
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. "Approval needed: client visit to Indore, by Wed 13 Nov"

It states the topic, the action needed and the deadline.
