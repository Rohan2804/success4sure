Writing Professional Emails That Get Replies

Write work emails with clear subject lines, a direct opening request and an easy-to-scan body. You will learn to rewrite vague emails so that recipients know exactly what to do and by when.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Subject lines that say what is inside

The subject line decides whether your email is opened now, later or never. A good subject tells the reader the topic and, if needed, the action and deadline. Compare "Query" or "Regarding the matter" with "Approval needed: Q3 travel budget by Thu 14 Nov".

Useful prefixes include "Action needed", "FYI" (for information only) and "Decision". For example, "FYI: revised branch timings from 1 Dec" tells the reader no reply is expected, while "Decision: choose venue for Q4 offsite by Mon" signals that a choice is needed. Keep subjects under about ten words so they do not get cut off on mobile.

When a long email thread changes topic, update the subject line. Otherwise people searching their inbox for the new topic will not find it.

Do
  • Include the topic, action and date in the subject
  • Update the subject when the topic changes
Don't
  • Use subjects like "Hi", "Urgent" or "Query" alone
  • Write the whole message in the subject line

Put the request in the first two lines

Many people read only the first few lines of an email, especially on their phones. If your request is in the fourth paragraph, it may never be seen. State what you need near the top, then give the background.

Before: "Dear Sir, Hope you are doing well. As discussed in last month's meeting, we have been working on the vendor onboarding process, and there were several challenges... (three paragraphs later) ...so kindly approve."

After: "Hi Rajesh, Could you approve the onboarding of Shree Logistics as a vendor by Friday? Their documents are verified and the quote is ₹1.2 lakh lower than the current vendor. Details are below if you need them."

The second email can be acted on in thirty seconds.

Do
  • State the request or purpose in the opening lines
  • Give a specific deadline
Don't
  • Bury the ask under paragraphs of background

Make the body easy to scan

Short paragraphs of two or three sentences are easier to read than one dense block. If you are asking more than one question, number them so the reply can say "1. Yes, 2. Next week". Use bullet points for lists of items, dates or documents.

One email should ideally cover one topic. If you need a decision on leave and a separate update on a client account, send two emails with clear subjects; each is then easy to find and forward.

Attachments need a mention in the body ("I've attached the signed KYC form") and a sensible file name, such as "Sharma_KYC_Nov2026.pdf" rather than "scan0045.pdf".

Do
  • Number your questions
  • Name attachments clearly and mention them in the body
Don't
  • Mix unrelated topics in one email
  • Send long blocks of unbroken text

Tone, CC and the final check

Professional email in India is often a little formal, and that is fine. But avoid outdated phrases such as "Please do the needful", "Kindly revert back" or "PFA" without context. "Please share the updated file by Thursday" or "Please find the report attached" are clearer.

Use CC only for people who need to know; use BCC sparingly and never to quietly expose a colleague. Before you reply-all, ask whether every person on the thread needs your reply.

Before clicking Send, check four things: the recipient is correct, the attachment is actually attached, names and figures are accurate, and the tone would be acceptable if forwarded to your manager. For emotionally charged emails, save a draft and reread it after twenty minutes.

Do
  • Reread for names, numbers and attachments before sending
  • Wait before sending an email written in frustration
Don't
  • Write in ALL CAPS or use many exclamation marks
  • Reply-all when only the sender needs your answer

Key takeaways

  • Write subject lines that name the topic, action and deadline.
  • Put your request in the first two lines.
  • Use short paragraphs, numbered questions and clear attachments.
  • Keep one email to one topic.
  • Check recipients, attachments, figures and tone before sending.

Quick check

Which subject line is most likely to get a timely response?

  1. "Approval needed: client visit to Indore, by Wed 13 Nov"
  2. "Urgent!!!"
  3. "Regarding the matter discussed"
  4. "Hi Sir"
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. "Approval needed: client visit to Indore, by Wed 13 Nov"

It states the topic, the action needed and the deadline.
