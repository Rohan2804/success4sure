Phone Calls and Video Meetings Etiquette

Handle work phone calls and video meetings in a way that is respectful of people's time and easy to follow. You will learn how to open and close calls, run your part of a video meeting, and follow up in writing.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Opening a phone call well

When you call someone at work, the first fifteen seconds set the tone. Introduce yourself, give the reason for the call, and check whether it is a good time: "Hello, this is Ananya from Success Capital. I'm calling about your SIP renewal; do you have five minutes now?"

If they say they are busy, ask when to call back and note the time. Calling back exactly when promised builds trust quickly. Avoid starting with a long "How are you, sir, how is the family" exchange with someone you barely know; a short greeting is enough.

When receiving calls, answer with your name and team: "Good morning, Rahul from customer service." If you cannot talk, say so briefly and give a callback time rather than half-listening.

Do
  • State your name, organisation and reason in the first sentence
  • Ask if it is a good time
Don't
  • Call clients late in the evening or very early without prior agreement
  • Take a call while driving or in a noisy place if you can avoid it

Keeping calls clear and closing them properly

Phone calls lose all visual cues, so speak a little slower than usual and confirm important details by repeating them: "So that's account number ending 4521, and you'd like the statement for April to September." Spell out names and email addresses using simple words: "S for Sunil, H for Hari."

Keep a notebook or open notes file while you talk. Writing down names, numbers and commitments during the call is far more reliable than remembering later.

Close by summarising the next steps: "I'll email the form today, and you'll send it back signed by Thursday. Is that right?" Then thank them and end the call. For any commitment involving money, documents or dates, send a short email or message confirming what was agreed.

Do
  • Repeat numbers and spellings back
  • Summarise next steps before hanging up
Don't
  • Rely on memory for details shared on the call
  • End a call without agreeing on what happens next

Video meetings: setup and presence

Join video meetings a minute or two early to check your audio and camera. Use headphones where possible to avoid echo, and choose a spot with light in front of you rather than behind you. A plain, tidy background or a simple blur is enough; nobody expects a studio.

Mute yourself when not speaking, especially in large meetings, and unmute before talking. If your connection is weak, it is acceptable to switch off video and say so: "My network is unstable, so I'll keep my camera off."

Camera norms differ between companies. In smaller meetings, having your camera on usually helps people read your reactions. Follow your team's practice, and if you must keep it off, stay engaged by speaking up and using the chat.

Do
  • Test audio and video before the meeting
  • Mute when not speaking
Don't
  • Eat, drive or reply to emails visibly on camera
  • Join from a noisy place without muting

Speaking up and following up in online meetings

Online meetings make it harder to judge when to speak. Use the raise-hand feature or say your name before your point: "This is Imran, a quick point on the budget." Keep contributions short, because long monologues are harder to follow on a screen.

If you share your screen, close unrelated tabs and turn off notifications first. A personal message popping up during a client presentation is embarrassing and can expose confidential information.

If you are the organiser, send an agenda beforehand and, afterwards, a short email listing decisions, owners and deadlines. Even if you are not the organiser, offering to send the summary is a simple way to be useful and visible.

Do
  • Say your name before speaking in larger calls
  • Close private tabs and silence notifications before screen sharing
Don't
  • Talk over others when there is audio lag
  • Leave without knowing the action items

Key takeaways

  • Open calls with your name, organisation, purpose and a time check.
  • Repeat key numbers and spellings, and take notes as you go.
  • End every call or meeting with clear next steps.
  • Test your setup, mute when silent, and prepare your screen before sharing.
  • Confirm important commitments in writing afterwards.

Quick check

Which is the best way to open a work call to a client you have not spoken to before?

  1. "Hi, Neha from Prime Wealth about your portfolio review. Is now a good time?"
  2. "Hello, who is this?"
  3. "Sir, how are you, how is your family, how is your business going these days?"
  4. "I'm calling to tell you about a new scheme."
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. "Hi, Neha from Prime Wealth about your portfolio review. Is now a good time?"

It introduces you, states the purpose, and respects their time.
