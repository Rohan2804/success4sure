Serving Clients over Video and Phone

Run client conversations over video and phone that are secure, compliant and easy for the client to follow. You will learn to confirm identity and consent, share screens safely, explain products suitably and send clear follow-up summaries.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Prepare as if the client were across the desk

Remote meetings with clients deserve the same preparation as a branch visit, and often more, because technical problems feel unprofessional. Send a short agenda with the meeting link in advance: "Review of your portfolio and discussion on your daughter's education goal, 30 minutes." Ask whether the client prefers a video call or a phone call; many senior clients are more comfortable on the phone.

Before the call, test your audio and internet, keep the client's documents and the latest statements ready, and make sure you're in a quiet, private place. A client discussing family finances should never hear a colleague's conversation or a television in the background, and nobody else should overhear the client's details.

Join a couple of minutes early. If the client struggles with the technology, stay patient and offer to switch to a phone call rather than spending fifteen minutes troubleshooting.

Do
  • Share a short agenda and ask the client's preferred mode
  • Take calls from a private, quiet place
Don't
  • Join late and search for documents during the call
  • Discuss client details where others can overhear

In the full lesson

  • Confirm identity, consent and records
  • Share screens safely
  • Explain products clearly and suitably
  • Close the loop with a written summary

Key takeaways

  • Prepare with an agenda, ready documents and a private, quiet place.
  • Verify identity through approved processes and never ask for OTPs or passwords.
  • Share one window, silence notifications and hide other clients' data.
  • Check understanding, put suitability first, disclose fees and never promise returns.
  • Send a same-day written summary with decisions, next steps and risks.

Quick check

A senior client struggles to join a video call for ten minutes. What should you do?

  1. Keep troubleshooting until the video works, since video is more personal
  2. Offer to continue on a phone call
  3. Reschedule for next week
  4. Ask their grandson to fix it later
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Offer to continue on a phone call

Switching to the phone respects the client's time and comfort.
