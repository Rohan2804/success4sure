Serving Clients over Video and Phone

Run client conversations over video and phone that are secure, compliant and easy for the client to follow. You will learn to confirm identity and consent, share screens safely, explain products suitably and send clear follow-up summaries.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Prepare as if the client were across the desk

Remote meetings with clients deserve the same preparation as a branch visit, and often more, because technical problems feel unprofessional. Send a short agenda with the meeting link in advance: "Review of your portfolio and discussion on your daughter's education goal, 30 minutes." Ask whether the client prefers a video call or a phone call; many senior clients are more comfortable on the phone.

Before the call, test your audio and internet, keep the client's documents and the latest statements ready, and make sure you're in a quiet, private place. A client discussing family finances should never hear a colleague's conversation or a television in the background, and nobody else should overhear the client's details.

Join a couple of minutes early. If the client struggles with the technology, stay patient and offer to switch to a phone call rather than spending fifteen minutes troubleshooting.

Do
  • Share a short agenda and ask the client's preferred mode
  • Take calls from a private, quiet place
Don't
  • Join late and search for documents during the call
  • Discuss client details where others can overhear

Confirm identity, consent and records

Over phone or video you can't always be sure who is on the other end. Follow your firm's procedure for verifying a client, such as calling only on the registered number or confirming details the firm has on record. Never ask a client to tell you their OTP, PIN or password; legitimate processes don't need you to know them, and saying so out loud helps protect clients from fraud.

If calls are recorded, inform the client at the start and follow your firm's consent process. Where a family member joins the call, confirm with the client that they're comfortable discussing their finances in that person's presence.

Record-keeping is part of good service. Note what was discussed, what was recommended and why, and what the client decided, in your firm's approved system. Regulators expect financial intermediaries to be able to show that advice and transactions were suitable and properly authorised.

Do
  • Verify the client using your firm's approved procedure
  • Inform clients when a call is being recorded
Don't
  • Ask clients to read out OTPs or passwords
  • Keep client conversation notes only on your personal phone

Share screens safely

Screen sharing helps explain a portfolio or a form, but it can expose information by accident. Before sharing, close unrelated tabs and documents, especially other clients' files, and turn on do-not-disturb so chat pop-ups don't appear. Share a single window rather than your whole screen whenever possible.

Mask sensitive data such as full account numbers or PAN where you can, and never leave your email inbox visible. Stop sharing as soon as you're done.

If you need to see the client's screen, for example to help them through an online form, ask them to share only that window and to keep passwords private while typing. Avoid asking clients to install remote-control software; your firm's approved processes should cover any guided support.

Do
  • Share one window, not your whole screen
  • Close other clients' files and silence notifications first
Don't
  • Leave your inbox or chat visible while sharing
  • Ask clients to give you remote control of their device

Explain products clearly and suitably

Remote conversations make it harder to read a client's reactions, so check understanding often. Pause after each key point and ask, "Does that make sense?" or better, "Could you tell me in your words how this fund's risk compares with an FD?" If the client repeats it accurately, you can move on.

Suitability comes first: discuss goals, time horizon and risk tolerance before any product. Explain risks plainly, disclose any fees or commission you receive, and never promise or imply guaranteed returns on market-linked products. Phrases like "this is completely safe" or "you will surely get 15%" are both misleading and unacceptable. A better answer is: "Past returns don't guarantee future returns. Let's look at the risk and your goal."

Don't rush a client into a decision on a call. Offer time to think and to discuss with family, and send the relevant documents so they can read them at their own pace.

Do
  • Ask the client to explain key points back in their own words
  • Disclose fees and commission clearly
Don't
  • Promise or imply guaranteed returns
  • Push for a decision before the call ends

Close the loop with a written summary

Soon after the call, ideally the same day, send a short summary through your firm's approved channel. Include what was discussed, any decisions, the next steps with owners and dates, and documents still pending. For example: "Thank you for your time today. Summary: we reviewed your portfolio and discussed the education goal for 2032. Next steps: you'll share the updated nominee form by Friday; I'll send the scheme documents for the two options we discussed. Please note that market-linked investments carry risk."

A written summary prevents misunderstandings, gives the client something to share with family and creates a record for both sides. It also shows professionalism, which builds long-term trust.

Do
  • Send a summary the same day with next steps and dates
  • Use your firm's approved channel for client communication
Don't
  • Rely only on memory of what was agreed
  • Leave out risks or pending documents from the summary

Key takeaways

  • Prepare with an agenda, ready documents and a private, quiet place.
  • Verify identity through approved processes and never ask for OTPs or passwords.
  • Share one window, silence notifications and hide other clients' data.
  • Check understanding, put suitability first, disclose fees and never promise returns.
  • Send a same-day written summary with decisions, next steps and risks.

Quick check

A senior client struggles to join a video call for ten minutes. What should you do?

  1. Keep troubleshooting until the video works, since video is more personal
  2. Offer to continue on a phone call
  3. Reschedule for next week
  4. Ask their grandson to fix it later
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Offer to continue on a phone call

Switching to the phone respects the client's time and comfort.
