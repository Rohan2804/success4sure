Teams, Slack and WhatsApp Etiquette for Work

Choose the right chat channel for each kind of message and write chats that respect colleagues' time and boundaries. You will learn practical habits for threads, mentions, voice notes and work WhatsApp groups.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Pick the right channel for the message

Most workplaces now use several channels at once: email, a team chat tool such as Microsoft Teams or Slack, and often WhatsApp. Each suits different messages. Team chat works well for quick questions and day-to-day coordination. Email is better for formal communication, external parties and anything that needs a clear record, such as approvals.

WhatsApp is common in Indian workplaces, especially with field teams and some clients. Before using it for work, check your organisation's policy. Many firms, particularly in financial services, require client conversations and documents to stay on approved, recorded channels. Never send customer data such as account details or ID copies on a personal chat app unless your employer has explicitly approved that channel.

A simple rule: the more formal, sensitive or long-lasting the content, the more it belongs in an official, searchable channel.

Do
  • Use email or approved systems for approvals and client records
  • Check your company's policy on WhatsApp for work
Don't
  • Share client documents on personal chat apps
  • Give formal approvals only through an emoji reaction

One complete message, in the right place

Chat makes it easy to send thoughts in fragments: "Hi", "one thing", "the report", "can you check". Each fragment can trigger a notification. Instead, write one complete message with everything the reader needs.

Reply inside threads rather than in the main channel, so a discussion about the Jaipur event does not bury an announcement about payroll. Use @mentions when you need a specific person to act, and use channel-wide alerts like @channel or @everyone only for genuinely important, time-sensitive information. Notifying fifty people about a misplaced charger trains them to ignore alerts.

Keep work groups for work. Daily good-morning images, forwarded videos and festival greetings in a project group make real updates hard to find. If your team enjoys that, suggest a separate social group.

Do
  • Send one complete message instead of many fragments
  • Reply in threads to keep channels tidy
Don't
  • Use @everyone for routine questions
  • Forward unrelated videos to work groups

Tone: chat strips away your voice

In person, a smile softens "ok". In chat, "ok." with a full stop can read as annoyed, especially to someone senior or new. A few extra words help: "Okay, thanks, I'll update it by noon" sounds cooperative without being over the top.

Emojis can add warmth but use them according to the team's style. A thumbs-up reaction is a fine way to acknowledge a message in many teams; a string of laughing faces in a reply to your manager's serious update is not. When in doubt, follow how your manager and senior colleagues write.

Voice notes are popular on WhatsApp, but at work they are hard to skim, search or listen to in a meeting. Prefer text for instructions and anything with numbers. If you must send a voice note, keep it short and add a one-line text summary.

Do
  • Add a few words of warmth to short replies
  • Mirror the team's level of formality
Don't
  • Send long voice notes with numbers or instructions
  • Reply in all capitals when you are frustrated

Respect time and boundaries

Chat makes everyone feel reachable at all hours. Unless something is urgent, avoid messaging colleagues late at night or on holidays. If you work odd hours, use the scheduled-send option where available, or begin with "No need to reply until tomorrow".

Set your status when you are in a meeting, on leave or focusing, and switch on do-not-disturb outside work hours if your role allows it. Do not expect instant replies to non-urgent messages, and do not follow up within ten minutes with "??".

Remember that chat is not private. Screenshots travel. Do not criticise colleagues, share gossip or post anything in a work group that you would not be comfortable seeing in front of HR.

Do
  • Schedule messages for working hours when you can
  • Keep your status up to date
Don't
  • Follow up with "??" after a few minutes
  • Write anything you would not want screenshotted

Key takeaways

  • Match the channel to the message: chat for quick work, email or approved systems for formal records.
  • Never share client data on unapproved personal apps.
  • Send one complete message and reply in threads.
  • Use @everyone rarely and keep work groups for work.
  • Add warmth to short replies, prefer text to voice notes and respect off-hours.

Quick check

A client wants to send you a copy of their PAN and address proof on your personal WhatsApp. What should you do first?

  1. Accept it, since it is faster
  2. Ask them to send it to your personal email instead, which feels more official
  3. Check and use your firm's approved channel for documents
  4. Ask them to post it in the team group
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Check and use your firm's approved channel for documents

Client documents should only move through channels your organisation has approved.
