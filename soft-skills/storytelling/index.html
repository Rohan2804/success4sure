Turn ideas into stories people remember — in pitches, presentations and posts.

About this Storytelling & Pitching course

People forget claims but remember stories. This storytelling skills course online gives you simple structures you can use at work straight away. You will shape any experience into a situation–challenge–result story, learn how to pitch an idea in about a minute with a clear ask, and practise business storytelling with numbers so that data becomes a message people remember. You will also write social posts people read to the end. Freshers build a story bank for interviews and GDs, and finance professionals learn to use honest, clearly labelled stories to explain investing concepts.

What you will learn

  • Shape any work experience into a short situation–challenge–result story
  • Pitch an idea in 60 seconds with one proof point and a clear ask
  • Turn a page of numbers into one memorable message with honest charts
  • Write social media posts with a strong first line and one honest story
  • Build a story bank of five or six stories for interviews and GDs
  • Explain goals, time and risk to clients with clearly labelled illustrations

Who this course is for

  • Freshers preparing for interviews and group discussions
  • Early-career professionals who present ideas to managers
  • Anyone who shares numbers or reports with a team
  • Distributors, advisers and RMs explaining investing to clients

FAQs

Who is the Storytelling & Pitching course for?

Freshers preparing for or starting their first job, and finance professionals such as mutual fund distributors, investment advisers and relationship managers. Core lessons suit everyone; one lesson is written for each of the two groups.

Is this course free?

Yes. All 6 lessons, 60 quiz questions with explanations and 18 practice scenarios are free. You can start without an account; log in with Google to save your progress.

How can I improve my storytelling skills at work?

Start with a simple three-part structure: the situation in one or two lines, a concrete challenge and a clear result with a lesson. Cut background people do not need, use specific details and practise telling the story aloud in under a minute. Small, regular practice in meetings and updates builds the skill quickly.

How do I pitch an idea in 60 seconds?

Open with the problem your listener cares about, state your idea in one sentence, give one strong proof point and end with a small, clear ask such as a trial or a meeting. Avoid listing every feature. One good example or number is more convincing than five general claims.

How do I use stories to answer interview questions?

Prepare five or six short stories from college, internships or projects that show skills like teamwork, problem-solving and handling setbacks. Use a situation, challenge and result structure, similar to the STAR method, and fit the story to the question. Keep each answer to around a minute and finish with what you learnt.

What is data storytelling?

Data storytelling means turning numbers into a message people understand and remember. Pick the one number that matters most, add context such as a comparison with last year or a target, and use simple comparisons people can picture. Keep charts simple and check every figure before you share it.
