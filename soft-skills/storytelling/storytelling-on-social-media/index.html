Storytelling on Social Media

Write short posts on LinkedIn and similar platforms that people read to the end. You will learn to open with a strong first line, tell one small story honestly and protect other people's privacy.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

The first line decides everything

On LinkedIn, Instagram or X, people scroll fast. Most posts show only the first line or two before a "see more" link, so that opening has one job: make the right reader want to continue.

A strong hook is specific and promises something. "I'm happy to share that I completed a course" is a statement many people make every day. "I failed my first mock interview because I couldn't explain my own project" is a moment — the reader wants to know what happened next.

Hooks can be a surprising result, a mistake, a question or a small scene: "At 9:40 pm, the client called to cancel." Avoid tricks such as fake cliffhangers ("You won't believe what my boss said...") followed by an ordinary post. Readers notice, and it costs you trust.

Do
  • Open with a specific moment, result or mistake
Don't
  • Use clickbait openings the post doesn't deliver on

In the full lesson

  • One post, one small story
  • Stay honest about what happened
  • Protect other people's privacy

Key takeaways

  • Write a specific first line that earns the "see more" click.
  • Tell one small story per post in short paragraphs.
  • Keep stories and numbers honest; credit the team.
  • Never share client details, screenshots or confidential information.
  • Ask before posting about others, and follow your company's policy.

Quick check

Which first line is most likely to make the right reader click "see more"?

  1. "I'm happy to share that I completed a course."
  2. "Hello connections! Hope everyone is doing well and having a really productive week."
  3. "I failed my first mock interview because I couldn't explain my own project."
  4. "You won't believe this!!!"
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. "I failed my first mock interview because I couldn't explain my own project."

A specific moment creates curiosity and promises a real story.
