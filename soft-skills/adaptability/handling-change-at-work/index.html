Handling Change at Work

Respond to reorganisations, new managers and new tools with clear questions, practical learning steps and constructive feedback. You will be able to stay productive and credible while things around you shift.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Why change feels hard

Even good changes at work can feel unsettling. A reorganisation, a new reporting line or a new software system takes away familiar routines and adds uncertainty about your role. Feeling anxious or irritated at first is a normal reaction, not a sign that you are bad at change.

A helpful first step is to sort the situation into two lists: what you can control and what you cannot. You cannot control whether the department is merged. You can control how quickly you learn the new process, how you speak about the change and how well you do your current work.

Focusing on the second list gives you something useful to do with the energy that worry would otherwise consume.

Do
  • Separate what you control from what you don't
Don't
  • Judge yourself for feeling unsettled at first

In the full lesson

  • Get clarity, not gossip
  • Starting fresh with a new manager
  • Learning a new tool or process
  • Raise concerns constructively

Key takeaways

  • Focus on what you can control during change.
  • Ask your manager specific questions instead of trusting rumours.
  • Help a new manager with a one-page summary and ask how they like updates.
  • Expect a learning dip with new tools; practise on low-stakes tasks.
  • Raise concerns as problem, impact and suggestion.

Quick check

Your department is being merged with another. Which is within your control?

  1. Whether the merger happens
  2. Who will be appointed to head the new merged department
  3. How quickly you learn the new processes
  4. The final team structure
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. How quickly you learn the new processes

Focusing on what you can influence turns worry into useful action.
