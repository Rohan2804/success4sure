About this Adaptability & Learning How to Learn course

This adaptability skills course online helps you keep learning and stay steady when work keeps changing. You will see how to learn new skills faster using self-testing, spaced practice and teaching others, and how to apply a growth mindset at work through feedback and calm reviews of mistakes. Lessons cover reorganisations, new managers and new tools, planning a career switch step by step, and turning years of competitive exam preparation into strong job skills. Finance professionals get a routine for keeping up with regulatory changes and certifications.

What you will learn

  • Replace rereading and cramming with active recall, spaced practice and a weekly plan
  • Rewrite self-limiting thoughts and use feedback as data rather than a verdict
  • Ask clear questions and learn new tools quickly during reorganisations or manager changes
  • Plan a career switch by mapping transferable skills and testing with small experiments
  • Turn competitive exam preparation habits into learning skills for open-ended work tasks
  • Track regulatory updates from reliable sources and keep finance certifications current

Who this course is for

  • Freshers and professionals who need to learn new skills quickly
  • Employees facing restructuring, new managers or new tools
  • People considering a career change into a new field
  • Former government exam aspirants starting their first corporate job

FAQs

Who is the Adaptability & Learning How to Learn course for?

Freshers preparing for or starting their first job, and finance professionals such as mutual fund distributors, investment advisers and relationship managers. Core lessons suit everyone; one lesson is written for each of the two groups.

Is this course free?

Yes. All 6 lessons, 60 quiz questions with explanations and 18 practice scenarios are free. Create a free account (one click with Google) to start; your progress is saved across devices.

How can I learn new skills faster?

Test yourself instead of rereading, spread your practice across several short sessions and explain what you learned to someone else. These three techniques are well supported by research. Build them into a simple weekly plan with fixed slots, and skip popular myths such as learning only in your preferred style.

What does a growth mindset mean at work?

It means believing your abilities can improve with effort, good strategies and feedback, and acting on that belief. In practice you rewrite thoughts like "I'm just bad at this", treat feedback as useful data, review mistakes calmly and choose stretch goals. It is not blind positivity or ignoring real limits.

How do I handle sudden change at work?

Ask your manager clear questions about what is changing, what stays the same and what is expected of you, rather than relying on office gossip. Break any new tool or process into small learning steps, keep delivering your core work and raise concerns constructively with suggestions, not just complaints.

How do I plan a career switch in India?

Start with a clear reason for switching, then research what the new job is really like by talking to people who do it. Map your transferable skills and genuine gaps, test the move with short courses or side projects, and build a financial buffer before resigning. Prepare a confident story for interviews.
