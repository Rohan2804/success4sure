Discussing Salary and Offers with Confidence

Understand how CTC, fixed pay, variable pay and in-hand salary differ, and discuss expectations, notice periods and competing offers calmly. You will evaluate an offer on more than just the headline number.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Decode the offer: CTC versus in-hand

Indian offers are usually quoted as CTC, cost to company. CTC is the total the employer expects to spend on you in a year, and it often includes items you will not receive as monthly cash: the employer's provident fund contribution, gratuity provision, insurance premiums, and sometimes one-time joining bonuses or annual variable pay.

Your in-hand or take-home pay is what reaches your bank account each month after deductions such as your own PF contribution, professional tax and income tax. Depending on the structure, the gap between CTC divided by twelve and actual in-hand pay can be significant.

Always ask for, or look carefully at, the salary break-up (often called an annexure). Separate fixed pay from variable pay, and check how and when variable pay is paid. A variable component tied to targets or company performance is not guaranteed, so count it carefully when comparing offers.

Do
  • Ask for the full salary break-up before deciding
  • Estimate your monthly in-hand pay separately from CTC
Don't
  • Compare two offers on CTC alone
  • Assume the variable pay will be paid in full

Answering "What are your salary expectations?"

Do your homework before the interview: talk to seniors, alumni and people in similar roles, and look at multiple sources rather than one website. Turn this into a realistic range for the role, city and your experience.

Freshers in campus placements often have little room to negotiate, as packages are fixed for the batch. In that case, it is fine to say, "I understand the package for this role is standard; I am more focused on the learning and the role itself."

For lateral moves, you can share a range anchored on research and your current pay. For example: "My current CTC is X, with Y fixed. Based on the responsibilities here and what I have learned about similar roles, I am looking for something in the range of A to B, depending on the structure." If asked early, it is reasonable to ask to understand the role first, but avoid dodging the question repeatedly.

Be accurate about your current salary. Many employers verify it through payslips or background checks, and inflating it can lead to a withdrawn offer.

Negotiating respectfully

Negotiation in India is normal for experienced hires, and a polite, reasoned request rarely damages an offer. The key is to anchor on value and facts, not on need or pressure.

A useful pattern is appreciation, then the specific ask, then the reason. "Thank you, I am excited about this role. I was hoping the fixed component could be closer to X, because the role includes managing a larger client book than my current one and I would be giving up a retention bonus due in four months. Is there any flexibility?"

If salary cannot move, other things sometimes can: a joining bonus to cover a lost bonus, the joining date, an earlier review cycle, a better designation, or relocation support. Know in advance which matters most to you.

Never invent a competing offer. If you do have one, you can mention it honestly without turning it into a threat.

Do
  • Lead with appreciation and a specific, reasoned ask
  • Consider joining date, review timing and bonuses as well as salary
Don't
  • Bluff about other offers
  • Make demands based on personal expenses alone

Notice periods and accepting offers

Notice periods in Indian companies commonly range from 30 to 90 days, and new employers know this. State yours honestly. You can request an early release from your current employer, and some companies let you buy out the remaining notice, but do not promise a joining date you cannot control.

Once you accept an offer, treat it as a commitment. Accepting several offers and dropping out on the joining day is common enough that recruiters keep track of it, and the industry is smaller than it looks. If you change your mind, inform the company as early as possible.

Before signing, read the appointment letter: probation period, notice period after confirmation, bond or service agreement clauses, transfer liability, and any clawback conditions on joining bonuses. Ask HR to clarify anything that is unclear; that is a reasonable request, not a sign of mistrust.

Key takeaways

  • CTC is not your monthly in-hand pay; always study the salary break-up.
  • Treat variable pay as uncertain when comparing offers.
  • Base your expected range on research, and be truthful about your current salary.
  • Negotiate with appreciation, a specific ask and a reason.
  • Be honest about notice periods and treat an accepted offer as a commitment.
  • Read bond, probation and clawback clauses before signing.

Quick check

Which item is usually part of CTC but not part of your monthly in-hand pay?

  1. Basic salary
  2. House rent allowance (HRA)
  3. Employer's PF contribution
  4. Special or conveyance allowance
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Employer's PF contribution

The employer's PF contribution is counted in CTC but goes to your PF account, not your bank account each month.
