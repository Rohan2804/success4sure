A Resume and LinkedIn Profile That Get Shortlisted

Write a one- or two-page resume and a matching LinkedIn profile that show evidence of results, pass a quick screen and stay accurate. You will rewrite weak duty-style bullet points into outcome-based ones.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

How resumes are actually read

A recruiter screening for a bank or financial services role may look at dozens of resumes for one opening. The first pass is quick: role titles, employers or college, dates, certifications, and whether the top third of the page matches the job. Many companies also use applicant tracking systems that search for keywords from the job description.

So your resume has two readers: software that checks for relevant terms, and a busy person who decides in seconds whether to read further. Both reward the same things: clear headings, standard section names, relevant keywords used honestly, and evidence near the top.

Freshers should aim for one page. Professionals with several years of experience can use two, but the first page must stand on its own.

Structure that works

A practical order: name and contact details (phone, a professional email, city, LinkedIn link); a two- or three-line profile summary; experience or internships; education; certifications; skills; and optional achievements or activities. Freshers can place education above experience.

You do not need a photograph, date of birth, father's name, religion, marital status or a signed declaration at the bottom unless a specific employer or government form asks for them. These take space and add nothing to your suitability.

Use a clean single-column layout with a standard font. Fancy templates with graphics, tables and icons often confuse tracking software and are harder to read on a phone, which is where many recruiters first open them. Save and send it as a PDF named sensibly, for example Priya_Sharma_Resume.pdf.

Do
  • Keep sections in a standard order with plain headings
  • Send a PDF with your name in the file name
Don't
  • Add personal details that are irrelevant to the job
  • Use heavy graphics or multi-column templates

From duties to results

The most common weakness in Indian resumes is a list of duties copied from a job description: "Responsible for handling customers", "Worked on mutual fund sales". These tell the reader what the job was, not how well you did it.

Rewrite each bullet as action plus result plus scale. Before: "Responsible for client servicing." After: "Managed a book of about 120 HNI and affluent families; ran quarterly portfolio reviews and resolved service requests within agreed timelines." Before: "Did internship in accounts." After: "Reconciled 3 months of vendor ledgers for a mid-sized distributor during a 6-week internship, identifying duplicate entries that the finance team then corrected."

Numbers help, but they must be true and you must be able to explain them. If you do not know the exact figure, use an honest approximation such as "about" or "over", and never present your team's result as yours alone. Interviewers in finance often ask how a number was calculated.

Do
  • Start bullets with a strong verb
  • Add scale or outcome wherever it is true
Don't
  • Inflate numbers, titles or responsibilities
  • List a certification you have not yet cleared as completed

Tailoring and certifications

Keep a master resume with everything, then make a tailored copy for each type of role. For a branch operations job, move KYC, compliance and process accuracy higher. For a sales or RM role, lead with client relationships and outcomes.

List certifications exactly as they are: the full name, issuing body and year. If you are still preparing, write "NISM Series V-A: exam scheduled" or "in progress", never "certified". Recruiters in regulated businesses verify these, and a false claim can end your candidacy or later your job.

LinkedIn as your public resume

Your LinkedIn profile should match your resume on titles, employers and dates; mismatches raise questions. Use a clear, professional headshot and a headline that says what you do and want, such as "B.Com graduate | Banking operations and customer service" or "Relationship Manager | Mutual funds and insurance | NISM certified", rather than just "Student" or "Looking for opportunities".

The About section can be a slightly warmer, first-person version of your profile summary. Add skills that match the roles you want, and ask a former manager or professor for a short recommendation.

If you work in financial services, be careful with posts. Do not share client information, promise returns or recommend specific products publicly; your employer and regulations may restrict this. Sharing learning, industry news with your own thoughtful view, or a course you completed is safe and builds credibility.

Key takeaways

  • Your resume is read by both software and a busy recruiter, so keep it clear and keyword-relevant.
  • Replace duty statements with action, result and scale.
  • Every number and certification must be true and explainable.
  • Tailor a copy of your master resume for each type of role.
  • Keep LinkedIn consistent with your resume and professional in tone, especially in regulated jobs.

Quick check

Which resume bullet is strongest?

  1. "Responsible for mutual fund sales and related customer servicing."
  2. "Handled various customer-related activities across the branch counters."
  3. "Hard-working team player with good communication and people skills."
  4. "Opened about 60 new SIP accounts in six months via branch walk-ins."
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. "Opened about 60 new SIP accounts in six months via branch walk-ins."

It shows action, scale and outcome rather than listing a duty or adjectives.
