Common HR Questions and the STAR Method

Recognise what common HR questions are testing and answer behavioural questions with clear, evidence-based STAR stories. You will build a small bank of real examples you can adapt on the spot.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

What HR rounds are looking for

The HR round is not a formality. While the technical or functional panel checks whether you can do the job, HR checks whether you will do it well with other people, stay long enough to be worth training, and handle pressure without creating problems.

Most HR questions fall into a few families: motivation ("Why this company?"), self-awareness ("What is your weakness?"), behaviour ("Tell me about a time you handled conflict"), and practicalities (relocation, notice period, shifts). Once you know which family a question belongs to, it becomes much easier to answer.

Honesty matters more than polish here. HR interviewers hear rehearsed answers all day and are good at spotting them.

Key takeaways

  • HR questions fall into families: motivation, self-awareness, behaviour and practicalities.
  • STAR keeps behavioural answers focused: short Situation and Task, detailed Action, clear Result.
  • Use "I" to make your own contribution visible.
  • Pick a real weakness and show what you are doing to improve it.
  • A bank of six to eight real stories covers most behavioural questions.

Quick check

In a STAR answer, which part should usually take the most time?

  1. Situation
  2. Task
  3. Action
  4. Result
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Action

The Action shows your own behaviour and decisions, which is what the interviewer is assessing.
