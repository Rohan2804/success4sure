Investing Basics: Compounding, Risk and Time

Understand the core ideas behind investing: compounding, inflation, risk versus return, diversification and time horizon. You will learn to judge claims critically and see why starting early and investing regularly matter.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Educational content only — not investment, tax or financial advice. Consider your own situation or consult a SEBI-registered investment adviser before making financial decisions.

Why money needs to grow: inflation

Prices tend to rise over time. This is inflation, and it quietly reduces what your money can buy. For example, if prices rise by 6% a year, something that costs ₹100 today would cost about ₹179 in ten years.

So money that sits idle for long periods loses buying power, even though the number in your account stays the same. That is the main reason people invest money they will not need for many years: to try to grow it faster than prices rise.

This does not mean all savings must be invested. Your emergency fund and money for near-term needs belong somewhere stable. Investing is for goals that are several years away, after the basics such as clearing expensive debt are in place.

Do
  • Separate short-term money from long-term money
  • Clear high-interest debt before investing
Don't
  • Assume money in an account keeps its value over decades

In the full lesson

  • Compounding: growth on growth
  • Risk and return go together
  • Diversification, time horizon and regular investing

Key takeaways

  • Inflation reduces what idle money can buy over time.
  • Compounding rewards starting early and staying invested.
  • Higher potential return comes with higher risk; nothing is high-return and risk-free.
  • Diversify, and match investments to each goal's time horizon.
  • A SIP is a method of regular investing, not a product or a promise.

Quick check

For example, prices rise 6% a year. What is roughly true of ₹100 kept idle for ten years?

  1. It will buy more than it does today
  2. It will buy about the same
  3. It will buy about what ₹56 buys today
  4. It will roughly double in value, as bank balances grow automatically
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. It will buy about what ₹56 buys today

At 6% a year, ₹100 has the buying power of about ₹56 in today's money after ten years.
