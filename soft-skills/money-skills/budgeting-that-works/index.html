Budgeting That Actually Works

Set up a simple budget you can actually stick to, using a split like 50/30/20 as a starting point. You will learn to spot where small UPI payments leak money and plan for yearly expenses before they surprise you.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Educational content only — not investment, tax or financial advice. Consider your own situation or consult a SEBI-registered investment adviser before making financial decisions.

Start with a simple split, then adjust

A budget is just a plan for where your money goes before the month begins. A popular starting point is the 50/30/20 split: about 50% of take-home pay for needs, 30% for wants and 20% for savings and debt repayment.

For example, on an in-hand salary of ₹40,000, that is ₹20,000 for needs such as rent, groceries, commute and phone; ₹12,000 for wants such as eating out, shopping and subscriptions; and ₹8,000 for savings.

Treat these numbers as a starting point, not a rule. If you share a flat in Mumbai, needs may take 60% and you might settle for 60/20/20. If you live with your parents, you may be able to save far more than 20%. The useful part is the habit of deciding in advance, not hitting exact percentages.

Do
  • Adjust the split to your city and situation
  • Decide the savings amount first
Don't
  • Give up because your numbers do not match 50/30/20 exactly

Needs, wants and the grey areas

Sorting spending into needs and wants sounds easy until you meet the grey areas. A phone plan is a need; the most expensive plan may not be. Getting to office is a need; a cab every day instead of the metro is partly a want.

A practical test: ask what the basic version costs. Count that as a need and the extra as a want. This is not about guilt. Wants are a planned part of the budget, and enjoying them is fine as long as they fit inside their share.

Write down your fixed needs first, because they are hardest to change: rent, EMIs, insurance, family support and commute. If they already take most of your pay, that is important information about your next rent or your next loan decision.

Do
  • Count the basic version as a need and the upgrade as a want
  • List fixed costs before anything else
Don't
  • Label every expense a need to avoid cutting it

Watch the UPI leak

UPI has made paying effortless, and that is exactly why small spends slip past. ₹40 for chai, ₹120 for a snack delivery, ₹199 for a subscription: none feels like much. But for example, ₹150 a day adds up to about ₹4,500 a month, which may be more than your monthly savings target.

Once a week, spend five minutes scrolling through your UPI and bank history. Do not judge, just notice. Most people find two or three patterns they did not realise, such as late-night food orders or subscriptions they forgot to cancel.

Some people find it helps to keep a separate account for day-to-day UPI spending and move only their wants budget into it each month. When it runs low, the month's wants are done.

Do
  • Review your UPI history once a week
  • Cancel subscriptions you have not used in a month
Don't
  • Ignore small payments because each one feels tiny

Plan for the expenses that are not monthly

Many budgets fail not because of daily spending but because of yearly or occasional costs: Diwali gifts, a wedding in the family, insurance premiums, a laptop repair or the security deposit for a new flat. They are predictable, but they arrive in lumps.

The fix is to divide them across the year. For example, if you expect to spend ₹18,000 on festivals and gifts every year, set aside ₹1,500 a month for it. When October comes, the money is ready and your regular budget is untouched.

Keep the budget itself simple: a notes app, a spreadsheet or a notebook all work. Review it monthly, and if one category keeps overflowing, change the plan rather than blaming yourself. A budget you adjust is better than a perfect one you abandon.

Do
  • Divide yearly costs into monthly amounts
  • Review and adjust the budget every month
Don't
  • Treat festivals and premiums as surprises every year

Key takeaways

  • Use 50/30/20 as a starting point and adjust it to your life.
  • Count the basic version as a need and the upgrade as a want.
  • Review UPI and bank history weekly to catch small leaks.
  • Turn yearly costs into a monthly set-aside.
  • Adjust a budget that is not working instead of abandoning it.

Quick check

Your take-home pay is ₹50,000. Under a 50/30/20 split, how much goes to savings?

  1. ₹15,000
  2. ₹25,000
  3. ₹10,000
  4. ₹5,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹10,000

20% of ₹50,000 is ₹10,000.
