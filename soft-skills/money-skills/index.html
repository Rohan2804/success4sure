About this Money Skills for Young Earners course

Your first salary brings freedom, and also new questions. This personal finance course for beginners explains the basics in plain language. You will learn how to plan your first salary, why CTC differs from your in-hand pay and why an emergency fund comes first. Practical budgeting tips for India cover the 50/30/20 split, small UPI payments and yearly expenses. You will also understand credit cards, EMIs and buy-now-pay-later, core investing ideas and common scams. The course is educational only and does not recommend any product or promise returns.

What you will learn

  • Understand the difference between CTC and in-hand salary on your payslip
  • Build an emergency fund and automate saving with a pay-yourself-first habit
  • Set up a budget using 50/30/20 and plan for non-monthly expenses
  • Avoid the minimum-due trap and understand how credit scores and EMIs work
  • Understand compounding, inflation, risk, diversification and time horizon in plain words
  • Spot fake job, loan app and UPI collect-request scams and respond quickly

Who this course is for

  • Freshers who have just received their first salary
  • Students and young earners new to managing money
  • Anyone confused by credit cards, EMIs or buy-now-pay-later
  • Finance professionals who want better money-habit conversations with clients

Why is my in-hand salary lower than my CTC?

CTC, or cost to company, includes items that do not reach your bank account each month, such as the employer's provident fund contribution, gratuity, insurance and some allowances or bonuses. Deductions like your own PF share, professional tax and income tax are also subtracted. Your payslip shows the exact breakdown.

What is the 50/30/20 budget rule?

It is a simple starting split: about 50 percent of take-home pay for needs such as rent and bills, 30 percent for wants and 20 percent for saving. It is a guide, not a fixed rule. In a high-rent city you may need to adjust it, so track your spending and tweak the split.

What happens if I pay only the minimum due on my credit card?

Paying only the minimum due avoids a late fee, but interest is charged on the remaining balance, usually at a high rate, and often on new purchases too. The debt can grow quickly and take a long time to clear. Paying the full statement amount each month avoids interest.

How can I recognise a UPI or job scam?

Warning signs include being asked to pay a fee for a job, earn money by completing simple tasks, approve a UPI collect request to 'receive' money, share an OTP or install a screen-sharing app. Never share OTPs or PINs. If you are targeted, contact your bank immediately and report it on the cybercrime helpline 1930.
