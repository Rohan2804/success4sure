Money Scams Targeting Students and Freshers

Recognise the money scams most often aimed at students and freshers, from fake job fees to UPI collect-request fraud. You will learn the warning signs, what never to share, and exactly what to do if you are targeted.

Last updated 5 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Educational content only — not investment, tax or financial advice. Consider your own situation or consult a SEBI-registered investment adviser before making financial decisions.

Fake jobs and task scams

Job seekers are prime targets. A message on WhatsApp or a job portal offers an interview or an offer letter, then asks for a "registration fee", "training kit charge" or "security deposit". Genuine employers do not ask candidates to pay for a job, an interview or an offer letter. If the offer arrived without any real interview, treat it as a warning sign.

Task scams are a newer twist. You are offered easy money to like videos, review hotels or rate products. The first few tasks pay small amounts, often ₹100 to ₹300, to build trust. Then you are moved to a Telegram group and asked to "invest" in prepaid tasks for bigger rewards. The balance on your screen grows, but when you try to withdraw, there is always another fee.

A simple rule: if you have to pay money to earn money from a stranger online, stop.

Do
  • Verify jobs on the company's official careers page
  • Search the recruiter's name and company domain
Don't
  • Pay any fee for an interview, offer letter or training kit
  • Deposit money to unlock task earnings

In the full lesson

  • Loan apps and "tips" groups
  • OTP, UPI collect requests and screen sharing
  • Money mules and what to do if you are targeted

Key takeaways

  • Genuine employers never charge for interviews, offer letters or training kits.
  • If you must pay a stranger to earn money online, it is a scam.
  • You never need a UPI PIN or OTP to receive money.
  • Check lender and adviser registration; ignore profit screenshots.
  • If targeted, call 1930, report at cybercrime.gov.in and contact your bank at once.

Quick check

A company emails you an offer letter and asks for a ₹2,500 refundable "training kit" fee. What is it most likely?

  1. A standard onboarding step
  2. A job scam
  3. A government requirement
  4. A way to show commitment
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A job scam

Genuine employers do not charge candidates for offer letters or training kits.
