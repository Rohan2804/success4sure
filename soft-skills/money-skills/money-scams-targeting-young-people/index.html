Money Scams Targeting Students and Freshers

Recognise the money scams most often aimed at students and freshers, from fake job fees to UPI collect-request fraud. You will learn the warning signs, what never to share, and exactly what to do if you are targeted.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Educational content only — not investment, tax or financial advice. Consider your own situation or consult a SEBI-registered investment adviser before making financial decisions.

Fake jobs and task scams

Job seekers are prime targets. A message on WhatsApp or a job portal offers an interview or an offer letter, then asks for a "registration fee", "training kit charge" or "security deposit". Genuine employers do not ask candidates to pay for a job, an interview or an offer letter. If the offer arrived without any real interview, treat it as a warning sign.

Task scams are a newer twist. You are offered easy money to like videos, review hotels or rate products. The first few tasks pay small amounts, often ₹100 to ₹300, to build trust. Then you are moved to a Telegram group and asked to "invest" in prepaid tasks for bigger rewards. The balance on your screen grows, but when you try to withdraw, there is always another fee.

A simple rule: if you have to pay money to earn money from a stranger online, stop.

Do
  • Verify jobs on the company's official careers page
  • Search the recruiter's name and company domain
Don't
  • Pay any fee for an interview, offer letter or training kit
  • Deposit money to unlock task earnings

Loan apps and "tips" groups

Instant loan apps advertise money in minutes with no documents. Some are predatory: they ask for access to your contacts and photos, charge hidden fees, shorten repayment periods without warning and harass borrowers and their families. If you need to borrow, use lenders regulated by the RBI or their disclosed partners, read the total cost, and never grant contacts or gallery access.

Investment "tips" groups on WhatsApp and Telegram show screenshots of big profits and claim insider knowledge. Members are often pushed to install an unfamiliar trading app or website that shows impressive gains, but withdrawals are blocked or require more deposits. Many of the happy members are part of the scam.

In India, anyone giving investment advice for a fee must be registered with SEBI. A large following, confident language and profit screenshots prove nothing.

Do
  • Check lender and adviser registration on official regulator websites
  • Leave groups that promise sure-shot profits
Don't
  • Install trading apps shared through chat links
  • Believe profit screenshots from strangers

OTP, UPI collect requests and screen sharing

Here is the single most useful fact: you never need to enter your UPI PIN or share an OTP to receive money. If someone says "enter your PIN to receive the refund" or sends a collect request labelled as a payment to you, approving it will send money out of your account.

Common scripts include a buyer on a resale site who says they will "send money" via a QR code, a fake customer care number found through a search engine, or a caller claiming your KYC has expired and your account will be blocked today. Some ask you to install a screen-sharing app so they can "help", which lets them watch your OTPs.

Banks and genuine services will not ask for your OTP, PIN, CVV or full card details over a call or chat. When in doubt, hang up and call the number printed on your card or shown on the bank's official website.

Do
  • Get customer care numbers only from official websites or apps
  • Decline collect requests you did not expect
Don't
  • Share OTP, UPI PIN or CVV with anyone
  • Install screen-sharing apps at a caller's request

Money mules and what to do if you are targeted

Students are sometimes offered a commission to "lend" their bank account or UPI ID to receive and forward money. This is not a side income. Those accounts are used to move stolen money, and the account holder can face frozen accounts and police action.

If you have lost money or shared details, act fast, because the first hours matter. Call the national cybercrime helpline 1930 and report the incident at cybercrime.gov.in. Call your bank's official helpline to block your card or UPI and report the transaction. Keep screenshots of messages, phone numbers, UPI IDs and transaction references.

Feeling embarrassed is normal, but scammers are skilled professionals and anyone can be targeted. Telling friends and family what happened also helps protect them.

Do
  • Report quickly on 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in
  • Save screenshots and transaction IDs
Don't
  • Let anyone use your bank account or UPI ID
  • Stay silent out of embarrassment

Key takeaways

  • Genuine employers never charge for interviews, offer letters or training kits.
  • If you must pay a stranger to earn money online, it is a scam.
  • You never need a UPI PIN or OTP to receive money.
  • Check lender and adviser registration; ignore profit screenshots.
  • If targeted, call 1930, report at cybercrime.gov.in and contact your bank at once.
