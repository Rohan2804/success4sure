Planning Your Week in 20 Minutes

Run a simple 20-minute weekly planning routine: review last week, capture everything, choose three outcomes and place them in your calendar. You will also learn a five-minute daily check to keep the plan realistic.

Last updated 5 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Why plan the week, not just the day

Daily to-do lists are useful, but they tend to fill up with whatever shouted loudest that morning. A weekly plan gives you a wider view: which deadlines are coming, which days are full of meetings, and where the time for important work can actually fit.

It does not need to be elaborate. Twenty minutes on Sunday evening or first thing Monday, with a notebook or your calendar open, is enough. The routine below has four five-minute steps. Once it becomes a habit, many people finish it in fifteen.

Do
  • Pick a fixed time for weekly planning, such as Sunday evening
  • Keep the routine short enough to do every week
Don't
  • Plan only one day at a time when deadlines are days away

In the full lesson

  • Steps 1 and 2: Review and capture (10 minutes)
  • Step 3: Choose three outcomes (5 minutes)
  • Step 4: Put it in the calendar (5 minutes)
  • The five-minute daily check

Key takeaways

  • Spend 20 minutes each week: review, capture, choose, schedule.
  • Write down everything pending, including personal tasks.
  • Choose up to three outcomes written as finished results.
  • Place focus blocks in the calendar and leave buffer time.
  • Do a five-minute daily check and adjust, don't abandon.

Quick check

Which is written as an outcome rather than an activity?

  1. "Work on the training deck"
  2. "Look into the vendor issue"
  3. "Think about the budget"
  4. "Budget sheet sent to finance by Wednesday"
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. "Budget sheet sent to finance by Wednesday"

An outcome names a finished result and a day, so you know when it is done.
