About this Productivity & Focus course

This productivity course online is for anyone who ends the day busy but unsure what actually moved forward. You will learn how to focus on work by cutting unnecessary notifications, protecting deep-work blocks and planning your week in just 20 minutes. Lessons show how to tell busy-work from real progress, renegotiate low-value requests and stop over-polishing. If you want to be more productive at work while preparing for exams or a course, there is a realistic routine for that too, plus a lesson on running productive days of client meetings.

What you will learn

  • Audit notifications and move your phone so it stops pulling you away
  • Plan deep-work blocks with a clear finish line and protect them from interruptions
  • Run a 20-minute weekly plan and a five-minute daily check
  • Spot busy-work, judge your day by results and renegotiate low-value requests
  • Fit exam preparation or upskilling around a full-time job with fixed study slots
  • Cluster client meetings, prepare in ten minutes and finish notes the same day

Who this course is for

  • Freshers and young professionals distracted by their phones
  • Professionals who feel busy all day but achieve little
  • Working people preparing for exams or courses alongside a job
  • Relationship managers and advisers with packed client meeting days

Who is the Productivity & Focus course for?

How can I focus on work without getting distracted by my phone?

Turn off non-essential notifications, keep your phone out of sight and reach during focused work and check it at set times rather than whenever it buzzes. If work messages arrive on your phone, separate work and personal apps where possible. Small changes to where the phone sits make a surprisingly big difference.

What is deep work and how do I start?

Deep work is uninterrupted time spent on your most demanding tasks, such as analysis, writing or planning. Start with one 60–90 minute block a day, decide the finish line before you begin, block it in your calendar and silence interruptions. End by noting the next step so restarting is easy.

How do I plan my week effectively?

Spend about 20 minutes once a week: review last week, capture every pending task, choose three outcomes that matter most and place them in your calendar first. Then do a five-minute check each morning to adjust for new requests. Planning the week keeps important work from being swallowed by urgent tasks.

How can I study for exams while working full time?

Map your real free time honestly, choose one main goal per season and fix regular study slots instead of waiting for spare time. Keep work and study separate, protect your sleep and plan lighter study weeks during busy work periods. A weekly check helps you adjust without guilt.
