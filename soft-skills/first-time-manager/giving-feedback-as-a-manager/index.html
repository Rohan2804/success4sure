Giving Feedback as a Manager

Give specific praise, useful corrective feedback and handle difficult conversations calmly. You will learn a simple situation-behaviour-impact structure and phrases you can use the same day.

Last updated 5 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Feedback is part of the job, not an extra

Many new managers avoid feedback because it feels awkward, especially with people who were recently their peers. So they save it all for the annual appraisal. The result is a team member who hears in March about a problem that started in July, and feels ambushed.

Good managers give small, frequent feedback, both positive and corrective, close to the moment it happens. A two-minute conversation on Tuesday about Monday's client call is more useful than a paragraph in a review form months later.

Aim for a healthy balance. If the only time people hear from you is when something goes wrong, they will start to dread your messages. If you only ever praise, your praise loses value and real problems grow.

Do
  • Give feedback within a day or two of the event
  • Give both recognition and correction regularly
Don't
  • Save all feedback for the annual appraisal

In the full lesson

  • Use situation, behaviour, impact
  • Recognition that actually means something
  • Correcting in private, and in the right setting
  • Handling a difficult conversation

Key takeaways

  • Give small, frequent feedback close to the event.
  • Use situation, behaviour, impact — then ask what they'd do differently.
  • Make praise specific, sincere and fairly shared.
  • Praise in public; correct in private.
  • Prepare for difficult conversations and end with clear next steps.

Quick check

Which is the most useful corrective feedback?

  1. "You need to be more careful with your work, especially with the client reports."
  2. "In Monday's Mehta update the figures were last quarter's, and the client doubted our accuracy."
  3. "You're always making silly mistakes in reports and it reflects badly on us."
  4. "Please improve your attention to detail going forward, as this keeps happening with client work."
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. "In Monday's Mehta update the figures were last quarter's, and the client doubted our accuracy."

It names the situation, behaviour and impact, so the person knows exactly what to fix.
