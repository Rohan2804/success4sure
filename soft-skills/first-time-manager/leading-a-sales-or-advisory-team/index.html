Leading a Sales or Advisory Team Ethically

Lead relationship managers or advisers so that targets are met through suitable advice, not mis-selling. You will learn how to set balanced goals, coach client conversations, handle complaints and escalate compliance concerns.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Your team copies what you reward

As a new branch or team manager in a bank, AMC distributor or advisory firm, you sit between business targets from above and clients who trust your team with their savings. That position carries real influence: your team will learn far more from what you celebrate than from any compliance training.

If the only thing praised in the morning huddle is the day's highest sale, people hear "sell at any cost." If you also recognise the relationship manager who talked a 62-year-old client out of a high-risk product that did not fit her needs, they hear that suitability matters here.

Targets are a normal part of financial services. The ethical question is not whether to have them but how they are met. Say it plainly and often: "I want the numbers, but only from the right product for the right client."

Do
  • Recognise good advice, not just big sales
  • Repeat that suitability comes first
Don't
  • Celebrate only the highest number of the day

Set goals that do not reward mis-selling

Look beyond sales volume when you review your team. Useful quality signals include complete risk-profiling and need-analysis records, complaints received, early surrenders, redemptions or cancellations soon after a sale, and feedback from client reviews. A spike in policies cancelled within the free-look period or funds redeemed within weeks is a warning sign.

Discuss how a number was achieved, not just whether it was. "Your SIP numbers are strong this month. Walk me through two of those clients: what were their goals and why this fund?" makes quality part of every review.

Be careful about pushing particular products because they pay higher commission or because there is a contest running. Recommendations must follow the client's needs, risk profile and time horizon. Clients should also be told, in line with applicable rules, what they are paying and how the adviser or distributor is paid.

Do
  • Track complaints and early exits alongside sales
  • Ask how each number was achieved
Don't
  • Push a product because it pays more commission
  • Let contests override client needs

Coach the client conversation

The best way to raise both quality and results is to coach real conversations. Join client meetings or listen to calls with consent and in line with company policy, then give feedback using specific examples.

Watch for risky phrases. "This fund is as safe as an FD", "Guaranteed 12% returns" or "This NFO will double your money" are misleading. Coach replacements: "Mutual funds carry market risk; this one's risk level is moderately high, and returns are not guaranteed. Past performance doesn't promise future returns."

Help your team ask better questions before recommending anything: What is this money for? When will you need it? How would you feel if it fell 15% in a bad year? A relationship manager who understands the client's goal can recommend with confidence and is less tempted to oversell.

Do
  • Coach with specific phrases from real calls
  • Train needs-first questions
Don't
  • Let "guaranteed" or "as safe as an FD" go uncorrected

Handle complaints properly

When a client complains, your first job is to listen and understand, not to defend your team. "I understand you were told this policy had no lock-in. Let me look into exactly what happened and come back to you by Thursday" is calm and specific.

Then collect the facts: the documents signed, the risk profile, call notes and what the team member says. Follow your firm's grievance process and timelines, and record the complaint in the official system. Do not try to settle it quietly to protect your branch's numbers.

After resolving it, look for patterns. If three complaints in a quarter mention the same product or the same explanation, that is a coaching or process issue, not bad luck.

Do
  • Acknowledge, investigate and respond by a stated date
  • Log every complaint in the official system
Don't
  • Defend your team before you know the facts
  • Hide complaints to protect branch numbers

Escalate compliance concerns

Sometimes you will see something worse than a weak conversation: a signature that looks forged, transactions done without a client's instruction, a senior colleague pressuring the team to switch clients' investments for commission, or a target that can only be met by mis-selling.

Do not ignore it or handle it quietly. Write down what you saw with dates and facts, and escalate through your compliance team or the firm's whistle-blower or ethics channel. Raising a concern in good faith is part of your job as a manager.

Treat the people involved fairly. Do not accuse anyone in front of the team or decide guilt yourself; let the proper process establish the facts. And if you yourself are pressured to look the other way, remember that the client's interest and your professional registration both depend on getting this right.

Do
  • Record facts and escalate through compliance or ethics channels
  • Let the formal process decide
Don't
  • Cover up a problem to protect a team member
  • Accuse someone publicly before facts are established

Key takeaways

  • Your team copies what you praise — recognise suitable advice, not just sales.
  • Review complaints, early exits and needs-analysis quality alongside volume.
  • Coach real conversations and correct any promise of returns.
  • Handle complaints by listening, investigating and logging them properly.
  • Escalate serious concerns through compliance; never cover them up.

Quick check

Which huddle message best supports ethical selling?

  1. "Whoever sells the most today gets an early leave."
  2. "I want the numbers, but only from the right product for the right client."
  3. "Focus on the product with the best payout this month."
  4. "Don't overthink suitability; clients are adults and can decide for themselves."
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. "I want the numbers, but only from the right product for the right client."

It sets the target and the standard for how it is met.
