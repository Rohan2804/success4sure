Running Useful One-on-Ones

Run regular one-on-ones that your team finds useful rather than a status check. You will learn how to set a rhythm, use good questions and follow through on what you hear.

Last updated 5 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

What a one-on-one is for

A one-on-one is a short, regular meeting between you and one team member. Its main purpose is not to collect status updates; you can get those from a tracker or a standup. It is the person's time to raise concerns, get help, discuss growth and build a working relationship with you.

Think of it as preventive maintenance. Small issues, such as confusion about priorities, friction with a colleague or a growing interest in another role, surface early here instead of turning into a resignation letter.

A useful rule of thumb: the team member should do most of the talking. If you speak for 25 minutes of a 30-minute slot, it has become your meeting, not theirs.

Do
  • Treat it as their meeting more than yours
Don't
  • Turn it into a project status update

In the full lesson

  • Set a rhythm and protect it
  • A simple structure
  • Questions that open people up
  • Follow through, and know your limits

Key takeaways

  • A one-on-one is the team member's time, not a status update.
  • Hold it regularly and reschedule rather than cancel.
  • Use a light structure: their topics, your topics, actions.
  • Ask open, specific questions and then listen.
  • Follow through on every action you agree to.

Quick check

What is the main purpose of a one-on-one?

  1. To collect detailed status updates on every task and deadline the person has
  2. To check attendance and log-in times
  3. To give the team member time to raise concerns, get help and discuss growth
  4. To review the team's overall targets
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. To give the team member time to raise concerns, get help and discuss growth

Status updates belong in trackers; one-on-ones are for the person.
