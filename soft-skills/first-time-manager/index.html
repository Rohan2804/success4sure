First-Time Manager

Make the jump from peer to boss — delegate, give feedback and lead your first team.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · Our editorial process

Start lesson 1Open in the app

About this First-Time Manager course

Being promoted is exciting, but managing people you used to sit beside is a different job. This first time manager training online helps you handle the move from peer to boss, reset relationships fairly and earn credibility in your first 90 days. You will pick up practical new manager tips, build delegation skills, give specific feedback with a simple structure and run one-on-ones your team actually values. If you are not managing yet, you will learn to show leadership without the title. Sales and advisory team leaders learn to meet targets without rewarding mis-selling.

What you will learn

  • Reset relationships and set fair boundaries after being promoted over former peers
  • Decide what to delegate and brief people so they can deliver
  • Give praise and corrective feedback using situation, behaviour and impact
  • Run regular one-on-ones with good questions and clear follow-through
  • Show leadership in your first job through reliability, initiative and peer influence
  • Lead advisers or RMs with balanced goals, coaching and proper complaint handling

Who this course is for

  • Newly promoted team leads and first-time managers
  • Professionals expecting to manage a team soon
  • Freshers who want to show leadership early
  • Branch heads and leaders of sales or advisory teams

FAQs

Who is the First-Time Manager course for?

Freshers preparing for or starting their first job, and finance professionals such as mutual fund distributors, investment advisers and relationship managers. Core lessons suit everyone; one lesson is written for each of the two groups.

Is this course free?

Yes. All 6 lessons, 60 quiz questions with explanations and 18 practice scenarios are free. Create a free account (one click with Google) to start; your progress is saved across devices.

How do I manage people who used to be my peers?

Have an honest reset conversation early, acknowledging that the relationship has changed and asking how you can support each person. Stay friendly but be visibly fair in decisions, avoid favourites and stop trying to be the team's best individual worker. Credibility comes from consistent actions over your first few months.

How should a new manager delegate work?

Choose tasks that match each person's skills and growth, then brief clearly: the outcome, why it matters, the deadline, resources and how you will check in. Give more freedom to experienced people and more structure to newer ones. Follow up at agreed points rather than hovering, which is the line between support and micromanaging.

What questions should I ask in a one-on-one meeting?

Use open questions such as 'What is going well?', 'What is getting in your way?', 'What would you like more or less of from me?' and 'What are you hoping to learn this quarter?'. Let the team member lead most of the time, note what you hear and follow through on what you promise.

How can I show leadership without a manager title?

Start by being completely reliable: meet deadlines, communicate early and own mistakes. Then take sensible initiative, such as fixing a small process or helping a new joiner, while keeping your manager informed. Influence peers by listening and sharing credit, and tell your manager calmly that you are interested in leading.
