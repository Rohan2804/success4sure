Taking Feedback and Working with Your First Manager

Receive feedback without getting defensive, turn vague comments into specific actions using the SBI model, and build a strong working relationship with your first manager.

Last updated 5 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Why feedback feels different at work

During exam preparation, feedback is clear: a mock test score tells you exactly where you stand. At work, comments are quicker, less structured and sometimes delivered in passing, such as 'This needs more work' or 'Be more proactive.'

It is natural to feel defensive, especially if you were a top performer in college or cleared a competitive exam. But early feedback is information, not a verdict on your ability. A manager who takes time to correct you is usually investing in you; the more worrying sign is silence.

Your aim in the first six months is to shorten the gap between doing something and learning how it landed.

Key takeaways

  • Treat early feedback as information about how your work landed.
  • Use Situation–Behaviour–Impact to turn vague comments into specifics.
  • Ask your manager early how they like updates and what you can decide alone.
  • Bring a possible solution along with every problem you raise.
  • Ask specific feedback questions often and keep a simple log.

Quick check

Your manager says, 'Your reports need to be more detailed.' What is the best follow-up?

  1. Add more pages to every report from now on
  2. Explain why you kept the reports short
  3. Ask for an example and what they expected instead
  4. Ask colleagues whether they agree with the manager
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Ask for an example and what they expected instead

A concrete example shows you exactly what 'more detailed' means to this manager.
