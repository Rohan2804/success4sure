Handling Client Objections and Difficult Conversations

Handle client concerns about market falls, fees and unsuitable requests with a calm, structured approach. You will learn to stay transparent, avoid promising returns and say no when a request isn't right for the client.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

A framework for any objection

Objections are normal. A client who questions your recommendation is engaged, not hostile. A simple five-step sequence keeps you calm and useful: Listen, Acknowledge, Clarify, Respond, Confirm. Listen fully without jumping in. Acknowledge the concern: 'It's understandable to feel uneasy when the portfolio is down.' Clarify what lies behind it: 'Is it the overall fall, or a worry about a particular goal?' Respond with facts relevant to their situation. Confirm what happens next and record it.

The aim is not to win the conversation or close a sale. It is to help the client make an informed decision that fits their goals and risk profile, even if that decision is to do nothing or invest less.

Keep notes of significant conversations, especially when a client acts against your suggestion. Clear records protect both the client and you.

In the full lesson

  • When markets fall
  • Questions about fees and how you are paid
  • Pushy clients and unsuitable requests

Key takeaways

  • Use Listen, Acknowledge, Clarify, Respond, Confirm for any objection.
  • In market falls, return to the client's goals and never predict recoveries or returns.
  • Explain plainly how you are paid and what the client receives for it.
  • Say no to unsuitable requests, offer alternatives and document the outcome.
  • Refuse rule-breaking requests outright and escalate to compliance if pushed.

Quick check

A client calls during a sharp market fall and asks, 'Should I sell everything?' What is the best first step?

  1. Acknowledge the worry; ask about the goal and timeline
  2. Assure them the market will recover within a few months
  3. Advise them to sell to avoid further losses
  4. Tell them to stop checking their portfolio until things improve
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Acknowledge the worry; ask about the goal and timeline

Understanding the goal and time horizon is the basis for any sound response; predictions and reflex advice are not.
