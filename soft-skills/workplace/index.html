About this Workplace Effectiveness course

This workplace skills training online covers the everyday habits that make you effective and easy to work with. You will learn to prioritise by urgency and importance, plan realistic weeks, set clear roles in a team and disagree without damaging relationships. Lessons on emotional intelligence at work and how to handle conflict at work give you a step-by-step approach for tense moments. You will also build presentations around one clear message, take feedback without getting defensive and, for finance roles, handle client objections calmly and honestly.

What you will learn

  • Sort tasks by urgency and importance and plan realistic weeks and days
  • Clarify team roles, become dependable and disagree constructively before committing to decisions
  • Pause, name and choose your response when emotions run high at work
  • Build a presentation around one message and handle tough questions with confidence
  • Turn vague feedback into specific actions using the SBI model
  • Handle client concerns about market falls and fees without promising returns

Who this course is for

  • Freshers who want to be seen as reliable from week one
  • Team members juggling many requests and tight deadlines
  • Professionals who dread presenting or receiving critical feedback
  • Client-facing finance staff handling objections and difficult conversations

FAQs

Who is the Workplace Effectiveness course for?

Freshers preparing for or starting their first job, and finance professionals such as mutual fund distributors, investment advisers and relationship managers. Core lessons suit everyone; one lesson is written for each of the two groups.

Is this course free?

Yes. All 6 lessons, 60 quiz questions with explanations and 18 practice scenarios are free. Create a free account (one click with Google) to start; your progress is saved across devices.

What are the most important workplace skills for freshers?

Time management, teamwork, emotional intelligence, presenting ideas and taking feedback well matter most early on. Together they show you can deliver on time, work smoothly with others and keep improving. Technical skills get you hired, but these everyday habits decide how much responsibility you are trusted with next.

How do I manage my time better at work?

Sort tasks by urgency and importance before starting them, plan your week first and then each day, and block time for focused work. When new requests arrive, ask about priority and agree a realistic timeline instead of saying yes to everything. Flag likely delays early, not on the deadline.

How should I handle conflict with a colleague?

Pause before reacting, name what you are feeling and choose a calm response. Then talk privately, describe the specific issue rather than the person, listen to their view and agree a next step together. If the conflict involves harassment, discrimination or ethics, use your organisation's formal channels instead.

How do I take negative feedback without getting defensive?

Listen fully, thank the person and ask for a specific example. The SBI model helps: clarify the Situation, the Behaviour observed and its Impact. Then agree one or two actions you will take and check back later. Treating feedback as information about your work, not your worth, makes it easier.
