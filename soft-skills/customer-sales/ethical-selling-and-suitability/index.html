Ethical Selling: Suitability, Disclosure and Saying No

Run a suitability-first sales process as a distributor, adviser or relationship manager. You will learn to assess a client's situation and risk, disclose risks, costs and your earnings clearly, document the basis of your recommendation and decline sales that do not fit.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Suitability comes before the product

Ethical selling starts with one question: does this product fit this client? Regulators such as SEBI, and industry bodies such as AMFI, expect intermediaries to put the client's interest first, avoid mis-selling and recommend only what is suitable. Your organisation's policies will set out the exact process; this lesson covers the conversation behind it.

A suitability assessment looks at the client's goals, time horizon, income stability, existing investments and loans, need for liquidity, and willingness and ability to take risk. A 62-year-old retiree who needs monthly income has a very different profile from a 28-year-old with a stable salary investing for twenty years, even if both say "I want good returns."

A risk-profiling questionnaire is a tool, not a tick-box. Go through it with the client, explain the questions in plain language and check that their answers match what they told you earlier. If a client says they are comfortable with high risk but also says they will need the money for a house down payment next year, explore the contradiction before recommending anything.

Also be clear about your role. A mutual fund distributor, a registered investment adviser and a bank relationship manager have different responsibilities and permissions. Stay within what your registration and employer allow.

Do
  • Assess goals, horizon, liquidity needs and risk capacity first
  • Discuss contradictions in the risk profile with the client
Don't
  • Treat the risk questionnaire as a formality
  • Step outside what your registration and role permit

In the full lesson

  • Disclose risks, costs and how you are paid
  • Document the basis of your recommendation
  • How to say no to an unsuitable sale

Key takeaways

  • Assess goals, horizon, liquidity and risk before recommending any product.
  • Never promise returns; explain risks, lock-ins and costs in plain words.
  • Disclose how you are paid, in line with applicable rules.
  • Document what you recommended, why, and what you disclosed.
  • Decline unsuitable sales and explain the no in terms of the client's goals.

Quick check

A client says, "I want high returns," but also needs the money for a flat down payment in 10 months. What should you do?

  1. Recommend a high-growth equity fund, since higher returns are what the client asked for
  2. Explore the contradiction and prioritise the short-term need
  3. Split the money equally across all risk levels
  4. Let the client choose from your full product list
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Explore the contradiction and prioritise the short-term need

The short horizon limits how much risk is suitable, whatever the stated preference.
