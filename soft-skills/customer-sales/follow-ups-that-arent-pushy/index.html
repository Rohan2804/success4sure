Follow-Ups That Aren't Pushy

Follow up with customers in a way that is helpful rather than annoying. You will learn to agree the next step before a conversation ends, add value in each message, choose the right channel and timing, and close the loop gracefully.

Last updated 5 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Agree the next step before you leave

The easiest follow-up is one the customer has already agreed to. Before ending any meeting or call, ask: "What would be a good next step for you?" or "Would it help if I sent the comparison by Thursday and called you on Friday evening?" Once the customer agrees, your follow-up is expected rather than intrusive.

Note the agreed time, channel and topic in your CRM or diary immediately. Customers notice when you call exactly when you said you would, and they notice when you forget.

If the customer says "I'll get back to you," it is fine to ask gently, "Of course. If I haven't heard by next Wednesday, may I check in?" Most people say yes, and you now have permission to follow up.

Do
  • Agree a specific next step and time before ending
  • Ask permission to check in if the customer will "get back to you"
Don't
  • End a meeting with a vague "I'll be in touch"
  • Forget to record what you promised

In the full lesson

  • Every follow-up should add something
  • Choose the right channel and timing
  • Know when to close the loop

Key takeaways

  • Agree the next step and time before the conversation ends.
  • Make every follow-up useful: a document, an answer or a summary.
  • Use the customer's preferred channel and respect their time.
  • Space follow-ups over several working days, not hours.
  • After two or three unanswered follow-ups, close the loop politely.

Quick check

What is the best way to end a first meeting with a customer who is interested but undecided?

  1. "I'll be in touch soon."
  2. "Please decide by tonight, because I need to close my numbers this week."
  3. "Shall I send the comparison by Thursday and call you Friday evening?"
  4. "Call me whenever you are ready."
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. "Shall I send the comparison by Thursday and call you Friday evening?"

Agreeing a specific next step makes your follow-up expected rather than intrusive.
