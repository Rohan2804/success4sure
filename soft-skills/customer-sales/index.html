About this Customer & Sales Conversations course

Good sales conversations feel like help, not pressure. This sales communication skills course shows you how to build rapport with customers quickly, ask discovery questions before you recommend anything and follow up in a way that adds value. You will also learn when and how to ask happy customers for referrals while respecting privacy. Freshers in bank branches, call centres and retail build customer service skills for handling queries and complaints calmly. Distributors, advisers and relationship managers learn a suitability-first process with clear disclosure, so ethical selling becomes part of everyday work.

What you will learn

  • Open customer conversations with care and find genuine common ground quickly
  • Ask open discovery questions and confirm needs before recommending anything
  • Agree next steps and send follow-ups that add value, not pressure
  • Ask satisfied customers for referrals at the right moment with consent
  • Handle customer queries and complaints calmly with honest timelines and escalation
  • Assess suitability, disclose costs and earnings, and decline unsuitable sales

Who this course is for

  • Freshers in branch banking, call centre or retail roles
  • Early-career sales and business development professionals
  • Anyone who finds follow-ups awkward or pushy
  • Mutual fund distributors, advisers and relationship managers

FAQs

Who is the Customer & Sales Conversations course for?

Freshers preparing for or starting their first job, and finance professionals such as mutual fund distributors, investment advisers and relationship managers. Core lessons suit everyone; one lesson is written for each of the two groups.

How do I build rapport with a customer quickly?

Spend two minutes preparing, greet the person warmly and use their name. Listen visibly by nodding, summarising and asking follow-up questions, and find genuine common ground rather than forced small talk. Matching the customer's pace, whether brisk or relaxed, also helps people feel comfortable.

What is needs-based or consultative selling?

It means understanding what the customer actually needs before recommending anything. You ask open questions, go one level deeper to find the reason behind each answer, summarise what you heard and check it is correct. Only then do you link your recommendation to those specific needs.

How do I follow up with a customer without being pushy?

Agree the next step and timing before the conversation ends, so your follow-up is expected. Make every message add something, such as an answer, a document or a useful update, and use the channel the customer prefers. If there is no response after a few tries, close the loop politely.

How should I handle an angry customer complaint?

Listen fully without interrupting, acknowledge the problem and apologise for the experience. Ask questions to understand the facts, explain what you can do and give an honest, realistic timeline. If the issue is beyond your authority, escalate it properly and tell the customer who will contact them and when.
