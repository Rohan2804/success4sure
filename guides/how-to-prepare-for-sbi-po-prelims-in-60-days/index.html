How to Prepare for SBI PO Prelims in 60 Days

Knowing how to prepare for SBI PO prelims starts with accepting that it is one of the tougher banking prelims: puzzles are longer, data interpretation is heavier and reading passages demand inference. Sixty days is a realistic window to get ready if you study three to four hours daily. This guide gives you an eight-week plan with weekly targets for English, Quantitative Aptitude and Reasoning, explains approximately where the marks lie, and sets out a mock-test and revision schedule. It also covers how to raise your practice level to SBI difficulty without losing accuracy.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

SBI PO prelims pattern (indicative)

Our mock tests follow an indicative pattern of 100 questions, 100 marks and 60 minutes, with 0.25 of a mark deducted for each wrong answer, split into English, Quant and Reasoning with sectional time limits as used in recent cycles. Treat this as indicative — confirm in the latest official notification on sbi.co.in/careers, which also lists eligibility and the later stages.

With sectional timing, you cannot borrow time from a strong section to rescue a weak one. Each section needs its own plan and its own attempt strategy.

  • English Language: about 30 questions (approximately)
  • Quantitative Aptitude: about 35 questions (approximately)
  • Reasoning Ability: about 35 questions (approximately)
  • Negative marking: 0.25 per wrong answer (indicative)

Where the marks are: approximate weightage

Based on the syllabus and the usual shape of the paper, Reasoning is dominated by puzzles and seating arrangements — often more than half the section — with inequalities, syllogism, coding-decoding, blood relations and direction filling the rest. In Quant, data interpretation sets, quadratic equations, number series and arithmetic word problems carry most of the marks; SBI has often used caselet and missing-data DI.

English typically leans on reading comprehension with inference questions, plus cloze tests, error spotting, para jumbles and word usage. Strong readers can make English their safest section.

The 60-day SBI PO plan, week by week

Split each study day into about 80 minutes of Reasoning, 80 minutes of Quant, 45 minutes of English and 15 minutes of revision. Read one serious long-form article daily and summarise it in two lines.

  • Week 1: Quant — simplification, percentages, ratio, averages; Reasoning — inequalities, coding-decoding, direction; English — grammar rules, RC basics
  • Week 2: Quant — number series (including wrong-number), quadratic equations; Reasoning — linear and circular seating; English — error spotting, cloze
  • Week 3: Quant — profit and loss, interest, time and work, speed and distance; Reasoning — floor, box and month-based puzzles; English — para jumbles, word usage
  • Week 4: Quant — table, bar, line and pie DI; Reasoning — syllogism, blood relations, input-output basics; first full mock
  • Week 5: Quant — caselet and missing-data DI, probability, mensuration; Reasoning — mixed and uncertain-number puzzles; English — inference RC; two mocks
  • Week 6: SBI-level puzzle and DI sets daily, sectional tests; three mocks
  • Week 7: four to five mocks with deep analysis, error-notebook revision
  • Week 8: daily mock at your exam slot, formula and notebook revision, no new topics in the last three days

Raising your level to SBI difficulty

Many candidates do well in moderate mocks and then struggle with SBI's longer sets. The fix is gradual: from week five, include at least one hard puzzle and one hard DI set in every practice session, even if you cannot finish them in time at first.

Learn to judge a set quickly. Read the puzzle's conditions once; if you cannot fix at least two positions within a minute, it may be a set to leave. In DI, check whether the questions need heavy calculation before committing.

  • Practise puzzles with uncertain numbers of people or floors
  • Practise DI where one value must be derived before others
  • Time every set: target 5–6 minutes per puzzle, 6–7 per DI set
  • Note which set types you should skip in the exam

Mock-test and revision schedule

From week four, taking full-length timed mock tests and reviewing every explanation is the backbone of your plan. Analyse each mock for at least an hour: list attempted, correct and wrong per section, then classify each error as concept, calculation or selection. Write fixes into your error notebook.

Vary your mocks between moderate and SBI-level difficulty so you learn to stay calm when a paper feels hard. A tough mock is practice in judgement, not a reason to panic.

  • Weeks 1–3: topic-wise practice only
  • Week 4: 1 mock; weeks 5–6: 2–3 mocks per week
  • Weeks 7–8: 4–6 mocks per week
  • Every Sunday: error-notebook and formula-sheet revision

Section-wise tips

Reasoning: build a consistent diagram shorthand and attempt the quick topics — inequalities, syllogism, coding — first. Quant: secure quadratic equations, series and simplification early, then move to DI and arithmetic. English: read the RC questions before the passage to know what to look for, and do not over-think vocabulary items.

Across sections, keep accuracy high. Under negative marking, ten careful extra attempts beat twenty careless ones.

In English, keep a short list of grammar rules you repeatedly get wrong and revise it before every mock. In Quant, learn to recognise when approximate values are enough; if the options are far apart, exact calculation wastes time you will need for the next DI set. In Reasoning, practise reconstructing the puzzle quickly from the conditions that fix positions first, such as 'sits at an end' or 'lives on the top floor'.

Common mistakes SBI PO aspirants make

One frequent mistake is preparing only at moderate level and being shocked by the actual paper's longer puzzles. Another is spending too much of the first month on rarely asked topics instead of puzzles, DI and RC, which carry most of the marks. A third is taking mocks without proper analysis, which repeats the same errors.

Also avoid the trap of comparing your scores with others online. Mock difficulty varies widely between providers. Measure progress only against your own earlier scores and accuracy, and keep improving the specific topics your error notebook points to.

  • Practise at SBI-level difficulty from week five
  • Spend the most time on puzzles, DI and RC
  • Analyse every mock for at least an hour
  • Compare only with your own past performance

Before the exam

Download your call letter from sbi.co.in/careers, check the reporting time and documents, and keep your sleep aligned with your slot during the final week. Avoid starting new books or topics in the last few days; revision and calm mocks serve you better.

FAQs

Is 60 days enough to prepare for SBI PO prelims?

Sixty days is enough for most graduates to cover the syllabus and build speed with three to four hours of daily study and around 20 to 25 full mocks. Beginners who find puzzles and DI difficult may need an additional month to reach SBI-level comfort.

What is the hardest section in SBI PO prelims?

Most candidates find Reasoning the hardest because of long, multi-variable puzzles, followed by DI-heavy Quant. English can be the most scoring for strong readers. Practise hard puzzles daily from the second month to build confidence.

How should I practise for SBI PO puzzles?

Learn a clear diagram shorthand, solve two or three puzzles daily under a timer, and gradually move to uncertain-number and multi-variable puzzles. After each set, review how you could have reached the first fixed position faster; that step is where most time is saved.
