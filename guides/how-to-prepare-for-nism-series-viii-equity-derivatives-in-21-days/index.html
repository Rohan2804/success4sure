How to Prepare for NISM Series VIII in 21 Days

Many candidates ask how to prepare for NISM Series VIII because it has a reputation as one of the tougher NISM papers. The Equity Derivatives exam combines definitions with numericals on futures pricing, option payoffs, margins and strategies, and the pass mark is higher than several other NISM exams. A three-week plan works well for most working professionals: it leaves enough time for the options chapters, which need practice rather than reading, and for several timed mock tests. This guide sets out the plan week by week, with approximate chapter weightage and a revision routine.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Know the pattern before you plan

The indicative pattern is 100 questions for 100 marks in 120 minutes, with a passing score of 60 percent and negative marking of 0.25 marks per wrong answer. These details are indicative — confirm in the latest official notification on nism.ac.in before you register, because NISM revises workbooks and exam structures periodically.

A 60 percent pass line with negative marking means you need around 65 to 70 correct answers with few wrong ones. That is achievable, but only if the calculation-based chapters are solid. Candidates who skip options pricing and strategies typically find themselves stuck in the mid-50s.

Is three weeks right for you?

Twenty-one days assumes about two hours on weekdays and three hours on weekends. If you already trade futures and options, you can compress Week 1 into three or four days and spend the saved time on extra mock tests. If you are new to markets altogether, stretch the plan to four weeks by giving Introduction to Options and the strategies chapter one more week each; these are the chapters where rushed preparation shows up most in scores.

Whatever your background, download the latest workbook from nism.ac.in and use it as the primary source. Lot sizes, margin frameworks and position limits are periodically revised by SEBI and the exchanges, and questions follow the workbook edition rather than older notes or videos.

Approximate chapter weightage

NISM does not publish an official mark split. Based on the syllabus and the length of workbook chapters, the following is an approximate guide to where questions tend to concentrate.

  • Heaviest: Introduction to Options (payoffs, moneyness, premium components, Greeks) and Strategies using Equity Futures and Options.
  • Heavy: Introduction to Forwards and Futures (cost of carry, basis, hedging with beta) and Trading Mechanism (order types, contract specifications, position limits).
  • Medium: Clearing and Settlement System (margins, mark-to-market, settlement of futures and options) and Legal and Regulatory Environment.
  • Lighter but scoring: Basics of Derivatives, Understanding Index, Accounting and Taxation, and Sales Practices and Investor Protection.

Week 1: foundations and futures

Use the first week to build vocabulary and get comfortable with futures, which are simpler than options and underpin everything that follows. Study about two hours a day and finish each day with chapter-wise practice questions.

  • Days 1–2: Basics of Derivatives — forwards, futures, options and swaps; participants (hedgers, speculators, arbitrageurs); exchange-traded vs OTC.
  • Day 3: Understanding Index — free-float market capitalisation, index construction, impact cost, index funds and ETFs.
  • Days 4–6: Introduction to Forwards and Futures — cost of carry model, fair value, basis and convergence, hedging a portfolio with index futures using beta, calendar spreads.
  • Day 7: Revision of Week 1 and a 40-question mixed practice set on these chapters.

Week 2: options and strategies

This is the most important week. Do not try to memorise payoff tables; draw them. For every strategy, sketch the payoff at expiry and write down the maximum profit, maximum loss and breakeven.

  • Days 8–10: Introduction to Options — calls and puts, intrinsic and time value, moneyness, put-call parity, factors affecting premium, and the Greeks (delta, gamma, theta, vega, rho) at a conceptual level.
  • Days 11–13: Strategies using Equity Futures and Options — protective put, covered call, bull and bear spreads, straddles, strangles, butterflies, and hedging with futures and options.
  • Day 14: First full-length timed mock test, followed by a careful review of every options and strategies question.

Week 3: mechanics, regulation and mocks

The final week covers the operational and regulatory chapters, which are fact-based and quicker to learn, and builds exam stamina through mock tests.

  • Day 15: Trading Mechanism — order types, contract specifications, position limits, trading workstation basics.
  • Day 16: Clearing and Settlement System — clearing members, initial and exposure margins, mark-to-market, final settlement, physical settlement of stock derivatives.
  • Day 17: Legal and Regulatory Environment, Accounting and Taxation, and Sales Practices and Investor Protection.
  • Day 18: Second full-length mock; review and update your error log.
  • Day 19: Re-solve all options and futures numericals you got wrong; third mock in the evening.
  • Day 20: Fourth mock, then revise formula sheet and margin concepts.
  • Day 21: Light revision only — formula sheet, error log, strategy breakevens. Sleep early.

Build a one-page formula sheet

Most Series VIII numericals rely on a short list of relationships. Keeping them on one page and revising daily from Week 2 onwards is the single most effective habit for this exam.

  • Futures fair value under cost of carry, and the meaning of basis.
  • Number of futures contracts needed to hedge a portfolio using its beta.
  • Intrinsic value and time value of calls and puts; put-call parity.
  • Breakeven, maximum profit and maximum loss for each standard strategy.
  • Mark-to-market calculation on a futures position over a few days.

How to use mock tests well

Full-length timed mock tests are where Series VIII preparation comes together, because they reveal whether you can switch between a conceptual question and a calculation without losing time. Sit each mock in a single 120-minute block and then review every explanation, including for questions you got right by luck.

Track your accuracy on attempted questions. If it is below about 80 percent, you are attempting too many uncertain questions; with a 60 percent pass line, discipline in skipping matters as much as knowledge.

Exam-day approach

Start with a quick pass through all 100 questions, answering the factual ones immediately. Mark numericals and long strategy questions for a second pass, when you can give them full attention. Use rough paper for every payoff question — sketching takes seconds and prevents sign errors on puts and short positions. Only guess where you have eliminated at least two options.

In the final 15 minutes, review marked questions in order of confidence: first those where you had eliminated two options, then those where you had eliminated one. Do not change an answer you were sure of earlier unless you spot a clear calculation error; second-guessing under time pressure usually lowers the score.

FAQs

Is NISM Series VIII difficult?

It is one of the more demanding NISM exams because of its 60 percent pass mark, negative marking and calculation-based questions on futures, options and strategies. Candidates who practise payoffs and numericals rather than only reading the workbook generally find it manageable within three to four weeks.

Which chapters should I focus on for NISM VIII?

Based on the syllabus, Introduction to Options and Strategies using Equity Futures and Options usually carry the most questions, followed by Forwards and Futures and the Trading Mechanism chapter. Regulation, taxation and investor protection are lighter and fact-based, so revise them quickly but do not skip them.

Do I need a calculator for NISM Series VIII?

The test is computer-based and the calculations are generally simple arithmetic. Practise doing them quickly on rough paper. Check the current candidate instructions on nism.ac.in for what is allowed at the test centre, as rules on calculators can change.
