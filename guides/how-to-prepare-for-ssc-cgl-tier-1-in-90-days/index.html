How to Prepare for SSC CGL Tier 1 in 90 Days

Three months is a practical window if you want to know how to prepare for SSC CGL Tier 1 properly. The paper covers four subjects — General Intelligence and Reasoning, General Awareness, Quantitative Aptitude and English Comprehension — and each needs a different kind of work: concepts and formulas for Quant, rules and reading for English, pattern practice for Reasoning, and steady memory work for General Awareness. This guide splits 90 days into three phases, gives week-by-week targets, explains approximately where marks come from, and sets out a mock-test and revision cycle. It assumes about four hours of daily study.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

SSC CGL Tier 1 pattern (indicative)

Our mock tests follow an indicative pattern of 100 questions for 200 marks in 60 minutes, with negative marking of 0.25 of a question's marks for each wrong answer. The four sections carry roughly equal numbers of questions. This is indicative — confirm in the latest official notification on ssc.gov.in, which also covers eligibility, posts and later tiers.

With about 36 seconds per question on average, Tier 1 is a speed-and-accuracy test. Your preparation must build fast recall of facts and formulas, not just understanding.

  • General Intelligence & Reasoning: about 25 questions
  • General Awareness: about 25 questions
  • Quantitative Aptitude: about 25 questions
  • English Comprehension: about 25 questions
  • Negative marking: 0.25 of marks per wrong answer (indicative)

Approximate weightage within each subject

Based on the syllabus and the usual paper, Quant leans on arithmetic (percentages, ratio, profit and loss, interest, time and work, speed and distance), advanced maths (algebra, geometry, trigonometry, mensuration) and a little DI. Reasoning covers analogy, classification, series, coding-decoding, blood relations, Venn diagrams, mirror and water images, paper folding, embedded figures and syllogism.

English is built on vocabulary (synonyms, antonyms, idioms, one-word substitution, spellings), grammar (error spotting, sentence improvement, active-passive, narration) and comprehension (cloze, RC). General Awareness spans history, polity, geography, economics, general science, static GK and current affairs.

In practice, arithmetic and advanced maths have each contributed a substantial part of Quant, so neither can be skipped. In Reasoning, non-verbal questions such as mirror images and paper folding are quick to learn and should not be ignored. In English, vocabulary and grammar together usually carry more marks than comprehension, which makes daily vocabulary work worthwhile.

The 90-day plan: three phases

Split the 90 days into a foundation phase, a practice phase and a mock phase. Study all four subjects every week so none falls behind; about 75 minutes Quant, 60 minutes Reasoning, 60 minutes English and 45 minutes General Awareness each day.

  • Weeks 1–2: Quant — number system, percentages, ratio, averages; Reasoning — analogy, classification, series; English — parts of speech, error spotting rules; GA — Indian polity basics
  • Weeks 3–4: Quant — profit and loss, interest, time and work, speed and distance; Reasoning — coding-decoding, blood relations, direction; English — vocabulary lists, idioms; GA — modern Indian history
  • Weeks 5–6: Quant — algebra and geometry; Reasoning — Venn diagrams, syllogism, non-verbal (mirror, water image, paper folding); English — sentence improvement, voice, narration; GA — geography and economy
  • Weeks 7–8: Quant — trigonometry, mensuration, DI; Reasoning — mixed practice; English — cloze and RC; GA — general science (physics, chemistry, biology); first full mocks begin in week 7
  • Weeks 9–10: three mocks per week with deep analysis; revise weak chapters; current affairs of the last six to eight months
  • Weeks 11–12: four to five mocks per week; formula and vocabulary revision; static GK revision
  • Final days: one mock a day at your exam slot time, light revision, rest before the exam

Making General Awareness stick

General Awareness is where many candidates either gain a big lead or lose ground, because it cannot be solved by speed — you either know the answer or you do not. Short daily sessions work far better than long weekly ones.

Keep compact notes by subject and revise them in a spaced cycle: the next day, after a week, after a month. Use practice questions to test recall rather than re-reading.

  • Daily: 15 minutes of current affairs, 30 minutes of static GK or science
  • Weekly: 50-question GA quiz from the week's topics
  • Monthly: full revision of your GA notes

Mock tests and revision cycle

From week seven, taking full-length timed mock tests and reviewing every explanation is the centre of your plan. After each mock, note your time per section and the source of each mistake. In SSC CGL, many errors come from rushing easy questions, so watch for silly mistakes as closely as for gaps in knowledge.

Decide your section order through mocks. Many candidates start with General Awareness or English because those questions are fast, then move to Reasoning and finish with Quant. Try two or three orders and settle on one by week ten.

  • Weeks 1–6: topic-wise and sectional practice
  • Weeks 7–8: one to two mocks per week
  • Weeks 9–10: three mocks per week
  • Weeks 11–12: four to five mocks per week
  • Every Sunday: error notebook, formula sheet and vocabulary revision

Tips for beginners and working candidates

If you are starting from scratch in Quant, spend an extra week on arithmetic basics before moving to advanced maths. If you work full-time, two hours on weekdays and six to seven on weekends can still cover this plan, but extend it to about 120 days.

Whatever your schedule, keep a fixed daily slot and protect your mock days. Consistency over 90 days matters more than any single resource.

Common mistakes SSC CGL aspirants make

Many candidates spend months on Quant and leave General Awareness for the last few weeks. Since GA needs repeated revision to stick, this almost always costs marks. Others memorise long vocabulary lists without revision, forgetting most of them within a month.

Another mistake is not practising under the real time limit until late. Tier 1 gives you very little time per question, so time yourself on every practice set from week one. Finally, avoid skipping analysis after mocks; a mock without review mainly measures your current level rather than improving it.

  • Start GA from Day 1, in short daily sessions
  • Revise vocabulary on a spaced schedule
  • Time every practice set from the first week
  • Analyse each mock before taking the next

Official updates

SSC can change the Tier 1 pattern, marking scheme and the structure of later tiers. Check ssc.gov.in for the current notification, exam calendar and admit cards, and rely on it over any third-party summary.

FAQs

Is 90 days enough for SSC CGL Tier 1?

For most graduates, 90 days of about four hours daily is enough to cover the Tier 1 syllabus, build speed and complete 25 to 30 full mocks. If your Quant or English basics are weak, or you work full-time, plan for four months instead.

Which section should I attempt first in SSC CGL Tier 1?

Many candidates start with General Awareness or English because they are quick, then do Reasoning and finish with Quant. There is no single right order; test two or three orders in mocks and stick with the one that gives you the best accuracy and time balance.

How do I prepare General Awareness for SSC CGL?

Study daily in short sessions: current affairs for 15 minutes and one static subject such as polity, history, geography or science for 30 minutes. Keep compact notes, revise them on a spaced schedule and test yourself with practice questions instead of re-reading.
