How to Prepare for SBI Clerk Prelims in 30 Days

If the notification is out and you are asking how to prepare for SBI Clerk prelims in a month, a tight 30-day plan can work — provided you already know school-level maths and basic English grammar. SBI Clerk, officially the Junior Associate recruitment, tests English Language, Numerical Ability and Reasoning Ability at prelims, under strict time limits. This guide gives you a day-by-day plan grouped into four blocks, explains approximately where the marks come from, and fixes your mock tests and revision into the schedule. Plan for four focused hours a day; if you have less, extend each block by a few days.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

SBI Clerk prelims pattern (indicative)

Our mock tests follow an indicative pattern of 100 questions, 100 marks and 60 minutes, with 0.25 of a mark deducted for each wrong answer, split into English, Numerical Ability and Reasoning with sectional time limits as used in recent cycles. This is indicative — confirm in the latest official notification on sbi.co.in/careers, which also sets out eligibility, the local language rule and the mains pattern.

Thirty days does not leave room to master everything, so the plan below concentrates on high-weight topics first and treats the rest as bonus marks.

  • English Language: about 30 questions (approximately)
  • Numerical Ability: about 35 questions (approximately)
  • Reasoning Ability: about 35 questions (approximately)
  • Negative marking: 0.25 per wrong answer (indicative)

High-weight topics to prioritise

Based on the syllabus and the usual paper, simplification and approximation, number series, data interpretation and arithmetic word problems make up most of Numerical Ability. In Reasoning, puzzles and seating arrangements are the largest block, followed by inequalities, syllogism, coding-decoding, alphanumeric series and blood relations. In English, reading comprehension, cloze test, error spotting and sentence arrangement carry most marks.

In 30 days, secure the quick-scoring topics — simplification, inequalities, syllogism, error spotting — first. They need less practice to become reliable and give you a base score before you tackle puzzles and DI.

The 30-day SBI Clerk plan

Each day includes about 90 minutes of Numerical Ability, 90 minutes of Reasoning, 45 minutes of English and 15 minutes of revision. Do 20 simplification questions daily throughout; it is the quickest way to improve calculation speed.

  • Days 1–5: tables, squares, cubes, simplification, approximation, percentages; inequalities, coding-decoding, direction; grammar basics and error spotting
  • Days 6–10: number series, ratio, averages, ages; linear and circular seating; fillers and cloze test
  • Days 11–15: profit and loss, interest, time and work, speed and distance; floor and box puzzles, syllogism, blood relations; RC practice; full mock on Day 15
  • Days 16–20: data interpretation (table, bar, pie), quadratic equations, mixtures; mixed puzzles, alphanumeric series, order and ranking; para jumbles; two mocks
  • Days 21–25: one full mock daily plus targeted practice on weakest topics
  • Days 26–28: one full mock daily, error-notebook revision, formula sheet
  • Days 29–30: one light mock at your exam slot time, revision only, early night before the exam

Daily routine that fits a working day

If you are working or in college, split the four hours into two blocks: two hours early morning for Numerical Ability and English, two hours in the evening for Reasoning and revision. Mornings are usually better for calculation-heavy work.

Keep weekends for mocks and analysis from Day 15 onwards. A full mock takes an hour; its analysis should take at least another hour.

Mock tests and revision

From the middle of the month, taking full-length timed mock tests and reviewing the explanations becomes the most valuable part of your day. In each review, identify questions you got wrong, questions you skipped but could have solved, and questions you should have skipped. Each category needs a different fix.

Maintain a short error notebook and a one-page formula sheet. Revise both every evening during the last ten days.

Use the same section order in every mock from Day 21 onwards, and practise at the same time of day as your exam slot during the final week. Familiarity with the routine reduces stress and decision-making on the day, leaving more attention for the questions themselves.

  • Days 1–14: topic-wise practice with timers
  • Day 15: first full mock
  • Days 16–20: two mocks; Days 21–28: one mock daily
  • Days 29–30: one light mock, revision only

Speed and accuracy tips

In Numerical Ability, round sensibly in approximation questions and learn to spot series patterns by testing differences and ratios first. In Reasoning, attempt inequalities and syllogism early and choose puzzles that open up within a minute. In English, read RC questions before the passage and avoid lingering on any one item.

With negative marking, attempt only when you can eliminate at least two options. Track your accuracy in mocks and adjust your attempt count accordingly.

For calculation speed, learn squares up to 30, cubes up to 20 and percentage values of common fractions such as one-eighth or one-twelfth. These come up constantly in simplification and DI and save seconds on almost every question.

What to skip in a 30-day plan

A month is short, so you must be deliberate about what you leave out. Rarely asked or very time-consuming topics — complex probability, advanced mensuration, unusual puzzle types — can wait until after prelims or be skipped. Do not spend a day on a topic that might bring one question.

Similarly, avoid switching study material midway. One reliable source per section, completed fully, is better than three started and abandoned. If a topic still feels weak after two focused sessions, move on and return to it in revision rather than letting it eat into your schedule.

  • Skip or postpone: complex probability, rare puzzle types, advanced mensuration
  • Keep: simplification, series, DI, arithmetic, puzzles, inequalities, RC, cloze
  • One source per section, completed fully
  • Two sessions maximum on any stubborn topic before moving on

Staying consistent for 30 days

A short plan fails most often because of a missed day that turns into three. Set a fixed start time, keep your phone away during study blocks, and track each day's targets on a simple checklist. If you do miss a day, do not try to double the next day's load; carry over only the highest-weight tasks.

Language test and official details

SBI Clerk is a state-wise recruitment, and candidates are expected to know the official local language of the state they apply for; a language test may apply. Read the state-wise rules carefully in the notification on sbi.co.in/careers before applying, and check the site regularly for call letters and updates.

FAQs

Can I prepare for SBI Clerk prelims in 30 days?

Yes, if you already have school-level maths and basic English, and can study about four focused hours a day. Prioritise high-weight topics, start full mocks by Day 15, and analyse every mock. Beginners with weaker basics should allow 45 to 60 days instead.

Which topics give the most marks in SBI Clerk prelims?

Based on the syllabus, simplification and approximation, number series and DI lead in Numerical Ability; puzzles and seating arrangement lead in Reasoning; and reading comprehension, cloze test and error spotting lead in English. Secure quick topics first, then build puzzle and DI skills.

How many mock tests should I take for SBI Clerk in 30 days?

Around 12 to 15 full-length mocks from Day 15 to Day 30 is realistic, alongside sectional tests. Taking more without proper analysis gives limited benefit. Spend at least as long reviewing each mock as you did taking it.
