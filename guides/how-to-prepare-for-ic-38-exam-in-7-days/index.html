How to Prepare for IC-38 Exam in 7 Days: Study Plan

If you are wondering how to prepare for IC 38 exam without losing weeks to it, the answer is a short, structured plan. IC-38 is the IRDAI pre-recruitment exam for insurance agents and point-of-sale persons, and its syllabus is concept-heavy rather than calculation-heavy. Most candidates who already have the study material from their insurer or training institute can cover it properly in seven focused days of two to three hours each. This guide gives you a day-by-day plan, tells you which chapters tend to carry more questions, and shows where mock tests and revision fit so that you walk into the exam calm and ready.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

IC-38 exam pattern at a glance

Our mock tests follow an indicative pattern of 50 multiple-choice questions, 50 marks and 60 minutes, with no negative marking. Treat this as indicative — confirm in the latest official notification, because the Insurance Institute of India and IRDAI can revise the number of questions, the pass percentage and the format for life, general, health or composite licences. Check insuranceinstituteofindia.com and your insurer's licensing team for current details.

With roughly a minute or more per question and no penalty for wrong answers, IC-38 is not a speed test. It rewards clear understanding of insurance principles, product features and regulations. That is why a short plan works: you are learning a fixed body of concepts, not building years of aptitude.

  • Questions: 50 (indicative)
  • Duration: 60 minutes (indicative)
  • Negative marking: none in the indicative pattern — attempt every question
  • Mode: computer-based, usually at an authorised centre or online as per current rules

Chapter weightage: where to spend your time

Based on the syllabus, the chapters you will study are Introduction to Insurance, Customer Service and Grievances, Regulatory Aspects of Agency, Legal Principles of Insurance, Life Insurance and Financial Planning, Life Insurance Products, Pricing and Valuation, Documentation and Underwriting, Life Insurance Claims, Health Insurance and General Insurance. Exact weightage is not published as a fixed table, so treat the guidance below as approximate.

Documentation and Underwriting, Health Insurance, Life Insurance Products, Legal Principles and General Insurance tend to produce a larger share of questions because they contain many definitions and rules. Pricing and Valuation, Claims and Financial Planning are smaller but easy to score once you learn the terms.

  • Higher share (approximately): Documentation & Underwriting, Health Insurance, Life Insurance Products, Legal Principles, General Insurance
  • Medium share: Introduction to Insurance, Customer Service & Grievances, Regulatory Aspects of Agency
  • Smaller share: Life Insurance & Financial Planning, Pricing & Valuation, Life Insurance Claims

The 7-day IC-38 study plan

Plan for two to three hours on weekdays and a little more on the last two days. Read each chapter once with attention, write short notes of definitions and numbers such as free-look periods or grace periods as given in your study material, and solve chapter questions the same day. Always check numbers against the current official material, since regulations change.

  • Day 1: Introduction to Insurance and Legal Principles (utmost good faith, insurable interest, indemnity, subrogation, contribution, proximate cause) — 30 chapter questions
  • Day 2: Regulatory Aspects of Agency and Customer Service & Grievances (agent code of conduct, licensing, ombudsman, grievance redressal) — 30 questions
  • Day 3: Life Insurance & Financial Planning and Life Insurance Products (term, endowment, whole life, ULIPs, riders) — 40 questions
  • Day 4: Pricing & Valuation, Documentation & Underwriting (proposal form, KYC, medical and non-medical underwriting) — 40 questions
  • Day 5: Life Insurance Claims, Health Insurance and General Insurance (claim types, cashless and reimbursement, motor, fire, property basics) — 40 questions
  • Day 6: First full-length timed mock test in the morning, detailed review in the evening, re-read your two weakest chapters
  • Day 7: Second full-length mock test, quick revision of your notes and definitions, light reading only in the last two hours

How to study each chapter efficiently

IC-38 questions are mostly direct: a definition, a feature of a product, the right step in a process, or the correct principle for a situation. Read actively. After each topic, close the book and explain the concept in one or two sentences. If you cannot, read it again.

Create a one-page sheet for terms that look alike, such as indemnity versus subrogation, surrender value versus paid-up value, or a rider versus a base policy. Many wrong answers in this exam come from confusing two similar terms rather than not knowing either.

  • Highlight every term in bold in your study material and write a one-line meaning
  • Group regulatory points (agent duties, prohibited practices) into a single list
  • Practise situation questions: 'Which principle applies when...' style items
  • Revise your sheet for ten minutes before starting each new day

Revision and mock-test schedule

Mock tests are where the plan comes together. Taking full-length timed mock tests on Days 6 and 7, and reviewing the explanation for every question you got wrong or guessed, is the fastest way to find gaps. Do not just look at your score; note the chapter of each mistake and go back to that page of your notes.

If your scores are steady and your mistakes are scattered rather than concentrated in one chapter, you are close to ready. If one chapter keeps causing errors, spend an extra hour on it before the exam. Our practice questions are original and written to the syllabus — they are for practice, not a copy of what you will see.

  • After each chapter: 20–40 practice questions
  • Day 6: Mock test 1 + 90-minute error review
  • Day 7: Mock test 2 + 60-minute review + final notes revision
  • Exam morning: one short read of your one-page sheet, nothing new

Exam-day tips for IC-38

Reach the centre early with the documents your insurer or the exam body asks for, and confirm the reporting time and ID requirements in your hall ticket or official communication. Inside, read each question fully; words like 'not', 'except' and 'first' change the answer.

Since there is no negative marking in the indicative pattern, never leave a question blank. Mark doubtful ones, finish the paper, then return to them with the time you have left. Most candidates finish with ten to twenty minutes to spare, so use that time to recheck questions on principles and regulations.

Common mistakes to avoid

The biggest mistake is reading the material once and skipping practice. The second is memorising numbers from an old guide instead of the current syllabus. A third is ignoring the smaller chapters; Claims and Pricing are short, and leaving them out throws away easy marks.

  • Studying from outdated notes — always use the current official study material
  • Skipping Health and General Insurance if you are taking a life licence, without checking whether they are in your syllabus
  • Not reviewing mock-test mistakes
  • Cramming new chapters on the last day

FAQs

Is 7 days enough to prepare for the IC-38 exam?

For most candidates, yes. The syllabus is conceptual, and two to three focused hours a day for a week is usually enough to read every chapter, practise questions and take two full mock tests. If you are new to insurance terms or can study only an hour a day, stretch the plan to 10–14 days.

Is there negative marking in the IC-38 exam?

In the indicative pattern used for our mock tests there is no negative marking, so you should attempt every question. Rules can change, so confirm the current marking scheme in the latest official communication from the Insurance Institute of India or your insurer before your exam date.

Which chapters are most important for IC-38?

Based on the syllabus, Documentation and Underwriting, Health Insurance, Life Insurance Products, Legal Principles and General Insurance tend to carry more questions. Do not skip smaller chapters such as Claims and Pricing, though; they are short and easy to score once the key terms are clear.
