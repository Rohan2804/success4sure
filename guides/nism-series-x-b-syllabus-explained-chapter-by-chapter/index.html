NISM Series X-B Syllabus Explained Chapter by Chapter

The NISM Series X-B syllabus looks short on paper — a dozen or so chapters — but each one goes deep into a part of personal financial planning. Investment Adviser Level 2 moves from knowing products to advising on protection, retirement, tax and succession for a real client. This guide explains the syllabus chapter by chapter in the order most candidates study it, describes what each chapter actually tests, and points out where questions tend to be factual and where they become applied. Use it alongside the current workbook from nism.ac.in, which remains the authoritative source.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

How the syllabus is organised

The chapters fall into five blocks: insurance and risk management, retirement planning, taxation, estate planning, and comprehensive planning through case studies. The first four blocks build knowledge; the fifth tests whether you can combine them. The indicative exam pattern is 100 questions for 100 marks in 180 minutes, with a 60 percent pass mark and 0.25 negative marking — indicative, so confirm in the latest official notification on nism.ac.in.

The chapter names below follow the topics in the current syllabus as we understand it. NISM revises workbooks periodically, so check the edition and its effective date before you begin.

Block 1: insurance and risk management

Risk Management & Insurance Concepts introduces how risks are identified and handled — avoidance, reduction, retention and transfer — and the core principles of insurance such as insurable interest, utmost good faith, indemnity and subrogation. Questions here are mostly definitional and reward careful reading.

Life Insurance Products covers term plans, endowment and money-back policies, whole life, ULIPs and annuities. Expect questions on product features and on which product suits a described client, for example a young earner with dependants who needs maximum cover at minimum cost.

Health & General Insurance explains indemnity-based and benefit-based health covers, key terms such as waiting periods, co-payment and sub-limits, and property, motor and liability insurance at the level an adviser needs.

Insurance Needs Analysis is the first calculation-heavy chapter. It covers the human life value approach and need-based approaches, and asks you to work out how much cover a client requires. Practise these until you can complete one in a few minutes.

Block 2: retirement planning

Retirement Planning Concepts sets the stage: the accumulation and distribution phases, the effect of inflation and longevity, and the replacement-ratio idea of estimating expenses after retirement. Questions are conceptual but often include a short numerical.

Retirement Products & Pension Schemes describes the employer-linked and voluntary retirement products covered in the workbook, along with annuity options. Learn the features, contribution and withdrawal rules as the workbook states them, and verify any rules that may have changed since publication.

Retirement Corpus & Distribution Planning is the second major calculation chapter. You estimate expenses at retirement, compute the corpus needed to fund them for the expected retirement period, and work out how much the client must save each year. It relies heavily on time value of money from X-A.

Block 3: taxation

Income Tax Fundamentals covers residential status, heads of income, computation of total income, common deductions and the tax regimes as described in the workbook. Questions test whether you can classify income correctly and apply basic rules.

Taxation of Investment Products is one of the most important chapters. It explains how gains and income from equity shares, mutual funds, bonds, deposits, gold, real estate and insurance policies are taxed. Because tax law changes through annual Finance Acts, rely only on the current workbook edition and note the year it refers to. A product-by-product tax table is the most efficient way to revise this chapter.

Block 4: estate planning

Wealth Management & Estate Planning Concepts explains the objectives of estate planning — orderly transfer of wealth, minimising disputes, providing for dependants — and the tools available.

Wills, Succession & Trusts covers the essentials of a valid will, types of wills, probate, the broad framework of succession at the level covered in the workbook, the difference between nomination and inheritance, and the use of private trusts. This chapter is full of terminology, so careful reading matters more than calculation.

Block 5: comprehensive financial planning and case studies

This chapter ties everything together. A case describes a client — age, income, expenses, family, assets, liabilities, goals — followed by several questions. You may be asked to identify the insurance gap, estimate retirement savings needed, assess the tax effect of a recommendation or point out an estate planning issue.

Case questions are where most time is spent in the exam. Read all sub-questions before calculating, because some can be answered from the facts alone. Practise every case in the workbook, then take full-length timed mock tests to get used to switching between short and long questions.

  • Underline each number in the case as you use it, so you do not reuse or miss data.
  • Write intermediate results on rough paper; later sub-questions often build on them.
  • If a calculation result does not match any option, recheck inflation and the number of years before redoing everything.

How X-B questions are typically framed

Knowing the style of questions helps you study each chapter the right way. Broadly, X-B questions fall into four types, and each block of the syllabus leans towards one or two of them.

  • Definition questions: what a term means — for example indemnity, probate or annuity — common in the insurance and estate planning blocks.
  • Suitability questions: which product or approach fits a described client — common in life insurance, retirement products and wealth management.
  • Calculation questions: insurance need, retirement corpus, annual savings required or tax on a gain — common in needs analysis, retirement corpus and taxation chapters.
  • Case-based sets: a client profile followed by several linked questions drawing on all blocks.

Where to focus your preparation

Based on the syllabus, the case studies, taxation of investment products, retirement corpus planning and insurance needs analysis tend to carry the most weight and deserve the most practice. The concept chapters on insurance, retirement and estate planning are shorter and quicker to master, but they supply easy marks and should be read at least twice.

Combine chapter-wise practice questions after each block with three or four full-length mock tests in the final ten days, and review every explanation carefully. That pairing exposes both knowledge gaps and timing problems before the actual exam.

A practical rhythm is one block per week for four weeks, followed by a final week focused only on case studies and mocks. Keep a separate page of terms for the estate planning chapter and a separate page of formulas for insurance and retirement, and read both every day in the last week.

FAQs

How many chapters are there in the NISM X-B syllabus?

The current syllabus covers roughly a dozen topics grouped into insurance, retirement planning, taxation, estate planning and comprehensive planning with case studies. NISM revises workbooks periodically, so download the latest edition from nism.ac.in and check its chapter list before you start.

Which part of the X-B syllabus is hardest?

Most candidates find taxation of investment products and the comprehensive case studies hardest. Taxation needs precise, current rules, and case studies require combining insurance, retirement and tax calculations for one client. Regular practice on full cases is the best way to build confidence.

Does the X-B syllabus include calculations?

Yes. Insurance needs analysis, retirement corpus and savings calculations, and tax on investment income all involve arithmetic, often using time value of money concepts from X-A. The maths is not advanced, but it needs practice to complete within the time available.
