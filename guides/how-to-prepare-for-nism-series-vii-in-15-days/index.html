How to Prepare for NISM Series VII in 15 Days

If you work in a broker's back office or are about to, you will soon need to know how to prepare for NISM Series VII. Securities Operations and Risk Management tests the operational core of the stock market: market participants, broking operations, margins and risk management, clearing, settlement, investor grievances and other services provided by brokers. It is more process-driven than calculation-heavy, which makes a focused 15-day plan realistic for most working professionals. This guide gives a day-by-day schedule, approximate chapter weightage, a revision method and a mock-test routine.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Exam pattern at a glance

The indicative pattern is 100 questions for 100 marks in 120 minutes, with a pass mark of 50 percent and negative marking of 0.25 marks per wrong answer. Treat these as indicative — confirm in the latest official notification on nism.ac.in.

Two hours is ample for the number of questions, because most are factual or short process questions. The real challenge is precision: settlement cycles, margin types, the sequence of steps in pay-in and pay-out, and who is responsible for what. Negative marking punishes the 'I think it's this one' style of answering, so build clear, confident recall.

Is 15 days right for you?

The plan assumes about two hours on weekdays and a little more at weekends. If you already work in broking operations, many chapters will feel familiar and you could compress the first five days into three, using the time for an extra mock test. If you have never worked in the securities industry, stretch the plan to about three weeks: give two extra days to risk management and settlement, which are the most process-heavy chapters, and one extra day to broking operations.

Before you start, download the current workbook from nism.ac.in and check its effective date. Settlement cycles, margin frameworks and grievance procedures have all been revised in recent years, and questions follow the workbook edition rather than older notes or videos. Studying from an outdated source is one of the most common reasons well-prepared candidates still lose marks in this exam.

It also helps to keep the purpose of the exam in mind. Series VII is meant to make sure operations staff can handle client money and securities correctly. When you study a process, imagine yourself performing it at your desk; asking 'what would I check next?' makes sequences far easier to remember than rote reading.

Approximate chapter weightage

There is no official chapter-wise mark split. Based on the syllabus and the length of each workbook chapter, the following grouping is approximate.

  • High: Risk Management; Clearing Process; Settlement Process.
  • Medium: Introduction to Securities Broking Operations; Market Participants in the Securities Market; Investor Grievances and Arbitration.
  • Lower but direct: Introduction to the Securities Market; Other Services Provided by Brokers.
  • Numerical questions, where they appear, usually involve margins, mark-to-market, obligations or basic settlement amounts.

Days 1–5: market structure and broking operations

Study about two hours a day. Read the workbook chapter, write a one-page summary and finish with chapter-wise practice questions.

  • Day 1: Introduction to the Securities Market — primary and secondary markets, products, regulators, market infrastructure institutions.
  • Day 2: Market Participants in the Securities Market — exchanges, clearing corporations, depositories, brokers, custodians, banks and their roles.
  • Days 3–4: Introduction to Securities Broking Operations — client onboarding, KYC, account opening documents, order management, contract notes, trading terminals.
  • Day 5: Revision of Days 1–4 and a mixed practice set.

Days 6–10: risk, clearing and settlement

These three chapters are the heart of the exam. Draw flowcharts as you study; operations questions are much easier when you can picture the flow of funds and securities.

  • Days 6–7: Risk Management — types of margins, margin collection and reporting, mark-to-market, position limits, surveillance and risk containment measures.
  • Day 8: Clearing Process — the role of the clearing corporation, novation, clearing members, determination of obligations.
  • Day 9: Settlement Process — settlement cycles, pay-in and pay-out of funds and securities, shortages, auctions and close-out.
  • Day 10: First full-length timed mock test and a detailed review of every explanation.

Days 11–15: grievances, other services and mocks

The last stretch covers the remaining chapters and builds exam stamina.

  • Day 11: Investor Grievances and Arbitration — grievance redressal mechanisms, the online dispute resolution framework, arbitration process as described in the workbook.
  • Day 12: Other Services Provided by Brokers — depository services, margin trading facility, mutual fund and IPO distribution, and similar services; then a second mock.
  • Day 13: Revise risk management, clearing and settlement with your flowcharts; rework every error from the mocks.
  • Day 14: Third full-length mock and review.
  • Day 15: Light revision of summaries and error log only. Sleep early.

Revision method for an operations exam

Operations questions reward knowing sequences and responsibilities. For each process — account opening, order to trade, clearing, settlement, grievance redressal — write the steps in order and the entity responsible for each. Test yourself by covering one column and recalling the other.

Keep a separate sheet for numbers and timelines mentioned in the workbook, such as settlement cycles and deadlines. Exchanges and SEBI revise these periodically, so take them only from the current workbook edition on nism.ac.in, not from old notes or videos.

  • Draw the clearing and settlement flow at least three times from memory.
  • List each type of margin with what it covers and when it is collected.
  • Note who handles each kind of grievance and at what stage arbitration applies.

Using mock tests well

Three full-length timed mock tests are usually enough for Series VII if you review them properly. Sit each in a single 120-minute block, then go through the explanations for every wrong and guessed answer. Sort mistakes by chapter; if most come from risk management or settlement, return to your flowcharts rather than re-reading the whole chapter.

Also track your accuracy on attempted questions. With a 50 percent pass mark, a candidate who attempts 80 questions at 85 percent accuracy is far safer than one who attempts all 100 at 65 percent, because each wrong answer also costs a quarter mark. If your accuracy is low, the fix is usually more careful reading of process questions rather than more hours with the workbook.

Exam-day tips

Do one complete pass answering everything you are sure of and marking the rest. Most candidates finish this in about 70 minutes. Use the remaining time for marked questions, attempting only those where you can eliminate two options. Read process questions twice; a word like 'before', 'after' or 'not' often decides the answer.

FAQs

Is 15 days enough for NISM Series VII?

For most candidates who can study around two hours a day, 15 days is enough, especially if you already work in broking operations. Complete beginners may want three weeks, giving more time to risk management, clearing and settlement, and to taking additional timed practice tests.

Which chapters are most important for NISM VII?

Based on the syllabus, Risk Management, Clearing Process and Settlement Process tend to carry the most weight, followed by broking operations, market participants and investor grievances. NISM does not publish an official split, so read every chapter and use weightage only to plan revision.

Are there calculations in NISM Series VII?

There are some, mainly on margins, mark-to-market and settlement obligations, but the exam is largely process-based. Most questions test whether you know the correct sequence of steps, the entity responsible and the rules described in the workbook.
