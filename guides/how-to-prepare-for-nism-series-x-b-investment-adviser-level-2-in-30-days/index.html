How to Prepare for NISM Series X-B in 30 Days

Once Level 1 is behind you, the next question is how to prepare for NISM Series X-B efficiently. Investment Adviser Level 2 is about comprehensive financial planning: insurance and risk management, retirement planning, income tax and taxation of investment products, estate planning and integrated case studies. It rewards application more than recall. A 30-day plan suits most working candidates and allows enough time for the tax chapters and case-study practice. This guide gives a week-by-week schedule, approximate chapter weightage, revision techniques and a mock-test routine for the three-hour exam.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Pattern and approach

The indicative pattern is 100 questions carrying 100 marks in 180 minutes, with a pass mark of 60 percent and 0.25 negative marking per wrong answer. Treat this as indicative — confirm in the latest official notification on nism.ac.in.

Because X-B includes case-based questions that require several steps, the 180-minute duration is useful but not lavish. Candidates who know the concepts but have not practised multi-step problems often run short of time in the last third of the paper. The plan below therefore starts case practice early rather than leaving it to the final week.

It also helps to remember the purpose of the certification. X-B tests whether you can build a sensible plan for a real household, so while studying each chapter, ask yourself how you would explain the concept to a client in two sentences. If you cannot, the concept is not yet clear enough for an applied question.

Approximate chapter weightage

There is no official chapter-wise mark split. Based on the syllabus and the depth of each workbook chapter, the following grouping is approximate.

  • High: Comprehensive Financial Planning & Case Studies; Taxation of Investment Products; Retirement Corpus & Distribution Planning; Insurance Needs Analysis.
  • Medium: Income Tax Fundamentals; Life Insurance Products; Retirement Products & Pension Schemes; Wills, Succession & Trusts.
  • Lower but direct: Risk Management & Insurance Concepts; Health & General Insurance; Retirement Planning Concepts; Wealth Management & Estate Planning Concepts.
  • Calculation-heavy areas: human life value and need-based insurance methods, retirement corpus and annuity calculations, capital gains and tax computation on investments.

Week 1: insurance and risk

Start with insurance because it introduces the needs-based thinking used throughout X-B. Study about two hours on weekdays and three to four hours on weekends, ending each day with chapter-wise practice questions.

  • Days 1–2: Risk Management & Insurance Concepts — risk identification, risk retention vs transfer, principles of insurance.
  • Days 3–4: Life Insurance Products — term, endowment, ULIPs, annuities; features and suitability.
  • Day 5: Health & General Insurance — indemnity vs benefit plans, key policy terms, property and liability cover.
  • Day 6: Insurance Needs Analysis — human life value and need-based methods; solve at least 15 problems.
  • Day 7: Revision and a mixed practice set on Week 1 chapters.

Week 2: retirement planning

Retirement questions draw heavily on time value of money from X-A. Revise your TVM formula sheet on Day 8 before starting.

  • Day 8: TVM refresher and Retirement Planning Concepts — accumulation and distribution phases, inflation, life expectancy.
  • Days 9–10: Retirement Products & Pension Schemes — employer-linked and voluntary schemes covered in the workbook, annuity options.
  • Days 11–13: Retirement Corpus & Distribution Planning — estimating expenses at retirement, corpus required, savings needed per year, withdrawal strategies.
  • Day 14: First full-length timed mock test and a detailed review.

Week 3: tax and estate planning

Tax rules are revised through Finance Acts, so study only from the current workbook edition on nism.ac.in and note the assessment year it refers to.

  • Days 15–16: Income Tax Fundamentals — heads of income, residential status, deductions and the regimes as described in the workbook.
  • Days 17–18: Taxation of Investment Products — capital gains on equity, debt, mutual funds, property and gold; tax treatment of interest, dividends and insurance proceeds.
  • Day 19: Wealth Management & Estate Planning Concepts.
  • Day 20: Wills, Succession & Trusts — types of wills, succession under personal laws at the level covered in the workbook, nomination vs inheritance, private trusts.
  • Day 21: Second full-length mock and review.

Week 4: case studies and mocks

The final stretch is about integration. Case studies combine insurance, retirement, tax and estate concepts for one client, so practise them in full rather than as isolated sub-questions.

  • Days 22–23: Comprehensive Financial Planning & Case Studies — work through every case in the workbook, writing out each step.
  • Day 24: Third mock; add mistakes to the error log.
  • Day 25: Revise tax rules product by product.
  • Day 26: Re-solve all insurance and retirement numericals from the error log.
  • Day 27: Fourth mock and review.
  • Days 28–29: Revise weakest chapters and estate planning notes.
  • Day 30: Light revision of formula sheet, tax table and error log.

Revision tips specific to X-B

Build a tax table with one row per product — equity shares, equity funds, debt funds, bonds, fixed deposits, gold, property, insurance policies — and columns for holding period rules, rate treatment and exemptions as given in the current workbook. Most tax questions can be answered by reading one row.

For case studies, write a fixed sequence on the first page of your notes: understand the client's goals, compute insurance gap, compute retirement gap, consider tax implications, then consider estate issues. Following the same sequence every time saves minutes in the exam.

  • Practise insurance need and retirement corpus calculations until each takes under three minutes.
  • Review full-length mock explanations in detail, especially for case questions.
  • Read the wills and succession chapter twice for terminology.

Who needs a longer plan

Thirty days works for candidates who have recently cleared X-A and still remember time value of money. If more than a few months have passed since Level 1, add three to four days at the start purely for TVM and portfolio return revision. If you have never studied income tax, add another week to Block 3; the tax chapters cover many products and rules, and questions test specifics such as holding periods and the treatment of different kinds of income. Those already working in insurance or tax advisory can shorten the corresponding week and invest the time in additional case-study practice, which tends to be the deciding factor between a narrow miss and a comfortable pass.

Exam-day strategy

Do a first pass of about 90 minutes answering direct questions and short calculations. Use the next 70 minutes for case studies and longer numericals, and keep 20 minutes to review. With negative marking, attempt a doubtful question only after eliminating at least two options. If a case-study set looks long, read all its sub-questions first; often one or two can be answered without the full calculation.

FAQs

Is 30 days enough for NISM Series X-B?

For most candidates who have recently cleared X-A and can study two to three hours a day, 30 days is realistic. Those without exposure to taxation or insurance should allow five to six weeks, giving extra time to tax rules, retirement corpus calculations and case-study practice.

Which chapters are most important in NISM X-B?

Based on the syllabus, comprehensive financial planning and case studies, taxation of investment products, retirement corpus planning and insurance needs analysis tend to carry the most weight. NISM does not publish an official split, so cover every chapter and use this only for prioritising.

Do I need to clear X-A before attempting X-B?

Check the current eligibility rules on nism.ac.in, as these can change. Regardless of rules, preparing for X-A first is sensible because X-B's retirement and case-study questions use X-A's time value of money, product and portfolio concepts throughout.
