IC-38 Syllabus Explained Chapter by Chapter

The IC 38 syllabus looks long on paper, but it is really a tour of how insurance works: the principles behind it, the products agents sell, the paperwork, claims and the rules agents must follow. Knowing what each chapter is actually about helps you study with purpose instead of reading pages blindly. This guide walks through the syllabus chapter by chapter, lists the terms most worth learning, and explains the type of question each chapter tends to produce. Use it alongside your official study material, which remains the final word on scope, and confirm the latest syllabus with the Insurance Institute of India.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

How the IC-38 syllabus is organised

IC-38 is the pre-recruitment exam for insurance agents and point-of-sale persons. The Insurance Institute of India prepares the study material, and the syllabus differs slightly for life, general, health and composite licences. Our indicative mock-test pattern is 50 questions in 60 minutes with no negative marking — indicative, confirm in the latest official notification at insuranceinstituteofindia.com.

The chapters fall into four blocks: foundations (introduction and legal principles), products (life, health and general), process (documentation, underwriting, pricing and claims) and conduct (regulation and customer service). Studying block by block makes it easier to see how ideas connect.

Foundations: Introduction to Insurance and Legal Principles

Introduction to Insurance explains risk, the pooling of premiums from many people to pay the losses of a few, the history of insurance in India and the role of insurance in the economy. Questions are usually definitional: what insurance does, what risk management means, and which risks are insurable.

Legal Principles of Insurance is one of the most important chapters. It covers the elements of a valid contract and the core principles of insurance. Expect situation questions that ask which principle applies.

A useful way to remember the legal principles is to attach each one to a simple everyday example. Insurable interest: you can insure your own house but not a stranger's. Utmost good faith: you must disclose a known heart condition in a life proposal. Proximate cause: a fire that follows a storm may be treated differently from a storm that damages a roof directly. Examples like these make situation questions much easier.

  • Utmost good faith (uberrimae fidei) and material facts
  • Insurable interest — who can insure what
  • Indemnity, subrogation and contribution (mainly general insurance)
  • Proximate cause — the dominant cause of a loss
  • Offer, acceptance, consideration and capacity to contract

Products: Life, Health and General Insurance

Life Insurance and Financial Planning introduces the human life value concept, life stages and why people need protection and savings. Life Insurance Products then covers term plans, endowment, whole life, money-back, unit-linked plans and riders. Learn what each product is designed for and its key features; comparison questions are common.

Health Insurance covers hospitalisation policies, family floater plans, critical illness, top-up cover, cashless and reimbursement claims, and the role of third-party administrators. General Insurance introduces motor, fire, property, marine and liability covers at a basic level. Check whether your licence type includes all of these chapters.

When comparing products, focus on three questions for each one: who is it meant for, does it have a savings or investment element, and what happens at maturity or on death. Writing a small comparison table with these three columns for term, endowment, whole life, money-back and unit-linked plans covers a large share of product questions in a single page of notes.

  • Term vs endowment vs ULIP: purpose, savings element, risk
  • Riders: what they add and when they apply
  • Health: sum insured, waiting periods, co-payment, exclusions — learn terms from current material
  • Motor: own damage vs third-party liability

Process: Documentation, Underwriting, Pricing and Claims

Documentation and Underwriting is usually the heaviest chapter. It covers the proposal form, KYC, age proof, medical and non-medical underwriting, and how an insurer decides whether to accept a risk and on what terms. Learn the purpose of each document and the sequence from proposal to policy issue.

Pricing and Valuation explains, at a basic level, how premiums are set (mortality, interest, expenses), bonuses and surplus. Life Insurance Claims covers maturity, survival benefit and death claims, nomination, assignment and the documents required. These chapters are shorter, so a single careful reading plus practice questions usually suffices.

  • Proposal form, free-look period and policy document — verify current rules
  • Standard, sub-standard and declined lives
  • Nomination vs assignment
  • Early vs non-early death claims

Conduct: Regulatory Aspects and Customer Service

Regulatory Aspects of Agency covers who regulates insurance, how agents are licensed, the code of conduct, commission rules at a broad level and practices agents must avoid, such as mis-selling or rebating. These questions are often straightforward if you have read the rules once.

Customer Service and Grievances explains why service matters, how grievances are handled, the role of the insurance ombudsman and the protections for policyholders. Expect questions on the correct escalation path and the agent's role in after-sales service.

Approximate chapter weightage

There is no officially published fixed weightage, but based on the syllabus and the depth of each chapter, the split below is a reasonable guide for planning. Use it to decide how long to spend, not to skip anything.

  • Higher: Documentation & Underwriting, Health Insurance, Life Insurance Products, Legal Principles, General Insurance
  • Medium: Introduction to Insurance, Regulatory Aspects of Agency, Customer Service & Grievances
  • Lower: Life Insurance & Financial Planning, Pricing & Valuation, Life Insurance Claims

Terms that candidates most often confuse

Across the syllabus, a handful of pairs and groups of terms account for a large share of wrong answers. Learning them side by side, with one example each, is one of the quickest ways to raise your accuracy. Write each pair on a single line in your notes and test yourself until you can explain the difference in one sentence.

Situation questions are the other common trap. A question may describe a policyholder, a loss and a claim, and ask which principle or rule applies. Practise reading the scenario, underlining the key fact and matching it to the principle before you look at the options.

  • Indemnity vs subrogation vs contribution
  • Nomination vs assignment
  • Surrender value vs paid-up value
  • Cashless vs reimbursement claim
  • Medical vs non-medical underwriting
  • Grievance with the insurer vs complaint to the insurance ombudsman

How to use this syllabus while preparing

Turn each chapter into a checklist of the terms above and tick them off only when you can explain them without notes. Practise chapter-wise questions as soon as you finish reading, then take full-length timed mock tests once all chapters are covered and review the explanation for every mistake.

Keep your official study material as the base text. Guides like this one simplify and organise; they cannot replace the current syllabus, which may be updated by the Insurance Institute of India or IRDAI. Before your exam, check insuranceinstituteofindia.com and irdai.gov.in for any revision.

FAQs

How many chapters are in the IC-38 syllabus?

The study material is organised into chapters covering insurance basics, legal principles, life, health and general insurance products, documentation and underwriting, pricing, claims, regulation and customer service. The exact number and order depend on the licence type and edition, so check the current material from the Insurance Institute of India.

Is the IC-38 syllabus the same for life and general insurance agents?

The foundation chapters overlap, but the product, underwriting and claims chapters differ between life, general, health and composite licences. Confirm which modules apply to your licence with your insurer's licensing team and the official syllabus before you start studying.

Which IC-38 chapter is the hardest?

Most candidates find Documentation and Underwriting and Legal Principles the hardest, because they contain many similar-sounding terms and situation-based questions. Make a one-page sheet of definitions for these chapters and practise applying each principle to short scenarios.
