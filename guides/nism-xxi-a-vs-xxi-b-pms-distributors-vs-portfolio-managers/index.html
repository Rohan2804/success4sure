NISM XXI-A vs XXI-B: Which PMS Certification Do You Need?

Two NISM certifications relate to Portfolio Management Services, and the NISM XXI-A vs XXI-B question confuses many candidates. Both cover PMS, both carry negative marking and both have a 60 percent pass mark in their indicative patterns, yet they are meant for different roles. Series XXI-A is for distributors who sell PMS products to clients. Series XXI-B is for people working inside a portfolio management firm, typically in investment decision-making or key managerial roles. This guide explains who needs which, how the syllabus and pattern differ, and how to prepare.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

The short answer

If you are a relationship manager, wealth manager or distributor who introduces clients to PMS products offered by portfolio managers, XXI-A is the relevant certification. If you work for a portfolio manager — particularly as the principal officer or in a role involved in investment decisions — XXI-B is the relevant one.

The precise categories of persons who must hold each certificate are set by SEBI regulations and circulars, which are revised from time to time. Confirm the current requirement on sebi.gov.in and check the exam details on nism.ac.in before you register.

Pattern comparison

The indicative patterns below are based on the current exam structure as we understand it. Treat them as indicative — confirm in the latest official notification on nism.ac.in.

  • XXI-A (PMS Distributors): 100 questions, 100 marks, 120 minutes, pass mark 60 percent, 0.25 negative marking per wrong answer.
  • XXI-B (Portfolio Managers): 100 questions, 100 marks, 180 minutes, pass mark 60 percent, 0.25 negative marking per wrong answer.
  • The extra hour in XXI-B reflects deeper, more analytical questions, including more on portfolio construction, analysis and risk.
  • Both papers require similar exam discipline because of the same pass mark and negative marking.

What XXI-A focuses on

XXI-A is built around what a distributor needs to explain PMS correctly and sell it responsibly. It covers investment basics across equity, fixed income and derivatives, how PMS compares with mutual funds and other collective investment vehicles, the role and operations of portfolio managers, the portfolio management process at a conceptual level, performance measurement and reporting, taxation, and the regulatory, governance and ethical obligations relevant to distribution.

The emphasis is on understanding products, communicating risks and costs clearly, matching PMS to suitable clients and following the conduct rules that apply to distributors.

What XXI-B focuses on

XXI-B goes further into how a portfolio manager actually runs money. Beyond the shared foundations, it typically covers equity and fixed income analysis in more depth, portfolio construction and risk management, the investment decision process, operational and compliance responsibilities of the portfolio manager, performance measurement and attribution, and the regulatory framework from the manager's side.

Questions are more analytical. Where XXI-A might ask what a risk-adjusted ratio indicates, XXI-B is more likely to expect you to apply it or compare portfolios using it. Check the current XXI-B workbook on nism.ac.in for its exact chapter list, as NISM revises workbooks periodically.

Because XXI-A candidates often come from mutual fund distribution, it is worth noting what feels new: the ownership of securities directly in the client's name, the customised nature of portfolios, fee structures that may include performance-linked components, and the specific disclosure and reporting obligations that apply to PMS. These are the areas where questions tend to separate prepared candidates from those relying on mutual fund knowledge alone.

Key differences at a glance

Seen side by side, the two certifications differ in purpose and depth more than in topic names.

Put simply, XXI-A asks whether you can explain and recommend a PMS responsibly, while XXI-B asks whether you can run one responsibly. Keeping that distinction in mind makes it easier to read each workbook with the right emphasis.

  • Audience: XXI-A for distributors of PMS; XXI-B for persons in the portfolio manager's organisation, especially in investment and key managerial roles.
  • Perspective: XXI-A explains PMS from the client-facing side; XXI-B from the investment and operational side.
  • Depth: XXI-B goes deeper into analysis, portfolio construction and risk.
  • Duration: 120 minutes for XXI-A versus 180 minutes for XXI-B in the indicative patterns.
  • Preparation time: typically two to three weeks for XXI-A and four to six weeks for XXI-B.

Can one certificate substitute for the other?

Generally, no. Each certificate is linked to specific roles by SEBI's requirements, so you should take the one that matches your role. Some professionals move between roles over their careers — a distributor who later joins a portfolio management firm, for example — and may eventually need both.

If you expect such a move, preparing for XXI-A first builds a useful foundation. Most of its investment and PMS concepts reappear in XXI-B, where they are extended with deeper analysis and the manager's responsibilities.

Questions to ask before you register

If you are still unsure which certificate applies to you, a few practical questions usually settle it. Discuss them with your compliance team, who will know how your firm interprets the current regulatory requirements.

  • Is your employer a SEBI-registered portfolio manager, or a distributor that offers PMS products from several portfolio managers?
  • Does your role involve selecting securities, constructing portfolios or approving investment decisions, or does it involve explaining and recommending PMS to clients?
  • Are you, or will you be, designated as a principal officer or in another key managerial position?
  • Does your appointment letter, onboarding checklist or compliance manual name a specific NISM series?
  • Are you planning a move from distribution to portfolio management, or the reverse, in the next few years?

How to prepare for each

For XXI-A, a two-to-three-week plan works for most working professionals. Prioritise the role of portfolio managers, operational aspects, the portfolio management process and regulation, and build a comparison table of PMS, mutual funds and AIFs. Practise return and risk-adjusted ratio calculations until they are quick.

For XXI-B, plan four to six weeks. Spend more time on equity and fixed income analysis, portfolio construction, risk measures and performance evaluation, and read the regulatory chapters from the portfolio manager's perspective.

For both, finish with several full-length timed mock tests and review the explanations carefully. Because both exams deduct a quarter mark per wrong answer, track your accuracy on attempted questions and skip questions where you cannot eliminate at least two options.

If you have cleared XXI-A and later move to a portfolio manager, revisit your XXI-A notes before starting XXI-B; the overlap will save you a week or more.

  • Download the current workbook for your exam from nism.ac.in.
  • Practise chapter-wise questions after every chapter.
  • Keep a formula sheet and a regulatory notes sheet, revised daily in the final week.

FAQs

Is NISM XXI-B harder than XXI-A?

Generally yes. XXI-B is meant for professionals involved in managing portfolios, so it goes deeper into analysis, portfolio construction and risk, and the indicative pattern allows 180 minutes against 120 for XXI-A. Both have a 60 percent pass mark and negative marking in their indicative patterns.

I distribute PMS products. Do I need XXI-B?

Distributors of PMS products are generally covered by Series XXI-A. XXI-B is aimed at persons working within a portfolio management firm, particularly in investment decision-making or key managerial roles. Confirm the latest requirements on sebi.gov.in and nism.ac.in, as they can change.

Does XXI-A preparation help with XXI-B?

Yes. The investment basics, PMS concepts, performance measurement and regulatory framework from XXI-A form a solid base for XXI-B. You will still need to add deeper equity and fixed income analysis, portfolio construction, risk management and the portfolio manager's operational and compliance responsibilities.
