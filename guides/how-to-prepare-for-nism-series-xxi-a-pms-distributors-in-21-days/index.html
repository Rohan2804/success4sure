How to Prepare for NISM Series XXI-A in 21 Days

Relationship managers who distribute Portfolio Management Services usually need this certification, and the most common question is how to prepare for NISM Series XXI-A alongside a full-time job. The syllabus starts with investment basics across equity, fixed income and derivatives, then moves to collective investment vehicles, the role and operations of portfolio managers, the portfolio management process, performance measurement, taxation and SEBI's PMS regulations. A 21-day plan gives enough time for the calculation chapters and several full-length tests. This guide lays out the schedule week by week, with approximate weightage and a revision routine.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Exam pattern and preparation approach

The indicative pattern is 100 questions carrying 100 marks in 120 minutes, with a pass mark of 60 percent and negative marking of 0.25 marks per wrong answer. Treat these as indicative — confirm in the latest official notification on nism.ac.in.

The 60 percent pass line is the key number. It means you need around 65 correct answers with few mistakes, so you cannot rely on the regulatory chapter alone. Candidates with a mutual fund background often find the investment chapters comfortable but are caught out by PMS-specific operational and regulatory details, and by performance measurement questions.

Who should adjust the timeline

Twenty-one days assumes about two hours on weekdays and three hours at weekends. If you have recently cleared NISM V-A or X-A, the investment foundation chapters will feel familiar; you can compress Week 1 into four days and use the saved time for an extra mock test or deeper work on PMS operations. If equity valuation and bond maths are new to you, extend Week 1 to ten days, because later chapters on the portfolio process and performance measurement assume this foundation.

Also check the latest workbook on nism.ac.in before you start. SEBI has revised PMS regulations, disclosure requirements and performance reporting norms several times in recent years, and NISM updates the workbook to reflect them. Studying from an outdated edition is a common and avoidable cause of lost marks, especially in the regulatory chapter.

It is worth asking your employer or compliance team whether they have a preferred exam window, since many firms schedule certification deadlines around onboarding or annual renewal cycles.

Approximate chapter weightage

NISM does not publish an official chapter-wise split. Based on the syllabus and the depth of workbook chapters, the following grouping is approximate.

  • High: Role of Portfolio Managers; Operational Aspects of Portfolio Managers; Portfolio Management Process; Regulatory, Governance and Ethical Aspects of Portfolio Managers.
  • Medium: Investing in Stocks; Investing in Fixed Income Securities; Performance Measurement and Evaluation of Portfolio Managers; Collective Investment Vehicles.
  • Lower but direct: Investments; Introduction to Securities Markets; Derivatives; Taxation.
  • Calculation areas: returns (absolute, annualised, time-weighted concepts as covered in the workbook), risk measures, risk-adjusted ratios and basic bond and equity valuation.

Week 1: investment foundations

Study about two hours on weekdays and three hours at weekends. End each day with chapter-wise practice questions while the material is fresh.

  • Day 1: Investments — the concept of investment, risk and return, asset classes.
  • Day 2: Introduction to Securities Markets — primary and secondary markets, participants and regulators.
  • Days 3–4: Investing in Stocks — equity research basics, valuation ratios, approaches to stock selection.
  • Days 5–6: Investing in Fixed Income Securities — bond features, yields, price-yield relationship, credit risk and duration at the level covered in the workbook.
  • Day 7: Derivatives — futures and options basics and their use in portfolios; revision of Week 1.

Week 2: PMS in depth

This is the most important week. Focus on how a PMS differs from other managed products and how it is run day to day.

  • Day 8: Collective Investment Vehicles — mutual funds, AIFs and other pooled products, and how PMS compares.
  • Days 9–10: Role of Portfolio Managers — discretionary, non-discretionary and advisory services; the portfolio manager's responsibilities; client agreements and disclosure documents.
  • Days 11–12: Operational Aspects of Portfolio Managers — client onboarding, account structure, custody, fees and their computation methods as described in the workbook, reporting to clients.
  • Days 13–14: Portfolio Management Process — investment policy, asset allocation, strategy, rebalancing; then a first full-length timed mock test with review.

Week 3: performance, tax, regulation and mocks

The final week covers the remaining technical and regulatory chapters and builds exam stamina.

  • Days 15–16: Performance Measurement and Evaluation of Portfolio Managers — return calculations, benchmarks, risk-adjusted measures such as Sharpe and Treynor, and the performance reporting norms described in the workbook.
  • Day 17: Taxation — tax treatment of PMS investments as described in the current workbook edition.
  • Day 18: Regulatory, Governance and Ethical Aspects of Portfolio Managers — SEBI PMS regulations, distributor obligations, code of conduct. Read twice.
  • Day 19: Second full-length mock and detailed review.
  • Day 20: Rework every error; third mock in the evening.
  • Day 21: Light revision of formula sheet, regulatory notes and error log.

Revision techniques for XXI-A

Create a comparison table of PMS, mutual funds and AIFs with rows for structure, minimum investment as stated in the workbook, ownership of securities, customisation, fees, transparency and taxation. Many questions ask how PMS differs from a fund, and one table answers most of them. Take any thresholds or limits only from the current workbook, because SEBI revises them.

For performance measurement, keep a formula sheet and re-solve every problem you got wrong in practice. For regulation, write each key obligation as a short question-and-answer card and revise the cards on at least four separate days.

  • Maintain an error log with chapter, concept and correction.
  • Revise the PMS vs mutual fund vs AIF table daily in Week 3.
  • Practise fee computation examples from the workbook by hand.

Mock tests and exam-day approach

Take three full-length timed mock tests in the final week, each in a single 120-minute sitting, and review the explanation for every wrong or guessed answer. Track accuracy on attempted questions; with a 60 percent pass mark and negative marking, aim to keep it above 80 percent.

On exam day, answer everything you are sure of in a first pass, marking calculation and long scenario questions. Use the remaining time for marked questions and guess only where you have eliminated at least two options. Keep ten minutes at the end to check for unanswered questions you meant to attempt.

After the exam, keep your comparison table and regulatory notes. They double as a handy reference when explaining PMS features, costs and risks to clients, which is the everyday work the certification is designed to support.

FAQs

Is 21 days enough to prepare for NISM Series XXI-A?

For most working professionals who can study two to three hours a day, 21 days is a realistic window. Those who have already cleared NISM V-A or work in wealth management may need around two weeks. Complete beginners should allow four weeks, with extra time on fixed income and performance measurement.

Which chapters are most important for NISM XXI-A?

Based on the syllabus, the chapters on the role of portfolio managers, operational aspects, the portfolio management process and regulation tend to carry the most weight. Investment basics, performance measurement and collective investment vehicles follow. NISM does not publish an official split.

Does NISM XXI-A have calculation questions?

Yes, though they are not the majority. Expect some questions on returns, risk measures, risk-adjusted ratios, bond yields and possibly fee computation. The arithmetic is simple, but practising the formulas from the workbook makes these questions quick and reliable.
