SBI Clerk Syllabus Explained Section by Section

The SBI Clerk syllabus is short to list but wide in practice. Prelims tests English Language, Numerical Ability and Reasoning Ability, and mains adds General or Financial Awareness and General English along with harder reasoning and quantitative questions. Knowing what each topic actually involves, and approximately how much it counts, helps you study the right things in the right order. This guide explains the prelims syllabus section by section, outlines the mains additions, and suggests how to turn the syllabus into a study plan. Always confirm the latest syllabus in the official notification on sbi.co.in/careers.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Exam structure in brief (indicative)

SBI Clerk is the Junior Associate (Customer Support and Sales) recruitment. Our mock tests use an indicative prelims pattern of 100 questions, 100 marks and 60 minutes, with 0.25 negative marking and sectional time limits as used in recent cycles. Indicative — confirm in the latest official notification.

Mains is a separate, longer paper with its own sections, and a local language proficiency test may apply. Final selection details are set out in each year's notification.

English Language syllabus

English tests reading, grammar and sentence sense rather than rare vocabulary. Reading comprehension usually carries the most questions, with passages on economy, society or general topics. Cloze tests check vocabulary in context and grammar together.

Grammar-based questions include error spotting, sentence improvement and fillers. Arrangement questions include para jumbles and sentence rearrangement. Word-usage items such as word swap or contextual meaning appear in some cycles.

Vocabulary is best built from reading rather than word lists. Read one editorial or long article daily, note four or five unfamiliar words with the sentence in which they appeared, and revise them weekly. This helps both RC and cloze questions.

  • Reading comprehension: main idea, detail, inference, tone, vocabulary in context
  • Cloze test: one passage, several blanks
  • Error spotting and sentence improvement: subject-verb agreement, tenses, prepositions, articles
  • Fillers and word usage
  • Para jumbles and sentence rearrangement

Numerical Ability syllabus

This section rewards calculation speed. Simplification and approximation is usually the biggest block, followed by number series and data interpretation. Arithmetic word problems cover the standard topics from school and early college maths.

Quadratic equations, where you compare two variables, appear in many banking papers and are quick marks once practised. DI typically uses tables, bar graphs, pie charts or line graphs with percentage and ratio calculations.

Simplification questions usually involve fractions, decimals, percentages, squares, cubes and roots, so memorising tables and common values is part of the syllabus in practice. Approximation questions allow rounding, which is a skill worth practising separately.

  • Simplification and approximation (approximately the largest share)
  • Number series: missing and wrong-number series
  • Data interpretation: table, bar, line, pie, caselet
  • Arithmetic: percentages, ratio and proportion, averages, ages, profit and loss, simple and compound interest, time and work, speed and distance, mixtures, partnership
  • Quadratic equations and basic mensuration and probability

Reasoning Ability syllabus

Reasoning is dominated by puzzles and seating arrangements: linear, circular, floor-based, box-based and scheduling puzzles. These take the most time and carry the largest share of marks in the section, approximately.

The remaining questions come from quick topics that you should aim to solve in under a minute each.

Puzzle types vary between cycles, so practise a range: people sitting in rows or around tables, floors of a building, boxes stacked one above another, and schedules across days or months. The method — fixing definite positions first — stays the same.

  • Puzzles and seating arrangement (approximately the largest share)
  • Inequalities: direct and coded
  • Syllogism, including 'only a few' and possibility cases
  • Coding-decoding and alphanumeric series
  • Blood relations, direction sense, order and ranking

What mains adds

Mains repeats reasoning, quantitative aptitude and English at a higher level and adds General or Financial Awareness, which covers current affairs, banking and financial terms, and static general knowledge. Recent cycles have combined reasoning with computer aptitude. Confirm the current mains sections and timings in the official notification.

General awareness cannot be crammed in a week, so read the news and a banking-awareness summary for 15 to 20 minutes daily, even while preparing for prelims.

For computer aptitude, revise basics such as hardware and software terms, operating systems, MS Office features, internet and networking terms, and common keyboard shortcuts. Questions are usually factual and quick, so a compact set of notes revised regularly is enough for most candidates.

Approximate weightage and study order

Since the official notification lists topics but not marks per topic, use the following as a planning guide based on the syllabus and the usual paper. Study high-weight and quick topics first, then the time-consuming ones.

  • First: simplification, inequalities, syllogism, error spotting, fillers
  • Second: number series, coding-decoding, cloze test, arithmetic basics
  • Third: puzzles and seating, data interpretation, reading comprehension speed
  • Ongoing: general and financial awareness for mains

How each section tends to be tested

Knowing the syllabus is half the job; knowing how each topic appears is the other half. In English, RC questions often ask for the main idea, a specific detail and the meaning of a word in context, while cloze tests mix vocabulary and grammar. In Numerical Ability, DI questions usually test percentage change, ratios and averages from the given data, so arithmetic speed carries over directly.

In Reasoning, puzzles typically come as sets of five questions based on one arrangement, so solving the arrangement once correctly earns several marks. Inequalities and syllogism appear as quick stand-alone items. Practising each topic in the form it actually appears makes your preparation far more efficient.

  • RC: main idea, detail, inference and vocabulary in context
  • DI: percentage change, ratio and average from tables and charts
  • Puzzles: one arrangement, several questions
  • Quick items: inequalities, syllogism, coding, error spotting

Common syllabus mistakes to avoid

Do not study topics from older or unrelated exam syllabi that are no longer asked, and do not skip a topic just because it was absent in one paper. Use the current notification as your boundary, and give extra time to high-weight topics without ignoring the rest entirely.

Turning the syllabus into practice

Convert each bullet above into a checklist. Practise 20 to 30 timed questions per topic, then mixed sectional tests, and finally full-length timed mock tests with a careful review of explanations for every error. Track which topics cost you the most marks and revisit them weekly.

Use the official notification on sbi.co.in/careers as the final authority on the syllabus, sections and timing, because SBI can revise them between cycles.

FAQs

What is the syllabus for SBI Clerk prelims?

Prelims covers English Language (reading comprehension, cloze, error spotting, fillers, para jumbles), Numerical Ability (simplification, series, DI, arithmetic, quadratic equations) and Reasoning Ability (puzzles, seating, inequalities, syllogism, coding, blood relations, direction). Confirm the current syllabus in the official notification.

Is general awareness part of SBI Clerk prelims?

General awareness has not been part of SBI Clerk prelims in recent cycles; it is tested in mains along with financial awareness. Still, start reading daily news early, since mains follows soon after prelims. Check the latest notification for the exact sections.

Which topics are most important in the SBI Clerk syllabus?

Based on the syllabus, simplification, number series and DI in Numerical Ability, puzzles and seating in Reasoning, and reading comprehension and cloze in English carry the most marks. Quick topics like inequalities and error spotting are also important because they score fast.
