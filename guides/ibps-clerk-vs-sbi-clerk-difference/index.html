IBPS Clerk vs SBI Clerk: Differences Explained

IBPS Clerk vs SBI Clerk is a choice many bank aspirants think they have to make, but in practice most write both. The two exams recruit clerical staff, test the same three areas at prelims and share most of their syllabus. They differ in the conducting body, which bank you join, how the papers tend to feel, and parts of the selection process such as the local language test. This guide sets out the differences clearly, shows how one preparation plan can cover both, and helps you prioritise if the dates clash. For eligibility, dates and vacancies, rely only on ibps.in and sbi.co.in/careers.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Who conducts each exam and which bank you join

IBPS Clerk is conducted by the Institute of Banking Personnel Selection for participating public sector banks; after the result, candidates are allotted to a bank, usually within the state they applied for. SBI Clerk, officially the Junior Associate (Customer Support and Sales) recruitment, is conducted by the State Bank of India for its own branches.

Both are state-wise recruitments, so you apply for one state and are generally expected to know its official local language. That makes the choice of state as important as the choice of exam.

Exam pattern compared (indicative)

In our mock tests, both prelims follow the same indicative pattern: 100 questions, 100 marks, 60 minutes and 0.25 negative marking, across English Language, Numerical Ability and Reasoning Ability, with sectional time limits used in recent cycles. This is indicative — confirm in the latest official notification for each exam.

Both mains exams add General or Financial Awareness and a computer or general English component alongside reasoning and quantitative sections. Neither clerk exam has had an interview in recent cycles; instead there is a local language proficiency test for candidates who need it. Confirm every stage in the current notification.

  • Prelims sections: English, Numerical Ability, Reasoning in both (indicative)
  • Prelims: 100 questions in 60 minutes in both (indicative)
  • Negative marking: 0.25 per wrong answer in both (indicative)
  • No interview in recent cycles; local language test applies as per notification

Difficulty and paper style

SBI Clerk is often described as slightly tougher at prelims, especially in Reasoning puzzles and in Numerical Ability, where arithmetic word problems can be longer. IBPS Clerk tends to reward speed on a large number of moderate questions, particularly simplification and number series.

In English, both use reading comprehension, cloze tests and error-based questions. SBI Clerk passages have sometimes been longer. Year-to-year variation is large, so do not read too much into one shift; prepare for a moderate-to-moderately-difficult level in both.

Remember that difficulty is relative to the others in your shift. On a tougher paper, attempts fall for everyone, so keep accuracy high and avoid forcing extra attempts just to match your best mock. Practising one harder mock a week helps you stay calm when the actual paper surprises you.

Syllabus overlap and differences in emphasis

At prelims, the overlap is almost complete. Daily practice in simplification, number series, arithmetic, DI, puzzles, inequalities, syllogism and reading comprehension serves both exams equally. Even in mains, the general and financial awareness preparation is shared.

The differences are mostly in emphasis. Expect slightly harder puzzles and arithmetic in SBI Clerk, and a stronger premium on raw calculation speed in IBPS Clerk.

  • Shared Numerical Ability: simplification, approximation, number series, arithmetic, DI
  • Shared Reasoning: puzzles, seating, inequalities, syllogism, coding-decoding, alphanumeric series
  • Shared English: RC, cloze test, error spotting, fillers, para jumbles
  • Extra for SBI: harder puzzles and longer arithmetic word problems
  • Extra for IBPS: maximum simplification and series speed

Which should you prioritise?

If you have a clear preference for SBI's branch network and brand, make SBI Clerk your main target and write IBPS Clerk as a parallel chance. If you are open to any public sector bank in your state, write both and let the calendar decide your final-month focus.

For beginners, start with IBPS-level speed drills to build a base, then add SBI-level puzzles and arithmetic. For candidates already scoring well in moderate mocks, practise at SBI level so that IBPS feels comfortable on the day.

Also consider timing. If the two prelims fall close together, prepare with SBI-level difficulty throughout so that you are ready for either, and switch only your mock style in the final week before each exam.

  • Clear SBI preference: SBI Clerk first, IBPS Clerk as backup
  • Open to any PSU bank in your state: write both with equal effort
  • Beginner: build speed with IBPS-style drills, then raise difficulty
  • Experienced: practise at SBI difficulty throughout

One plan for both exams

Use a single daily routine: simplification and series every day, puzzles every day, and one timed RC passage. Change only the style of your mock tests depending on which exam is next. Taking full-length timed mock tests and reviewing the explanations for every mistake will do more for both exams than switching study material.

Keep one error notebook and one formula sheet. In the fortnight before each prelims, take mocks at your exam slot time and practise the section order you intend to use. After the first exam, take a day of light revision and then switch to the second exam's mock style.

Local language and state choice

Both exams expect candidates to know the official language of the state they apply for, and a language test may be conducted for those who did not study it at school. Check the exact rule and the list of languages for each state in the notification before applying.

Choose the state where you can genuinely work and communicate. A state with more posts may look attractive, but language proficiency and comfort with local customers matter for the job.

Mains differences worth knowing

In mains, both exams add general or financial awareness, and both test reasoning, quantitative aptitude and English at a higher level, with computer aptitude combined into one section in recent cycles. The number of questions, sectional timings and marks differ between the two, and they can change each year.

For preparation, the practical difference is small. Daily current affairs, banking awareness and basic computer knowledge serve both exams. If both mains fall close together, revise general awareness continuously and adjust only your mock style for each exam.

Where to check official details

Eligibility, age limits, state-wise posts, dates and the selection process change from cycle to cycle. Read the full notification on ibps.in for IBPS Clerk and sbi.co.in/careers for SBI Clerk before you apply, and check both regularly for updates and corrigenda.

FAQs

Is SBI Clerk tougher than IBPS Clerk?

Many aspirants find SBI Clerk prelims slightly tougher, mainly in puzzles and arithmetic, but difficulty changes every year and across shifts. Preparing at SBI-level difficulty after your basics are strong usually covers IBPS Clerk comfortably as well.

Is there an interview in IBPS Clerk or SBI Clerk?

Neither exam has used an interview in recent cycles. Selection has been based on mains performance, subject to a local language proficiency test where applicable. Always confirm the current selection process in the latest official notification, since it can change.

Can I apply for both IBPS Clerk and SBI Clerk?

Yes, eligible candidates can apply for both, since they are separate recruitments. The syllabus overlaps heavily, so one preparation plan serves both. Plan your final weeks around whichever exam comes first, and check each notification for eligibility and state rules.
