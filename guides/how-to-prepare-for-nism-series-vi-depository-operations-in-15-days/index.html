How to Prepare for NISM Series VI in 15 Days

Employees of depository participants often have to clear the Depository Operations certification within weeks of joining, so knowing how to prepare for NISM Series VI efficiently matters. The syllabus covers the depository system, NSDL and CDSL, account opening and operations, transmission and nomination, dematerialisation, trading and settlement, pledge, corporate actions, debt and government securities, foreign portfolio investors and investor services. It is detailed rather than difficult, and the pass mark is higher than some NISM papers. This guide gives a 15-day plan, approximate weightage, a revision routine and a mock-test schedule.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Exam pattern and why precision matters

The indicative pattern is 100 questions carrying 100 marks in 120 minutes, with a pass mark of 60 percent and negative marking of 0.25 marks per wrong answer. Treat these as indicative — confirm in the latest official notification on nism.ac.in before you register.

A 60 percent pass line with negative marking means you need roughly 65 correct answers with few mistakes. Most Series VI questions test specific procedures — which form is used, who signs, what happens when a holder dies, how a pledge is closed — so vague familiarity is not enough. The plan below is built around learning each procedure step by step.

Approximate chapter weightage

NISM does not publish an official chapter-wise split. Based on the syllabus and the length of each workbook chapter, this grouping is approximate.

  • High: DP Functions – Account Opening; DP Functions – Account Operations; DP Functions – Dematerialisation; DP Functions – Transmission and Nomination.
  • Medium: Introduction to Depository; Depository and its Business Partners; DP Functions – Trading and Settlement; Special Services – Pledge & Hypothecation; Special Services – Public Offering / Corporate Actions.
  • Lower but direct: Introduction to the Indian Capital Market; Special Services – Debt Instruments & Government Securities; Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPI); Investor Services.
  • Very few questions involve calculations; nearly all test procedures, roles and definitions.

Who should adjust the plan

Fifteen days assumes about two hours on weekdays and three hours at weekends. If you already work at a DP desk handling account opening and transfers, several chapters will be familiar and you could finish in ten days, spending the saved time on mock tests. If you are new to the securities industry, allow about three weeks and spend extra time on the account operations and transmission chapters, which carry many questions and involve several similar-looking procedures.

Use the current workbook from nism.ac.in as your only reference for procedures and timelines. Depository processes are updated through SEBI circulars and depository operating instructions, and questions follow the workbook edition.

Days 1–5: the depository system and account opening

Read each chapter, write a one-page summary and finish with chapter-wise practice questions.

  • Day 1: Introduction to the Indian Capital Market — primary and secondary markets, intermediaries, the shift from physical to electronic holding.
  • Day 2: Introduction to Depository — the depository concept, benefits of dematerialised holding, the legal framework, NSDL and CDSL.
  • Day 3: Depository and its Business Partners — DPs, issuers, RTAs, clearing corporations and their relationship with the depository.
  • Days 4–5: DP Functions – Account Opening — types of beneficial owner accounts, KYC, documents for individuals and non-individuals, joint holdings, account closure.

Days 6–10: core DP operations

These chapters contain the most questions. Draw a flow diagram for every process and note which form or instruction is involved at each step.

  • Day 6: DP Functions – Account Operations — delivery instructions, off-market and market transfers, freezing and unfreezing, change of details.
  • Day 7: DP Functions – Transmission and Nomination — nomination rules, transmission on death of a sole or joint holder, documents required.
  • Day 8: DP Functions – Dematerialisation — the demat request flow from client to DP to RTA to depository, rematerialisation, rejection reasons.
  • Day 9: DP Functions – Trading and Settlement — pay-in and pay-out through the depository, settlement-related instructions.
  • Day 10: First full-length timed mock test and a detailed review of every explanation.

Days 11–15: special services, revision and mocks

The final stretch covers the special services chapters and builds exam rhythm.

  • Day 11: Special Services – Pledge & Hypothecation; Special Services – Public Offering / Corporate Actions.
  • Day 12: Special Services – Debt Instruments & Government Securities; Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPI); Investor Services. Second mock in the evening.
  • Day 13: Revise account operations, transmission and dematerialisation using your flow diagrams; rework every mock error.
  • Day 14: Third full-length mock and review.
  • Day 15: Light revision of summaries and error log only; check your test centre details.

A revision method built for procedures

For every process, write three things: the trigger (what the client wants), the steps in order with the entity responsible, and the outcome. Then test yourself by covering the steps and reconstructing them. This works far better than re-reading, because exam questions often present the steps out of order or swap the responsible entity.

Keep a comparison table for pairs that are easy to confuse: nomination versus transmission, pledge versus hypothecation, freezing versus closing an account, market versus off-market transfer, dematerialisation versus rematerialisation. A large share of wrong answers in this exam comes from mixing up such pairs.

  • Revise flow diagrams daily from Day 6 onwards.
  • Keep a short sheet of timelines and limits from the workbook.
  • Maintain an error log and read it every evening in the final week.

Common mistakes to avoid

The most frequent mistake in Series VI is treating NSDL and CDSL terminology as interchangeable without checking how the workbook describes each process. Another is studying from summaries or older notes; depository procedures, forms and timelines are revised through SEBI circulars, and outdated details produce confidently wrong answers. A third is skipping the shorter chapters on FPIs, government securities and investor services because they look peripheral. They are quick to learn and supply direct, factual questions that help you clear the 60 percent line without stress.

Mock tests and exam-day approach

Three full-length timed mock tests in the final week are usually enough. Sit each one in a single 120-minute block and then review the explanations for every wrong and guessed answer; that review is where most improvement happens. Track accuracy on attempted questions and aim to keep it above 80 percent.

On exam day, answer every question you are sure of in a first pass of about 70 minutes, marking the rest. Return to marked questions and attempt only those where you can eliminate at least two options. Read procedural questions twice; words like 'not', 'first' and 'only' frequently decide the answer.

FAQs

Is 15 days enough to prepare for NISM Series VI?

For most candidates who can study around two hours a day, especially those already working at a depository participant, 15 days is enough. Freshers to the securities industry should allow about three weeks, giving extra time to account operations, transmission and dematerialisation procedures.

Which chapters are most important in NISM VI?

Based on the syllabus, the DP functions chapters — account opening, account operations, transmission and nomination, and dematerialisation — tend to carry the most questions. Pledge, corporate actions and trading and settlement follow. NISM does not publish an official split, so read every chapter.

What is the pass mark for NISM Series VI?

The indicative pattern has a pass mark of 60 percent, with 100 questions in 120 minutes and 0.25 negative marking per wrong answer. Patterns can change, so confirm the current details in the latest official notification on nism.ac.in before booking your exam.
