NISM Series VIII Toughest Chapters and How to Score

Most candidates who fall short in Equity Derivatives lose marks in the same places, which is why knowing the NISM Series VIII toughest chapters in advance is so useful. The exam's indicative pattern — 100 questions, 120 minutes, a 60 percent pass mark and 0.25 negative marking (confirm in the latest official notification on nism.ac.in) — leaves little room for weak areas. This guide walks through the four chapters that trouble candidates most, explains why they are hard, and gives specific techniques to turn each into a scoring area.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Why some chapters feel harder

Series VIII questions fall into two kinds: factual recall and applied reasoning. Factual questions on trading rules, regulation or taxation are straightforward if you have read the workbook carefully. Applied questions — what is the payoff of this position, how many contracts are needed to hedge, what is the breakeven — require you to work through a small problem under time pressure.

The chapters below are dominated by applied questions. They are hard not because the maths is advanced (it is basic arithmetic) but because a single sign error or confusion between long and short positions produces a wrong answer that also appears among the options.

1. Introduction to Options

This chapter is the heart of the exam. Candidates struggle with moneyness for puts, the split of premium into intrinsic and time value, put-call parity, and the direction in which each factor moves call and put prices. The Greeks add a further layer: you need to know what delta, gamma, theta, vega and rho measure, their typical ranges, and how they behave for in-the-money, at-the-money and out-of-the-money options.

How to score: make a two-column table — calls on the left, puts on the right — and fill in moneyness conditions, intrinsic value formula, and the effect of a rise in spot price, strike, volatility, time to expiry and interest rate. Revise it until you can reproduce it from memory. Then solve at least 30 option questions across chapter-wise practice sets.

  • A call is in the money when spot is above strike; a put when spot is below strike.
  • Intrinsic value can never be negative; time value tends to decay towards zero at expiry.
  • Higher volatility generally raises the premium of both calls and puts.

2. Strategies using Equity Futures and Options

Strategy questions describe a combination — say, buying one call and selling another at a higher strike — and ask for maximum profit, maximum loss, breakeven or the market view the strategy expresses. Candidates who memorise names without understanding payoffs get confused when the question uses different strikes or quantities.

How to score: for every strategy, draw the expiry payoff on rough paper with the underlying price on the horizontal axis. Mark the strikes, then calculate profit or loss at a price below the lower strike, between strikes and above the higher strike. Three points are enough to see the shape and read off the answer.

  • Bull call spread and bull put spread: moderately bullish, limited profit and limited loss.
  • Long straddle and long strangle: expect a big move in either direction, loss limited to premium paid.
  • Short straddle: expects low volatility, loss potentially unlimited.
  • Protective put and covered call: combine a stock or futures position with an option to change the risk profile.

3. Introduction to Forwards and Futures

The cost of carry model, basis, convergence at expiry and beta-based hedging are the trouble spots. Questions may give a spot price, interest rate, dividend and days to expiry and ask for the fair futures price, or give a portfolio value and beta and ask how many index futures contracts to sell.

How to score: practise the fair value calculation in both simple and continuous compounding forms if the current workbook uses both, and always check whether the dividend reduces the carry. For hedging, remember the logic: portfolio value multiplied by beta, divided by the value of one futures contract (futures price multiplied by lot size). Work through at least ten such problems.

4. Clearing and Settlement System

This chapter is less about calculation and more about precise operational rules, which is why it surprises candidates. Questions test the types of margins, who collects them from whom, how daily mark-to-market settlement works, and how final settlement differs for index and stock derivatives.

How to score: draw the flow of money from the client to the trading member to the clearing member to the clearing corporation. Then work through a three-day mark-to-market example on a long futures position, writing down the daily settlement amount. Once you have done it by hand, MCQs on this topic become much easier.

Easier chapters you should not neglect

Because so much effort goes into the four tough chapters, candidates sometimes under-prepare the lighter ones and lose easy marks. These chapters are short, factual and quick to revise, so they offer some of the best marks-per-hour in the syllabus.

  • Basics of Derivatives: definitions, history, types of participants, exchange-traded versus OTC contracts.
  • Understanding Index: free-float methodology, impact cost, index maintenance and uses of an index.
  • Trading Mechanism: order types, contract specifications, position limits, and the role of trading members.
  • Legal and Regulatory Environment: the role of SEBI and the exchanges, and key regulatory provisions covered in the workbook.
  • Accounting and Taxation: accounting treatment of futures and options and the tax treatment of derivatives income as described in the current workbook edition.
  • Sales Practices and Investor Protection: suitability, risk disclosure and grievance redressal.

Turning weak areas into scoring areas

Spend roughly 60 percent of your total preparation time on these four chapters and 40 percent on the rest. That sounds lopsided, but the remaining chapters — Basics of Derivatives, Understanding Index, Trading Mechanism, Legal and Regulatory Environment, Accounting and Taxation, and Sales Practices and Investor Protection — are largely factual and respond well to two careful readings plus practice questions.

Use full-length timed mock tests to confirm progress. After each attempt, sort your mistakes by chapter. If options or strategies still dominate your error list, go back to drawing payoffs rather than re-reading theory. Reviewing the explanation for every wrong answer is what converts a 55 into a 65.

  • Keep an error log with the chapter, the concept and the one-line correction.
  • Re-attempt every wrong numerical a day later without looking at the solution.
  • Revise the formula sheet and the calls-vs-puts table every day in the final week.

Managing these chapters on exam day

On the day, do not let a long strategy question break your rhythm. Mark it, finish the factual questions first, and return with a fresh mind. When you do attempt it, sketch the payoff — it takes less than a minute and catches most errors. With negative marking, skip any applied question where you cannot narrow the options down to two.

FAQs

Which is the toughest chapter in NISM Series VIII?

Most candidates find Introduction to Options and the strategies chapter the hardest, because they combine concepts like moneyness and the Greeks with payoff calculations. These chapters also tend to carry a large share of questions, so they deserve the most practice time.

How many numerical questions come in NISM Series VIII?

The number varies by paper and NISM does not publish a fixed count. Expect a meaningful share of questions on futures pricing, hedging, option payoffs, strategies and mark-to-market. The arithmetic is simple, but you need to practise to avoid sign and direction errors.

How can I improve my score in options strategies?

Draw the expiry payoff for every strategy instead of memorising names. Calculate profit or loss at three underlying prices — below, between and above the strikes — to find breakevens and limits. Then practise mixed strategy questions in timed conditions and review each explanation.
