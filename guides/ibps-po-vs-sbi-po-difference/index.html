IBPS PO vs SBI PO: Differences and Which to Target

IBPS PO vs SBI PO is one of the most common questions bank aspirants ask, and the honest answer is that most serious candidates prepare for both. The two exams recruit probationary officers, share almost the same syllabus and test similar skills. They differ in who conducts them, which banks you join, how the papers feel and how the final selection works. This guide compares the two clearly, explains where the preparation overlaps and where it differs, and helps you decide how to prioritise if your time is limited. For eligibility, dates and vacancies, always rely on ibps.in and sbi.co.in/careers.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

Who conducts each exam and where you work

IBPS PO is conducted by the Institute of Banking Personnel Selection for participating public sector banks. After the final result, candidates are allotted to one of these banks based on merit and preference. SBI PO is conducted by the State Bank of India for its own recruitment, so selected candidates join SBI directly.

That single difference shapes a lot: the work culture, postings and career path are those of the bank you join. If you have a strong preference for SBI, its exam is your direct route; if you are open to several public sector banks, IBPS PO widens your options. Many candidates simply write both.

Both roles are officer-grade positions with responsibility for branch operations, customers and, over time, credit and team management. Postings and transfers follow each bank's policies, which you can read about on the respective career pages. Think about where you are willing to work and the kind of organisation you prefer, not only about which exam seems easier.

Exam pattern compared (indicative)

Both prelims are structured similarly in our mock tests: 100 questions, 100 marks, 60 minutes and 0.25 negative marking, across English, Quantitative Aptitude and Reasoning. Both have used sectional time limits in recent cycles. This is indicative — confirm in the latest official notification for each exam.

The mains and the final stage differ more. Both mains papers include objective sections and a descriptive component, and both have a later interview stage, while SBI has also included a group exercise in some cycles. Check the current notification, because these stages are revised from time to time.

  • Prelims sections: English, Quant, Reasoning in both (indicative)
  • Prelims questions and time: 100 in 60 minutes in both (indicative)
  • Negative marking: 0.25 per wrong answer in both (indicative)
  • Final stage: interview for IBPS PO; SBI has used group exercise plus interview in some cycles

Difficulty and paper style

Candidates generally find SBI PO slightly tougher, especially in Reasoning and Quant, where puzzles and data interpretation sets tend to be longer and more calculation-heavy. IBPS PO prelims is often described as more balanced, though the difficulty varies by year and shift.

In English, SBI PO has often leaned towards longer reading comprehension passages and inference-based questions, while IBPS PO tests a mix of RC, cloze and grammar. These are tendencies, not rules; prepare for both styles.

Remember that difficulty is relative to everyone else in your shift. A harder paper usually means fewer attempts across the board, so the aim on the day is to make good choices and keep accuracy high, not to match the attempts you managed in an easier mock.

Syllabus overlap: one preparation, two exams

The core syllabus overlaps almost entirely, which is why preparing for one prepares you for the other. Your daily practice in arithmetic, DI, puzzles, inequalities and reading comprehension serves both exams equally.

The main differences appear in emphasis rather than content. SBI papers reward speed on harder puzzles and DI; IBPS papers reward accuracy across a wider spread of moderate questions.

  • Common Quant: simplification, number series, quadratic equations, arithmetic, DI
  • Common Reasoning: puzzles, seating arrangement, inequalities, syllogism, coding-decoding, blood relations
  • Common English: reading comprehension, cloze test, error spotting, para jumbles, fillers
  • Extra focus for SBI: harder puzzles, longer DI sets, inference-based RC

Which one should you prioritise?

If you are a beginner, build your base using a moderate-level plan suited to IBPS PO, then raise the difficulty with SBI-level puzzles and DI in the last few weeks. If you are already scoring well in moderate mocks, practise at SBI level from the start, since harder practice makes the IBPS paper feel more comfortable.

Look at the calendar too. Whichever exam comes first in a given year deserves the final-month focus, but do not drop the other. A week of switching to the other exam's style before its date is usually enough.

  • Prefer SBI specifically: prioritise SBI PO, write IBPS PO as a parallel chance
  • Open to any public sector bank: write both; IBPS PO offers more bank options
  • Beginner: start with IBPS-level difficulty, then move up
  • Already scoring well: practise at SBI-level difficulty throughout

Preparing for both without burning out

Use a single daily routine for both exams, and switch only your mock tests. Take full-length timed mock tests in the style of the upcoming exam, and review the explanations for every error. Keep one error notebook and one formula sheet rather than separate ones.

In the final fortnight before each prelims, take mocks at the same time of day as your exam slot and practise the sectional order you plan to use. Then reset for the next exam with a day of light revision.

Mains and later stages: what changes

Prelims is a qualifying round in both exams, so your final position depends on mains and the final stage. Both mains papers test reasoning, quantitative aptitude or data analysis, English and general, economy or banking awareness, and include a descriptive paper on letter and essay writing. The weight of each part and the time limits differ, so read each notification carefully.

Because general and banking awareness and descriptive writing are needed in both mains exams, start them early. Fifteen to twenty minutes of news and banking awareness daily, and one essay or letter each week, will serve both exams without disturbing your prelims plan.

  • Shared mains areas: reasoning, data analysis, English, general and banking awareness
  • Descriptive writing: letter and essay in both (confirm in notification)
  • SBI final stage has included a group exercise in some cycles; IBPS uses an interview

Where to check official details

Eligibility, age limits, the number of attempts, dates and the selection process change between cycles. Read the full notification on ibps.in for IBPS PO and sbi.co.in/careers for SBI PO before applying, and keep both pages bookmarked for updates.

FAQs

Is SBI PO tougher than IBPS PO?

Many candidates find SBI PO slightly tougher, with longer puzzles and more calculation-heavy DI, but the difference varies by year. Preparing at SBI-level difficulty once your basics are strong usually makes the IBPS PO paper feel manageable.

Is the syllabus of IBPS PO and SBI PO the same?

The prelims syllabus overlaps almost completely: English, Quantitative Aptitude and Reasoning with the same core topics. Differences appear in emphasis, difficulty and the later stages. Always confirm the current syllabus and stages in each exam's official notification.

Can I prepare for IBPS PO and SBI PO together?

Yes, and most aspirants do. Follow one study routine covering the shared syllabus, and change only the style of your mock tests depending on which exam comes next. Keep a single error notebook so your weak areas improve for both exams at once.
