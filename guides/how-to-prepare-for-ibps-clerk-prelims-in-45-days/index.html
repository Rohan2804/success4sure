How to Prepare for IBPS Clerk Prelims in 45 Days

If you want to know how to prepare for IBPS Clerk prelims in a focused way, 45 days of steady work is a sensible target for most graduates. The prelims checks three things — Numerical Ability, Reasoning Ability and English Language — and the questions are moderate in level but tightly timed. That means your plan must build both basics and speed. This guide gives you a six-and-a-half-week schedule with clear weekly targets, explains approximately which topics carry the most marks, and shows when to switch from topic practice to full-length mocks. It assumes about three hours a day; stretch it if you are starting from zero.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team

IBPS Clerk prelims pattern (indicative)

Our mock tests use an indicative pattern of 100 questions, 100 marks and 60 minutes, with 0.25 of a mark deducted for each wrong answer. Recent cycles have used three sections with separate time limits. This is indicative — confirm in the latest official notification on ibps.in, which also covers eligibility, language options and the schedule.

The level of questions in IBPS Clerk is usually a notch below IBPS PO, but the time pressure is similar. Clerk candidates who do well tend to have very fast calculation and a good eye for which puzzle or DI set to attempt first.

  • English Language: about 30 questions (approximately)
  • Numerical Ability: about 35 questions (approximately)
  • Reasoning Ability: about 35 questions (approximately)
  • Negative marking: 0.25 per wrong answer (indicative)

Topic weightage: what decides your score

Based on the syllabus and the usual shape of the paper, simplification and approximation is the single biggest scoring area in Numerical Ability, followed by number series, data interpretation and arithmetic word problems such as percentages, profit and loss, averages, ratio, interest, and time and work. In Reasoning, puzzles and seating arrangements take a large share, with inequalities, syllogism, coding-decoding, alphanumeric series, blood relations and direction sense making up the rest.

In English, reading comprehension and cloze test carry the most questions, alongside error spotting, fillers, word swap and sentence rearrangement. Because the questions are moderate, accuracy in these high-weight areas matters more than attempting rare question types.

The 45-day IBPS Clerk plan, week by week

Divide each day roughly as follows: 70 minutes Numerical Ability, 70 minutes Reasoning, 30 minutes English and 10 minutes of formula or vocabulary revision. Do 20 simplification questions every single day from Day 1; it is the fastest way to build calculation speed.

  • Week 1 (Days 1–7): tables, squares, cubes, fractions; simplification, approximation, percentages; inequalities, coding-decoding; basic grammar and RC
  • Week 2 (Days 8–14): number series, ratio, averages, ages; linear seating, direction, blood relations; error spotting and fillers
  • Week 3 (Days 15–21): profit and loss, interest, time and work, speed and distance; circular seating, floor and box puzzles; cloze test, para jumbles; first full mock on Day 21
  • Week 4 (Days 22–28): data interpretation (table, bar, pie), mixtures, quadratic equations; syllogism, order and ranking, alphanumeric series; two mocks
  • Week 5 (Days 29–35): sectional tests daily in your weakest section; mixed puzzles; RC speed work; three mocks
  • Week 6 (Days 36–42): one full mock daily with analysis, error-notebook revision
  • Days 43–45: two light mocks at your exam slot time, formula revision, rest the evening before

How to build speed for the clerk exam

Speed in IBPS Clerk mostly comes from Numerical Ability. Memorise squares up to 30, cubes up to 20, and percentage equivalents of common fractions. Practise approximation by rounding sensibly rather than calculating exact values. In number series, learn to test differences, ratios and alternating patterns in that order.

In Reasoning, draw puzzle diagrams with a consistent shorthand and practise identifying easy puzzles within the first 30 seconds. In English, avoid getting stuck on one hard question; mark it and move on, then return if time remains.

  • Daily: 20 simplification + 10 number series questions
  • Every second day: two puzzles under a five-minute timer each
  • Daily: one RC passage with a strict time limit
  • Weekly: one sectional test per section

Mock tests and analysis

From Day 21, taking full-length timed mock tests and reviewing every explanation becomes the core of your plan. After each mock, sort your mistakes into concept gaps, calculation slips and poor question choices. Concept gaps need study; slips need slower checking on easy questions; poor choices need a better attempt order.

Also note your section-wise time use. If you are spending too long on one puzzle or DI set, practise abandoning sets that do not open up quickly. That single habit often adds more marks than learning a new topic.

Keep an error notebook with three columns — topic, what went wrong, and the fix — and revise it every Sunday and again in the final week. By the end of the plan this notebook becomes your most personal and useful revision material, because it lists exactly the mistakes you are most likely to repeat under pressure.

Attempt strategy and negative marking

With 0.25 negative marking in the indicative pattern, random guessing is costly, but a well-reasoned guess after eliminating two options is usually worth taking. Track your accuracy in mocks; if it falls well below the mid-80s percentage range, slow down slightly and attempt fewer but surer questions.

Start each section with the question types you are fastest at — typically inequalities and simplification — to build momentum, then move to puzzles or DI.

Common mistakes clerk aspirants make

The most common mistake is collecting too many books and courses and finishing none. Pick one source per section and complete it. The second is avoiding mocks until you feel fully ready; that moment never comes, and late mocks leave no time to fix weaknesses.

Another frequent error is ignoring the smaller quick-scoring topics such as inequalities, coding-decoding and fillers. They take little time to learn and are among the fastest marks in the paper. Finally, do not neglect sleep in the final week; tired candidates make calculation slips that cost far more than an extra hour of study gains.

Final week checklist

In the last week, stop learning new topics. Revise formulas, your error notebook and your puzzle shorthand. Keep your sleep cycle aligned with your exam slot, and check the admit card, reporting time and ID requirements on ibps.in well in advance.

On the day, read each instruction screen carefully, and if sectional timing applies, use every minute of each section rather than finishing early and waiting.

FAQs

Is 45 days enough for IBPS Clerk prelims?

For most graduates with basic school maths and English, 45 days of three focused hours a day is enough to cover the prelims syllabus and complete 20 or more full mocks. If you are starting from zero or can study only an hour daily, give yourself 60 to 75 days instead.

Which section is easiest to score in IBPS Clerk prelims?

It depends on your background. Many candidates score fastest in Numerical Ability once simplification and number series are strong, while good readers find English quickest. Reasoning puzzles take the longest to master. Identify your strongest section through early mocks and protect it while improving the others.

Should I study for IBPS Clerk mains during prelims preparation?

Focus on prelims until the exam, since clearing it comes first. However, reading the news daily and revising banking awareness for 15 to 20 minutes a day helps for mains without affecting your prelims plan. Start dedicated mains preparation immediately after prelims.
