Customer Lost Money in Cyber Fraud: Bank Interview Answer

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “A customer tells you money was stolen from his account after a phone call. What will you do?”

Why interviewers ask this

Digital fraud complaints such as fake KYC calls, screen-sharing apps and UPI collect scams are now common at branches. Panels ask this to check whether you know the basic response and whether you treat a distressed customer with empathy. Speed matters: blocking channels quickly and reporting promptly can affect whether money is recovered and the customer's liability under RBI's customer protection framework. They also want to see that you do not blame the customer, that you document properly, and that you use the occasion to educate. This is a very practical question, and a sequenced answer scores well.

How to answer

  1. Start with immediate action: help the customer block the debit card, UPI and net banking, or freeze the affected channel, through the bank's system or helpline.
  2. Record the complaint formally with details of time, amount, transaction IDs and the caller's number, and give the customer an acknowledgement number.
  3. Guide the customer to report on the national cyber crime helpline 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in and to file a police complaint if needed.
  4. Explain, without promising outcomes, that RBI's customer protection rules limit liability in many cases when fraud is reported promptly, and that the bank will investigate.
  5. Stay calm and kind, avoid blaming, and end with simple safety tips such as never sharing OTPs or PINs and never installing apps on a caller's instructions.

Sample answers

Fresher (IBPS PO interview)

First I would calm the customer and act fast, because time matters in fraud cases. I would immediately help him block his debit card, UPI and net banking access. Then I would register the fraud complaint in the bank's system with all details: the time of the call, the caller's number, and the transaction references, and give him the complaint number. I would also help him report it on the 1930 cyber crime helpline or the national portal, since quick reporting can sometimes help freeze the money in the receiving account. I would not promise he will get the money back, but I would explain that the bank will investigate and that prompt reporting helps under RBI's rules. Finally, without lecturing, I would remind him never to share OTPs.

Candidate with bank experience (RRB Officer interview)

I have handled two such cases at my branch. The customers were usually embarrassed and scared, so the first thing I did was reassure them that they had done the right thing by coming quickly. We blocked all channels, raised the complaint and noted the beneficiary account details from the transaction. I helped them call 1930 from the branch itself. I informed the branch manager because these cases need follow-up with the nodal officer. Later, we organised a short fraud awareness session for our pension customers, because many frauds target senior citizens.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Blaming the customer for sharing the OTP before helping.
  • Promising a full refund.
  • Telling the customer to go to the police first and come back later.
  • Not knowing the 1930 helpline or the idea of reporting within a short time.

FAQs

What does zero liability mean?

Under RBI's framework on limiting customer liability in unauthorised electronic transactions, a customer may have zero or limited liability depending on whether the fault lies with the bank, a third party, or the customer, and on how quickly it is reported. Check the current RBI circular for exact timelines.

Should I mention the 1930 helpline in an interview?

Yes. It is the national cyber crime helpline run under the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre, and knowing it shows practical awareness. Also mention the cybercrime.gov.in portal for filing complaints.
