How Do You Handle Rejection in Sales? Interview Answers

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “How do you handle rejection in sales?”

Why interviewers ask this

In insurance, banking sales and fintech field roles, most calls and meetings end in a no. Attrition in these roles is high partly because people take rejection personally. Interviewers ask this to judge resilience and self-awareness. They want to hear that you understand rejection is normal, that you do not let one no affect the next meeting, and that you learn from it, for example by reviewing your pitch or timing. They also want to see that you will stay respectful with the customer and keep the door open for the future, rather than becoming pushy or giving up.

How to answer

  1. Show that you see rejection as a normal part of sales and not as a personal failure, ideally using a real example.
  2. Explain your routine after a no: a quick reflection on what happened, a note in your log, and moving on to the next call.
  3. Describe how you learn from patterns, such as noticing many rejections at the same objection and improving your pitch for it.
  4. Mention keeping the relationship: thanking the customer, asking if you can follow up later, and leaving information.
  5. Add something that keeps you motivated, like tracking activity numbers, small wins or support from a mentor.

Sample answers

Fresher (applying for insurance sales)

During college I volunteered to get sponsors for our annual fest. I approached around 40 local businesses and only six agreed. The first few rejections bothered me, but I started noting why people said no. Many said they had already spent their marketing budget, so I began approaching them earlier in the quarter, and I prepared a short one-page proposal showing what they would get. That improved my results. I learnt that a no is often about timing or the way you ask, not about you. In insurance sales I expect many rejections, and I plan to treat each one as information, thank the person politely, and move on to the next call.

Experienced (bank sales officer moving to fintech sales)

In my current role selling credit cards and personal loans, I make about [number] calls a day, and most say no. What helps me is focusing on what I control: the number of calls, the quality of my conversation and follow-ups. After a tough day I review my notes and identify one thing to change tomorrow. I also keep a simple record of customers who said 'not now', because some of them come back months later when their needs change. I never argue with a customer who refuses, since that only damages future chances.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying rejection does not affect you at all, which sounds unrealistic.
  • Describing pressuring the customer until they agree.
  • Blaming customers or the product for every rejection.
  • Giving a theoretical answer with no example.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What would you do after ten rejections in a row?
  • How do you stay motivated in a slow month?
  • Tell me about a time you turned a no into a yes.
  • How many calls do you think you can make in a day?

FAQs

What if I have no sales experience?

Use any situation where you asked for something and were refused: sponsorships, internship applications, fund-raising, selling at a college stall, or even exam failures you bounced back from. Show what you learnt and how you kept going.

Should I mention mental health or stress?

You can briefly mention healthy habits that help you reset, such as exercise or talking to a mentor. Keep the focus on resilience and learning. Interviewers want to see that you have practical ways to handle pressure.
