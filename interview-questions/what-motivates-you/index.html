What Motivates You? Interview Answers That Sound Real

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “What motivates you?”

Why interviewers ask this

The interviewer is trying to predict how you will behave on an ordinary Tuesday, not on your first day. If what drives you lines up with what the job rewards, you are more likely to stay engaged and perform. Someone motivated by solving puzzles may love credit analysis and hate cold calling; someone who enjoys people and targets may be the reverse. The question also reveals self-knowledge. Vague answers like 'growth' or 'success' do not help them place you, while a concrete motivator with an example does.

How to answer

  1. Think about times you lost track of time or felt proud of work, and identify what was common to them.
  2. Name one or two specific motivators, such as solving problems, helping customers, meeting a measurable goal or learning a new skill.
  3. Prove it with a short example, ideally with a result.
  4. Connect the motivator to the daily reality of this job, not just its best moments.

Sample answers

Fresher (applying for an operations analyst role)

I am most motivated when I can fix something that is not working properly. During my internship at [company], the team was manually matching payment records every week, and it took nearly a full day. I built a simple Excel template with lookup formulas that cut it to about two hours. Nobody had asked me to do it, but I enjoyed seeing the time saved. An operations role has a lot of processes like that, and I would enjoy improving them as I learn.

Experienced (insurance advisor moving to a bancassurance role)

Two things keep me going. The first is a clear target. I like knowing what I need to achieve each month and tracking my progress every week. The second is the moment a claim is settled for a customer I advised. Last year a family I had sold a health policy to had a hospitalisation, and helping them through the cashless process made me feel the work matters. In a bancassurance role I would have both: targets and a large base of customers who need proper advice.

Fresher (MBA, applying for a business analyst role)

I am motivated by learning something new and then using it quickly. In my second year I taught myself basic SQL through free online material because our live project needed it, and within three weeks I was pulling the data our team used for the final presentation. I enjoy that cycle of being a beginner, getting stuck, figuring it out and then being useful. A business analyst role keeps throwing new problems and tools at you, which suits me.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying money is your only motivation, which suggests you will leave for a small hike.
  • Choosing a motivator that the job cannot offer, such as variety for a highly repetitive role.
  • Giving one-word answers like 'success' without explanation.
  • Describing a motivator with no example to support it.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What demotivates you?
  • How do you stay motivated during routine work?
  • Tell me about a time you were not motivated.

FAQs

Is it wrong to say money motivates me?

Money is a normal motivator, especially in sales roles with incentives, and honesty is fine. But pair it with something about the work itself, such as hitting targets or building client relationships. A candidate who mentions only money seems likely to switch jobs for a slightly higher offer.

How is this different from 'Why do you want this job?'

Motivation is about what drives you in general, across jobs and situations. 'Why this job' is specific to the role and company. Your answers should support each other: what motivates you should be something this job provides, which is why the example you choose matters.
