Disagreed With Your Manager? How to Answer in Interview

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager.”

Why interviewers ask this

Interviewers want employees who think for themselves but also respect hierarchy, which matters a lot in Indian organisations, especially banks. The question checks whether you can raise concerns with facts and in private, listen to your manager's reasoning, and commit to the final decision even if it goes against you. Candidates who never disagree may be seen as passive; those who describe arguing in front of others or going over their manager's head raise a red flag. The ideal story shows respectful challenge and a professional outcome.

How to answer

  1. Choose a work-related disagreement about method, priorities or a decision, not personality.
  2. Explain how you raised it: privately, at the right time, with data or examples.
  3. Show that you listened to your manager's reasons and were open to being wrong.
  4. Describe the outcome, including if the manager's decision stood, and how you supported it.

Sample answers

Experienced (sales process change)

My manager wanted us to call all existing customers to pitch a new credit card in one week. I felt that calling customers who had recently complained would hurt their relationship with the bank. I raised it with him privately and showed a list of about forty customers with open complaints. He agreed to remove them from the campaign until their issues were resolved, though he kept the one-week timeline for the rest. I made sure my calls were completed on time. We got a good response, and we avoided irritating unhappy customers.

Experienced (decision stood)

In my previous job, my manager decided to move our monthly reconciliation to a new template that I thought was more complicated. I explained my concern with an example of where errors might creep in. He listened, but explained that the new format was needed for audit consistency across branches. I accepted that and focused on making it work. I created a short guide for my team, and within two months it became routine. I learnt that managers often have context I do not see.

Fresher (internship)

During my internship, my supervisor asked me to use last year's data for a market estimate. I felt more recent data from a public source would be better, so I showed her both. She agreed to use the newer data and asked me to note the source clearly.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Describing an argument in front of others or a dramatic confrontation.
  • Going over your manager's head without first discussing it with them.
  • Telling a story where you sulked or refused to comply.
  • Choosing a story where you were simply right and your manager looked foolish.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What would you do if your manager asked you to do something unethical?
  • How do you raise concerns with seniors?
  • What if you still disagreed after the decision?

FAQs

Is it risky to say I disagreed with a manager?

Not if the story shows respect, facts and acceptance of the final decision. Interviewers want employees who raise valid concerns. The risk is in the tone: avoid any hint of contempt for the manager, and show how you supported the outcome.

What if I have never disagreed with a manager?

Think more broadly: a professor, a project guide, a senior in a college committee or an internship supervisor. If you truly have none, say you would raise concerns privately with facts and then describe what you would do, but a real example is always stronger.
