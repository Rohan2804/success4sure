What Are Your Strengths? Best Answers With Examples

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “What are your strengths?”

Why interviewers ask this

Interviewers ask this to see whether your idea of yourself matches the evidence in your resume, and whether the strengths you choose are useful for the job. Almost everyone says they are hardworking or a team player, so the panel listens for something more specific: a strength with a story behind it. They are also judging honesty. If you claim excellent communication but speak in a confused way, the gap is obvious. A good answer names one or two relevant strengths and proves them with an example in under a minute.

How to answer

  1. Choose two strengths at most, ideally ones the job description asks for, such as analytical skill for credit roles or persuasion for sales.
  2. Describe each with a specific behaviour, not a label. 'I double-check every figure before sending a report' is stronger than 'I am detail-oriented'.
  3. Support it with one short example from college, an internship or work, including what happened as a result.
  4. Link the strength to the role in a single sentence so the interviewer does not have to make the connection.

Sample answers

Fresher (B.Tech, applying for a credit analyst role)

One of my strengths is that I am comfortable working with numbers and finding patterns in them. In my final-year project on [topic], I cleaned and analysed a dataset of [size] records in Python and Excel, and spotted an error in the source data that changed our conclusion. The second is persistence. When I prepared for [exam or certification], I made a weekly plan and stuck to it for four months while attending college. For a credit analyst role, I think both help: you need to read financial statements carefully and keep going through a lot of files without losing attention.

Experienced (4 years in branch banking)

My biggest strength is building trust with customers over time. At [bank], many of the customers in my portfolio were senior citizens who were wary of new products. I took time to explain things in Marathi, followed up after every service request and never pushed something unsuitable. Over the years a good share of my new business came from their referrals. A second strength is that I handle audits calmly because I keep my documentation updated every day instead of rushing before inspections. Both are useful in the relationship manager role you are hiring for.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Naming five or six strengths in a list, which makes none of them believable.
  • Choosing strengths that do not matter for the role, like singing for a back-office job.
  • Giving no example, or an example that does not actually show the strength.
  • Sounding boastful, for example 'I never make mistakes'.

Likely follow-up questions

  • Can you give another example of that strength?
  • Which strength would your friends say you have?
  • How has that strength ever worked against you?

FAQs

Should my strengths and weaknesses be connected?

They can be, and it often sounds honest. For example, being very thorough can make you slow when deadlines are tight. Just make sure the weakness is a real one you are working on, not a strength in disguise, and that you explain the steps you are taking to manage it.

What if I cannot think of an example?

Look at your last two or three years: projects, internships, exam preparation, family responsibilities, part-time work or college events. Write down moments where you solved a problem or got praise. Most people have examples; they just have not noticed them. Pick the one where you can explain what you did step by step.
