Why Do You Want to Join RBI? Grade B and Assistant Answers

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Why do you want to join the Reserve Bank of India?”

Why interviewers ask this

RBI is a regulator and central bank, not a commercial bank, and its work is very different from branch banking. Panels for RBI Grade B and Assistant interviews want to see that you know this difference: monetary policy, regulation and supervision of banks and NBFCs, currency management, payment systems, foreign exchange management and the government's banker role. They also look for genuine interest in policy or regulatory work, analytical ability and integrity, since RBI officers handle sensitive information. Candidates who talk only about prestige, perks or 'serving the nation' in general terms make a weak impression.

How to answer

  1. Connect your interest to a specific function of RBI, such as monetary policy, banking supervision, payment systems or financial inclusion, and say what drew you to it.
  2. Show awareness of your background's fit: economics, finance, law or statistics skills that are useful in RBI's departments.
  3. Mention the public impact: RBI's decisions affect inflation, bank safety and depositors across the country.
  4. Acknowledge the responsibility, such as confidentiality and integrity, since officers regularly deal with confidential and market-sensitive information.
  5. Close with the kind of work you hope to do in the first few years, accepting that postings and departments may be anywhere in the country.

Sample answers

Economics postgraduate (RBI Grade B interview)

My interest in RBI comes from my master's dissertation on [topic, for example monetary transmission to lending rates]. While working on it I read the Monetary Policy Reports and the Financial Stability Reports regularly, and I realised I want to work where this analysis actually feeds into decisions. RBI also does much more than monetary policy: it supervises banks and NBFCs, runs payment systems and manages currency. Each of these affects ordinary people, whether it is a depositor's safety or the cost of a home loan. I understand that the job requires discretion and integrity because of the sensitive information involved. In my first few years I would like to work in a department like [DEPR, supervision or regulation] and build a strong foundation.

Graduate with bank experience (RBI Assistant interview)

I have worked in a bank branch for [number] years, so I have seen RBI's circulars from the receiving end: KYC rules, customer service guidelines and the Integrated Ombudsman Scheme. I became interested in the side that frames and supervises these rules. As an Assistant I know the work is mostly administrative support, record keeping and processing in departments like currency management or banking operations, and I am comfortable with that. I want to work in an institution whose decisions protect depositors across the country, and I would also like to grow through internal exams over time.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Treating RBI like a commercial bank and talking about serving customers at counters.
  • Talking mainly about salary, perks or prestige.
  • Being unable to name the Governor, the MPC or basic RBI functions.
  • Claiming expertise in policy you cannot back up in follow-ups.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What are the main functions of RBI?
  • What is the difference between RBI's regulation and supervision roles?
  • What recent RBI policy measure did you find interesting?
  • Why not SEBI or NABARD?

FAQs

How much current affairs should I prepare for an RBI interview?

Read the latest monetary policy statement, the Governor's statement and summaries of the Financial Stability Report and Annual Report. Know major recent circulars affecting banks and NBFCs. Depth on a few topics is better than headlines on many.

Is it okay to say I also applied to SEBI or NABARD?

Yes, many candidates appear for all three. If asked, say so honestly, but explain why RBI's specific role interests you most. Have a clear, consistent reason linked to your background so it does not sound like RBI is just one option among many.
