Why a Public Sector Bank and Not a Private Bank?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Why do you want to work in a public sector bank and not a private bank?”

Why interviewers ask this

This is a test of maturity and motivation. Many candidates answer by criticising private banks for pressure and long hours, which makes the panel wonder whether you are avoiding hard work. Public sector banks have targets too, along with heavy footfall, rural branches and government scheme work. The panel wants to hear that you value the reach and social role of a public sector bank, understand the realities of the job, and can speak respectfully about competitors. They also want to see if you have thought about long-term career growth through internal promotion exams and rotation across functions.

How to answer

  1. Begin with a positive reason for choosing a public sector bank, such as its reach in rural and semi-urban areas or its role in implementing government schemes like Jan Dhan or Mudra.
  2. Mention the breadth of exposure: credit, agriculture lending, recovery, forex and administration through transfers and rotations.
  3. Acknowledge that private banks are strong in technology and customer service, and say you respect them, so you do not sound bitter.
  4. Admit that public sector banks also have targets and pressure, showing you are not expecting an easy job.
  5. Finish with how this choice fits your long-term plan, such as growing through promotion exams into a branch head or specialist role.

Sample answers

Fresher (B.Sc Agriculture, applying for IBPS PO)

I studied agriculture, and during my rural work experience programme I saw that in many villages the only formal bank nearby was a public sector branch or a regional rural bank. Farmers there depended on that branch for crop loans and subsidy payments. I want to work where that impact is direct. Private banks do excellent work, especially in technology and service speed, but their focus is often on urban and higher-value customers. In a public sector bank I can use my agriculture background in priority sector lending and Kisan Credit Card appraisal. I know there are targets and long days here too, and I am ready for that. Over time I would like to grow through the promotion process into a branch manager role in a rural or semi-urban area.

Candidate with private bank experience (sales officer, applying for SBI PO)

I have spent [number] years in a private bank selling liabilities products, so I know the private sector model well and I have learnt a lot there about customer service and targets. What I missed was depth. My role was limited to one product line. In a public sector bank a probationary officer rotates across operations, credit, and sometimes specialised roles, and serves a much wider range of customers, from pensioners to MSMEs to farmers. I am also drawn to the scale at which public sector banks deliver government programmes. I am not looking for less pressure; I am looking for broader responsibility and a longer career path.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Calling private banks exploitative or saying they 'fire people anytime'.
  • Implying public sector jobs are relaxed or that you expect fixed hours always.
  • Talking only about pay scale, pension or perks.
  • Giving a generic social service answer with no link to your own background.

Likely follow-up questions

  • Name a few government schemes delivered through public sector banks.
  • What do private banks do better than public sector banks?
  • How would you handle sales targets for insurance or mutual funds at a branch?
  • What do you know about our bank's recent performance?

FAQs

Can I mention pension or benefits at all?

You can mention long-term stability briefly, but avoid making benefits the centre of your answer. Pension rules for new joiners also differ from what many candidates assume, so focus on the work, the reach of the bank and career growth instead.

What if I have already applied to private banks too?

That is normal and you do not need to hide it. If asked, say you applied widely but your preference is the public sector for the reasons you gave. Being honest and consistent matters more than pretending you applied nowhere else.
