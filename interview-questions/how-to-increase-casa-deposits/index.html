How Would You Increase CASA Deposits? Bank PO Answer

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “How would you increase CASA deposits at your branch?”

Why interviewers ask this

CASA, the share of current and savings account deposits, is one of the most watched numbers for any bank because these deposits cost much less than fixed deposits. Branch managers carry CASA targets, and PO interview panels increasingly ask candidates how they would help. The question checks whether you understand why CASA matters for a bank's net interest margin, and whether you can think like a business person: identifying target customers, using existing relationships, and offering service rather than just pushing accounts. Practical, specific ideas score much better than generic ones like 'advertise more'.

How to answer

  1. Define CASA briefly and explain why banks value it: low-cost funds improve the margin between lending and borrowing rates.
  2. Identify target segments near your branch, such as shopkeepers for current accounts, salaried employees of local offices, schools, hospitals and government departments.
  3. Suggest practical actions: salary account tie-ups, QR code and POS for merchants, payroll for local businesses, and activating existing low-balance accounts.
  4. Mention service as a driver: quick account opening, good digital banking, a responsive branch, and keeping funds of loan customers routed through the branch.
  5. Show you understand ethics: no forced or unsuitable accounts, and accounts that are actually used rather than opened just to meet numbers.

Sample answers

Fresher (SBI PO interview)

CASA means current account and savings account deposits. They are important because the bank pays little or no interest on them compared to fixed deposits, so a higher CASA ratio improves profitability. At a branch level, I would first map the area. If there are many shops, I would approach them for current accounts with QR codes and quick settlement, because merchants care about easy collections. If there are schools, hospitals or factories, I would try for salary account tie-ups, since one tie-up can bring hundreds of savings accounts. I would also look at our own loan customers and request them to route business through current accounts with us. And I would follow up on dormant accounts, because reactivating them is easier than opening new ones.

Candidate with private bank sales experience (IBPS PO interview)

In my earlier job I had a monthly CASA target, so I learnt that quality matters more than count. Opening twenty zero-balance accounts that are never used does not help. I focused on customers with real cash flow: traders, doctors, small manufacturers. I offered them something useful, like a dedicated contact person, quick cheque book delivery and a working digital banking setup. Salary tie-ups with local employers worked very well. In a public sector branch I would add government departments and institutions in the area, and also ensure that subsidy and pension customers have a good experience so they keep balances with us.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Not knowing what CASA stands for or why it is cheaper for the bank.
  • Suggesting only advertisements or social media campaigns.
  • Proposing to open accounts without customer need just to meet numbers.
  • Ignoring existing customers and focusing only on new ones.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What is the CASA ratio?
  • What is net interest margin?
  • Why are banks finding it harder to raise CASA today?
  • How would you deal with a CASA target you think is unrealistic?

FAQs

Should I quote a bank's CASA ratio?

Only if you have checked the bank's latest results. CASA ratios change every quarter. It is enough to say that banks track it closely and that it has been under pressure when customers shift to higher-yielding term deposits or other investments.

Is the CASA question only for PO interviews?

It is most common in PO, marketing officer and branch-level interviews, but clerks may also be asked how they would help the branch grow business. A short, practical answer about salary accounts and merchant accounts works for any level.
