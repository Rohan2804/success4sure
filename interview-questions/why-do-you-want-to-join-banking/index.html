Why Do You Want to Join Banking? Interview Answers

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Why do you want to join the banking sector?”

Why interviewers ask this

Almost every IBPS, SBI and RRB interview panel asks this early, often right after your introduction. The panel has seen hundreds of candidates who say 'job security' and stop there. They want to know whether you understand what bankers actually do every day: opening accounts, handling cash and complaints, appraising loans, chasing recovery and meeting deposit targets. They are also checking whether you will stay for the long haul, accept transfers and postings, and represent the bank respectfully in front of customers. A thoughtful answer signals that you chose banking on purpose and have some idea of the work, not just the exam.

How to answer

  1. Start with one genuine reason that connects to your background, such as a commerce degree, a family member's small business loan, or an internship at a branch or cooperative bank.
  2. Show you understand the role: mention one or two daily tasks of a PO or clerk, such as customer service, credit appraisal, or supporting government schemes at the branch.
  3. Link banking to something bigger than yourself, for example financial inclusion, credit for small businesses, or helping first-time account holders use digital payments.
  4. Mention stability and growth honestly but briefly; panels accept that security matters, they just do not want it to be your only reason.
  5. Close with what you hope to contribute in the first few years, keeping it realistic and specific to the post you applied for.

Sample answers

Fresher (B.Com graduate, applying for IBPS PO)

During my B.Com I studied banking and financial services as an elective, and what stayed with me was how much a small loan can change a family's situation. My uncle runs a small [type of shop] and the working capital loan he got from a public sector bank helped him survive a bad season. I want to be on the other side of that table. I understand that a PO starts with branch work like account opening, cash, customer queries and loan processing, and I am ready for that. I also like that banking offers a clear growth path through promotions and exposure to different areas like credit, forex and recovery. In the first few years I want to learn branch operations thoroughly and become someone customers trust.

Engineering graduate (applying for SBI PO)

I am an engineer, so this may sound like a switch, but my final-year project was on [project topic involving data or payments], and that made me curious about how money actually moves in the economy. Banking today is a mix of people skills and technology: UPI, mobile banking, credit scoring. I enjoy problem solving and I enjoy talking to people, and a branch is one of the few places where you do both every day. I also want a career where my work has a visible impact, whether it is helping a farmer understand a Kisan Credit Card or helping a small trader go digital. I am prepared to start with core branch work and learn from the ground up.

Working professional (two years in a private company, applying for IBPS Clerk)

I have worked for two years in [current role] where I handled client billing and payments, which meant dealing with bank staff almost every week. I realised I enjoyed the finance side of my job much more than the rest. I want a role where serving customers and handling money accurately is the core of the work, not a side task. A clerk's job at the counter, with account opening, deposits, passbook updates and helping customers with digital services, suits my strengths of accuracy and patience. I also see a clear path to grow through internal promotions.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying only 'job security' or 'government job' with nothing about the work itself.
  • Giving a memorised speech about the economy that has nothing to do with you.
  • Claiming you love banking but being unable to name basic products like a savings account, fixed deposit or Kisan Credit Card.
  • Hinting that you are only using the bank job as a stepping stone to civil services or MBA.
  • Overselling, such as promising to 'transform the bank' in your first year.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What does a PO do on a typical day at a branch?
  • If you had a private bank offer with a higher salary, would you take it?
  • What will you do if you are posted far from your home state?
  • Which government schemes are run through bank branches?

FAQs

Is it wrong to say job security is a reason?

No. Panels know stability matters, especially for public sector jobs. The problem is when it is the only reason. Mention it once, briefly, and spend most of your answer on why the work itself interests you and what you can contribute.

How long should this answer be?

Aim for about 45 to 75 seconds. That is enough for one personal reason, a line about the role, and a closing line about your goals. If the panel wants more detail they will ask follow-up questions.

More Banking jobs questions

  • Why do you want to work in a public sector bank and not a private bank?
  • What is an NPA? Explain it in your own words.
  • What is the repo rate and how does it affect a bank's customers?
  • What is inflation and how does RBI control it?
  • A customer is shouting at your counter because his work is delayed. What will you do?
  • Are you ready to work in a rural branch or a remote area?
  • What is financial inclusion and why is it important for India?
  • Will digital banking make bank branches irrelevant?
