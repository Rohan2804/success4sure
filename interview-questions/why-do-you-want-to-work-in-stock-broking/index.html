Why Stock Broking? Interview Answer for Broking Firms

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Why do you want to work in a stock broking firm?”

Why interviewers ask this

Broking firms hire dealers, relationship managers, back-office staff, risk and compliance staff and customer support. Interviewers ask this to see if your interest is genuine and sustainable, since broking jobs involve market hours, pressure, compliance-heavy processes and targets. They also want to filter out candidates who are mainly attracted by personal trading or 'getting rich from the market'. A good answer shows that you understand how a broker serves clients, the importance of risk management and SEBI rules, and how your role contributes, while showing real interest in markets.

How to answer

  1. Explain your interest in markets with something concrete, such as following them since college, a NISM certification, or a project on market microstructure.
  2. Show understanding of the broker's role: execution, margins, risk management, client onboarding, and investor protection under SEBI and exchange rules.
  3. Link to the specific role you applied for and the skills you bring, such as speed and accuracy for dealing or patience for client support.
  4. Acknowledge the realities of the job: fast pace, fixed market hours, strict compliance discipline and handling upset clients after losses.
  5. Avoid making personal trading the centrepiece of your answer, especially stories about options trading profits or quick gains.

Sample answers

Fresher (B.Com with NISM VIII, applying for dealer role)

I have followed the markets since my second year of B.Com, and I cleared NISM Series VIII to understand derivatives properly. What interests me about broking is the speed and accuracy it demands. A dealer has to place orders correctly within seconds, understand margins and client limits, and stay calm when markets move sharply. I also understand that a broker's job is not to tell clients what to buy, but to execute properly and protect them through risk controls. I know the work is intense during market hours and that compliance rules are strict, and I am comfortable with that kind of structure.

Career switcher (from BPO customer service to broking support)

I have spent two years in customer service at [company], and I want to move into financial services. Broking interests me because I already invest through a demat account and I know how stressful it is for clients when an order fails or funds are blocked. In a support role I could use my communication skills while learning the market side, like margin rules and settlement cycles. I have started preparing for NISM Series VII on securities operations and risk management to build that knowledge.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying you want to work there to learn trading tricks for yourself.
  • Boasting about personal F&O profits.
  • Not knowing basic terms like margin, settlement or square-off.
  • Ignoring compliance and risk management in your answer.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What is the current settlement cycle in Indian equity markets?
  • What is a margin call?
  • Do you trade personally? How?
  • How would you handle a client who lost money and blames you?

FAQs

Should I mention that I trade personally?

If asked, answer honestly and briefly. Explain that you invest or trade within a small, disciplined budget and that you understand the risks. Avoid presenting large derivatives profits as proof of skill, and know that firms have rules on employee trading.

Which NISM certifications help for broking roles?

Series VII on securities operations and risk management and Series VIII on equity derivatives are commonly relevant. Requirements differ by role and firm, so check the job description and current SEBI and NISM requirements.
