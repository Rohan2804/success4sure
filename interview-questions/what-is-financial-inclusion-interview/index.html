What Is Financial Inclusion? Bank Interview Answer

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “What is financial inclusion and why is it important for India?”

Why interviewers ask this

Financial inclusion is central to the mandate of public sector banks, RRBs, NABARD and RBI, so it comes up often in their interviews. The weak answer is 'opening bank accounts for the poor'. The panel wants to see that you understand inclusion as access plus actual usage of affordable services: savings, credit, insurance, pension and payments. They also want to know whether you can name the main programmes, such as PMJDY, PMJJBY, PMSBY, APY and business correspondents, and think about the remaining gaps, like dormant accounts, low credit access for small farmers, and digital fraud risk for first-time users.

How to answer

  1. Define it as ensuring that vulnerable and low-income groups have access to affordable, appropriate financial services and actually use them.
  2. List the building blocks: a basic account, small credit, micro-insurance, pension, and safe digital payments, with one scheme example for each.
  3. Explain why it matters: it brings savings into the formal system, reduces dependence on moneylenders, and lets subsidies reach people directly through DBT.
  4. Point out the challenge that remains, such as zero-balance or dormant accounts, low financial literacy, or women's limited access to credit.
  5. Say what you would do as a branch officer, such as literacy camps, working with business correspondents and SHGs, and following up on dormant accounts.

Sample answers

Fresher (IBPS PO interview)

Financial inclusion means making sure that people who are usually left out, like small farmers, daily wage workers and women in rural areas, can access and use basic financial services at a fair cost. It starts with a bank account, which PMJDY has helped with on a huge scale, but it does not stop there. People also need small credit through schemes like Mudra or SHG loans, cheap insurance like PMJJBY and PMSBY, and pension through Atal Pension Yojana. It matters because when people save in a bank instead of at home, and borrow from a bank instead of a moneylender, their financial security improves. The challenge now is usage: many accounts are opened but used only for receiving subsidies. As an officer, I would focus on literacy camps and making customers comfortable with digital payments safely.

Postgraduate (NABARD Grade A interview)

I see financial inclusion as having three layers: access, usage and quality. Access has improved dramatically through PMJDY, business correspondents and India Stack, including Aadhaar-based payments and UPI. Usage and quality are the harder parts. Small and marginal farmers still depend on informal credit in many regions, and women entrepreneurs often lack collateral. SHG-bank linkage, which NABARD pioneered, has been one of the most effective models because it uses group guarantees. I would also highlight financial literacy and grievance redress, because new users are most vulnerable to fraud. Inclusion only works if people trust the system.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Defining it only as opening bank accounts.
  • Listing schemes mechanically without saying what each does.
  • Quoting account numbers or percentages you have not verified.
  • Ignoring the role of business correspondents and digital channels.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What is a business correspondent?
  • What are the features of a PMJDY account?
  • What is the Financial Inclusion Index published by RBI?
  • How would you activate dormant Jan Dhan accounts at your branch?

FAQs

Which schemes should I definitely know?

Know the basics of PMJDY, PMJJBY, PMSBY, Atal Pension Yojana, Mudra loans, Stand-Up India, SHG-bank linkage and Kisan Credit Card. You do not need every limit by heart, but know who each scheme is for and the core benefit. Check current premiums and limits on official sites.

Is digital payment part of financial inclusion?

Yes. UPI, Aadhaar-enabled payment systems and RuPay cards make it easier for people to transact without visiting a branch. But digital inclusion also needs literacy and fraud awareness, so mention both the opportunity and the risk in your answer.
