Why Are You Changing Careers? Answers for Career Switchers

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Why do you want to change your career?”

Why interviewers ask this

Career switchers raise a natural doubt: will you leave this field too once the novelty wears off? The interviewer wants a story that makes the switch look deliberate rather than random. They are checking what pulled you towards the new field (not just what pushed you out of the old one), what concrete steps you have taken, and which of your existing skills carry over. An answer that shows research, preparation and transferable strengths turns a risk into a fair bet.

How to answer

  1. Start with the 'pull': what attracted you to the new field, ideally with a moment or experience that triggered it.
  2. Show proof of commitment, such as a certification, course, part-time work, or self-study with something to show for it.
  3. Name two or three transferable skills from your old career and how they apply here.
  4. Briefly acknowledge what you need to learn, and show you are ready to start at the appropriate level.

Sample answers

IT professional moving into banking operations

I spent three years in IT support for a bank's core banking system at [company]. The more tickets I solved, the more interested I became in the banking processes behind them, such as why a loan account would go into a particular status or how clearing worked. I started preparing for JAIIB on my own and cleared [papers]. I now want to work on the business side directly. My technical background will help with systems and data, and I am ready to learn operations from the ground up.

School teacher moving into insurance sales

I taught commerce at a CBSE school for four years. I enjoyed explaining difficult ideas simply, and many parents started asking me about savings and insurance after school meetings. That made me realise I wanted to help adults with financial decisions. I cleared the IRDAI IC-38 exam and worked part-time with an agency during vacations. Teaching gave me patience, clear communication and the ability to handle many personalities, which I think is useful in advisory sales.

Mechanical engineer moving into equity research

I worked for two years as a production engineer at an auto component plant. Around the time our company was preparing for a capacity expansion, I started reading annual reports of listed auto companies to understand how the industry was growing. I found that I enjoyed that analysis more than my shop-floor work. I have completed [certification] and built valuation models for two listed companies on my own. My plant experience gives me a practical understanding of manufacturing costs that pure finance graduates may not have.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Focusing only on what you disliked about your previous career.
  • Having no proof of preparation, which makes the switch look impulsive.
  • Expecting your old seniority or salary in a field where you are new.
  • Explaining the switch with vague phrases like 'I wanted a change'.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What if you find this field boring too?
  • Are you okay starting at a junior level?
  • What have you done to learn about this industry?

FAQs

Will I have to take a salary cut when switching careers?

Sometimes, especially if the new role values experience you do not yet have in that field. Some transferable skills may help you negotiate a fair level. Research typical salaries for the role and decide your minimum in advance. Treat a short-term dip as an investment only if you are sure about the switch.

Do certifications really help when switching into finance?

They help show commitment and give you a base of knowledge, such as NISM certifications for securities markets, JAIIB-type study for banking or IC-38 for insurance. They do not replace experience, but they give interviewers something concrete to discuss and reduce the doubt about whether you understand the new field.
