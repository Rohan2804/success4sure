How to Explain Backlogs in an Interview Honestly

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Why did you have backlogs in college?”

Why interviewers ask this

Backlogs, also called arrears or ATKT, are common in Indian universities, especially in engineering. Some companies have eligibility rules about active backlogs at the time of joining, so HR may first need to confirm the current status. Beyond the rule, the interviewer wants to know whether the backlog reflects a deeper problem with effort or understanding, and how you responded to the setback. Owning the mistake, showing that you cleared it and pointing to steady performance afterwards turns a weakness into evidence of resilience.

How to answer

  1. Confirm the facts first: how many backlogs, in which subjects, and whether all are cleared now.
  2. Give an honest reason in one or two sentences, such as adjusting to college, weak basics in a subject, or illness.
  3. Explain what you changed, such as a new study approach or extra help, and show the improvement in later semesters.
  4. Make sure you know the company's backlog policy, and do not hide an active backlog.

Sample answers

Engineering fresher (one backlog in Mathematics, now cleared)

I had one backlog in Engineering Mathematics in my second semester. Honestly, I did not take the subject seriously enough while I was adjusting to hostel life and a new city, and my basics from Class 12 were weak. I cleared it in the next attempt by solving previous chapters step by step with a senior's help. After that, I did not have any backlog, and my average in the last four semesters was [score]. It taught me not to underestimate a subject just because it seems difficult or boring.

B.Com fresher (two backlogs during a family problem)

In my second year I had two backlogs, in Cost Accounting and Business Law, during a period when my mother was unwell and I was handling a lot at home. I cleared both in the supplementary exams the same year. I also scored [marks] in Cost Accounting the second time, which shows I understood the subject once I could focus. I have no pending backlogs now.

MBA fresher (backlog in Statistics)

I had a backlog in Business Statistics in my first semester. I came from an arts background and had not studied statistics before. I joined a peer study group and practised extra problems on weekends, and I cleared it in the re-exam. I liked the subject so much later that I chose a project on [topic] that used regression analysis.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Lying about backlogs, which will be caught at document verification.
  • Blaming professors, the university or the paper setter.
  • Not knowing exactly how many backlogs you had or when you cleared them.
  • Getting defensive or nervous when the topic comes up.

Likely follow-up questions

  • How did you prepare differently the second time?
  • Do you have any active backlogs now?
  • Why should we trust your performance in our training exams?

FAQs

Can I get a job with backlogs in my record?

Many employers accept candidates with cleared backlogs, though some campus recruiters set limits on the number or on active backlogs. Read each company's eligibility criteria carefully. Strong later grades, certifications and a well-explained story improve your chances in the interview.

Should I bring up backlogs before the interviewer asks?

Usually not in the opening. But if the topic arises, or if your marksheet will clearly show it, address it calmly and directly. Do not wait for the interviewer to discover it during verification, because that creates a trust problem much bigger than the backlog itself.
