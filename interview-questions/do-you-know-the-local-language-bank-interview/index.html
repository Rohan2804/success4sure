Do You Know the Local Language? Bank Interview Answer

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Can you speak the local language of the state you have applied for?”

Why interviewers ask this

In IBPS and SBI recruitment you apply for a specific state, and banks often require proficiency in that state's official language, sometimes tested separately. RRBs depend even more on local language because rural customers may speak nothing else. The panel asks this to confirm you can serve customers, fill forms, explain products and build trust. They may switch to the language mid-interview to test you. They are also checking honesty: claiming fluency you do not have is quickly exposed. A sincere answer with evidence of your level and a plan to improve is what they want.

How to answer

  1. State your level honestly: native speaker, comfortable in conversation, can read and write, or currently learning.
  2. Back it up with evidence, such as schooling in that language, years lived in the state, or having passed it as a subject in Class 10.
  3. If the panel switches to the language, respond in it, even if simply, rather than refusing or switching back immediately.
  4. If you are still learning, describe a specific plan, like classes, practising with local friends, or reading local newspapers.
  5. Connect it to customer service, explaining why local language matters at a branch, especially for elderly and rural customers.

Sample answers

Native speaker (RRB Officer interview)

Yes, [language] is my mother tongue. I studied in [language] medium till Class 10, so I can read, write and speak it fluently. I think this is very important in a rural branch, because many customers, especially older farmers and women in SHGs, are more comfortable explaining their problems in their own language. They also trust officers more when they are spoken to in it. I can help with filling forms, explaining loan terms and conducting financial literacy camps in [language].

Candidate from another state (IBPS PO interview)

I am from [home state], but I have lived in [applied state] for the last four years for my graduation, so I can speak and understand [language] for everyday conversation. My reading is decent, and I passed the language as part of [course or exam, if applicable]. Writing is my weaker area, so I have started practising with local newspapers and with my friends here. I know that in a branch, especially outside the city, customers will expect me to speak their language, and I want to be fully comfortable within my first few months.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Claiming full fluency and then failing to answer a simple question in the language.
  • Saying English and Hindi are enough for banking everywhere.
  • Getting flustered when the panel switches language.
  • Having no plan to improve if your level is basic.

Likely follow-up questions

  • Please introduce yourself in [language].
  • How will you serve a customer if you do not understand their dialect?
  • Why did you apply for this state and not your home state?
  • Explain a fixed deposit in [language].

FAQs

Is local language proficiency mandatory?

It depends on the recruitment notification. Many IBPS and SBI notifications say candidates should be proficient in the official language of the state applied for, and some include a language proficiency test. Read the specific notification for your exam.

How do I prepare if the panel may switch languages?

Practise your introduction, why banking, and simple product explanations in the local language. Ask a friend from the state to do a mock interview in that language. Even simple, correct sentences show effort and respect.
