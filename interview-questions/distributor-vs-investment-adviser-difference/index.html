Distributor vs Investment Adviser: Interview Answer

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “What is the difference between a mutual fund distributor and an investment adviser?”

Why interviewers ask this

SEBI has separated distribution from advice, and this distinction matters for anyone working in mutual fund sales, wealth management or advisory. Interviewers ask it to test regulatory understanding and to see whether you know your own role's limits. Candidates applying for distributor roles sometimes call themselves 'advisers', which can be misleading. The panel wants a clear comparison of registration, how each is paid, which plans they use, and their duty to the client, along with an awareness of conflict of interest. It also helps them see whether you are thinking about NISM X-A and X-B certifications for the future.

How to answer

  1. Explain the distributor role: registered with AMFI after clearing NISM Series V-A, earns commission from AMCs, and offers regular plans of schemes.
  2. Explain the adviser role: registered with SEBI under the Investment Advisers Regulations, charges a fee directly to the client, and generally recommends direct plans.
  3. Point out the difference in duty: advisers have a fiduciary-like obligation and must do risk profiling and suitability, while distributors follow AMFI's code of conduct.
  4. Mention the conflict of interest issue that led to the separation, which is why one entity generally cannot do both for the same client.
  5. Connect it to the role you are applying for and show you know its boundaries.

Sample answers

Fresher (MF distribution firm interview)

A mutual fund distributor, or MFD, is registered with AMFI, has cleared NISM Series V-A, and helps clients invest in regular plans. The distributor earns commission from the AMC, which is included in the expense ratio. A SEBI-registered investment adviser, or RIA, gives advice for a fee paid by the client, usually recommends direct plans, and does not earn commission from product providers. Advisers must clear NISM X-A and X-B and meet SEBI's qualification and registration requirements. The separation is meant to reduce conflict of interest. Since I am applying for a distributor role, I understand I should describe myself as a distributor, provide information honestly and not call myself an adviser.

Experienced (wealth RM aspiring to be an RIA)

The main difference is who pays and what that does to incentives. A distributor is paid by the product manufacturer, so there can be a pull toward products with higher commission. An RIA is paid by the client, so the advice should be independent of products. SEBI also requires RIAs to do risk profiling, suitability and maintain records, and it restricts them from earning distribution commission for the same client. I am currently a distributor but preparing for X-A and X-B because I would like to move to fee-based advice in a few years.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying both are the same or using 'adviser' loosely for distributors.
  • Not knowing which NISM certifications apply to each role.
  • Claiming distributors cannot give any guidance at all.
  • Getting the plan types or payment models mixed up.

FAQs

What is an ARN?

An ARN, or AMFI Registration Number, is the registration a mutual fund distributor obtains from AMFI after clearing the required NISM certification and completing KYD. It identifies the distributor on client transactions and is used for commission payments.

Are there limits on how much an RIA can charge?

SEBI has issued rules on the fee models and limits for investment advisers. These have been revised over time, so check the latest SEBI Investment Advisers Regulations and circulars before quoting any figures.
