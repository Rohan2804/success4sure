Describe Yourself in Three Words: Answers With Proof

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “How would you describe yourself in three words?”

Why interviewers ask this

This looks like a quick question, but it forces you to compress your personality into something memorable. The interviewer is checking whether you know yourself, whether the words you pick suit the role, and whether you can justify them without rambling. They also notice if your words contradict what they have already seen, for example calling yourself 'confident' while avoiding eye contact. Three well-chosen words with a one-line example each will be remembered long after a list of ten adjectives is forgotten.

How to answer

  1. Shortlist words that are true for you, then keep the three most relevant to the job. 'Methodical' suits audit, 'persuasive' suits sales.
  2. Avoid the most overused words such as hardworking, honest and dedicated unless you have a striking example.
  3. Give one sentence of proof for each word, drawn from college, internships, work or family responsibilities.
  4. Deliver it in about 45 seconds. This is meant to be crisp, so do not turn each word into a long story.

Sample answers

Fresher (B.Com, applying for an audit assistant role)

I would say methodical, curious and reliable. Methodical because I keep checklists for everything, even my exam revision, and in my internship I used one to make sure no vouchers were missed during a stock audit. Curious because I keep asking why a number looks odd until I understand it. And reliable because in three years as class representative, I never missed passing on an update or deadline to my classmates.

Experienced (field sales, two-wheeler loans)

Persistent, friendly and organised. Persistent because many of my customers say no on the first visit, and I keep following up politely until they decide. Friendly because dealers in my area call me directly when they have a customer, which only happens when people like working with you. And organised because I track every lead in a simple sheet, so nothing slips even when I visit eight or ten dealers a day.

Fresher (engineering graduate, applying for a fintech product role)

Analytical, adaptable and patient. Analytical because I like breaking a problem into data, which is what I did for my final-year project on [topic]. Adaptable because I switched from mechanical engineering coursework to learning SQL and product basics on my own in the last year. And patient because I tutored school students for two years, and explaining the same concept five different ways taught me to stay calm.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Choosing words with no link to the job you are applying for.
  • Giving the words without any examples, which makes them sound like guesses.
  • Picking words that clash, such as 'spontaneous' for a compliance role.
  • Using clichés that every other candidate uses.

Likely follow-up questions

  • Which of these three is strongest?
  • What word would your critics use?
  • How would your manager describe you?

FAQs

Can I use the same three words in every interview?

Only if they fit every role. Ideally keep a list of five or six true words with examples, and choose three depending on the job. A relationship manager role and a risk analyst role reward different traits, so the words you lead with should change.

What if the interviewer asks for one word only?

Pick the word most relevant to the job and give a single, strong example. For a sales role, something like 'persistent' with a short follow-up story works. Do not try to squeeze three words into one answer.
