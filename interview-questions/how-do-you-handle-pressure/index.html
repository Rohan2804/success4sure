How Do You Handle Pressure? Interview Answers That Work

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “How do you handle pressure and stress at work?”

Why interviewers ask this

Most finance roles involve pressure: month-end targets in sales, audit deadlines, long queues at a bank branch, market volatility or year-end closing. The interviewer wants to know whether you stay calm, keep quality up and make sensible decisions when things get busy, or whether you panic, cut corners or burn out. A believable answer describes practical methods you use, backed by an example, instead of claiming that pressure never affects you. It also helps to show you recognise the difference between healthy pressure and unhealthy stress.

How to answer

  1. Acknowledge that pressure is part of the job and that you handle it with specific habits.
  2. Name two or three practical methods, such as prioritising, breaking tasks down, early communication or short breaks.
  3. Give a concrete example where you used these methods and what the result was.
  4. Optionally mention how you recover after a busy period, which shows self-awareness.

Sample answers

Fresher (exams and internship together)

In my final semester, my internship overlapped with my university exams for two weeks. I made a timetable that blocked my mornings for study and afternoons for the internship, and I told my internship supervisor in advance about my exam dates. When I felt overwhelmed, I broke tasks into small pieces and finished the most important one first. I completed my internship report on time and cleared all my exams. I learnt that most of my stress comes from not knowing what to do first, so I now plan first.

Experienced (branch operations during year-end)

During March, our branch has heavy work because of year-end closing, tax-saving investments and audit preparation. Last year our team was also short-staffed. I made a daily list of tasks sorted by deadline and impact, and I checked with my manager every morning on priorities. I avoided last-minute rushes by finishing reconciliations a few days before the deadline. We closed without any audit observations on my areas. After such periods, I make sure to take a day off to recharge.

Experienced (sales role)

Sales pressure is constant for me, especially in the last week of the month. I handle it by building my pipeline early, tracking it every day and focusing on customers most likely to close. When I miss a target, I review what happened instead of panicking. That routine keeps me steady even in a bad month.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying 'I never feel stress', which is not believable.
  • Describing unhealthy habits like skipping sleep for days as a strength.
  • Giving no example of handling pressure.
  • Admitting you avoid pressure, for a role that clearly involves it.

Likely follow-up questions

  • Tell me about a time pressure got the better of you.
  • How do you prioritise when everything is urgent?
  • What do you do to relax?

FAQs

Is it okay to admit that pressure affects me?

Yes. Saying you feel pressure but manage it with specific habits is more honest and believable than claiming you are unaffected. Interviewers look for coping methods and judgement, not superhuman calm. Just avoid describing situations where you consistently broke down.

How should I answer if the role is known to be very high-pressure?

Acknowledge it directly and show you understand what it involves, such as targets or tight deadlines. Give examples from your own life where you sustained effort over time. You can also ask how the team manages busy periods, which shows you are thinking practically.

More Behavioural questions

  • Tell me about a time you failed.
  • Tell me about a conflict you had with a colleague.
  • Tell me about a time you led a team.
  • Tell me about a mistake you made at work and how you handled it.
  • Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager.
  • How do you handle criticism or negative feedback?
  • Tell me about a time you had to manage several deadlines at once.
  • Tell me about a time you dealt with a difficult customer.
