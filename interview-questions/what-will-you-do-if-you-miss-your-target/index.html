What If You Miss Your Sales Target? Interview Answer

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “What will you do if you miss your monthly sales target?”

Why interviewers ask this

Every salesperson misses a target at some point. Interviewers ask this to see how you react: with excuses, panic, unethical shortcuts or a calm plan. They want to see ownership, the ability to analyse what went wrong, using data like calls, meetings and conversion rates, and a concrete recovery plan. They also want to know if you will communicate early with your manager rather than hiding the shortfall until month-end. Candidates who say 'I will never miss a target' sound naive, and those who blame the market or product sound like future problems.

How to answer

  1. Accept that it can happen and take ownership instead of blaming leads, products or the market.
  2. Explain how you would analyse the gap: activity levels, conversion at each stage, average ticket size and lost deals.
  3. Describe a recovery plan, such as increasing quality meetings, re-contacting warm leads, or focusing on an underused segment.
  4. Mention involving your manager early, asking for coaching or joint calls rather than hiding the problem.
  5. State that you would not cut ethical corners, like mis-selling or pushing unsuitable products, to close the gap.

Sample answers

Fresher (credit card sales interview)

If I miss a target, I would first look at my own numbers honestly. Did I make enough calls and meetings? If my activity was fine but conversions were low, maybe my pitch or the customer segment was wrong. If activity was low, I need better planning. I would discuss this with my team leader early, not at the end of the month, and ask for feedback, maybe by having him join a few of my calls. Then I would set a clear plan for the next month, like adding [number] extra meetings a week and following up on all pending applications. I would not try to make up numbers by pushing cards to people who do not need them, because those lead to complaints and cancellations.

Experienced (insurance advisor interview)

I missed my target twice last year. Both times, when I analysed it, I found my pipeline had dried up because I had stopped prospecting while closing a few large policies. Since then I spend a fixed hour every morning on new prospects, regardless of how busy I am with closures. I also look at renewals and service, because satisfied clients bring referrals. When I see I am likely to fall short by mid-month, I tell my manager and we agree on specific actions. That has kept me within reach of target even in slow months.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Claiming you will never miss a target.
  • Blaming the product, leads or economy.
  • Saying you would push any product to anyone to make up numbers.
  • Not mentioning any analysis or plan.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What if you miss it three months in a row?
  • How do you track your pipeline?
  • What is a good conversion rate in your view?
  • How would you react if a colleague is meeting targets through mis-selling?

FAQs

Should I admit I have missed targets before?

Yes, if it is true. Interviewers trust candidates who admit past shortfalls and explain what they changed. Pair the admission with the analysis and improvement that followed, so the story ends with learning and better results.

What is a sales pipeline?

A pipeline is the list of prospects at different stages: first contact, meeting, proposal, documentation and closure. Tracking it shows where deals get stuck and whether you have enough prospects to meet future targets.
