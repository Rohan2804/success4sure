Why Is KYC Important? Bank Interview Answer and Scenario

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Why is KYC important, and what will you do if a customer refuses to give documents?”

Why interviewers ask this

KYC is the first line of defence against money laundering, fraud and benami accounts, and branch staff deal with it daily during account opening and periodic updation. Interviewers want to know if you understand why KYC exists beyond 'RBI rules', and whether you will hold the line when a customer pushes back. The scenario part tests your customer handling: refusing without being rude, explaining the reason, offering acceptable alternatives like officially valid documents or video KYC, and escalating rather than bending rules. Integrity on compliance is a key trait banks look for in new recruits.

How to answer

  1. Explain KYC as verifying a customer's identity and address and understanding the nature of their financial activity, under RBI's KYC directions and the PMLA.
  2. Give the reasons: preventing money laundering and terror financing, stopping fake or benami accounts, and protecting genuine customers from identity misuse.
  3. For the refusal scenario, say you would explain politely why the documents are mandatory and that the rule applies to everyone.
  4. Offer acceptable alternatives, such as other officially valid documents, Aadhaar-based e-KYC with consent, or a small account with limited features where permitted.
  5. State clearly that you would not open the account or process the transaction without KYC, and would involve the branch manager if the customer insists.

Sample answers

Fresher (SBI Clerk interview)

KYC means Know Your Customer. The bank verifies who the customer is and where they live, using officially valid documents like Aadhaar, PAN or a passport. It is important because without it, criminals could open accounts in fake names to move illegal money, and genuine people could have accounts opened in their name without knowing. If a customer refuses to give documents, I would calmly explain that this is an RBI requirement for every customer, not something I am asking personally, and that it actually protects his money. I would tell him which other documents are accepted. If he still refuses, I cannot open the account, and I would politely involve the branch manager so he hears it from a senior person too.

Graduate with private bank experience (IBPS PO interview)

KYC has two sides. Identity verification prevents fake and benami accounts, and customer due diligence helps the bank see whether transactions match the customer's profile, which is how suspicious transactions get flagged under anti-money laundering rules. In my earlier job I once had an existing customer who refused to submit updated documents for re-KYC because he felt the bank already knew him. I explained that periodic updation is mandatory and that his account could face restrictions otherwise. I offered to help him complete it through the bank's digital option, and he did it the same day. Being firm on the rule and flexible on the method usually works.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying you would open the account anyway if the customer is a known person.
  • Explaining KYC only as 'collecting photocopies'.
  • Being rude or accusatory, implying the customer is a criminal.
  • Not knowing basic documents or the idea of periodic re-KYC.

FAQs

What is periodic updation of KYC?

Banks must update customer KYC periodically, with frequency based on the customer's risk category, as per RBI's Master Direction on KYC. Low-risk customers have longer intervals and high-risk customers shorter. Check the current RBI direction for exact intervals rather than quoting old figures.

Is KYC part of Anti-Money Laundering rules?

Yes. KYC is the customer-identification part of the broader AML and counter-terror financing framework under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act. Banks also monitor transactions and report suspicious ones to the Financial Intelligence Unit - India.
