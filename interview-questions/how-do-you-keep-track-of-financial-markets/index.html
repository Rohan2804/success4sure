How Do You Track Financial Markets? Interview Answer

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “How do you keep yourself updated on financial markets?”

Why interviewers ask this

In finance roles the interviewer expects genuine curiosity about markets and the economy. This question checks whether you have a real habit or just claim interest. It is also a trap: whatever source or topic you mention can lead to deeper questions, such as 'What did the RBI say last week?' or 'Why did bank stocks fall yesterday?'. The panel wants to hear a specific routine, credible sources such as RBI, SEBI, AMFI, company filings and business newspapers, and evidence that you think about what you read rather than consuming social media tips.

How to answer

  1. Describe a specific routine, for example a business newspaper in the morning, a quick market close review, and weekly deeper reading.
  2. Name credible sources: RBI and SEBI press releases, AMFI monthly data, exchange announcements, company annual reports and earnings calls.
  3. Give one example of something you followed recently and what you personally concluded or learnt from it.
  4. Mention any practice that deepens learning, such as maintaining notes, tracking a few companies, or a paper portfolio.
  5. Be careful with social media and tip channels; if you mention them at all, say how you verify the information independently.

Sample answers

Fresher (MBA Finance, AMC research support interview)

I read [business newspaper] every morning and check the market close every evening, mainly index movement, sector performance and FII and DII flows. Every month I look at AMFI's data on mutual fund flows, because it shows how retail investors are behaving. I also follow RBI's monetary policy statements closely. Recently I followed [recent event] and noticed that [what you observed]. I track five companies in [sector] by reading their quarterly results and listening to earnings calls when possible. I keep a simple notebook where I write what I expected and what actually happened, which helps me learn from my mistakes.

Experienced (bank relationship manager moving to a wealth firm)

My clients ask me about markets daily, so I have a routine. I scan the main business news before 9 am and prepare two or three points I can share if clients ask. I rely on AMC factsheets, research notes from our approved sources and RBI updates rather than social media. Every weekend I spend an hour reading a longer piece, like an RBI bulletin article or a company's annual report. I avoid giving opinions on short-term market direction to clients, but I make sure I can explain what has happened and why.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Naming only YouTube, Telegram or Twitter tip channels as your source.
  • Saying 'I read the newspaper' with no specifics.
  • Mentioning a source or event you cannot discuss in follow-ups.
  • Claiming to follow everything, from crypto to commodities, without depth.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What happened in the market this week?
  • What is the current Nifty or Sensex level?
  • What did you learn from the last RBI policy?
  • Which company results did you read recently?

FAQs

Should I know the exact Nifty level?

For market-facing roles like dealing, research or wealth management, know the approximate index level and the recent trend. For operations roles, a general idea is enough. Check on the day of your interview.

Is it okay to mention finance influencers?

You can mention that you follow some educators, but make reliable primary sources the core of your answer. Also mention that you verify information independently. Regulators have raised concerns about unregistered influencers giving advice, so show awareness.
