Where Do You See Yourself in 5 Years? Sample Answers

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Where do you see yourself in five years?”

Why interviewers ask this

Hiring and training a new employee costs money and effort, so the interviewer wants to know whether your plans fit with what the organisation can offer. They are checking three things: whether you have thought about your career at all, whether your goals are realistic, and whether you are likely to leave soon for a government job, an MBA or another city. An answer that is ambitious but grounded in this role shows maturity. An answer that has nothing to do with the company makes them doubt your commitment.

How to answer

  1. Research the normal growth path for the role, such as officer to manager in a bank or analyst to senior analyst in a broking firm.
  2. Talk about skills and responsibility you want to grow into, not only a designation or salary figure.
  3. Connect your goal to this organisation, showing you see a future here.
  4. Stay flexible. Say you hope to reach the goal through performance, not that you expect a promotion on a fixed date.

Sample answers

Fresher (applying for a probationary officer role)

In five years I would like to be a confident officer who has worked across different areas of the branch, such as retail lending, operations and customer service, and has handled responsibility independently. I would also like to complete my JAIIB and CAIIB during that time, because I understand they strengthen both knowledge and career growth in banking. If my performance is good, I hope to be ready for a role like branch manager or a specialised credit position. Mainly I want to be known as someone the branch can rely on.

Experienced (3 years in mutual fund operations, applying for a research role)

Over the next five years I want to build a solid track record in equity research. In the first couple of years, I would focus on learning your process, covering one or two sectors in depth and getting comfortable with valuation models. I am also preparing for [certification such as CFA level or NISM Research Analyst]. By year five I would like to be trusted with independent coverage and to mentor newer analysts. My operations background gives me a good understanding of fund flows, and I see this role as the place to combine that with research skills.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying 'in your chair' or 'running my own business', which can sound dismissive or signal you will leave.
  • Mentioning plans that clearly conflict with the job, such as preparing for UPSC full-time.
  • Giving a vague answer like 'I just want to grow' with nothing specific.
  • Naming an unrealistic title, such as becoming a vice president in five years as a fresher.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What are you doing now to reach that goal?
  • What will you do if that promotion does not come?
  • Are you planning to do an MBA?

FAQs

What if I am genuinely unsure about my five-year plan?

That is common, especially for freshers. Focus on skills and the kind of responsibility you want rather than a precise title. You can honestly say you want to master this role first and then specialise in an area like credit or wealth management, depending on where you show the most aptitude.

Should I mention plans for higher studies?

Mention them only if they support the role, such as part-time certifications or a weekend course. Saying you plan a full-time MBA in two years tells the employer you may leave soon. If you are unsure about higher studies, it is better not to raise it unless asked directly.
