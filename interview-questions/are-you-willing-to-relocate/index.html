Are You Willing to Relocate? How to Answer Honestly

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Are you willing to relocate?”

Why interviewers ask this

Many finance jobs in India, especially in public sector banks, regional rural banks, insurance companies and NBFCs, involve postings or transfers to other towns and states. The interviewer needs to know whether you will accept a posting or resign within months. They are also checking whether you understand the job: a PO aspirant who refuses rural postings has not researched the role. An honest answer, even a conditional one, is better than a cheerful yes that you go back on after joining, which wastes the organisation's training effort.

How to answer

  1. Find out in advance whether the role involves transfers, rural postings or field travel, and how often.
  2. Decide your real position before the interview, so you do not improvise under pressure.
  3. If yes, say so clearly and add a reason, such as wanting exposure to different markets.
  4. If you have a constraint, state it calmly and offer flexibility within it, such as a preferred region or a timeline.

Sample answers

Fresher (bank PO interview)

Yes, I am fully willing to relocate. I understand that officers in public sector banks are posted across the country, including rural and semi-urban branches, and I see that as part of the job. In fact I think rural postings would teach me a lot about agricultural lending and financial inclusion, which I have only read about. I lived in a hostel for four years during graduation, so I am used to adjusting to a new place, food and language.

Experienced (with family responsibilities)

I am open to relocation within Maharashtra and Gujarat, where I can still reach my parents in Nashik within a few hours. My father had surgery last year and I need to be available if something comes up. Within that region, I am happy to move wherever the business needs me, and I am comfortable with frequent travel for client meetings. If a move outside these states becomes necessary after a couple of years, I would be willing to discuss it then.

Fresher (field sales role at an NBFC with district travel)

Yes, I am comfortable relocating and travelling. I have a two-wheeler licence and I know the districts around [town] well, since I grew up there. I understand that this role means visiting customers and dealers across several towns every week, and possibly moving to another branch later. I would actually prefer to start in a smaller location where I can learn the business closely before moving to a bigger city.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying yes to everything and then refusing the first transfer order.
  • Answering with a flat no and no explanation.
  • Listing preferred cities that are clearly unrealistic for the role, like only Mumbai for a rural lending job.
  • Bringing up relocation allowance before showing willingness.

Likely follow-up questions

  • Would you be comfortable in a village branch with limited facilities?
  • How does your family feel about you moving?
  • Can you speak the local language there?

FAQs

Can I ask about the relocation policy during the interview?

Yes, but ask after showing basic willingness, and frame it as planning, for example asking how postings are decided in the first year. Questions about allowances and accommodation are better kept for HR discussions or the offer stage, once the employer has shown interest in you.

Will a conditional answer cost me the job?

It might for roles where all-India mobility is essential. But a false yes can cost you much more later, including disciplinary trouble or resignation. Many private employers can accommodate region preferences. Be honest, keep the condition as narrow as possible, and show flexibility in other areas.
