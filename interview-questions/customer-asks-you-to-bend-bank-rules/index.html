Customer Asks You to Bend Bank Rules: How to Answer

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “A regular customer asks you to skip a procedure just this once. What will you do?”

Why interviewers ask this

Branch staff face this pressure constantly: a trusted local businessman wants a third-party cash withdrawal without proper authority, a relative asks you to backdate a deposit, or a big depositor wants a cheque cleared despite a signature mismatch. Many bank frauds start with a small favour. The panel is testing integrity, judgement and your ability to say no without losing the customer. They want to hear that you follow procedure every time, explain the reason respectfully, offer a legitimate way to get the work done, and escalate to a senior when needed. Any hint that relationships can override rules is a serious red flag.

How to answer

  1. State your principle in the first line: you would not skip the procedure, regardless of how well you know the customer.
  2. Explain how you would say it politely, making clear the rule protects the customer's own money and applies to everyone.
  3. Offer a legitimate alternative, such as a proper authority letter, the account holder visiting, a fresh cheque, or a digital transfer.
  4. Say you would inform the branch manager if the customer insists or is influential, so the decision is documented and not personal.
  5. Briefly mention why it matters: frauds, customer disputes and personal accountability for the staff member who processed it.

Sample answers

Fresher (IBPS Clerk interview)

I would not skip the procedure. Even if the customer comes daily and everyone knows him, if something goes wrong later, for example a family member disputes the withdrawal, the bank and I would be responsible. I would tell him politely, 'Sir, I know you, but the system does not. This rule protects your own account.' Then I would tell him what exactly is needed, maybe the account holder's signature or a proper authorisation letter, so he can complete it quickly. If he insists or gets upset, I would request the branch manager to speak to him. Most good customers understand when you explain respectfully.

Candidate with branch experience (PO interview)

This happened to me in my previous role at a cooperative bank. A senior trader wanted us to accept a cheque with an overwriting on the date, saying he would initial it later. I told him we could not process it as per clearing rules and offered to keep his deposit slip ready if he issued a fresh cheque. He was irritated but sent a new cheque within an hour. My manager later told me that the same customer respected the branch more after that. I believe if you bend once, you are expected to bend again, so consistency is the safest approach.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying you would do it once if the customer is very important or the manager is busy.
  • Lecturing or insulting the customer about honesty.
  • Refusing without offering any way to get the work done properly.
  • Saying you would ask a colleague to do it instead of you.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What if the branch manager asks you to do it?
  • What if the customer threatens to close his account?
  • What is the risk of processing a third-party cash withdrawal without proper authority?
  • What is staff accountability in bank frauds?

FAQs

What if my senior tells me to skip the rule?

Politely ask for the instruction in writing or ask the senior to authorise it in the system under their own login, as per the bank's delegation of powers. If the instruction is clearly against rules, decline respectfully and, if needed, escalate through the bank's whistle-blower or vigilance channel.

Will refusing hurt the branch's business?

In the short term a customer may be annoyed, but long-term relationships depend on trust. Customers also feel safer knowing their own accounts cannot be operated casually. Offering a quick, legitimate alternative usually preserves the relationship.
