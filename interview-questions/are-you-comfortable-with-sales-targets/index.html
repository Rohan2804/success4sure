Are You Comfortable With Sales Targets? Interview Answer

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Are you comfortable working with sales targets?”

Why interviewers ask this

Targets are central to sales roles in insurance, bank cross-selling, credit cards, loans and fintech. Interviewers ask this to see if you understand that, and to weed out people who will quit after the first difficult month. A simple 'yes' is not enough. They want to know how you break a target into daily activity, how you handle pressure, and whether you understand that targets must be met ethically, without mis-selling. Candidates who talk about planning, discipline and customer focus, rather than just enthusiasm, come across as dependable.

How to answer

  1. Give a clear yes and show that you understand targets are a normal, fair way to measure performance in sales.
  2. Explain how you break a monthly target into weekly and daily activities, such as calls, meetings and proposals.
  3. Share an example of working toward a numerical goal, from a past job, internship or college role.
  4. Mention how you handle a slow period, such as analysing your pipeline, asking for help, or focusing on existing customers.
  5. Draw an ethical line: you will meet targets by finding the right customers, not by mis-selling or hiding product terms.

Sample answers

Fresher (bank sales executive interview)

Yes, I am comfortable. I think targets give clarity on what is expected. When I get a monthly target, I would work backwards. For example, if I need 20 savings accounts and I know roughly one in five meetings converts, I need around 100 good conversations, which is about five a day. Breaking it down that way makes it manageable. During my internship at [company] I had a target of [number] survey responses or leads and I tracked my progress daily in a sheet. I would also be honest with customers. I would rather meet my target with the right customers than get complaints later.

Experienced (insurance advisor moving to a bancassurance role)

I have worked with monthly and quarterly targets for [number] years. Some months I exceeded them, and a few times I fell short. What I learnt is that the pipeline matters more than the last week. I keep at least two to three times my target in active prospects and I follow up consistently. In the last week of a quarter there is always pressure to push any policy to anyone; I avoid that, because a policy that lapses or gets cancelled in the free-look period hurts both the customer and the company.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying you can achieve any target without knowing what it is.
  • Admitting you dislike targets or find them unfair.
  • Describing meeting targets by pushing unsuitable products.
  • Having no method for planning your activity.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What will you do if you miss your target for two months?
  • How would you plan your first month here?
  • What is the free-look period in insurance?
  • What if your target seems unrealistic?

FAQs

Should I ask about the target size in the interview?

Yes, it is reasonable to ask how targets are set, how performance is reviewed and what support new joiners get. Ask in a curious, positive way near the end of the interview rather than as a condition.

What if I have never had a target before?

Use any numerical goal you worked toward, such as fund-raising, recruiting members for a club, selling event tickets or a study plan. Show how you planned, tracked and adjusted. That demonstrates the habit interviewers want.
