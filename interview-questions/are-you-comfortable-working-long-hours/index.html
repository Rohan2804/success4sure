Can You Work Long Hours or Weekends? How to Answer

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Are you comfortable working long hours or weekends?”

Why interviewers ask this

Some roles in finance genuinely have heavy periods: quarter-end closing in accounts, March year-end in banks, audits, or month-end targets in sales. The interviewer wants to know whether you understand that and will cope, or whether you will be unhappy and leave. They are not usually asking you to promise unlimited hours. A balanced answer acknowledges that peak periods happen, shows you have handled extra effort before, and makes clear you plan your work well so long hours are not your normal way of operating.

How to answer

  1. Find out what the role's busy periods are, such as year-end closing, audit season or target month-ends.
  2. Say you are willing to put in extra time when the work needs it, with an example from your past.
  3. Show that you manage time well so routine work does not spill over unnecessarily.
  4. If you have a genuine constraint, such as caring for a parent on certain days, mention it honestly and offer alternatives.

Sample answers

Fresher (bank clerk or officer role)

Yes. I understand that banks have heavy periods, especially around March year-end closing and during audits, and that branches sometimes work on weekends for special drives. I am fine with that. During my final-year exams I also volunteered for my college fest, and there were weeks when I worked late every night. I would rather plan my regular work well so that the extra hours go only where they are actually needed.

Experienced (accounts executive, family constraint)

I am comfortable staying late during month-end and quarter-end closing; I have done that for three years in my current job. The only constraint I have is on Sunday mornings, when I take my mother for her weekly treatment. If weekend work is needed, I can come in on Saturday or work Sunday afternoon. I have always managed this without affecting deadlines.

Experienced (sales role)

In sales, the end of the month is always busy, and I am used to it. I have often met customers on Sundays because that is when salaried people are free. What helps me is front-loading my pipeline in the first two weeks, so the last week is about closing rather than starting from zero.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Promising to work any hours without limit, which sounds unrealistic.
  • Refusing outright without understanding the role's demands.
  • Complaining about long hours in your previous job.
  • Hiding a genuine constraint that will surface after joining.

Likely follow-up questions

  • How do you handle work-life balance?
  • What would you do if a deadline clashes with a family event?
  • How many hours did you work in your last job?

FAQs

Is it a red flag if an interviewer asks about long hours?

Not necessarily. Many roles have predictable peak periods. Ask politely how often extra hours are needed and during which times of year. If the answer suggests long hours are the daily norm, factor that into your decision. You are allowed to evaluate the job too.

Can I ask about overtime or compensatory leave?

It is reasonable to ask at the offer stage or with HR, phrased as wanting to understand the policy. In the main interview, it is better to focus on showing willingness first. Policies differ between banks, NBFCs and private firms, so ask instead of assuming.
