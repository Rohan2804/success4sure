Tell Me About a Time You Failed: STAR Answers

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Tell me about a time you failed.”

Why interviewers ask this

Everyone fails at something, so this question is not a trap about whether you have failed. It tests ownership, honesty and learning. The interviewer wants to hear a real failure with real consequences, see whether you blame others or take responsibility, and know what you do differently now. In finance roles, where errors can cost customers money, a candidate who can analyse their own mistakes calmly is valuable. A story where the 'failure' is secretly a success, or where someone else is at fault, tells the panel the opposite.

How to answer

  1. Choose a genuine failure that mattered but was not catastrophic or unethical, ideally from the last few years.
  2. Set the context briefly, then state clearly what went wrong and your part in it.
  3. Spend most of the answer on what you learnt and the specific changes you made.
  4. If possible, end with a later situation where the lesson helped you succeed.

Sample answers

Fresher (college event)

In my second year, I was in charge of sponsorships for our department fest. I was confident that two local companies would sponsor us because they had done so the year before, so I did not approach anyone else until three weeks before the event. Both declined because of budget cuts, and we had to reduce the prize money and cancel one event. It was my responsibility, and I felt bad facing the team. Since then I always have a backup plan and start early. The next year, as a senior, I approached twelve sponsors from the start, and we exceeded our target.

Experienced (sales target missed)

In my first year as a relationship officer at [bank], I missed my quarterly target for insurance cross-sell by a wide margin. I had spent most of my time on a few large customers who kept postponing decisions, and I neglected the steady stream of smaller customers walking into the branch. My manager pointed out that my pipeline was not balanced. I started tracking leads in a simple sheet, spreading time across segments and following up within forty-eight hours. The next two quarters, I met my targets, and I still use the same tracking habit.

Fresher (competitive exam)

I did not clear the IBPS PO mains in my first attempt. Looking back, I had relied on reading theory and had taken very few full-length mock tests, so my time management in the exam was poor. For the next attempt, I took regular timed mocks, analysed every one of them and worked specifically on my weak sections. That changed how I approach any preparation now.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Choosing a fake failure, like 'I worked so hard that I fell ill'.
  • Blaming teammates, teachers or bad luck.
  • Picking a failure involving dishonesty or a serious ethical lapse.
  • Ending without explaining what you learnt or changed.

Likely follow-up questions

  • How did your team react?
  • What would you do differently today?
  • Tell me about another failure.

FAQs

Can I talk about failing an exam as my failure?

Yes, if you can explain what went wrong in your preparation and what you changed. Exam failures are common in India and interviewers relate to them. Work or team failures can be stronger if you have them, because they show how you handle responsibility towards others.

How long should my failure story be?

About one and a half to two minutes when spoken. Keep the situation brief, be clear about your mistake, and spend most of the time on the lesson and changes. Practise aloud so you do not get emotional or defensive while telling it.
