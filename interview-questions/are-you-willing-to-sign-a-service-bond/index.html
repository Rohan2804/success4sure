Willing to Sign a Service Bond? How to Answer in Interview

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Are you willing to sign a service agreement or bond?”

Why interviewers ask this

Some employers, particularly those that invest in long paid training, ask freshers to sign a service agreement: a commitment to stay for a period or repay part of the training cost if they leave early. Certain bank and IT recruiters use them. The interviewer wants to know whether you are comfortable with the commitment, and whether hesitation signals that you are already planning to leave. A good answer shows you are serious about staying while being sensible about understanding the terms before signing anything.

How to answer

  1. Ask about the terms politely if they have not been explained: duration, amount, and what it covers.
  2. If you are planning to stay long-term anyway, say so; the bond then makes little difference to you.
  3. Avoid committing to a bond you know you cannot honour, for example if you expect a government exam result soon.
  4. Remember to read the actual document carefully before signing, and ask a trusted adult or mentor if anything is unclear.

Sample answers

Fresher (graduate trainee programme with a bond)

Yes, I understand why you have it. The training programme is long and the company invests a lot in new trainees. I am looking for a place to build my career for several years, not just a first job, so a commitment period fits with my own plans. I would just like to understand the exact duration and terms before I sign, so I know my obligations clearly.

Fresher (with an honest concern)

I am open to it, and I do plan to stay. Could you tell me the duration and what amount would be payable in case of early exit? I ask because my family depends on my income, and I want to be fully sure of what I am agreeing to. If the terms are reasonable, I have no hesitation in signing it.

Fresher (bank PO aspirant, asked by a private bank)

Yes. I have stopped preparing for other exams and want to build my career here. A bond only matters if someone plans to leave early, and that is not my plan. I would still like to read the agreement properly and understand the duration and repayment clause, but I do not see it as a problem for me.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying yes immediately without understanding the terms.
  • Showing strong reluctance without explaining why.
  • Agreeing to a bond while secretly planning to leave for another job or exam.
  • Arguing about the legality of bonds in the interview.
  • Assuming every company uses bonds and raising the topic yourself when nobody has mentioned it.

Likely follow-up questions

  • Do you have any other offers or exam results pending?
  • How long do you see yourself staying with us?
  • What would make you leave early?

FAQs

Are service bonds legally enforceable in India?

This depends on the terms and circumstances, and courts have looked at reasonableness in different cases. This is not legal advice. If you have concerns about a specific agreement, read it carefully and consult a qualified person before signing rather than relying on general opinions.

Can I negotiate the bond terms?

For freshers in large campus programmes, terms are usually standard and not negotiable. In smaller organisations or lateral roles, you can sometimes ask for clarification or adjustment. Ask politely and at the offer stage, not during the first interview.
