CRR vs SLR: How to Explain the Difference in an Interview

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “What is the difference between CRR and SLR?”

Why interviewers ask this

CRR and SLR are basic reserve requirements, and panels use this question to separate candidates who memorised acronyms from those who understand banking. It also leads naturally into questions on liquidity, monetary policy and the bank's balance sheet. The panel wants a crisp comparison: what each requires, where the money sits, whether it earns income, who sets it and what it is used for. A candidate who explains the impact on a bank's capacity to lend, with a simple example, stands out. Getting the basics confused here can colour the panel's view of your preparation.

How to answer

  1. Define CRR: a share of a bank's net demand and time liabilities that must be kept as cash balance with RBI, which earns no interest.
  2. Define SLR: a share of NDTL that the bank must keep in liquid assets such as cash, gold or approved government securities, which it holds itself and which can earn interest.
  3. Use a simple example: if a bank has ₹100 crore of deposits, show how part goes to CRR, part to SLR and the rest is available to lend.
  4. Explain the purpose: CRR is a monetary policy and liquidity tool, while SLR ensures bank solvency and also creates demand for government securities.
  5. Say that both rates are set by RBI within legal limits and change from time to time, so you would quote current values only after checking.

Sample answers

Fresher (IBPS PO interview)

Both are reserves banks must maintain, but they differ in form and purpose. CRR, or Cash Reserve Ratio, is the portion of a bank's net demand and time liabilities that it must keep as cash with RBI. The bank earns no interest on it. SLR, or Statutory Liquidity Ratio, is the portion it must keep with itself in safe liquid assets, mostly government securities, and these do earn interest. Suppose a bank has ₹100 crore of deposits. If CRR is x percent and SLR is y percent, that much cannot be lent, and the remainder is available for loans. When RBI cuts CRR, more money is released for lending. CRR is mainly a liquidity and monetary tool; SLR is more about keeping banks safe.

Commerce postgraduate (RBI Assistant interview)

The key difference is where the money is held and whether it earns a return. CRR is held as cash with RBI, so it earns nothing, and RBI uses it to influence liquidity in the system. SLR is held by the bank itself, largely in government securities, so it earns interest and acts as a buffer for solvency. CRR is maintained under the RBI Act, and SLR under the Banking Regulation Act. Both are calculated on net demand and time liabilities. The current rates are set by RBI, and I would check the latest notification before quoting them.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying both are kept with RBI.
  • Saying CRR earns interest.
  • Quoting outdated percentages confidently.
  • Not knowing what net demand and time liabilities mean.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What are net demand and time liabilities?
  • Why would RBI cut CRR?
  • What is the Liquidity Coverage Ratio?
  • How does SLR help the government?

FAQs

Do I need to know the exact current CRR and SLR?

Interviewers often ask, so check the latest values on the RBI website or in the most recent monetary policy statement before your interview. Do not rely on coaching notes from a previous year, since RBI can change CRR during the year.

What is the difference between SLR and LCR?

SLR is a statutory requirement to hold liquid assets as a share of NDTL. The Liquidity Coverage Ratio is a Basel III requirement that banks hold enough high-quality liquid assets to cover net cash outflows over a 30-day stress period. They overlap but serve different regulatory purposes.
