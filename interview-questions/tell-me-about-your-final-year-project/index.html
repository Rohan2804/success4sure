Tell Me About Your Final Year Project: How to Answer

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Tell me about your final-year project.”

Why interviewers ask this

For freshers, the final-year project is often the closest thing to real work experience. Interviewers ask about it to see whether you actually did the work or just signed the report, whether you understand the 'why' behind your choices, and whether you can explain something technical to a non-expert. It is also a chance to judge teamwork, problem-solving and honesty about limitations. A clear two-minute explanation that covers the problem, your role, the method and the result, including what went wrong, tells them more than your marksheet.

How to answer

  1. Start with the problem in one sentence, in plain language, and why it mattered.
  2. Explain your specific role, especially if it was a group project. Use 'I' for what you did and 'we' for what the team did.
  3. Describe the method and tools simply, then the result, with numbers if you have them.
  4. Mention one limitation or something you would do differently. This shows real understanding.

Sample answers

MBA Finance fresher

My project was on the performance of large-cap mutual funds compared with their benchmark index over five years. I wanted to see whether actively managed funds were justifying their higher expense ratios. I collected NAV and index data for [number] funds from AMFI and fund house websites, and calculated returns, standard deviation and Sharpe ratios in Excel. The result was mixed: some funds outperformed after costs, many did not. The limitation was that I used a single five-year period, which can be misleading. If I did it again, I would use rolling returns.

B.Tech fresher (applying for a fintech role)

Our group of four built a simple model to predict whether a loan applicant might default, using a public dataset of about [number] records. I handled data cleaning and feature selection in Python. Many fields had missing values, so I spent most of my time deciding which to drop and which to fill. Our logistic regression model had reasonable accuracy, but I learnt that accuracy alone can mislead when defaults are rare, so we also checked precision and recall.

B.Com fresher

My project studied how small shopkeepers in [town] adopted UPI payments. I surveyed around [number] shop owners with a questionnaire, asking about usage, problems and cash dependence. Most had started using QR codes, but many still preferred cash for supplier payments. The survey taught me how to ask neutral questions and handle people who did not want to share details.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Being unable to explain basic concepts used in your own project.
  • Taking credit for the whole team's work, or giving no clarity about your role.
  • Using heavy jargon without explaining it.
  • Claiming results you cannot support when questioned.

Likely follow-up questions

  • Why did you choose this topic?
  • What was the biggest problem you faced?
  • How would this be useful for our company?

FAQs

What if my project was not related to the job?

Focus on transferable skills such as data handling, research, teamwork or presenting findings. Explain briefly and then link one skill to the role. Interviewers understand that freshers do not always choose projects with a specific employer in mind.

How should I prepare to discuss my project?

Re-read your report a few days before the interview. Write down the problem, method, result and one limitation. Practise explaining it to a friend from another stream in two minutes. Be ready for basic questions on any formula, test or tool you used.
