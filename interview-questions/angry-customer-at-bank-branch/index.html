Angry Customer at the Bank Branch: Situational Answer

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “A customer is shouting at your counter because his work is delayed. What will you do?”

Why interviewers ask this

Branch staff face crowded halls, server downtime and customers who have waited an hour. This situational question, common in IBPS Clerk, PO and RRB interviews, checks your temperament more than your knowledge. The panel wants to see whether you stay calm, separate the person from the problem, follow the bank's procedures, and protect the dignity of both the customer and the other people waiting. It also checks whether you know when to involve the branch manager and whether you understand that customers have a formal grievance route. Unlike a behavioural question, you do not need a past story; you need a sensible, practical plan.

How to answer

  1. Say you would first acknowledge the customer calmly and lower the temperature: a polite greeting, eye contact and a short apology for the wait, without arguing.
  2. Find out the actual issue, for example a pending passbook update, a failed transaction reversal, or a loan document query, and check what is causing the delay.
  3. Give a clear, honest next step and a realistic time, and if it is outside your power, involve the senior officer or branch manager rather than making promises.
  4. Keep the queue in mind: if the conversation will take time, request a colleague to handle the counter or ask the customer to sit at a help desk.
  5. Mention follow-up and the grievance route, such as giving a complaint number, and say you would note any recurring problem so the branch can fix it.

Sample answers

Fresher (IBPS Clerk interview)

First I would stay calm and not take it personally, because usually the customer is angry at the situation, not at me. I would say something like, 'Sir, I understand you have been waiting a long time, I am sorry for that. Please tell me exactly what work is pending.' If it is something I can do, like updating his passbook or checking a transaction, I would do it right away. If the delay is because the server is down, I would explain that honestly and tell him what time is likely, or offer an alternative like the passbook printing kiosk. If he still keeps shouting and others are waiting, I would politely request my senior to step in. I would not argue or raise my voice.

Candidate with branch experience (PO interview, former BC agent)

I worked as a business correspondent for [period], so I have seen this often. My approach is to move the customer slightly away from the counter if possible, to a chair near the help desk, because shouting in front of a queue makes everyone tense. Then I listen fully before saying anything. Once I know the issue, say a debit for a failed ATM transaction, I check the account, raise the dispute in the system in front of him, and give him the reference number and the usual timeline. If he wants to escalate, I tell him about the bank's grievance process. Later I would inform the branch manager in case this is part of a pattern.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying you would call security straight away for a customer who is only upset.
  • Promising a time or outcome you cannot control just to calm the customer.
  • Blaming colleagues, the system or the head office in front of the customer.
  • Ignoring the rest of the queue while dealing with one person for a long time.
  • Answering with only 'I will be polite' without any concrete steps.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What if the customer uses abusive language?
  • What if the customer is wrong and is demanding something against rules?
  • What is the Banking Ombudsman, now under the RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme?
  • How would you handle an elderly customer who cannot use digital channels?

FAQs

How is this different from 'Tell me about a difficult customer'?

That behavioural question asks for a real past story. This one is situational: you describe what you would do in a hypothetical branch scenario. You can still mention a past experience briefly, but the panel mainly wants a sensible step-by-step plan that follows bank procedure.

Should I mention the Ombudsman?

A short mention that customers can approach the bank's grievance cell and then the RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme shows awareness of customer rights. Keep it brief and focus on solving the issue at the branch first.
