What Is Repo Rate? Bank Interview Answer With Example

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “What is the repo rate and how does it affect a bank's customers?”

Why interviewers ask this

Repo rate changes make headlines after every Monetary Policy Committee meeting, so interviewers expect banking aspirants to understand them. The question tests whether you can link a policy rate to real customers: home loan borrowers, depositors, and businesses. It also checks whether you follow current affairs. Panels often ask the current rate, so they are partly testing your preparation and partly your honesty if you are unsure. The best answers explain the mechanism clearly, connect it to external benchmark lending rates, and avoid confusing repo with reverse repo or other tools.

How to answer

  1. Define repo rate simply: the rate at which RBI lends short-term money to banks against government securities.
  2. Explain the chain: when repo rises, banks' cost of funds rises, and loans linked to an external benchmark like the repo rate become costlier fairly quickly.
  3. Give a customer example, such as a floating-rate home loan where the EMI or tenure increases after a hike.
  4. Mention the other side: deposit rates usually rise too, though often with a lag, which helps savers and senior citizens.
  5. If you know the current rate from the latest policy, state it; if not, say you will check the latest MPC announcement rather than guessing.

Sample answers

Fresher (IBPS PO interview)

Repo rate is the interest rate at which the RBI lends money to commercial banks for a short period, with government securities as collateral. It is one of the main tools RBI uses to control inflation. When RBI raises the repo rate, borrowing becomes costlier for banks, and they pass it on to customers. For example, most new floating-rate home loans are now linked to an external benchmark, often the repo rate, so a hike can increase a customer's EMI or extend the loan tenure within a few months. On the other side, banks also raise fixed deposit rates, which benefits savers. When RBI cuts the rate, the reverse happens. The rate is decided by the Monetary Policy Committee, and I follow its announcements on the RBI website.

Candidate who is unsure of the current figure

Repo rate is RBI's lending rate to banks for short-term funds against securities. It works like a signal for the whole economy. If inflation is high, RBI raises it, loans become costlier, people and businesses borrow and spend a little less, and price pressure eases. For a branch customer, the most visible effect is on floating-rate loans like home and vehicle loans linked to an external benchmark. I read about the last MPC decision but I do not want to quote the exact figure wrongly, so I would confirm it from the RBI's latest policy statement.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Mixing up repo and reverse repo or saying repo is the rate banks pay to customers.
  • Quoting an old repo rate confidently.
  • Saying a repo hike immediately changes all existing fixed deposits; fixed-rate deposits already booked do not change.
  • Not connecting the rate to any customer or business example.

FAQs

Do I need to know the current repo rate for the interview?

Yes, it is a common question. Check the latest Monetary Policy Committee statement on the RBI website a day before your interview. Rates change during the year, so do not rely on figures from old notes or coaching material.

What is the difference between repo and reverse repo?

Under repo, RBI lends to banks against securities. Under reverse repo, banks park surplus money with RBI. In recent years the Standing Deposit Facility has become the floor of the policy corridor, so mention that if the panel asks about absorbing liquidity.
