Overqualified for Bank Clerk? How to Answer the Question

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “You are a postgraduate. Why are you applying for a clerk post?”

Why interviewers ask this

Many clerk candidates hold MBAs, M.Coms or engineering degrees, and panels worry that they will leave as soon as they clear a PO exam or will feel bored with clerical work. The question tests honesty and commitment. The panel wants to hear that you value the role itself, the customer contact, the operational learning and the internal promotion path, rather than treating it as a waiting room. It is fine to have ambitions; it is not fine to show disrespect for the job you are interviewing for. A calm, sincere answer that respects the clerk role usually works best.

How to answer

  1. Acknowledge your qualification without sounding superior; say you see the clerk role as a strong foundation, not a lesser job.
  2. Explain what the role offers: hands-on knowledge of branch operations, cash, accounts and customers that officers rely on.
  3. Mention the internal promotion route, which lets experienced clerks move to officer cadre through the bank's own process.
  4. Show how your education will help you do the job better, such as understanding products, explaining them to customers, or using technology.
  5. If you are honest that you may also attempt officer exams, frame it as growing within banking, not leaving it.

Sample answers

MBA (Finance) graduate (IBPS Clerk interview)

I do have an MBA, but I see the clerk role as the real foundation of banking. A clerk handles accounts, cash, KYC, passbooks and the first conversation with almost every customer, so I will learn how a branch actually runs, which no classroom taught me. Many respected branch managers started as clerks and moved up through internal promotions, and I would be happy to follow that path. My MBA will help me explain products like mutual funds or insurance to customers clearly and support the branch's business. I am applying because I want a long career in banking, and starting at the counter is a good place to learn it.

M.Com graduate who also applied for PO (SBI Clerk interview)

I will be honest, I have also appeared for PO exams, because I want to grow in banking. But I am applying for clerk with full seriousness. If I am selected, I will join and give my best. Working at the counter will make me a better officer later, whether through promotion or another route, because I will understand customer problems and operations first-hand. My M.Com background makes accounting and reconciliation easy for me, so I can be productive quickly.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying you are only joining until you clear PO or another exam.
  • Implying clerical work is beneath you.
  • Hiding that you have appeared for officer exams if the panel asks directly.
  • Giving a vague answer about 'any government job is good'.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What will you do if you clear PO next year?
  • Won't you get bored with repetitive work?
  • What are the duties of a bank clerk?
  • How does internal promotion work in banks?

FAQs

Is it risky to admit I am also preparing for PO?

Panels know many clerk candidates attempt officer exams. Lying is riskier than a measured truth. Present it as growth within banking and stress that you will take the clerk job seriously for as long as you hold it.

Does a higher degree help in a clerk interview?

It can, if you show how it makes you better at the role, for example better product knowledge or accounting skills. It hurts only when it sounds like you think the role is too small for you.
