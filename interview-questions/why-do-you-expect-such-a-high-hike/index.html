Why Do You Expect a High Hike? How to Justify It

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Why are you asking for such a big salary hike?”

Why interviewers ask this

When your expected salary is well above your current CTC, the employer wants to know whether the jump is justified or just ambitious. They are checking whether you understand market rates, whether the new role carries genuinely more responsibility, and whether you can defend your position with facts rather than emotion. Your tone matters too: calm reasoning suggests professionalism, while defensiveness or arrogance does not. A good answer gives two or three solid reasons and leaves room for discussion.

How to answer

  1. Explain that your current CTC may be below market, if true, and why (for example, a small organisation or limited increments).
  2. Point to the higher responsibility of the new role, such as a larger portfolio, team or geography.
  3. Mention specific skills or results that add value, like certifications, target achievement or process improvements.
  4. Stay open to discussion, and consider how the total package is structured, not just the fixed pay.

Sample answers

Experienced (moving from a small firm)

I understand the hike looks large on paper. My current CTC is lower than the market rate for my experience because I have been at a small firm where annual increments have been limited. Over the last two years, my work has grown significantly: I now independently handle [responsibility]. This role also includes managing a team, which I am not doing now. My expectation is based on what similar roles in the market are paying, but I am open to discussing how we structure the package.

Experienced (bigger role)

The role you are offering is a step up from mine. I would move from handling a single branch's customers to covering a region with [number] branches and reporting to the zonal head. That is a significant increase in responsibility and travel. My expectation reflects that. I have consistently met my targets over the last three years, which I can show through my appraisal letters.

Experienced (rare skill)

My expectation reflects the specialised skills this role needs. I have [certification or experience] and hands-on experience with [system or process], which is not common in the market. I am confident this will reduce your training time and help me contribute quickly. I am happy to walk you through specific situations where I used these skills, so you can judge whether the expectation is fair.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying 'everyone gets this much when switching' without reasons.
  • Revealing personal financial needs like loans as the main reason.
  • Refusing to discuss or adjust your expectation.
  • Exaggerating responsibilities or results.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What if we offer a smaller hike with an early review?
  • What is your minimum acceptable figure?
  • Do you have another offer at this level?

FAQs

What is a typical hike when switching jobs in India?

It varies widely by industry, role, demand for your skills and your current pay. There is no fixed rule, so avoid quoting a standard percentage. Research similar roles and focus on what the new role is worth rather than a hike percentage on your current pay.

Should I mention personal reasons like an EMI for needing a higher salary?

Generally no. Salary discussions should be based on the value you bring and the market rate for the role. Personal financial pressures, while real, do not usually influence the employer and can make you seem less focused on the job.
