Tell Me About a Time You Persuaded Someone (Examples)

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Tell me about a time you persuaded someone to change their mind.”

Why interviewers ask this

Persuasion is part of many finance jobs: convincing a customer to start a SIP, a manager to try a new process, or a credit team to reconsider a file. The interviewer wants to see how you influence people, whether by understanding their concerns and using evidence, or by pressure and repetition. Persuasion without honesty is a red flag in regulated industries, so they also listen for whether you respected the other person's interests. A strong answer shows preparation, empathy, facts and a respectful outcome, even if the person did not fully agree.

How to answer

  1. Choose a situation where someone initially disagreed with you on something that mattered.
  2. Explain how you understood their concerns before making your case.
  3. Describe how you persuaded them: data, examples, a small trial or addressing their specific worry.
  4. Share the outcome and what you learnt about influencing people.

Sample answers

Experienced (mutual fund distribution)

A customer in his thirties wanted to stop his SIP because the market had fallen and his fund was showing a loss. I understood his worry, so I did not argue. I showed him his own statement and explained how his monthly instalments were buying more units at lower prices. I also reminded him that his goal, his daughter's education, was twelve years away. I made it clear the decision was his and that past patterns do not guarantee future returns. He decided to continue. The key was helping him look at his own goal rather than the daily market.

Experienced (process change)

In my operations team, we used to scan documents at the end of the day, which caused delays. I suggested scanning at the counter itself. My manager was worried it would slow down service. I proposed a one-week trial at one counter and tracked the time taken. The difference was small and the end-of-day backlog disappeared, so he adopted it across the branch.

Fresher (college)

My project team wanted to use a simple survey with fifteen responses. I felt it was too small. I showed them two examples where small samples gave misleading results, and offered to collect extra responses myself. They agreed, and our project got good marks.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Describing pressure or repetition as persuasion.
  • Telling a story where you pushed a product the customer did not need.
  • Not explaining how you understood the other person's view.
  • Choosing a trivial disagreement.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What if they still had not agreed?
  • Tell me about a time you failed to persuade someone.
  • How do you persuade senior people?

FAQs

Is persuasion the same as sales?

Sales is one form of persuasion, but this question covers any situation where you changed someone's mind, including colleagues and managers. In all cases, interviewers want to see honesty, preparation and respect for the other person's interests, not just a win.

What if the other person was right in the end?

That can make a good story if you show you listened and changed your view. It demonstrates open-mindedness. But for this question, try to choose an example where your persuasion led to a positive outcome.
