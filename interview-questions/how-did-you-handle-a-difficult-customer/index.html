How Did You Handle a Difficult Customer? Sample Answers

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Tell me about a time you dealt with a difficult customer.”

Why interviewers ask this

Customer-facing roles in banks, NBFCs and insurance companies regularly deal with frustrated people: a rejected loan, a delayed claim, a failed transaction or a misunderstood product. The interviewer wants to see empathy, composure and the ability to resolve the problem within the rules. They also check whether you know when to escalate and whether you protect the organisation's reputation without making false promises. The best answers show you listened first, took ownership of the next steps and stayed honest, even when you could not give the customer what they wanted.

How to answer

  1. Set the scene briefly: who the customer was and why they were upset.
  2. Describe how you calmed the situation, for example by listening fully, acknowledging the problem and moving to a quieter place.
  3. Explain what you did to solve it or to give a clear answer, including any escalation within the rules.
  4. Share the outcome and what you learnt about handling customers.

Sample answers

Experienced (bank branch)

A senior citizen came to the branch very angry because a failed ATM withdrawal of ₹10,000 had been debited from his account but no cash was dispensed. He was shouting at the counter. I invited him to sit at my desk, offered water and listened without interrupting. I checked his account, confirmed the debit, and raised a complaint in the system right in front of him. I explained the usual process and timelines, and gave him the complaint number. I also called him when the amount was reversed. He later came back to thank me and opened a fixed deposit with us.

Experienced (insurance claims support)

A customer called because her health insurance claim had been partly rejected, and she felt cheated. I listened, then walked her through the policy document and showed her the specific room rent limit that had caused the deduction. I could not change the decision, but I explained how to request a review if she had additional documents, and I followed up after a week. She was still unhappy about the outcome but thanked me for being honest. I learnt that clarity often matters as much as the result.

Fresher (retail part-time job)

While working weekends at a mobile store, a customer insisted on a refund for a phone damaged by water, which was not covered. I stayed calm, explained the warranty terms, and helped him book a paid repair at the service centre. He left calmer than he came in.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Making promises you cannot keep, such as guaranteeing a claim approval.
  • Describing the customer in a mocking or disrespectful way.
  • Breaking rules to please the customer.
  • Telling a story where you just passed the customer to someone else.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What would you do if the customer started abusing you?
  • When would you escalate to your manager?
  • How do you handle a customer who is wrong?

FAQs

What if I have never worked with customers?

Use any situation where you dealt with an upset person: helping at a family shop, handling complaints in a college event, or managing an angry member in a group project. Explain how you listened, stayed calm and solved the problem.

Should I mention grievance processes like the banking ombudsman?

If relevant, briefly mentioning that you informed the customer about the bank's grievance process shows awareness of customer rights. Keep the focus on what you did to solve the problem. Avoid quoting specific timelines or rules unless you are sure they are current.
