Managing Multiple Deadlines: Interview Answer Examples

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Tell me about a time you had to manage several deadlines at once.”

Why interviewers ask this

Juggling tasks is daily life in most finance jobs: a branch officer may have customer queries, loan files and compliance reports due on the same day. This question checks your system for prioritising, not just your willingness to work hard. The interviewer wants to hear how you decide what matters most, how you communicate when something will slip, and whether quality holds up when you are stretched. Vague answers like 'I manage my time well' tell them nothing; a specific example with your method and the outcome does.

How to answer

  1. Describe the situation with the actual tasks and deadlines that clashed.
  2. Explain how you prioritised: by deadline, impact on customers, regulatory importance or your manager's guidance.
  3. Mention any communication, such as telling stakeholders early that a lower-priority task would be delayed.
  4. Share the result and the habit or tool you still use, such as a daily list or calendar blocks.

Sample answers

Experienced (accounts executive)

Last March, I had GST return filing, a statutory audit query list and the year-end vendor reconciliation all due in the same week. I listed everything with its deadline and consequence. GST filing had a fixed legal due date and penalties, so it came first. The audit queries were next because the auditors were on-site for limited days. For vendor reconciliation, I told my manager that I could finish the top twenty vendors by the deadline and the rest two days later, and he agreed. Everything was completed without penalties. I now plan March work from mid-February.

Fresher (college)

In my final semester, I had a project submission, a placement aptitude test and my role as treasurer for the commerce fest, all in the same ten days. I made a day-by-day plan and blocked fixed hours for each. I asked a junior to help with fest payments and trained her for one evening. I submitted the project a day early, cleared the aptitude test and closed the fest accounts on time. Delegating was hard for me, but it was the only way.

Experienced (relationship manager)

At quarter-end, I often have customer meetings, internal reports and compliance documentation due together. I start each day by marking what is customer-facing, what is regulatory and what can wait, and I check with my manager when two items are both urgent. It keeps me from spending the morning on easy tasks while important ones slip.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying you just work longer hours to finish everything.
  • Giving no method for prioritising.
  • Describing a situation where you missed deadlines without telling anyone.
  • Picking an example with only one easy deadline.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What do you do when two urgent tasks come from two different managers?
  • Which tools do you use to plan your work?
  • Tell me about a time you missed a deadline.

FAQs

What prioritisation method should I mention?

Name what you actually use. A simple approach is to rank tasks by urgency and importance, giving top priority to customer-impacting and regulatory deadlines. Some people use a daily to-do list, others calendar blocks. The interviewer cares more that you have a consistent method than which one it is.

Is it okay to say I asked my manager for help prioritising?

Yes, especially when tasks come from different people or conflict. It shows good judgement and communication. Just make sure you also show your own thinking, such as suggesting an order and asking the manager to confirm it, rather than simply asking what to do.
