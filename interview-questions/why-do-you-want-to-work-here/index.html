Why Do You Want to Work Here? How to Answer With Research

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Why do you want to work for our company?”

Why interviewers ask this

Interviewers ask this to separate candidates who chose them from those who applied to every opening on a job portal. They want evidence that you have looked into the company's business, values or recent developments, and that your reasons will survive the first difficult month. A good answer also shows how you research before acting, which matters in finance roles where preparation is the job. Generic praise like 'it is a reputed company' tells them nothing and can make the rest of your interview sound rehearsed.

How to answer

  1. Research three concrete things: what the company sells and to whom, something recent such as a new branch push or product, and how the role fits into the business.
  2. Use annual reports, the company website, investor presentations, news and LinkedIn posts by employees as sources.
  3. Give one reason about the company and one about the role, then link both to your goals.
  4. Keep it honest. If location or stability genuinely matter to you, mention them briefly alongside the stronger reasons.

Sample answers

Fresher (campus interview with a private bank)

I have been following your bank's expansion into semi-urban branches, especially in [region], and the focus on small business lending there. I come from [town], and I have seen how hard it is for local shop owners to get formal credit, so that work feels meaningful to me. I also read about your graduate trainee programme, which rotates trainees across branch operations and sales in the first year. As a fresher, that kind of structured learning is exactly what I need. So both the direction of the bank and the way you train new people make this my first choice.

Experienced (moving from a broking firm to an AMC)

I have spent three years with a broking house serving retail investors, and I have noticed that many of my clients do better with disciplined SIP investing than with frequent trading. Your fund house has invested heavily in investor education and simple products, and I liked the [campaign or initiative] you ran last year. The role also lets me work with distributors across the region, which builds on my client relationships while moving me into long-term investing. I see a clear match between what I have learnt and where your business is going.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying 'it is a big brand' or 'it is a good company' without any detail.
  • Getting basic facts wrong, such as the company's main products or headquarters.
  • Focusing only on salary, perks or work-from-home options.
  • Reading out the 'About us' page instead of giving your own reasons.

Likely follow-up questions

  • Who do you think are our main competitors?
  • What do you know about our recent results?
  • Which other companies have you applied to?

FAQs

How much research is enough before an interview?

Spend at least an hour. Know the company's main businesses, its size in rough terms, the role's place in the organisation and one or two recent developments. For listed companies, the latest investor presentation is usually the quickest source. You do not need to memorise figures, but you should speak about the business with confidence.

Is it okay to say I want a stable job?

Stability is a legitimate reason, especially for public sector banks, but it should not be your only reason. Pair it with something about the work itself, such as serving customers in your region or learning credit, so the panel sees you will be engaged and not just secure.
