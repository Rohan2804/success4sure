Why Are Your Marks Low? How to Answer in an Interview

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Why are your marks or grades low?”

Why interviewers ask this

Marks are often the only hard data a recruiter has about a fresher, so a low percentage or CGPA naturally draws questions. The interviewer is not trying to humiliate you; they want to understand whether the marks reflect your ability, your effort, or circumstances. They also want to see how you handle an uncomfortable question. Candidates who become defensive or make excuses do poorly. Candidates who accept the marks calmly, explain briefly and redirect to stronger evidence of their ability usually recover well.

How to answer

  1. Accept the marks without excuses, and give a brief honest reason if there is one, such as a weak start, a part-time job or family duties.
  2. Point to evidence that shows your ability more accurately: an upward trend, strong marks in key subjects, projects, internships or certifications.
  3. Show what you learnt about your own study habits and how you now work differently.
  4. Bring the focus back to the job with a skill or achievement that matters for the role.

Sample answers

Engineering fresher (CGPA 6.4)

My CGPA is 6.4, and I know it is lower than many candidates here. In the first two years I spent too much time on our college technical club and did not balance it with studies. My grades improved from the fifth semester, when I scored around 7.5. The club work also gave me real experience: I led a team of six that built [project], and I learnt Python and data analysis on my own. For an analytics role, I think that practical work shows my ability better than my early marks.

B.Com fresher (worked part-time)

I scored 58 per cent in my graduation. From my second year, I worked about 20 hours a week at a local CA's office to support my family, and that left less time for studies. I do not regret it, because I learnt GST filing, Tally and bank reconciliation in a real office, which most freshers only read about. My marks in Accountancy and Taxation were above 70. I am confident I can handle the work in this accounts role.

Bank PO candidate (60 per cent graduation)

My graduation percentage is 60. I was not very focused in college, and I accept that. What changed my approach was preparing for this exam: I studied systematically for over a year, cleared the prelims and mains, and improved my reasoning and quantitative scores steadily across mock tests. I think that preparation shows the discipline I now have.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Blaming teachers, the university or the evaluation system.
  • Saying 'marks do not matter', which sounds dismissive.
  • Giving long emotional stories instead of a brief explanation.
  • Failing to mention anything that shows your real ability.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What was your best subject and why?
  • What did you do outside studies?
  • How will you handle our internal training exams?

FAQs

Do low marks matter in a bank PO interview?

Bank PO selection is driven largely by the written exam and the interview, and graduation marks usually matter only for eligibility. Panels may still ask about low marks. A calm, honest answer that points to your exam preparation discipline is usually enough.

How do I compensate for low marks on my resume?

Highlight projects, internships, certifications such as NISM or IRDAI modules, part-time work and responsibilities in college. Put these higher on the page. If later semesters were better, show the trend. Employers want evidence of ability, and marks are only one form of it.
