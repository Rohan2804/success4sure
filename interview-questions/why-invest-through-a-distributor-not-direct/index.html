Why Invest Through You, Not Direct Plans? Interview Answer

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Why should a client invest through you when direct plans have lower costs?”

Why interviewers ask this

Since SEBI introduced direct plans, every mutual fund distributor and bank wealth RM faces this question from clients. Interviewers ask it to check whether you understand the expense ratio difference and can justify your value honestly. Candidates who deny the cost difference or criticise direct plans sound defensive or misleading. The panel wants to hear that you respect the client's choice, that you know who benefits from direct plans, and that you can articulate the service you add, such as goal planning, behavioural coaching during market falls, paperwork, portfolio reviews and family-level support.

How to answer

  1. Admit openly that regular plans have a higher expense ratio because they include distributor commission, while direct plans do not.
  2. Say that direct plans suit investors who are confident to choose funds, rebalance and stay disciplined on their own.
  3. Explain the value you provide: understanding goals, choosing suitable schemes, handling KYC and paperwork, regular reviews and support during market falls.
  4. Mention that behaviour, such as not panicking or chasing past returns, can matter as much as cost for many investors.
  5. Respect the client's choice, and mention that registered investment advisers charge fees and use direct plans, as another option.

Sample answers

Fresher (MF distributor support role)

I would be honest with the client. Direct plans do have a lower expense ratio because there is no distributor commission, and over many years that difference adds up. If he is comfortable selecting funds, tracking them and staying calm in market falls, direct plans can be a good choice for him. But many investors want help. I would explain what I would do: understand his goals, choose suitable funds, set up SIPs, handle KYC and nominations, review the portfolio every year, and be available when markets fall so he does not make panic decisions. Some clients value that service; others do not need it. Either way, I would want him to decide with full information.

Experienced distributor (wealth RM interview)

I have this conversation often. I show the client the expense ratio of both plans so he sees the difference clearly. Then I talk about what I actually do. In the 2020 fall, several of my clients wanted to stop their SIPs, and I spent hours on calls helping them stick to their plans. Many of them are now well ahead because they did not exit at the bottom. I also help families with nominations, transmissions after a death, and consolidated reporting. If a client wants to go direct, I respect that. Clients who stay with me do so because they see the value.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Denying that regular plans cost more.
  • Discouraging direct plans by calling them risky.
  • Claiming your fund selection will always beat the cost difference.
  • Not knowing the difference between a distributor and an investment adviser.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What is TER?
  • How is a distributor paid?
  • What would you do if your client switches to direct plans?
  • Can a distributor also give investment advice for a fee?

FAQs

How much more does a regular plan cost?

The difference in expense ratio between direct and regular plans varies by scheme and AMC, and it changes over time. Check the current TER of both plans on the AMC's or AMFI's website rather than quoting a general figure.

Is it legal for distributors to advise clients?

Distributors can provide incidental guidance linked to the products they distribute, but SEBI separates distribution from fee-based investment advice. Investment advisers must be SEBI-registered. Check the current SEBI regulations for the exact boundaries.
