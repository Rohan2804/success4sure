Ethical Dilemma Interview Question: How to Answer

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Tell me about a time you faced an ethical dilemma at work.”

Why interviewers ask this

Integrity is central in banking, insurance and securities roles, where staff handle customer money, confidential data and regulated products. Interviewers ask this to see whether you recognise ethical issues, whether you would bend rules under target pressure, and how you raise concerns. They are not looking for heroics. They want a calm story showing that you followed the rules, used the right channels and protected customers, even when it was inconvenient. Answers that suggest you would compromise 'just once' can end an interview quickly.

How to answer

  1. Pick a realistic dilemma, such as pressure to skip a process step, a gift from a customer, or a request to share confidential information.
  2. Explain the conflict clearly: what the easy option was and why it was wrong.
  3. Describe what you did, including consulting your manager or compliance, and how you handled any pushback.
  4. Share the outcome and your principle for similar situations.

Sample answers

Experienced (insurance sales)

A customer wanted a traditional endowment policy mainly because he believed it would give high, assured returns, based on what a friend had told him. Selling it would have helped my monthly target. But when I understood his needs, he actually needed a term plan for protection and a separate savings route. I explained the difference honestly and recommended the term plan. My premium for that month was lower, but he later referred two colleagues to me. I believe a customer who is misled will eventually complain, and it is not worth it.

Experienced (bank operations)

A long-standing customer asked me to open an account for his nephew without the nephew being present, promising to bring documents later. My senior colleague said it would be fine because the customer was well known. I was uncomfortable, because KYC rules require proper verification. I politely explained the requirement to the customer and offered a quick appointment the next day. I also mentioned my concern to my branch manager, who supported me. The nephew came the next day and the account was opened properly.

Fresher (college)

During a college exam, a friend asked me to share answers on our phones. I said no, even though he was upset with me for a while. Later he told me he was glad I refused, because the invigilator caught another group.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Describing a situation where you bent the rules and justifying it.
  • Choosing an example that is not really an ethical issue.
  • Accusing a specific person in a way that sounds like gossip.
  • Giving a preachy speech without a real situation.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What would you do if your manager asked you to mis-sell?
  • Would you report a colleague who was breaking rules?
  • How would you handle a gift from a customer?

FAQs

What if I have never faced an ethical dilemma?

Think about college, internships or part-time work: pressure to copy, misreport hours, share confidential information or exaggerate. If you truly have none, describe how you would handle a likely situation in the role, such as target pressure to mis-sell, and refer to following policy and escalating to compliance.

Why do banks and insurers stress ethics in interviews?

These businesses are regulated by bodies such as the RBI, SEBI and IRDAI, and handle customer money and data. Mis-selling, KYC lapses or data leaks can lead to penalties and customer harm. Employers want people who will follow rules even under target pressure.
