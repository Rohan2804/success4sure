What Does a Relationship Manager Do? Interview Answer

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “What do you understand about the role of a relationship manager?”

Why interviewers ask this

Relationship manager is one of the most common titles in Indian banks, NBFCs, wealth firms and brokerages, and the role varies: branch banking RM, wealth RM, SME or business banking RM, or priority banking RM. Interviewers ask this to check whether you understand the job beyond 'handling clients'. They want to hear about managing a portfolio of customers, growing balances and relationships, cross-selling suitable products, ensuring service quality and complying with KYC and suitability rules. A specific answer shows you researched the role and will not be surprised by its sales component.

How to answer

  1. Describe the core: an RM owns a portfolio of clients and is their main point of contact for banking or investment needs.
  2. Explain the two sides, growing the relationship through deposits, investments and loans, and retaining clients through good service.
  3. Mention typical metrics, such as portfolio size, new acquisitions, product penetration, client retention and complaint levels.
  4. Include the compliance responsibilities too: periodic KYC updates, suitability checks, clear disclosure, and protecting client data.
  5. Connect your answer to the specific type of RM role you applied for and the skills or experience you bring to it.

Sample answers

Fresher (MBA, applying for branch banking RM)

A relationship manager is the main contact for a group of the bank's customers, usually those with higher balances. The RM's job is to understand each customer's needs, make sure their banking experience is smooth, and offer relevant products like fixed deposits, mutual funds, insurance or loans at the right time. RMs are measured on things like growth in the portfolio's balances, new clients, product holding and retention. Service is a big part, because a client who is unhappy will move money elsewhere. The RM also handles KYC updates and must make sure every product sold is suitable. I think my communication skills and my NISM V-A certification will help me build trust quickly.

Experienced (sales officer moving to SME relationship manager)

For an SME RM, the client is a business, so the relationship covers current accounts, working capital, term loans, trade finance and the owner's personal banking. The RM coordinates with credit, operations and product teams, and is responsible for early warning signs like delayed payments or falling turnover. I have sold current accounts and business loans for [number] years, so I know many local businesses already. What I want to build is deeper relationship management, where I support a client's growth over years rather than one transaction.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Describing the RM role as only customer service, ignoring sales.
  • Describing it as only sales, ignoring service and compliance.
  • Not knowing which type of RM role you applied for.
  • Using vague phrases like 'managing relationships' without specifics.

Likely follow-up questions

  • How would you handle a client who wants to move his money to another bank?
  • How many clients can one RM manage effectively?
  • What products would you offer a client with a large FD maturing?
  • How do you balance sales targets with client interests?

FAQs

Is a relationship manager role a sales job?

Largely, yes. Most RM roles have targets for acquisition, balances and product sales. But unlike pure acquisition roles, RMs also handle service and retention for an existing portfolio. Understanding both sides is important in the interview.

What certifications help for RM roles?

For roles involving mutual funds, NISM Series V-A is commonly needed, and an IRDAI-mandated certification is needed for selling insurance. Some wealth roles prefer NISM X-A or CFP. Check the job description and your employer's requirements.
