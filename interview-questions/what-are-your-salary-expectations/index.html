What Are Your Salary Expectations? How to Answer

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “What are your salary expectations?”

Why interviewers ask this

Employers ask this to check whether you fit their budget, whether your expectations are realistic for the role and market, and how you value your own skills. Quoting too high can rule you out; quoting too low can leave money on the table and sometimes makes the employer doubt your experience. The interviewer also watches how comfortably you discuss money, which matters in roles where you will negotiate with customers. A calm answer based on research, expressed as a range and linked to the role's responsibilities, works best.

How to answer

  1. Research the market before the interview: job portals, salary surveys, seniors and peers in similar roles, and the company's campus offers if public.
  2. Give a range rather than a single figure, with the lower end being an amount you would genuinely accept.
  3. Talk in CTC or in-hand terms consistently, and clarify which one you mean.
  4. Link your range to the role's responsibilities and your skills, and show flexibility on the overall package.

Sample answers

Fresher (off-campus, analyst role)

From what I have researched for analyst roles at NBFCs in [city], freshers usually get in the range of [₹X] to [₹Y] lakh per annum CTC. Given my internship in credit and my certification, I would expect something in that range, ideally towards the middle. But my main priority is the learning and the role itself, so I am open to discussing the complete package, including any training or variable components.

Experienced (3 years, moving to a larger bank)

My current CTC is [₹X] lakh. For this role, which has a larger portfolio and team responsibility, I am looking for something in the range of [₹Y] to [₹Z] lakh CTC. I have based that on what similar roles in the market are offering and the additional responsibility. I am flexible on how the package is structured between fixed and variable pay, as long as the overall figure is fair.

Career switcher (moving into a new field)

Since I am moving into a new field, I understand I may not be at the same level as someone with direct experience. I would expect a package in line with what you offer people with similar transferable skills, perhaps in the [₹X] to [₹Y] range. I would rather join at a fair level and grow based on performance.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Quoting a figure without any research.
  • Giving a range where even the lowest figure is above what you would actually accept.
  • Saying 'anything is fine', which suggests you undervalue yourself.
  • Mixing up CTC, gross and in-hand salary during the discussion.

Likely follow-up questions

  • Is that negotiable?
  • What is the minimum you would accept?
  • What if we can only offer the lower end?

FAQs

Should I give my expected salary as CTC or in-hand?

In India, most companies discuss salary in CTC, which includes fixed pay, employer PF contribution, gratuity, insurance and any variable component. In-hand salary is what reaches your bank account after deductions. Clarify which figure the company uses and keep your own numbers consistent.

What if the interviewer insists on one number?

Give the figure you would be happy with, ideally around the middle of your range. Back it with a short reason, such as market research or additional responsibility. Then add that you are open to discussing the overall package so the conversation stays flexible.
