Customer Says 'I'll Think About It': Sales Interview Answer

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “The customer says, 'I will think about it.' What do you do next?”

Why interviewers ask this

'I will think about it' is the most common way customers delay a decision, and it often hides a specific concern: price, trust, a spouse's opinion or not understanding the product. Interviewers ask this to see whether you can uncover the real objection politely, without pressuring the customer. They also want to see if you set a concrete next step instead of leaving the deal hanging. Pushy answers, such as creating fake urgency or saying the offer ends today when it does not, are red flags. Balanced answers respect the customer's need for time while keeping the conversation moving.

How to answer

  1. Acknowledge the request respectfully, since buying financial products is an important decision and taking time is reasonable.
  2. Ask a gentle question to uncover the concern, such as whether anything is unclear, or whether the premium or EMI feels high.
  3. Address the specific concern if one comes up, with facts, a comparison or a smaller option.
  4. Agree on a clear next step: a date and time to call, documents to share, or a meeting with the spouse or family.
  5. Follow up as promised, and accept a final no gracefully if that is the answer.

Sample answers

Fresher (insurance sales interview)

I would say, 'Of course, sir, it is an important decision and you should be comfortable.' Then I would ask, 'Just so I can help you decide, is there any part that is not clear, or is it more about the premium?' Often the customer then tells me the real concern. If it is the premium, I could show a lower cover or a different payment frequency. If he wants to discuss with his wife, I would offer to explain it to both of them together. Before leaving, I would fix a time, like 'Shall I call you on Saturday at 11?' and send the brochure on WhatsApp. I would not say the offer ends today if it does not.

Experienced (home loan sales officer)

In home loans, customers often compare several lenders, so 'I will think about it' usually means they are waiting for another quote. I ask directly but politely whether they are comparing offers and what matters most to them, rate, processing fee or speed. Then I make sure they compare correctly, including fees and the reset terms of the floating rate. I give them a written summary of our offer and set a follow-up date. Some come back, some do not, but they remember that I was transparent.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Creating false urgency, such as an offer that 'ends today' when it does not.
  • Leaving without a specific follow-up plan.
  • Repeating the whole pitch again instead of finding the concern.
  • Getting irritated or showing disappointment.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What if the customer does not pick up your follow-up call?
  • How many times would you follow up before stopping?
  • How do you handle 'your product is too expensive'?
  • What if the customer's spouse is against the purchase?

FAQs

How many follow-ups are acceptable?

There is no fixed number, but two or three well-spaced, useful follow-ups are usually reasonable. Each should add something, like a clarification or document. If the customer clearly declines, stop and note it. Respecting a no protects your reputation.

Is creating urgency always wrong?

Real urgency is fine, such as a genuine offer deadline or the fact that insurance premiums rise with age. False urgency is misleading and can amount to mis-selling. Use only facts you can verify.
