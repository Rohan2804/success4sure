Sell Me This Pen: How to Answer in a Sales Interview

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Sell me this pen.”

Why interviewers ask this

This classic role-play is still common in insurance, bank sales, fintech and FMCG interviews. The interviewer does not care about the pen. They want to see whether you start by understanding the buyer or by listing features. Strong salespeople ask questions, find a need, connect the product to that need, handle an objection and ask for the sale. The exercise also tests confidence, listening, and how you react when the interviewer is deliberately difficult. Candidates who launch into 'This pen is smooth, stylish and affordable' usually miss the point.

How to answer

  1. Start with questions instead of features, such as when the interviewer last used a pen, what they use it for, and what annoys them about pens.
  2. Listen for a need, like signing documents in meetings, a pen that never works when needed, or wanting to make a good impression with clients.
  3. Connect one or two features of the pen to that specific need, in the buyer's language.
  4. Handle an objection calmly, for example price or 'I already have a pen', by going back to the need.
  5. Close confidently by asking for the decision, such as offering the pen and asking how many they would like.

Sample answers

Fresher (insurance sales interview)

Before I tell you about this pen, may I ask you something? When did you last sign something important? ... Right, today itself, approval letters. And what pen did you use? ... Whichever one was on the desk. Has it ever happened that you were in a meeting with a client and the pen did not write? ... That is exactly the moment this pen is made for. It has a [smooth refill] that starts instantly, and its [metal body] looks professional when you are signing in front of clients. It also fits in your shirt pocket, so you always have it. Since you sign documents every day, wouldn't it make sense to have one pen you can depend on? Shall I give you this one, or would you like a pack of two, one for the office and one for your bag?

Experienced (bank relationship manager interview)

Sir, before I sell you anything, can I ask how you usually use a pen in your day? ... Mostly for notes in meetings and signing approvals. Do you prefer writing notes by hand or typing? ... By hand, because it helps you remember. Then the important thing for you is comfort over long writing and reliability. This pen has a [cushioned grip] so your hand does not tire, and it [does not smudge], so notes stay clean. You mentioned price might be a concern. Think of it this way: if it lasts a year, it costs you less than a cup of tea a week, and it is the one tool you use in every meeting. Can I put your name down for one?

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Listing features without asking a single question.
  • Getting flustered or arguing when the interviewer says 'I don't need a pen'.
  • Using exaggerated claims like 'the best pen in the world'.
  • Never asking for the sale at the end.
  • Talking for too long without letting the buyer speak.

Likely follow-up questions

  • I don't need a pen. Now what?
  • It is too expensive. Convince me.
  • Now sell me this water bottle (or a term plan).
  • Why did you ask those questions first?

FAQs

What if the interviewer refuses to answer my questions?

Stay calm and make reasonable assumptions aloud, for example, 'Most managers sign several documents a day.' Then continue. The interviewer may be testing your composure. Do not argue or give up; adapt and move to a confident close.

Is it okay to be creative or funny?

A light touch is fine, but structure matters more. Many candidates try clever tricks they saw in films. Interviewers usually prefer a simple, needs-based conversation that shows you can sell real financial products to real customers.
