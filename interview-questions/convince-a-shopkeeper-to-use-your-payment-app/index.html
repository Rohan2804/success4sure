Convince a Shopkeeper to Use Your Payment App: Interview

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “How would you convince a kirana shop owner to start using our payment app or QR?”

Why interviewers ask this

Fintech companies, payment aggregators and banks hire large field sales teams to onboard merchants. This role-play tests whether you can sell to a busy small business owner who has little time and many suppliers asking for attention. Interviewers check whether you understand the merchant's real concerns: settlement speed, charges, sound box alerts, fraud, and the hassle of multiple QR codes. They also look for practical sales sense, like visiting at quiet hours, speaking the local language, and offering a quick setup. Honesty about charges and terms matters as much as persuasion.

How to answer

  1. Mention timing and approach: visit during a quiet hour, greet respectfully, and speak in the shopkeeper's language.
  2. Ask what he currently uses for payments and what problems he faces, such as fake payment screenshots, delayed settlement or customers without cash.
  3. Link your product's features to those problems, such as instant payment alerts through a sound box, next-day settlement or a simple app dashboard.
  4. Be transparent about any charges, rental or terms, and do not overpromise features you are not sure of.
  5. Offer a quick, easy start, such as same-day KYC and QR setup, and a follow-up visit to make sure it works.

Sample answers

Fresher (fintech field sales interview)

I would visit in the afternoon when the shop is less busy. I would greet him and ask, 'Bhaiya, do customers pay you through UPI these days?' Most say yes. Then I would ask if he has ever had a problem with fake payment screenshots or not hearing whether money came in when the shop is crowded. That is where our sound box helps; it announces every payment aloud. I would show him the app and how he can see the day's total at closing time. I would clearly tell him about any rental or charges so there is no surprise later. If he agrees, I can complete his KYC on the spot and set up the QR. I would come back in a week to check it is working.

Experienced (FMCG distributor sales moving to fintech)

From my FMCG experience, I know shopkeepers trust people who solve problems and come back. Before pitching, I would check what other payment QRs are already on the counter. If he has three, I would not ask him to remove them; I would show what extra benefit ours gives, such as [credit line for inventory, faster settlement, or loyalty features], if our company offers it. I would also mention that many shops on the same street have started using it, if that is true. Finally, I would leave my number and make sure his first settlement goes smoothly, because that one experience decides whether he recommends us.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Pitching features in English jargon to a busy shopkeeper.
  • Hiding rental charges or terms.
  • Visiting during peak hours and insisting on attention.
  • Badmouthing competitor apps.
  • Promising loans or credit features that the company may not offer him.

Likely follow-up questions

  • He says he already has three QR codes. What now?
  • He is worried about charges. How do you respond?
  • How many merchants can you onboard in a day?
  • How would you get him to actually use the app after onboarding?

FAQs

Do merchants pay charges on UPI?

Charges depend on the payment mode, the provider and current regulations. Person-to-merchant UPI has generally been free for most small merchants, but services like sound boxes may involve rental. Always check your company's current pricing and any NPCI or RBI updates.

Why do interviewers care about local language?

Small merchants often prefer their own language and trust people who speak it. Field sales success depends on rapport. Mentioning that you can pitch in the local language strengthens your answer considerably.
