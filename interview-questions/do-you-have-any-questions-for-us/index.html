Questions to Ask the Interviewer at the End of an Interview

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Do you have any questions for us?”

Why interviewers ask this

Saying 'No, everything is clear' wastes your last chance to make an impression. Interviewers use this moment to judge curiosity, seriousness and how well you listened. Good questions show you are evaluating the role as a career decision, not just accepting the first offer. They also reveal your priorities: questions about learning and performance land differently from questions only about leave and perks. A couple of thoughtful questions can turn the end of an interview into a real conversation and leave the panel with a sharper memory of you.

How to answer

  1. Prepare four or five questions in advance, so that you still have two left after some get answered during the interview.
  2. Ask about the work: what a typical day looks like, what success looks like in the first six months, how performance is measured.
  3. Ask about learning and the team: training, mentoring, who you would work with most closely.
  4. Keep salary, leave and perks for the HR or offer stage unless the interviewer brings them up.
  5. End by asking about next steps in the process, which is practical and shows interest.

Sample answers

Fresher (campus placement, private bank)

Yes, thank you. I wanted to ask what the first six months look like for a graduate trainee here. Is there a fixed training schedule before the branch posting? And what do your best trainees usually do differently in their first year? Lastly, could you tell me what the next step in the selection process is and roughly when we might hear back?

Experienced (interviewing for a credit manager role)

I have a couple of questions. How is the credit team structured here, and how much sanctioning authority does this role carry at the start? I would also like to understand the biggest challenge the team is facing this year, whether it is portfolio quality, turnaround time or something else. And how do you measure success for this role at the end of the first year?

Panel interview for a bank PO (when asked by the chairperson)

Thank you, sir. If I may ask one question: for a newly joined officer, which area would you suggest focusing on first to become useful to a branch quickly, whether it is credit, operations or customer service? I would like to start preparing for it in my own time.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying 'No questions' because you did not prepare.
  • Asking something already answered on the website or in the interview itself.
  • Opening with salary, leave or work-from-home questions in a first-round interview.
  • Asking too many questions when the panel is clearly short on time.

Likely follow-up questions

  • Is there anything else you would like us to know about you?
  • What would make you choose another offer over ours?

FAQs

How many questions should I ask at the end?

Two or three is usually right. Read the room: if the interview has run long or the panel looks rushed, ask one good question and thank them. If they seem relaxed and engaged, you can ask a little more. Quality matters more than the number of questions.

Should I ask questions in a government bank interview panel?

PSU bank panels do not always invite questions, and when they do, one short, respectful question is enough. Asking about the role, training or how to prepare for your first posting is appropriate. Avoid questions about pay scales, transfers or perks, which are publicly available or decided by policy.

More HR basics questions

  • Tell me about yourself.
  • Why should we hire you?
  • What are your strengths?
  • What is your greatest weakness?
  • Where do you see yourself in five years?
  • Why do you want to work for our company?
  • Why did you leave your last job?
  • What do you know about our company?
