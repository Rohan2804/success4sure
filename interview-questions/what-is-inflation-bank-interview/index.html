What Is Inflation? How to Answer in a Bank Interview

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “What is inflation and how does RBI control it?”

Why interviewers ask this

Inflation affects every bank customer: the pensioner whose savings lose value, the borrower whose EMI may change, and the farmer whose input costs rise. Panels in bank PO, RBI Grade B and NABARD interviews ask this to check whether you can explain an economic idea simply and connect it to policy. They also want to know if you understand RBI's role under the inflation targeting framework and the tools it uses. A candidate who explains inflation using a real household example, rather than only definitions, comes across as someone who can explain products and policy to customers too.

How to answer

  1. Define inflation plainly as a general rise in prices over time, which means the same money buys less.
  2. Use a household example, such as a monthly grocery bill that rises from ₹8,000 to ₹8,500 for the same items, which is roughly 6 percent inflation.
  3. Name the measure RBI targets, which is CPI inflation, and mention the target band set under the flexible inflation targeting framework (as of 2026 it is 4 percent with a 2 to 6 percent band; check the RBI website).
  4. Explain the tools: repo rate changes, liquidity management through operations like variable rate repo or reverse repo, and the CRR.
  5. Add one line on the limits of monetary policy, such as supply shocks in food or fuel that rate hikes cannot fix quickly.

Sample answers

Fresher (IBPS PO interview)

Inflation means prices of goods and services rise over time, so the purchasing power of money falls. For example, if my family spent ₹8,000 a month on groceries last year and the same items now cost ₹8,500, that is about 6 percent inflation for our basket. RBI tracks consumer price inflation, and under the inflation targeting framework the target is 4 percent with a band of 2 to 6 percent. To control inflation RBI mainly uses the repo rate. If it raises the rate, loans become costlier, spending slows and demand-driven price rise cools down. It can also manage liquidity in the system. But if inflation is due to vegetables or crude oil, the government's supply measures matter as much as RBI's action.

Postgraduate (RBI Grade B interview)

Inflation is a sustained rise in the general price level. It hurts people on fixed incomes most, like pensioners and daily wage workers, which is why price stability is RBI's primary objective under the amended RBI Act. The Monetary Policy Committee targets headline CPI inflation within a tolerance band. Its main instrument is the policy repo rate, supported by liquidity operations within the corridor formed by the Standing Deposit Facility and the Marginal Standing Facility. Transmission works through bank lending and deposit rates, asset prices and expectations. I would add that a good share of Indian inflation comes from food, which is driven by monsoon and supply chains, so coordination with fiscal measures like buffer stock releases is important.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Giving only a definition with no example or link to RBI.
  • Confusing WPI and CPI or saying RBI targets WPI.
  • Stating the current inflation figure without having checked it.
  • Claiming RBI can control all types of inflation through interest rates alone.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What is the difference between CPI and WPI?
  • What is core inflation?
  • What is stagflation?
  • How does inflation affect a fixed deposit holder?

FAQs

What is real interest rate and why does it matter?

Real interest rate is roughly the nominal rate minus inflation. If a fixed deposit pays 7 percent and inflation is 5 percent, the real return is about 2 percent. Mentioning this shows you can connect inflation to a depositor's actual gain, which panels appreciate.

Is some inflation good for the economy?

Mild, stable inflation is generally considered healthy because it encourages spending and investment, while deflation can make people postpone purchases. That is why RBI targets a moderate level rather than zero. Very high or volatile inflation, however, hurts savers and planning.
