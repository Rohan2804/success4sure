What Do You Know About Our Company? How to Prepare

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “What do you know about our company?”

Why interviewers ask this

This question tests preparation, which employers treat as a predictor of how you will handle real work. A candidate who has not read the company website before an interview is unlikely to read a credit policy carefully either. The panel also wants to know whether you understand the business you are joining: who the customers are, how the company earns, and where it is heading. A clear, accurate summary in your own words, with one sensible observation, makes a far better impression than a list of memorised facts.

How to answer

  1. Cover the basics: when and where it started, main business lines, key customer segments and approximate scale, such as number of branches or states.
  2. Add one recent development from news, a results presentation or the company's social media, such as a new product or market.
  3. Mention how your role fits into the business, for example how branch sales feed the bank's retail deposit growth.
  4. Finish with what interests you, turning facts into a reason for wanting the job.

Sample answers

Fresher (interview with a general insurance company)

From my research, you are one of the larger private general insurers in India, offering motor, health, home and commercial policies through agents, banks and your own website. I noticed that a big part of your recent growth has come from health insurance, and you have been promoting app-based claim intimation. I also read that you are expanding in tier-2 and tier-3 cities. For an underwriting trainee role, that means a lot of new customer profiles to assess, which is the part that interests me most.

Experienced (switching to a fintech lending company)

You are a digital lending platform that works with partner NBFCs to offer small personal and business loans, largely through your app, and your customer base is mostly young salaried borrowers and small merchants. From your last funding announcement and interviews with your founders, the focus now is on better collections and responsible lending under the RBI's digital lending guidelines. My experience in collections strategy at [company] fits directly into that, which is why I applied.

Fresher (applying to a public sector bank as a clerk or officer)

Your bank is one of the oldest public sector banks in the country, with a large branch network in [state] and a strong presence in agricultural and MSME lending. I know many families in my district who bank with you because there is a branch even in smaller towns. I have also seen your push towards digital services through the mobile app and UPI. I would like to be part of a bank that serves both rural and urban customers, and that reach is what draws me here.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Knowing nothing beyond the company name and logo.
  • Quoting outdated or wrong figures with confidence.
  • Reading a long list of numbers without any opinion or connection to the role.
  • Mentioning only negative news, such as a penalty or controversy, without context.

Likely follow-up questions

  • Who are our competitors and how are we different?
  • What do you think we should improve?
  • Have you used any of our products?

FAQs

Where can I find reliable information about a company?

Start with the company's own website, especially the investor relations section with annual reports and presentations. Then check recent news in business newspapers, the company's LinkedIn page and, for regulated entities, announcements on the RBI, SEBI or IRDAI websites. Use reviews by employees carefully, as they are individual opinions.

Should I mention a recent controversy about the company?

Only if it is directly relevant and you can discuss it neutrally. It is usually better to show awareness through positive or factual developments. If the interviewer raises a controversy, respond calmly and without judgement, and focus on how the company has responded.
