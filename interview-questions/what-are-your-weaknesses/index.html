What Is Your Greatest Weakness? Honest Answers That Work

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “What is your greatest weakness?”

Why interviewers ask this

No interviewer expects a perfect candidate. This question tests self-awareness and maturity: can you look at yourself honestly, and do you do anything about your shortcomings? It also checks whether you will be coachable once hired, because a person who cannot name a weakness usually finds feedback hard to accept. Panels in India hear 'I am a perfectionist' and 'I work too hard' many times a day, so those answers suggest you are either hiding something or did not prepare. A real, manageable weakness plus a clear improvement plan is what they want.

How to answer

  1. Pick a genuine weakness that is not a core requirement of the job. Poor public speaking is fine for an analyst, risky for a sales role.
  2. Describe it briefly and specifically, with a small example of when it caused a problem.
  3. Spend most of your answer on what you are doing about it: a course, a habit, a tool, or feedback you asked for.
  4. End with where you stand now, showing progress without claiming the weakness has vanished completely.

Sample answers

Fresher (BBA, applying for a customer service role)

I find it hard to speak up in large groups. In college I often had ideas in group discussions but waited too long and someone else said them first. In my final year I decided to work on it. I joined our college debate club and made it a rule to speak within the first five minutes of every discussion. I also volunteered to anchor one department event. I am still more comfortable one-on-one, but I no longer hold back in meetings, and handling customers individually actually suits me well.

Experienced (2 years as an operations executive)

My weakness was saying yes to every request, which meant I sometimes took on more than I could finish properly. Last year two reports went out late because I had agreed to help another team at the same time. Since then I check my current workload before committing, and if something will slip I tell my manager early and ask which task is the priority. I also use a simple task list in Excel every morning. My manager has said my deadlines are much more reliable now, though I still need to remind myself to say no politely.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying you have no weakness, or giving a disguised strength like 'I care too much about my work'.
  • Choosing a weakness that is central to the job, such as poor attention to detail for an audit role.
  • Confessing something serious, such as frequent absenteeism, without any improvement story.
  • Talking only about the problem and never about the fix.

Likely follow-up questions

  • How would we notice that weakness if we hired you?
  • What feedback have you received about it?
  • Tell me about another area you are working on.

FAQs

Is 'I am a perfectionist' a bad answer?

It is not wrong, but it has been used so often that most interviewers treat it as a dodge. If perfectionism really is your issue, make it concrete: say you spend too long polishing slides or reports, give an example where it delayed work, and explain how you now set time limits.

Can I mention a technical skill gap as a weakness?

Yes, and it is often a safe, honest choice, such as advanced Excel functions, SQL or a regional language. Make sure the skill is not essential on day one, and explain exactly how you are learning it, for example a course you have started or practice you do every week.
