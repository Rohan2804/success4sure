How Do You Handle Criticism? Answers With Real Examples

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “How do you handle criticism or negative feedback?”

Why interviewers ask this

Every new employee receives correction, especially in the first year. The interviewer wants to know whether you will listen, improve and come back stronger, or become defensive, demotivated or resentful. Coachability is one of the most important qualities employers look for in freshers and early-career staff. A good answer shows a mature attitude to feedback, an example of specific criticism, and the visible change you made. It also helps to show that you sometimes ask for feedback yourself instead of waiting for it.

How to answer

  1. Explain your general approach briefly: listen fully, ask questions to understand, avoid reacting immediately.
  2. Give a specific example of criticism you received, including what it was about.
  3. Describe the concrete action you took and how your work improved.
  4. Mention how you seek feedback proactively now.

Sample answers

Fresher (internship report)

During my internship, my manager returned my first project report with red marks on almost every page. She said it was too long and the main findings were hidden. I felt discouraged for a moment, but I asked her to show me an example of a report she liked. I rewrote mine with a one-page summary at the start and moved the details to an appendix. She approved the second version, and I now write every report with the summary first. I also ask for feedback on drafts early instead of waiting until the end.

Experienced (customer handling)

My branch manager once told me that I was too abrupt with elderly customers when the branch was busy. I did not realise it, and honestly my first reaction was to explain that I was trying to reduce the queue. But I thought about it and realised he was right. I started taking a few extra moments to explain things slowly, and I asked a colleague to tell me if she noticed it again. A few months later, two customers praised my patience in the feedback register.

Experienced (presentation skills)

In a quarterly review, my head of department said my slides had too much text. I took a short online course on presentations and asked a colleague to review my next deck. My next review was much better received, and I now keep each slide to one main message with the numbers in a table below it.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying you always accept criticism happily, without any example.
  • Describing criticism as unfair or blaming the person who gave it.
  • Choosing criticism about something trivial.
  • Not showing any actual change after the feedback.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What do you do if the criticism is unfair?
  • What was the last feedback you received?
  • How do you give feedback to others?

FAQs

What if I genuinely think the criticism is wrong?

Listen fully first, then ask questions to understand the specific concern. If you still disagree, explain your view calmly with facts. Sometimes the feedback has a valid point even if the delivery is poor. Show that you consider it seriously before deciding it is wrong.

How do I answer if I have not received much feedback?

Talk about feedback from teachers, project guides, coaches or internship supervisors. You can also describe how you seek feedback, for example asking a senior to review your work. Freshers who show they want feedback make a good impression.
