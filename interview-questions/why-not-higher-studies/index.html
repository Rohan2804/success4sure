Why Not Higher Studies? How Freshers Should Answer

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Why are you not going for higher studies?”

Why interviewers ask this

Interviewers in India ask this because many freshers take a job and leave within a year for an MBA, an M.Com, or study abroad. Training a new hire takes months, so the company wants to know whether you have thought through your decision. They also want to see whether your choice is positive (you want experience first) rather than negative (you could not get admission). A clear, honest answer that values work experience and shows any further study will be part-time or later reassures them that you are committed.

How to answer

  1. Give your positive reason for working now, such as gaining practical experience, financial independence or clarity about specialisation.
  2. If you plan to study later, say so honestly but put it two or three years out, and explain how work experience will help.
  3. Mention part-time learning you plan alongside the job, such as certifications or distance courses.
  4. If family finances are a factor, you can mention it briefly and without embarrassment.

Sample answers

B.Com fresher

I did think about doing an M.Com or MBA straight away, but I felt I would get much more out of it after some work experience. Right now I know the theory, but I do not know which area of finance I am best suited to. Working in operations at a bank will show me that. I also want to support my family financially, since my father retires next year. If I do study further, it will likely be part-time while I keep working.

Engineering fresher (asked about an MBA)

Many of my friends are preparing for CAT, and I considered it. But I think an MBA is most valuable when you can connect classroom concepts with real experience. I would like to work for at least three to four years first, and in the meantime build my finance knowledge through NISM certifications and on-the-job learning. If an MBA makes sense later, an executive or part-time programme would let me continue working.

Fresher with a postgraduate offer declined

I did get admission to an M.Sc programme, but I declined it after thinking carefully. I realised I enjoy applying ideas more than researching them, and an analyst job lets me do that every day. I may consider a part-time master's degree later, once I know which area I want to specialise in, but for now I want to give this role my full attention.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying you could not get admission anywhere, without any positive reason.
  • Saying you will start an MBA next year, which signals you will leave soon.
  • Dismissing higher education as useless.
  • Giving a vague answer like 'not decided yet'.

Likely follow-up questions

  • Are you preparing for CAT or GMAT right now?
  • Would you consider a part-time MBA?
  • What if your friends all go for higher studies?

FAQs

Should I tell the interviewer if I plan an MBA in two years?

Be honest if asked directly, but frame it carefully: say it is a possibility after you have built solid experience, and that you would prefer options that let you keep working. Volunteering a firm plan to leave soon can reduce your chances, especially in roles with long training.

Is it better for my career to work before an MBA?

Many people find work experience makes an MBA more useful and helps them choose a specialisation. Some programmes also prefer candidates with experience. It depends on your goals and situation; this is a personal decision, so speak to mentors and seniors before committing.
