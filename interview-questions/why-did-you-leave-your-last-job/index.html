Why Did You Leave Your Last Job? Safe, Honest Answers

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Why did you leave your last job?”

Why interviewers ask this

Employers want to know whether the reasons you left your last job will make you leave this one too. They are also listening for warning signs: conflict with managers, performance trouble, or a habit of blaming others. Done well, this answer shows you make career decisions thoughtfully and speak professionally about former employers. Since background verification is common in Indian finance companies, the panel may also cross-check what you say with your previous HR, so consistency and honesty matter as much as polish.

How to answer

  1. Give the real reason in one or two neutral sentences: growth, learning, relocation, a layoff, a family need or a contract ending.
  2. Avoid criticising your old manager or company, even if your complaint is fair.
  3. Shift quickly to what you are looking for now and why this role offers it.
  4. If you were let go or laid off, say so plainly and mention what you did afterwards, such as upskilling or freelancing.

Sample answers

Experienced (left for growth after 3 years in a small NBFC)

I spent three good years at [NBFC], where I learnt gold loan and two-wheeler loan processing from the ground up. The company is small, and over the last year my work had become repetitive with limited scope to move into credit assessment, which is what I want to do next. I discussed this with my manager, but there were no openings in that team. So I decided to look for a larger organisation with a proper credit function, and this role is exactly that move.

Laid off (sales role, company restructured)

My previous company, [company], closed its regional sales office in [city] last year as part of a restructuring, and my role was one of several that were made redundant. It was not related to performance; I was meeting my targets. Since then I have used the time to complete the IRDAI [certification] and to do short-term work with a local insurance agency. I am looking for a stable role where I can build long-term client relationships, which is why this position appeals to me.

Relocation (moved cities after marriage)

I was working as an accounts executive in Pune, and I moved to Hyderabad after my marriage earlier this year. My previous employer did not have an office here, so I resigned on good terms, and my manager has agreed to be a reference. I am now settled here for the long term and keen to continue in finance and accounts with an organisation that can use my experience in GST filing and reconciliations.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Blaming a 'toxic' boss or 'useless' company, which makes you look hard to manage.
  • Giving a story that does not match your relieving letter or what your former HR will say.
  • Saying only 'for better salary', which suggests you will leave again for a small raise.
  • Over-explaining with a long, emotional account.

Likely follow-up questions

  • Did you have another offer before you resigned?
  • What would your last manager say about you?
  • What did you not like about your last role?

FAQs

Should I tell the truth if I was fired?

Yes, but keep it short and factual, take responsibility for your part, and focus on what you learnt and changed. Lying is risky because background checks and reference calls are common in banks and NBFCs. Many employers will consider you if you show you have learnt from it.

What if I left because of a bad manager?

Do not lead with that. Frame it around what you need now, such as a team with clear processes or more structured feedback. If pressed, you can say the working style was not a good fit and describe it neutrally, without names or personal comments.
