Insider Information Scenario: Finance Interview Answer

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “A friend at a listed company tells you about an upcoming merger before it is announced. What do you do?”

Why interviewers ask this

Broking firms, AMCs, research houses and investment banks handle confidential and price-sensitive information daily, and SEBI's Prohibition of Insider Trading Regulations apply strictly. Interviewers ask this to see whether you recognise unpublished price-sensitive information, understand that trading on it or passing it on is prohibited, and know how to respond inside your firm. They also want to see that you will not try to be clever, for example by having a relative trade instead. Firms face heavy penalties for employee misconduct, so a clear, firm answer is essential.

How to answer

  1. Identify the information as unpublished price-sensitive information, since a merger not yet announced can clearly affect the share price.
  2. State plainly that you would not trade in the stock, and would not tell anyone else, including family and friends, or let them trade.
  3. Say you would inform your firm's compliance team as per its code of conduct, especially if the company is on your firm's restricted or watch list.
  4. Mention telling your friend politely that he should not share such information, since it puts both of you at risk.
  5. Show you understand the consequences: SEBI penalties, prosecution, job loss and damage to the firm's reputation.

Sample answers

Fresher (equity research trainee interview)

This is unpublished price-sensitive information, because a merger can move the share price sharply once it is announced. Under SEBI's insider trading regulations, trading on it or passing it on is prohibited, so I would not buy or sell that stock, and I would not mention it to anyone, not even my family. I would also tell my friend that he should not have shared it and that it could get both of us in trouble. Since I work in a research or broking firm, I would inform my compliance officer, because the firm may need to restrict trading in that stock or check whether any of our work is affected. I would rather lose a possible profit than my career.

Experienced (dealer at a broking firm)

In our firm we have a clear process: any UPSI that reaches us by any route is reported to compliance immediately, and the stock may be added to a restricted list. So I would not trade, not tip anyone, and I would report it. I have also learnt to stop such conversations early. Once a client hinted at 'news' about a company where his relative worked; I told him I could not discuss it and recorded the interaction. Regulators look at trading patterns closely, and even innocent-looking trades by relatives can attract investigation.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying you would trade a small amount because it is harmless.
  • Suggesting a relative or friend could trade instead of you.
  • Thinking it is fine as long as you do not tell anyone at work.
  • Not knowing the term UPSI or the role of compliance.

FAQs

What does UPSI mean?

Unpublished Price Sensitive Information is information about a company or its securities that is not generally available and is likely to materially affect the price when it becomes public, such as financial results, mergers, dividends or major changes in management.

What is front-running?

Front-running is trading ahead of a large client or fund order using knowledge of that order, to profit from the expected price movement. SEBI treats it as a fraudulent and unfair trade practice. Employees of brokers and AMCs must be especially careful about it.
