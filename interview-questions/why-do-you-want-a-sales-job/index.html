Why Do You Want a Sales Job? Answers for Finance Sales

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Why do you want to work in sales?”

Why interviewers ask this

Many candidates apply for sales roles because they are the most available jobs in financial services, not because they want to sell. Interviewers know this and ask the question to find people who will stay through the tough first months. They listen for genuine reasons: enjoying conversations, liking measurable results, wanting fast growth, or wanting to help customers make good decisions. Incentives are a fair reason but should not be the only one. The panel also checks whether you understand what the job involves, such as field travel, rejection, targets and paperwork.

How to answer

  1. Give one or two genuine reasons with evidence, such as enjoying persuading people, liking clear targets, or past experiences in college events or a family business.
  2. Show that you understand the job's realities: frequent rejection, monthly targets, field travel, repeated follow-ups and documentation.
  3. Connect sales to the financial product you will sell, explaining why helping people with insurance, loans or investments interests you.
  4. Mention earnings and incentive-linked growth honestly but briefly, as one motivation among several rather than the main one.
  5. Close with how you see your sales career developing over a few years, such as moving to team leader or relationship manager.

Sample answers

Fresher (BBA, applying for bank sales executive)

I have always enjoyed talking to people and convincing them. In college I handled sponsorships for our fest and got [number] sponsors, and I liked the challenge of turning a no into a yes. Sales is also one of the few jobs where your results are clear every month, and that motivates me. I chose financial products because they actually matter in people's lives; a good loan or insurance decision can help a family a lot. I know the job involves a lot of calls, rejection and field visits, and I am ready for that. The incentives are attractive too, but what I really want is to become someone customers trust and to grow into a relationship manager role.

Career switcher (from back-office operations to insurance sales)

I have spent two years in operations processing policy documents, and I kept noticing that the customers who understood their policies well were the ones handled by good advisors. I want to be that person. Operations taught me product details and paperwork accuracy, which many new salespeople struggle with. I also want a role where my effort directly shows in my results. I understand there will be months with low numbers, but I am prepared to build slowly through referrals and service.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying you applied because you could not find another job.
  • Talking only about incentives and money.
  • Describing yourself as 'a people person' with no evidence.
  • Underestimating the hard parts of the job.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What do you think is the hardest part of a sales job?
  • Sell me this product.
  • How much do you expect to earn through incentives?
  • Where do you see yourself in three years in sales?

FAQs

Is it okay to say money motivates me?

Yes, sales is one of the few roles where earnings depend on performance, and interviewers know that. Just do not make it your only reason. Pair it with enjoying customer interaction and achieving clear goals.

I am an introvert. Can I still do well in sales?

Many successful salespeople are introverts who listen well and prepare thoroughly. You can mention that you are a good listener and organised with follow-ups. Interviewers value consistency and trust more than loudness.

More Sales roles questions

  • Sell me this pen.
  • How do you handle rejection in sales?
  • Are you comfortable working with sales targets?
  • How would you approach a cold call to a customer who has never heard of us?
  • How would you cross-sell to an existing customer without being pushy?
  • Sell me a term insurance policy.
  • What do you understand about the role of a relationship manager?
  • What will you do if you miss your monthly sales target?
