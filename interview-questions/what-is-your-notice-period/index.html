What Is Your Notice Period? How to Answer in Interview

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “What is your notice period?”

Why interviewers ask this

Notice periods in India commonly range from 30 to 90 days, and longer notice can be a real obstacle when an employer needs someone soon. The interviewer is checking your earliest realistic joining date, whether you can negotiate an early release, and whether you will honour commitments to your current employer. They also listen for honesty: candidates who promise immediate joining and then cannot get released create problems. A clear, accurate answer with any flexibility you genuinely have helps the hiring manager plan.

How to answer

  1. State your contractual notice period exactly, as written in your appointment letter or HR policy.
  2. Mention any flexibility: leave balance you can adjust, a buyout option or the possibility of early release.
  3. Give a realistic earliest joining date and avoid overpromising.
  4. Show professionalism by mentioning that you will complete a proper handover.

Sample answers

Experienced (90-day notice period)

My notice period is 90 days, as per my appointment letter. My company does allow early release in some cases if the handover is complete, and I also have about fifteen days of leave balance that may be adjustable, subject to approval. Realistically, I could join in about 60 to 75 days. If you need me sooner, I would be open to discussing a buyout, but I would want to complete a proper handover first.

Experienced (30-day notice period)

My notice period is 30 days. Once I receive your offer letter, I will resign the same week. I expect to finish my handover in that period, so I can join within about a month. I do not anticipate any delay, since my manager is aware I am looking for a larger role. I will prepare a handover note for my pending files during the notice period so that my team is not left with gaps when I leave.

Serving notice already

I have already resigned, and I am serving my notice period. My last working day is [date], so I can join from [date]. My relieving letter will be issued on my last day, and I can share it with you then. I have already handed over most of my files to my replacement, so there is no risk of my current employer extending my last date.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying you can join immediately when your notice period is 90 days.
  • Suggesting you might leave without serving notice.
  • Not knowing your own notice period or buyout policy.
  • Overcommitting to an early date without checking with your employer.

Likely follow-up questions

  • Can you negotiate an early release?
  • Will your current company make a counter-offer?
  • Would you consider a notice buyout?

FAQs

What is a notice period buyout?

A buyout means paying your current employer, or having your new employer pay, salary for the remaining notice days so you can leave early. Whether it is allowed depends on your employer's policy and your contract. Check with HR and get any arrangement in writing.

Can I leave without serving my notice period?

Leaving without serving notice or settling it can lead to withheld dues, problems with your relieving letter and issues in background checks. It also damages your professional reputation. Always discuss options like early release or buyout with your employer instead.
