How Do You Approach Cold Calling? Sales Interview Answer

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “How would you approach a cold call to a customer who has never heard of us?”

Why interviewers ask this

Telesales and field sales in insurance, credit cards, personal loans and fintech often begin with cold calls. Interviewers want to know if you can open a conversation politely, earn a minute of attention, ask good questions and handle a quick no without being rude. They also check whether you know the rules: calling only permitted numbers, respecting Do Not Disturb preferences under TRAI rules, identifying yourself and your company clearly, and never misrepresenting the product. Many interviews include a live mock call, so a structured approach you can actually deliver is better than theory.

How to answer

  1. Prepare before calling: know who the customer is likely to be, the one problem your product solves, and what you want from the call, such as a meeting.
  2. Open with your name, company and reason in one or two sentences, then ask permission to continue.
  3. Ask one or two short questions to understand the customer's situation, rather than reading a long script.
  4. Offer a specific next step, such as sending details on WhatsApp or a short meeting, and confirm a time.
  5. Respect a no politely, follow DND and consent rules, and log the call outcome for follow-up.

Sample answers

Fresher (fintech telesales interview, live mock call)

Good afternoon, am I speaking with Mr Sharma? My name is [name] and I am calling from [company], a [payments or lending] app. I will take just one minute; is this a good time? ... Thank you. We help small shop owners accept digital payments and get settlements the next morning. May I ask, do you currently accept UPI at your shop? ... And do you sometimes face delays in getting the money in your account? ... I understand. Many shop owners in your area use our QR with a [settlement feature]. If it is convenient, I can send you the details on WhatsApp and our field executive can visit on Thursday at 11. Would that work?

Experienced (credit card sales moving to insurance telesales)

I believe the first ten seconds decide the call. I always state my name, company and purpose clearly, because customers get many fraud calls and they need to know who I am. I then ask permission. If they say they are busy, I ask for a better time instead of continuing. My goal is not to sell on the first call but to qualify and set a next step. I keep notes in the CRM on what they said, so the next call feels personal. I also never pressure anyone who says they are not interested, and I check DND status as per our process.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Reading a long script without pausing for the customer.
  • Not identifying yourself and your company clearly.
  • Pushing ahead when the customer says they are busy.
  • Making exaggerated claims like 'zero charges forever' without being sure.
  • Ignoring DND preferences and consent requirements.

Likely follow-up questions

  • The customer says 'I am not interested'. What next?
  • How many calls can you make in a day?
  • What is the DND registry?
  • How do you sound different from fraud callers?

FAQs

What is the DND registry and why does it matter?

The National Customer Preference Register lets telecom subscribers block unsolicited commercial calls and messages in chosen categories. Companies must follow TRAI rules on commercial communication. Mention that you would follow your company's compliance process on calling lists and consent.

How long should my opening be?

Ten to fifteen seconds: name, company, reason and a permission question. Customers decide quickly whether to listen. A short, respectful opening earns more time than a long pitch delivered too fast.
