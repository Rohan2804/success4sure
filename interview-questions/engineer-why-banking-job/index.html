Engineer, Why Banking? How to Answer in a Bank Interview

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “You are an engineer. Why do you want a banking job?”

Why interviewers ask this

A large share of bank PO and clerk candidates are engineers, and panels worry they will leave for a tech job or feel misplaced in branch work. The question checks three things: whether your switch is deliberate, whether you will stay, and whether your engineering skills add value. A defensive answer ('there are no jobs in my field') signals that banking is a fallback. A good answer shows a positive pull toward banking, connects analytical or technical skills to banking needs like digital channels, data and credit analysis, and accepts the non-technical nature of branch work honestly.

How to answer

  1. Acknowledge the switch directly and calmly; do not sound apologetic or defensive about your engineering degree or your marks.
  2. Give a positive reason for banking, such as interest in finance, customer interaction, or the social role of banks, ideally with a personal trigger.
  3. Show how engineering helps: problem-solving, comfort with numbers and technology, process thinking, and understanding digital systems like UPI and core banking.
  4. Address the stay question: say you understand branch work is largely operations and customers, and that you see a long career here.
  5. Keep the answer forward-looking and avoid criticising the engineering job market, your college or your earlier employer.

Sample answers

Mechanical engineering graduate (IBPS PO interview)

I did enjoy engineering, but during college I found myself more interested in the cost and finance side of projects than in design. I took an elective in engineering economics and later started reading about how banks evaluate a project loan, things like cash flows and repayment capacity. Banking lets me use that analytical side while working with people every day, which I enjoy more than working only with machines. I know a PO's early years are mostly branch operations and customer service, and I am ready for that. Later, my technical background could help in areas like MSME credit for manufacturing units, where understanding the machinery and process helps in appraisal.

Computer science graduate with one year in IT (SBI PO interview)

I worked for a year as a [role] at [IT company], and I was on a project for a banking client. That was my first real exposure to banking, and I found the business side more interesting than the code. I want a career where I deal directly with customers and decisions, not just with systems. At the same time, banks are becoming technology companies in many ways, with mobile banking, digital lending and fraud monitoring, so my background will be useful. I have thought about this carefully; I am not looking at banking as a temporary option.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying you could not get an engineering job or that the IT sector is unstable.
  • Claiming you will join the bank's IT department when you applied for a generalist post.
  • Having no answer for 'Then why did you do engineering?'
  • Overloading the answer with technical jargon.

Likely follow-up questions

  • Then why did you choose engineering in the first place?
  • Why should we not consider you a flight risk?
  • How can technology reduce NPAs?
  • Explain how UPI works in simple words.

FAQs

Should I apply for IT Officer instead of PO?

Only if you actually want a technical role. IT Officer is a specialist post with different work. If you apply for PO, be clear in the interview that you want generalist banking. Mixing the two reasons in one answer can confuse the panel.

How do I answer 'Why did you study engineering then?'

Be honest and brief. Many people choose engineering at 17 because of family advice or peer trends. Say what you gained from it, such as analytical thinking, and that during college you discovered your interest in finance. Panels accept honest, reflective answers.

More Banking jobs questions

  • Why do you want to join the banking sector?
  • Why do you want to work in a public sector bank and not a private bank?
  • What is an NPA? Explain it in your own words.
  • What is the repo rate and how does it affect a bank's customers?
  • What is inflation and how does RBI control it?
  • A customer is shouting at your counter because his work is delayed. What will you do?
  • Are you ready to work in a rural branch or a remote area?
  • What is financial inclusion and why is it important for India?
