Client Asks for a Stock Tip: Broking and RM Interview Answer

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “A client asks you which stock he should buy today. What do you say?”

Why interviewers ask this

In broking firms, dealers, relationship managers and support staff are asked for 'tips' all the time. SEBI requires that research recommendations come from registered research analysts and advice from registered investment advisers, and firms have strict policies on what employees can say. Interviewers ask this to see whether you know these boundaries and can still be helpful to the client. They also check whether you will avoid the temptation to impress clients with personal opinions. A good answer is polite, firm about the rules, and redirects the client to approved research and a goal-based approach.

How to answer

  1. Say politely that you cannot give personal stock tips, explaining that recommendations must come from registered research or advisory functions as per SEBI rules and firm policy.
  2. Offer what you can provide: the firm's published research reports, tools, educational material, or a connection to the advisory desk if the firm has one.
  3. Ask about the client's goal and risk appetite, and remind him gently not to act on unverified tips from social media or groups.
  4. If the client places an order on his own decision, execute it properly and record that it was client-initiated.
  5. Keep the tone warm and friendly throughout, so the client feels helped rather than brushed off or lectured.

Sample answers

Fresher (dealer role at a broking firm)

I would say, 'Sir, I understand you want a good idea, but as a dealer I am not allowed to give personal stock recommendations. SEBI rules say research and advice must come from registered research analysts or advisers.' Then I would tell him that our research team publishes reports, and I can share the latest ones through the approved channel. I would also ask if he is investing for long term or trading, because that changes which reports are relevant. If he decides to buy something on his own, I would place the order correctly and make sure it is recorded as his decision. I would not share my personal opinion, even casually.

Experienced (relationship manager at a full-service broker)

Clients ask me this daily, especially when markets are hot. I always say I cannot tip but I can help them use our research properly. I send the research desk's report with the rationale and risks, not just the target price. For clients who want continuous guidance, I explain our advisory or PMS options if they fit the client's profile. I also warn them about stock tips circulating on messaging apps, because SEBI has acted against several such operators. Most clients respect the clarity.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Giving a stock name casually to please the client.
  • Sharing tips from social media or friends.
  • Being rude or dismissive about the client's request.
  • Not knowing that research recommendations have regulatory requirements.

FAQs

Can a broker's employee ever give an opinion on a stock?

Firms typically allow sharing approved research and educational content but restrict personal recommendations. Rules depend on registration and role. Follow your firm's compliance policy, and when unsure, share only approved research with its disclosures.

What is a pump and dump scheme?

It is a manipulation where operators push a stock's price up through misleading tips, often on social media or messaging groups, then sell their holdings to unsuspecting buyers, causing the price to crash. SEBI has taken enforcement action against such schemes.
