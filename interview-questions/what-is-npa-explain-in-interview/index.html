What Is an NPA? How to Explain It in a Bank Interview

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “What is an NPA? Explain it in your own words.”

Why interviewers ask this

NPA is one of the most frequently asked technical questions in bank PO, SO and RBI Assistant interviews. The panel is not testing whether you memorised the definition; they want to see if you understand why a bad loan matters to a bank and to the economy. A good answer explains the concept in plain words, gives the basic classification idea, and links it to profitability, provisioning and the bank's ability to lend more. Panels often push further into recovery tools such as SARFAESI or IBC, so a clear base answer helps you handle those follow-ups calmly.

How to answer

  1. Give a plain definition first: a loan or advance becomes non-performing when the borrower stops paying interest or instalments for a set period, generally 90 days for most term loans.
  2. Use a simple example with numbers, such as a ₹5 lakh business loan where the EMI has not been paid for over three months.
  3. Explain why it matters: the bank stops earning interest on it, must set aside money as provisions, and has less capital available to lend.
  4. Mention the broad categories, substandard, doubtful and loss, without getting lost in exact timelines unless asked.
  5. End with how banks deal with NPAs, such as early follow-up, restructuring where allowed, SARFAESI action or resolution under the IBC.

Sample answers

Fresher (B.A. Economics, IBPS PO interview)

An NPA is a loan that has stopped earning money for the bank. Suppose a shopkeeper takes a ₹5 lakh loan and stops paying his instalments. Once the dues remain unpaid beyond the period set by RBI, which is generally 90 days for most loans, the bank has to classify it as a non-performing asset. After that, the bank cannot book that interest as income, and it has to keep aside part of its profit as a provision in case the money never comes back. So NPAs reduce profit and also reduce the money available for fresh lending. That is why branches focus heavily on early follow-up, before an account slips into NPA.

Candidate with commerce background (RBI Assistant interview)

In simple terms, an NPA is a loan where the borrower has stopped servicing it. RBI's norms say that if interest or principal is overdue for more than 90 days, the account becomes non-performing. Then it moves through stages: first substandard, then doubtful, and finally loss, with higher provisioning at each stage. High NPAs weaken a bank's balance sheet and capital, and if they are widespread, banks become cautious about lending, which slows growth in the economy. For recovery, banks use tools like the SARFAESI Act to take possession of secured assets, Lok Adalats and DRTs for smaller and larger cases, and the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code for corporate borrowers.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Reciting a textbook definition with no example or explanation of impact.
  • Getting the 90-day concept wrong or confusing NPA with a written-off loan.
  • Quoting the current gross NPA ratio of the banking system from memory when you are not sure of the figure.
  • Blaming all NPAs on corruption or wilful defaulters; many arise from genuine business failure.

FAQs

Should I quote the latest NPA figures?

Only if you have read them recently from a reliable source such as the RBI Financial Stability Report and are confident. Otherwise talk about the trend in general terms. A wrong number can lead to tough follow-up questions, while a clear concept explanation never hurts you.

What is the difference between gross and net NPA?

Gross NPA is the total value of loans classified as non-performing. Net NPA is gross NPA minus the provisions the bank has already set aside against them. Net NPA shows the part of bad loans that is not yet covered by provisions.
