Manager Pushes You to Mis-Sell: Ethics Interview Answer

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Your manager asks you to push a high-commission product that does not suit the client. What will you do?”

Why interviewers ask this

Mis-selling of insurance, ULIPs, structured products and high-commission funds has been a recurring problem in Indian financial services, and regulators have tightened rules repeatedly. Employers now face penalties, complaints and reputational damage from it, so many interviewers deliberately ask this ethics scenario. They want to know whether you will protect the client, while also staying professional with your manager and understanding business pressure. Extreme answers in either direction, such as 'I will follow the manager' or 'I will report him to SEBI immediately', both worry panels. A measured answer that shows integrity, judgement and correct escalation wins.

How to answer

  1. State your principle: you will not recommend a product that does not fit the client's needs, even under target pressure.
  2. Describe talking to the manager first, privately and respectfully, explaining the specific suitability concern with facts about the client.
  3. Offer a constructive alternative, such as a suitable product for this client and other clients for whom the target product genuinely fits.
  4. If the pressure continues or turns into instructions to misrepresent, use the formal channel such as compliance or the whistle-blower policy.
  5. Show you understand the business side: targets are real, but complaints, refunds and regulatory penalties cost far more than one sale.

Sample answers

Fresher (bancassurance officer interview)

I would not sell it to that client. But I would also not create a confrontation. I would speak to my manager privately and say, 'Sir, this customer is 62, needs regular income and has no other savings. A long-term product with a high premium will lock his money and he may not be able to continue.' I would suggest a product that suits him and also tell my manager that I will focus on finding customers for whom the target product is actually right, like younger salaried clients with long-term goals. If the manager still insisted that I mis-sell, I would raise it with compliance through the proper channel. I want to meet targets, but in a way I can defend if the customer complains later.

Experienced (MF distributor moving to an AMC)

I faced something similar when a new fund offer had higher incentives and we were asked to switch clients into it. Two of my clients were in well-performing funds with exit loads still applicable, so switching would have cost them money for no clear benefit. I explained this to my team leader with the numbers. He agreed to let me skip those clients, and I offered the NFO only to new investors where the category fitted. It did not hurt my relationship with him; if anything, he trusted my judgement more.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying you would follow the manager because targets matter.
  • Jumping straight to the regulator without trying internal channels.
  • Criticising your manager or organisation in an emotional way.
  • Ignoring the business reality of targets completely.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What if you lose your incentive because of this?
  • What is the difference between cross-selling and mis-selling?
  • What does your company's whistle-blower policy mean to you?
  • What if the client himself insists on that product?

FAQs

Will this answer make me look like I cannot handle targets?

Not if you show you are target-oriented. Mention that you will find the right customers for the product and that you understand the business. Panels want honest sellers, not people who avoid selling.

What is churning, and is it related?

Churning means moving a client between products or schemes mainly to earn fresh commission. It is a common form of mis-selling. Regulators and AMFI's code of conduct discourage it. Mentioning it shows awareness of practical ethics issues.
