How to Answer 'Why Should We Hire You?' (Sample Answers)

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Why should we hire you?”

Why interviewers ask this

By the time this question comes, the interviewer has usually formed a view. They want to see whether you can summarise your own case in a convincing, evidence-backed way, the same way you would later pitch an idea to a manager or a product to a customer. They are also checking self-awareness: do you understand what the role actually needs, or are you giving the same answer you gave at five other companies? Confidence without arrogance, and specifics instead of adjectives, are what separate strong answers.

How to answer

  1. Re-read the job description before the interview and pick the two or three needs that matter most, for example accuracy, customer handling or target achievement.
  2. Match each need with one proof from your background, such as a project, an internship task, a certification or a result at your current job.
  3. Add one thing that makes you a slightly different hire, such as a regional language, comfort with field work or a strong Excel skill.
  4. Close with a short line about commitment or fit, showing you want this specific role and not just any job.

Sample answers

Fresher (MBA Finance, applying to an AMC as a sales trainee)

From what I understood of the role, you need someone who can explain mutual funds simply to distributors and investors, and who is comfortable travelling and following up. During my MBA I cleared the NISM Series V-A exam, so I am not starting from zero on products and regulations. In my summer internship at [company], I called around [number] prospective investors and learnt how to handle objections politely. I also speak Marathi and Hindi fluently, which should help in this territory. I know I still have a lot to learn, but I am ready for the field work this role demands and I want to build my career in asset management.

Career switcher (2 years in IT support, applying for a bank operations role)

This role needs accuracy, patience with processes and the ability to handle a high volume of requests without errors. That has been my daily work for two years in IT support at [company], where I handled [number] tickets a month against service timelines and documented every fix. I am used to following checklists and escalating correctly when something is outside my authority, which I believe matters a lot in banking operations. I have also completed [course or JAIIB preparation] to learn the banking side. So you would get someone who already has the discipline and is serious about building domain knowledge.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Listing generic adjectives like hardworking, honest and passionate without any proof.
  • Comparing yourself with other candidates or claiming you are the best person they will meet.
  • Talking only about what you want from the company, not what you bring to it.
  • Giving an answer so long that the interviewer loses the main point.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What if we hire someone with more experience?
  • What is one skill you still need to build for this role?
  • How soon could you be productive here?

FAQs

Can a fresher answer this without work experience?

Yes. Use projects, internships, part-time work, college responsibilities and certifications as your evidence. A fresher who shows they understood the role and has prepared for it is often more convincing than one who just claims to be a fast learner. Be honest that you will need training, and show how you learn.

How is this different from 'What are your strengths?'

Strengths are about you in general. 'Why should we hire you' is about the match between you and this particular job. So the same strength, say accuracy, should be framed around the role's need, such as error-free KYC checks, and backed by an example the interviewer can relate to.
