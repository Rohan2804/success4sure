What Are Your Short-Term Goals? Answers for Freshers

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “What are your short-term career goals?”

Why interviewers ask this

Short-term goals tell the interviewer what you will focus on in the first year or two, which is exactly the period when the company is investing in your training. They want goals that are realistic, tied to the role and within your control, such as mastering a process or clearing a certification. Goals that point away from the job, like cracking a government exam or leaving for an MBA, worry them. Well-chosen goals also show that you understand what the role actually involves day to day.

How to answer

  1. Limit the horizon to one or two years and name two or three goals at most.
  2. Make at least one goal about mastering the core work of the role, such as learning credit appraisal or the branch's operations.
  3. Add one learning goal that supports the job, such as a NISM module, JAIIB or an Excel or SQL skill.
  4. Keep goals measurable where possible, and show how they lead to your longer-term direction.

Sample answers

Fresher (graduate trainee at a private bank)

In the first year, my main goal is to understand branch banking thoroughly: account opening, KYC, cash and clearing operations, and the basics of retail loans. I want to reach the point where my manager can trust me to handle a counter or a set of customers independently. Alongside that, I plan to clear the NISM Series V-A exam so I can support mutual fund sales at the branch. In about two years, I would like to move into a role with some sales or credit responsibility.

Fresher (MBA, equity research associate)

My first goal is to become fully comfortable with your research process: building and updating financial models, writing results updates and tracking the companies in my sector. I would like to cover two or three companies end to end within the first year, with my senior's review. I am also preparing for the NISM Research Analyst certification. By the end of two years, I hope to contribute ideas in team discussions, not just data.

Fresher (B.Com, insurance operations)

In the next year I want to learn policy servicing and claims documentation thoroughly and reduce errors in my work to almost none. I also want to clear the relevant IRDAI exam so that I understand products better. After that, I would like to move towards underwriting, which interests me most.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Naming goals that are not connected to this job at all.
  • Mentioning a promotion within six months, which sounds unrealistic.
  • Giving only vague goals like 'learn and grow'.
  • Listing too many goals, which makes none of them seem serious.

Likely follow-up questions

  • How will you measure whether you met these goals?
  • What will you do if you do not get the role you want after two years?
  • What is your long-term goal?

FAQs

How are short-term goals different from the five-year question?

Short-term goals are about the next one or two years and should focus on learning the job and building skills. The five-year answer is about direction and responsibility. Your two answers should connect, with the short-term goals forming the first steps towards the longer-term one.

Is it okay to mention certifications as goals?

Yes, as long as they support the role. NISM modules for securities and mutual fund roles, IRDAI exams for insurance, or JAIIB for banking are all relevant. Mention them as part of doing your job better, not as a way to move elsewhere.
