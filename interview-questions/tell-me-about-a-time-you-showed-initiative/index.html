Tell Me About a Time You Showed Initiative (Examples)

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Tell me about a time you showed initiative.”

Why interviewers ask this

Employers value people who spot problems or opportunities and act on them without waiting for instructions. This question tests whether you go beyond your job description, how you judge when to act on your own, and whether your initiatives create real value. The interviewer also checks that you respect boundaries: in regulated areas like banking, acting alone without approval can be dangerous. The best answers show you noticed something, took sensible action within your authority, involved the right people, and made a measurable difference.

How to answer

  1. Choose a situation where you were not asked to act but saw a need.
  2. Explain what you noticed and why it mattered.
  3. Describe what you did, including how you involved your manager or team where approval was needed.
  4. Share the result, with numbers if possible, and whether the change continued.

Sample answers

Experienced (bank branch)

At our branch, customers often waited in the main queue just to update their KYC documents, which took a few minutes each. I suggested to my manager that we set up a separate help desk for KYC updates for two hours every morning. He agreed, and I trained one of the newer staff to handle it with me. The main queue became shorter, and pending KYC updates at our branch went down noticeably over the next quarter. The arrangement is still in use.

Fresher (internship)

During my internship at a mutual fund distributor, I noticed the team was manually tracking SIP renewals in a long Excel file and sometimes missed follow-ups. In my free time, I built a simple dashboard with conditional formatting that highlighted SIPs due in the next fifteen days. I showed it to my supervisor, who started using it for the team's weekly review. It was a small thing, but it helped them call clients before mandates lapsed.

Fresher (college)

Our college placement cell had no mock interview programme. I spoke to the placement officer and three alumni working in banks, and organised a weekend mock interview session for forty students. It became a regular event the next year. I had no official role in the placement cell, so I made sure the placement officer approved every step before I invited anyone.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Describing an initiative that broke rules or bypassed approval.
  • Choosing something that was actually part of your assigned job.
  • Being unable to show any result from your initiative.
  • Taking sole credit when others contributed.

Likely follow-up questions

  • How did your manager react?
  • Have you ever taken initiative that did not work?
  • How do you decide when to act on your own?

FAQs

How is initiative different from going beyond expectations?

Initiative is about starting something nobody asked you to do. Going beyond expectations can also mean doing an assigned task exceptionally well. For this question, pick a story where you identified the need yourself.

What if my initiative was a small one?

Small initiatives are fine if they show good judgement and had some impact. Interviewers often find modest, realistic examples more believable than grand claims. Explain the problem, your idea, how you implemented it and what changed.
