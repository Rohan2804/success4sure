Sell Me a Term Insurance Policy: Interview Role-Play Tips

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Sell me a term insurance policy.”

Why interviewers ask this

Insurance and bancassurance interviews often swap the pen for a real product. Term insurance is a favourite because it is simple, genuinely useful and frequently misunderstood. Interviewers check whether you can identify the need, estimate cover in a sensible way, explain features such as sum assured, policy term and exclusions, and handle common objections like 'I get nothing back if I survive'. They also watch for mis-selling signals, such as pushing return-of-premium variants without explanation, or making claims about claim settlement you cannot support.

How to answer

  1. Begin with questions about dependants, income, loans and existing cover, since term insurance is about protecting people who depend on the buyer's income.
  2. Explain the product simply: a fixed premium for a chosen term, and if the insured dies during that term, the nominee receives the sum assured.
  3. Show a rough cover calculation, such as a multiple of annual income plus outstanding loans, while saying the exact need depends on the family's situation.
  4. Handle the 'no maturity benefit' objection by comparing it to vehicle or health insurance, which also pay only on an event.
  5. Mention honest disclosure: full and correct health information, exclusions, and checking the insurer's claim settlement data on IRDAI or the insurer's site.

Sample answers

Fresher (life insurance sales interview)

Sir, can I ask, who depends on your income today? ... Your wife, two children and your parents. And do you have any loans? ... A home loan of about ₹30 lakh. If, God forbid, something happened to you, how would the EMI and the children's education be managed? ... That is exactly what term insurance is for. You pay a fixed yearly premium, and if anything happens to you during the policy term, your family gets a lump sum, say ₹1 crore, which can clear the loan and support them. You mentioned you get nothing back if you survive. That is true, just like your car insurance, but because there is no savings part, the premium is much lower than other life policies. The key is to disclose your health details fully so the claim is smooth. Shall we work out the right cover together?

Experienced (bancassurance officer interview)

I always begin with the family, not the product. For a 32-year-old salaried customer with a home loan, I calculate a rough cover based on income replacement and liabilities and show it on paper. I compare two or three term options on premium, term and riders like accidental death, and explain exclusions. When customers ask about return of premium versions, I explain clearly that the premium is much higher and they can compare that with buying pure term and investing the difference elsewhere. I let them decide. I also tell them to inform their nominee about the policy.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Promising that every claim will be paid.
  • Advising the customer to hide health conditions or smoking to reduce premium.
  • Pushing return-of-premium or savings plans without explaining the cost difference.
  • Quoting a premium without knowing age, health and cover.

Likely follow-up questions

  • The customer says he already has a policy from his employer. What next?
  • What is a rider?
  • What is the claim settlement ratio, and is it the only thing to check?
  • Why is non-disclosure dangerous?

FAQs

Should I quote claim settlement ratios in the role-play?

Only general guidance. Tell the customer that insurers publish claim settlement data and IRDAI's annual report has industry figures, and that they should check the latest numbers. Avoid quoting a specific insurer's ratio from memory.

How much cover should I suggest?

Interviewers want a reasoned approach, not a fixed rule. Mention income replacement for a number of years plus loans and major goals, minus existing cover and assets. Say the final figure depends on the family's needs and the insurer's underwriting.
