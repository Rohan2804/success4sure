How Do You Define Success? Answers That Show Your Values

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “How do you define success?”

Why interviewers ask this

This is a values question in disguise. The interviewer wants to see what you measure yourself against, whether that matches what the organisation rewards, and whether your ideas are mature. A definition based entirely on salary or titles can signal short-term thinking. A definition so abstract that it never mentions results can signal someone who avoids accountability. Good answers mix outcomes (meeting targets, doing quality work) with how you get there (learning, helping others, integrity), and include a small example of you living that definition.

How to answer

  1. Give a short, personal definition in one or two sentences rather than a quotation.
  2. Include both results and the way you achieve them, such as hitting a target without misselling.
  3. Support it with an example of a moment you considered a success.
  4. Show how this role would let you be successful by your own definition.

Sample answers

Fresher (applying for an analyst role)

For me, success means being trusted with more responsibility because I handled the last task well. In my internship, I started with simple data entry, and by the end my manager let me prepare a section of the monthly MIS report on my own. That felt like success more than any certificate. In this role, I would measure myself by how soon I can work independently on analysis.

Experienced (sales manager, mutual fund distribution)

I see success as meeting my numbers in a way I would be comfortable explaining to a customer five years later. Selling a product is easy for one quarter, but if the client stays invested and refers their family, that is the real result. Last year my team met its SIP target, and our cancellation rate was among the lowest in the region. That combination is what I aim for.

Experienced (team lead in back-office operations)

Success for me is when my team can run smoothly even when I am on leave. That means people are trained, processes are documented and everyone knows how to escalate. Last year I took two weeks off during a busy month and the team cleared all its work within the turnaround time. I count that as more of a success than any individual award I have got.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Defining success only as money, cars or titles.
  • Quoting a famous person instead of giving your own view.
  • Giving an answer with no link to work at all.
  • Being so vague that it could mean anything.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What is your biggest success so far?
  • How would you measure success in this role?
  • Have you ever felt like a failure?

FAQs

Can I include personal goals like family in my definition?

Yes, briefly. Saying that success includes balance with family or health is honest and many interviewers respect it. Just make sure your answer still shows you care about doing good work and delivering results, so it does not sound like work is secondary.

Is this question common in Indian campus interviews?

It appears often in HR rounds, both on campus and in lateral hiring, sometimes phrased as 'What does success mean to you?' Preparing a two-sentence definition and an example will also help with related questions on motivation, goals and achievements.
