What Is Your Biggest Achievement? Answers for Freshers

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “What is your biggest achievement so far?”

Why interviewers ask this

This question shows what you value and how you work. The interviewer is less interested in how impressive the achievement sounds than in the effort, obstacles and decisions behind it. A fresher who describes clearing a backlog-heavy semester while working part-time can make a stronger impression than one who lists a college trophy. It also tests storytelling: can you explain a situation, your actions and the result in a structured way? Your choice should ideally reveal a quality the job needs.

How to answer

  1. Choose an achievement where you did most of the work and overcame a real obstacle, not just a rank or award.
  2. Use the STAR structure: situation, task, action and result, spending most of the time on your actions.
  3. Quantify the result where you honestly can, such as money raised, time saved or marks improved.
  4. End with what it taught you and why it is relevant to this role.

Sample answers

Fresher (B.Com, college event)

My biggest achievement was organising the finance quiz for our inter-college fest in my final year. The previous year's event had very few participants and the budget had been cut. I took responsibility for sponsorship, approached [number] local businesses and secured enough to cover prizes. I also promoted it through WhatsApp groups of other colleges. We ended up with [number] teams, more than double the previous year. I learnt how to pitch an idea to people who owe you nothing, which I think is useful in a sales-oriented banking role.

Fresher (first in family to graduate)

I am the first person in my family to complete graduation. I studied in a Marathi-medium school and found English-medium lectures very hard in my first year. I watched lectures again on YouTube, read newspapers daily and practised speaking with friends. By my final year, I was presenting seminars in English and my marks had improved from [first-year score] to [final score]. It taught me that consistent effort over time works.

Fresher (MBA, internship result)

During my internship, I noticed that the team's loan file checklist was not catching missing documents. I proposed a revised checklist, which my manager adopted for the team. That small change reduced files sent back for corrections, and my manager mentioned it in my feedback.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Choosing an achievement with no personal effort, like being born into a family business.
  • Listing many achievements without explaining any of them.
  • Exaggerating results that cannot be verified.
  • Talking only about the result and skipping your actions.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What was the hardest part?
  • What would you do differently?
  • Do you have an achievement related to work or academics?

FAQs

Can a personal achievement count, or must it be academic?

Personal achievements are fine if they show useful qualities like persistence, leadership or learning. Overcoming a language barrier, supporting family while studying or running a small business can be powerful. Explain the actions you took and connect the lesson to the job.

What is the STAR method?

STAR stands for Situation, Task, Action and Result. You briefly set the scene, explain what you had to achieve, describe what you specifically did, and share the outcome. It keeps answers to achievement and behavioural questions structured and stops you from rambling.
