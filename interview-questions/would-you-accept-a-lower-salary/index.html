Would You Accept a Lower Salary? How to Respond

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Would you accept a lower salary than you expected?”

Why interviewers ask this

This question usually means the employer likes you but has a budget constraint, or is testing how firm your expectation is. They want to know whether you are flexible, whether you are genuinely interested in the role beyond money, and where your real floor is. Answering 'yes, anything is fine' weakens your position; answering 'no' too fast may end the conversation. A balanced answer shows interest, asks about the full package and growth path, and states clearly the conditions under which you would accept.

How to answer

  1. Do not answer immediately with yes or no; ask what figure they have in mind and how the package is structured.
  2. Know your walk-away figure in advance, based on your expenses, current pay and market research.
  3. Explore alternatives: an early salary review, joining bonus, higher variable pay, or other benefits.
  4. If the role is genuinely valuable for your career, say so, and accept only if the terms still work for you.

Sample answers

Experienced (open to a slight reduction)

I am really interested in this role, so I am open to discussing it. Could you share the figure you have in mind and how the fixed and variable parts are structured? If the fixed component is lower than what I expected, I would appreciate a salary review after six months based on performance. If that is possible, I can be flexible.

Career switcher (accepting lower pay for a new field)

Yes, I expected that moving into equity research might mean starting at a lower level than my current IT salary. I have planned for that. What matters more to me is the learning and the growth path. Could you tell me how salary progression usually works for associates after the first year?

Experienced (cannot go lower)

I appreciate your openness. I am very keen on the role, but I would not be able to accept below [₹X] lakh CTC, since that is close to my current package and the move involves relocating to a costlier city. If there is any way to reach that figure, through a joining bonus or relocation support, I would be happy to proceed. I would rather be upfront about this now than accept and feel unsettled later, because I want to commit fully to this role.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Agreeing immediately to any reduction without understanding it.
  • Rejecting outright without exploring alternatives.
  • Accepting a figure you cannot live with and resenting it later.
  • Not getting agreed conditions, like an early review, in writing.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What is your absolute minimum?
  • Would a higher variable component work for you?
  • How soon could you join if we agree?

FAQs

Is it wise to take a pay cut for a better role?

It can be, if the role offers significantly better learning, brand value or growth, and you can manage your expenses. It is a personal decision. Think about where the role will take you in two to three years, not just the immediate salary.

How do I ensure a promised salary review actually happens?

Ask for it to be mentioned in the offer letter or a written email from HR, with the timeline and basis for the review. Verbal promises are easily forgotten when managers or policies change. A written confirmation protects both you and the employer.
