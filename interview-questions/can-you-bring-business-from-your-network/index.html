Can You Bring Business From Your Network? Sales Interview

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Can you bring business from your own network?”

Why interviewers ask this

Insurance agencies, broking firms, wealth desks and some NBFCs ask this because early business often comes from a salesperson's own contacts. The interviewer wants to estimate how quickly you can start producing and whether you understand ethical prospecting. They are also wary of candidates who overpromise, saying they will bring dozens of clients, and of those who would pressure relatives. A strong answer gives a realistic picture of your network, a respectful way to approach it, and, crucially, a plan to build new prospects after the warm market is exhausted.

How to answer

  1. Be realistic about your network: describe the kinds of contacts you have, such as alumni, former colleagues, community groups or local businesses, without inflating numbers.
  2. Explain how you would approach them respectfully, starting with understanding their needs and not pressuring anyone.
  3. Mention that you would only offer products that genuinely suit them, since a bad sale to a relative damages both relationship and reputation.
  4. Show a plan beyond your network: referrals, local events, workplace sessions, digital presence within compliance rules, and company leads.
  5. Mention data and consent: using only contacts who are willing to hear from you, and following company rules on communication.

Sample answers

Fresher (insurance agency recruitment interview)

I have a reasonable network: my college alumni group, my father's colleagues and neighbours in our housing society. I would not want to approach them with a hard sell, though. My plan is to first share simple information about why term and health insurance matter, and let interested people come to me. A few people have already asked me about health insurance after a family member's hospitalisation. But I know my network alone will not sustain me for long. I would ask satisfied clients for referrals, hold small awareness sessions in our society and at local offices, and work the leads the company provides.

Experienced (moving from a bank to a broking firm as RM)

Over [number] years at the bank I built relationships with many customers, but I want to be careful here. Those customers belong to the bank, and I cannot use its data. What I can bring is my professional reputation and contacts who know me personally, such as business owners I have stayed in touch with outside work. I would inform them that I have moved and let them choose. Beyond that, I plan to grow through referrals and investor education sessions. I expect it to take a few months to build a meaningful book.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Promising a large number of clients you cannot deliver.
  • Planning to sell mainly to relatives under pressure.
  • Suggesting you will take client data from your previous employer.
  • Having no plan once your personal network is exhausted.

Likely follow-up questions

  • How many people do you think will buy from you in the first three months?
  • What will you do after approaching your network?
  • Can you use your previous employer's client list?
  • How would you ask for referrals?

FAQs

Is it legal to contact my previous employer's clients?

Using your previous employer's client data or confidential lists can breach your employment agreement and data protection rules. You can inform personal contacts that you have moved, but check your agreement and avoid taking any client records. When in doubt, consult HR or legal.

What if I do not have a large network?

Say so honestly and focus on your plan to build one: company leads, referrals, community events and consistent follow-up. Many successful salespeople started without a network. Interviewers value a realistic plan more than a big claim.
