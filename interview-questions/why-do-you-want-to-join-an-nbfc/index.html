Why Join an NBFC Instead of a Bank? Interview Answer

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Why do you want to join an NBFC instead of a bank?”

Why interviewers ask this

NBFCs, from large housing and vehicle finance companies to microfinance and gold loan firms, hire many freshers and bank exam aspirants. Interviewers ask this to check whether you see the NBFC as a real career or only a backup while you prepare for bank exams. They want you to understand what NBFCs do differently: specialised products, faster processes, reach into segments banks find harder to serve, and field-heavy roles. They also look for awareness that NBFCs are regulated by RBI under scale-based regulation and cannot accept demand deposits like banks. A specific, informed answer signals commitment.

How to answer

  1. Show you understand the NBFC model, including specialised lending, quicker turnaround and focus on segments like MSMEs, vehicle buyers or self-employed borrowers.
  2. Connect it to your interests or strengths, such as fieldwork, credit assessment, customer contact or speed of decision-making.
  3. Mention the learning opportunities: early responsibility, hands-on exposure to underwriting, collections and real customer behaviour in the field.
  4. Show regulatory awareness briefly: NBFCs are regulated by RBI, cannot accept demand deposits, and follow fair practices codes.
  5. Address commitment directly, without criticising banks, by explaining why this particular role fits your longer-term career plan.

Sample answers

Fresher (B.Com, applying for credit officer in a vehicle finance NBFC)

I have seen how many small transporters and taxi owners in my town get vehicle loans from NBFCs because they have irregular income records that banks find hard to assess. NBFCs have built expertise in understanding such customers through field visits and local knowledge. I want to learn that kind of practical credit assessment. In a bank my early years would be mostly branch operations; here I would work directly on underwriting and customer evaluation from the start. I know NBFCs are regulated by RBI and have their own fair practices rules, especially on recovery, and I am comfortable with field work and targets. I see this as a long-term career in lending.

Bank exam aspirant (applying for NBFC sales role)

I will be honest, I prepared for bank exams for a year. But during that time I realised I am more interested in lending and customer-facing work than in a generalist branch role. An NBFC offers that from day one. I like the pace, the performance-based growth, and the chance to understand one product deeply, like [home loans or MSME loans]. I also know that collections and customer follow-ups are part of the job, and I am prepared for that.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying you are joining only until you clear a bank exam.
  • Calling NBFCs 'easier' or 'less regulated' than banks.
  • Not knowing what the company lends for or who its customers are.
  • Speaking negatively about banks to justify your choice.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What is the difference between a bank and an NBFC?
  • What is scale-based regulation of NBFCs?
  • How would you assess a borrower without income tax returns?
  • How would you handle collections ethically?

FAQs

Can NBFCs accept deposits?

NBFCs cannot accept demand deposits like savings or current accounts. Only some deposit-taking NBFCs, with RBI permission, can accept term deposits under specific rules. Most NBFCs raise funds through bank loans, bonds and other borrowings.

Will NBFC experience help if I later move to a bank?

Often yes, especially in credit, MSME lending or collections roles, since practical underwriting experience is valued. Mention it as building skills rather than planning to leave, if the topic comes up.
