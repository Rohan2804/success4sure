Counter-Offer From Your Employer? How to Answer

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “What will you do if your current employer makes a counter-offer?”

Why interviewers ask this

Recruiters ask this because candidates sometimes accept a new offer only to use it as leverage, then stay after their current employer raises their pay. This costs the hiring company time and effort. The interviewer wants reassurance that you are moving for genuine reasons, such as role, learning or growth, which a salary increase alone would not fix. Your answer also shows how thoughtfully you make career decisions. A clear statement of your reasons for moving, and how you would handle a counter-offer professionally, builds trust.

How to answer

  1. Restate your main reasons for leaving, which should be about the role and growth, not just money.
  2. Say honestly that you would politely decline a counter-offer if those reasons are not addressed by money alone.
  3. Avoid sounding dismissive of your current employer; show respect for their effort to retain you.
  4. Think through your own answer before resigning, so you are not swayed in the moment.

Sample answers

Experienced (clear reasons to move)

I am not looking to change only for salary. My main reason is that I want to move into credit underwriting, and my current company does not have that function at my location. Even if they offer a higher salary, it would not change that. So if they make a counter-offer, I would thank them but decline. I would make sure to complete my handover properly and leave on good terms.

Experienced (honest about considering it)

I would listen to it respectfully, because my manager has supported me for three years. But I have thought about this decision carefully. The reason I am moving is the larger scope and the regional responsibility your role offers, which my current employer cannot match in the near future. So I expect to decline, and I would tell you immediately if anything changed.

Experienced (has declined before)

I was in this situation once before, when I left my first job. My employer offered a raise, but my reasons for leaving were about learning and growth, so I declined. I would handle it the same way now, politely and with a proper handover. I still keep in touch with that manager, and I think leaving professionally is what made that possible.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying you will consider all offers and decide later.
  • Implying you are using this offer as leverage.
  • Criticising your current employer for not paying you enough.
  • Promising you will never consider a counter-offer, which may not be believable.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What would make you stay at your current job?
  • Have you discussed your move with your manager?
  • When will you resign?

FAQs

Is it a good idea to accept a counter-offer?

It depends. If your reasons for leaving were only about pay, a counter-offer might solve them. But if the issues are about role, growth, manager or culture, a raise rarely fixes them. Also consider that your current employer now knows you were looking to leave. Weigh this carefully.

How do I decline a counter-offer politely?

Thank your manager sincerely for the offer and for their support. Explain briefly that your decision is based on a career move, not salary, and that you have committed to the new role. Offer to complete a thorough handover. Keep the tone positive to preserve the relationship.
