How to Answer 'Tell Me About Yourself' in an Interview

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Tell me about yourself.”

Why interviewers ask this

This is rarely a polite icebreaker. The panel is watching how you organise information under mild pressure, whether you can be brief, and which parts of your life you think matter for this role. Your first ninety seconds also set the agenda: whatever you mention, they will probably ask about next. A rambling family history or a recital of your marksheet tells them you have not thought about what the job needs. A crisp, relevant summary tells them you prepare, you communicate clearly and you understand why you are in the room.

How to answer

  1. Use a present-past-future order: what you are doing now, the two or three experiences that led here, and why this role is the logical next step.
  2. Keep it to 60–90 seconds when spoken aloud. Time yourself with a phone stopwatch; most first drafts run past three minutes.
  3. Pick facts that connect to the job description. For a bank role, mention customer contact, numbers or compliance work before your college cricket captaincy.
  4. Plant one or two hooks you are happy to be asked about, such as a project, an internship result or a certification like a NISM module you have cleared.
  5. End with a clear bridge, for example 'that is why this role at your bank interests me', so the interviewer knows you have finished.

Sample answers

Fresher (B.Com, campus placement for an NBFC)

I recently completed my B.Com from [your college] with accounting and taxation as my main subjects. During my final year I did a two-month internship at [firm name], where I helped reconcile customer loan accounts and prepared simple monthly reports in Excel. That was the first time I saw how small errors in data affect real customers, and it made me careful about checking my work. Outside class I coordinated the commerce fest, which taught me to handle vendors and deadlines. I have also cleared the NISM [module name] certification to strengthen my basics. I am now looking for a role where I can learn lending operations properly, and the credit operations position here fits that very well.

Experienced (3 years in insurance sales, moving to a bank relationship role)

I currently work as a sales executive with [insurer], handling retail health and term policies for customers in [city]. Over three years I have built a client base largely through referrals, and I have learnt how to explain products honestly, especially exclusions and waiting periods, so customers do not feel misled later. Before that I completed my BBA from [college]. I now want to move into a broader relationship role where I can serve a customer's savings, loan and insurance needs together, instead of selling one product. Your relationship manager opening matches that, and my experience of handling customer objections and renewals should help me settle in quickly.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Repeating your resume line by line instead of choosing what matters for this job.
  • Starting with personal details like your native place, parents' professions and siblings, unless asked.
  • Speaking for four or five minutes without a pause, leaving no room for the interviewer.
  • Memorising a script word for word, so it sounds robotic and falls apart if interrupted.
  • Ending abruptly without linking your story to the role you are interviewing for.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What did you actually do in that internship?
  • Why did you choose commerce after Class 12?
  • What would your previous manager say about you?

FAQs

Should I mention my hobbies when answering 'Tell me about yourself'?

Only if a hobby shows something useful for the role, such as discipline from long-distance running or leadership from organising a club. Keep it to one line near the end. If the panel wants to know more about your interests, they will usually ask a separate hobbies question.

Is the answer different in a bank PO interview?

The structure is the same, but PO panels often ask candidates to introduce themselves in a minute and then pick on details such as your home state, graduation subject or work gap. Keep the introduction short and factual, and be ready to talk about anything you mention, including your district and its economy.
