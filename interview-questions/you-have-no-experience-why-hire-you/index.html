No Experience? How Freshers Answer 'Why Hire You?'

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “You have no work experience. Why should we hire you?”

Why interviewers ask this

This question is a mild stress test. Every fresher lacks experience, and the interviewer knows that, so they are really checking whether you can stay composed, think on your feet and make a case for yourself using what you do have. They also want to see whether you have realistic expectations about learning. Answers that apologise or say 'I am a fast learner' with nothing behind it fall flat. Answers that point to transferable evidence and a clear willingness to learn on the job give them confidence.

How to answer

  1. Acknowledge the point calmly in one sentence. Do not argue that you do have experience if you do not.
  2. Show proxy experience: internships, projects, part-time work, college responsibilities or family business involvement.
  3. Point to preparation specific to the role, such as certifications, courses or research into the job.
  4. Add the advantages of hiring a fresher, such as adaptability and no fixed habits, without sounding arrogant.

Sample answers

Fresher (B.Com, applying for an insurance sales role)

You are right that I have not worked full-time yet. But I have done some things that are close to this job. During college, I sold products for a local business at exhibitions, and I learnt how to approach strangers and handle rejection. I have also cleared the IRDAI IC-38 exam, so I know the basics of life insurance products. As a fresher, I do not have any fixed habits, so I can learn your sales process exactly the way you want.

Fresher (MBA, applying for a credit analyst role)

I do not have full-time experience, but I have spent the last two months of my summer internship at [bank or NBFC] in their credit team. I helped prepare credit appraisal notes for small business loans, which included analysing balance sheets and bank statements. I have also built ratio analysis models for three listed companies on my own. I know there is a lot I still need to learn, but I will not need training on the basics.

Fresher (engineer, applying for operations at a bank)

I have no banking experience, but my engineering course taught me to follow processes carefully and solve problems step by step. I was the coordinator for our department's technical fest, where I managed a budget of [amount] and vendor payments. I am ready to start at the basics and learn your systems properly.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Apologising repeatedly for your lack of experience.
  • Saying only 'I am a quick learner' with no proof.
  • Exaggerating internships or projects to sound experienced.
  • Getting irritated by the question.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What did you actually do in your internship?
  • How long will it take you to become productive?
  • What do you expect from our training?

FAQs

Are internships counted as work experience?

Employers usually treat internships as relevant exposure rather than formal experience. Mention them with specifics: the organisation, duration, tasks and any result. A well-described two-month internship can be very persuasive, especially if the work is similar to the role you are applying for.

How can I build relevant experience before placements?

Take internships, even short or unpaid ones; volunteer for college finance or event roles; do live projects; complete certifications like NISM or IC-38; and build small portfolios such as a stock analysis or Excel model. These give you specific examples to talk about.

  • Why did you have backlogs in college?
  • Why are your marks or grades low?
  • Tell me about your final-year project.
  • Why did you choose your graduation stream?
  • What did you learn from your internship?
  • What are your short-term career goals?
  • Why are you not going for higher studies?
