Why Do You Want to Work in an AMC? Interview Answer

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Why do you want to work in an asset management company?”

Why interviewers ask this

Asset management companies hire for many roles: distributor sales, investor services, operations, compliance, product and research. Interviewers ask this to see whether you understand what an AMC does and where your role fits. Many candidates talk only about the stock market, even when interviewing for a sales or operations role. The panel wants evidence of interest, such as following the industry, understanding distribution channels and knowing that mutual funds are a long-term savings product for millions of investors. Specific knowledge about the company, its key funds and channels makes a strong impression.

How to answer

  1. Explain your understanding of what an AMC does: pooling money from investors and managing schemes under SEBI's mutual fund regulations, with a trustee structure.
  2. Connect your interest to the specific role, whether distributor relationships, investor service, operations or research.
  3. Mention an industry trend that excites you, such as growth in SIP investors or new investors from smaller cities.
  4. Show company-specific preparation, like key schemes, presence in B30 cities or channel focus, without overstating numbers.
  5. Close with what you hope to learn and contribute in your first two to three years.

Sample answers

Fresher (MBA, applying for sales role in an AMC)

An AMC sits at the centre of how Indian households are moving from physical savings to financial ones. I find that shift exciting, especially the growth of SIPs among young earners and in smaller towns. I applied for the sales role because I enjoy building relationships, and in an AMC sales role I would work with distributors, banks and IFAs to help them serve their investors better. I have NISM V-A, and during my internship at [firm] I helped organise investor awareness sessions, which showed me how little many people know about mutual funds. I have looked at your [fund category] lineup and your focus on [channel or region]. In the first few years I want to build a strong distributor network in my territory.

Experienced (bank operations, applying for AMC operations role)

I have worked for three years in bank operations, handling reconciliations and payments. I want to move to an AMC because mutual fund operations involve a similar discipline but in a growing industry, with daily NAV processes, transactions through RTAs and exchanges, and strict regulatory timelines. Accuracy matters because errors directly affect investors. I like that responsibility. I am also keen to understand fund accounting and compliance as I grow.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Talking only about stock picking for a sales or operations role.
  • Confusing AMCs with stockbrokers or banks.
  • Quoting the company's AUM or rank incorrectly.
  • Saying you want to become a fund manager in two years.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What is the role of a trustee in a mutual fund?
  • What is an RTA?
  • Who are our main competitors?
  • How do AMCs earn money?

FAQs

How do I research an AMC before the interview?

Read the AMC's website, latest factsheet and a few scheme information documents. Check AMFI's website for industry-level data. Note the AMC's flagship funds, distribution focus and any recent news. Avoid quoting rankings unless you have checked them recently.

Do I need NISM V-A for an AMC job?

It is generally required for sales and distribution-facing roles and is useful for many others. Some AMCs ask new hires to complete it within a set time. Having it already shows initiative and basic product knowledge.
