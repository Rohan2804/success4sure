Tell Me About a Mistake You Made at Work (Sample Answers)

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The question: “Tell me about a mistake you made at work and how you handled it.”

Why interviewers ask this

In finance and banking, mistakes are inevitable, but hidden mistakes are dangerous. A wrong account debit, a missed KYC document or an error in a report can harm customers and the organisation. The interviewer wants to see that you notice your own errors, report them quickly, fix them, and put checks in place so they do not repeat. They are also testing honesty: anyone claiming never to make mistakes is either inexperienced or not being truthful. The handling matters more than the mistake itself.

How to answer

  1. Pick a real but recoverable mistake, ideally one you discovered yourself.
  2. Explain what happened in a sentence or two, owning your part clearly.
  3. Describe the immediate steps: informing your manager, correcting the error, communicating with anyone affected.
  4. Explain the process change or habit you introduced to prevent a repeat.

Sample answers

Experienced (bank operations)

In my first year at the branch, I processed a fixed deposit with the wrong tenure because I misread the customer's form during a busy afternoon. I noticed it the next morning while checking my entries. I told my manager immediately, called the customer to apologise and corrected it the same day with his signature on a fresh request. Since then, I read back key details to the customer before final processing, and I do a short self-check of my entries before leaving each day. I have not had a similar error since.

Experienced (MIS reporting)

I once sent a monthly sales report to senior management with a formula error that double-counted one region's numbers. A regional manager spotted it within an hour. I took responsibility, sent a corrected version with a short note explaining the error, and then checked every formula in the template. I also added a total check that compares my figures with the source system before sending. It was embarrassing, but it made my reports much more reliable.

Fresher (internship)

During my internship, I filed some documents in the wrong client folder on a shared drive. I realised it while preparing a summary and told my supervisor right away. We fixed it within the day, and I started using a naming checklist for every upload.

Use these as a structure, not a script — replace the placeholders with your own facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying you have never made a mistake.
  • Choosing a serious ethical or compliance breach.
  • Blaming the system or colleagues for your error.
  • Leaving out how you prevented it from happening again.

Likely follow-up questions

  • How did your manager react?
  • What would you do if you found a mistake made by your senior?
  • How do you check your own work?

FAQs

What kind of mistake is too serious to mention?

Avoid anything involving dishonesty, fraud, data misuse, or violations of regulations, even if minor. Also avoid mistakes that caused large losses and were never corrected. Choose an error of carelessness or judgement that you caught and fixed.

How is this different from the failure question?

A failure is usually a goal you did not achieve. A mistake is a specific wrong action, like an incorrect entry or a missed step. The structure of the answer is similar, but for a mistake, focus more on the correction and the prevention steps.
