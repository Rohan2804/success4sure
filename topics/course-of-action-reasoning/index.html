Course of Action Questions: Practical, Feasible, Relevant

Course of action questions describe a problem — a train accident, a sudden rise in cyber fraud, a water shortage — and suggest two or three actions. You choose which should be pursued. They appear in IBPS PO and SBI PO mains, RBI Grade B, and the decision-making parts of CMAT and MAT. Unlike conclusions, these questions test administrative judgement: a good action is practical, proportionate to the problem and addresses its cause or reduces its harm.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Three tests for any action

First, is it practical — can it actually be done by the authority concerned? Second, is it proportionate — does it fit the scale of the problem? Third, is it relevant — does it solve or reduce the problem, either immediately or in the long run? An action must pass all three.

Short-term relief and long-term fix

Both kinds can be valid. After floods, sending relief material is short-term; strengthening embankments is long-term. Many correct answers have both actions following because they work at different time scales.

Extreme actions usually fail

Banning all online transactions because of fraud, or shutting a whole department after one complaint, is disproportionate. Words like 'ban', 'immediately close' and 'dismiss everyone' are red flags.

Investigation is often valid

When the cause is unknown, setting up an inquiry is a sensible first step. When the cause is already stated, a fresh inquiry adds little unless responsibility needs to be fixed.

Who is expected to act

Read the statement for the authority involved — a bank, a municipal body, a school, the railways. An action that only a different authority could take, or that expects ordinary citizens to do the administration's job, is usually weak. For example, after a pothole-related accident, 'the municipal corporation should repair the road' follows, but 'citizens should repair potholes themselves' does not.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Ask: Would a responsible official actually do this tomorrow? If not, it does not follow.
  • Awareness campaigns are valid when the problem involves public behaviour (fraud, health, safety).
  • Punishing the guilty follows when negligence is clear from the statement; punishing a whole group does not.
  • An action that creates a bigger problem than it solves does not follow.
  • If two actions are both reasonable and complementary, choose 'both follow'.
  • When the statement describes a one-time incident, prefer actions aimed at that incident and its cause over sweeping new rules for everyone.

Worked examples

Example 1

Statement: 'Several customers of a bank lost money after sharing OTPs with callers pretending to be bank staff.' Courses of action: I. The bank should run an awareness campaign reminding customers never to share OTPs. II. The bank should stop sending OTPs altogether.

Solution

I addresses the cause (customers being tricked) and is practical. II is disproportionate and would weaken security for everyone. Only I follows.

Example 2

Statement: 'A large number of commuters were stranded after heavy rain flooded a railway line in the city.' Courses of action: I. The railways should arrange alternative transport for stranded passengers. II. The drainage along the railway line should be reviewed and improved.

Solution

I gives short-term relief and II prevents recurrence. Both are practical and relevant. Both follow.

Example 3

Statement: 'Many students complain that the results of a university exam were published with errors.' Courses of action: I. The university should check the results and issue corrected marks. II. The university should cancel the exam and hold it again.

Solution

The problem is in result processing, not the exam itself, so I fixes it. II is disproportionate and penalises students for an administrative error. Only I follows.

Example 4

Statement: 'Several cases of dengue have been reported from one ward of the city in the past two weeks.' Courses of action: I. The civic body should carry out fogging and remove stagnant water in that ward. II. All schools in the state should be closed for a month.

Solution

I targets the cause (mosquito breeding) in the affected area and is a standard, practical response. II is far out of proportion — a problem in one ward does not justify statewide school closures. Only I follows.

Practice questions

Question 1

Statement: 'A number of street lights in a residential colony have stopped working, leading to complaints about safety.' Courses of action: I. The municipal body should repair the lights quickly. II. Residents should be told to stay indoors after dark.

  1. Only I follows
  2. Only II follows
  3. Both follow
  4. Neither follows
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Only I follows

Repairing the lights solves the problem directly and is practical. Asking residents to stay indoors is impractical and does not address the cause.

Question 2

Statement: 'A pharmacy was found selling medicines past their expiry date.' Courses of action: I. All pharmacies in the state should be closed. II. The authorities should take action against this pharmacy under the applicable law.

  1. Only I follows
  2. Only II follows
  3. Both follow
  4. Neither follows
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Only II follows

Closing every pharmacy is disproportionate and harms patients. Acting against the pharmacy found at fault is practical and relevant.

Question 3

Statement: 'Water supply in a town has become irregular because a main pipeline is leaking.' Courses of action: I. The pipeline should be repaired on priority. II. Water tankers should be sent to affected areas until the repair is done.

  1. Only I follows
  2. Only II follows
  3. Both follow
  4. Neither follows
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Both follow

Repair is the long-term fix and tankers give immediate relief. Both actions are practical and complement each other, so both follow.

Question 4

Statement: 'A few candidates were found using mobile phones during an exam at one centre.' Courses of action: I. The entire exam across the country should be cancelled. II. Mobile phones should be banned for all citizens on exam days.

  1. Only I follows
  2. Only II follows
  3. Both follow
  4. Neither follows
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Neither follows

Both actions are grossly disproportionate to a problem at one centre. Stricter frisking or action against those candidates would be appropriate instead.

Question 5

Statement: 'Many senior citizens find it hard to use a bank's mobile app.' Courses of action: I. The bank should offer simple help desks or tutorials for senior citizens. II. The bank should collect feedback to simplify the app.

  1. Both follow
  2. Only I follows
  3. Only II follows
  4. Neither follows
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Both follow

Help desks give immediate support, and feedback helps improve the app over time. Both are practical, relevant steps.

FAQs

Can an action follow if it only reduces the problem rather than solving it?

Yes. Many real problems cannot be removed instantly. If the action meaningfully reduces harm or helps prevent a repeat, and it is practical, it follows. Relief measures after disasters are a classic example: they do not stop floods but clearly should be pursued.

Is 'set up an inquiry committee' always a correct action?

No. It follows when the cause is unclear or responsibility needs fixing. If the statement already gives the cause and the fix is obvious, an inquiry alone is weak. Judge it like any other action: is it practical, relevant and proportionate to the problem?
