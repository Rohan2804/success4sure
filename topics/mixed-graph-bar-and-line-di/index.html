Mixed Graph DI: Combining Bar and Line Charts

Mixed or combined graphs pair two chart types — commonly bars for totals and a line for a percentage — so that useful numbers come only from combining them. IBPS PO mains, SBI PO, RBI Grade B and CAT use this format, and it now appears in prelims as well. The calculations are straightforward multiplications, but each question needs values from both charts, so a small derived table saves a lot of time.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Two axes, two meanings

The bars usually use the left axis (units, students, ₹ crore) and the line uses the right axis (percentages). Confirm which series belongs to which axis before reading. Reading a percentage off the wrong axis is the most common error in this format.

Derive the hidden series

The questions rarely ask about the bars or the line alone. If bars show production and the line shows the percentage sold, the key derived values are units sold (= production × % sold) and unsold units (= production − sold). Compute both for every year first.

Sold = Produced × % sold / 100

Percent of different bases

A higher percentage does not always mean a higher amount. 88% of 75 thousand (66 thousand) exceeds 70% of 80 thousand (56 thousand), but 80% of 50 thousand (40 thousand) is less than 75% of 60 thousand (45 thousand). Always compare actual derived values.

Weighted overall rates

An overall percentage across years is total derived value ÷ total base, which weights each year by its size. The simple average of yearly percentages ignores those weights and is usually slightly different, so check which one the question wants.

Growth on derived values

Growth questions on mixed graphs usually refer to the derived series. A rise in pass percentage with a fall in candidates can still reduce the number who pass. Compute the derived values first, then the growth.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Build a four-row table: base value, percentage, derived value, complement — then answer.
  • Multiplying by a percentage like 75% or 88% is faster as a fraction (3/4) or as (1 − 0.12).
  • If the question asks for the complement (failures, unsold units), subtract from the base rather than recomputing.
  • Ratios of derived values can often be simplified before multiplying out.

Worked examples

Example 1

A factory produced 50, 60, 80 and 75 thousand units in 2019–2022, and the line shows that 80%, 75%, 70% and 88% of production was sold. How many units were sold in 2021?

Solution

Read the 2021 bar (80 thousand) and the 2021 point on the line (70%). Units sold = 70% of 80 thousand = 56 thousand. Neither chart alone gives this number, which is the essence of a mixed graph.

Example 2

Find the total unsold units over the four years.

Solution

Sold: 40, 45, 56, 66 thousand. Unsold: 10, 15, 24, 9 thousand. Total unsold = 58 thousand.

Example 3

In which year were the most units sold?

Solution

Compare derived values, not bars: sold units are 40, 45, 56 and 66 thousand. The answer is 2022, with 66 thousand units, although 2021 had the tallest production bar (80 thousand). The high 88% sale rate in 2022 more than made up for lower production.

Example 4

Find the percentage increase in units sold from 2019 to 2022.

Solution

(66 − 40)/40 × 100 = 65%.

Example 5

Find the average number of units sold per year.

Solution

(40 + 45 + 56 + 66)/4 = 207/4 = 51.75 thousand units.

Example 6

What was the overall percentage of production sold over the four years?

Solution

Total produced = 50 + 60 + 80 + 75 = 265 thousand; total sold = 207 thousand. Overall rate = 207/265 × 100 ≈ 78.1%. This is not the simple average of the four percentages (78.25%), because each year’s rate applies to a different base.

Practice questions

Question 1

Candidates appeared: 800, 900, 1,000, 1,200 in 2020–2023; pass rates 60%, 70%, 72%, 65%. How many passed in 2022?

  1. 700
  2. 720
  3. 750
  4. 680
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 720

72% of 1,000 candidates is 720, combining the bar value with the line percentage.

Question 2

Using the same data, the total number of failures over the four years is:

  1. 1,210
  2. 1,350
  3. 1,180
  4. 1,290
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 1,290

Failures are 320, 270, 280 and 420, which add up to 1,290 candidates.

Question 3

Using the same data, the percentage increase in the number passing from 2020 to 2023 is:

  1. 50%
  2. 5%
  3. 62.5%
  4. 60%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 62.5%

Passes rose from 480 to 780, an increase of 300 on 480, which is 62.5 percent.

Question 4

Using the same data, which year had the most failures?

  1. 2020
  2. 2023
  3. 2021
  4. 2022
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 2023

Failures were 320, 270, 280 and 420, so 2023 had the most despite having the most passes too.

Question 5

Using the same data, the ratio of passes in 2021 to passes in 2023 is:

  1. 7 : 8
  2. 3 : 4
  3. 14 : 13
  4. 21 : 26
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 21 : 26

Passes were 630 and 780, and dividing both by 30 gives the ratio 21 : 26.

FAQs

What is the most common trap in mixed graph DI?

Comparing percentages when the question asks about actual numbers, or vice versa. A year with the highest pass rate need not have the most passes. Always ask whether the question is about the rate or the derived amount before answering.

Do mixed graphs appear in prelims or only mains?

They are more common in mains papers of IBPS PO, SBI PO and RBI Grade B, but simpler bar-plus-line sets have appeared in prelims. Practising both levels is useful because the method is identical.
