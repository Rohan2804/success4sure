Mixtures and Alligation Rule with Solved Examples

Mixtures and alligation is a high-value topic in IBPS PO, SBI PO and RBI Grade B, where it often carries one or two questions, and in SSC CGL. Alligation is really a weighted-average shortcut, so the same cross diagram solves problems on grocery prices, milk and water, alloys, average marks of boys and girls, and even interest rates on split investments. Learning to spot the "two groups and one mean" structure is the key skill.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The alligation rule

When two ingredients with values c (cheaper) and d (dearer) are mixed to get a mean value m, they must be in the ratio (d − m) : (m − c). The value can be price per kg, percentage of milk, marks or speed — anything that averages by weight.

Cheaper : Dearer = (d − m) : (m − c)

Adding a pure component

If water is added to a milk–water mixture, the quantity of milk does not change. Find milk first, use the target ratio to find the required water, and subtract the water already present. This avoids setting up an equation.

Mixing with a profit

When a mixture is sold at a price that includes profit, first remove the profit to get the mixture’s cost price. Only then apply alligation. Selling at ₹60 with 20% profit means the mixture costs ₹50 per kg.

Combining vessels

For two vessels with different ratios mixed in equal quantity, convert each ratio to a fraction of milk (3 : 1 means 3/4 milk) and average the fractions. For unequal quantities, use weighted averages of these fractions.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Write the cross: c and d on top, m in the middle, (d − m) and (m − c) below — the bottom numbers give the ratio.
  • The mean must lie between the two values; if it does not, re-read the question.
  • Alligation works for averages of groups too: class averages 60 (boys) and 75 (girls) with overall 66 give boys : girls = 9 : 6 = 3 : 2.
  • To mix equal quantities, the mean is the simple average of the two values.
  • Convert ratios to fractions of the whole (e.g. 7 : 3 → 70% milk) to apply alligation directly.

Worked examples

Example 1

In what ratio must rice at ₹40 per kg be mixed with rice at ₹55 per kg to get a mixture worth ₹50 per kg?

Solution

Cheaper : dearer = (55 − 50) : (50 − 40) = 5 : 10 = 1 : 2.

Example 2

A 40-litre mixture has milk and water in the ratio 5 : 3. How much water must be added to make the ratio 1 : 1?

Solution

Milk = 25 L, water = 15 L. For 1 : 1, water must equal 25 L, so add 10 litres.

Example 3

A shopkeeper mixes tea costing ₹45 per kg and ₹60 per kg and sells the mixture at ₹60 per kg, gaining 20%. Find the mixing ratio.

Solution

Cost of mixture = 60 ÷ 1.2 = ₹50 per kg. Ratio = (60 − 50) : (50 − 45) = 10 : 5 = 2 : 1, cheaper to dearer.

Example 4

Two vessels contain milk and water in ratios 3 : 1 and 5 : 3. Equal quantities are mixed. Find the ratio of milk to water in the new mixture.

Solution

Milk fractions are 3/4 = 6/8 and 5/8. Average = (6/8 + 5/8)/2 = 11/16. So milk : water = 11 : 5.

Practice questions

Question 1

In what ratio must sugar at ₹30 per kg be mixed with sugar at ₹45 per kg to get a mixture worth ₹36 per kg?

  1. 2 : 3
  2. 1 : 2
  3. 3 : 2
  4. 3 : 1
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 3 : 2

Cheaper : dearer = (45 − 36) : (36 − 30) = 9 : 6, which simplifies to 3 : 2.

Question 2

60 litres of a mixture contain milk and water in the ratio 7 : 3. How much water must be added to make the ratio 7 : 5?

  1. 10 litres
  2. 15 litres
  3. 12 litres
  4. 8 litres
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 12 litres

Milk is 42 L, so water must become 30 L; it is 18 L now, so 12 L of water is added.

Question 3

Sugar at ₹60 per kg and ₹80 per kg is mixed in the ratio 2 : 3. The cost of the mixture per kg is:

  1. ₹70
  2. ₹74
  3. ₹76
  4. ₹72
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹72

Weighted mean = (2 × 60 + 3 × 80)/5 = (120 + 240)/5 = 360/5 = ₹72 per kg.

Question 4

Vessel A has milk and water in the ratio 2 : 1 and vessel B in the ratio 4 : 1. Equal quantities are mixed. The milk-to-water ratio is:

  1. 3 : 1
  2. 11 : 4
  3. 6 : 2
  4. 7 : 3
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 11 : 4

Milk fractions are 2/3 and 4/5; their average is 11/15, so milk : water = 11 : 4.

Question 5

In a class of 50, boys average 60 marks and girls average 75 marks. The class average is 66. How many boys are there?

  1. 30
  2. 20
  3. 25
  4. 32
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 30

Boys : girls = (75 − 66) : (66 − 60) = 9 : 6 = 3 : 2, so boys are 3/5 of 50, which is 30.

FAQs

Can alligation be used for simple interest problems?

Yes. If a sum is lent partly at 6% and partly at 9% and the overall rate is 7.5%, alligation gives the ratio of the two parts directly: (9 − 7.5) : (7.5 − 6) = 1 : 1. Any problem with two groups and one weighted average fits.

Which value goes on which side of the alligation cross?

Put the lower value on the left and the higher on the right with the mean in the middle. The difference on the left of the bottom row belongs to the cheaper item. If you swap them, you get the inverse ratio, which is a common trap answer.
