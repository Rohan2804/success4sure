Direct and Indirect Speech: Tense, Pronoun and Time Rules

Narration (direct and indirect speech) questions ask you to convert a sentence from quoted words to reported form, or back. SSC CGL, CHSL and MTS include them in many English sections, and the same rules help in error spotting. Each conversion involves up to four changes — reporting verb, tense, pronouns and words of time and place — and errors usually come from forgetting one of them. Checking all four in order makes this topic very reliable.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Reporting verb

'Said' stays 'said' for statements, or 'said to' becomes 'told'. Questions use 'asked' or 'enquired'; commands use 'ordered', 'told' or 'requested'; exclamations use 'exclaimed with joy/sorrow'. The conjunction changes too: 'that' for statements, 'if/whether' for yes-no questions, the question word itself for wh-questions, and 'to' + verb for commands.

Tense backshift

If the reporting verb is in the past, move the reported tense one step back: present → past, present continuous → past continuous, present perfect → past perfect, simple past → past perfect, will → would, can → could, may → might.

No backshift cases

If the reporting verb is in the present or future ('He says'), the tense does not change. Universal truths and habitual facts usually keep their tense: 'The teacher said that the earth revolves around the sun.'

Pronoun changes

First-person pronouns change to match the speaker, second-person to match the listener, and third-person usually stays the same. 'He said to me, "I like your idea"' → 'He told me that he liked my idea.'

Time and place words

now → then, today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, yesterday → the previous day, ago → before, this → that, here → there, next week → the following week.

Exclamations, wishes and suggestions

Exclamations change to statements with an expressive reporting verb: 'He said, "What a beautiful view!"' becomes 'He exclaimed that it was a very beautiful view.' Wishes use 'wished' or 'prayed': 'She said, "May you succeed!"' becomes 'She wished that I might succeed.' Suggestions with 'Let us' become 'suggested that we should…'. Interjections such as 'Alas' or 'Hurrah' disappear and are replaced by 'exclaimed with sorrow' or 'exclaimed with joy'.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Change in this order: reporting verb, conjunction, pronouns, tense, time words.
  • In reported questions, use statement word order: 'She asked where I lived', not 'where did I live'.
  • For negative commands, use 'not to': 'Don't shout' → 'He told me not to shout.'
  • 'Must' usually becomes 'had to' when it shows obligation.
  • Remove quotation marks and question marks in indirect speech; reported questions end with a full stop.
  • 'Let's' in suggestions becomes 'suggested that we should…' or 'proposed that…'.

Worked examples

Example 1

Convert: He said, "I am busy today."

Solution

Statement, past reporting verb: He said that he was busy that day. (I → he, am → was, today → that day)

Example 2

Convert: She said to me, "Have you finished your work?"

Solution

Yes-no question: She asked me if I had finished my work. (said to → asked, if, you → I, your → my, have finished → had finished)

Example 3

Convert: The officer said to the clerk, "Submit the report by evening."

Solution

Command: The officer ordered the clerk to submit the report by evening.

Example 4

Convert: He said, "Where do you live?"

Solution

Wh-question with statement word order: He asked me where I lived. ('where did I live' is the common wrong option.)

Example 5

Convert: The teacher said, "Water boils at 100 °C at sea level."

Solution

A scientific fact keeps its tense: The teacher said that water boils at 100 °C at sea level.

Practice questions

Question 1

Choose the indirect form: Ravi said, "I will call you tomorrow."

  1. Ravi said that he would call me the next day.
  2. Ravi said that I will call you tomorrow.
  3. Ravi said that he will call me tomorrow.
  4. Ravi told that he would call me tomorrow.
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Ravi said that he would call me the next day.

Will becomes would, I becomes he, you becomes me and tomorrow becomes the next day. Only the option with all four changes is correct.

Question 2

Choose the indirect form: She said to him, "Please help me."

  1. She told him please help her.
  2. She requested him to help her.
  3. She said him to help me.
  4. She asked that he helps her.
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. She requested him to help her.

A polite request uses 'requested' with 'to' plus the verb. The pronoun me changes to her to match the speaker.

Question 3

Choose the indirect form: He said, "Why are you late?"

  1. He asked me why was I late.
  2. He said why I am late.
  3. He asked me why I was late.
  4. He asked me that why I was late.
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. He asked me why I was late.

A wh-question keeps the question word, uses statement order and backshifts the tense, giving 'why I was late' without 'that'.

Question 4

Choose the indirect form: The doctor said, "Don't eat oily food."

  1. The doctor said don't eat oily food.
  2. The doctor advised me to not eating oily food.
  3. The doctor told that I should not eat oily food.
  4. The doctor advised me not to eat oily food.
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The doctor advised me not to eat oily food.

Negative advice uses 'not to' plus the base verb after an advising verb. 'Advised me not to eat' is correct.

Question 5

Choose the indirect form: She says, "I am learning French."

  1. She says that she is learning French.
  2. She says that she was learning French.
  3. She said that she is learning French.
  4. She says that I am learning French.
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. She says that she is learning French.

The reporting verb 'says' is present tense, so there is no backshift. Only the pronoun changes from I to she.

FAQs

When does the tense not change in indirect speech?

When the reporting verb is in the present or future tense, and when the reported words state a universal truth, a scientific fact or a habitual action that is still true. In such cases only pronouns and, where needed, time words change.

Why is 'He asked me where did I live' wrong?

Reported questions are no longer real questions, so they take normal statement word order: subject before verb. The correct form is 'He asked me where I lived'. The auxiliary 'did' disappears, and the sentence ends with a full stop, not a question mark.
