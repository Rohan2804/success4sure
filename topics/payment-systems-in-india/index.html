Payment Systems in India: NEFT, RTGS, IMPS, UPI and More

Payment systems are a frequent topic in RBI Grade B, IBPS and SBI mains, JAIIB and CAIIB, and bank interviews, where candidates may be asked to compare NEFT with RTGS or explain how UPI works. The core facts — which system is run by whom, whether settlement is in batches or real time, and what each is used for — are stable. Transaction limits and charges change more often, so confirm them from the RBI or NPCI before quoting numbers.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Legal and institutional framework

The Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 gives the RBI powers to regulate and supervise payment systems. The RBI itself runs NEFT and RTGS. The National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) runs retail systems such as IMPS, UPI, NACH, RuPay, AePS, BBPS and the cheque truncation system.

NEFT and RTGS

NEFT settles transactions in half-hourly batches and has no minimum amount. RTGS settles each transaction individually and in real time, and is meant for large-value payments with a minimum of ₹2 lakh. Both have been available 24×7 since NEFT went round-the-clock in December 2019 and RTGS in December 2020.

IMPS and UPI

IMPS, launched in 2010, allows instant 24×7 interbank transfers using account number and IFSC or mobile number. UPI, launched in 2016, builds on IMPS rails and lets users pay using a virtual payment address or QR code from a single app linked to multiple bank accounts.

Bulk and cheque systems

NACH handles bulk and repetitive payments such as salary credits, dividends, EMIs and subsidy transfers. The Cheque Truncation System (CTS) clears cheques using images instead of moving physical cheques.

Inclusion-focused systems

AePS (Aadhaar Enabled Payment System) lets customers withdraw cash or check balances at a business correspondent using Aadhaar authentication. BBPS (Bharat Bill Payment System) offers a single interoperable platform for paying utility and other recurring bills.

Cards

RuPay is India's domestic card network, run by NPCI, used for debit cards including PMJDY accounts, and also for some credit cards. RuPay credit cards can be linked to UPI.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • RBI runs NEFT and RTGS; NPCI runs IMPS, UPI, NACH, CTS, AePS, BBPS and RuPay.
  • NEFT = batches; RTGS = real time, gross, minimum ₹2 lakh.
  • NEFT 24×7 from December 2019; RTGS 24×7 from December 2020.
  • IMPS 2010; UPI 2016; NPCI 2008.
  • Payment and Settlement Systems Act: 2007.
  • Check limits and charges on the RBI and NPCI websites before quoting them.

Worked examples

Example 1

A company must pay a supplier ₹50 lakh with immediate final settlement. Which system is best?

Solution

RTGS, because it settles each transaction individually and in real time, and the amount is well above the ₹2 lakh minimum.

Example 2

A student wants to send ₹500 to a friend instantly using only the friend's mobile-linked UPI ID. Which system works?

Solution

UPI. It allows instant transfers using a virtual payment address without sharing account numbers, around the clock.

Example 3

A company pays monthly salaries to 5,000 employees. Which system is designed for this?

Solution

NACH (National Automated Clearing House), which processes bulk, repetitive credits and debits such as salaries and EMIs.

Example 4

A rural customer without a smartphone wants to withdraw cash from her bank account at a village business correspondent. Which system helps?

Solution

AePS, which authenticates the customer through Aadhaar biometrics at the business correspondent's micro-ATM.

Example 5

A customer sends ₹10,000 through NEFT at 11:50 p.m. on a Sunday. Will it be processed?

Solution

Yes. NEFT has run 24×7, including holidays, since December 2019, and settles in half-hourly batches, so the payment goes out in the next batch. Before 2019, NEFT worked only during banking hours on working days.

Which payment system settles each transaction individually and in real time for large values?

  1. RTGS
  2. NEFT
  3. NACH
  4. CTS

Answer: A. RTGS

RTGS stands for Real Time Gross Settlement. Each transaction is settled individually as it occurs, with a minimum amount of ₹2 lakh.

Question 2

Which organisation operates UPI?

  1. RBI
  2. NPCI
  3. SEBI
  4. SBI

Answer: B. NPCI

UPI is operated by the National Payments Corporation of India, which also runs IMPS, NACH and RuPay.

Which Act governs payment and settlement systems in India?

  2. FEMA, 1999
  3. Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007

The Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 empowers the RBI to regulate and supervise payment systems in India.

  2. RTGS

  4. NACH

Answer: D. NACH

NACH (National Automated Clearing House) processes high-volume, repetitive credits and debits like salaries, dividends and loan EMIs.

UPI was launched by NPCI in 2016. IMPS, on which UPI builds, had been launched earlier in 2010.

FAQs

Is UPI regulated by the RBI or NPCI?

NPCI operates UPI, but the RBI regulates payment systems under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007, and NPCI itself is authorised by the RBI. So operational rules come from NPCI, while overall regulation and policy direction come from the RBI.

Why are UPI limits different in different sources?

NPCI and the RBI have revised limits for certain categories, such as tax payments, hospitals and education, and banks may set their own lower limits. Always check the latest NPCI circulars or your bank's page for current limits instead of relying on older notes.
