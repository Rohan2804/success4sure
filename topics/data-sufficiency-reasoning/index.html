Data Sufficiency in Reasoning: Method and Answer Options

Data sufficiency (DS) questions in reasoning do not ask you to find the answer — they ask whether the given statements are enough to find it. IBPS PO, SBI PO and RBI Grade B mains include DS sets, and the same idea appears in CAT and CMAT. Questions are drawn from every other reasoning topic: blood relations, directions, ranking, coding and seating. The skill is discipline: test each statement alone first, and only then together, without carrying information from one into the other.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

What 'sufficient' means

A statement is sufficient if it leads to exactly one answer. For a yes/no question, a definite 'no' is also sufficient. If it allows two or more answers, it is not sufficient, no matter how likely one answer looks.

The standard options

Usually five: I alone is sufficient; II alone is sufficient; either I alone or II alone is sufficient; both together are needed; even both together are not sufficient. Some papers add a sixth option for 'both are needed and either is sufficient'. Read the option list once at the start of the set.

Test alone, then together

Cover statement II with your hand and test I. Then cover I and test II. Only if both fail alone, combine them. The classic mistake is using a fact from I while judging II alone.

Do not over-solve

You only need to know whether a unique answer exists. In a ranking question you do not have to find the full order; just check whether one person is fixed at the top.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • If both statements alone give the same unique answer, choose 'either alone is sufficient', not 'both together'.
  • Gender gaps are a common trap in blood-relation DS: 'child of' does not tell you son or daughter.
  • In coding DS, a word's code is fixed only when it is the single unmatched word after eliminating common words.
  • For direction DS, one statement that links both points through a known reference is usually enough.
  • Before marking 'both together not sufficient', try one concrete counter-example; if you cannot build one, re-check.

Worked examples

Example 1

How is M related to K? I. M is the brother of N, and N is the daughter of K. II. K is the husband of P, and P is the mother of M.

Solution

From I, M is a male child of K, so M is K's son — sufficient. From II, M is K's child, but M's gender is unknown, so 'son' or 'daughter' cannot be fixed — not sufficient. Answer: I alone is sufficient.

Example 2

In which direction is P from Q? I. R is to the north of Q, and P is to the east of R. II. Q is to the west of S, and P is to the north of S.

Solution

From I, P is north and east of Q, so P is north-east of Q — sufficient. From II, S is east of Q and P is north of S, so again P is north-east of Q — sufficient. Answer: either statement alone is sufficient.

Example 3

Who is the tallest among A, B, C, D and E? I. A is taller than B and C but shorter than D. II. E is shorter than B.

Solution

I alone leaves E unplaced, so E or D could be tallest. II alone says nothing about A, C or D. Together: D > A > B > E and A > C, so D is the tallest. Both statements are needed.

Example 4

What is the code for 'gold'? I. 'gold silver rate' is 'la mi to' and 'silver price' is 'mi sa'. II. 'gold coin' is 'to re' and 'coin buy' is 're ka'.

Solution

I gives silver = mi, but gold and rate share la and to — not sufficient. II gives coin = re, so gold = to — sufficient. Answer: II alone is sufficient.

Practice questions

Question 1

On which day of the week did Rina visit the bank? I. She visited after Tuesday but before Thursday. II. She visited on a weekday.

  1. Statement II alone is sufficient
  2. Statement I alone is sufficient
  3. Either statement alone is sufficient
  4. Both statements together are needed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Statement I alone is sufficient

Statement I leaves only Wednesday, a unique answer. Statement II allows five different weekdays, so only statement I is sufficient.

Question 2

What is the code for 'pen'? I. 'pen cap' is written as '4 7'. II. 'pen book' is '4 9' and 'book bag' is '9 2'.

  1. Statement I alone is sufficient
  2. Either statement alone is sufficient
  3. Statement II alone is sufficient
  4. Both statements together are needed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Statement II alone is sufficient

Statement I leaves pen as either 4 or 7. Statement II fixes book as 9 because it is common to both phrases, so pen must be 4.

Question 3

Is T sitting at an end of a row of five people facing north? I. R sits in the middle and T sits second to the right of R. II. Exactly four people sit to the left of T.

  1. Statement I alone is sufficient
  2. Statement II alone is sufficient
  3. Both statements together are needed
  4. Either statement alone is sufficient
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Either statement alone is sufficient

From I, R is third and T is fifth, the right end. From II, four people to T's left also puts T fifth. Each statement alone answers the question.

Question 4

Among P, Q, R and S, who is the heaviest? I. P is heavier than Q and R. II. R is heavier than S.

  1. Both statements together are needed
  2. Statement I alone is sufficient
  3. Statement II alone is sufficient
  4. Either statement alone is sufficient
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Both statements together are needed

I alone leaves S unplaced and II alone says nothing about P or Q. Together P is heavier than Q and R, and R is heavier than S, so P is the heaviest.

Question 5

Is X the father of Y? I. Y is the son of Z, and Z is the wife of X. II. X is the brother of W, and W is the aunt of Y.

  1. Statement II alone is sufficient
  2. Statement I alone is sufficient
  3. Either statement alone is sufficient
  4. Both statements together are needed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Statement I alone is sufficient

From I, Z is Y's mother and X is her husband, so X is Y's father. From II, X could be Y's father or an uncle, so only I is sufficient.

FAQs

Is a definite 'No' answer considered sufficient?

Yes. Data sufficiency only checks whether the question can be answered with certainty. If a statement proves that X is definitely not the father of Y, the question 'Is X the father of Y?' is answered, so that statement is sufficient. Many candidates lose marks by treating 'no' as insufficient.

How is reasoning DS different from quant DS?

The logic of the options is identical. Reasoning DS draws on puzzles, coding, directions and relations, so the traps are gender, ambiguous order and multiple arrangements. Quant DS relies on equations and number properties. Practising both sharpens the same habit of testing statements independently.
