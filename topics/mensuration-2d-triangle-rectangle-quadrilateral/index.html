Mensuration 2D: Triangle, Rectangle, Rhombus, Trapezium

Two-dimensional mensuration covers the area and perimeter of plane figures. SSC CGL and CHSL test it heavily, often three or four questions with geometry, and IBPS PO and SBI PO include one mensuration question in many prelims papers, frequently in quantity comparison. Questions are formula-driven, so a clean formula sheet and recognition of common Pythagorean triples (3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17) matter more than clever tricks.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Rectangles and squares

Rectangle: area = l × b, perimeter = 2(l + b), diagonal = √(l² + b²). Square: area = a², perimeter = 4a, diagonal = a√2, so area also equals diagonal²/2. Fencing cost uses perimeter; flooring or tiling uses area.

Square area = d² / 2

Triangles

Area = ½ × base × height. When only the three sides are known, use Heron’s formula with s = (a + b + c)/2. For an equilateral triangle of side a, area = (√3/4)a² and height = (√3/2)a.

Area = √[s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)]

Rhombus, parallelogram and trapezium

Rhombus area = ½ × d1 × d2; its diagonals bisect at right angles, so side = √((d1/2)² + (d2/2)²). Parallelogram area = base × height. Trapezium area = ½ × (sum of parallel sides) × height.

Trapezium = ½(a + b)h

Paths around or inside a field

For a path of width w outside an l × b rectangle, path area = (l + 2w)(b + 2w) − lb = 2w(l + b + 2w). For a path inside, use (l × b) − (l − 2w)(b − 2w). Cross roads through the middle are l × w + b × w − w².

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Learn triples: 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25, 9-40-41 and their multiples like 6-8-10.
  • Heron’s formula with 13-14-15 gives 84 and with 9-10-17 gives 36 — common exam triangles.
  • If every side of a figure is scaled by k, its area scales by k² and perimeter by k.
  • Among rectangles of fixed perimeter, the square has the largest area.
  • Cross roads: two 3 m roads through a 50 m × 30 m park cover 50 × 3 + 30 × 3 − 3 × 3 = 231 m², because the central square is counted twice.

Worked examples

Example 1

A rectangular field is 60 m long and 40 m wide. A path 2.5 m wide runs around it on the outside. Find the area of the path.

Solution

Outer dimensions are 65 m × 45 m = 2,925 m². Field = 2,400 m². Path = 525 m². Formula check: 2 × 2.5 × (60 + 40 + 5) = 525 m².

Example 2

Find the area of a triangle with sides 13 cm, 14 cm and 15 cm.

Solution

s = 21. Area = √(21 × 8 × 7 × 6) = √7,056 = 84 cm².

Example 3

Find the area of an equilateral triangle of side 12 cm.

Solution

Area = (√3/4) × 144 = 36√3 ≈ 62.35 cm².

Example 4

The diagonals of a rhombus are 24 cm and 10 cm. Find its area and side.

Solution

Area = ½ × 24 × 10 = 120 cm². Half-diagonals are 12 and 5, so side = 13 cm.

Example 5

A square plot has an area of 1,600 m². Find the cost of fencing it at ₹25 per metre.

Solution

Side = 40 m, perimeter = 160 m. Cost = 160 × 25 = ₹4,000. Note that the rate is per metre, so it applies to the perimeter, not to the area of 1,600 m².

Example 6

The parallel sides of a trapezium-shaped plot are 18 m and 12 m and the distance between them is 8 m. Find its area and the cost of levelling it at ₹15 per m².

Solution

Area = ½ × (18 + 12) × 8 = 15 × 8 = 120 m². Cost = 120 × 15 = ₹1,800. Here the rate is per square metre, so it multiplies the area. Always match the unit of the rate before multiplying.

Practice questions

Question 1

A rectangle has perimeter 78 m and length 24 m. Its area is:

  1. 336 m²
  2. 384 m²
  3. 360 m²
  4. 390 m²
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 360 m²

Breadth = 78/2 − 24 = 15 m, so the area is 24 × 15 = 360 square metres.

Question 2

The area of a triangle with sides 9 cm, 10 cm and 17 cm is:

  1. 36 cm²
  2. 45 cm²
  3. 42 cm²
  4. 30 cm²
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 36 cm²

With s = 18, Heron’s formula gives √(18 × 9 × 8 × 1) = √1,296 = 36 square centimetres.

Question 3

The diagonals of a rhombus are 16 cm and 30 cm. Its side is:

  1. 15 cm
  2. 23 cm
  3. 19 cm
  4. 17 cm
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 17 cm

Half-diagonals are 8 and 15 cm, which form an 8-15-17 right triangle, so the side is 17 cm.

Question 4

A garden is 30 m × 20 m. A path 2 m wide runs inside along its edges. The area of the path is:

  1. 200 m²
  2. 184 m²
  3. 216 m²
  4. 176 m²
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 184 m²

Inner area is 26 × 16 = 416 m², so the path covers 600 − 416 = 184 square metres.

Question 5

The diagonal of a square is 12√2 m. Its area is:

  1. 144 m²
  2. 288 m²
  3. 72 m²
  4. 169 m²
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 144 m²

Area = diagonal²/2 = 288/2 = 144 m², equivalently a side of 12 m squared.

FAQs

Do I need Heron’s formula for bank exams?

Occasionally. Most bank questions give base and height or a right triangle, but Heron’s formula is useful when three sides are given, especially in SSC. Memorise the standard triangles 13-14-15 (area 84) and 5-12-13 (area 30) to save time.

How do I avoid unit mistakes in mensuration?

Convert all lengths to the same unit before calculating, and remember that area units are squared: 1 m² = 10,000 cm² and 1 hectare = 10,000 m². Cost questions usually give a rate per metre or per square metre; match it to perimeter or area accordingly.
