Discount and Marked Price: Formulas and Tricks

Discount questions extend profit and loss by adding a third price, the marked price (MP) or list price. IBPS Clerk and SBI Clerk ask one question regularly, SSC CGL and CHSL ask two or three, and DI caselets about shops often include marked price and discount. Festive sales in India, with offers such as "flat 20% + extra 10%" or "buy 2 get 1 free", are exactly the situations these questions model.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Three prices

Marked price is the printed price, selling price is what the customer pays after discount, and cost price is what the seller paid. Discount is always calculated on the marked price, while profit is always calculated on cost price. Mixing the two bases is the main source of errors.

SP = MP × (100 − d) / 100

Successive discounts

Two discounts of a% and b% are not equal to a single discount of (a + b)%. The single equivalent discount is a + b − ab/100. Flat 20% plus extra 10% equals 28%, not 30%. For three discounts, combine two first and then the third.

Equivalent = a + b − ab/100

Linking markup, discount and profit

If the seller marks the item m% above cost and then gives d% discount, SP = CP × (1 + m/100)(1 − d/100). The ratio MP : CP equals (100 + profit%) : (100 − discount%), which answers "how much above cost should he mark" questions in one line.

MP / CP = (100 + p) / (100 − d)

Free-item offers

In "buy x get y free", the customer pays for x items but takes x + y. The effective discount is y/(x + y) × 100. Buy 3 get 1 free is a 25% discount, not 33.33%.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Equivalent of 10% and 10% is 19%; of 20% and 20% is 36%; of 10% and 20% is 28%.
  • Marked 25% above cost and sold at 20% discount gives no profit, no loss (1.25 × 0.8 = 1).
  • Marked 50% above cost with a 20% discount gives 20% profit (1.5 × 0.8 = 1.2).
  • For "buy x get y free", the discount fraction is y/(x + y); for "buy x for the price of y" it is (x − y)/x.
  • If you know SP and two successive discounts, divide by both factors to get MP: SP ÷ 0.8 ÷ 0.9.

Worked examples

Example 1

A jacket is marked at ₹2,500 and two successive discounts of 20% and 10% are given. Find the selling price and the single equivalent discount.

Solution

SP = 2,500 × 0.8 × 0.9 = ₹1,800. Equivalent discount = 20 + 10 − (20 × 10)/100 = 28%, which matches ₹700 off ₹2,500.

Example 2

A trader marks goods 40% above cost price and allows a discount of 25%. Find his profit percentage.

Solution

Take CP = 100, so MP = 140. SP = 140 × 0.75 = 105. Profit = 5%.

Example 3

A store offers "buy 3 shirts, get 1 free". What is the effective discount?

Solution

The customer pays for 3 but takes 4. Discount = 1/4 × 100 = 25%.

Example 4

A shopkeeper wants a profit of 20% after allowing a 10% discount. By what percent above cost should he mark his goods?

Solution

MP/CP = 120/90 = 4/3, so the marked price must be 33.33% above cost price.

Example 5

A washing machine is marked at ₹5,000. After a 15% discount the dealer still earns a profit of 6.25%. Find the cost price.

Solution

SP = 5,000 × 0.85 = ₹4,250. Since this includes a 6.25% profit, CP = 4,250 ÷ 1.0625 = ₹4,000. Note that 6.25% = 1/16, so CP = 4,250 × 16/17, which is quicker to compute mentally.

Practice questions

Question 1

A bag is marked at ₹1,200 and sold at a discount of 15%. Find the selling price.

  1. ₹1,080
  2. ₹1,020
  3. ₹1,050
  4. ₹1,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹1,020

SP = 1,200 × 0.85 = ₹1,020, since 15% of 1,200 is ₹180 off the marked price.

Question 2

Two successive discounts of 20% and 5% are equivalent to a single discount of:

  1. 24%
  2. 25%
  3. 23%
  4. 24.5%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 24%

Equivalent discount = 20 + 5 − (20 × 5)/100 = 25 − 1 = 24 percent overall.

Question 3

An item is marked 50% above cost and sold at 20% discount. The profit percentage is:

  1. 30%
  2. 25%
  3. 10%
  4. 20%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 20%

Taking CP as 100, MP is 150 and SP is 150 × 0.8 = 120, so the profit is 20 percent.

Question 4

An offer says "buy 4, get 1 free". The effective discount is:

  1. 25%
  2. 15%
  3. 20%
  4. 22.5%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 20%

The buyer pays for 4 but receives 5 items, so the discount is 1/5 × 100 = 20 percent.

Question 5

An article costs ₹800. The seller wants 25% profit after giving a 20% discount. What should the marked price be?

  1. ₹1,200
  2. ₹1,250
  3. ₹1,150
  4. ₹1,280
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹1,250

Required SP = 800 × 1.25 = ₹1,000, and MP = 1,000 ÷ 0.8 = ₹1,250 so that 20% off gives ₹1,000.

FAQs

Is discount calculated on cost price?

No. Discount is always on the marked price, because that is the price shown to the customer. Profit or loss is on cost price. Keeping these two bases separate solves most discount questions correctly.

Why is 20% plus 10% not 30%?

The second discount applies to the already reduced price. After 20% off ₹100 the price is ₹80, and 10% of ₹80 is only ₹8, so the total discount is ₹28 or 28%. The formula a + b − ab/100 captures this.
