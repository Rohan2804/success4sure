Prepositions in English: Time, Place and Fixed Pairings

Prepositions are small words with a big share of English errors. SSC CGL, CHSL and MTS error-spotting and fill-in-the-blank questions test them often, and IBPS and SBI cloze tests and fillers frequently hinge on one preposition. Some uses follow clear rules, like 'in', 'on' and 'at' for time and place; many others are fixed pairings that simply have to be learned, such as 'comply with' or 'accused of'. Indian English habits like 'discuss about' are frequent traps.

In, on, at for time

'At' for exact times and short points (at 9 a.m., at noon, at night). 'On' for days and dates (on Monday, on 15 August, on Diwali day). 'In' for longer periods (in July, in 2026, in the morning, in summer).

In, on, at for place

'At' for a point or address (at the bank, at the station). 'On' for surfaces and lines (on the table, on the road, on page 10). 'In' for enclosed spaces and larger areas (in the room, in Mumbai, in India).

Since and for

'Since' marks the starting point (since 2020, since Monday); 'for' marks the length of time (for six years, for two days). Both are normally used with perfect tenses: 'She has worked here since 2020 / for six years.'

Verbs that need no preposition

Some verbs take a direct object without a preposition: discuss, describe, reach, enter (a room), attack, resemble, approach, order. 'Discuss about', 'reach to' and 'enter into the room' are errors (though 'enter into an agreement' is correct).

Fixed pairings

Learn common pairs: accused of, afraid of, comply with, consist of, depend on, insist on, interested in, married to, prefer to, senior to, superior to, congratulate on, abstain from, aim at, apply for (a job), apply to (a person or body).

Between and among; beside and besides

'Between' for two people or things, 'among' for more than two. 'Beside' means next to; 'besides' means in addition to. 'Divide the money between the two brothers' but 'among the five sisters'. Adjectives also take fixed prepositions: aware of, capable of, good at, keen on, responsible for, satisfied with, similar to, jealous of, proud of, eligible for. 'She is eligible for the scholarship' and 'He is responsible for the branch' are typical exam sentences, and options often swap 'for' with 'to' or 'of' to test you.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • 'Since' never goes with a duration: 'since five years' is always wrong.
  • 'Married to', not 'married with'; 'senior to', not 'senior than'.
  • 'Comprise' takes no 'of'; 'consist of' does: 'The board comprises five members' / 'consists of five members'.
  • 'Different from' is the safest choice in exams; avoid 'different than'.
  • 'In time' means early enough; 'on time' means punctually.
  • When unsure, recall a standard phrase from newspapers: 'comply with norms', 'insist on transparency'.

Worked examples

Example 1

Fill in: 'The exam starts ______ 10 a.m. ______ Sunday ______ March.'

Solution

Exact time → at; day → on; month → in. 'The exam starts at 10 a.m. on Sunday in March.'

Example 2

Correct the error: 'We will discuss about the proposal tomorrow.'

Solution

'Discuss' takes a direct object. Correct: 'We will discuss the proposal tomorrow.'

Example 3

Correct the error: 'She has been living in Pune since three years.'

Solution

'Three years' is a duration, so use 'for'. Correct: 'She has been living in Pune for three years.'

Example 4

Fill in: 'All branches must comply ______ the new KYC norms.'

Solution

'Comply' takes 'with'. Answer: with.

Example 5

Fill in: 'The profit was divided ______ the three partners.'

Solution

More than two people → 'among'. Answer: among.

Example 6

Fill in: 'She applied ______ the post of probationary officer and sent her form ______ the recruitment board.'

Solution

You apply 'for' a job or post and 'to' the organisation or person who receives the application. Answer: for, to. Mixing these two is a frequent error in cover letters as well as in exams.

Practice questions

Question 1

Choose the correct preposition: 'He was accused ______ cheating in the exam.'

  1. for
  2. with
  3. on
  4. of
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. of

'Accuse' is followed by 'of' in standard English. 'Accused of cheating' is the correct fixed pairing.

Question 2

Choose the correct preposition: 'The train reached ______ Chennai late at night.'

  1. no preposition
  2. to
  3. at
  4. in
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. no preposition

'Reach' takes a direct object, so no preposition is needed. 'The train reached Chennai' is correct.

Question 3

Choose the correct preposition: 'She is married ______ a doctor.'

  1. with
  2. to
  3. by
  4. for
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. to

The correct fixed expression is 'married to'. 'Married with' is a common Indian-English error tested in exams.

Question 4

Choose the correct preposition: 'Besides English, he speaks Kannada and Telugu. He sat ______ me during the meeting.'

  1. besides
  2. between
  3. beside
  4. among
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. beside

'Beside' means next to, which fits sitting during a meeting. 'Besides' means in addition to, as used in the first sentence.

Question 5

Choose the correct preposition: 'I prefer working in the morning ______ working late at night.'

  1. than
  2. over than
  3. from
  4. to
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. to

'Prefer' is followed by 'to' when comparing two things. 'Prefer working in the morning to working late' is correct.

FAQs

Is 'in the night' wrong?

Standard usage is 'at night' for night-time in general and 'in the night' for something happening during a particular night ('I woke up in the night'). In exam sentences about general habits, 'at night' is the expected answer, while 'in the morning', 'in the afternoon' and 'in the evening' use 'in'.

How do I learn fixed prepositions efficiently?

Make a list of about 100 common verb-preposition and adjective-preposition pairs with one example sentence each. Revise a section daily and test yourself with fill-in-the-blank drills. Noticing these pairs while reading newspapers reinforces them much faster than memorising alone.
