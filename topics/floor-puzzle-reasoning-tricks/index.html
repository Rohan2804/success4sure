Floor Puzzles in Reasoning: Step-by-Step Solving Method

Floor puzzles ask you to place people on the floors of a building using clues such as 'lives immediately above' or 'only two people live between'. IBPS PO, SBI PO, IBPS Clerk and RBI Grade B prelims commonly include one floor or floor-and-flat puzzle carrying five questions, and mains papers add a second variable such as city or profession. The method is mechanical: a vertical table, definite clues first, then cases, then negative clues.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Number from the bottom

The lowest floor is floor 1 unless the question says the ground floor is numbered 0. Write floors top to bottom in a column on your rough sheet, with the highest number at the top, exactly as a building looks. Read the first line of the puzzle carefully for 'ground floor' wording.

Above, immediately above and between

'Above' means any higher floor. 'Immediately above' means the very next floor. 'Only two people live between X and Y' means their floor numbers differ by three. 'X lives on an odd-numbered floor' cuts the options in half and is often the clue that kills a case.

Floor-and-flat variants

Some puzzles have two flats per floor (flat A to the west, flat B to the east). Draw a grid with floors as rows and flats as columns. 'Same flat type' means same column; 'directly above' means same column, next row.

Extra attributes

When each person also has a colour, city or job, add columns to the table. Never start a new table for the second attribute; keeping one table makes cross-clues ('the person who likes red lives above the doctor') easy to apply.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • The top and bottom floors behave like the two ends of a row; clues tying someone to them are your anchors.
  • 'Only two people between' in a seven-floor building, with one person on floor 7, puts the other on floor 4.
  • When a clue gives a range ('B lives above A'), hold it until a definite clue narrows it.
  • Use odd/even floor clues to eliminate cases rather than to place people first.
  • Before answering, re-read every clue once against the final table; it takes 20 seconds and catches slips.

Worked examples

Example 1

Seven people A–G live on a seven-floor building, one per floor (floor 1 is the lowest). D lives on the top floor. Only two people live between D and E. A lives immediately below E. B lives on an even-numbered floor above E. C lives on a floor below F. G does not live on floor 1 or 2. Find the arrangement.

Solution

D = 7. Two people between D and E puts E on floor 4. A = 3. B is above E on an even floor: 6. Floors 1, 2 and 5 remain for C, F and G. G is not on 1 or 2, so G = 5. C is below F, so C = 1 and F = 2. Building from the bottom: C, F, A, E, G, B, D.

Example 2

Using the building above, who lives between A and B, and how many floors above C does B live?

Solution

A is on floor 3 and B on floor 6, so E (4) and G (5) live between them. B is on floor 6 and C on floor 1, so B lives five floors above C.

Example 3

Six people live on floors 1–6. A lives on floor 2. B lives three floors above A. C lives immediately above B. D lives below A. E lives below F. Who lives on floor 4?

Solution

A = 2, B = 5, C = 6, and D must be on floor 1. Floors 3 and 4 remain for E and F. E is below F, so E = 3 and F = 4. F lives on floor 4.

Practice questions

Question 1

Five people P, Q, R, S and T live on floors 1 to 5 (1 is the lowest). R lives on floor 3. P lives immediately above R. T lives below R. Q does not live on floor 5 and lives below T. S lives above T. Who lives on the top floor?

  1. P
  2. Q
  3. T
  4. S
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. S

R is on floor 3 and P on floor 4. T and Q must share floors 1 and 2 with Q below T, so Q = 1 and T = 2. Only floor 5 is left, which goes to S.

Question 2

In a seven-floor building, D lives on floor 7 and exactly two people live between D and E. On which floor does E live?

  1. Floor 4
  2. Floor 3
  3. Floor 5
  4. Floor 2
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Floor 4

Two people between them means their floor numbers differ by three. Since D is on the top floor, E must be on floor 7 − 3 = 4.

Question 3

Six people live on floors 1–6. A lives on floor 2. B lives three floors above A. C lives immediately above B. D lives below A. E lives below F. Who lives immediately above E?

  1. B
  2. F
  3. C
  4. D
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. F

A = 2, B = 5, C = 6 and D = 1. E and F take floors 3 and 4 with E below F, so F lives immediately above E.

Question 4

In a floor puzzle, 'X lives above Y' is given. What can you conclude without other clues?

  1. X lives on the floor just above Y
  2. X lives on the top floor
  3. X's floor number is greater than Y's
  4. Only one person lives between X and Y
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. X's floor number is greater than Y's

'Above' without 'immediately' means any higher floor. It only tells you X's floor number is higher than Y's, not how many floors separate them.

Question 5

Seven people live on floors 1–7. C lives on floor 1, F on floor 2, A on floor 3, E on floor 4, G on floor 5, B on floor 6 and D on floor 7. How many people live between F and G?

  1. Three
  2. One
  3. Four
  4. Two
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Two

F is on floor 2 and G on floor 5. The floors between them are 3 and 4, occupied by A and E, so two people live between them.

FAQs

What if the puzzle says the ground floor is numbered 0?

Then the lowest floor is 0 and the top floor of a seven-storey building is 6. Odd and even clues change accordingly, so re-label your table before placing anyone. This single detail flips many answers, which is why examiners include it.

Should I solve floor puzzles in the order the clues are given?

No. Scan all clues first and start with the definite ones (top floor, bottom floor, a fixed floor number). Then use clues linked to already-placed people. Use negative and range clues last to choose between cases.
