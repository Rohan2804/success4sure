Banking Awareness for Interviews: Key Terms Explained

Bank interviews for IBPS PO, SBI PO, RBI Grade B and specialist posts almost always test basic banking awareness: what a bank earns from, how bad loans are classified, why capital matters. The same terms appear in mains General Awareness, JAIIB and CAIIB. Interviewers care less about memorised definitions than about whether you can explain a term in plain words with an example. This page covers the static concepts; ratios and limits change, so confirm current figures from RBI circulars.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

CASA and cost of funds

CASA is the share of current and savings account deposits in total deposits. These deposits pay low or no interest, so a higher CASA ratio lowers a bank's cost of funds. Example: if a bank has ₹300 crore of CASA out of ₹1,000 crore deposits, its CASA ratio is 30%.

Net interest margin

NIM measures how much a bank earns from lending and investing over what it pays on deposits and borrowings, relative to its earning assets. It is a core indicator of profitability for a lending business.

Non-performing assets

A loan generally becomes an NPA when interest or principal remains overdue for more than 90 days. NPAs are classified as substandard, doubtful and loss assets depending on how long they remain non-performing. Gross NPA is before provisions; net NPA subtracts provisions held.

Provisioning and capital

Provision coverage ratio shows how much of gross NPAs is covered by provisions. Capital to risk-weighted assets ratio (CRAR) measures a bank's capital against its risk exposure, following the Basel III framework as implemented by the RBI. Higher capital means a bank can absorb more losses.

Priority sector lending

The RBI requires banks to lend a portion of their adjusted net bank credit to priority sectors such as agriculture, MSMEs, education, housing and weaker sections, with sub-targets. Domestic scheduled commercial banks have an overall target of 40%; check the RBI master direction for current targets.

Recovery laws and KYC

The SARFAESI Act, 2002 lets secured lenders take possession of and sell secured assets without court intervention. The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 provides a time-bound resolution process through the NCLT. KYC norms under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act require banks to verify customer identity and address and to update records periodically.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • NPA trigger: overdue for more than 90 days.
  • High CASA = cheaper funds = better margins.
  • Gross NPA − provisions = net NPA (in simple terms).
  • SARFAESI = enforce security without court; IBC = resolve insolvency through NCLT.
  • Priority sector overall target for domestic SCBs: 40% of ANBC (verify current norms).
  • In interviews, define the term, give a one-line example, and add why it matters to a bank.

Worked examples

Example 1

Interview: 'What is CASA and why do banks want a high CASA ratio?'

Solution

CASA is the share of current and savings deposits in total deposits. These deposits cost less than fixed deposits, so a bank with a higher CASA ratio has lower funding costs and can lend competitively while protecting its margin. Example: ₹300 crore CASA out of ₹1,000 crore deposits is a 30% ratio.

Example 2

A borrower's EMI due on 1 June remains unpaid. Roughly when would the loan be classified as an NPA if nothing is paid?

Solution

Once the amount stays overdue for more than 90 days, the account becomes an NPA, so around the end of August or beginning of September, based on the RBI's day-end classification rules.

Example 3

A bank has gross NPAs of ₹120 crore and provisions of ₹45 crore. What is its approximate net NPA and provision coverage?

Solution

Net NPA ≈ 120 − 45 = ₹75 crore. Provision coverage ratio ≈ 45 ÷ 120 = 37.5% (ignoring technical write-offs for simplicity).

Example 4

Interview: 'How is SARFAESI different from IBC?'

Solution

SARFAESI is a tool for secured creditors to recover dues by taking over and selling collateral without going to court. IBC is a broader insolvency process, run through the NCLT, that tries to resolve the whole company, often by finding a new owner, within a time limit.

Practice questions

Question 1

After how many days of non-payment does a loan generally become an NPA?

  1. More than 30 days
  2. More than 60 days
  3. More than 180 days
  4. More than 90 days
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. More than 90 days

Under RBI norms, a loan is generally classified as non-performing when interest or principal remains overdue for more than 90 days.

Question 2

A bank has ₹300 crore in current and savings deposits out of total deposits of ₹1,000 crore. What is its CASA ratio?

  1. 30%
  2. 3%
  3. 70%
  4. 33%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 30%

CASA ratio equals CASA deposits divided by total deposits, so 300 ÷ 1,000 = 30%.

Question 3

Which law allows secured lenders to take possession of collateral without court intervention?

  1. Banking Regulation Act
  2. SARFAESI Act
  3. RBI Act
  4. Negotiable Instruments Act
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. SARFAESI Act

The SARFAESI Act, 2002 empowers secured creditors to enforce their security interest and recover dues without approaching a court.

Question 4

Which international framework guides capital adequacy norms for Indian banks?

  1. FEMA
  2. SARFAESI
  3. Basel III
  4. IBC
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Basel III

The RBI implements the Basel III framework for capital adequacy, which sets minimum capital and buffer requirements for banks.

Question 5

Under the IBC, which body hears corporate insolvency cases?

  1. Supreme Court
  2. RBI
  3. SEBI
  4. National Company Law Tribunal
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. National Company Law Tribunal

Corporate insolvency resolution under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 is handled by the National Company Law Tribunal.

FAQs

How should I prepare banking awareness for an interview?

Pick about 40 core terms — CASA, NIM, NPA, PCR, CRAR, repo, CRR, SLR, priority sector, KYC, SARFAESI, IBC and similar — and practise explaining each in two or three sentences with an example. Add recent developments from RBI press releases. Interviewers value clarity and accuracy over long memorised answers.

Will interviewers ask for exact current ratios?

Sometimes, especially in RBI and PO interviews. Keep a short note of current figures such as the repo rate, CRR, SLR and the system's gross NPA level from the latest RBI Financial Stability Report, and be ready to say 'as per the latest RBI data' when quoting them.
