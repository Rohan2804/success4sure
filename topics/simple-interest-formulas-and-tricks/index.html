Simple Interest Formulas and Tricks for Competitive Exams

Simple interest (SI) is one of the most scoring arithmetic topics in IBPS Clerk, SBI Clerk, SSC CHSL and SSC CGL. Expect one question on its own and another combined with compound interest. Because interest is earned only on the original principal, every year adds the same rupee amount, which makes most SI questions solvable with a straight-line, "per-year" way of thinking rather than heavy formulas.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The formula

SI = P × R × T / 100, where P is the principal in rupees, R the rate per cent per annum and T the time in years. Amount A = P + SI. Months must be converted to years: 9 months = 0.75 year, 3 years 6 months = 3.5 years. For days, divide by 365.

SI = P × R × T / 100

Interest per year is constant

Since interest is on the principal only, the amount grows by the same figure each year. If a sum amounts to ₹8,960 after 3 years and ₹9,600 after 5 years, the 2-year gap shows ₹640 of interest, so ₹320 a year. Subtract 3 × 320 from ₹8,960 to get the principal.

Multiples of the principal

A sum becomes n times itself when the interest equals (n − 1) × P. So the time needed is (n − 1) × 100 / R. If a sum doubles in 8 years, R = 12.5%, and it becomes four times (interest = 3P) in 24 years.

T = (n − 1) × 100 / R

Splitting a sum

When a total is lent in two parts at different rates and the combined interest is known, set one part as x and write a single equation. Alligation also works: the overall rate sits between the two rates, and the distances give the ratio of the parts.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Work in "per cent of principal": at 8% for 3.5 years, the interest is 28% of P.
  • If the time and rate are interchanged, SI stays the same: P at 5% for 8 years = P at 8% for 5 years.
  • When two amounts are given for two times, the difference ÷ time gap = yearly interest.
  • Rate × time = 100 means the sum has doubled; = 200 means it has tripled.
  • For a rate like 6.25% (1/16), think in fractions: SI for 4 years is 1/4 of the principal.

Worked examples

Example 1

Find the simple interest on ₹12,000 at 8% per annum for 3 years 6 months.

Solution

T = 3.5 years. SI = 12,000 × 8 × 3.5 / 100 = ₹3,360. Alternatively, 8 × 3.5 = 28% of ₹12,000 = ₹3,360.

Example 2

A sum amounts to ₹8,960 in 3 years and ₹9,600 in 5 years at simple interest. Find the principal and the rate.

Solution

Interest for 2 years = 9,600 − 8,960 = ₹640, so ₹320 per year. Interest for 3 years = ₹960, principal = 8,960 − 960 = ₹8,000. Rate = 320/8,000 × 100 = 4% per annum.

Example 3

A sum doubles in 8 years at simple interest. In how many years will it become four times?

Solution

Doubling means interest = P in 8 years, so R = 100/8 = 12.5%. Four times needs interest = 3P, which takes 3 × 8 = 24 years.

Example 4

Rohit lends ₹10,000 in two parts, one at 6% and the other at 9% simple interest. The total yearly interest is ₹750. How much did he lend at 6%?

Solution

Let x be lent at 6%. Then 0.06x + 0.09(10,000 − x) = 750, so 900 − 0.03x = 750 and x = ₹5,000. The other part is also ₹5,000, which makes sense because 7.5% is exactly halfway between 6% and 9%.

Practice questions

Question 1

Find the simple interest on ₹6,500 at 7% per annum for 4 years.

  1. ₹1,820
  2. ₹1,750
  3. ₹1,880
  4. ₹1,920
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹1,820

SI = 6,500 × 7 × 4 / 100 = 6,500 × 0.28 = ₹1,820 for the four years.

Question 2

A sum becomes 1.5 times itself in 5 years at simple interest. The rate of interest is:

  1. 8%
  2. 12%
  3. 10%
  4. 15%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 10%

Interest equals 0.5 P in 5 years, so the rate is 50/5 = 10 percent per annum.

Question 3

The simple interest on a sum for 3 years at 9% per annum is ₹2,430. The sum is:

  1. ₹8,100
  2. ₹9,500
  3. ₹8,500
  4. ₹9,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹9,000

P = SI × 100 / (R × T) = 2,430 × 100 / 27 = ₹9,000 as the principal.

Question 4

A sum amounts to ₹5,600 in 2 years and ₹6,200 in 4 years at simple interest. The principal is:

  1. ₹4,800
  2. ₹5,000
  3. ₹5,200
  4. ₹4,400
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹5,000

Yearly interest is (6,200 − 5,600)/2 = ₹300, so principal = 5,600 − 600 = ₹5,000.

Question 5

A sum triples itself in 20 years at simple interest. In how many years will it become five times?

  1. 35 years
  2. 30 years
  3. 50 years
  4. 40 years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 40 years

Tripling means interest 2P in 20 years, so 10% a year; five times needs 4P, which takes 40 years.

FAQs

Is simple interest used in real Indian banking?

Most bank deposits compound interest quarterly, but simple interest concepts are used for short-term loans, some gold loans and interest on overdue amounts. In exams, SI is tested mainly as a calculation skill and as a base for comparing with compound interest.

How do I convert days into years for SI questions?

Divide the number of days by 365. Count days carefully between dates: usually the day of deposit is excluded and the day of withdrawal included. Exam questions mostly use friendly numbers like 73 days, which is exactly 1/5 of a year.
