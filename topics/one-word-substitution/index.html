One-Word Substitution: High-Frequency Words and Roots

One-word substitution asks you to replace a phrase or sentence with a single word — for example, 'a person who cannot be corrected' becomes 'incorrigible'. SSC CGL, CHSL and MTS ask two or three such questions per English section, and the same vocabulary helps in bank-exam fillers and reading comprehension. Many words share roots with other exam words, so learning them in themed groups is far more effective than memorising a random list.

People by habit or role

Philanthropist (one who loves and helps mankind), misanthrope (one who hates mankind), optimist and pessimist, introvert and extrovert, polyglot (one who speaks many languages), novice (a beginner), veteran (an experienced person), connoisseur (an expert judge of art or taste).

Fears and '-cide' words

Fears end in '-phobia': hydrophobia (water), claustrophobia (closed spaces), acrophobia (heights). Killing words end in '-cide': homicide (a person), suicide (oneself), fratricide (a brother), genocide (a whole group).

Government and society

Democracy (rule by the people), autocracy (rule by one person with absolute power), bureaucracy (rule by officials), plutocracy (rule by the wealthy), anarchy (absence of government), theocracy (rule by religious leaders).

Places and collections

Apiary (bees), aviary (birds), aquarium (fish), granary (grain), arsenal (weapons), archives (records), mint (where coins are made), dispensary (where medicines are given).

Study of things

'-logy' means study: ornithology (birds), anthropology (humans), seismology (earthquakes), etymology (word origins), entomology (insects). Etymology and entomology are a favourite look-alike pair.

Words about speech and writing

Several frequent words describe speaking or writing: soliloquy (speaking to oneself, especially in a play), monologue (a long speech by one person), eulogy (praise for someone, often after death), epitaph (words written on a tomb), autobiography (a life story written by the person), biography (a life story written by someone else), anonymous (of unknown name), and pseudonym (a false name used by a writer).

Shortcuts and tricks

  • 'Omni-' means all: omniscient (all-knowing), omnipotent (all-powerful), omnipresent (present everywhere).
  • '-cracy' means rule by; '-archy' also means rule (monarchy, anarchy).
  • 'In-' or 'im-' often means not: incorrigible (cannot be corrected), invincible (cannot be defeated), illegible (cannot be read).
  • Pair opposites: optimist–pessimist, introvert–extrovert, theist–atheist.
  • Watch look-alikes: etymology (words) versus entomology (insects); aviary (birds) versus apiary (bees).
  • When unsure, break the option into root + suffix and check which combination matches the phrase.
  • '-ist' and '-er' usually mark a person (philatelist: stamp collector; numismatist: coin collector), while '-ary' and '-ium' often mark a place (granary, aquarium).

Worked examples

Example 1

A person who speaks many languages: polyglot, linguist, translator, orator.

Solution

'Poly' means many and 'glot' relates to tongue or language. A linguist studies language and need not speak many. Answer: polyglot.

Example 2

A place where bees are kept: aviary, apiary, aquarium, granary.

Solution

An apiary houses bees ('apis' is Latin for bee). An aviary is for birds — the usual trap. Answer: apiary.

Example 3

Government by the wealthy: aristocracy, plutocracy, autocracy, democracy.

Solution

'Pluto' relates to wealth, and '-cracy' means rule. Aristocracy is rule by nobles. Answer: plutocracy.

Example 4

Handwriting that cannot be read: illegible, ineligible, illiterate, indelible.

Solution

'Legible' means readable, so 'illegible' means not readable. 'Ineligible' means not qualified and 'indelible' means cannot be erased. Answer: illegible.

Example 5

The study of insects: etymology, entomology, ornithology, zoology.

Solution

Entomology is the study of insects. Etymology is the study of word origins — a close look-alike. Answer: entomology.

Example 6

A life history of a person written by himself or herself: biography, autobiography, memoir, chronicle.

Solution

'Auto' means self, so autobiography is a life story written by the person. A biography is written by someone else. A memoir is close but usually covers selected experiences rather than a full life. Answer: autobiography.

Practice questions

Question 1

One who cannot be corrected:

  1. Invincible
  2. Infallible
  3. Incorrigible
  4. Indelible
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Incorrigible

Incorrigible describes a person whose bad habits cannot be corrected. Invincible means unbeatable and infallible means never wrong.

Question 2

Fear of high places:

  1. Claustrophobia
  2. Hydrophobia
  3. Xenophobia
  4. Acrophobia
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Acrophobia

Acrophobia is the fear of heights. Claustrophobia is fear of closed spaces, hydrophobia fear of water and xenophobia fear of foreigners.

Question 3

One who knows everything:

  1. Omniscient
  2. Omnipotent
  3. Omnipresent
  4. Omnivorous
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Omniscient

'Omni' means all and 'scient' relates to knowing, so omniscient means all-knowing. Omnipotent means all-powerful.

Question 4

A place where coins are made:

  1. Arsenal
  2. Mint
  3. Archive
  4. Treasury
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Mint

A mint is where coins are produced. An arsenal stores weapons, an archive stores records, and a treasury manages public money.

Question 5

A person who hates mankind:

  1. Philanthropist
  2. Misogynist
  3. Misanthrope
  4. Anarchist
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Misanthrope

A misanthrope dislikes humankind in general. A philanthropist helps people, and a misogynist specifically dislikes women.

FAQs

What is the best way to learn one-word substitutions?

Learn them in themed groups — people, fears, killing, government, places, study — along with their roots. Grouping makes look-alikes easier to separate. Revise the same set several times over two weeks, then mix groups in self-tests to check recall under pressure.

Are one-word substitutions asked in bank exams?

Not usually as a separate question type, but the vocabulary appears in fillers, cloze tests and reading comprehension. SSC exams test them directly. If you are preparing for both, the time spent on one-word substitutions supports your bank-exam English as well.
