Data Sufficiency in Quant: Method, Traps and Examples

Data sufficiency (DS) asks whether given statements are enough to answer a question, not for the answer itself. IBPS PO, SBI PO, RBI Grade B and IBPS Clerk mains include DS sets in the quantitative and DI sections, and CAT and CMAT test it in DI and logical reasoning. DS rewards disciplined testing of each statement and punishes the habit of solving completely or assuming facts that are not stated.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Standard answer choices

The usual choices are: (a) statement I alone is sufficient; (b) statement II alone is sufficient; (c) either statement alone is sufficient; (d) both together are needed; (e) even both together are not sufficient. Some papers add a third statement, but the logic is the same.

Test in a fixed order

First test statement I alone, forgetting II. Then test II alone, forgetting I. Only if neither is sufficient alone, combine them. Mixing information from the other statement while testing one alone is the most common error.

Sufficient means a unique answer

A statement is sufficient only if it leads to exactly one value (or a definite yes or no). x² = 16 gives two values, so it is not sufficient for "what is x?" unless another fact rules one out. For yes/no questions, a definite "no" is also sufficient.

Do not solve fully

You need only to know that an answer can be found. Two independent linear equations in two unknowns are sufficient; you do not have to solve them. This saves time across the set.

Hidden dependence

Two statements that are really the same information (one equation is a multiple of the other) do not combine into a solution. Check that combined statements add genuinely new information.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Count unknowns and independent equations: n unknowns need n independent equations.
  • Ratios alone rarely give actual values; you also need one absolute quantity.
  • For speed questions, you need any two of distance, speed and time.
  • Yes/no questions: a consistent "no" counts as sufficient.
  • Never use outside assumptions such as "ages are integers" unless the question says so.

Worked examples

Example 1

What is x? I. 3x + 2y = 12. II. x − y = −1.

Solution

I alone has two unknowns — not sufficient. II alone also has two unknowns — not sufficient. Together they give x = 2, y = 3. Answer: both together are needed.

Example 2

Is the integer x even? I. 3x is even. II. x² is even.

Solution

I: 3x even means x even (3 is odd) — sufficient. II: x² even means x even — sufficient. Answer: either statement alone is sufficient.

Example 3

What is A’s age? I. A is 5 years older than B. II. B’s age is half of C’s age.

Solution

Even together there are three unknowns and only two relations. Answer: not sufficient even together.

Example 4

What is the speed of a train? I. It passes a pole in 12 seconds. II. It is 240 m long.

Solution

I gives time only; II gives distance only. Together, speed = 240/12 = 20 m/s. Answer: both together.

Example 5

What is the profit percentage on an item? I. Its cost price is ₹800. II. Its selling price is ₹920.

Solution

Each statement alone lacks the other price. Together, profit = ₹120, i.e. 15%. Answer: both together.

Example 6

What is x, given x² = 16? I. x > 0. II. x is an integer.

Solution

x = 4 or −4. I rules out −4, so x = 4 — sufficient. II allows both values — not sufficient. Answer: statement I alone.

Practice questions

Question 1

What is the ratio of the ages of A and B? I. A is twice as old as B. II. The sum of their ages is 45.

  1. Statement I alone is sufficient
  2. Statement II alone is sufficient
  3. Both statements together are needed
  4. Either statement alone is sufficient

Answer: A. Statement I alone is sufficient

Statement I fixes the ratio at 2 : 1 directly, while the sum alone cannot give the ratio.

Question 2

What is the rate of simple interest? I. A sum doubles in 8 years. II. SI on ₹1,000 for 4 years is ₹500.

  1. Statement I alone is sufficient
  2. Statement II alone is sufficient
  3. Both statements together are needed
  4. Either statement alone is sufficient
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Either statement alone is sufficient

I gives 100/8 = 12.5% and II gives 500/(1,000 × 4) = 12.5%, so each statement works alone.

Question 3

What is the area of a rectangle? I. Its perimeter is 30 cm. II. Its diagonal is 12 cm.

  1. Statement I alone is sufficient
  2. Both statements together are needed
  3. Statement II alone is sufficient
  4. Not sufficient even together
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Both statements together are needed

Together l + b = 15 and l² + b² = 144 give lb = (225 − 144)/2 = 40.5, but neither statement alone fixes the area.

Question 4

Is n divisible by 6? I. n is divisible by 3. II. n is divisible by 2.

  1. Statement I alone is sufficient
  2. Both statements together are needed
  3. Either statement alone is sufficient
  4. Not sufficient even together
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Both statements together are needed

Divisibility by 6 needs both 2 and 3; each statement alone leaves the answer open, but together they confirm it.

Question 5

How many students are in a row? I. Ravi is 12th from the left. II. Ravi is 20th from the right.

  1. Both statements together are needed
  2. Statement I alone is sufficient
  3. Statement II alone is sufficient
  4. Either statement alone is sufficient
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Both statements together are needed

Together the total is 12 + 20 − 1 = 31 students; each position alone says nothing about the total.

FAQs

Do I need to find the actual answer in data sufficiency?

No. You only need to decide whether the statements allow a unique answer. Stopping once sufficiency is clear saves time. Computing fully is useful only when you are unsure whether the solution is unique.

What is the biggest trap in data sufficiency?

Using information from one statement while judging the other alone, and assuming facts not stated, such as values being positive or whole numbers. Treat each statement in isolation first, and rely only on what the question and statements actually say.
