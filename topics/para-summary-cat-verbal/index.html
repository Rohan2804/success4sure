Para Summary Questions for CAT and MBA Entrances

Para summary questions give a short paragraph and four options, and ask which option best captures its essence. CAT includes a few of them in the verbal section every year, and CMAT, MAT and some bank mains papers use similar 'main idea' or 'best summary' questions. Unlike reading comprehension, there is no long passage — but the options are deliberately close, so the skill is judging scope precisely: not too narrow, not too broad, and not adding anything new.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Find the core claim

Most paragraphs have one central claim and supporting material — examples, data, background. Before reading the options, write the core claim in under 15 words. If you cannot, read the paragraph again focusing on its first and last sentences.

Scope: not too narrow, not too broad

An option that covers only an example is too narrow. An option that generalises beyond the paragraph ('all economies', 'always') is too broad. The correct summary matches the paragraph's own scope.

No new information

A summary must not add ideas, causes or recommendations absent from the paragraph. Options that sound sensible but bring in outside reasoning are traps.

Keep the author's stance

If the author is cautious ('may', 'some evidence suggests'), the summary must be cautious too. If the author criticises a view, the summary must reflect that criticism, not simply state the view.

Contrast paragraphs

Many paragraphs present a common belief and then challenge it. The summary should capture the challenge, since that is the author's point. Options that repeat only the common belief are wrong.

Elimination works best

Rather than looking for a perfect option, eliminate those that are too narrow, too broad, distorted or add new ideas. The last one standing is usually right, even if its wording is not how you would have put it.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Read the first and last sentences twice; the core claim usually sits in one of them.
  • Cross out examples and statistics mentally; they are support, not the summary.
  • Watch for changed modals: 'can' versus 'will', 'some' versus 'most'.
  • If two options look similar, compare them word by word and find the one that distorts.
  • Do not choose an option just because it uses the paragraph's exact words; correct summaries often paraphrase.
  • If an option would fit a different paragraph on the same topic equally well, it is probably too broad.

Worked examples

Example 1

Paragraph: 'Digital payments in India have grown rapidly, driven by cheap smartphones and interoperable systems like UPI. Yet a sizeable share of small transactions in rural markets still happens in cash, as many sellers worry about connectivity and unfamiliar dispute processes.' Best summary?

Solution

Core claim: digital payments have grown fast, but cash persists in rural small transactions due to practical concerns. An option saying only 'UPI made payments interoperable' is too narrow; 'Cash will soon disappear' contradicts the paragraph. The best option balances growth with continuing rural cash use and its reasons.

Example 2

Paragraph: 'It is often assumed that higher interest rates always cool inflation quickly. However, when inflation is driven by supply shocks such as poor harvests, rate hikes act slowly and may hurt growth before prices respond.' Best summary?

Solution

The author challenges the belief that rate hikes always work quickly, especially for supply-driven inflation. A summary stating 'Rate hikes control inflation' repeats the belief being challenged. The best summary: rate hikes are less effective and slower against supply-driven inflation.

Example 3

Paragraph: 'Some studies suggest that short daily revision sessions improve long-term recall more than occasional long sessions.' Which summary is best? (a) Long study sessions are useless. (b) Frequent short revision may aid long-term memory better than infrequent long sessions. (c) Students should revise for exactly 20 minutes daily.

Solution

(a) is extreme and (c) adds a specific number not in the paragraph. (b) keeps the cautious 'may' and the comparison. Answer: (b).

Practice questions

Question 1

Paragraph: 'Many first-time investors chase recent top performers, but past returns often fail to predict future performance, especially over short periods.' Best summary?

  1. Recent strong returns are an unreliable guide to future returns, particularly in the short term.
  2. Investors should never invest in top-performing funds.
  3. Past returns always predict future returns.
  4. First-time investors earn the highest returns.
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Recent strong returns are an unreliable guide to future returns, particularly in the short term.

The paragraph cautions that past returns are unreliable predictors, especially short term. The other options are extreme, reversed or unsupported by the text.

Question 2

Paragraph: 'Libraries in several cities are reinventing themselves as community spaces, hosting workshops and digital-skills classes alongside lending books.' Best summary?

  1. Libraries have stopped lending books.
  2. Some urban libraries are expanding their role beyond book lending into community activities.
  3. Digital-skills classes are the only reason people visit libraries.
  4. All libraries in India now host workshops.
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Some urban libraries are expanding their role beyond book lending into community activities.

The paragraph describes libraries adding activities alongside lending. The correct option keeps 'some' and 'beyond lending' without exaggerating scope.

Question 3

Which option is usually WRONG as a para summary?

  1. One that paraphrases the main claim
  2. One that keeps the author's cautious tone
  3. One that summarises only an example used in the paragraph
  4. One that matches the paragraph's scope
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. One that summarises only an example used in the paragraph

A summary built only on an example is too narrow because it misses the central claim. The other three describe features of a good summary.

Question 4

Paragraph: 'While remote work saves commuting time, managers report that informal learning among junior staff has declined.' Best summary?

  1. Remote work should be banned.
  2. Commuting time has increased for everyone.
  3. Junior staff prefer working from office.
  4. Remote work offers time savings but may reduce informal learning for junior employees.
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Remote work offers time savings but may reduce informal learning for junior employees.

The paragraph balances a benefit with a reported drawback. Only the option mentioning both time savings and reduced informal learning captures both sides without adding a new claim.

Question 5

In para summary questions, what should you do with statistics and examples in the paragraph?

  1. Treat them as support for the core claim, not the summary itself
  2. Include all of them in your answer
  3. Ignore the paragraph's conclusion
  4. Choose the option with the most numbers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Treat them as support for the core claim, not the summary itself

Examples and data support the main idea. A good summary states the central claim they support rather than repeating the supporting details.

FAQs

How long should a good summary option be?

Length does not decide correctness. Some correct summaries are short; others are long because the paragraph has two balanced ideas. Judge scope, tone and accuracy instead. A long option is wrong if it adds ideas, and a short one is wrong if it leaves out the core claim.

Is para summary the same as finding the title of a passage?

They are related but not identical. A title can be catchy and brief, while a summary must capture the central claim accurately and completely. In para summary questions, prefer the option that states the idea clearly, even if it would make a dull title.
