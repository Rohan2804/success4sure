Mathematical Operations: Symbol Substitution and BODMAS

Mathematical operations questions replace the usual signs with other signs or letters — '+' means '×', 'P' means '÷' — and ask you to evaluate an expression or find the correct equation. SSC CGL, CHSL and MTS papers carry one or two such questions most of the time, and bank clerk prelims sometimes include them. Errors come from two places: substituting a sign wrongly, and forgetting BODMAS after substitution. A careful rewrite solves both.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Rewrite before calculating

Copy the expression with the real signs on your rough sheet, symbol by symbol, before doing any arithmetic. Substituting and calculating at the same time is the most common source of mistakes.

Apply BODMAS after substitution

After rewriting, follow the usual order: brackets, then division and multiplication from left to right, then addition and subtraction from left to right. 8 × 3 − 12 ÷ 4 + 2 is 24 − 3 + 2 = 23, not ((8 × 3 − 12) ÷ 4) + 2.

Interchange questions

'If + and × are interchanged, which equation is correct?' Apply the swap to each option and evaluate. Only one option should balance. Interchanging numbers (for example 3 and 4) works the same way: swap first, then calculate.

Letter-coded signs

Signs may be letters: P for +, Q for −, R for ×, S for ÷. Write a small key at the top of your work and refer to it for each symbol.

Balancing equations

Another variant gives an equation with missing signs, such as 8 _ 4 _ 2 _ 6 = 10, and asks which set of signs makes it true. Test each option by placing the signs and applying BODMAS. For 8 ÷ 4 × 2 + 6, the value is 2 × 2 + 6 = 10, so the order ÷, ×, + works. Start with the options containing division, since they produce whole numbers less often and are eliminated quickly.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Keep the sign key visible; re-read it once for each symbol rather than trusting memory.
  • Evaluate division first in substituted expressions; it often gives whole numbers that confirm you substituted correctly.
  • If your answer is a fraction but all options are whole numbers, recheck the substitution.
  • In interchange questions, test the option with the simplest numbers first.
  • Do not change the numbers' order — only the signs are substituted.

Worked examples

Example 1

If '+' means '×', '×' means '−', '−' means '÷' and '÷' means '+', find 8 + 3 × 12 − 4 ÷ 2.

Solution

Rewrite: 8 × 3 − 12 ÷ 4 + 2. BODMAS: 24 − 3 + 2 = 23.

Example 2

If 'P' means '+', 'Q' means '−', 'R' means '×' and 'S' means '÷', find 18 S 3 P 4 R 5 Q 6.

Solution

Rewrite: 18 ÷ 3 + 4 × 5 − 6 = 6 + 20 − 6 = 20.

Example 3

If '+' and '×' are interchanged, what is the value of 6 + 4 × 3 − 2?

Solution

After the swap the expression is 6 × 4 + 3 − 2 = 24 + 3 − 2 = 25. Without the swap it would have been 6 + 12 − 2 = 16, a common wrong option.

Example 4

If '+' and '×' are interchanged, which equation is correct: 3 + 4 × 2 = 14, 5 + 2 × 6 = 17, 6 + 3 × 3 = 20, 2 + 7 × 1 = 16?

Solution

Swap and evaluate each: 3 × 4 + 2 = 14 ✓; 5 × 2 + 6 = 16 ✗; 6 × 3 + 3 = 21 ✗; 2 × 7 + 1 = 15 ✗. Only the first equation is correct.

Example 5

Which set of signs makes 8 _ 4 _ 2 _ 6 = 10 correct: (÷, ×, +), (+, ÷, −), (×, −, +)?

Solution

8 ÷ 4 × 2 + 6 = 4 + 6 = 10 ✓. 8 + 4 ÷ 2 − 6 = 8 + 2 − 6 = 4 ✗. 8 × 4 − 2 + 6 = 36 ✗. The first set is correct.

Practice questions

Question 1

If '+' means '−', '−' means '+', '×' means '÷' and '÷' means '×', find 20 + 36 × 4 − 5 ÷ 3.

  1. 24
  2. 28
  3. 20
  4. 26
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 26

Rewriting gives 20 − 36 ÷ 4 + 5 × 3. That equals 20 − 9 + 15, which is 26.

Question 2

If '×' means '+', '+' means '×', '−' means '÷' and '÷' means '−', find 9 × 4 + 6 − 12 ÷ 3.

  1. 8
  2. 10
  3. 6
  4. 12
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 8

Rewriting gives 9 + 4 × 6 ÷ 12 − 3. Then 4 × 6 ÷ 12 = 2, so the value is 9 + 2 − 3 = 8.

Question 3

If 'A' means '÷', 'B' means '×', 'C' means '+' and 'D' means '−', find 40 A 8 B 3 C 7 D 2.

  1. 18
  2. 20
  3. 22
  4. 24
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 20

Rewriting gives 40 ÷ 8 × 3 + 7 − 2. Left to right, 40 ÷ 8 = 5 and 5 × 3 = 15, so 15 + 7 − 2 = 20.

Question 4

If '+' and '×' are interchanged, what is the value of 6 + 4 × 3 − 2?

  1. 16
  2. 22
  3. 25
  4. 18
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 25

After swapping, the expression reads 6 × 4 + 3 − 2. That gives 24 + 3 − 2 = 25.

Question 5

If '−' means '+', '+' means '÷', '÷' means '×' and '×' means '−', find 7 ÷ 2 − 10 + 5 × 3.

  1. 15
  2. 11
  3. 17
  4. 13
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 13

Rewriting gives 7 × 2 + 10 ÷ 5 − 3. That equals 14 + 2 − 3 = 13.

FAQs

Do I apply BODMAS before or after substituting signs?

Always after. The question changes what each symbol means, so the expression's structure is known only once the real signs are in place. Rewrite the full expression with real signs, then apply BODMAS normally.

What if two options seem correct in an interchange question?

Re-check your evaluation, especially the order of division and multiplication. Exam setters design only one option to balance. Also confirm you swapped both signs everywhere, including signs on the right-hand side if any appear there.
