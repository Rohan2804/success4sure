Cloze Test Tricks: Grammar Clues, Context and Collocations

A cloze test is a short passage with blanks, each followed by options. IBPS PO, IBPS Clerk, SBI PO and SBI Clerk prelims frequently include a cloze test of five to seven blanks, and SSC CGL and CHSL papers have their own cloze passages. It tests grammar, vocabulary and comprehension together. Unlike fillers, the blanks share a context, so reading the whole passage first gives you clues that single sentences cannot.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Read the passage once before filling

Skim the full passage to understand its topic and tone. A passage about rising non-performing assets will favour words like 'stress', 'default' and 'recovery'; a positive passage about financial inclusion will favour 'expanded', 'enabled' and 'access'.

Grammar decides first

Check the part of speech needed. After an article you need a noun or adjective; after 'to' you often need a base verb; after 'has' you need a past participle. Eliminating grammatically wrong options usually leaves two choices.

Collocations

Some words naturally pair: 'take a decision', 'pose a threat', 'bridge the gap', 'meet the target', 'heavy rainfall', 'strong demand'. If an option breaks a standard pairing, it is probably wrong even if its meaning is close.

Logical connectors

Blanks before a clause often need a connector. 'However' shows contrast, 'therefore' shows result, 'moreover' adds a point, 'despite' is followed by a noun. Read the sentences on both sides of the blank to decide the relationship.

Tone and direction of the passage

Every cloze passage has a direction — it may argue that a reform is working, warn about a risk, or describe a problem and a fix. Words that oppose this direction are usually wrong even if they are grammatically fine. In a passage praising improved rural banking access, 'hampered' or 'curtailed' would clash with the message, while 'expanded' or 'strengthened' fit naturally.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Fill the easiest blanks first; they reveal the passage's direction for the harder ones.
  • Watch tense consistency: if the passage is in past tense, a present-tense option is usually wrong.
  • Prepositions often decide the answer: 'comply with', 'depend on', 'aim at', 'insist on'.
  • Re-read the completed sentence; if it sounds awkward aloud, check the collocation.
  • When two options mean the same, neither is likely to be correct unless one fits the grammar better.
  • After 'one of the', use a plural noun ('one of the biggest challenges'); this simple check removes many wrong options.

Worked examples

Example 1

'The new policy is expected to ______ the gap between rural and urban access to credit.' Options: bridge, fill, cross, close up.

Solution

'Bridge the gap' is the standard collocation for reducing a difference. 'Fill' and 'close up' are less idiomatic here, and 'cross' changes the meaning. Answer: bridge.

Example 2

'Exports grew strongly last year. ______, imports grew even faster, widening the trade deficit.' Options: Therefore, However, Moreover, Because.

Solution

The second sentence contrasts with the positive first one (deficit widened despite export growth). 'However' signals contrast. 'Therefore' would imply exports caused import growth. Answer: However.

Example 3

'The committee has ______ its report to the ministry.' Options: submit, submitted, submitting, submits.

Solution

'Has' requires a past participle to form the present perfect tense. Answer: submitted.

Example 4

'Rising cyber fraud ______ a serious threat to digital payments.' Options: poses, makes, gives, does.

Solution

'Pose a threat' is the standard collocation. The subject 'Rising cyber fraud' is singular, so 'poses'. Answer: poses.

Example 5

'Thanks to wider broadband coverage, many villages have ______ access to online banking for the first time.' Options: lost, gained, denied, refused.

Solution

The opening 'Thanks to wider broadband coverage' signals a positive change, and 'for the first time' shows something new was obtained. 'Gained access' fits both the meaning and the standard collocation. Answer: gained.

Practice questions

Question 1

'The bank aims to ______ its lending target before the end of the financial year.' Choose the best word.

  1. fill
  2. reach up
  3. do
  4. meet
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. meet

'Meet a target' is the natural collocation for achieving a set goal. The other options are either unidiomatic or change the meaning.

Question 2

'______ the heavy rain, the match started on time.' Choose the best option.

  1. Despite
  2. Although
  3. Because
  4. However
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Despite

A noun phrase ('the heavy rain') follows the blank, and the sentence shows contrast. 'Despite' takes a noun; 'although' needs a full clause.

Question 3

'The minister ______ the new scheme in Delhi last week.' Choose the correct form.

  1. launch
  2. launched
  3. launches
  4. has launched
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. launched

'Last week' marks a completed action in the past, so the simple past 'launched' is correct. The present perfect does not combine with a specific past time.

Question 4

'Many small firms depend ______ timely payments from large buyers.' Choose the correct preposition.

  1. from
  2. with
  3. on
  4. of
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. on

The verb 'depend' takes the preposition 'on'. 'Depend from' and 'depend with' are incorrect in standard English.

Question 5

'Prices remained stable for months. ______, the sudden spike surprised economists.' Choose the best connector.

  1. Moreover
  2. Similarly
  3. Unless
  4. Therefore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Therefore

Months of stable prices make the spike unexpected, so the surprise follows as a result. 'Therefore' shows that cause-and-result link.

FAQs

How many cloze test blanks usually appear in bank prelims?

In recent IBPS and SBI prelims papers a cloze test, when asked, has generally had five to seven blanks, but the number and even the presence of a cloze test vary by year. Prepare for it along with fillers and word-swap questions, since they test the same skills.

What if two options fit both grammar and meaning?

Look for the collocation or the tone of the passage. One word usually pairs more naturally ('pose a threat' rather than 'make a threat') or matches the passage's positive or negative direction better. Reading the next sentence often settles the choice.
