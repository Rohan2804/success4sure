Single and Double Fillers: Grammar Clues and Elimination

Filler questions give a sentence with one or two blanks and ask for the word or pair that best completes it. IBPS PO, SBI PO and the clerk exams use single and double fillers often, sometimes as a set of five, and SSC papers include single-blank fill-in-the-blanks. Fillers reward vocabulary, but most can be solved with grammar and logic alone: the sentence usually tells you the part of speech and whether the missing word is positive or negative.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Predict before you look

Read the sentence, cover the options and guess a simple word for the blank. Then pick the option closest to your guess. This prevents attractive but unrelated words from pulling you away.

Signal words

Contrast words ('although', 'but', 'despite', 'yet') mean the blank opposes the other half of the sentence. Support words ('and', 'because', 'moreover', a semicolon) mean the blank continues the same direction.

Double fillers: solve the easier blank first

In double-blank questions, decide one blank with certainty and eliminate every option whose word for that blank fails. Then test the remaining pairs for the second blank. Never choose a pair because one word fits perfectly while the other is doubtful.

Grammar fit

Check part of speech, singular or plural, and the preposition that follows the blank. 'He was accused ___ fraud' needs 'of', so the verb must be one that takes 'of', such as 'accused' or 'convicted'.

Collocations

Some word pairs are fixed: 'stringent norms', 'heavy losses', 'robust growth', 'curb inflation', 'avert a crisis'. If an option breaks such a pairing, it is likely wrong.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Positive or negative? Mark the blank + or − from context and drop options of the wrong charge.
  • After 'a', the next word starts with a consonant sound; after 'an', a vowel sound — useful when the article sits before the blank.
  • If two options are synonyms, both are usually wrong unless one fits the grammar better.
  • In double fillers, the second blank often depends on a word in the first half; read the whole sentence before choosing.
  • Formal sentences about policy and economy favour formal words ('mitigate', 'bolster') over casual ones.
  • Re-read the completed sentence; a correct filler sounds natural in context.

Worked examples

Example 1

'Although the company reported higher sales, its profits ______ because of rising costs.' Options: soared, declined, doubled, stabilised.

Solution

'Although' sets up a contrast with higher sales, so profits must have gone the other way. 'Declined' fits. Answer: declined.

Example 2

'The RBI took steps to ______ inflation, which had ______ the comfort zone.' Options: (a) curb, exceeded (b) boost, crossed (c) curb, entered (d) fuel, exceeded.

Solution

Steps by a central bank against inflation aim to reduce it, so blank one is 'curb', removing (b) and (d). Inflation that prompts action has gone beyond the comfort zone, so 'exceeded' fits better than 'entered'. Answer: (a).

Example 3

'The new norms are quite ______; even minor violations attract penalties.' Options: lenient, stringent, vague, optional.

Solution

The semicolon introduces support: minor violations being penalised shows strictness. 'Stringent' fits. Answer: stringent.

Example 4

'She was ______ of the charges after a detailed inquiry.' Options: acquitted, convicted, accused, blamed.

Solution

'After a detailed inquiry' and 'of the charges' suggest a final decision. Both 'acquitted' and 'convicted' take 'of', but 'accused' is usually before an inquiry. With no negative signal, 'acquitted' (freed) is the natural reading. Answer: acquitted.

Example 5

'Timely rains helped ______ a drought that experts had feared.' Options: avert, invite, cause, prolong.

Solution

Rains that help against a feared drought prevent it. 'Avert a drought' is the standard collocation. Answer: avert.

Practice questions

Question 1

'Despite repeated warnings, the residents ______ to leave the flood-prone area.' Choose the best word.

  1. agreed
  2. hurried
  3. planned
  4. refused
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. refused

'Despite repeated warnings' signals a contrast, so the residents did not do what they were told. 'Refused' fits that meaning.

Question 2

'The economy showed ______ growth, ______ expectations of a slowdown.' Choose the best pair.

  1. robust, defying
  2. weak, confirming
  3. slow, meeting
  4. robust, confirming
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. robust, defying

Robust growth goes against expectations of a slowdown, so 'defying' fits the second blank. 'Robust, confirming' is contradictory.

Question 3

'The bank ______ the loan application because the documents were incomplete.' Choose the best word.

  1. approved
  2. rejected
  3. celebrated
  4. accelerated
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. rejected

Incomplete documents are a reason not to proceed with the application. 'Rejected' fits the cause given in the sentence.

Question 4

'Her explanation was so ______ that nobody could understand it.' Choose the best word.

  1. lucid
  2. brief
  3. convoluted
  4. simple
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. convoluted

If nobody could understand the explanation, it must have been complicated. 'Convoluted' means complex and hard to follow, while 'lucid' means clear.

Question 5

'Strict monitoring helped ______ losses that could have run into crores.' Choose the best word.

  1. increase
  2. announce
  3. record
  4. prevent
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. prevent

The phrase 'could have run into crores' shows the losses did not happen. Monitoring therefore helped prevent them.

FAQs

How do I improve vocabulary for filler questions?

Read one quality editorial or business article daily and note five unfamiliar words with the sentence they appeared in. Revise the list weekly. Learning words in context, along with their usual pairings ('mitigate risk', 'stringent norms'), helps far more than memorising long word lists.

What should I do if no option fits perfectly?

Choose the option that fits both grammar and the direction of the sentence best. Exam fillers sometimes use a slightly formal or unusual word. Eliminate options that are clearly wrong in charge (positive versus negative) or grammar, and pick from what remains.
