Trigonometry for SSC: Ratios, Values and Identities

Trigonometry is an important part of SSC CGL and CHSL, contributing three to five questions per paper, and it underpins heights and distances. Bank exams do not test it. Most SSC trigonometry questions need only the standard-angle table, three identities and the complementary-angle rule. With those, questions that look like heavy algebra reduce to one or two lines.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Ratios and the standard table

In a right triangle, sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse, cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse and tan θ = opposite/adjacent; cosec, sec and cot are their reciprocals. Standard values: sin 30° = 1/2, sin 45° = 1/√2, sin 60° = √3/2, sin 90° = 1; cos reverses this order; tan 30° = 1/√3, tan 45° = 1, tan 60° = √3.

Pythagorean identities

sin²θ + cos²θ = 1; sec²θ − tan²θ = 1; cosec²θ − cot²θ = 1. The last two factor as (sec θ + tan θ)(sec θ − tan θ) = 1, so if sec θ + tan θ = 2, then sec θ − tan θ = 1/2.

sin²θ + cos²θ = 1

Complementary angles

sin(90° − θ) = cos θ, tan(90° − θ) = cot θ and sec(90° − θ) = cosec θ. So sin²17° + sin²73° = sin²17° + cos²17° = 1, and tan 10° tan 80° = tan 10° cot 10° = 1.

Reciprocal pairs

cosec θ = 1/sin θ, sec θ = 1/cos θ and cot θ = 1/tan θ, so sin θ × cosec θ = 1 and tan θ × cot θ = 1. Many SSC simplifications end with one of these products, so rewrite every ratio in terms of sin and cos when an expression looks messy.

Finding ratios from one ratio

Given one ratio of an acute angle, draw a right triangle with those sides and find the third side by Pythagoras. tan θ = 3/4 gives a 3-4-5 triangle, so sin θ = 3/5 and cos θ = 4/5.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Memorise sin 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90° as √0/2, √1/2, √2/2, √3/2, √4/2.
  • Angles adding to 90° in a product of tans cancel in pairs.
  • Substitute θ = 45° or 30° to test identity-based options quickly.
  • 1/(1 + tan²θ) = cos²θ and 1/(1 + cot²θ) = sin²θ.
  • Triples 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17 give ratios like 12/13 and 8/17 directly.

Worked examples

Example 1

Find sin 30° + cos 60° + tan 45°.

Solution

From the table, sin 30° = 1/2, cos 60° = 1/2 and tan 45° = 1. The sum is 1/2 + 1/2 + 1 = 2. Note that sin 30° = cos 60° is itself an example of the complementary-angle rule.

Example 2

If tan θ = 3/4 and θ is acute, find sin θ + cos θ.

Solution

Draw a right triangle with opposite side 3 and adjacent side 4; the hypotenuse is √(9 + 16) = 5. Then sin θ = 3/5 and cos θ = 4/5, so the sum is 7/5. The actual angle (about 36.87°) is never needed.

Example 3

Evaluate sin²17° + sin²73°.

Solution

sin 73° = cos 17°, so the expression is sin²17° + cos²17° = 1.

Example 4

If sec θ + tan θ = 2, find sec θ − tan θ.

Solution

Since sec²θ − tan²θ = 1, (sec θ − tan θ) = 1/(sec θ + tan θ) = 1/2.

Example 5

Evaluate tan 10° × tan 20° × tan 70° × tan 80°.

Solution

tan 80° = cot 10° and tan 70° = cot 20°. Pairs multiply to 1, so the product is 1.

Example 6

Simplify sin²θ + 1/(1 + tan²θ).

Solution

Since 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ, the second term is 1/sec²θ = cos²θ. The expression becomes sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 for every θ where it is defined. Checking with θ = 45°: 1/2 + 1/(1 + 1) = 1, which confirms the answer.

Practice questions

Question 1

2 sin 30° × cos 60° = ?

  1. 1
  2. √3/2
  3. 1/2
  4. 1/4
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 1/2

sin 30° and cos 60° both equal 1/2, so 2 × 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/2.

Question 2

If cos θ = 5/13 and θ is acute, then tan θ = ?

  1. 5/12
  2. 12/13
  3. 13/5
  4. 12/5
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 12/5

The triangle is 5-12-13 with adjacent side 5 and opposite side 12, so tan θ = 12/5.

Question 3

cos²25° + cos²65° = ?

  1. 0
  2. 1
  3. 2
  4. 1/2
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 1

cos 65° = sin 25°, so the expression becomes cos²25° + sin²25°, which equals 1.

Question 4

If cosec θ − cot θ = 1/3, then cosec θ + cot θ = ?

  1. 1/3
  2. 3
  3. 2
  4. 9
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 3

Since cosec²θ − cot²θ = 1, the sum is the reciprocal of the difference, which is 3.

Question 5

tan²60° − sec²45° = ?

  1. 2
  2. 0
  3. 1
  4. √3 − √2
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 1

tan²60° = 3 and sec²45° = 2, so the difference is 3 − 2 = 1.

FAQs

Do bank exams ask trigonometry?

No. IBPS, SBI and RBI quantitative sections focus on arithmetic, DI and number-based topics. Trigonometry is important for SSC CGL and CHSL and for some state-level exams. Bank aspirants can skip it unless they are also preparing for SSC.

How do I remember which ratios are positive in which quadrant?

SSC questions almost always use acute angles, where all ratios are positive. If an obtuse angle appears, use the rule "All, Sin, Tan, Cos" for quadrants one to four: only the named ratios and their reciprocals are positive in each quadrant.
