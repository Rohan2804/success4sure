Circle Theorems: Chords, Tangents and Cyclic Quadrilaterals

Circle geometry is the second pillar of SSC CGL geometry after triangles and often yields two questions per shift. CAT also tests circle theorems in combination with triangles. The theorems are few and visual: a perpendicular from the centre bisects a chord, the angle at the centre is double the angle at the circumference, opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°, and tangents meet radii at right angles.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Chords

The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects it. So for a chord of length 2l at distance d from the centre of a circle with radius r, r² = l² + d². Equal chords are equidistant from the centre.

r² = (half chord)² + d²

Angles in a circle

The angle subtended by an arc at the centre is twice the angle it subtends anywhere on the remaining circumference. Angles in the same segment are equal, and the angle in a semicircle is 90°.

Cyclic quadrilaterals

If all four vertices lie on a circle, opposite angles add to 180°, and an exterior angle equals the interior opposite angle.

Tangents

A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact. The two tangents from an external point are equal. Tangent length from a point at distance d from the centre = √(d² − r²). For a point P outside, PT² = PA × PB for any secant PAB.

PT² = PA × PB

Intersecting chords and common tangents

If chords AB and CD meet at P inside the circle, PA × PB = PC × PD. For two circles with radii r₁, r₂ and centres d apart, the direct common tangent has length √(d² − (r₁ − r₂)²) and the transverse one √(d² − (r₁ + r₂)²).

Direct = √(d² − (r₁ − r₂)²)

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Triples help: radius 10 with chord 16 gives distance 6; radius 13 with chord 24 gives 5.
  • If an angle at the centre is reflex, the angle at the circumference is half of the reflex angle.
  • A parallelogram inscribed in a circle must be a rectangle.
  • Tangents from a point to a circle make equal angles with the line joining the point to the centre.

Worked examples

Example 1

A chord of length 16 cm is drawn in a circle of radius 10 cm. Find its distance from the centre.

Solution

Drop a perpendicular from the centre; it bisects the chord, so half chord = 8 cm. The radius, half chord and distance form a right triangle: d = √(100 − 64) = 6 cm.

Example 2

An arc subtends 140° at the centre. What angle does it subtend at a point on the remaining circumference?

Solution

Half of 140° = 70°.

Example 3

In cyclic quadrilateral ABCD, ∠A = 75°. Find ∠C.

Solution

Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°, so ∠C = 180° − 75° = 105°. The exterior angle at C would equal ∠A, i.e. 75°.

Example 4

A point is 13 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 5 cm. Find the length of the tangent from the point.

Solution

√(169 − 25) = √144 = 12 cm.

Example 5

Chords AB and CD intersect at P. If PA = 4 cm, PB = 6 cm and PC = 3 cm, find PD.

Solution

PA × PB = PC × PD → 24 = 3 × PD → PD = 8 cm.

Example 6

Two circles have radii 8 cm and 3 cm and their centres are 13 cm apart. Find the lengths of the direct and transverse common tangents.

Solution

Direct = √(169 − 25) = 12 cm. Transverse = √(169 − 121) = √48 = 4√3 ≈ 6.93 cm.

Practice questions

Question 1

A chord of a circle of radius 13 cm is 24 cm long. Its distance from the centre is:

  1. 12 cm
  2. 7 cm
  3. 5 cm
  4. 11 cm
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 5 cm

Half chord is 12 cm, so the distance is √(169 − 144) = √25 = 5 cm.

Question 2

An arc subtends 35° at a point on the circumference. The angle it subtends at the centre is:

  1. 35°
  2. 70°
  3. 17.5°
  4. 145°
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 70°

The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference, so it is 70 degrees.

Question 3

In a cyclic quadrilateral one angle is 112°. The angle opposite to it is:

  1. 112°
  2. 78°
  3. 58°
  4. 68°
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 68°

Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°, so the opposite angle is 68°.

Question 4

The tangent from a point 25 cm from the centre of a circle is 24 cm long. The radius is:

  1. 7 cm
  2. 5 cm
  3. 9 cm
  4. 1 cm
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 7 cm

Radius = √(625 − 576) = √49 = 7 cm, since the tangent meets the radius at a right angle.

Question 5

From an external point P, a secant meets a circle at A and B with PA = 4 cm and PB = 9 cm. The tangent length from P is:

  1. 5 cm
  2. 13 cm
  3. 36 cm
  4. 6 cm
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 6 cm

PT² = PA × PB = 36, so the tangent length is 6 cm by the tangent–secant theorem.

FAQs

Why is the angle in a semicircle always 90°?

The diameter subtends a straight angle of 180° at the centre. By the centre-angle theorem, it subtends half of that, 90°, at any point on the circle. This fact turns many circle questions into right-triangle questions.

How do I remember direct versus transverse common tangent formulas?

Direct tangents do not cross between the circles, so the radii are on the same side and you subtract them. Transverse tangents cross between the circles, so the radii are on opposite sides and you add them. Both use d² minus the square of that result.
