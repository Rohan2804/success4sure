Profit and Loss Shortcuts with Solved Examples

Profit and loss gives one to three questions in IBPS PO, SBI Clerk and RBI Grade B prelims, and it is a favourite in SSC CGL and CHSL arithmetic. It also shows up in DI caselets where you must compute profit percentages for several shops. The concepts are simple; marks are lost by calculating profit percentage on selling price instead of cost price, or by mixing up per-item and total figures.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Basic relations

Cost price (CP) is what the seller paid; selling price (SP) is what the buyer pays. Profit = SP − CP and Loss = CP − SP. Profit or loss percentage is always on CP unless the question explicitly says "on selling price".

SP = CP × (100 + profit%) / 100

Working backwards to CP

When SP and the profit or loss percentage are given, divide rather than subtract. If an article sold for ₹1,104 involves an 8% loss, CP = 1,104 ÷ 0.92 = ₹1,200. Subtracting 8% of ₹1,104 is the classic mistake.

CP = SP × 100 / (100 ± profit or loss %)

Same SP, equal gain and loss

If two articles are sold at the same price, one at x% profit and the other at x% loss, the seller always makes an overall loss of x²/100 percent. The two cost prices are different, so the gains and losses do not cancel.

Loss % = x² / 100

Dishonest dealers

A dealer who claims to sell at cost price but gives less weight earns a gain equal to error ÷ (true weight − error) × 100. Giving 900 g instead of 1 kg means the error is 100 g on 900 g actually given, so gain = 11.11%.

Gain % = error / (true value − error) × 100

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Buy a items for ₹x and sell b items for ₹x: profit% = (a − b)/b × 100. Buy 12, sell 10 for the same money gives 20%.
  • Profit on SP of r% equals profit on CP of r/(100 − r) × 100 percent. 20% on SP is 25% on CP.
  • If selling at ₹S1 gives x% loss and at ₹S2 gives y% profit, CP = (S2 − S1) × 100 / (x + y).
  • Convert profit percentages to fractions: 25% profit means SP = 5/4 of CP, 12.5% means SP = 9/8 of CP.
  • When an answer comes out as an odd decimal like 5.26%, check if it is 100/19 — many dealer questions produce fractions with 19, 9 or 99.

Worked examples

Example 1

A shopkeeper buys 12 pens for ₹100 and sells 10 pens for ₹100. Find his profit percentage.

Solution

CP of one pen = 100/12 = ₹8.33 and SP of one pen = ₹10. Profit = 1.67 on 8.33, which is 20%. Shortcut: (12 − 10)/10 × 100 = 20%.

Example 2

Two mobile phones are sold for ₹9,900 each. One is sold at 10% profit and the other at 10% loss. Find the overall result.

Solution

CP of first = 9,900 ÷ 1.1 = ₹9,000; CP of second = 9,900 ÷ 0.9 = ₹11,000. Total CP = ₹20,000, total SP = ₹19,800, so there is a loss of ₹200, which is 1%. Formula check: 10²/100 = 1% loss.

Example 3

A grocer claims to sell rice at cost price but uses a 900 g weight for 1 kg. Find his gain percentage.

Solution

Error = 100 g, actual quantity given = 900 g. Gain = 100/900 × 100 = 11.11%.

Example 4

By selling a fan for ₹1,104, a trader loses 8%. At what price should he sell it to gain 10%?

Solution

CP = 1,104 ÷ 0.92 = ₹1,200. Required SP = 1,200 × 1.10 = ₹1,320.

Practice questions

Question 1

An article costing ₹640 is sold at a profit of 15%. Find the selling price.

  1. ₹724
  2. ₹746
  3. ₹704
  4. ₹736
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹736

SP = 640 × 1.15 = 640 + 96 = ₹736, since 15% of 640 is 96.

Question 2

An article sold for ₹1,350 results in a loss of 10%. What was its cost price?

  1. ₹1,485
  2. ₹1,500
  3. ₹1,215
  4. ₹1,450
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹1,500

CP = 1,350 ÷ 0.90 = ₹1,500. Subtracting 10% of 1,350 would wrongly give ₹1,215.

Question 3

A vendor buys bananas at 6 for ₹10 and sells them at 4 for ₹10. His profit percentage is:

  1. 33.33%
  2. 40%
  3. 25%
  4. 50%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 50%

Using (a − b)/b × 100 with a = 6 and b = 4 gives 2/4 × 100 = 50 percent profit.

Question 4

Two cycles are sold at ₹4,800 each, one at 20% profit and the other at 20% loss. The overall result is:

  1. 4% loss
  2. No profit, no loss
  3. 2% loss
  4. 4% profit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 4% loss

Same SP with equal profit and loss percentages always gives a loss of 20²/100 = 4 percent.

Question 5

A dealer sells goods at cost price but uses 950 g instead of 1 kg. His gain is about:

  1. 5%
  2. 4.75%
  3. 5.26%
  4. 5.5%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 5.26%

Gain = 50/950 × 100 = 5.26 percent, because the error of 50 g is measured against the 950 g actually given.

FAQs

Is profit percentage calculated on cost price or selling price?

On cost price, unless the question clearly says "profit on selling price". Bank and SSC questions follow this convention. If a question mentions margin on selling price, convert it: a 20% margin on SP equals a 25% profit on CP.

Why is there a loss when profit and loss percentages are equal?

Because the two cost prices differ. The article sold at a loss had a higher cost price, so the rupee loss on it is larger than the rupee gain on the other. The net effect is always a loss of x²/100 percent when the selling prices are equal.
