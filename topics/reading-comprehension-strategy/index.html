Reading Comprehension Strategy for Bank, SSC and CAT

Reading comprehension (RC) is the single biggest block in most English sections. IBPS PO, SBI PO and IBPS Clerk prelims usually carry one or two passages with about eight to ten questions in total, RBI Grade B includes RCs in its English section, SSC CGL and CHSL ask a short passage with a few questions, and in CAT the RC questions form most of the verbal section. A consistent method — how to read, what to mark, how to judge options — matters more than reading speed alone.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Read for structure, not detail

On the first read, note what each paragraph does: introduces a problem, gives an example, presents a counter-view, concludes. A two-word margin note per paragraph ('problem', 'RBI view') lets you locate details later in seconds.

Question types

Direct-fact questions ask what the passage says. Inference questions ask what must be true based on it. Main-idea and title questions test the whole passage. Tone questions ask about the author's attitude. Vocabulary-in-context questions ask what a word means in that sentence, which may differ from its dictionary meaning.

Judging options

The correct option is supported by the passage, usually in different words. Wrong options are often too extreme ('always', 'never', 'completely'), partly true, true in general but not stated, or a distortion of a detail. Paraphrase is a good sign; copied phrases with one changed word are a warning.

Order of attack

In bank prelims, many toppers glance at the questions first to know what to look for, then read the passage once. In CAT, reading the passage fully first works better because questions are inference-heavy. Try both methods in mocks and keep the one that gives better accuracy.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • For 'title' questions, choose the option that covers the whole passage, not just one paragraph.
  • For tone, look at adjectives and adverbs the author uses ('worryingly', 'commendable').
  • If two options look right, pick the one that needs fewer assumptions.
  • Vocabulary-in-context: substitute each option into the sentence and keep the one that preserves meaning.
  • Do not use outside knowledge; an option can be factually true and still wrong if the passage does not support it.
  • Attempt RC passages on familiar topics (economy, banking, technology) first if there is a choice.

Worked examples

Example 1

Passage: 'Small savings schemes have long been popular in rural India because they combine safety with modest returns. Yet younger savers increasingly prefer digital options that let them invest small amounts through a phone. Post offices have responded by offering online access to several schemes.' Which of the following can be inferred from the passage? (a) Rural savers have stopped using small savings schemes. (b) Post offices are adapting to changing saver preferences. (c) Digital investments are safer than small savings schemes.

Solution

The passage says post offices responded to younger savers' digital preference by offering online access, so (b) is a fair inference. (a) is extreme — the passage says the schemes 'have long been popular'. (c) compares safety, which the passage never does. Answer: (b).

Example 2

Passage: 'Small savings schemes have long been popular in rural India because they combine safety with modest returns. Yet younger savers increasingly prefer digital options that let them invest small amounts through a phone. Post offices have responded by offering online access to several schemes.' What is the author's tone?

Solution

The author reports facts about trends and responses without praising or criticising anyone. The tone is neutral or informative, not critical, alarmed or celebratory.

Example 3

In the sentence 'The committee tabled the proposal until the next meeting', what does 'tabled' most nearly mean?

Solution

In Indian and British usage, 'table' often means to present for discussion, but here 'until the next meeting' shows the proposal was set aside for later. In context, 'postponed' fits best. This is why context, not the dictionary's first meaning, decides vocabulary questions.

Example 4

A passage discusses how UPI has made small payments easier and also mentions concerns about cyber fraud. Which title is best: (a) The Rise of Cyber Fraud (b) UPI: Convenience and Its Risks (c) How to File a Fraud Complaint?

Solution

(a) covers only the concern and (c) is not discussed. (b) captures both the benefit and the risk, so it reflects the whole passage. Answer: (b).

Practice questions

Question 1

Passage: 'Small savings schemes have long been popular in rural India because they combine safety with modest returns. Yet younger savers increasingly prefer digital options that let them invest small amounts through a phone. Post offices have responded by offering online access to several schemes.' According to the passage, why are small savings schemes popular in rural India?

  1. They offer the highest returns
  2. They can only be bought online
  3. They combine safety with modest returns
  4. They are preferred by younger savers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. They combine safety with modest returns

The first sentence states that the schemes are popular in rural India because they combine safety with modest returns.

Question 2

Passage: 'Small savings schemes have long been popular in rural India because they combine safety with modest returns. Yet younger savers increasingly prefer digital options that let them invest small amounts through a phone. Post offices have responded by offering online access to several schemes.' Which statement is NOT supported by the passage?

  1. Younger savers like investing through phones
  2. Post offices offer online access to several schemes
  3. Small savings schemes give modest returns
  4. Post offices have stopped offering small savings schemes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Post offices have stopped offering small savings schemes

The passage says post offices responded by offering online access, which means they continue the schemes. The claim that they stopped is unsupported.

Question 3

Passage: 'Small savings schemes have long been popular in rural India because they combine safety with modest returns. Yet younger savers increasingly prefer digital options that let them invest small amounts through a phone. Post offices have responded by offering online access to several schemes.' The word 'modest' in the passage is closest in meaning to:

  1. Moderate
  2. Shy
  3. Humble
  4. Excessive
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Moderate

In the context of returns, 'modest' describes an amount that is reasonable but not large, so 'moderate' fits. 'Shy' and 'humble' describe people.

Question 4

Which option is usually a warning sign in reading comprehension answer choices?

  1. A paraphrase of a sentence in the passage
  2. An absolute word like 'always' or 'never'
  3. A statement covering the main idea
  4. A cautious word like 'some'
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. An absolute word like 'always' or 'never'

Extreme words make an option claim more than most passages support. Correct options are typically cautious paraphrases of what the passage actually says.

Question 5

A passage describes rising farm incomes in one state after an irrigation project. Which conclusion is best supported?

  1. Irrigation always doubles farm incomes
  2. All Indian states have rising farm incomes
  3. The irrigation project may have contributed to higher farm incomes there
  4. Farm incomes will keep rising forever
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The irrigation project may have contributed to higher farm incomes there

The passage links one project to one state's incomes, so a cautious statement about contribution is supported. The other options generalise or predict beyond the passage.

FAQs

Should I read the questions before the passage?

For fact-heavy bank prelims passages, skimming the questions first helps you read with a purpose. For inference-heavy CAT passages, reading the whole passage first usually works better. Test both approaches in at least five mock tests and keep the one that gives you higher accuracy, not just speed.

How can I improve reading speed for RC?

Read one editorial or long-form article daily on economy, banking, science or society, and summarise each paragraph in a few words. Over a few weeks this builds both speed and the habit of spotting structure. Avoid sub-vocalising every word; read in phrases instead.
