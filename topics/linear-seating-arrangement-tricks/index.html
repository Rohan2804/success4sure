Linear Seating Arrangement: Facing North, South and Mixed

Linear seating arrangement is one of the heaviest areas in bank exam reasoning. IBPS PO, SBI PO and RBI Grade B prelims often carry one row-based puzzle of five questions, and the mains papers use double-row versions. IBPS and SBI Clerk prefer simpler single-row puzzles. The skill is not cleverness but order: fix definite information first, branch into cases only when forced, and know how left and right change with the direction people face.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Left and right depend on facing direction

For people facing north (away from you, as you look at the page), their left is your left. For people facing south (towards you), their left is your right. In mixed-facing puzzles, mark each person's direction with an arrow before placing neighbours.

Immediate versus 'second to the left'

'Immediately to the left' means adjacent. 'Second to the left' means one person in between. 'To the left' alone means anywhere on that side. Misreading these is the biggest source of wrong rows.

Definite clues first

Clues like 'sits at an extreme end', 'sits exactly in the middle' or 'third from the left end' fix a position. Place them first, then attach relative clues to them. Negative clues ('does not sit at an end') are used last, to kill cases.

Case-making

When a clue allows two positions, draw both rows side by side and carry them forward together. Usually one case dies within two more clues. Writing cases neatly beats trying to hold them in memory.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Number seats 1 to n from the left end of the page. For north-facing people, 'k places to the right' means seat + k.
  • For south-facing people, 'k places to the right' means seat − k on your page numbering.
  • 'Only two people sit between A and B' means their seat numbers differ by 3.
  • In a row of n people, the middle seat (odd n) is (n + 1) / 2; it is often a definite anchor.
  • If a question asks 'how many people sit between', subtract seat numbers and then subtract one.

Worked examples

Example 1

Seven friends A, B, C, D, E, F and G sit in a row facing north. G sits exactly in the middle. A sits immediately to the left of G. D sits at the right end. Only two people sit between C and E. C does not sit at an end. F is not an immediate neighbour of E. B sits somewhere to the right of E. Find the arrangement.

Solution

Seats 1–7 from the left. G = 4, A = 3, D = 7. C and E need seat numbers differing by 3, with C not at seat 1 or 7. Free seats are 1, 2, 5 and 6, so (C, E) is (2, 5) or (5, 2). If E = 2, B to the right of E and F not beside E leaves F at seat 1 next to E — not allowed. So C = 2, E = 5. B must be right of E, so B = 6 and F = 1. Row: F C A G E B D.

Example 2

In the row above, who sits second to the right of A, and how many people sit between F and B?

Solution

A is in seat 3; second to the right is seat 5, which is E. F is in seat 1 and B in seat 6, so 6 − 1 − 1 = 4 people sit between them.

Example 3

Five people sit in a row facing south. P sits at the extreme right end of the row (from P's own point of view). On the page, where should you draw P if you number seats from your left?

Solution

People facing south face you. Their right is your left. So P, at their extreme right, goes in seat 1 at your left end. Drawing this correctly first avoids reversing the entire arrangement later.

Practice questions

Question 1

Five persons J, K, L, M and N sit in a row facing north. L sits in the middle. K sits at the left end. M sits immediately to the right of L. N is not an immediate neighbour of K. Who sits at the right end?

  1. J
  2. N
  3. M
  4. K
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. N

L takes seat 3, K seat 1 and M seat 4. Seats 2 and 5 remain for J and N; N cannot sit next to K, so N is in seat 5 at the right end.

Question 2

Six people sit in a row facing north. U is third from the left end and V sits second to the right of U. How many people sit to the right of V?

  1. Two
  2. Three
  3. One
  4. None
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. One

U is in seat 3, so V is in seat 5. In a row of six only seat 6 lies to the right of V, so exactly one person sits there.

Question 3

Five people A, B, C, D and E sit in a row facing north. A sits at the right end. E sits immediately to the left of A. B sits second to the left of D. D does not sit at an end. Who sits in the middle?

  1. B
  2. C
  3. E
  4. D
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. D

A is in seat 5 and E in seat 4. D must be in seat 2 or 3 and B two seats to its left; only D = 3 with B = 1 works. C takes seat 2, so D sits in the middle.

Question 4

Six people P, Q, R, S, T and U sit in a row facing north. P sits fourth to the left of Q. Q does not sit at the right end. R sits immediately to the right of Q. S does not sit at an end, and T sits second to the right of S. Who sits between S and T?

  1. U
  2. P
  3. Q
  4. R
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. U

Q must be in seat 5 (seat 6 is ruled out), so P = 1 and R = 6. Seats 2, 3 and 4 remain; T = S + 2 gives S = 2 and T = 4, leaving U between them in seat 3.

Question 5

Seven people sit in a row, all facing south. G sits at the extreme left end from G's own point of view, and H sits third to the right of G. Which seat does H occupy?

  1. Second from the right end
  2. Exactly in the middle
  3. At the right end
  4. Third from the left end
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Exactly in the middle

Counting in the row's own frame, G is at position 1 from the left and H is three places to the right, at position 4. In a row of seven, position 4 is the exact middle.

FAQs

Why does my arrangement come out mirror-reversed?

Usually because some people face south and you placed them as if they faced north. Mark every person's facing direction before reading relative clues. For south-facing people, their left is your right, so 'to the left of A' moves towards your right side of the page.

How long should a linear seating puzzle take?

A five-question puzzle with seven or eight people typically takes six to eight minutes for a well-practised candidate in prelims, including answering the questions. If you are past ten minutes and still in three cases, it is often smarter to move on and return later.
