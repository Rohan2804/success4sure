Caselet DI: How to Convert Paragraph Data into a Table

Caselet DI presents data as a paragraph instead of a chart, so the first job is to organise it. IBPS PO mains, SBI PO, RBI Grade B and recent prelims papers include caselets regularly, and CAT’s DI section often starts from text too. The arithmetic is usually simple percentages; what separates fast and slow candidates is how quickly they convert the text into a clean table and how carefully they track which base each percentage refers to.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Build a table first

Read the whole caselet once, decide the rows and columns (for example, gender × stream), and fill the table as you read the second time. Most questions then become look-ups or one-line calculations. Do not answer questions directly from the text — it is slower and error-prone.

Nested percentages

Caselets layer percentages: "45% of students are girls; of the girls, 40% chose Science". The second percentage applies to girls, not to all students. Compute each layer in order: girls = 540, girls in Science = 40% of 540 = 216.

Use "rest" and totals as checks

Phrases like "the rest chose Arts" give the last category by subtraction. Always check that each row adds up to its total; a mismatch reveals a misread percentage before it costs marks.

Questions often cross categories

Expect questions like "what percentage of Science students are girls?" — the base here is all Science students, not all girls. Re-identify the base for every question.

Whole-number sanity check

Counts of people, loans or items must be whole numbers. If a calculation gives 189.6 students, you have probably applied a percentage to the wrong base. Exam setters choose numbers so that the correct path gives integers.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Draw the grid before reading the questions; it typically takes under a minute and saves several later.
  • Write totals in a final row and column so cross-category questions are instant.
  • When a caselet gives ratios, convert them to actual numbers immediately.
  • Underline the words "of the", "remaining" and "rest" — they signal a change of base.

Worked examples

Example 1

A school has 1,200 students; 45% are girls. Of the girls, 40% opted for Science, 35% for Commerce and the rest for Arts. Of the boys, 50% chose Science, 30% Commerce and the rest Arts. Tabulate the data.

Solution

Girls = 540: Science 216, Commerce 189, Arts 135. Boys = 660: Science 330, Commerce 198, Arts 132. Row totals check: 216 + 189 + 135 = 540 and 330 + 198 + 132 = 660.

Example 2

How many students chose Science in all?

Solution

Add the Science column of the table: 216 girls + 330 boys = 546 students, which is 45.5% of the school. Without the table you would have to recompute both layers of percentages here.

Example 3

How many students chose Arts in all?

Solution

Arts = 135 girls + 132 boys = 267 students. Check the grand total: Science 546 + Commerce 387 + Arts 267 = 1,200, matching the school strength.

Example 4

Find the ratio of girls to boys in Commerce.

Solution

189 : 198 = 21 : 22 (dividing both by 9).

Example 5

What percentage of Science students are girls?

Solution

Base is all Science students (546). 216/546 × 100 ≈ 39.56%. Using girls (540) as the base would wrongly give 40%.

Example 6

How many more girls than boys chose Arts?

Solution

135 − 132 = 3. Even though girls are fewer overall, a larger share of them chose Arts (25% against 20%).

Practice questions

Question 1

A branch received 800 loan applications: 40% home, 35% vehicle, rest personal. 75% of home, 60% of vehicle and 40% of personal applications were approved. How many were approved in total?

  1. 480
  2. 512
  3. 464
  4. 488
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 488

Approved: home 240, vehicle 168 and personal 80, which together make 488 applications.

Question 2

Using the same data, the overall approval rate is:

  1. 61%
  2. 58%
  3. 64%
  4. 60%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 61%

488 approved out of 800 applications gives 488/800 × 100 = 61 percent.

Question 3

Using the same data, how many vehicle loan applications were rejected?

  1. 168
  2. 112
  3. 120
  4. 98
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 112

Vehicle applications were 280 and 168 were approved, so 280 − 168 = 112 were rejected.

Question 4

Using the same data, personal loans form about what percentage of all approved loans?

  1. 10%
  2. 25%
  3. 16.39%
  4. 40%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 16.39%

Personal approvals are 80 of 488 total approvals, and 80/488 × 100 ≈ 16.39 percent.

Question 5

Using the same data, the ratio of approved home loans to approved vehicle loans is:

  1. 8 : 7
  2. 5 : 4
  3. 4 : 3
  4. 10 : 7
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 10 : 7

240 : 168 simplifies to 10 : 7 when both terms are divided by 24.

FAQs

Why are caselets considered harder than graph-based DI?

Because the data are not organised for you. The calculations are usually no harder, but extracting and tabulating the numbers takes time, and misreading a base is easy. Building a table first neutralises most of that difficulty.

Can a caselet have data that cannot be fully determined?

Yes. Some caselets deliberately leave one value unknown and ask which questions can be answered. In such cases, compute what you can and recognise when a question requires the missing piece. These overlap with data sufficiency thinking.
