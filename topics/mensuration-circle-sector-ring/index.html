Circle Mensuration: Area, Circumference, Sector and Ring

Circle-based mensuration questions — wheels, circular parks, tracks and sectors — appear regularly in SSC CGL and CHSL and in bank quantity-comparison questions. Exam setters choose radii that are multiples of 7 so that π = 22/7 cancels neatly, which is a strong hint about how to calculate. The key formulas are few; most errors come from mixing radius and diameter or from forgetting the straight edges of semicircles and sectors.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Basic formulas

Circumference = 2πr and area = πr². With r = 7, the circumference is 44 and the area 154; with r = 14, they are 88 and 616; with r = 21, 132 and 1,386. These values recur across exams.

C = 2πr; A = πr²

Wheels and revolutions

In one revolution a wheel covers its circumference. Distance = number of revolutions × 2πr. Convert km to cm or m carefully: 1.1 km = 1,10,000 cm.

Sectors and arcs

A sector with central angle θ is θ/360 of the circle. Arc length = (θ/360) × 2πr and sector area = (θ/360) × πr². The perimeter of a sector adds two radii to the arc; a semicircle’s perimeter is πr + 2r.

Sector area = (θ/360)πr²

Rings and circular paths

The area of a ring between radii R and r is π(R² − r²) = π(R + r)(R − r). For a path of width w around a circular park of radius r, use R = r + w.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Use π = 22/7 when the radius is a multiple of 7, and 3.14 otherwise, unless the question specifies.
  • If the radius doubles, area becomes 4 times (a 300% increase) while circumference doubles.
  • The largest circle inside a square of side a has radius a/2; the leftover area is a² − πa²/4.
  • Area from circumference: A = C²/(4π). For C = 88, area = 616.
  • Semicircle perimeter = r(π + 2) = 36r/7 with π = 22/7.
  • Widening a circular track by w adds 2πw to its circumference whatever the original size: an inner circumference of 440 m with a 14 m wide track gives an outer circumference of 440 + 88 = 528 m.

Worked examples

Example 1

A wheel of radius 35 cm rolls 1.1 km. How many revolutions does it make?

Solution

Circumference = 2 × 22/7 × 35 = 220 cm. Distance = 1,10,000 cm. Revolutions = 1,10,000 ÷ 220 = 500.

Example 2

A circular park of radius 21 m has a 3.5 m wide path around it on the outside. Find the area of the path.

Solution

R = 24.5 m. Area = 22/7 × (24.5² − 21²) = 22/7 × 159.25 = 500.5 m². Using (R + r)(R − r) = 45.5 × 3.5 makes the arithmetic easier.

Example 3

Find the area and arc length of a sector with radius 14 cm and angle 60°.

Solution

Area = (60/360) × 22/7 × 196 = 616/6 ≈ 102.67 cm². Arc = (60/360) × 88 ≈ 14.67 cm.

Example 4

The circumference of a circle is 88 cm. Find its area.

Solution

r = 88 ÷ (2 × 22/7) = 14 cm. Area = 22/7 × 196 = 616 cm².

Example 5

The largest possible circle is cut from a square sheet of side 14 cm. How much sheet is wasted?

Solution

Radius = 7 cm, circle area = 154 cm². Square = 196 cm². Waste = 42 cm², which is about 21% of the sheet whatever the size of the square, since 1 − π/4 ≈ 0.215.

Example 6

The minute hand of a clock is 21 cm long. What area does it sweep in 20 minutes, and how far does its tip travel?

Solution

In 20 minutes the hand turns 20/60 of a full circle, i.e. 120°. Area swept = (120/360) × 22/7 × 441 = 462 cm². Distance moved by the tip = (1/3) × 2 × 22/7 × 21 = 44 cm. Clock-hand questions are just sector questions in disguise.

Practice questions

Question 1

The circumference of a circle is 132 cm. Its area is:

  1. 1,256 cm²
  2. 1,386 cm²
  3. 1,474 cm²
  4. 1,232 cm²
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 1,386 cm²

r = 132 ÷ (44/7) = 21 cm, so the area is 22/7 × 441 = 1,386 square centimetres.

Question 2

A wheel of diameter 70 cm makes 400 revolutions. The distance covered is:

  1. 440 m
  2. 220 m
  3. 880 m
  4. 1,760 m
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 880 m

Circumference is 22/7 × 70 = 220 cm, so 400 revolutions cover 88,000 cm, which is 880 metres.

Question 3

The area of a quadrant (90° sector) of a circle of radius 14 cm is:

  1. 616 cm²
  2. 308 cm²
  3. 44 cm²
  4. 154 cm²
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 154 cm²

A quadrant is one-fourth of the circle, so its area is 616/4 = 154 square centimetres.

Question 4

If the radius of a circle increases from 7 cm to 14 cm, the area increases by:

  1. 100%
  2. 200%
  3. 300%
  4. 400%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 300%

Doubling the radius makes the area four times as large, which is an increase of 300 percent.

Question 5

The perimeter of a semicircle of radius 7 cm is:

  1. 36 cm
  2. 22 cm
  3. 29 cm
  4. 44 cm
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 36 cm

Perimeter = πr + 2r = 22 + 14 = 36 cm, including the diameter as the straight edge.

FAQs

Should I use π = 22/7 or 3.14?

Use whatever the question specifies. If it is silent, check the radius: multiples of 7 suggest 22/7, while radii like 10 or 5 suggest 3.14. Options are usually designed around one of them, so compute with the value that makes an option match exactly.

Why does a semicircle’s perimeter include the diameter?

Because the perimeter is the full boundary of the shape, and a semicircle is closed by its straight edge. Only the curved length is πr; the diameter 2r must be added. Questions on fencing semicircular plots test this point.
