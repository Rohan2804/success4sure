Blood Relations Reasoning: Family Tree Method and Codes

Blood relations appear in SSC CGL, CHSL and MTS almost every shift, and in IBPS, SBI and RBI prelims either as standalone questions or as a coded set of three. The concepts never change: generations, gender and marriage links. Mistakes come from rushing — assuming a person's gender from a name, or confusing 'my mother's only son' with a brother when the speaker could be that son. A neat family tree drawn in 15 seconds prevents both errors.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Standard family tree notation

Use a square or '+' for male and a circle or '−' for female, a vertical line for parent–child, a horizontal line for siblings, and a double line for husband–wife. Put each generation on its own level. Drawing on levels shows at a glance whether the answer is grandfather (two levels up) or uncle (one level up, sideways).

Never assume gender

A name like 'Kiran' or 'Ashu' could be male or female. If the question does not state gender, the answer may be 'cannot be determined' or a gender-neutral word like 'child' or 'sibling'. Options are often built around this trap.

Maternal versus paternal

Relatives on the mother's side are maternal: mother's father is maternal grandfather, mother's brother is maternal uncle (mama). Father's side is paternal. Indian exams like asking this because Hindi words (chacha, mama, bua, mausi) make the difference natural for many candidates.

Coded relations

Symbols are defined as relations, for example 'P × Q' means P is the mother of Q. Decode the expression link by link, from left to right, drawing as you go. Each new symbol adds one person to the tree, so the final relation is usually two or three links long.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • 'My grandfather's only son' is the speaker's father; 'my mother's only daughter' is the speaker herself or her sister, depending on who is speaking.
  • In-laws: your spouse's brother is your brother-in-law, and so is your sister's husband.
  • When a statement says 'only son' or 'only daughter', it fixes one person uniquely; use that to collapse the tree.
  • Reading a chain like 'A is the brother of B, B is the daughter of C': A is C's child too, and A is male, so A is C's son.
  • Check the direction of the question: 'How is A related to B?' asks for A's relation, not B's.

Worked examples

Example 1

Pointing to a man, Riya says, 'He is the son of my grandfather's only son.' How is the man related to Riya?

Solution

Riya's grandfather's only son is Riya's father. The son of Riya's father is Riya's brother (Riya is female, so she cannot be that son). Answer: brother.

Example 2

A is the brother of B. B is the daughter of C. C is the husband of D. How is A related to D?

Solution

B is the daughter of C and D (C and D are married). A is B's brother, so A is also their child, and A is male. A is D's son.

Example 3

'P × Q' means P is the mother of Q; 'P + Q' means P is the brother of Q; 'P − Q' means P is the father of Q. What does M × N + O − R show about M and R?

Solution

M is the mother of N. N is the brother of O, so M is O's mother too. O is the father of R. So M is R's father's mother: M is the paternal grandmother of R.

Example 4

Pointing to a woman, Arjun says, 'Her mother is the only daughter of my mother.' How is the woman related to Arjun?

Solution

The only daughter of Arjun's mother is Arjun's sister (Arjun is male). The woman is his sister's daughter, so she is Arjun's niece.

Practice questions

Question 1

Pointing to a boy, Neha says, 'He is the son of my mother's only son.' How is the boy related to Neha?

  1. Brother
  2. Son
  3. Cousin
  4. Nephew
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Nephew

Neha's mother's only son is Neha's brother, because Neha is female. The boy is her brother's son, which makes him Neha's nephew.

Question 2

X is the sister of Y. Y is the father of Z. Z is the son of W, who is a woman. How is X related to W?

  1. Sister-in-law
  2. Sister
  3. Mother-in-law
  4. Aunt
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Sister-in-law

W is Z's mother and Y is Z's father, so W is Y's wife. X is Y's sister, which makes X the sister-in-law of W.

Question 3

'A # B' means A is the wife of B; 'A @ B' means A is the son of B. If P @ Q # R, how is R related to P?

  1. Brother
  2. Father
  3. Uncle
  4. Grandfather
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Father

P is the son of Q, and Q is the wife of R. So R is Q's husband and P's father.

Question 4

How is Rahul's father's only brother's wife related to Rahul?

  1. Mother
  2. Sister-in-law
  3. Aunt
  4. Cousin
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Aunt

Rahul's father's only brother is Rahul's paternal uncle. That uncle's wife is Rahul's aunt (chachi).

Question 5

K is the father of L. L is the brother of M. M is the mother of N. How is K related to N?

  1. Paternal grandfather
  2. Uncle
  3. Father
  4. Maternal grandfather
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Maternal grandfather

M is a daughter of K, and N is M's child. K is the father of N's mother, so K is N's maternal grandfather.

FAQs

What if the gender of a person is not given?

Do not assume it from the name. If the relation depends on gender, look for a gender-neutral option such as 'child' or 'sibling', or choose 'cannot be determined'. Examiners deliberately use names like Kiran and Ashu to test this.

Is it worth drawing a family tree for simple questions?

Yes. A three-person tree takes ten seconds and removes guesswork. For coded questions with three or four symbols, solving mentally is the main cause of wrong answers. Over a few weeks of practice your drawings become fast and compact.
