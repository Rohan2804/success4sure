Probability with Cards, Balls and Independent Events

Card, ball and "two people attempt a problem" questions form the harder half of exam probability. IBPS PO mains, SBI PO, RBI Grade B and CAT use them, and they often come as a set of two or three questions on the same bag of balls. The method is consistent: count favourable selections with combinations, divide by total selections, and be clear about whether items are replaced between draws.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The 52-card deck

A standard deck has 4 suits (spades and clubs are black; hearts and diamonds are red) of 13 cards each: A, 2–10, J, Q, K. There are 4 of each rank, 12 face cards (J, Q, K) and 26 red cards. For "king or heart", add 4 and 13 and subtract the king of hearts: 16/52 = 4/13.

Drawing balls together

Drawing several balls at once is a combination: P = (ways to choose the favourable balls) ÷ (ways to choose any balls). From 5 red, 4 blue and 3 green, two reds: 5C2/12C2 = 10/66 = 5/33.

P = favourable selections / nCr

With and without replacement

With replacement, each draw has the same probabilities and they multiply. Without replacement, the second draw depends on the first, so the denominator and counts shrink: two aces without replacement = 4/52 × 3/51 = 1/221.

Independent people or events

For independent events, P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B). The chance that a problem is solved by at least one of two people is 1 − P(both fail). If A solves with probability 1/3 and B with 1/4, P(solved) = 1 − (2/3)(3/4) = 1/2.

P(at least one) = 1 − (1 − p)(1 − q)

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Exactly one of each colour from three colours: multiply single choices (a × b × c) over nC3.
  • Face cards = 12, honours (A, K, Q, J) = 16, red face cards = 6.
  • "Without replacement" draws one by one give the same answer as drawing together.
  • P(exactly one of A, B) = p(1 − q) + q(1 − p).
  • Check that all case probabilities add to 1 when a question asks several parts on the same bag.

Worked examples

Example 1

A card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck. What is the probability that it is a king or a heart?

Solution

Kings: 4; hearts: 13; king of hearts counted twice. Favourable = 16. P = 16/52 = 4/13.

Example 2

A bag has 5 red, 4 blue and 3 green balls. Two balls are drawn at random. Find the probability that both are red.

Solution

Total ways = 12C2 = 66. Favourable = 5C2 = 10. P = 10/66 = 5/33.

Example 3

From the same bag, 3 balls are drawn. What is the probability that they are of different colours?

Solution

Favourable = 5 × 4 × 3 = 60. Total = 12C3 = 220. P = 60/220 = 3/11.

Example 4

Two cards are drawn one after another without replacement. What is the probability that both are aces?

Solution

P = 4/52 × 3/51 = 12/2,652 = 1/221. The same answer comes from 4C2/52C2 = 6/1,326.

Example 5

A can solve a problem with probability 1/3 and B with probability 1/4, independently. What is the probability that the problem is solved?

Solution

P(both fail) = 2/3 × 3/4 = 1/2. P(solved) = 1 − 1/2 = 1/2.

Example 6

Two cards are drawn together from a deck. What is the probability that exactly one is a king?

Solution

Choose 1 king from 4 and 1 non-king from 48: 4 × 48 = 192. Total = 52C2 = 1,326. P = 192/1,326 = 32/221.

Practice questions

Question 1

A card is drawn from a deck of 52. The probability that it is a face card is:

  1. 1/13
  2. 3/13
  3. 4/13
  4. 3/26
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 3/13

There are 12 face cards (J, Q, K in four suits), so the probability is 12/52 = 3/13.

Question 2

A bag has 6 white and 4 black balls. Two are drawn at random. The probability that both are black is:

  1. 4/25
  2. 1/5
  3. 2/5
  4. 2/15
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 2/15

Favourable = 4C2 = 6 and total = 10C2 = 45, so the probability is 6/45 = 2/15.

Question 3

From the same bag (6 white, 4 black), two balls are drawn. The probability of one white and one black is:

  1. 8/15
  2. 12/25
  3. 2/5
  4. 1/2
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 8/15

Favourable = 6 × 4 = 24 out of 45 selections, which reduces to 8/15.

Question 4

A card is drawn from a deck. The probability that it is red or a queen is:

  1. 15/26
  2. 1/2
  3. 7/13
  4. 8/13
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 7/13

Red cards 26 plus queens 4 minus 2 red queens gives 28 cards, so 28/52 = 7/13.

Question 5

A hits a target with probability 2/5 and B with probability 1/2. If both shoot once, the probability that the target is hit is:

  1. 1/5
  2. 7/10
  3. 9/10
  4. 3/10
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 7/10

P(both miss) = 3/5 × 1/2 = 3/10, so P(target hit) = 1 − 3/10 = 7/10.

FAQs

Is drawing two balls together the same as drawing them one after another?

Yes, provided the first ball is not replaced. Drawing together counts unordered pairs (nC2), while one-by-one counts ordered pairs, but both numerator and denominator change by the same factor, so the probability is identical.

What does "independent" mean in probability questions?

Two events are independent if one happening does not change the chance of the other. Two people solving a problem separately or two separate coin tosses are independent, so their probabilities multiply. Drawing cards without replacement is not independent.
