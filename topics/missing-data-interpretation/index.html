Missing Data Interpretation: Filling Gaps in DI Tables

Missing DI is a table in which some cells are blank and must be worked out from the others before any question can be answered. It is a regular feature of IBPS PO mains, SBI PO mains and RBI Grade B, and it has started appearing in prelims too. These sets test arithmetic relationships — part and whole, percentage and amount, revenue, cost and profit — more than chart reading.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Know the relationship in each row

Every row hides a formula: total = online + offline; revenue = cost + profit; profit % = profit/cost × 100. Write the formula above the table so you can fill any two-of-three situation immediately.

Revenue = Cost + Profit

Fill the whole table first

Complete every blank before reading the questions. Questions usually need several filled cells, and having the full table avoids recomputation. If one cell cannot be found, note it — a question may ask which value cannot be determined.

Percent of what?

A blank may be given as "online sales are 30% of total" or "profit is 20% of cost". Identify the base carefully. Profit as a percentage of revenue and as a percentage of cost give different numbers, and setters use both.

Chains of blanks

Sometimes one blank can only be found after another is filled — for example, a total from one row is needed to compute a percentage in another. Work in the order the information allows, and keep a short note of which cells depend on which, so a single correction can be carried through.

Cross-check with totals

If the table gives a grand total, add your filled values to confirm. A mismatch means an earlier cell is wrong, and it is cheaper to fix it now than after answering three questions.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • From part and percentage: total = part ÷ percentage. 120 being 30% of total gives total 400.
  • From total and percentage of the complement: if 75% is offline and offline = 360, total = 480.
  • Profit % on cost p with revenue R gives cost = R × 100/(100 + p).
  • Write filled values in a different colour or with a mark so you know which were derived.

Worked examples

Example 1

Shop sales: A total 400, online 35%. B online 120, which is 30% of its total. C total 500, offline 300. D offline 360, online 25% of total. Find B’s total and offline sales.

Solution

Online sales of 120 are 30% of B’s total, so total = 120 ÷ 0.30 = 400. Offline = 400 − 120 = 280. Dividing by the percentage, not multiplying, is the key step whenever a part and its percentage are given.

Example 2

What percentage of C’s sales were online?

Solution

C online = 500 − 300 = 200. Percentage = 200/500 × 100 = 40%.

Example 3

Find D’s total and online sales.

Solution

Online is 25%, so offline is the remaining 75% of total. Total = 360 ÷ 0.75 = 480 and online = 480 − 360 = 120. Using the complement percentage is what makes this row solvable.

Example 4

Find the total online sales of all four shops.

Solution

A online = 35% of 400 = 140. Total = 140 + 120 + 200 + 120 = 580.

Example 5

Find the ratio of A’s offline sales to D’s offline sales.

Solution

A offline = 400 − 140 = 260; D offline = 360. Ratio = 260 : 360 = 13 : 18.

Example 6

Which shop had the highest share of online sales, and which had the lowest?

Solution

Online shares: A 35%, B 30%, C 40%, D 25%. C has the highest and D the lowest share. In absolute terms, however, C (200) is also the largest online seller, while B and D tie at 120 units — another reminder that share and amount can rank differently.

Practice questions

Question 1

Company data (₹ lakh): P revenue 500, cost 400. Q cost 600, profit 20% on cost. R revenue 900, profit 150. S revenue 650, profit 30% on cost. Q’s revenue is:

  1. ₹720 lakh
  2. ₹750 lakh
  3. ₹660 lakh
  4. ₹780 lakh
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹720 lakh

Q’s profit is 20% of 600 = ₹120 lakh, so revenue = 600 + 120 = ₹720 lakh.

Question 2

Using the same data, R’s profit percentage on cost is:

  1. 16.67%
  2. 25%
  3. 20%
  4. 18%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 20%

R’s cost = 900 − 150 = 750, and 150/750 × 100 = 20 percent profit on cost.

Question 3

Using the same data, S’s cost is:

  1. ₹455 lakh
  2. ₹500 lakh
  3. ₹520 lakh
  4. ₹480 lakh
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹500 lakh

Revenue is 130% of cost, so cost = 650 ÷ 1.3 = ₹500 lakh and profit is ₹150 lakh.

Question 4

Using the same data, the total profit of all four companies is:

  1. ₹520 lakh
  2. ₹500 lakh
  3. ₹470 lakh
  4. ₹550 lakh
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹520 lakh

Profits are P 100, Q 120, R 150 and S 150, which add up to ₹520 lakh.

Question 5

Using the same data, which company has the highest profit percentage on cost?

  1. P
  2. Q
  3. S
  4. R
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. S

Profit percentages are P 25%, Q 20%, R 20% and S 30%, so company S is the highest.

FAQs

Are missing DI sets only in mains exams?

They are most common in IBPS PO, SBI PO and RBI Grade B mains, but simpler versions have appeared in prelims. Practising them builds the habit of reading relationships between columns, which also helps with ordinary table and caselet sets.

What if a blank cell cannot be found from the given data?

Then the table is intentionally incomplete. A question may ask for that value with an option such as "cannot be determined". Make sure you have used every given relation before choosing that option.
