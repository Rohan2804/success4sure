Percentage Tricks and Formulas for Bank and SSC Exams

Percentage is the backbone of arithmetic in IBPS PO, IBPS Clerk, SBI PO/Clerk, RBI Grade B and SSC CGL/CHSL. Direct percentage questions are only one or two per paper, but the same skill drives profit and loss, interest, data interpretation and quantity comparison, so weak percentage speed slows down almost a third of the quant section. The aim is not formula memory but instant conversion between fractions and percentages and a clear sense of which quantity is the base.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

What a percentage really is

A percentage is a fraction with denominator 100. "x% of N" means N × x/100. The harder part in exams is identifying the base: "A is 20% more than B" uses B as the base, while "B is what percent less than A" uses A. Most wrong answers in this chapter come from using the wrong base, not from bad arithmetic.

x% of N = N × x / 100

Fraction–percentage table

Learn these by heart: 1/2 = 50%, 1/3 = 33.33%, 1/4 = 25%, 1/5 = 20%, 1/6 = 16.67%, 1/7 = 14.28%, 1/8 = 12.5%, 1/9 = 11.11%, 1/11 = 9.09%, 1/12 = 8.33%, 1/16 = 6.25%. With these, 37.5% of 640 becomes 3/8 × 640 = 240 in one step instead of a long multiplication.

More than / less than

If A is r% more than B, then B is less than A by r/(100 + r) × 100 percent. If A is r% less than B, then B is more than A by r/(100 − r) × 100 percent. The same rule handles price-rise and consumption questions: when price rises by r%, consumption must fall by r/(100 + r) × 100 percent to keep spending unchanged.

B less than A = r / (100 + r) × 100

Marks and elections

Pass-mark questions give marks scored and the shortfall; add them to get the pass mark, then divide by the pass percentage. Election questions give the winning margin; the margin equals the difference of the two vote-share percentages applied to valid votes cast, not to total voters.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Swap trick: x% of y = y% of x. So 16% of 25 = 25% of 16 = 4.
  • For 15% of a number, take 10% and add half of it: 15% of 860 = 86 + 43 = 129.
  • Increase by r% means multiply by (100 + r)/100: a 12.5% rise is × 9/8, so ₹1,600 becomes ₹1,800.
  • Decrease by 20% = × 4/5 and decrease by 25% = × 3/4; recognising the fraction saves time in long chains.
  • If the answer must be a whole number of people or marks, eliminate options that make the base a fraction.
  • 1% of a number is just the decimal moved two places: 1% of 3,450 = 34.5, so 7% = 241.5.

Worked examples

Example 1

A’s salary is 25% more than B’s. By what percent is B’s salary less than A’s?

Solution

Take B = 100, so A = 125. B is less than A by 25 on a base of 125, so 25/125 × 100 = 20%. Using the rule r/(100 + r) × 100 = 25/125 × 100 gives the same 20% directly.

Example 2

A candidate needs 40% marks to pass. He scores 185 marks and fails by 35 marks. Find the maximum marks.

Solution

Pass mark = 185 + 35 = 220. This equals 40% of the maximum, so maximum = 220 ÷ 0.40 = 550 marks.

Example 3

The price of edible oil rises by 25%. By what percent should a family reduce its consumption so that its monthly expenditure on oil stays the same?

Solution

Reduction = 25/(100 + 25) × 100 = 20%. Check: if the family earlier bought 10 litres at ₹100, spending ₹1,000, the new price is ₹125 and ₹1,000 now buys 8 litres, which is 20% less.

Example 4

In a municipal ward, 60% of registered voters voted. The winner got 55% of the votes cast and won by 1,800 votes. There were only two candidates. Find the number of registered voters.

Solution

The loser got 45% of votes cast, so the margin is 10% of votes cast = 1,800. Votes cast = 18,000. These are 60% of registered voters, so registered voters = 18,000 ÷ 0.60 = 30,000.

Practice questions

Question 1

What is 35% of 840?

  1. 294
  2. 284
  3. 304
  4. 296
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 294

35% = 30% + 5%: 30% of 840 is 252 and 5% is 42, so the total is 294.

Question 2

A’s income is 20% less than B’s income. By what percent is B’s income more than A’s?

  1. 20%
  2. 22.5%
  3. 30%
  4. 25%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 25%

Take B = 100, so A = 80. B exceeds A by 20 on a base of 80, which is 20/80 × 100 = 25%.

Question 3

A student needs 33% marks to pass. She gets 210 marks and fails by 21 marks. What are the maximum marks?

  1. 630
  2. 700
  3. 660
  4. 750
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 700

Pass mark = 210 + 21 = 231, which is 33% of the maximum, so the maximum is 231 ÷ 0.33 = 700.

Question 4

If the price of rice rises by 20%, by how much must consumption fall so that expenditure is unchanged?

  1. 20%
  2. 15%
  3. 16.67%
  4. 18.5%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 16.67%

Required reduction = 20/(100 + 20) × 100 = 16.67%, i.e. one-sixth of the earlier consumption.

Question 5

12% of a number is 54. What is 35% of the same number?

  1. 150
  2. 157.5
  3. 162.5
  4. 155
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 157.5

The number is 54 ÷ 0.12 = 450, and 35% of 450 equals 157.5.

FAQs

How many percentage questions come in IBPS PO prelims?

Usually one to three direct arithmetic questions use percentage, but percentage calculation appears inside almost every DI set and in profit and loss, interest and quantity-comparison questions. Treat it as a core speed skill rather than a single topic to tick off.

Should I memorise fractions up to 1/20?

Yes, at least up to 1/12 plus 1/15, 1/16 and 1/20. These cover most exam percentages such as 8.33%, 6.25% and 14.28%. Knowing them turns multiplication into simple division and cuts calculation time noticeably in DI sets.
