Problems on Ages: Ratio Method and Solved Questions

Problems on ages are a steady single question in IBPS Clerk, SBI Clerk, SSC CHSL and SSC MTS papers, and they occasionally appear as statements in data sufficiency. They are pure linear-equation questions dressed in family language. The fastest approach combines one key fact — the age gap between two people never changes — with option elimination, so many questions can be solved without writing an equation at all.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The constant gap

Two people age at the same rate, so the difference in their ages is fixed forever. If a father is 30 years older than his son today, he was 30 years older ten years ago and will be 30 years older in twenty years. Many ratio-change questions are solved by matching this gap across two ratios.

Ratio-difference method

If present ages are 4 : 5 and after 6 years they become 5 : 6, both ratios have a difference of 1 part. Hence 1 part in the first ratio equals 1 part in the second, and the change of 1 part (from 4 to 5) equals the 6 years. So 1 part = 6 years and present ages are 24 and 30.

Writing equations

Let the unknown present age be x. "n years ago" means subtract n from each person, "after n years" means add n to each. Always shift every person in the question, not just one. Then form the equation from the statement and solve.

Age t years ago = present age − t

Average-age questions

When a family’s average age is given at one time and asked at another, multiply by the number of members, add the years passed for each member, and handle newborns separately because a child born later contributes only its own age.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Check options against both conditions — most age questions have one option that fits quickly.
  • If A is k times B now, the gap = (k − 1) × B’s age. Use it to cross-check.
  • For n children born at equal intervals of d years with sum S, the youngest = (S − d × n(n − 1)/2) ÷ n.
  • Present ages in ratio a : b with sum S give ages aS/(a + b) and bS/(a + b).
  • A ratio can only increase towards 1 as time passes; if a future ratio is further from 1, re-read the question.

Worked examples

Example 1

The present ages of A and B are in the ratio 4 : 5. After 6 years, the ratio will be 5 : 6. Find their present ages.

Solution

Both ratios have a difference of 1 part, so the parts are directly comparable. A moves from 4 parts to 5 parts in 6 years, so 1 part = 6 years. Present ages: 24 and 30. Check: 30/36 = 5/6.

Example 2

A father is three times as old as his son. After 15 years, he will be twice as old as his son. Find their present ages.

Solution

Let the son be s. Then 3s + 15 = 2(s + 15), so s = 15 and the father is 45. The gap of 30 years stays constant: after 15 years they are 30 and 60.

Example 3

The sum of the ages of 5 children born at intervals of 3 years each is 50 years. Find the age of the youngest.

Solution

Ages are x, x + 3, x + 6, x + 9, x + 12, so 5x + 30 = 50 and x = 4 years.

Example 4

Ten years ago, A was half of B in age. The ratio of their present ages is 3 : 4. What are their present ages?

Solution

Present ages 3x and 4x. Ten years ago: 3x − 10 = (4x − 10)/2, so 6x − 20 = 4x − 10 and x = 5. Present ages: 15 and 20 years. Check: ten years ago they were 5 and 10.

Practice questions

Question 1

The present ages of two brothers are in the ratio 5 : 7 and their sum is 48 years. What will be the elder brother’s age after 5 years?

  1. 28 years
  2. 33 years
  3. 25 years
  4. 35 years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 33 years

The ages are 20 and 28 years, so the elder brother will be 28 + 5 = 33 years old.

Question 2

A mother is four times as old as her daughter. In 5 years she will be three times as old. The mother’s present age is:

  1. 36 years
  2. 44 years
  3. 40 years
  4. 32 years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 40 years

With daughter d, 4d + 5 = 3(d + 5) gives d = 10, so the mother is 40 years old.

Question 3

Eight years ago, A was three times as old as B. Now A is twice as old as B. A’s present age is:

  1. 24 years
  2. 16 years
  3. 36 years
  4. 32 years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 32 years

With A = 2B, 2B − 8 = 3(B − 8) gives B = 16, so A is 32 years old today.

Question 4

At their marriage, the average age of a husband and wife was 23 years. Five years later, with a 1-year-old child, the family’s average age is:

  1. 18 years
  2. 20 years
  3. 19 years
  4. 21 years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 19 years

The couple total 46 + 10 = 56 after five years, plus the child’s 1 gives 57, and 57 ÷ 3 = 19.

Question 5

The sum of the present ages of a father and son is 60 years. Six years ago, the father was five times as old as the son. The son’s present age is:

  1. 14 years
  2. 12 years
  3. 16 years
  4. 10 years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 14 years

From F + S = 60 and F − 6 = 5(S − 6), we get 6S = 84, so the son is 14 years old.

FAQs

Can age problems be solved without algebra?

Often yes. The ratio-difference method and option checking handle most bank-exam age questions. Algebra is still the safest fallback when the statement involves three people or both past and future conditions. Use whichever is faster for the specific wording.

Why do age problems appear in data sufficiency?

Because each statement gives one relation, and setters can test whether two relations are independent. For example, a ratio and a sum together fix the ages, while two ratios at the same moment may not. Practising equations helps you judge sufficiency quickly.
