Para Jumbles: Finding the Opening Line and Linking Pairs

Para jumbles (sentence rearrangement) give four to six sentences in random order and ask for the correct sequence. IBPS PO and SBI PO prelims and mains often include a set of five questions, SSC papers ask single rearrangement questions, and CAT uses the harder 'type in the answer' version without options. The solution rarely comes from reading every arrangement; it comes from finding the opening sentence and a few fixed pairs.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The opening sentence

The first sentence introduces the topic and can stand alone. It usually has no pronoun pointing back ('this', 'they', 'such'), no contrast word ('however', 'but') and no connector ('therefore', 'also') at the start. A general statement or a named subject ('The Reserve Bank of India…') is a strong candidate.

Mandatory pairs

Some sentences must follow others. A sentence starting with 'This policy…' needs a previous sentence that names the policy. 'However' needs a statement to contrast with. Identify two or three such pairs and the order largely fixes itself.

Time and logical order

Look for dates, 'first', 'later', 'finally', or a problem–cause–solution flow. Events and arguments usually move forward in time or from general to specific.

The closing sentence

Conclusions often start with 'Thus', 'Therefore', 'In short' or give an outcome or recommendation. If a sentence clearly concludes, place it last and work backwards.

Use the options

When options are given, check which options start with your chosen opening sentence and contain your mandatory pairs. Usually only one survives.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Acronyms are written in full first: 'Unified Payments Interface (UPI)' comes before a sentence using just 'UPI'.
  • Articles help: 'a scheme' introduces something new; 'the scheme' refers back to it.
  • If two sentences both look like openers, the more general one usually comes first.
  • In CAT, find the pair links first and then decide the opener; there are no options to lean on.
  • Read the final order once from start to finish; it should flow like a paragraph.
  • Sentences giving an example ('For instance…') follow the general point they illustrate, never the other way round.

Worked examples

Example 1

Arrange: (A) This has made it easier for small shops to accept digital payments. (B) The Unified Payments Interface (UPI) was launched in 2016. (C) As a result, cash use for small purchases has fallen in many cities. (D) It allows instant transfers between bank accounts using a mobile phone.

Solution

B introduces UPI by full name and date, so it opens. D uses 'It' to describe UPI, so B–D. A uses 'This' to refer to the instant-transfer feature, so D–A. C begins 'As a result', giving the outcome. Order: BDAC.

Example 2

Arrange: (P) However, many of them still lack access to formal credit. (Q) Small farmers form a large share of India's agricultural workforce. (R) Schemes like the Kisan Credit Card aim to close this gap. (S) They often borrow from informal lenders at high interest rates.

Solution

Q names the subject and stands alone, so it opens. P contrasts with Q ('However… them'). S explains the consequence ('They… borrow from informal lenders'). R presents the solution and refers to 'this gap'. Order: QPSR.

Example 3

Which is the best opening sentence? (a) Therefore, the deadline was extended. (b) The income tax department sets a deadline for filing returns each year. (c) This caused a rush on the portal. (d) However, many taxpayers missed it.

Solution

(a), (c) and (d) begin with words that need an earlier sentence: 'Therefore', 'This' and 'However'. (b) introduces the topic independently. Answer: (b).

Practice questions

Question 1

Which sentence is most likely to open a paragraph?

  1. However, it also regulates payment systems.
  2. This role was given to it by law.
  3. The Reserve Bank of India is the country's central bank.
  4. As a result, it influences interest rates.
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The Reserve Bank of India is the country's central bank.

The opening sentence introduces the subject without referring back. The others start with 'However', 'This' or 'As a result', which depend on an earlier sentence.

Question 2

Arrange: (A) They help track spending easily. (B) Many people now use budgeting apps. (C) As a result, some users save more each month.

  1. ACB
  2. CBA
  3. BCA
  4. BAC
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. BAC

B introduces budgeting apps, A uses 'They' to describe them, and C gives the result. The logical order is B, A, C.

Question 3

A sentence begins with 'This scheme'. What must the sentence before it contain?

  1. A mention of the scheme
  2. A contrast word
  3. A conclusion
  4. A question
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A mention of the scheme

'This scheme' refers back to something already introduced. The preceding sentence must name or describe the scheme for the reference to make sense.

Question 4

Arrange: (P) Finally, the results are declared online. (Q) First, candidates register on the portal. (R) Then they appear for the online exam.

  1. PQR
  2. QRP
  3. RQP
  4. QPR
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. QRP

Time markers decide the order: 'First' (Q), 'Then' (R) and 'Finally' (P). The correct sequence is Q, R, P.

Question 5

Which word at the start of a sentence most strongly suggests it is the concluding sentence?

  1. However
  2. Moreover
  3. Thus
  4. Firstly
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Thus

'Thus' introduces a result or summary, which typically closes a paragraph. 'However' and 'Moreover' continue an argument, and 'Firstly' begins a sequence.

FAQs

How do I solve para jumbles without options, as in CAT?

Find two or three mandatory pairs using pronouns, connectors and acronyms, then decide the opener and closer. Build the chain around these fixed blocks. Always read the final sequence aloud mentally to check flow, because there is no option list to catch mistakes.

What if two sentences both seem like good openers?

Pick the more general one, or the one that introduces a name or term the other uses. A sentence that defines or introduces a concept almost always comes before a sentence that discusses its effects or examples.
