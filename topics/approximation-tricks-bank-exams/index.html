Approximation Questions: Rounding Tricks for Bank Prelims

Approximation is the sibling of simplification: the numbers are deliberately awkward (39.97% of 601.02), and the options are spaced far enough apart that sensible rounding gives the answer. IBPS PO, IBPS Clerk, SBI PO and SBI Clerk prelims often carry a five-question approximation block, and the same skill is essential for DI, where exact calculation wastes time. The aim is not precision but choosing the right option in under 25 seconds.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Round to the nearest friendly number

Round each number to the nearest integer or to a value that makes the operation easy: 39.97% becomes 40%, 601.02 becomes 600, 24.97 becomes 25. Because the options are usually 10% or more apart, these small changes do not affect which option is closest.

Keep errors balanced

When you round one factor up in a product, round the other down if possible, so the errors partly cancel. In division, rounding the numerator and denominator in the same direction keeps the quotient close to the true value.

Square roots and powers

Round inside the root to the nearest perfect square: √1599 ≈ √1600 = 40 and √624.8 ≈ √625 = 25. For squares like 29.97², use 30² = 900; the error is about 2 × 30 × 0.03 ≈ 1.8, negligible against options.

Approximation inside DI

In data interpretation, you often need values like 1,847 as a percentage of 6,112. Round to 1,850 ÷ 6,100 ≈ 0.303, i.e. about 30%. Using fraction benchmarks (1/3 ≈ 33.3%, 3/10 = 30%) lets you place the answer between two options without long division, which is where most DI time is saved.

Finding "?" approximately

Rearrange first, then approximate: 44.89% of 799.9 − ? = 15.01² becomes 360 − ? ≈ 225, so ? ≈ 135. If an option is very close to your estimate and the next is far away, choose without further checking.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Treat x.97 to x.99 as x + 1 and x.01 to x.03 as x.
  • Percentages near 25, 50, 75, 20, 12.5 convert to fractions: 24.98% ≈ 1/4.
  • Look at the gap between options before calculating — wide gaps allow rougher rounding.
  • For a ÷ b × c, compute the division first if b divides a neatly after rounding.
  • Write intermediate results; a slip in a mental chain is the main source of error, not rounding.

Worked examples

Example 1

39.97% of 601.02 + 19.98 × 15.01 ≈ ?

Solution

≈ 40% of 600 + 20 × 15 = 240 + 300 = 540. The exact value is about 540.1.

Example 2

√1599 × 6.02 ≈ ?

Solution

√1600 = 40, so 40 × 6 = 240.

Example 3

2999.8 ÷ 24.97 × 4.99 ≈ ?

Solution

≈ 3000 ÷ 25 × 5 = 120 × 5 = 600.

Example 4

44.89% of 799.9 − ? ≈ 15.01²

Solution

45% of 800 = 360 and 15² = 225. So ? ≈ 360 − 225 = 135.

Example 5

1/7 of 3,499.88 ≈ ?

Solution

3,500 ÷ 7 = 500. Rounding 3,499.88 to 3,500 is natural here because 3,500 is a multiple of 7, which turns the division into a one-step answer.

Example 6

(7.98)³ ÷ 15.97 + 63.02% of 299.8 ≈ ?

Solution

Round to 8³ ÷ 16 + 63% of 300 = 512 ÷ 16 + 189 = 32 + 189 = 221. The exact value is about 220.7, so 221 or the nearest option is safe. Notice that 8³ and 16 were chosen so that the division is exact once rounded.

Round to 4,800 ÷ 40 = 120; both numbers rounded up slightly, so the quotient barely changes.

Question 4

  1. 450
  2. 550
  3. 400
  4. 500

Answer: D. 500

Use 30² − 20² = 900 − 400 = 500, or (30 + 20)(30 − 20) = 500 by the difference of squares.

Answer: D. 130

FAQs

How far apart are options in approximation questions?

Typically 5–15% apart, so rounding errors of 1–2% do not matter. If two options are very close, round more carefully, keeping one decimal place, or check the direction of your rounding errors.

Should I approximate in DI questions too?

Yes, whenever the options are reasonably spaced. DI questions often ask for a percentage or ratio where two-significant-figure accuracy is enough. Exact calculation should be reserved for cases where options differ by less than about 2%.
