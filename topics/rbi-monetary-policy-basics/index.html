RBI and Monetary Policy Basics: Repo, CRR, SLR and MPC

Questions on the Reserve Bank of India and monetary policy are certain in RBI Grade B Phase I, IBPS PO and SBI PO mains, JAIIB and CAIIB, and they come up in almost every bank interview. Rates change through the year, so this page focuses on the stable framework: what the RBI is, who sets policy, and how each tool works. For the latest rate figures, always check the RBI's most recent monetary policy statement on rbi.org.in.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Origin and status

The RBI was set up on 1 April 1935 under the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934. It was nationalised in 1949 and is wholly owned by the Government of India. Its central office is in Mumbai. It issues currency notes (except the one-rupee note, which the Government issues), regulates banks, manages foreign exchange under FEMA, acts as banker to the government and banks, and oversees payment systems.

Monetary Policy Committee

Since 2016, under amendments to the RBI Act, a six-member Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) sets the policy repo rate. Three members are from the RBI, including the Governor, who has a casting vote, and three are external members appointed by the central government. The framework is flexible inflation targeting based on CPI inflation; the target band has been 4% with a tolerance of ±2%. Check the RBI for the current target period.

Policy rates

The repo rate is the rate at which the RBI lends to banks against government securities overnight. The Standing Deposit Facility (SDF), introduced in 2022, lets banks park surplus funds with the RBI without collateral and forms the floor of the policy corridor. The Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) lets banks borrow overnight at a rate above repo in emergencies and forms the ceiling. The bank rate is aligned with the MSF rate.

Reserve requirements

The Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) is the share of a bank's net demand and time liabilities that must be kept as cash balance with the RBI; it earns no interest. The Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) is the share that banks must hold in liquid assets such as cash, gold and approved government securities.

Open market operations

Through open market operations (OMOs), the RBI buys government securities to inject liquidity or sells them to absorb it. Variable-rate repo and reverse repo auctions fine-tune short-term liquidity.

Direction of policy

To fight inflation, the RBI may raise the repo rate or tighten liquidity, making loans costlier. To support growth, it may cut rates or add liquidity. Policy works with a lag, often several quarters, before it fully affects prices and output.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Corridor: SDF at the bottom, repo in the middle, MSF at the top.
  • CRR is held with the RBI as cash; SLR is held by the bank itself in liquid assets.
  • MPC: six members, Governor's casting vote, decision by majority.
  • Higher CRR or repo → less money with banks → tighter policy (hawkish).
  • The RBI is the lender of last resort to banks.
  • Learn the concept now; update the actual rate numbers from the latest policy before your exam.

Worked examples

Example 1

The RBI raises the repo rate. What is the likely effect on home-loan EMIs linked to an external benchmark?

Solution

Many floating home loans are linked to the repo rate through the external benchmark lending rate. A repo hike raises the benchmark, so the loan rate and either the EMI or the tenure increases after the reset date.

Example 2

A bank has net demand and time liabilities of ₹10,000 crore. If CRR were 4%, how much must it keep with the RBI?

Solution

4% of ₹10,000 crore = ₹400 crore, held as cash balance with the RBI. (The 4% figure is only for illustration; check the current CRR.)

Example 3

Which tool would the RBI use to absorb surplus liquidity overnight without giving collateral to banks?

Solution

The Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) absorbs liquidity without the RBI having to provide government securities as collateral. That is why it replaced the fixed-rate reverse repo as the floor of the corridor.

Example 4

Who has the casting vote in the MPC if votes are tied?

Solution

The RBI Governor. With six members, a 3–3 tie is possible, and the Governor's second or casting vote decides.

Practice questions

Question 1

In which year was the Reserve Bank of India established?

  1. 1949
  2. 1947
  3. 1935
  4. 1969
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 1935

The RBI began operations on 1 April 1935 under the RBI Act of 1934. It was nationalised later, in 1949.

Question 2

How many members does the Monetary Policy Committee have?

  1. Five
  2. Seven
  3. Four
  4. Six
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Six

The MPC has six members: three from the RBI, including the Governor, and three external members appointed by the central government.

Question 3

Which facility forms the floor of the RBI's liquidity adjustment corridor?

  1. Standing Deposit Facility
  2. Marginal Standing Facility
  3. Bank rate
  4. Cash Reserve Ratio
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Standing Deposit Facility

Since 2022 the Standing Deposit Facility, which absorbs surplus funds without collateral, has formed the floor of the corridor. MSF forms the ceiling.

Question 4

Banks must keep a portion of their liabilities as cash balance with the RBI. What is this called?

  1. Statutory Liquidity Ratio
  2. Cash Reserve Ratio
  3. Repo rate
  4. Priority sector lending
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Cash Reserve Ratio

The Cash Reserve Ratio is the share of net demand and time liabilities held as cash with the RBI. SLR is held by banks in liquid assets.

Question 5

When the RBI buys government securities in the open market, what is the effect on liquidity?

  1. It reduces liquidity
  2. It has no effect
  3. It increases liquidity
  4. It changes the CRR
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. It increases liquidity

Buying securities pays money to banks, which adds rupee liquidity to the system. Selling securities has the opposite effect.

FAQs

Should I memorise the current repo rate for bank exams?

Yes, but only from the latest RBI policy statement shortly before your exam, because rates can change at any of the policy meetings held during the year. Learn the concepts and relationships now, and keep a small updated note of the current repo, SDF, MSF, CRR and SLR figures.

What is the difference between repo and reverse repo today?

Repo is the RBI lending to banks against securities. The fixed-rate reverse repo still exists but is no longer the floor of the corridor; the Standing Deposit Facility took that role in 2022. For current rates and their roles, refer to the RBI's latest monetary policy statement.
