Mean, Median and Mode: Statistics Basics for SSC and Banks

Basic statistics — mean, median, mode and range — is part of the SSC CGL Tier 2 syllabus and appears in SSC CHSL and in bank DI questions that ask for "median sales" or "average production". The ideas are simple, but questions add twists: an even number of observations, a transformed data set, or the empirical relation linking the three averages. Ordering the data before anything else prevents most mistakes.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Mean

The arithmetic mean is the sum of observations divided by their count. If every observation is multiplied by k and then increased by c, the new mean is k × old mean + c. Adding the same number to every value shifts the mean by that number but does not change the range.

New mean = k × mean + c

Median

Arrange the data in order. For an odd count n, the median is the ((n + 1)/2)-th value; for an even count, it is the average of the two middle values. The median is not affected by extreme values, which is why it is preferred for incomes and house prices.

Mode and range

The mode is the most frequent value; a data set can have more than one mode or none. The range is the largest value minus the smallest and measures spread crudely.

Empirical relation

For moderately skewed distributions, Mode ≈ 3 × Median − 2 × Mean. SSC questions use this as an exact formula: with mean 40 and median 42, mode = 126 − 80 = 46.

Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean

Weighted mean

When values carry different weights (credits, quantities, number of students), multiply each value by its weight, add and divide by the total weight. A score of 60 with weight 2 and 75 with weight 3 gives (120 + 225)/5 = 69.

Weighted mean = Σwx / Σw

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Always sort before finding a median — unsorted data is the most common trap.
  • Mean of an AP-like set equals its median.
  • If one value changes, the mean changes by (change ÷ count); the median may not change at all.
  • For grouped data in DI tables, compute totals per category first, then averages.

Worked examples

Example 1

Find the mean, median and range of 7, 3, 9, 5, 11.

Solution

Sum = 35, count = 5, mean = 7. Sorted: 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, so the median is 7. Range = 11 − 3 = 8.

Example 2

Find the median of 4, 8, 6, 10, 12, 14.

Solution

Sorted: 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14. The middle values are 8 and 10, so the median is 9.

Example 3

Find the mode of 3, 5, 5, 7, 8, 5, 7.

Solution

Count frequencies: 3 once, 5 three times, 7 twice, 8 once. The value 5 appears most often, so the mode is 5. The mean is 40/7 ≈ 5.71, close to but different from the mode.

Example 4

In a distribution, the mean is 40 and the median is 42. Estimate the mode.

Solution

Mode = 3 × 42 − 2 × 40 = 126 − 80 = 46.

Example 5

The mean of 10 observations is 25. Each is multiplied by 2 and then 5 is added. Find the new mean.

Solution

New mean = 2 × 25 + 5 = 55. There is no need to know the individual observations.

Example 6

A student scores 60 in a 2-credit paper and 75 in a 3-credit paper. Find the credit-weighted average.

Solution

(60 × 2 + 75 × 3)/5 = (120 + 225)/5 = 345/5 = 69. The simple average, 67.5, understates the weight of the bigger paper.

Practice questions

Question 1

The median of 12, 7, 15, 9, 10 is:

  1. 15
  2. 9
  3. 10
  4. 12
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 10

Sorted, the data are 7, 9, 10, 12, 15, so the middle (third) value 10 is the median.

Question 2

The median of 3, 8, 5, 12, 10, 6 is:

  1. 5
  2. 7
  3. 8
  4. 6.5
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 7

Sorted: 3, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12; the two middle values 6 and 8 average to 7.

Question 3

If the mean of a distribution is 22 and its median is 20, the mode by the empirical relation is:

  1. 16
  2. 18
  3. 24
  4. 26
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 16

Mode = 3 × 20 − 2 × 22 = 60 − 44 = 16 using the empirical relation.

Question 4

The mean of 5 numbers is 18. If each number is increased by 4, the new mean is:

  1. 22
  2. 18
  3. 20
  4. 38
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 22

Adding 4 to every value raises the mean by exactly 4, so the new mean is 22.

Question 5

The range of 23, 41, 17, 35, 29 is:

  1. 18
  2. 12
  3. 6
  4. 24
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 24

Range = largest − smallest = 41 − 17 = 24, which measures the spread of the data.

FAQs

When is the median better than the mean?

When the data contain extreme values. In a small office where nine staff earn ₹30,000 and the owner earns ₹10,00,000 a month, the mean salary is misleading, while the median reflects the typical employee. DI questions sometimes test this understanding.

Is the empirical relation exact?

No, it is an approximation for moderately skewed distributions. SSC questions, however, treat it as a formula and expect you to use it directly. Outside exams, the mode should be found from the data itself.
