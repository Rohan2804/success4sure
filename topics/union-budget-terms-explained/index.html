Union Budget Terms: Fiscal, Revenue and Primary Deficit

Budget terminology is tested in RBI Grade B (economic and social issues and GA), IBPS and SBI mains, SSC CGL and CHSL General Awareness, and CMAT. Every year's budget numbers change, but the definitions do not: what counts as revenue or capital, and how the different deficits are calculated. Once these definitions are clear, you can read any budget headline and answer conceptual or numerical questions with confidence.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Constitutional basis

The Union Budget is called the Annual Financial Statement under Article 112 of the Constitution. Money bills are defined in Article 110. Government money flows through the Consolidated Fund of India (Article 266), the Contingency Fund of India (Article 267) and the Public Account.

Revenue and capital

Revenue receipts (taxes, dividends, interest received) neither create liabilities nor reduce assets. Capital receipts do one of those, for example borrowings, loan recoveries and disinvestment. Revenue expenditure (salaries, interest, subsidies) creates no assets; capital expenditure creates assets or reduces liabilities, for example building roads or repaying loans.

Fiscal deficit

Fiscal deficit is the government's total borrowing requirement for the year.

Fiscal deficit = Total expenditure − (Revenue receipts + Non-debt capital receipts)

Revenue and primary deficit

Revenue deficit shows whether day-to-day spending is covered by revenue income. Primary deficit removes interest payments from the fiscal deficit to show current borrowing needs excluding past debt.

Revenue deficit = Revenue expenditure − Revenue receipts; Primary deficit = Fiscal deficit − Interest payments

Effective revenue deficit

Effective revenue deficit is the revenue deficit minus grants given for the creation of capital assets, recognising that some revenue spending (grants to states for asset creation) actually builds assets.

Vote on account and interim budget

A vote on account allows the government to draw money from the Consolidated Fund for a short period until the full budget is passed, usually in an election year. An interim budget presents a short-term statement of accounts in such years.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Non-debt capital receipts = loan recoveries + disinvestment and similar receipts.
  • Primary deficit = fiscal deficit − interest payments.
  • Zero primary deficit means the government is borrowing only to pay interest on past debt.
  • Article 112 = Annual Financial Statement; Article 110 = Money Bill.
  • Deficits are usually quoted as a percentage of GDP.
  • Use only the latest Budget documents (indiabudget.gov.in) for current deficit targets.
  • Gross fiscal deficit includes borrowing to pay interest; that is why primary deficit is always smaller than fiscal deficit when interest payments are positive.

Worked examples

Example 1

Illustrative figures (₹ lakh crore): total expenditure 50, revenue receipts 33, non-debt capital receipts 1, interest payments 11, revenue expenditure 38. Find the fiscal, primary and revenue deficits.

Solution

Fiscal deficit = 50 − (33 + 1) = 16. Primary deficit = 16 − 11 = 5. Revenue deficit = 38 − 33 = 5. These are teaching numbers, not actual budget data.

Example 2

Total expenditure is 20, revenue receipts 12, non-debt capital receipts 2 and interest payments 4. Find the fiscal and primary deficits.

Solution

Fiscal deficit = 20 − (12 + 2) = 6. Primary deficit = 6 − 4 = 2.

Example 3

Is money raised by selling a part of the government's stake in a public sector company a revenue or a capital receipt?

Solution

Disinvestment reduces the government's assets, so it is a capital receipt (a non-debt capital receipt). It reduces the fiscal deficit without adding debt.

Example 4

Revenue expenditure is 15 and revenue receipts are 12. What is the revenue deficit, and what does it signal?

Solution

Revenue deficit = 15 − 12 = 3. It shows that part of day-to-day spending is being financed by borrowing or capital receipts, which is generally seen as weaker fiscal quality.

Practice questions

Question 1

Under which Article of the Constitution is the Union Budget presented as the Annual Financial Statement?

  1. Article 110
  2. Article 112
  3. Article 266
  4. Article 280
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Article 112

Article 112 requires the President to cause the Annual Financial Statement to be laid before Parliament each year.

Question 2

If fiscal deficit is ₹6 lakh crore and interest payments are ₹4 lakh crore, what is the primary deficit?

  1. ₹10 lakh crore
  2. ₹4 lakh crore
  3. ₹2 lakh crore
  4. ₹1.5 lakh crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹2 lakh crore

Primary deficit equals fiscal deficit minus interest payments, so 6 − 4 = ₹2 lakh crore.

Question 3

Which of these is a capital receipt?

  1. Income tax collections
  2. Dividends from PSUs
  3. GST collections
  4. Borrowings by the government
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Borrowings by the government

Borrowings create a liability for the government, so they are capital receipts. Taxes and dividends are revenue receipts.

Question 4

Which of these is revenue expenditure?

  1. Paying salaries of employees
  2. Building a highway
  3. Repaying a loan
  4. Buying machinery for a factory
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Paying salaries of employees

Salaries do not create assets or reduce liabilities, so they are revenue expenditure. The other items create assets or reduce liabilities.

Question 5

Revenue expenditure is 15 and revenue receipts are 12 (same units). What is the revenue deficit?

  1. 27
  2. 3
  3. 1.25
  4. 12
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 3

Revenue deficit is revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts, which gives 15 − 12 = 3.

FAQs

Why is fiscal deficit considered the most important deficit?

Fiscal deficit shows the total amount the government needs to borrow in a year, which affects public debt, interest burden and pressure on interest rates in the economy. That is why governments announce fiscal deficit targets as a percentage of GDP and why rating agencies watch it closely.

Do I need current budget numbers for exams?

For RBI Grade B and bank mains, questions on the latest budget's deficit targets and major announcements are common, but they count as current affairs. Learn definitions from this page and update numbers from the official budget documents for the year of your exam.
