Compound Interest Shortcuts: Annual, Half-Yearly, Quarterly

Compound interest (CI) appears in almost every IBPS PO, SBI PO and RBI Grade B prelims paper, either as a stand-alone question or inside quantity comparison. SSC CGL also asks one or two. Indian fixed deposits compound quarterly, so the idea is practical as well. The exam challenge is speed: handling 2–3 year powers, changing the rate for half-yearly compounding and dealing with fractional years without a calculator.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Basic formula

Amount A = P(1 + R/100)ⁿ and CI = A − P. Each year, interest is earned on the previous year’s amount, so the interest grows every year. For 2 years at 10%, the factor is 1.21; for 3 years, 1.331.

A = P(1 + R/100)ⁿ

Half-yearly and quarterly

For half-yearly compounding, halve the rate and double the number of periods. For quarterly, divide the rate by 4 and multiply the periods by 4. ₹16,000 at 10% p.a. compounded half-yearly for 1 year uses 5% for 2 periods.

Half-yearly: A = P(1 + R/200)²ⁿ

Fractional years

For 2.5 years at R%, compound for 2 full years and then apply simple interest at R/2 for the last half year: A = P(1 + R/100)² × (1 + R/200). This is the convention used in exam questions.

Different rates in different years

When the rate changes from year to year, multiply the separate growth factors: A = P(1 + R1/100)(1 + R2/100)(1 + R3/100). The order of the rates does not change the final amount, which is a useful check when options look close.

Multiplying rule

If a sum becomes k times in n years at CI, it becomes k² times in 2n years and k³ times in 3n years. Doubling in 5 years means 8 times in 15 years. This works because growth factors multiply, unlike simple interest where they add.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Year-wise CI at 10% on ₹1,000: 100, 110, 121. Each year adds 10% of the previous year’s interest on top.
  • Effective annual rate for R% compounded half-yearly = R + R²/400. At 12%, it is 12.36%.
  • Factors worth remembering: 1.05² = 1.1025, 1.08² = 1.1664, 1.1² = 1.21, 1.2² = 1.44, 1.1³ = 1.331.
  • For a rate like 12.5% (1/8), write factor 9/8: two years gives 81/64, so pick principals that are multiples of 64.
  • If the amount for year n and year n + 1 is known, the rate = (difference ÷ earlier amount) × 100.

Worked examples

Example 1

Find the compound interest on ₹20,000 at 10% per annum for 2 years, compounded annually.

Solution

A = 20,000 × 1.21 = ₹24,200, so CI = ₹4,200. Year-wise: ₹2,000 in year 1 and ₹2,200 in year 2.

Example 2

Find the CI on ₹16,000 for 1 year at 10% per annum compounded half-yearly.

Solution

Rate per half year = 5%, periods = 2. A = 16,000 × 1.05² = 16,000 × 1.1025 = ₹17,640. CI = ₹1,640, which is ₹40 more than annual compounding would give.

Example 3

A sum doubles in 5 years at compound interest. In how many years will it become 8 times?

Solution

8 = 2³, so the time is 3 × 5 = 15 years.

Example 4

Find the amount on ₹10,000 for 2½ years at 10% per annum compound interest.

Solution

A = 10,000 × 1.1² × 1.05 = 10,000 × 1.21 × 1.05 = ₹12,705. CI = ₹2,705.

Example 5

Find the compound interest on ₹12,800 for 2 years at 12.5% per annum, compounded annually.

Solution

12.5% = 1/8, so the yearly factor is 9/8. A = 12,800 × 81/64 = 200 × 81 = ₹16,200. CI = ₹3,400. Choosing the fraction route avoids squaring 1.125, and 12,800 was picked by the setter precisely because it is divisible by 64.

Example 6

A deposit earns 5% in the first year and 10% in the second year, compounded annually. What is the amount on ₹40,000 after 2 years?

Solution

Multiply the separate factors: 40,000 × 1.05 × 1.10 = 42,000 × 1.10 = ₹46,200. The total interest is ₹6,200, which is 15.5%, slightly more than the 15% that simple interest at the same rates would give.

Practice questions

Question 1

Find the compound interest on ₹5,000 at 8% per annum for 2 years.

  1. ₹832
  2. ₹800
  3. ₹816
  4. ₹864
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹832

A = 5,000 × 1.1664 = ₹5,832, so CI = ₹832, which is ₹32 more than simple interest.

Question 2

Find the CI on ₹8,000 for 1 year at 20% per annum compounded half-yearly.

  1. ₹1,600
  2. ₹1,680
  3. ₹1,640
  4. ₹1,760
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹1,680

Use 10% per half year for 2 periods: 8,000 × 1.21 = ₹9,680, so CI = ₹1,680.

Question 3

A sum doubles in 4 years at compound interest. It will become 16 times in:

  1. 12 years
  2. 32 years
  3. 16 years
  4. 8 years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 16 years

16 = 2⁴, so the time needed is 4 × 4 = 16 years when the sum doubles every 4 years.

Question 4

A sum amounts to ₹8,820 in 2 years at 5% per annum compound interest. The principal is:

  1. ₹7,800
  2. ₹8,200
  3. ₹8,000
  4. ₹8,400
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹8,000

P = 8,820 ÷ 1.05² = 8,820 ÷ 1.1025 = ₹8,000, the original principal.

Question 5

What is the effective annual rate for 12% per annum compounded half-yearly?

  1. 12.36%
  2. 12%
  3. 12.24%
  4. 12.72%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 12.36%

Effective rate = 1.06² − 1 = 0.1236, i.e. 12.36 percent per year when compounding twice.

FAQs

Why do bank exams prefer rates like 10%, 20% and 12.5%?

Because the growth factors become friendly fractions such as 11/10, 6/5 and 9/8, allowing mental calculation without a calculator. When you see such rates, convert them to fractions and choose principal values that cancel cleanly. This speeds up two- and three-year questions considerably.

How does quarterly compounding compare with annual compounding?

For the same nominal rate, quarterly compounding always gives a slightly higher amount because interest is added more often. At 8% p.a., quarterly compounding gives an effective annual rate of about 8.24%. The difference grows with higher rates and longer periods.
