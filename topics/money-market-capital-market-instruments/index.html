Money Market vs Capital Market Instruments in India

Financial markets questions are common in RBI Grade B (Finance and GA), IBPS and SBI mains, JAIIB, CAIIB and NISM-adjacent interviews. The basic split is simple: the money market deals in short-term funds of up to one year, the capital market in long-term funds. Exams ask about tenors, issuers and regulators of instruments such as treasury bills, commercial paper and certificates of deposit, which are stable facts worth learning once.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Money market versus capital market

The money market handles borrowing and lending for up to one year and is regulated mainly by the RBI. The capital market handles long-term funds through equity and debt securities and is regulated mainly by SEBI (with the RBI for government securities).

Treasury bills

T-bills are short-term government debt issued by the RBI on behalf of the central government in three tenors: 91, 182 and 364 days. They are issued at a discount and redeemed at face value; the difference is the investor's return.

Commercial paper and certificates of deposit

Commercial paper (CP) is an unsecured promissory note issued by companies and other eligible entities, with maturity from 7 days to one year. Certificates of deposit (CDs) are issued by banks (7 days to one year) and certain financial institutions (one to three years), in dematerialised form.

Call, notice and term money

Call money is borrowing for one day (overnight); notice money for 2 to 14 days; term money for 15 days to one year. These markets mainly involve banks and primary dealers.

Repo and TREPS

In a market repo, one party sells securities with an agreement to buy them back later, effectively borrowing against collateral. TREPS (Tri-party Repo Dealing and Settlement), run by the Clearing Corporation of India, is a widely used collateralised short-term borrowing platform.

Capital market instruments

Equity shares give ownership and variable returns through dividends and price changes. Debentures and bonds are debt with fixed or floating interest. Government securities (G-secs) are long-term central government bonds; state development loans are state government bonds. Primary markets handle new issues; secondary markets handle trading.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Money market: up to 1 year; capital market: more than 1 year.
  • T-bills: 91, 182, 364 days, issued at a discount.
  • CP: companies, 7 days–1 year, unsecured.
  • CD: banks 7 days–1 year; FIs 1–3 years.
  • Call money = 1 day; notice money = 2–14 days; term money = 15 days–1 year.
  • SEBI regulates securities markets; the RBI regulates money markets and G-sec markets.
  • Primary market = new issues (IPO, new bond issue); secondary market = trading of existing securities on exchanges.

Worked examples

Example 1

A 91-day T-bill with face value ₹100 is issued at ₹98.50. What is the approximate annualised yield?

Solution

Gain = 100 − 98.50 = ₹1.50 on ₹98.50 for 91 days. Yield ≈ (1.50 ÷ 98.50) × (365 ÷ 91) × 100 ≈ 6.11% per annum.

Example 2

A large company needs funds for 90 days to manage working capital. Which money market instrument can it issue?

Solution

Commercial paper, an unsecured short-term promissory note that eligible companies can issue for 7 days to one year.

Example 3

A bank borrows from another bank for one day. Which market is this?

Solution

The call money market, which covers overnight borrowing and lending between banks and primary dealers.

Example 4

Is a 10-year government bond a money market or capital market instrument?

Solution

Capital market, because its maturity is more than one year. G-secs of long tenor form a major part of India's debt capital market.

Example 5

A bank wants to raise funds for six months from institutional investors through a negotiable instrument. What can it issue?

Solution

A certificate of deposit. Banks can issue CDs for 7 days to one year; they are negotiable and held in dematerialised form, unlike ordinary fixed deposits.

Practice questions

Question 1

Which tenor is NOT used for Indian treasury bills?

  1. 91 days
  2. 182 days
  3. 270 days
  4. 364 days
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 270 days

Treasury bills in India are issued in 91-day, 182-day and 364-day tenors. A 270-day T-bill is not issued.

Question 2

Which instrument is an unsecured short-term note issued by companies?

  1. Certificate of deposit
  2. Treasury bill
  3. Government security
  4. Commercial paper
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Commercial paper

Commercial paper is an unsecured promissory note issued by eligible companies for 7 days to one year.

Question 3

Borrowing between banks for 2 to 14 days is called:

  1. Notice money
  2. Call money
  3. Term money
  4. Repo money
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Notice money

Notice money covers interbank borrowing for 2 to 14 days. Call money is overnight and term money runs from 15 days to one year.

Question 4

Which regulator primarily oversees India's securities markets such as shares and corporate bonds?

  1. RBI
  2. SEBI
  3. IRDAI
  4. PFRDA
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. SEBI

SEBI regulates securities markets, including equity and corporate bond issuance and trading. The RBI handles money markets and government securities.

Question 5

How are treasury bills issued?

  1. At a premium with coupons
  2. At face value with monthly interest
  3. At a discount to face value
  4. Only in physical form
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. At a discount to face value

Treasury bills are zero-coupon instruments issued below face value and redeemed at face value, so the discount is the investor's return.

FAQs

What is the difference between a CD and a fixed deposit?

Both are bank deposits, but a certificate of deposit is a negotiable, dematerialised instrument that can be traded in the secondary market and is usually issued in large denominations to institutional investors. A fixed deposit is non-negotiable and is typically held by retail customers until maturity or premature withdrawal.

Who can invest in T-bills now?

Banks, financial institutions, companies and individuals can invest. Retail investors can participate through the RBI Retail Direct platform and through their brokers via non-competitive bidding. Check the RBI Retail Direct website for current processes.
