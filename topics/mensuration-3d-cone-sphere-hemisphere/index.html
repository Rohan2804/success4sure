Cone, Sphere and Hemisphere: Volume and Surface Formulas

Curved solids — cones, spheres and hemispheres — complete the 3D mensuration syllabus. SSC CGL asks them almost every shift, and IBPS PO and SBI PO use them in quantity comparison and in "melt and recast" word problems. Because the formulas contain fractions such as 1/3, 2/3 and 4/3, the risk is arithmetic slips rather than conceptual errors, so pick radii that cancel with 7 and 3, and check whether the answer needs curved or total surface.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Cone

For base radius r, height h and slant height l = √(r² + h²): volume = ⅓πr²h, curved surface = πrl and total surface = πr(l + r). A cone has one-third the volume of a cylinder with the same base and height.

V = ⅓πr²h; CSA = πrl

Sphere

Volume = (4/3)πr³ and surface area = 4πr². The surface of a sphere equals the curved surface of a cylinder that just encloses it, which is a neat check.

V = (4/3)πr³; S = 4πr²

Hemisphere

Volume = (2/3)πr³, curved surface = 2πr² and total surface (with the flat circular face) = 3πr². A solid hemisphere needs 3πr²; a hemispherical bowl without a lid uses 2πr² for the inside or outside.

TSA = 3πr²

Melting and recasting

Volume stays the same when a solid is melted into another shape. Equate the two volume formulas and solve for the unknown dimension; π usually cancels. For many small spheres from one large sphere, the count is (R/r)³.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Slant height triples: 3-4-5, 7-24-25, 5-12-13 — r and h are often chosen to make l whole.
  • Cone : hemisphere : cylinder volumes with equal r and h = r are in the ratio 1 : 2 : 3.
  • If a sphere’s radius doubles, surface area becomes 4 times and volume 8 times.
  • When melting, cancel π and common factors before multiplying — the answer is usually an integer.
  • Lead shots of radius 1 cm from a sphere of radius 4 cm: (4/1)³ = 64, since volume scales with the cube of the radius.

Worked examples

Example 1

A cone has radius 7 cm and height 24 cm. Find its slant height, volume and curved surface area.

Solution

l = √(49 + 576) = 25 cm. Volume = ⅓ × 22/7 × 49 × 24 = 1,232 cm³. CSA = 22/7 × 7 × 25 = 550 cm².

Example 2

Find the volume and surface area of a sphere of radius 21 cm.

Solution

Volume = 4/3 × 22/7 × 9,261 = 38,808 cm³. Surface area = 4 × 22/7 × 441 = 5,544 cm².

Example 3

Find the volume and total surface area of a solid hemisphere of radius 7 cm.

Solution

Volume = 2/3 × 22/7 × 343 ≈ 718.67 cm³. TSA = 3 × 22/7 × 49 = 462 cm².

Example 4

A metal sphere of radius 6 cm is melted and recast into a cone of base radius 6 cm. Find the height of the cone.

Solution

Sphere volume = (4/3)π × 216 = 288π. Cone volume = ⅓π × 36 × h = 12πh. So 12h = 288 and h = 24 cm.

Example 5

A conical tent has base radius 7 m and height 24 m. How much canvas is needed, and what is the cost at ₹40 per m²?

Solution

Slant height = 25 m, so canvas = curved surface = 22/7 × 7 × 25 = 550 m². Cost = 550 × 40 = ₹22,000. The floor of a tent is not covered with canvas, so the base area is not added.

Example 6

An ice-cream cone has radius 3.5 cm and height 12 cm and is filled with ice-cream that forms a hemisphere on top. Find the volume of ice-cream.

Solution

Cone part = ⅓ × π × 12.25 × 12 = 49π. Hemisphere = ⅔ × π × 42.875 ≈ 28.58π. Total ≈ 77.58π = 22/7 × 77.58 ≈ 243.83 cm³. Combined-solid questions simply add the volumes of the parts.

Practice questions

Question 1

The curved surface area of a cone with radius 3 cm and height 4 cm is:

  1. 12π cm²
  2. 20π cm²
  3. 24π cm²
  4. 15π cm²
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 15π cm²

Slant height is √(9 + 16) = 5 cm, so the curved surface is π × 3 × 5 = 15π cm².

Question 2

A sphere has surface area 616 cm². Its volume is about:

  1. 1,232 cm³
  2. 1,540 cm³
  3. 1,848 cm³
  4. 1,437.33 cm³
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 1,437.33 cm³

4πr² = 616 gives r² = 49 and r = 7, so volume = 4/3 × 22/7 × 343 ≈ 1,437.33 cm³.

Question 3

The total surface area of a solid hemisphere of radius 14 cm is:

  1. 1,232 cm²
  2. 2,464 cm²
  3. 1,848 cm²
  4. 616 cm²
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 1,848 cm²

TSA = 3πr² = 3 × 22/7 × 196 = 1,848 square centimetres including the flat face.

Question 4

A cone and a cylinder have the same base radius and height. The ratio of their volumes is:

  1. 1 : 2
  2. 1 : 3
  3. 2 : 3
  4. 3 : 1
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 1 : 3

A cone’s volume is one-third of a cylinder with the same base and height, so the ratio is 1 : 3.

Question 5

A sphere of radius 3 cm is melted into a cylinder of radius 2 cm. The height of the cylinder is:

  1. 9 cm
  2. 6 cm
  3. 12 cm
  4. 4.5 cm
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 9 cm

Sphere volume 36π equals cylinder volume 4πh, so the height h is 9 cm.

FAQs

Why does a hemisphere’s total surface area use 3πr²?

The curved part is half the sphere’s surface, 2πr², and the flat circular face adds πr². Together they give 3πr². If the question describes a bowl or a dome open at the base, use only 2πr².

Is slant height always needed for a cone?

Only for surface area. Volume uses the vertical height h. Many questions give the slant height and radius, so you must find h first using h = √(l² − r²) before applying the volume formula.
