Difference Between Compound and Simple Interest Tricks

Questions on the difference between compound and simple interest are a favourite of IBPS PO, SBI Clerk and SSC CGL setters because they test whether you understand why CI grows faster. The difference has neat closed formulas for two and three years, so a question that looks like it needs two full calculations can be finished in one line. Expect one such question in many bank prelims papers, often in quantity comparison form.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Why the gap appears

In the first year, CI and SI are equal. From the second year on, CI also earns interest on the first year’s interest. For two years, the extra is just the interest on year-1 interest, which equals P(R/100)².

Two years: CI − SI = P(R/100)²

Three-year gap

Over three years, the extra includes interest on two years of interest plus interest on interest. The closed form is P(R/100)² × (300 + R)/100. At 10% on ₹8,000 this gives 8,000 × 0.01 × 3.1 = ₹248.

Three years: CI − SI = P(R/100)²(300 + R)/100

Finding the rate from SI and CI

If two-year SI and CI on the same sum are given, the one-year SI is half the two-year SI. The extra (CI − SI) is interest on that one-year SI for one year, so R = (CI − SI) / (SI/2) × 100. Then P follows from the one-year SI.

Using the gap in quantity comparison

Since the two-year gap depends only on P and R², you can compare two scenarios without computing either interest fully. Doubling the rate quadruples the gap, while doubling the principal only doubles it. This makes comparison questions very quick.

Reverse questions

Given the difference, rate and time 2 years, the principal is D ÷ (R/100)². A ₹25 difference at 5% means P = 25 ÷ 0.0025 = ₹10,000. Always check whether the question says 2 or 3 years before choosing the formula.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • At 10%, the two-year difference is 1% of P; at 5% it is 0.25% of P; at 20% it is 4% of P.
  • Three-year difference ÷ two-year difference = (300 + R)/100. At 10% this ratio is 3.1.
  • For half-yearly compounding over 1 year, the gap from SI is P(R/200)².
  • If SI for 2 years is S and CI is C, the rate is 200(C − S)/S percent.

Worked examples

Example 1

Find the difference between CI and SI on ₹15,000 for 2 years at 6% per annum.

Solution

D = 15,000 × (6/100)² = 15,000 × 0.0036 = ₹54. Direct check: SI = ₹1,800 and CI = 15,000 × (1.1236 − 1) = ₹1,854.

Example 2

The difference between CI and SI on a sum for 2 years at 5% per annum is ₹25. Find the sum.

Solution

P = 25 ÷ (0.05)² = 25 ÷ 0.0025 = ₹10,000.

Example 3

Find the difference between CI and SI on ₹8,000 for 3 years at 10% per annum.

Solution

Using the formula: 8,000 × 0.01 × (310/100) = ₹248. Check: CI = 8,000 × 0.331 = ₹2,648 and SI = ₹2,400, difference ₹248.

Example 4

On a certain sum, the SI for 2 years is ₹1,200 and the CI for 2 years is ₹1,290. Find the rate and the sum.

Solution

SI for one year = ₹600. The extra ₹90 is interest on ₹600 for one year, so R = 90/600 × 100 = 15%. Principal = 600 ÷ 0.15 = ₹4,000.

Example 5

The difference between CI and SI on a sum for 3 years at 10% per annum is ₹31. Find the sum.

Solution

Three-year difference = P × 0.01 × 3.1 = 0.031P. So 0.031P = 31 and P = ₹1,000. Check: CI = 1,000 × 0.331 = ₹331 and SI = ₹300.

Example 6

Quantity I: CI − SI on ₹6,400 for 2 years at 12.5%. Quantity II: CI − SI on ₹10,000 for 2 years at 10%. Which is greater?

Solution

Quantity I = 6,400 × (1/8)² = 6,400/64 = ₹100. Quantity II = 10,000 × (1/10)² = ₹100. The two quantities are equal. Such pairs are common in IBPS quantity comparison, so do not assume the larger principal wins.

Practice questions

Question 1

Find the difference between CI and SI on ₹20,000 for 2 years at 4% per annum.

  1. ₹16
  2. ₹40
  3. ₹24
  4. ₹32
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹32

Difference = 20,000 × (0.04)² = 20,000 × 0.0016 = ₹32 for the two-year period.

Question 2

The difference between CI and SI for 2 years at 8% per annum is ₹48. The principal is:

  1. ₹6,000
  2. ₹7,500
  3. ₹8,000
  4. ₹7,200
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹7,500

P = 48 ÷ (0.08)² = 48 ÷ 0.0064 = ₹7,500 using the two-year difference formula.

Question 3

Find the difference between CI and SI on ₹5,000 for 3 years at 10% per annum.

  1. ₹150
  2. ₹50
  3. ₹165
  4. ₹155
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹155

Difference = 5,000 × 0.01 × 3.1 = ₹155; CI is ₹1,655 and SI is ₹1,500.

Question 4

The SI on a sum for 2 years is ₹800 and the CI is ₹840. The rate of interest is:

  1. 5%
  2. 10%
  3. 8%
  4. 12%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 10%

One-year SI is ₹400, and the extra ₹40 is interest on ₹400 for a year, so the rate is 10 percent.

Question 5

Find the difference between CI and SI on ₹12,500 for 2 years at 12% per annum.

  1. ₹150
  2. ₹120
  3. ₹180
  4. ₹200
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹180

Difference = 12,500 × (0.12)² = 12,500 × 0.0144 = ₹180 for two years.

FAQs

Does the CI − SI formula work for half-yearly compounding?

Yes, with adjustment. Replace R by the rate per period and n by the number of periods. For one year at 10% compounded half-yearly, the difference from simple interest is P × (0.05)². Simple interest is still calculated at the annual rate for the full year.

Is the first-year interest always equal under CI and SI?

Yes, when compounding is annual. Both methods charge R% on the same principal in year one. The difference starts from the second year. With half-yearly or quarterly compounding, CI pulls ahead within the first year itself.
