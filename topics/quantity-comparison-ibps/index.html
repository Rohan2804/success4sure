Quantity Comparison Questions: Strategy for IBPS and SBI

Quantity comparison is a newer question format in IBPS PO, IBPS Clerk, SBI PO and RBI Assistant prelims, often appearing as a block of three to five questions. Each question gives two quantities from different arithmetic topics — interest, speed, probability, mensuration or equations — and asks which is larger. It tests breadth across the syllabus and the judgement to stop calculating as soon as the comparison is clear.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Answer codes

The options are usually: Quantity I > Quantity II, Quantity I < Quantity II, Quantity I ≥ Quantity II, Quantity I ≤ Quantity II, and Quantity I = Quantity II or no relation can be established. Read the option list once at the start of the block; it stays the same for every question.

Evaluate each side independently

The two quantities come from unrelated mini-problems. Solve each separately, write the result, then compare. Do not carry assumptions from one side to the other, even when both mention the same numbers.

Watch for multiple values

Quadratics, absolute values and "x² = k" produce two values. Compare every value on one side with every value on the other, exactly as in quadratic-equation questions. Equal values in some pairs lead to ≥ or ≤ instead of > or <.

Stop when the order is clear

You do not always need exact answers. If Quantity I is clearly above 2,000 and Quantity II clearly below 1,800, the comparison is settled. Approximation and bounds save a lot of time in this block.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Simple interest over 3 years at 10% (30%) always beats compound interest over 2 years at 10% (21%) on the same principal.
  • Convert both sides to the same unit — km/h vs m/s is a classic trap.
  • Probabilities: compare fractions by cross-multiplying instead of converting to decimals.
  • If one side has a range of possible values (from a word problem with missing data), the answer is often "no relation".
  • Spend under a minute per pair; skip pairs needing long work on both sides.

Worked examples

Example 1

Quantity I: SI on ₹8,000 at 10% p.a. for 3 years. Quantity II: CI on ₹8,000 at 10% p.a. for 2 years.

Solution

I = 8,000 × 30% = ₹2,400. II = 8,000 × 0.21 = ₹1,680. Quantity I > Quantity II.

Example 2

Quantity I: x, where x² = 49. Quantity II: 7.

Solution

x = 7 or −7. Comparing with 7: equal in one case, smaller in the other. Quantity I ≤ Quantity II.

Example 3

Quantity I: speed (km/h) of a 150 m train that passes a pole in 10 seconds. Quantity II: 50 km/h.

Solution

I = 15 m/s = 54 km/h. 54 > 50, so Quantity I > Quantity II.

Example 4

Quantity I: probability of two heads when two coins are tossed. Quantity II: probability of a sum of 7 with two dice.

Solution

I: the four outcomes are HH, HT, TH, TT, so P = 1/4. II: sum 7 comes from six pairs out of 36, so P = 1/6. Cross-multiplying 1 × 6 > 1 × 4 shows 1/4 > 1/6, so Quantity I > Quantity II.

Example 5

Quantity I: days taken by A (12 days) and B (18 days) together. Quantity II: 7 days.

Solution

I = 36/5 = 7.2 days > 7, so Quantity I > Quantity II.

Example 6

Quantity I: ways to arrange 4 people in a row. Quantity II: ways to choose 3 people from 8.

Solution

I = 4! = 24. II = 8C3 = 56. Quantity I < Quantity II.

Practice questions

Question 1

Quantity I: 30% of 450. Quantity II: 45% of 300. Which is true?

  1. Quantity I > Quantity II
  2. Quantity I < Quantity II
  3. Quantity I = Quantity II
  4. No relation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Quantity I = Quantity II

Both quantities equal 135: 0.30 × 450 = 135 and 0.45 × 300 = 135, so they are equal.

Question 2

Quantity I: x, where x² − 5x + 6 = 0. Quantity II: 3. Which is true?

  1. Quantity I < Quantity II
  2. Quantity I ≤ Quantity II
  3. Quantity I ≥ Quantity II
  4. Quantity I = Quantity II
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Quantity I ≤ Quantity II

x = 2 or 3, so x is either smaller than or equal to 3, giving Quantity I ≤ Quantity II.

Question 3

Quantity I: CI − SI on ₹10,000 at 10% for 2 years. Quantity II: ₹90. Which is true?

  1. Quantity I > Quantity II
  2. Quantity I < Quantity II
  3. Quantity I = Quantity II
  4. Quantity I ≤ Quantity II
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Quantity I > Quantity II

The two-year difference is 10,000 × (0.1)² = ₹100, which is more than ₹90.

Question 4

Quantity I: average of the first 10 natural numbers. Quantity II: median of 2, 4, 6, 8. Which is true?

  1. Quantity I < Quantity II
  2. Quantity I > Quantity II
  3. Quantity I = Quantity II
  4. No relation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Quantity I > Quantity II

Quantity I is 5.5 and Quantity II is (4 + 6)/2 = 5, so Quantity I is larger.

Question 5

Quantity I: speed of the stream if downstream speed is 20 km/h and upstream 12 km/h. Quantity II: 16 km/h. Which is true?

  1. Quantity I > Quantity II
  2. Quantity I = Quantity II
  3. Quantity I ≥ Quantity II
  4. Quantity I < Quantity II
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Quantity I < Quantity II

Stream speed = (20 − 12)/2 = 4 km/h, which is less than 16 km/h (16 km/h is the boat’s speed).

FAQs

Which topics appear most in quantity comparison?

Interest, profit and loss, speed and distance, boats, probability, permutation, mensuration and quadratic equations are the usual sources. Essentially any arithmetic topic can be used, so broad coverage matters more than depth in one chapter for this block.

How do I handle a quantity that depends on unknown data?

If a word problem leaves a value undetermined, list the possible values or a range. If the range overlaps the other quantity, the answer is that no relation can be established. Setters include such questions deliberately to test this judgement.
