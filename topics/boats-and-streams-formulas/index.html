Boats and Streams: Upstream and Downstream Formulas

Boats and streams questions appear in nearly every IBPS PO, SBI PO and SBI Clerk prelims paper, often as a quantity comparison or a two-part word problem. SSC CGL also asks one regularly. The setting is simple — a boat or swimmer moving with or against a river current — and all questions rest on two speeds: downstream and upstream. Once those are written down, most problems become simple speed–time calculations.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Downstream and upstream speeds

If a boat’s speed in still water is b and the stream’s speed is s, then downstream speed D = b + s and upstream speed U = b − s. The current helps going down and opposes going up.

D = b + s; U = b − s

Recovering b and s

Adding and subtracting the two equations gives b = (D + U)/2 and s = (D − U)/2. If a boat covers 30 km downstream in 2 hours (15 km/h) and 18 km upstream in 3 hours (6 km/h), then b = 10.5 km/h and s = 4.5 km/h.

b = (D + U)/2; s = (D − U)/2

Round trips

For a trip of d km downstream and back, total time = d/(b + s) + d/(b − s). Because more time is spent going slowly upstream, the average speed for the round trip is less than b.

Time-ratio questions

If going upstream takes k times as long as going downstream over the same distance, then (b + s) = k(b − s), so b : s = (k + 1) : (k − 1).

b : s = (k + 1) : (k − 1)

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Write D and U first — every question becomes clear once both are known.
  • If upstream time is twice the downstream time, b : s = 3 : 1; if three times, 2 : 1.
  • Round-trip average speed = (b + s)(b − s)/b.
  • A floating object moves at the stream’s speed; a man rowing in still water moves at b.

Worked examples

Example 1

A boat’s speed in still water is 12 km/h and the stream flows at 3 km/h. How long does it take to go 45 km downstream and to come back?

Solution

D = 15 km/h, U = 9 km/h. Downstream 45 ÷ 15 = 3 hours; upstream 45 ÷ 9 = 5 hours.

Example 2

A boat covers 30 km downstream in 2 hours and 18 km upstream in 3 hours. Find the speed of the boat and of the stream.

Solution

D = 15 km/h and U = 6 km/h. b = (15 + 6)/2 = 10.5 km/h and s = (15 − 6)/2 = 4.5 km/h.

Example 3

A boat at 10 km/h in still water goes some distance downstream and returns in 5 hours in all. The stream speed is 2 km/h. Find the one-way distance.

Solution

D = 12 and U = 8 km/h. d/12 + d/8 = 5, so 5d/24 = 5 and d = 24 km.

Example 4

A man takes twice as long to row upstream as to row the same distance downstream. Find the ratio of the boat’s speed to the stream’s speed.

Solution

(b + s) = 2(b − s) gives b = 3s, so b : s = 3 : 1.

Example 5

A swimmer covers 4 km against the current in 1 hour and the same 4 km with the current in 40 minutes. Find her speed in still water and the speed of the current.

Solution

Upstream speed U = 4 km/h. Downstream speed D = 4 ÷ (40/60) = 6 km/h. So b = (6 + 4)/2 = 5 km/h and s = (6 − 4)/2 = 1 km/h. Converting 40 minutes into 2/3 hour before dividing is the step most students rush.

Example 6

A ferry goes 40 km downstream in 4 hours and returns 24 km upstream in the same time. How long would it take to cover 32 km in still water?

Solution

D = 40/4 = 10 km/h and U = 24/4 = 6 km/h, so b = 8 km/h. In still water 32 km takes 32 ÷ 8 = 4 hours. The stream speed of 2 km/h is not needed for this part, though setters often ask it as a follow-up.

Practice questions

Question 1

A boat’s speed in still water is 15 km/h and the stream flows at 3 km/h. Time to travel 72 km downstream is:

  1. 5 hours
  2. 6 hours
  3. 4.8 hours
  4. 4 hours
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 4 hours

Downstream speed is 15 + 3 = 18 km/h, and 72 ÷ 18 = 4 hours for the trip.

Question 2

A boat’s downstream speed is 16 km/h and upstream speed is 10 km/h. The speed of the stream is:

  1. 6 km/h
  2. 3 km/h
  3. 13 km/h
  4. 2 km/h
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 3 km/h

Stream speed = (D − U)/2 = (16 − 10)/2 = 3 km/h, half the gap between the two speeds.

Question 3

A boat runs at 9 km/h in still water; the stream flows at 3 km/h. A round trip takes 6 hours. The one-way distance is:

  1. 18 km
  2. 24 km
  3. 27 km
  4. 30 km
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 24 km

D = 12 and U = 6, so d/12 + d/6 = d/4 = 6 hours, giving a one-way distance of 24 km.

Question 4

Rowing upstream takes three times as long as rowing the same distance downstream. The ratio of boat speed to stream speed is:

  1. 2 : 1
  2. 3 : 1
  3. 3 : 2
  4. 4 : 1
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 2 : 1

b + s = 3(b − s) gives 2b = 4s, so b = 2s and the ratio is 2 : 1.

Question 5

A man rows at 5 km/h in still water and takes twice as long to go upstream as downstream. The stream’s speed is:

  1. 2 km/h
  2. 5/2 km/h
  3. 1 km/h
  4. 5/3 km/h
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 5/3 km/h

From 5 + s = 2(5 − s), we get 3s = 5, so the stream flows at 5/3 km/h.

FAQs

Why is the round-trip time not equal to the time in still water?

Because the boat spends more time going upstream at the slower speed than it saves going downstream. The losses and gains in speed are equal, but the time effect is not. So any current makes a round trip longer than the same trip in still water.

Do boats-and-streams questions appear in quantity comparison?

Yes, frequently in IBPS PO and SBI PO. A typical pair asks for the stream speed in one quantity and the time for a trip in the other. Writing D and U first lets you evaluate both quantities quickly and compare them.
