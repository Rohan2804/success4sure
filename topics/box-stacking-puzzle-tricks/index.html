Box and Stacking Puzzles: Method, Cases and Practice

Box puzzles are a variant of floor puzzles in which boxes are stacked one above another, often with an extra attribute like colour or number of items. They are common in IBPS PO, SBI PO and RBI Grade B prelims and mains, usually as a five-question set. The trickiest part is that clues are often tied to boxes that are themselves not yet placed, so neat case-making matters even more than in floor puzzles.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Stack orientation

Unless stated otherwise, the bottom box is position 1. Draw the stack vertically with position 1 at the bottom. Some questions number from the top; read the first line before you draw.

Boxes between

'Two boxes are kept between P and T' means their positions differ by three, without telling you which is higher. That creates two cases (P lower or T lower). Write both and keep them until a clue removes one.

Unknown-count stacks

Harder puzzles do not state how many boxes there are: 'Three boxes are kept between A and B, and A is the fourth box from the bottom.' Here you must work out the total from the clues. Keep the stack open at the top until all clues are used.

Attribute clues

If boxes have colours or contain a number of items, make a second column. Clues like 'the green box is kept immediately below the box with 12 items' connect columns; apply them only once one side is fixed.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Place the box tied to the top or bottom first; it is the only true anchor in a stack.
  • 'Immediately above' links two boxes as a single block; move the block together while testing cases.
  • When two cases survive, the question set usually contains a clue (often negative) that only one case passes — look for 'not' statements.
  • For unknown-count stacks, the answer to 'how many boxes are there' is fixed by the highest-placed box in the final case.
  • Count 'boxes between' as difference of positions minus one.

Worked examples

Example 1

Six boxes P, Q, R, S, T and U are kept one above another (position 1 is the bottom). Two boxes are kept between P and T. S is kept immediately above T. R is kept at the bottom. Q is kept above U but below T. Find the order.

Solution

R = 1. Q is below T and U is below Q, so at least R, U and Q sit under T, which makes T at least 4. S is directly above T, so T is 4 or 5. If T = 4, P would need position 1 or 7 to have two boxes between — both impossible. So T = 5, S = 6 and P = 2. U and Q take positions 3 and 4 with U below Q. Bottom to top: R, P, U, Q, T, S.

Example 2

In the stack above, how many boxes are kept between R and Q, and which box is immediately below T?

Solution

R is at 1 and Q at 4, so 4 − 1 − 1 = 2 boxes (P and U) are between them. Immediately below T (position 5) is Q (position 4).

Example 3

Six boxes G, H, J, K, L and M are stacked with position 1 at the bottom. K is third from the bottom. Only one box is kept between K and M. G is kept immediately above M. H is kept below K but not at the bottom. J is kept above L. Find the stack.

Solution

K = 3. One box between K and M puts M at 1 or 5. If M = 1, G = 2, and H (below K, not at the bottom) would need position 2 as well — a clash. So M = 5 and G = 6. H is below K and not at the bottom, so H = 2. Positions 1 and 4 remain; J must be above L, so L = 1 and J = 4. Bottom to top: L, H, K, J, M, G.

Practice questions

Question 1

Five boxes A, B, C, D and E are stacked, position 1 at the bottom. C is at the top. A is kept two positions below C. E is kept below A. B is not at the bottom. D is kept above A. Which box is at the bottom?

  1. E
  2. B
  3. D
  4. A
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. E

C = 5 and A = 3. D is above A, so D = 4. E is below A and B is not at the bottom, so E = 1 and B = 2.

Question 2

In a stack of six boxes, Q is at position 4 and T is at position 5 from the bottom. How many boxes are kept between Q and T?

  1. One
  2. None
  3. Two
  4. Three
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. None

Positions 4 and 5 are adjacent, so Q is immediately below T and no box lies between them.

Question 3

'Two boxes are kept between X and Y' in a stack where position 1 is the bottom. If X is at position 2, where is Y?

  1. Position 4
  2. Position 3
  3. Position 5
  4. Position 6
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Position 5

Positions must differ by three. Counting down from 2 gives −1, which does not exist, so Y must be at position 2 + 3 = 5.

Question 4

Six boxes are stacked with R at the bottom, then P, U, Q, T and S in order upward. Which box is kept immediately above U?

  1. T
  2. P
  3. S
  4. Q
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Q

Going upward the order is R, P, U, Q, T, S. The box directly above U, which is in position 3, is Q in position 4.

Question 5

In a stack, the box with 12 items is kept immediately below the green box, and the green box is at position 4. Where is the box with 12 items?

  1. Position 3
  2. Position 5
  3. Position 2
  4. Cannot be determined
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Position 3

'Immediately below' means the very next position downward. With the green box at position 4, the box with 12 items must be at position 3.

FAQs

How are box puzzles different from floor puzzles?

The logic is the same vertical ordering, but box puzzles more often hide the total number of boxes and attach attributes like colour or item count to positions. That means more case-making and more cross-column clues. If you are strong at floor puzzles, box puzzles need only a few extra practice sets.

What should I do if both cases survive till the end?

Re-read the clues; usually one clue was applied loosely, often a 'not' clue or 'immediately'. If both cases genuinely satisfy every clue, check whether the question you are answering has the same answer in both. Exam setters ensure the asked questions are answerable.
