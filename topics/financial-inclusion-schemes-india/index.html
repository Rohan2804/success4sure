Financial Inclusion Schemes: PMJDY, PMJJBY, PMSBY, APY

Government financial inclusion schemes are a favourite in RBI Grade B, IBPS and SBI mains, JAIIB, and bank interviews, where candidates are often asked to explain a scheme to a rural customer. The launch years, target groups and basic structure of these schemes are stable, but premiums, cover amounts and limits are revised from time to time. Learn the structure from this page and confirm the current figures on the official scheme or Department of Financial Services websites before your exam.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (PMJDY)

Launched in August 2014 to give every household access to a basic bank account. Accounts can be opened with zero balance, come with a RuPay debit card with accident insurance cover, and are eligible for an overdraft facility subject to conditions. PMJDY accounts are widely used for direct benefit transfers.

PMJJBY and PMSBY

Both launched in 2015 and run through banks with auto-debit. Pradhan Mantri Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana is a one-year renewable life insurance cover of ₹2 lakh for death from any cause, for account holders in a specified age band. Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana is a one-year accident cover of ₹2 lakh for accidental death or total disability (₹1 lakh for partial disability). Premiums were revised in 2022; check the current figures.

Atal Pension Yojana (APY)

Launched in 2015 for workers mainly in the unorganised sector, administered by PFRDA. Subscribers join between 18 and 40 years of age and choose a guaranteed monthly pension of ₹1,000 to ₹5,000 from age 60, with contributions depending on age at entry and the pension chosen. Income-tax payers have been excluded from joining since October 2022.

Pradhan Mantri Mudra Yojana (PMMY)

Launched in 2015 for non-farm micro and small enterprises, offering collateral-free loans through banks, NBFCs and MFIs in categories: Shishu (smallest loans), Kishore and Tarun. The upper limits of the categories have been revised over time, including a new Tarun Plus category; verify current limits from the Mudra website.

Stand-Up India

Launched in 2016 to support at least one SC or ST borrower and at least one woman borrower per bank branch in setting up a greenfield enterprise, with loans between ₹10 lakh and ₹1 crore.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • 2014: PMJDY. 2015: PMJJBY, PMSBY, APY, Mudra. 2016: Stand-Up India.
  • PMJJBY = life cover (any cause); PMSBY = accident cover only.
  • APY is administered by PFRDA; pension starts at 60.
  • Mudra loan categories in increasing size: Shishu → Kishore → Tarun.
  • Stand-Up India targets SC/ST and women entrepreneurs for greenfield projects.
  • For premiums, limits and age bands, always confirm the latest official figures.

Worked examples

Example 1

A 30-year-old customer asks which scheme covers death due to illness. What would you suggest?

Solution

PMSBY covers only accidents. For death from any cause, including illness, PMJJBY is the relevant scheme, as it provides ₹2 lakh life cover. The customer can enrol through their bank with auto-debit of the annual premium.

Example 2

A street vendor needs a small collateral-free loan to buy a cart. Which scheme fits?

Solution

The Shishu category of the Pradhan Mantri Mudra Yojana is meant for the smallest loans to micro-enterprises and does not require collateral. (Vendors may also qualify for PM SVANidhi, a separate scheme for street vendors.)

Example 3

A 45-year-old wants to join Atal Pension Yojana. Can she?

Solution

No. APY entry is allowed only between 18 and 40 years of age. She could consider other options such as the National Pension System.

Example 4

Interview question: 'How has PMJDY helped direct benefit transfer?'

Solution

PMJDY gave crores of people a bank account, and linking these accounts with Aadhaar and mobile numbers allowed subsidies and benefits to be credited directly. This reduced leakages and middlemen, though challenges such as dormant accounts and last-mile access remain.

Practice questions

Question 1

In which year was Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana launched?

  1. 2014
  2. 2015
  3. 2016
  4. 2012
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 2014

PMJDY was launched in August 2014 as a national mission to provide universal access to basic bank accounts.

Question 2

Which scheme provides accident insurance cover through banks?

  1. PMJJBY
  2. PMSBY
  3. APY
  4. PMMY
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. PMSBY

Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana is an accident insurance scheme. PMJJBY provides life cover for death from any cause.

Question 3

Which body administers Atal Pension Yojana?

  1. SEBI
  2. IRDAI
  3. PFRDA
  4. NABARD
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. PFRDA

Atal Pension Yojana is administered by the Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority, which also regulates the National Pension System.

Question 4

What is the order of Mudra loan categories from smallest to largest?

  1. Tarun, Kishore, Shishu
  2. Kishore, Shishu, Tarun
  3. Shishu, Tarun, Kishore
  4. Shishu, Kishore, Tarun
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Shishu, Kishore, Tarun

Mudra categories grow with the stage of business: Shishu for the smallest loans, then Kishore, then Tarun for larger loans.

Question 5

Stand-Up India supports which groups of entrepreneurs?

  1. SC/ST and women borrowers
  2. Only exporters
  3. Only farmers
  4. Only startups in IT
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. SC/ST and women borrowers

Stand-Up India aims to finance at least one SC or ST borrower and one woman borrower per bank branch for greenfield enterprises.

FAQs

Why do premiums of PMJJBY and PMSBY differ in older books?

Premiums were revised in 2022 after years at the original level, so older material shows outdated amounts. Cover amounts and age limits may also be revised. Always confirm the figures on the official Jan Suraksha or Department of Financial Services website before an exam or interview.

Are financial inclusion schemes asked in prelims?

Bank prelims usually do not have a General Awareness section, so these questions appear mainly in mains papers, RBI Grade B Phase I, JAIIB and CAIIB, and interviews. SSC General Awareness also asks scheme launch years and objectives occasionally.
