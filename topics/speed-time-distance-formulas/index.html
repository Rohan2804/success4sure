Speed, Time and Distance Formulas and Shortcut Tricks

Speed, time and distance (STD) is the parent chapter for trains, boats and races, so mastering it pays off several times over. IBPS PO, SBI PO and RBI Grade B include one or two STD-family questions in prelims, SSC CGL includes two or three, and CAT tests the ideas in tougher multi-stage problems. Most questions need just three tools: the D = S × T relation, inverse proportion between speed and time, and relative speed.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The core relation

Distance = Speed × Time. Keep units consistent: convert km/h to m/s by multiplying by 5/18 and m/s to km/h by multiplying by 18/5. So 72 km/h = 20 m/s and 15 m/s = 54 km/h. Minutes must be converted to hours (15 minutes = 1/4 hour) when speed is in km/h.

D = S × T; km/h × 5/18 = m/s

Speed and time are inversely proportional

For a fixed distance, if speed becomes a/b of the usual, time becomes b/a of the usual. If a person walks at 3/4 of usual speed and is 20 minutes late, the time becomes 4/3 of usual, so the extra 1/3 equals 20 minutes and the usual time is 60 minutes.

Relative speed

Two objects moving towards each other close the gap at the sum of their speeds; moving in the same direction, at the difference. Cars starting 200 km apart at 45 and 55 km/h towards each other meet after 200 ÷ 100 = 2 hours.

Opposite: S1 + S2; Same: S1 − S2

Average speed

Average speed = total distance ÷ total time. For equal distances at speeds x, y and z, it is the harmonic mean 3/(1/x + 1/y + 1/z). For two equal distances it reduces to 2xy/(x + y).

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Late-and-early problems: distance = (product of speeds ÷ difference of speeds) × total time difference.
  • Remember conversions: 18 km/h = 5 m/s, 36 = 10, 54 = 15, 72 = 20, 90 = 25, 108 = 30.
  • If two people start together and one reaches t hours early, compare their times, not speeds.
  • For meeting problems, the ratio of distances covered equals the ratio of speeds.
  • Average speed is always closer to the lower speed when distances are equal.

Worked examples

Example 1

A man walking at 5 km/h reaches his office 10 minutes late. Walking at 6 km/h, he reaches 5 minutes early. Find the distance.

Solution

The time difference is 15 minutes = 1/4 hour. d/5 − d/6 = 1/4 gives d/30 = 1/4, so d = 7.5 km. Shortcut: (5 × 6 ÷ 1) × 1/4 = 7.5 km.

Example 2

Delhi and Agra are about 200 km apart. Two cars leave the two cities towards each other at 45 km/h and 55 km/h. When do they meet?

Solution

Relative speed = 100 km/h, so they meet after 2 hours. The first car covers 90 km and the second 110 km.

Example 3

Walking at 3/4 of his usual speed, a man is 20 minutes late. What is his usual time?

Solution

New time = 4/3 of usual, so the extra 1/3 of usual time = 20 minutes. Usual time = 60 minutes.

Example 4

A cyclist covers three equal stretches at 30, 40 and 60 km/h. Find the average speed.

Solution

Take each stretch as 120 km (LCM). Times are 4, 3 and 2 hours. Total 360 km in 9 hours gives 40 km/h.

Example 5

Asha leaves home on a scooter at 10 km/h. Two hours later her brother follows on a bike at 15 km/h along the same road. When and where does he catch her?

Solution

In 2 hours Asha has a 20 km head start. The gap closes at the relative speed 15 − 10 = 5 km/h, so it takes 20 ÷ 5 = 4 hours after the brother starts. He catches her 15 × 4 = 60 km from home. Check: Asha has ridden 6 hours × 10 km/h = 60 km.

Practice questions

Question 1

A speed of 54 km/h equals:

  1. 12 m/s
  2. 15 m/s
  3. 18 m/s
  4. 13.5 m/s
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 15 m/s

Multiply by 5/18: 54 × 5/18 = 15 metres per second, a standard conversion worth memorising.

Question 2

A bus covers 360 km in 4 hours 30 minutes. Its average speed is:

  1. 75 km/h
  2. 90 km/h
  3. 72 km/h
  4. 80 km/h
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 80 km/h

Speed = 360 ÷ 4.5 = 80 km/h, after converting 30 minutes to half an hour.

Question 3

Walking at 4 km/h a student is 10 minutes late; at 5 km/h he is 5 minutes early. The distance is:

  1. 4 km
  2. 6 km
  3. 5 km
  4. 7.5 km
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 5 km

d/4 − d/5 = 15/60 hour, so d/20 = 1/4 and the distance is 5 km.

Question 4

Two cars start 300 km apart and drive towards each other at 70 km/h and 80 km/h. They meet after:

  1. 2 hours
  2. 2.5 hours
  3. 1.5 hours
  4. 3 hours
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 2 hours

Relative speed is 70 + 80 = 150 km/h, so the gap of 300 km closes in 2 hours.

Question 5

Travelling at 4/5 of his usual speed, a man is 15 minutes late. His usual time is:

  1. 45 minutes
  2. 60 minutes
  3. 75 minutes
  4. 50 minutes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 60 minutes

Time becomes 5/4 of usual, so the extra 1/4 equals 15 minutes and the usual time is 60 minutes.

FAQs

Should I always convert to m/s?

Only when lengths are in metres and time in seconds, as in train problems. If distances are in km and times in hours, stay in km/h. Converting unnecessarily adds steps and errors. Choose the unit system of the answer options.

What is the quickest way to solve meeting-point questions?

Use relative speed for the meeting time, then multiply each speed by that time for the distances. Alternatively, split the total distance in the ratio of speeds. Both methods avoid setting up two equations.
