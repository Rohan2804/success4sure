Triangle Geometry: Angles, Similarity and Centres

Geometry carries significant weight in SSC CGL (four to six questions) and CHSL, and triangles are its biggest sub-topic. CAT and CMAT also test triangle properties inside harder problems. The questions reward knowing a set of facts — angle sum, similarity ratios, properties of the centroid and incentre — and applying them quickly, rather than long constructions or proofs.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Angles

The interior angles of a triangle add to 180°. An exterior angle equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles. The largest side is opposite the largest angle. If the angles are in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4, they are 40°, 60° and 80°.

Exterior angle = sum of opposite interior angles

Pythagoras and triangle types

In a right triangle, hypotenuse² = sum of the squares of the other sides. For any triangle with sides a ≤ b ≤ c: c² = a² + b² means right-angled, c² < a² + b² acute, c² > a² + b² obtuse. Each side must be less than the sum of the other two.

Similarity

Similar triangles have equal angles and proportional sides. The ratio of their areas is the square of the ratio of corresponding sides, and so is the ratio of the squares of their altitudes or medians. Sides 3 : 5 give areas 9 : 25.

Area ratio = (side ratio)²

Centres

The centroid (meeting point of medians) divides each median in the ratio 2 : 1 from the vertex. The incentre (meeting point of angle bisectors) satisfies ∠BIC = 90° + A/2. The circumcentre is equidistant from the vertices; in a right triangle it is the mid-point of the hypotenuse.

∠BIC = 90° + ∠A/2

Angle bisector and mid-point theorems

The bisector of angle A divides BC in the ratio AB : AC. The segment joining the mid-points of two sides is parallel to the third side and half its length.

BD/DC = AB/AC

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Equilateral triangle of side a: height (√3/2)a, area (√3/4)a², inradius a/(2√3), circumradius a/√3.
  • In a right triangle, the median to the hypotenuse equals half the hypotenuse.
  • Area of a triangle formed by joining mid-points is 1/4 of the original.
  • Medians divide a triangle into six triangles of equal area.

Worked examples

Example 1

The angles of a triangle are in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4. Find them.

Solution

Total parts = 2 + 3 + 4 = 9, and these share 180°, so one part = 20°. Angles: 40°, 60°, 80°. All are less than 90°, so the triangle is acute-angled.

Example 2

An exterior angle of a triangle is 110° and one opposite interior angle is 45°. Find the other opposite interior angle.

Solution

Exterior angle = sum of opposite interior angles, so the other is 110° − 45° = 65°.

Example 3

Two similar triangles have corresponding sides in the ratio 3 : 5. Find the ratio of their areas.

Solution

Area ratio = 3² : 5² = 9 : 25.

Example 4

In triangle ABC, median AD = 12 cm and G is the centroid. Find AG.

Solution

The centroid divides the median 2 : 1 from the vertex, so AG = 2/3 × 12 = 8 cm and GD = 4 cm.

Example 5

In triangle ABC, AB = 6 cm, AC = 9 cm and BC = 10 cm. The bisector of angle A meets BC at D. Find BD.

Solution

By the angle bisector theorem, BD : DC = AB : AC = 6 : 9 = 2 : 3. BD = 10 × 2/5 = 4 cm and DC = 6 cm. A quick check: 4 + 6 = 10, the full length of BC.

Practice questions

Question 1

The angles of a triangle are in the ratio 1 : 2 : 3. The largest angle is:

  1. 90°
  2. 60°
  3. 120°
  4. 100°
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 90°

Six parts make 180°, so one part is 30° and the largest angle is 3 × 30° = 90°.

Question 2

A right triangle has legs 9 cm and 12 cm. Its hypotenuse is:

  1. 13 cm
  2. 15 cm
  3. 16 cm
  4. 21 cm
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 15 cm

Hypotenuse = √(81 + 144) = √225 = 15 cm, a multiple of the 3-4-5 triple.

Question 3

The areas of two similar triangles are in the ratio 16 : 49. Their corresponding sides are in the ratio:

  1. 16 : 49
  2. 8 : 7
  3. 4 : 7
  4. 2 : 7
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 4 : 7

Sides are in the square-root ratio of areas, so √16 : √49 = 4 : 7.

Question 4

The height of an equilateral triangle with side 6√3 cm is:

  1. 9 cm
  2. 6 cm
  3. 3√3 cm
  4. 12 cm
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 9 cm

Height = (√3/2) × 6√3 = 3 × 3 = 9 cm, using the standard equilateral-triangle formula.

Question 5

In triangle ABC, ∠A = 60° and I is the incentre. ∠BIC equals:

  1. 90°
  2. 150°
  3. 60°
  4. 120°
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 120°

∠BIC = 90° + A/2 = 90° + 30° = 120°, from the incentre angle property.

FAQs

Which triangle centres are most important for SSC?

The centroid (2 : 1 median ratio), the incentre (∠BIC = 90° + A/2) and the circumcentre (∠BOC = 2A for an acute triangle) cover most questions. The orthocentre property ∠BHC = 180° − A also appears occasionally. Learn all four angle results together.

How do I quickly check whether three lengths form a triangle?

Check that the sum of the two smaller lengths exceeds the largest. If it equals or is less than the largest, no triangle is possible. To classify the triangle, compare the square of the largest side with the sum of squares of the others.
