Idioms and Phrases for Exams: Meanings and Smart Guessing

Idioms and phrases are expressions whose meaning is different from the literal meaning of their words. SSC CGL, CHSL and MTS ask two or more idiom questions in most papers, and bank exams use them inside fillers, phrase replacement and reading comprehension. A few hundred idioms come up again and again, so steady learning pays off. For unfamiliar idioms, the sentence context and the image behind the phrase usually lead to the right meaning.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Literal versus figurative

'Spill the beans' is not about beans; it means revealing a secret. Options often include a literal reading as a trap. If an option describes the literal action, it is almost never the answer.

Common exam idioms

'A blessing in disguise' (a hidden advantage), 'bite the bullet' (face something unpleasant bravely), 'burn the midnight oil' (work late into the night), 'at the eleventh hour' (at the last moment), 'hit the nail on the head' (describe exactly), 'once in a blue moon' (rarely), 'a piece of cake' (very easy).

Phrasal verbs

Verb-plus-particle combinations are also tested: 'call off' (cancel), 'put off' (postpone), 'look into' (investigate), 'carry out' (perform), 'turn down' (reject), 'bring up' (raise a topic or a child), 'give in' (surrender).

Guessing from imagery

Picture the idiom. 'To be in the same boat' suggests sharing the same situation. 'To add fuel to the fire' suggests making things worse. The image usually points to the meaning even if you have not seen the idiom before.

Use the sentence

When the idiom is in a sentence, the rest of the sentence tests the meaning. 'The match was called off because of rain' tells you 'called off' means cancelled.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Eliminate literal-meaning options first.
  • Group idioms by theme: effort (burn the midnight oil, go the extra mile), time (at the eleventh hour, in the nick of time), honesty (above board, cut corners).
  • 'Put off' and 'call off' are often confused: put off is postpone, call off is cancel.
  • Learn 10 idioms a day with one sentence each; revise every weekend.
  • Indian contexts help memory: 'burning the midnight oil before exams', 'at the eleventh hour before the ITR deadline'.
  • For phrasal verbs, the particle often hints at direction: 'turn down' (refuse, lower), 'take up' (start).

Worked examples

Example 1

Meaning of 'at the eleventh hour': (a) at 11 o'clock (b) at the last moment (c) very early (d) after a long delay.

Solution

The idiom means doing something at the latest possible time. (a) is the literal trap. Answer: (b).

Example 2

'Many candidates burn the midnight oil before the exam.' What does the idiom mean?

Solution

It means studying or working late into the night. The image of an oil lamp burning at midnight suggests late-night work. Answer: work late into the night.

Example 3

'Losing that job turned out to be a blessing in disguise.' What does it mean?

Solution

Something that seemed bad but turned out to be good. The sentence shows the job loss led to something better later. Answer: a hidden advantage.

Example 4

Choose the correct phrasal verb: 'The board decided to ______ the meeting until next week.' Options: call off, put off, turn down, give in.

Solution

'Until next week' shows the meeting is postponed, not cancelled. 'Put off' means postpone. Answer: put off.

Example 5

Meaning of 'to add fuel to the fire': (a) to cook food (b) to make a bad situation worse (c) to solve a problem (d) to save energy.

Solution

Adding fuel makes a fire bigger, so the idiom means making a bad situation worse. Answer: (b).

Practice questions

Question 1

What does 'once in a blue moon' mean?

  1. Every month
  2. Very rarely
  3. At night
  4. Very often
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Very rarely

A blue moon is an uncommon event, so the idiom describes something that happens very rarely, not regularly or often.

Question 2

What does 'to spill the beans' mean?

  1. To waste food
  2. To make a mistake
  3. To reveal a secret
  4. To cook carelessly
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. To reveal a secret

The idiom means to reveal secret information, often by accident. Options about food are literal traps.

Question 3

Choose the meaning of the phrasal verb 'turn down' in 'She turned down the job offer.'

  1. Accepted
  2. Delayed
  3. Negotiated
  4. Rejected
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Rejected

To turn down an offer is to refuse or reject it. The sentence makes clear she did not take the job.

Question 4

What does 'to hit the nail on the head' mean?

  1. To describe something exactly right
  2. To hurt oneself
  3. To do carpentry
  4. To make a loud noise
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. To describe something exactly right

The idiom means to describe a situation or problem precisely. The carpentry image of striking the nail squarely suggests accuracy.

Question 5

What does 'a piece of cake' mean?

  1. A small reward
  2. Something very easy
  3. A sweet dish
  4. A shared benefit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Something very easy

The idiom describes a task that is very easy to do. Food-related options are literal readings and are incorrect.

FAQs

How many idioms should I learn for SSC?

Aim for around 300 to 400 commonly tested idioms and phrasal verbs, learned with example sentences. Many recur across papers. Revise them in themed groups and test yourself with mixed lists so that you can recognise them quickly in any question format.

Can I guess an idiom I have never seen?

Often, yes. Picture the literal image, decide whether it suggests something positive or negative, and check how it fits the sentence. Then eliminate literal-meaning options. This approach works for many unfamiliar idioms, though learning the common ones remains the most reliable strategy.
