Articles A, An and The: Rules, Sounds and Exceptions

Articles — a, an and the — look minor, but they appear in error spotting, fillers, cloze tests and sentence improvement across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS and bank exams. Indian speakers often drop articles or add them where English does not, and examiners use these habits as traps. The rules are short: 'a' and 'an' depend on sound, 'the' marks something specific or unique, and some nouns take no article at all.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

A versus an: sound, not spelling

Use 'an' before a vowel sound and 'a' before a consonant sound: an hour, an honest officer, an MBA (em-bee-ay), an SBI branch (es-bee-eye); but a university (yu-), a European (yu-), a one-day match (wun-), a useful book.

When to use 'the'

Use 'the' for something already mentioned or clear from context ('I bought a pen. The pen is blue'), unique things (the sun, the RBI), superlatives (the best), ordinals (the first), rivers, seas, mountain ranges and groups of islands (the Ganga, the Arabian Sea, the Himalayas, the Andamans), and newspapers and holy books (the Hindu, the Gita).

No article

Do not use an article before most proper nouns of people, cities, countries and single mountains (Delhi, India, Mount Everest), before languages (She speaks Marathi), meals (We had lunch), games (He plays cricket), and uncountable or plural nouns used in a general sense ('Honesty is the best policy', 'Banks offer loans').

Countries with 'the'

Countries with plural or descriptive names take 'the': the USA, the UK, the Netherlands, the Maldives.

Institutions

'Go to school / hospital / church' without an article when used for their main purpose; with 'the' when visiting for another reason: 'She went to the school to meet the principal.'

Relative clauses and 'who'

In a relative clause the verb agrees with the noun that 'who', 'which' or 'that' refers to. 'He is one of the officers who have been promoted' uses 'have' because 'who' refers to 'officers'. But 'He is the only one of the officers who has been promoted' uses 'has', since 'who' now refers to 'the only one'. This pair of sentences is a favourite in SSC error spotting, so learn both versions together.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Say the next word aloud: if it starts with a vowel sound, use 'an'.
  • Abbreviations read letter by letter follow the letter's sound: an LIC agent (el-), a UPI app (yu-).
  • 'The + adjective' means a group of people: the poor, the rich, the unemployed.
  • 'The' before a comparative in parallel phrases: 'The more you practise, the better you score.'
  • No article before 'most' meaning 'majority': 'Most candidates', but 'the most important'.
  • 'Both the' is correct; 'the both' is wrong.

Worked examples

Example 1

Fill in: 'He waited for ______ hour at ______ SBI branch.'

Solution

'Hour' begins with a silent h, so 'an hour'. 'SBI' is read 'es-bee-eye', starting with a vowel sound, so 'an SBI branch'. Answer: an, an.

Example 2

Fill in: 'She is ______ university topper and ______ honest person.'

Solution

'University' starts with a 'yu' consonant sound → 'a'. 'Honest' has a silent h → 'an'. Answer: a, an.

Example 3

Correct the error: 'The Mount Everest is the highest peak in the world.'

Solution

Single mountains take no article. Correct: 'Mount Everest is the highest peak in the world.' 'The highest' is correct because it is a superlative.

Example 4

Correct the error: 'He plays the cricket every evening.'

Solution

Names of games take no article. Correct: 'He plays cricket every evening.'

Example 5

Fill in: '______ Ganga is one of ______ longest rivers in India.'

Solution

Rivers take 'the', and 'longest' is a superlative, also with 'the'. Answer: The, the.

Practice questions

Question 1

Choose the correct article: 'She has applied for ______ MBA programme.'

  1. a
  2. the
  3. an
  4. no article
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. an

MBA is read letter by letter as em-bee-ay, which starts with a vowel sound. So 'an MBA programme' is correct.

Question 2

Choose the correct article: 'He is ______ European tourist.'

  1. an
  2. the
  3. no article
  4. a
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. a

'European' begins with a 'yu' sound, which is a consonant sound. So it takes 'a', not 'an'.

Question 3

Which sentence is correct?

  1. Honesty is the best policy.
  2. The honesty is the best policy.
  3. Honesty is a best policy.
  4. A honesty is the best policy.
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Honesty is the best policy.

Abstract nouns used in a general sense take no article, and superlatives like 'best' take 'the'.

Question 4

Which sentence is correct?

  1. She speaks the Tamil fluently.
  2. The Himalayas protect India from cold winds.
  3. He visited the Delhi last year.
  4. We had the dinner at eight.
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The Himalayas protect India from cold winds.

Mountain ranges take 'the', so 'The Himalayas' is correct. Languages, cities and meals in a general sense take no article.

Question 5

Choose the correct option: 'The government launched schemes for ______.'

  1. a poor
  2. poors
  3. the poor
  4. the poors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. the poor

'The' plus an adjective refers to a whole group of people. 'The poor' means poor people in general and takes no plural ending.

FAQs

Why is it 'a university' but 'an umbrella'?

Articles depend on the sound at the start of the next word, not the letter. 'University' starts with a 'yu' sound, which is a consonant sound, so it takes 'a'. 'Umbrella' starts with an 'uh' vowel sound, so it takes 'an'.

Do we say 'the RBI' or 'RBI'?

Both appear in Indian newspapers, but formal English uses 'the' with the full name of an institution that includes a common noun like 'Bank' or 'Commission': the Reserve Bank of India, the Election Commission. With abbreviations, 'the RBI' is common and safe. In exams, the option with 'the' is usually preferred.
