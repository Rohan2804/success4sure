Cause and Effect Reasoning: Independent and Common Causes

Cause and effect questions give two statements describing events and ask how they are related: is one the cause and the other its effect, are both effects of a common cause, or are they independent? RBI Grade B Phase I and IBPS and SBI PO mains include such questions, and they also appear in MBA entrance logical sections. Most errors come from looking at the order in which the statements are printed rather than the order in which the events must have happened.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The usual answer choices

Typically: statement I is the cause and II its effect; II is the cause and I its effect; both are independent causes; both are effects of independent causes; both are effects of some common cause. Read the option list once before starting a set.

Time order and mechanism

A cause must come before its effect and must plausibly produce it. 'Heavy rain lashed the city' and 'Several low-lying areas were waterlogged' — the rain clearly leads to waterlogging, so I is the cause.

Common cause

Two events may both result from a third, unstated event. 'Vegetable prices rose sharply' and 'Transport of goods was disrupted' could both follow from a nationwide transport strike or floods. Look for a single event that would explain both.

Independent events

If neither event can reasonably cause the other and no single event explains both, they are effects of independent causes. 'The bank raised its home-loan rate' and 'A cricket match was postponed due to rain' have nothing in common.

Immediate versus principal cause

Some questions ask for the immediate cause of an event, meaning the trigger right before it, rather than a deeper background reason. If a building collapsed during heavy rain and the building was known to be old and weak, the heavy rain is the immediate cause while the poor condition is the underlying cause. Read the question stem to see which one is being asked.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Ask 'Could A alone make B happen?' then 'Could B alone make A happen?' If neither, look for a common cause.
  • Do not assume the first statement is the cause; examiners often reverse the order.
  • Policy actions (raising rates, imposing a ban) are usually responses to a problem, so the problem is often the cause.
  • Weather events, strikes and policy changes are common hidden common causes.
  • If both statements describe actions by different authorities on unrelated matters, mark them independent.
  • When a statement begins with words like 'following', 'after' or 'in response to', it is usually the effect; find the event it refers to.

Worked examples

Example 1

I. Several low-lying areas of the city were waterlogged. II. The city received very heavy rainfall over the past two days.

Solution

Rainfall causes waterlogging, not the other way round. II is the cause and I its effect, even though I is printed first.

Example 2

I. The prices of vegetables rose sharply in the city's markets. II. Supply of goods from neighbouring states was disrupted.

Solution

A supply disruption reduces arrivals and pushes up prices. II is the cause and I the effect. (If the statements were 'prices rose' and 'many shops shut early', both might be effects of a common cause such as a strike.)

Example 3

I. The state government declared a holiday for all schools on Monday. II. Many offices in the state reported low attendance on Monday.

Solution

A school holiday does not by itself empty offices, and low office attendance does not cause a school holiday. Both could follow from a common cause, such as a severe weather warning or a major festival. Both are effects of a common cause.

Example 4

I. A bank increased the interest rate on its fixed deposits. II. A city traffic police department launched a helmet-awareness drive.

Solution

There is no plausible link between the two, and no single event explains both. They are effects of independent causes.

Practice questions

Question 1

I. Many flights were cancelled at the city airport. II. Dense fog reduced visibility to near zero in the morning.

  1. I is the cause and II is its effect
  2. II is the cause and I is its effect
  3. Both are independent causes
  4. Both are effects of independent causes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. II is the cause and I is its effect

Low visibility from fog makes it unsafe to fly, which leads to cancellations. Fog is clearly the cause and the cancellations are its effect.

Question 2

I. The government reduced the import duty on edible oils. II. Domestic prices of edible oils had risen sharply in the preceding months.

  1. I is the cause and II is its effect
  2. Both are effects of independent causes
  3. II is the cause and I is its effect
  4. Both are independent causes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. II is the cause and I is its effect

A duty cut is a policy response to high domestic prices. The earlier price rise is the cause, and the duty cut is the effect.

Question 3

I. Attendance at the local market was very low on Sunday. II. Several roads in the town were closed on Sunday.

  1. I is the cause and II is its effect
  2. Both are independent causes
  3. Both are effects of independent causes
  4. Both are effects of a common cause
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Both are effects of a common cause

Low attendance does not close roads, and road closures alone may not fully explain it. Both could result from one event, such as a festival procession or a curfew.

Question 4

I. A cricket stadium hosted a sold-out final. II. A bank launched a new credit card for students.

  1. Both are effects of independent causes
  2. I is the cause and II is its effect
  3. II is the cause and I is its effect
  4. Both are effects of a common cause
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Both are effects of independent causes

There is no plausible link between a cricket final and a student credit card launch. Each has its own unrelated reason, so they are effects of independent causes.

Question 5

I. Many students in the district could not reach their exam centres on time. II. Bus services in the district were suspended due to a strike.

  1. I is the cause and II is its effect
  2. II is the cause and I is its effect
  3. Both are effects of a common cause
  4. Both are independent causes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. II is the cause and I is its effect

Suspended bus services leave students without transport and delay them. The strike-related suspension is the cause and the delays are the effect.

FAQs

How do I tell 'common cause' from 'independent causes'?

Try to name one single, realistic event that would produce both statements. If you can — a strike, a storm, a festival, a policy decision — choose common cause. If every explanation needs two separate events, choose independent causes. Do not invent far-fetched links to force a common cause.

Is cause and effect still asked in bank exams?

It appears more in RBI Grade B and in PO mains reasoning than in prelims, and its frequency varies from year to year. Because the questions are short, a few practice sets are usually enough to become comfortable, so it is worth covering if you are preparing for mains.
