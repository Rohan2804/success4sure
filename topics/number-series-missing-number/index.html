Number Series Tricks: Find the Missing Number Fast

Number series is one of the highest-yield topics in bank prelims: IBPS PO, IBPS Clerk, SBI PO and SBI Clerk usually set a block of five series questions, either missing-number or wrong-number. RBI Assistant and LIC exams follow the same pattern. Each question can be solved in 20–30 seconds once you check patterns in a fixed order, which makes this block a reliable place to gain marks and save time for DI.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Check order

Look at how fast the series grows. Slow, steady growth suggests differences (first or second level). Roughly doubling or tripling suggests multiplication with an add-on (×2 + 1, ×3 − 2). Fast irregular growth suggests ×1 + 1, ×2 + 2, ×3 + 3 type patterns. Values near perfect squares or cubes suggest n² ± k or n³ ± k.

Difference patterns

Write the differences between consecutive terms. If they are not constant, check the differences of differences, or whether the differences themselves form a familiar sequence: squares (1, 4, 9, 16), cubes, primes or powers of 2. In 7, 9, 13, 21, 37 the differences 2, 4, 8, 16 double each time, so the next term is 37 + 32 = 69.

Multiplicative patterns

Divide each term by the previous one. If the ratio is near a constant, test "× k ± c": 5, 11, 23, 47, 95 follows ×2 + 1. If the ratio itself changes steadily (×0.5, ×1, ×1.5, ×2), the multiplier is growing by a fixed step.

Alternating and mixed series

If odd and even positions behave differently, split them into two series, or look for two operations alternating, such as +2 then ×2. These are common in IBPS Clerk. When nothing fits, compare each term with n², n³ or n! for its position n.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Know squares to 30, cubes to 20 and powers of 2 to 2¹² — they appear in nearly every set.
  • If differences are 1, 4, 9, 16…, the next difference is the next square; if 1, 8, 27…, the next cube.
  • For ×n + (n + 1) patterns, the series explodes quickly — the answer will be much larger than the last term.
  • Use the options: the next term usually has a predictable last digit; eliminate options that cannot fit.
  • Spend no more than 40 seconds; if stuck, move on and return after the other four.

Worked examples

Example 1

5, 11, 23, 47, 95, ?

Solution

Each term is double the previous plus 1: 5 × 2 + 1 = 11, 11 × 2 + 1 = 23 and so on. Next = 95 × 2 + 1 = 191.

Example 2

3, 8, 15, 24, 35, ?

Solution

Terms are 2² − 1, 3² − 1, 4² − 1, 5² − 1, 6² − 1. Next = 7² − 1 = 48. The differences 5, 7, 9, 11, 13 confirm it.

Example 3

7, 9, 13, 21, 37, ?

Solution

Differences are 2, 4, 8, 16, which double each time. Next difference = 32, so the answer is 37 + 32 = 69.

Example 4

4, 6, 15, 49, 201, ?

Solution

Pattern: ×1 + 2, ×2 + 3, ×3 + 4, ×4 + 5. Check: 4 × 1 + 2 = 6, 6 × 2 + 3 = 15, 15 × 3 + 4 = 49, 49 × 4 + 5 = 201. Next = 201 × 5 + 6 = 1,011.

Example 5

120, 60, 60, 90, 180, ?

Solution

Multipliers are ×0.5, ×1, ×1.5, ×2, rising by 0.5. Next multiplier is ×2.5, so 180 × 2.5 = 450.

FAQs

How many number series questions come in IBPS Clerk and PO prelims?

Typically a block of five questions, though the pattern varies by year and sometimes the block is replaced by another type such as quadratic equations. Because the block is short and scoring, regular practice of ten series a day is a common preparation strategy.

What should I do if no pattern fits within 40 seconds?

Skip the question and return later. Often a fresh look reveals an alternating or mixed pattern you missed. Spending two minutes on one series costs more marks elsewhere than the single question is worth.
