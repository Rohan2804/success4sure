Volume and Surface Area of Cube, Cuboid and Cylinder

Solid mensuration starts with the three shapes that have flat or straight sides: cubes, cuboids and cylinders. IBPS PO, SBI PO and RBI Grade B include a 3D question in many prelims papers, usually a tank, a box or a pipe, and SSC CGL asks several. These shapes also appear in quantity comparison, where you must compute two volumes quickly. Knowing which formula answers which practical phrase — paint, capacity, cloth, sheet — is half the job.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Cuboid

For length l, breadth b and height h: volume = lbh, total surface area = 2(lb + bh + hl), lateral (four walls) area = 2h(l + b), and the longest rod that fits = √(l² + b² + h²). Painting the four walls of a room uses lateral area; the floor and ceiling are extra.

TSA = 2(lb + bh + hl)

Cube

All edges equal a: volume = a³, total surface area = 6a², lateral area = 4a² and diagonal = a√3. If the edge doubles, surface area becomes 4 times and volume 8 times.

V = a³; TSA = 6a²

Cylinder

For radius r and height h: volume = πr²h, curved surface area = 2πrh, total surface area = 2πr(r + h). A closed tank needs total surface; an open drum needs curved surface plus one base; a pipe or a roller uses only the curved surface.

V = πr²h; CSA = 2πrh

Capacity and recasting

Capacity conversions: 1 m³ = 1,000 litres and 1,000 cm³ = 1 litre. When one solid is melted and recast into others, volume is conserved: number of new solids = volume of original ÷ volume of one new solid. Surface area is not conserved.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Pythagorean quadruples like 12-4-3 (13), 12-9-8 (17) and 2-3-6 (7) give clean cuboid diagonals.
  • For a cube, TSA ÷ 6 = a²; for volume, take the cube root — memorise cubes up to 15.
  • If a cylinder’s radius doubles and height halves, its volume doubles.
  • Number of small cubes of edge b from a cube of edge a is (a/b)³.
  • Rain or water-level problems: rise in level = volume added ÷ base area.

Worked examples

Example 1

A cuboid measures 12 cm × 4 cm × 3 cm. Find its volume, total surface area and diagonal.

Solution

Volume = 12 × 4 × 3 = 144 cm³. TSA = 2(48 + 12 + 36) = 192 cm². Diagonal = √(144 + 16 + 9) = √169 = 13 cm.

Example 2

A cube has a volume of 343 cm³. Find its total surface area.

Solution

Edge = ∛343 = 7 cm. TSA = 6 × 49 = 294 cm².

Example 3

A closed cylindrical drum has radius 7 cm and height 10 cm. Find its volume, curved surface area and total surface area.

Solution

Volume = 22/7 × 49 × 10 = 1,540 cm³. CSA = 2 × 22/7 × 7 × 10 = 440 cm². Each base is 154 cm², so TSA = 440 + 308 = 748 cm².

Example 4

An overhead tank measures 2 m × 1.5 m × 1 m. How many litres can it hold?

Solution

Volume = 3 m³. Since 1 m³ = 1,000 litres, capacity = 3,000 litres.

Example 5

A metal cube of edge 12 cm is melted and recast into small cubes of edge 3 cm. How many small cubes are formed?

Solution

Number = (12/3)³ = 4³ = 64 cubes. Volume is conserved: 1,728 cm³ ÷ 27 cm³ = 64.

Example 6

A rectangular water tank has a base of 8 m × 5 m. If 20,000 litres of water are pumped into it, by how much does the water level rise?

Solution

20,000 litres = 20 m³. Rise in level = volume ÷ base area = 20 ÷ 40 = 0.5 m, i.e. 50 cm. The same logic answers rainfall questions: rain of 2 cm on a 1,000 m² roof gives 20 m³ of water.

Practice questions

Question 1

The volume of a cuboid measuring 10 cm × 6 cm × 4 cm is:

  1. 200 cm³
  2. 248 cm³
  3. 120 cm³
  4. 240 cm³
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 240 cm³

Volume = length × breadth × height = 10 × 6 × 4 = 240 cubic centimetres.

Question 2

The total surface area of a cube is 486 cm². Its volume is:

  1. 512 cm³
  2. 729 cm³
  3. 1,000 cm³
  4. 648 cm³
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 729 cm³

Each face is 486/6 = 81 cm², so the edge is 9 cm and the volume is 9³ = 729 cm³.

Question 3

The volume of a cylinder with radius 14 cm and height 20 cm is:

  1. 6,160 cm³
  2. 1,760 cm³
  3. 12,320 cm³
  4. 12,600 cm³
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 12,320 cm³

Volume = 22/7 × 196 × 20 = 616 × 20 = 12,320 cubic centimetres.

Question 4

A hall is 12 m long, 9 m wide and 8 m high. The longest rod that can be placed in it is:

  1. 17 m
  2. 15 m
  3. 19 m
  4. 20 m
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 17 m

Longest rod = √(144 + 81 + 64) = √289 = 17 metres, the space diagonal of the hall.

Question 5

A cylinder of radius 14 cm has a curved surface area of 880 cm². Its height is:

  1. 12 cm
  2. 10 cm
  3. 8 cm
  4. 20 cm
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 10 cm

CSA = 2πrh gives h = 880 ÷ (2 × 22/7 × 14) = 880 ÷ 88 = 10 cm.

FAQs

When do I use curved surface area instead of total surface area?

Use curved surface area when the ends are open or not part of the job — a pipe, a roller, or the side wall of a well. Use total surface area for closed objects such as a sealed tin. An open-top tank needs curved area plus one base.

How are litres related to cubic metres?

One cubic metre holds 1,000 litres and one litre equals 1,000 cubic centimetres. Tank and water questions often give dimensions in metres but ask for litres, so multiply the volume in m³ by 1,000.
