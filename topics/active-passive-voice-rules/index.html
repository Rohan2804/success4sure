Active and Passive Voice Rules for Every Tense

Voice conversion is a regular SSC CGL, CHSL and MTS question: you are given a sentence in active voice and asked for the correct passive form, or the reverse. Bank exams do not usually ask it directly, but knowing passive structures helps with error spotting and sentence improvement. The rules are mechanical — the object becomes the subject, the verb takes 'be' plus the past participle, and the tense of 'be' matches the original tense — so careful practice gives near-certain marks.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

The basic change

Active: Subject + verb + object ('The clerk checked the form'). Passive: Object + form of 'be' + past participle + by + subject ('The form was checked by the clerk'). The tense is carried by 'be'; the main verb always becomes the past participle.

Tense table

Simple present: is/am/are + V3 (is written). Present continuous: is/am/are being + V3 (is being written). Present perfect: has/have been + V3 (has been written). Simple past: was/were + V3. Past continuous: was/were being + V3. Past perfect: had been + V3. Simple future: will be + V3. Future perfect: will have been + V3.

Tenses with no passive in exam usage

Perfect continuous tenses (has been writing, had been writing, will have been writing) and future continuous are generally not converted into passive in exam questions. If an option tries ('has been being written'), it is usually wrong.

Modals

Modal + be + V3: 'You must submit the form' → 'The form must be submitted by you'. 'They can solve it' → 'It can be solved by them'.

Imperatives and questions

Commands use 'Let': 'Close the door' → 'Let the door be closed'. Requests often use 'You are requested to…'. Questions keep question form: 'Did she write the letter?' → 'Was the letter written by her?'; 'Who wrote this?' → 'By whom was this written?'

Getting the past participle right

Many errors come from the wrong verb form rather than the wrong tense. Irregular verbs must use their third form: written (not wrote), chosen, taken, given, broken, begun, sung, spoken, stolen, forgotten. 'The letter was wrote by him' and 'The song was sang beautifully' are classic wrong options. Revise a list of the 60 to 70 most common irregular verbs with all three forms; it helps in voice, tense and error-spotting questions alike.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Pronouns change case: I → me, he → him, they → them, we → us, after 'by'.
  • When the doer is unknown or obvious ('Someone stole my bag'), drop the 'by' phrase: 'My bag was stolen.'
  • Check that 'be' agrees with the new subject: 'The letters were written', not 'was written'.
  • Verbs without objects (sleep, arrive, die) cannot be made passive.
  • With two objects, either can become the subject: 'He gave me a book' → 'I was given a book by him' or 'A book was given to me by him'.
  • Some verbs take 'with', 'to' or 'at' instead of 'by': 'known to', 'pleased with', 'surprised at'.

Worked examples

Example 1

Change to passive: 'The manager approves all loans.'

Solution

Simple present, plural object: 'All loans are approved by the manager.'

Example 2

Change to passive: 'They are repairing the road.'

Solution

Present continuous → are being + V3: 'The road is being repaired by them.' 'Road' is singular, so 'is'.

Example 3

Change to passive: 'She has completed the project.'

Solution

Present perfect → has been + V3: 'The project has been completed by her.'

Example 4

Change to passive: 'Open the window.'

Solution

Imperative → Let + object + be + V3: 'Let the window be opened.'

Example 5

Change to passive: 'Who taught you English?'

Solution

Question with 'who' → 'By whom were you taught English?' The past tense and question form are kept.

Practice questions

Question 1

Choose the passive form: 'The committee will announce the results tomorrow.'

  1. The results would be announced by the committee tomorrow.
  2. The results are announced by the committee tomorrow.
  3. The results will announced by the committee tomorrow.
  4. The results will be announced by the committee tomorrow.
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The results will be announced by the committee tomorrow.

Simple future becomes 'will be' plus the past participle. 'The results will be announced' keeps the tense and agrees with the plural subject.

Question 2

Choose the passive form: 'He was writing a letter.'

  1. A letter was being written by him.
  2. A letter is being written by him.
  3. A letter has been written by him.
  4. A letter was written by him.
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A letter was being written by him.

Past continuous changes to 'was/were being' plus the past participle. 'A letter was being written by him' is correct.

Question 3

Choose the passive form: 'You must follow the rules.'

  1. The rules must follow by you.
  2. The rules must be followed by you.
  3. The rules must have followed by you.
  4. The rules are followed by you.
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The rules must be followed by you.

With a modal, the passive uses modal plus 'be' plus the past participle: 'must be followed'.

Question 4

Choose the passive form: 'Did the bank send the statement?'

  1. Has the statement been sent by the bank?
  2. Is the statement sent by the bank?
  3. Was the statement sent by the bank?
  4. Did the statement sent by the bank?
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Was the statement sent by the bank?

A simple past question becomes 'Was/Were' plus subject plus past participle. 'Was the statement sent by the bank?' is correct.

Question 5

Choose the active form: 'The match was won by India.'

  1. India wins the match.
  2. India has won the match.
  3. India had won the match.
  4. India won the match.
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. India won the match.

The passive is in the simple past ('was won'), so the active must also be simple past: 'India won the match'.

FAQs

Can every sentence be changed into passive voice?

No. Only sentences with transitive verbs (verbs that take an object) can be made passive. 'She sleeps early' or 'The train arrived' have no object to become the subject. In exams, perfect continuous tenses are also generally treated as having no standard passive form.

Is 'by + agent' always needed in the passive?

No. If the doer is unknown, unimportant or obvious, it is usually dropped: 'The bridge was built in 1990.' Exam options sometimes keep 'by someone' or 'by people', which is acceptable but less natural; check the other options before choosing it.
