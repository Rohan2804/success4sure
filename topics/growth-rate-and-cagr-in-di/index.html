Growth Rate, CAGR and Index Numbers in DI Questions

Questions on growth appear in almost every DI set: "by what percent did sales grow", "what was the average annual growth" or "which year had the highest growth". RBI Grade B, IBPS PO mains, SBI PO and CAT go further with compound annual growth rate (CAGR), index numbers and market-share changes. Getting these right depends on understanding which base each growth figure uses.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Year-on-year and overall growth

Year-on-year growth compares each year with the previous one. Overall growth compares the last year with the first. Successive growths combine multiplicatively: 10% followed by 20% is an overall growth of 1.1 × 1.2 − 1 = 32%, not 30%.

Overall = (1 + g₁)(1 + g₂) − 1

CAGR

The compound annual growth rate is the constant yearly rate that takes the first value to the last: CAGR = (last/first)^(1/n) − 1 over n years. ₹200 crore growing to ₹242 crore in 2 years gives (1.21)^(1/2) − 1 = 10% a year. It differs from the simple average of yearly growth rates.

CAGR = (End/Start)^(1/n) − 1

Index numbers

An index sets a base year to 100 and expresses other years relative to it. If the 2022 index is 125 with 2020 = 100, the 2022 value is 25% above 2020. With an actual 2020 value of ₹80 crore, the 2022 value is ₹100 crore.

Percent versus percentage points

If a market share rises from 12% to 15%, it rose by 3 percentage points, which is a 25% increase in the share. Questions use both phrasings; options usually include both numbers.

Rule of 72 (approximate)

At a steady growth rate of r% a year, a value roughly doubles in 72/r years. At 8% that is about 9 years; at 12%, about 6 years. It is an approximation, useful for quick checks but not for exact answers.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Two-year CAGR from a ratio: 1.21 → 10%, 1.44 → 20%, 1.1025 → 5%, 1.69 → 30%.
  • Highest growth year: compare increase ÷ previous value for each year, not raw increases.
  • A fall of x% followed by a rise of x% leaves the value below the start.
  • Index-to-value: value = base value × index/100.

Worked examples

Example 1

A company’s revenue grows from ₹200 crore to ₹242 crore in 2 years. Find the CAGR.

Solution

Growth factor = 242/200 = 1.21. Since 1.21 = 1.1², the constant yearly factor is 1.1, so CAGR = 10% per year. The overall two-year growth is 21%, not 20%, because the second year grows on a larger base.

Example 2

Revenue was ₹500 crore in 2020, ₹550 crore in 2021 and ₹660 crore in 2022. Find the yearly and overall growth.

Solution

2021: 50/500 = 10%. 2022: 110/550 = 20%. Overall: 160/500 = 32%, which equals 1.1 × 1.2 − 1.

Example 3

With 2020 = 100, a sales index reads 125 in 2022. If 2020 sales were ₹80 crore, what were 2022 sales?

Solution

Value = base value × index/100 = 80 × 125/100 = ₹100 crore. The index itself has no unit; it only tells you that 2022 sales were 1.25 times the 2020 level.

Example 4

A bank’s market share rises from 12% to 15%. Describe the change in two ways.

Solution

It rose by 3 percentage points; relative to its earlier level, it rose by 3/12 = 25%.

Example 5

What annual growth rate takes a value from 400 to 576 in 2 years?

Solution

576/400 = 1.44 = 1.2², so the required CAGR is 20%.

Example 6

Roughly how long does a value growing at 8% a year take to double?

Solution

By the rule of 72, about 72/8 = 9 years. Check: 1.08⁹ ≈ 2.0.

Practice questions

Question 1

A value grows from 1,000 to 1,210 in 2 years. Its CAGR is:

  1. 10%
  2. 10.5%
  3. 21%
  4. 11%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 10%

1,210/1,000 = 1.21 = 1.1², so the compound annual growth rate is 10 percent.

Question 2

Sales grow by 20% in one year and 25% in the next. The overall growth is:

  1. 45%
  2. 47.5%
  3. 52.5%
  4. 50%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 50%

Overall factor = 1.2 × 1.25 = 1.5, so the overall growth over the two years is 50 percent.

Question 3

With 2019 = 100, an index is 140 in 2023. If the 2019 value was 250, the 2023 value is:

  1. 390
  2. 350
  3. 140
  4. 320
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 350

Value = 250 × 140/100 = 350, i.e. 40 percent above the base-year value.

Question 4

A firm’s market share rises from 8% to 10%. The percentage increase in its share is:

  1. 2%
  2. 20%
  3. 25%
  4. 12.5%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 25%

The share rose 2 percentage points on a base of 8, which is a 25 percent increase.

Question 5

By the rule of 72, a value growing at 12% a year roughly doubles in about:

  1. 8 years
  2. 12 years
  3. 6 years
  4. 4 years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 6 years

72 ÷ 12 = 6 years, and indeed 1.12⁶ is close to 2, confirming the approximation.

FAQs

Is CAGR the same as average growth rate?

No. The simple average of yearly growth rates ignores compounding and the changing base. CAGR is the single constant rate that links the start and end values. For growths of 10% and 20%, the simple average is 15% but the CAGR is about 14.9%.

Why do DI questions use index numbers?

Index numbers make comparisons across years easy without giving actual values, and they let setters combine data from different scales. Convert an index to a value only when the question needs an amount, using the base-year value given elsewhere in the set.
