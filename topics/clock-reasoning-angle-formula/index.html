Clock Problems: Angle Formula, Overlaps and Mirror Time

Clock questions appear regularly in SSC CGL, CHSL and MTS and occasionally in bank clerk prelims. They test three ideas: the angle between the hands at a given time, the times when the hands overlap, are opposite or at right angles, and the mirror image of a clock. All of them come from one fact — the minute hand moves 6° per minute and the hour hand 0.5° per minute. With the angle formula below, most questions take under a minute.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Speeds of the hands

The minute hand covers 360° in 60 minutes: 6° per minute. The hour hand covers 30° in 60 minutes: 0.5° per minute. So the minute hand gains 5.5° per minute over the hour hand, or 55 minute-spaces every 60 minutes.

Angle at a given time

Use the formula below with H in hours and M in minutes. If the result is more than 180°, subtract it from 360° to get the smaller angle.

Angle = |30H − 5.5M|

Overlaps, right angles and straight lines

The hands coincide 22 times in a day, are at right angles 44 times and point in opposite directions 22 times. Between H and H + 1 o'clock, they coincide at M = 60H/11 minutes past H.

Mirror image of a clock

For a mirror placed vertically, the image shows 11:60 minus the actual time (use 12:00 minus the time when the minutes are zero). 4:25 appears as 7:35.

Fast and slow clocks

If a clock gains or loses time at a steady rate, find the total gain over the period and add or subtract it. A clock that gains 4 minutes every hour, set right at 8 a.m., shows 8 × 4 = 32 minutes extra by 4 p.m. Questions also ask when two faulty clocks will show the same time again, which needs their relative gain to reach 12 hours (720 minutes).

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Coincide between H and H+1: M = (60/11) × H. Between 4 and 5: 240/11 = 21 9/11 minutes past 4.
  • Opposite between H and H+1: solve 5.5M = 30H + 180 (or 30H − 180 for later hours).
  • Right angle between H and H+1: solve 5.5M = 30H ± 90.
  • Mirror time: subtract from 11:60. 3:40 → 8:20.
  • At exact hours the angle is 30 × H (for H ≤ 6): 3 o'clock = 90°, 6 o'clock = 180°.
  • Between any two consecutive hours the hands make a right angle twice, except in the 2–3 and 8–9 o'clock windows where one of the two right angles falls exactly at 3 or 9.

Worked examples

Example 1

Find the angle between the hands at 3:40.

Solution

|30 × 3 − 5.5 × 40| = |90 − 220| = 130°.

Example 2

At what time between 4 and 5 o'clock are the hands together?

Solution

At 4:00 the hour hand is 120° ahead. The minute hand gains 5.5° per minute, so it takes 120 ÷ 5.5 = 240/11 = 21 9/11 minutes. Time: 21 9/11 minutes past 4.

Example 3

At what time between 4 and 5 o'clock are the hands opposite each other?

Solution

The minute hand must be 180° ahead of the hour hand: 5.5M = 120 + 180 = 300, so M = 300/5.5 = 54 6/11. Time: 54 6/11 minutes past 4.

Example 4

When are the hands first at a right angle between 2 and 3 o'clock?

Solution

At 2:00 the hour hand is 60° ahead. For a right angle with the minute hand ahead: 5.5M = 60 + 90 = 150, so M = 27 3/11. (The other case, 5.5M = 60 − 90, is negative, so it lies before 2:00.) First right angle: 27 3/11 minutes past 2.

Example 5

A clock shows 4:25. What time does its mirror image show?

Solution

Subtract from 11:60: 11:60 − 4:25 = 7:35.

Example 6

A clock is set right at 8 a.m. and gains 4 minutes every hour. What time does it show at 4 p.m. the same day?

Solution

From 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. is 8 hours. Gain = 8 × 4 = 32 minutes. The clock shows 4:32 p.m.

Practice questions

Question 1

What is the angle between the hands of a clock at 7:20?

  1. 100°
  2. 110°
  3. 90°
  4. 120°
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 100°

Using the formula, |30 × 7 − 5.5 × 20| = |210 − 110| = 100°, which is already less than 180°.

Question 2

What is the smaller angle between the hands at 9:15?

  1. 187.5°
  2. 172.5°
  3. 165°
  4. 180°
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 172.5°

|30 × 9 − 5.5 × 15| = |270 − 82.5| = 187.5°. The smaller angle is 360° − 187.5° = 172.5°.

Question 3

How many times do the hands of a clock coincide in a day?

  1. 24
  2. 48
  3. 22
  4. 44
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 22

In every 12 hours the hands coincide 11 times, because the meetings near 12 o'clock are shared. In 24 hours this gives 22 coincidences.

Question 4

A clock shows 2:15. What time does its mirror image show?

  1. 10:45
  2. 9:15
  3. 10:15
  4. 9:45
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 9:45

Mirror time equals 11:60 minus the actual time. 11:60 − 2:15 = 9:45.

Question 5

What is the angle between the hands at 4:20?

  1. 10°
  2. 0°
  3. 20°
  4. 5°
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 10°

|30 × 4 − 5.5 × 20| = |120 − 110| = 10°. The hour hand has moved 10° past the 4 while the minute hand is exactly on it.

FAQs

Why do hands coincide only 11 times in 12 hours, not 12?

The minute hand gains 55 minute-spaces per hour over the hour hand, so it needs 65 5/11 minutes to catch up again. In 12 hours that happens 11 times. The coincidence between 11 and 1 o'clock is the single meeting at 12.

Is the mirror-time rule different for a water image?

Yes. A water image flips the clock top to bottom, so 12 and 6 swap places while 3 and 9 stay where they are. The 11:60 shortcut does not apply; instead, flip each hand's position across the 3–9 line and read the new time. Mirror (vertical) images are far more common in exams.
