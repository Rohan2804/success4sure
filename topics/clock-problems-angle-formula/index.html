Clock Problems: Angle Between Hands and Coinciding Hands

Clock questions are short arithmetic puzzles that appear in SSC CGL and CHSL, occasionally in CAT, CMAT and MAT, and in the reasoning sections of some bank papers. They rely on one relative-speed idea: the minute hand gains 5.5° on the hour hand every minute. With that and one formula for the angle, nearly every clock question can be solved in under a minute.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Speeds of the hands

The minute hand moves 360° in 60 minutes, i.e. 6° per minute. The hour hand moves 30° per hour, i.e. 0.5° per minute. So the minute hand gains 5.5° per minute on the hour hand, and it gains 60 minute-spaces in 65 5/11 minutes.

Relative speed = 5.5° per minute

Angle between the hands

At H hours and M minutes, the angle is |30H − 5.5M| degrees. If the result exceeds 180°, subtract it from 360° to get the smaller angle. At 3:40 the angle is |90 − 220| = 130°.

θ = |30H − 5.5M|

Coinciding and right-angle times

Between H and H + 1 o’clock, the hands coincide when 30H = 5.5M, i.e. M = 60H/11 minutes past H. For a right angle, set |30H − 5.5M| = 90; for opposite hands, 180. The hands coincide 11 times in 12 hours (22 times a day) and are at right angles 22 times in 12 hours (44 times a day).

Coincide: M = 60H/11

Mirror images

The time seen in a vertical mirror is 11:60 minus the actual time (or 12:00 minus it when the minutes are zero). A clock showing 3:40 appears as 8:20 in the mirror.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Hands coincide every 65 5/11 minutes, not every 60 minutes.
  • At exactly H o’clock, the angle is 30H (or 360 − 30H if larger than 180).
  • Between 5 and 6 or 6 and 7, check both sides of the hour hand for right angles — two answers usually exist.
  • Mirror time trick: subtract from 11:60, so 4:25 becomes 7:35.
  • A clock that gains or loses time: compare clock minutes with real minutes as a ratio and scale.

Worked examples

Example 1

Find the angle between the hands at 3:40.

Solution

θ = |30 × 3 − 5.5 × 40| = |90 − 220| = 130°. Intuitively, the minute hand is at 240° (the 8) and the hour hand has moved two-thirds of the way from the 3 towards the 4, i.e. to 110°; the gap is 130°.

Example 2

Find the angle between the hands at 7:20.

Solution

θ = |30 × 7 − 5.5 × 20| = |210 − 110| = 100°. Since 100° is less than 180°, it is already the smaller angle.

Example 3

At what time between 4 and 5 o’clock do the hands coincide?

Solution

At 4:00 the minute hand is 20 minute-spaces (120°) behind. It gains 5.5° a minute, so it needs 120/5.5 = 240/11 = 21 9/11 minutes. The hands coincide at 21 9/11 minutes past 4, not at 4:20, because the hour hand keeps moving.

Example 4

At what time between 2 and 3 o’clock are the hands at right angles (after the minute hand passes the hour hand)?

Solution

|60 − 5.5M| = 90 with the minute hand ahead: 5.5M = 150, so M = 300/11 = 27 3/11 minutes past 2.

Example 5

A clock reads 3:40. What time does its mirror image show?

Solution

Subtract from 11:60: 11:60 − 3:40 = 8:20. You can verify by noting that 3:40 + 8:20 = 12:00, which holds for every mirror pair.

Example 6

How many times do the hands of a clock coincide in a day?

Solution

11 times every 12 hours, so 22 times in 24 hours. The coincidence near 12 o’clock is shared between two 12-hour periods, which is why it is not 24.

Practice questions

Question 1

The angle between the hands of a clock at 5:30 is:

  1. 0°
  2. 30°
  3. 15°
  4. 45°
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 15°

θ = |30 × 5 − 5.5 × 30| = |150 − 165| = 15 degrees, as the hour hand is halfway past 5.

Question 2

The smaller angle between the hands at 9:15 is:

  1. 180°
  2. 187.5°
  3. 172.5°
  4. 165°
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 172.5°

|270 − 82.5| = 187.5°, which exceeds 180°, so the smaller angle is 360 − 187.5 = 172.5°.

Question 3

Between 2 and 3 o’clock, the hands coincide at:

  1. 10 minutes past 2
  2. 10 10/11 minutes past 2
  3. 11 minutes past 2
  4. 10 5/11 minutes past 2
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 10 10/11 minutes past 2

M = 60 × 2/11 = 120/11 = 10 10/11 minutes past 2, using the coincidence formula.

Question 4

How many times are the hands of a clock at right angles in 12 hours?

  1. 22
  2. 24
  3. 11
  4. 44
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 22

They are at right angles twice in most hours, but only 22 times in 12 hours because some instances are shared.

Question 5

A clock shows 4:25. Its mirror image shows:

  1. 8:35
  2. 7:25
  3. 8:25
  4. 7:35
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 7:35

Subtract from 11:60: 11:60 − 4:25 = 7:35, which is the time seen in a vertical mirror.

FAQs

Why do the hands coincide only 11 times in 12 hours?

Because the minute hand laps the hour hand once every 65 5/11 minutes, slightly more than an hour. In 720 minutes it completes 720 ÷ (720/11) = 11 laps. The coincidence at 12 o’clock counts once, not twice.

Is the angle formula valid for any time?

Yes. |30H − 5.5M| gives the angle between the hands for any hour H from 1 to 12 and minute M from 0 to 59. If the result is more than 180°, subtract it from 360° when the question asks for the smaller angle.
