Subject-Verb Agreement Rules with Exam-Style Sentences

Subject-verb agreement is behind a large share of error spotting and sentence improvement questions in SSC CGL, CHSL, IBPS and SBI exams. The basic idea is simple — a singular subject takes a singular verb and a plural subject a plural verb — but exam sentences hide the true subject behind phrases, collective nouns and connectors like 'as well as'. Knowing about a dozen special rules lets you catch most of these errors in seconds.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Find the real subject

Ignore phrases between the subject and the verb. In 'The quality of the mangoes is good', the subject is 'quality', not 'mangoes'. Prepositional phrases ('of the…', 'with the…') and clauses ('who…') never change the number of the subject.

Connectors that do not add

'As well as', 'along with', 'together with', 'with' and 'in addition to' do not make the subject plural. 'The manager, along with his team, was present' — the verb follows 'manager'.

Either/neither and or/nor

With 'either…or' and 'neither…nor', the verb agrees with the nearer subject: 'Neither the clerk nor the officers were present'; 'Neither the officers nor the clerk was present'. 'Either' and 'neither' alone take singular verbs.

Each, every and one of

'Each', 'every', 'everyone', 'somebody' and 'one of the…' take singular verbs: 'Each of the students has a laptop'; 'One of my friends is a banker'. But 'one of those who' usually takes a plural verb, since 'who' refers to the plural group.

Collective nouns and amounts

Collective nouns take a singular verb when acting as one unit ('The jury has reached a verdict') and plural when members act individually ('The jury were divided'). Amounts of money, time and distance as one quantity are singular: 'Five lakh rupees is a large sum.'

Repeating the article

When two nouns refer to different people or things, repeat the article; when they refer to one person, use it once. 'The secretary and the treasurer were present' means two people. 'The secretary and treasurer was present' means one person holding both posts, so the verb is singular. Exams test this together with subject-verb agreement, so check both the article and the verb in such sentences.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • 'The number of' + plural noun → singular verb; 'A number of' + plural noun → plural verb.
  • Two subjects joined by 'and' are plural, unless they describe one thing ('Bread and butter is my breakfast').
  • Nouns like news, mathematics, economics and politics (as a subject) take singular verbs.
  • Scissors, trousers and spectacles take plural verbs, but 'a pair of scissors' takes singular.
  • 'Many a' + singular noun takes a singular verb: 'Many a candidate has tried.'
  • In 'There is / There are', the verb agrees with the noun that follows.

Worked examples

Example 1

Correct the error: 'The quality of these products are excellent.'

Solution

The subject is 'quality' (singular); 'of these products' is a modifier. Correct: 'The quality of these products is excellent.'

Example 2

Correct the error: 'The chairman, along with the directors, have arrived.'

Solution

'Along with' does not add to the subject. Correct: 'The chairman, along with the directors, has arrived.'

Example 3

Correct the error: 'A number of customers has complained.'

Solution

'A number of' means many and takes a plural verb. Correct: 'A number of customers have complained.'

Example 4

Correct the error: 'Economics are my favourite subject.'

Solution

Subjects like economics take singular verbs. Correct: 'Economics is my favourite subject.'

Example 5

Correct the error: 'There is many reasons for the delay.'

Solution

The verb agrees with 'reasons', which is plural. Correct: 'There are many reasons for the delay.'

Practice questions

Question 1

Choose the correct verb: 'The number of applicants ______ increased this year.'

  1. have
  2. has
  3. are
  4. were
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. has

'The number of' refers to a single figure, so it takes a singular verb. 'The number of applicants has increased' is correct.

Question 2

Choose the correct verb: 'Either the manager or his assistants ______ responsible.'

  1. is
  2. was
  3. are
  4. has been
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. are

With either…or, the verb agrees with the nearer subject. 'Assistants' is plural, so 'are' is correct.

Question 3

Choose the correct verb: 'Each of the branches ______ a security guard.'

  1. have
  2. are having
  3. were having
  4. has
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. has

'Each' is singular and takes a singular verb. 'Each of the branches has a security guard' is the correct form.

Question 4

Choose the correct verb: 'Ten kilometres ______ a long distance to walk daily.'

  1. is
  2. are
  3. were
  4. have been
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. is

A distance considered as one quantity takes a singular verb. 'Ten kilometres is a long distance' is correct.

Question 5

Choose the correct verb: 'The news of the merger ______ surprised everyone.'

  1. have
  2. has
  3. are
  4. were
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. has

'News' is an uncountable noun that takes a singular verb, so 'has surprised' is correct despite the final 's'.

FAQs

Is 'Everyone have finished' ever correct?

No. 'Everyone', 'everybody', 'someone' and 'nobody' are grammatically singular and take singular verbs: 'Everyone has finished.' The pronoun that refers back to them is often 'their' in modern English, but the verb itself stays singular.

How should I treat collective nouns like 'team' or 'committee'?

In exam questions, use a singular verb when the group acts as a single unit and a plural verb when members act separately or disagree. If the sentence shows division ('The committee were divided in their opinions'), plural is correct; otherwise singular is safer.
