Remainder and Unit Digit Tricks with Cyclicity

Remainder and unit-digit questions are SSC CGL and CHSL favourites and appear regularly in CAT, CMAT and MAT. Bank exams use them less often, mainly in RBI Grade B and data sufficiency. They look intimidating because of huge powers like 7⁹⁵, but the work reduces to small numbers once you use cyclicity and the fact that remainders can be multiplied and reduced at every step.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Cyclicity of unit digits

The unit digit of powers repeats in cycles: 2 → 2, 4, 8, 6; 3 → 3, 9, 7, 1; 7 → 7, 9, 3, 1; 8 → 8, 4, 2, 6 (cycle 4). Digits 0, 1, 5, 6 always repeat themselves; 4 and 9 alternate with cycle 2. Divide the power by 4 and use the remainder (a remainder of 0 means the 4th term).

Remainders of products and sums

The remainder of a product equals the remainder of the product of remainders. For (17 × 23 × 29) ÷ 12, use 5 × 11 × 5 = 275, which leaves 11. The same works for sums. Negative remainders can simplify further: 11 ≡ −1 (mod 12).

(a × b) mod n = [(a mod n)(b mod n)] mod n

Remainders of powers

Find a small power that leaves remainder 1 (or −1), then break the large power into that block. Since 2³ = 8 leaves 1 when divided by 7, 2⁵⁰ = (2³)¹⁶ × 2² leaves 4. For division by 5, 2⁴ = 16 leaves 1.

Divisor-change questions

If a number leaves remainder r when divided by D, and d is a factor of D, the remainder on division by d is r mod d. A number that leaves 75 on division by 296 leaves 75 mod 37 = 1 on division by 37, because 296 = 8 × 37.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Unit digit of a product depends only on the unit digits of the factors.
  • An even base not ending in 0, raised to a multiple of 4, ends in 6 (2¹⁰⁰, 8⁴⁰); powers of 4 end in 6 for any even exponent, such as 4¹⁰².
  • From 5! onward, factorials end in 0, so the unit digit of 1! + 2! + … + n! (n ≥ 4) is 3.
  • (aⁿ − bⁿ) is divisible by (a − b) always and by (a + b) when n is even.
  • Remainder of xⁿ ÷ (x − 1) is always 1; of xⁿ ÷ (x + 1) is 1 if n is even and x if n is odd.

Worked examples

Example 1

Find the unit digit of 7⁹⁵.

Solution

Cycle of 7: 7, 9, 3, 1. 95 ÷ 4 leaves remainder 3, so the unit digit is the 3rd term, 3.

Example 2

Find the unit digit of 2⁴³ × 3²².

Solution

2⁴³: 43 mod 4 = 3, unit digit 8. 3²²: 22 mod 4 = 2, unit digit 9. 8 × 9 = 72, so the unit digit is 2.

Example 3

Find the remainder when 2³¹ is divided by 5.

Solution

2⁴ = 16 leaves 1. 2³¹ = (2⁴)⁷ × 2³, so the remainder is 1 × 8 = 8, which leaves 3 on division by 5.

Example 4

A number leaves a remainder of 75 when divided by 296. What remainder will it leave when divided by 37?

Solution

296 = 8 × 37, so the remainder is 75 mod 37 = 1.

Example 5

Find the remainder when 17 × 23 × 29 is divided by 12.

Solution

Remainders are 5, 11, 5. 5 × 11 × 5 = 275 and 275 = 22 × 12 + 11, so the remainder is 11.

Practice questions

Question 1

The unit digit of 3⁶⁵ is:

  1. 9
  2. 3
  3. 7
  4. 1
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 3

The cycle of 3 is 3, 9, 7, 1; 65 leaves remainder 1 when divided by 4, so the unit digit is 3.

Question 2

The unit digit of 4¹⁰² is:

  1. 4
  2. 2
  3. 8
  4. 6
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 6

Powers of 4 end in 4 for odd exponents and 6 for even exponents, and 102 is even, so the unit digit is 6.

Question 3

The remainder when 2⁵⁰ is divided by 7 is:

  1. 1
  2. 4
  3. 2
  4. 3
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 4

2³ leaves 1 on division by 7, and 2⁵⁰ = (2³)¹⁶ × 2², so the remainder is 4.

Question 4

A number leaves remainder 39 when divided by 357. What is the remainder when it is divided by 17?

  1. 3
  2. 39
  3. 5
  4. 22
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 5

Since 357 = 21 × 17, the remainder is 39 mod 17 = 5, as 39 = 2 × 17 + 5.

Question 5

The unit digit of 1! + 2! + 3! + … + 50! is:

  1. 3
  2. 0
  3. 9
  4. 1
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 3

Factorials from 5! end in 0, so only 1 + 2 + 6 + 24 = 33 matters, giving a unit digit of 3.

FAQs

Why is the cycle length 4 for most digits?

Because multiplying the last digit repeatedly by the same digit can produce at most four different endings before repeating, for digits 2, 3, 7 and 8. Some digits repeat faster: 4 and 9 have cycle 2, and 0, 1, 5, 6 have cycle 1.

Can I use negative remainders?

Yes, they often simplify work. A remainder of 11 when dividing by 12 can be treated as −1, so 11⁵⁰ divided by 12 leaves (−1)⁵⁰ = 1. Convert back to a positive remainder at the end if the result is negative.
