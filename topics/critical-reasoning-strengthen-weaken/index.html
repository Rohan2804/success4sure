Critical Reasoning: Strengthen and Weaken the Argument

Strengthen and weaken questions give a short argument and ask which option makes it more convincing or less convincing. They are common in RBI Grade B Phase I, IBPS PO and SBI PO mains, and in CAT, CMAT and MAT verbal-logical sections. Each argument has a conclusion, some evidence, and an unstated gap between them. Correct answers almost always target that gap: a strengthener closes it, a weakener widens it.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Break the argument into parts

Find the conclusion first — often signalled by 'therefore', 'so', 'hence' or placed as the last sentence. Then identify the evidence. Finally ask: what must be true for this evidence to prove this conclusion? That missing link is the gap.

How strengtheners work

A strengthener supports the gap, for example by ruling out another explanation or showing the sample is representative. It does not need to prove the conclusion; it only needs to make it more likely.

How weakeners work

A weakener offers an alternative cause, shows the evidence is unrepresentative, or points out that the plan will not achieve its goal. It does not need to disprove the conclusion completely.

Out-of-scope options

Options that discuss a related topic but do not touch the conclusion–evidence link are traps. So are options that merely repeat the evidence, which neither strengthen nor weaken.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Cause-effect arguments ('X caused Y') are weakened by another cause for Y and strengthened by ruling other causes out.
  • Survey-based arguments are weakened by showing the sample was small or biased.
  • Plan-based arguments ('Do X to achieve Y') are weakened by showing X will not lead to Y or will create bigger problems.
  • Prefer options that address the exact group and time period in the argument.
  • If you cannot find the gap, test each option with 'Does this make the conclusion more or less likely?'

Worked examples

Example 1

Argument: 'Since the bank opened a branch in the village last year, the number of savings accounts there has doubled. So the branch caused more villagers to open accounts.' Which option weakens the argument most? (a) The branch has friendly staff. (b) In the same year, a government scheme offered zero-balance accounts with direct benefit transfers to every household in the village. (c) Many villagers work in agriculture.

Solution

(b) gives an alternative cause for the rise in accounts, which directly attacks the causal claim. (a) slightly supports the branch's role, and (c) is irrelevant. Answer: (b).

Example 2

Argument: 'Students who took our online mock tests scored higher in the final exam than those who did not. So the mock tests improve scores.' Which option strengthens the argument? (a) Mock tests are free. (b) Before taking the tests, both groups had similar scores in a diagnostic test. (c) Some students took the tests late at night.

Solution

The gap is that better students might simply choose to take mock tests. (b) shows the groups started at the same level, ruling out that alternative. Answer: (b).

Example 3

Argument: 'The city should widen its main road to reduce traffic jams.' Which option weakens the plan? (a) Studies of similar cities show that widened roads quickly attract more vehicles, and congestion returns within a few years. (b) Widening the road will need some trees to be cut. (c) Most residents own two-wheelers.

Solution

(a) shows the plan may not achieve its goal of reducing jams, which is the strongest weakener. (b) is a cost, but it does not show the plan fails. (c) is unrelated. Answer: (a).

Practice questions

Question 1

Argument: 'Sales of umbrellas in a town rose sharply in July. Therefore, the new advertising campaign launched in July increased umbrella sales.' Which option weakens it most?

  1. July is the peak monsoon month in that town and rainfall was heavy
  2. The campaign used local celebrities
  3. Umbrellas come in many colours
  4. The shops stayed open late in July
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. July is the peak monsoon month in that town and rainfall was heavy

Heavy monsoon rain is an alternative explanation for the rise in sales. It directly challenges the claim that the campaign caused the increase.

Question 2

Argument: 'Branches that introduced token systems have shorter customer waiting times, so token systems reduce waiting time.' Which option strengthens it?

  1. Token machines are expensive
  2. Waiting times at these branches fell right after tokens were introduced, with no change in staff numbers
  3. Some customers dislike tokens
  4. Token systems are common in hospitals
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Waiting times at these branches fell right after tokens were introduced, with no change in staff numbers

The option links the fall in waiting time to the token system and rules out an increase in staff as another cause, strengthening the argument.

Question 3

Argument: 'A survey of 50 people at a gym found that 90% prefer protein bars to biscuits. So most Indians prefer protein bars.' Which option weakens it most?

  1. Protein bars are sold online
  2. Biscuits are cheaper
  3. Gym members are not representative of the general population
  4. The survey was done in the morning
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Gym members are not representative of the general population

The conclusion generalises from a gym sample to all Indians. Showing the sample is unrepresentative attacks that gap directly.

Question 4

Argument: 'Schools should start later in the morning because teenagers who sleep more perform better in class.' Which option strengthens it?

  1. Some teachers prefer early starts
  2. School buses run on fixed routes
  3. Teenagers enjoy weekends
  4. In schools that shifted to later starts, students slept more on average
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. In schools that shifted to later starts, students slept more on average

The plan assumes later starts lead to more sleep. Evidence that students actually slept more after later starts closes that gap.

Question 5

Argument: 'The new metro line will reduce road traffic because many car users will switch to the metro.' Which option weakens it?

  1. Most car users in the city travel on routes the metro does not cover
  2. The metro has air-conditioned coaches
  3. Metro tickets can be bought with UPI
  4. The metro line was completed on time
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Most car users in the city travel on routes the metro does not cover

If the metro does not serve the routes car users take, few will switch. That undermines the assumption the plan depends on.

FAQs

Does a weakener need to prove the conclusion false?

No. A weakener only needs to make the conclusion less likely. Showing another possible cause or a biased sample is enough. Options that try to completely disprove the conclusion are often too extreme or go beyond what the argument discusses.

How are these questions different in CAT and bank exams?

CAT arguments are longer and the options are closer to each other, so you must read the conclusion precisely. Bank and RBI papers use shorter, everyday arguments, often with Indian contexts like banking, transport or schemes. The method — find the gap, then target it — is the same.
