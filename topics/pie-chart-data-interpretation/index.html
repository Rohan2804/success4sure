Pie Chart DI: Percentages, Degrees and Shortcut Methods

Pie charts show how a whole is divided into parts — a family budget, a company’s expenses or students by stream. IBPS PO, SBI PO, RBI Grade B and SSC CGL Tier 2 regularly include pie-chart sets, sometimes two pies side by side. The arithmetic is lighter than in bar or line graphs, because every value is a share of one total, so speed comes from working with percentages instead of converting everything to amounts.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Two formats

A pie may be labelled in percentages (adding to 100%) or in degrees (adding to 360°). Convert with: percentage = degrees × 100/360 and degrees = percentage × 3.6. A 108° sector is 30% of the whole; a 15% sector is 54°.

Degrees = % × 3.6

Amounts from shares

Value of a sector = share × total. With a ₹60,000 monthly budget, a 25% rent share is ₹15,000. With 720 students and a 120° sector, the sector has 720 × 120/360 = 240 students.

Sector value = total × (degrees/360)

Compare shares, not amounts

When both sectors belong to the same pie, their ratio equals the ratio of their percentages or degrees. Education 15% and transport 10% are in the ratio 3 : 2 without computing rupee amounts. Differences can likewise be computed as (difference in %) × total.

Pie linked to another chart

Some sets give the total in a table or bar graph and the break-up in a pie. Find the total for the relevant year from the first chart, then apply the pie’s percentage. Note which year or company the pie refers to; questions may ask you to combine figures across both charts.

Two pies

When two pies have different totals (two years or two companies), you cannot compare percentages directly. Convert each relevant sector to an actual amount using its own total before comparing.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • 1% of a ₹60,000 budget is ₹600; scale from there instead of multiplying each time.
  • 10° in a pie equals 2.78%; 36° = 10%; 18° = 5%; 72° = 20%; 90° = 25%.
  • If the total changes but shares stay fixed, every sector changes by the same percentage.
  • A sector’s central angle is never needed for amounts if percentages are given — skip the degree conversion.

Worked examples

Example 1

A family’s monthly income of ₹60,000 is spent as: rent 25%, food 30%, education 15%, transport 10%, savings 20%. How much is spent on rent?

Solution

25% of 60,000 = 60,000 ÷ 4 = ₹15,000. Converting 25% to the fraction 1/4 makes this a one-step division.

Example 2

What is the central angle of the food sector?

Solution

Every 1% of the pie is 3.6°, so 30% corresponds to 30 × 3.6 = 108°. Check: the five sectors (90°, 108°, 54°, 36°, 72°) add to 360°.

Example 3

By how much do savings exceed transport spending?

Solution

Work with the difference in shares: (20% − 10%) × 60,000 = 10% of 60,000 = ₹6,000. Computing savings (₹12,000) and transport (₹6,000) separately and subtracting gives the same answer but takes longer.

Example 4

Find the ratio of education to transport spending.

Solution

15 : 10 = 3 : 2. No rupee calculation is needed.

Example 5

If income rises by 10% and the percentages stay the same, how much will be spent on food?

Solution

New income = ₹66,000. Food = 30% × 66,000 = ₹19,800, which is also 10% more than the earlier ₹18,000.

Example 6

What is the combined central angle of rent and food, and what fraction of income do they take together?

Solution

Rent 25% + food 30% = 55% of income. Central angle = 55 × 3.6 = 198°, more than a semicircle. As an amount, 55% of ₹60,000 = ₹33,000, leaving ₹27,000 for the other three heads.

Practice questions

Question 1

720 students are split by stream: Commerce 120°, Science 90°, Arts 60°, Management 54°, Others 36°. How many study Commerce?

  1. 200
  2. 280
  3. 120
  4. 240
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 240

Commerce students = 720 × 120/360 = 720 ÷ 3 = 240 students.

Question 2

Using the same data, Science students form what percentage of the total?

  1. 20%
  2. 25%
  3. 30%
  4. 22.5%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 25%

90° out of 360° is one quarter of the circle, which equals 25 percent of all students.

Question 3

Using the same data, how many more students are in Commerce than in Arts?

  1. 60
  2. 180
  3. 120
  4. 100
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 120

The difference is 60° of 360°, i.e. one-sixth of 720, which is 120 students.

Question 4

Using the same data, Management students form what percentage of the total?

  1. 12%
  2. 18%
  3. 54%
  4. 15%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 15%

54° ÷ 360° × 100 = 15 percent, so 108 of the 720 students are in Management.

Question 5

Using the same data, the ratio of Science to Others is:

  1. 3 : 2
  2. 5 : 2
  3. 9 : 4
  4. 2 : 1
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 5 : 2

90° : 36° simplifies to 5 : 2 by dividing both angles by 18°.

FAQs

Do I need to convert degrees to percentages in every question?

No. For ratios and comparisons within the same pie, degrees work just as well as percentages. Convert only when you need an actual amount or when the options are in percentages. Skipping unnecessary conversions saves time across a five-question set.

How do I compare sectors from two pies with different totals?

Convert each sector to an actual value using its own pie’s total, then compare. For example, 20% of ₹50 lakh (₹10 lakh) is larger than 25% of ₹36 lakh (₹9 lakh), even though 25% is the bigger share.
