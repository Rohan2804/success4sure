Synonyms and Antonyms Strategy: Roots, Context and Traps

Synonym and antonym questions are a fixed part of SSC CGL, CHSL and MTS English, usually two to four questions per paper, and they appear in bank exams inside reading comprehension as vocabulary-in-context questions. Since no one can memorise the whole dictionary, the aim is to learn high-frequency exam words and to use roots, prefixes and context to handle unfamiliar ones. The biggest trap is a look-alike word that sounds similar but means something different.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Roots and prefixes

Many difficult words break down. 'Bene' means good (benevolent: kind), 'mal' means bad (malicious: intending harm), 'chron' means time (chronic: lasting a long time), 'loqu' means speak (loquacious: talkative). Prefixes like 'in-', 'un-', 'dis-' and 'mis-' often signal opposites.

Charge of the word

Even if you do not know the exact meaning, you often know whether a word feels positive or negative. 'Pernicious' sounds negative; its synonym must also be negative ('harmful'), and its antonym positive ('beneficial').

Context sentences

When the word is given in a sentence, the sentence often defines it. 'The manager was frugal, spending only on essentials' shows frugal means economical. Antonym: extravagant.

Look-alike traps

Exam options often include a word that looks or sounds like the question word: 'eminent' (distinguished) versus 'imminent' (about to happen), 'affect' versus 'effect', 'complement' versus 'compliment'. Read the options slowly.

Best fit, not any fit

More than one option may be loosely related. Choose the one closest in meaning and in strength: the synonym of 'furious' is 'enraged', not 'annoyed', which is milder.

Building a word bank that sticks

Keep a notebook or spreadsheet with four columns: word, meaning, synonym, antonym, plus one sentence you wrote yourself. Add words from editorials, mock tests and wrong answers. Review the list in short daily sessions and mark words you still miss. Words you write in your own sentence are remembered far longer than words you only read in a list, and the antonym column trains both question types together.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • For antonyms, first find a synonym in your head, then look for its opposite among the options.
  • Words ending in '-ous' and '-ive' are adjectives; the answer will usually be an adjective too.
  • If an option is a synonym in an antonym question, eliminate it immediately; setters add one to catch hurried readers.
  • Keep a list of high-frequency exam words: abate, alleviate, candid, cordial, diligent, frugal, lucid, obsolete, prudent, tranquil.
  • Learn words in pairs of opposites: lucid–obscure, prudent–reckless, cordial–hostile, abundant–scarce.
  • When stuck between two options, pick the one used in the same context as the question word.

Worked examples

Example 1

Choose the synonym of 'BENEVOLENT': cruel, kind, angry, careful.

Solution

'Bene' means good; benevolent means well-meaning and kind. Answer: kind.

Example 2

Choose the antonym of 'FRUGAL': thrifty, economical, extravagant, careful.

Solution

Frugal means spending little. 'Thrifty' and 'economical' are synonyms, a deliberate trap. The opposite is 'extravagant'. Answer: extravagant.

Example 3

Choose the synonym of 'IMMINENT': famous, about to happen, ancient, unclear.

Solution

'Imminent' means about to happen, as in 'imminent rain'. 'Famous' is the meaning of the look-alike 'eminent'. Answer: about to happen.

Example 4

Choose the antonym of 'ALLEVIATE': ease, relieve, aggravate, reduce.

Solution

Alleviate means to make less severe. 'Ease', 'relieve' and 'reduce' are synonyms. The opposite, to make worse, is 'aggravate'. Answer: aggravate.

Example 5

In 'Her candid reply surprised the interview panel', what does 'candid' mean?

Solution

'Candid' means frank and honest. The surprise suggests she spoke openly rather than giving a rehearsed answer. Synonym: frank.

Example 6

Choose the antonym of 'LUCID': clear, obscure, bright, simple.

Solution

Lucid means clear and easy to understand. 'Clear' and 'simple' are close in meaning, and 'bright' relates to light. The opposite, meaning unclear or hard to understand, is 'obscure'. Answer: obscure.

Practice questions

Question 1

Choose the synonym of 'DILIGENT'.

  1. Hard-working
  2. Lazy
  3. Clever
  4. Careless
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Hard-working

Diligent describes someone who works with steady care and effort, so 'hard-working' is the closest synonym. 'Lazy' and 'careless' are opposites.

Question 2

Choose the antonym of 'ABUNDANT'.

  1. Plentiful
  2. Scarce
  3. Ample
  4. Rich
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Scarce

Abundant means existing in large quantities. 'Plentiful' and 'ample' are synonyms, while 'scarce', meaning in short supply, is the opposite.

Question 3

Choose the synonym of 'OBSOLETE'.

  1. Modern
  2. Popular
  3. Outdated
  4. Expensive
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Outdated

Obsolete means no longer in use or out of date, as with old technology. 'Outdated' expresses the same idea.

Question 4

Choose the antonym of 'CORDIAL'.

  1. Warm
  2. Friendly
  3. Polite
  4. Hostile
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Hostile

Cordial means warm and friendly. The opposite, describing unfriendly and aggressive behaviour, is 'hostile'.

Question 5

Choose the synonym of 'PRUDENT'.

  1. Sensible
  2. Reckless
  3. Hasty
  4. Proud
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Sensible

Prudent means acting with care and thought for the future. 'Sensible' is closest; 'proud' is a look-alike trap with a different meaning.

FAQs

How many words should I learn for SSC synonyms and antonyms?

There is no fixed list, but learning around 800 to 1,000 high-frequency exam words, with their opposites and an example sentence, covers a large share of questions. Revise them in small daily sets and test yourself weekly; spaced revision works better than a single long session.

Are roots and prefixes reliable for unfamiliar words?

They give a strong clue, but not a guarantee, because some words have drifted from their roots. Use roots together with the word's charge and any context sentence. When all three point the same way, you can answer with reasonable confidence.
