Line Graph DI: Reading Trends and Solving Questions

Line graphs show how values change over time, and bank exams love them for that reason. IBPS PO, SBI PO and RBI Grade B prelims and mains often include a line-graph set, frequently with two or three lines plotted together, such as exports and imports or sales of two products. SSC CGL Tier 2 and CAT also use them. Questions ask about trends, the gap between lines and growth rates.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Slope tells the story

A steeper segment means a larger absolute change. However, percentage change depends on the starting level, so a gentler slope from a low base can be a bigger percentage rise. Never judge percentage growth by eye alone.

Gap between two lines

With two series, the vertical gap at each point is their difference — a trade surplus, a profit or a shortfall. The year with the widest gap has the largest difference. When lines cross, the sign of the difference changes.

Trend questions

Questions such as "in how many years did X fall" or "which month showed a decrease" need only the direction of each segment. Mark the up and down segments once and reuse the result for multiple questions.

Reading two scales

Some line graphs use a second vertical axis on the right for one of the series, often for percentages. Check which line belongs to which axis before reading values, because the same height can mean very different numbers on the two scales.

Averages and ratios over periods

For the average over several years, add the points and divide by the number of points. For ratios of totals, add each series separately first; the ratio of totals is not the average of yearly ratios.

Ratio of totals = ΣA / ΣB

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Tabulate the points of each line before answering — a 30-second investment for five questions.
  • Percentage-point change (from 20% to 25%) is not the same as percentage change (25% rise).
  • If a value rises from 60 to 72, the change is 20%; from 72 back to 60 is a 16.67% fall — bases differ.
  • Use the steepest segment as a first guess for "largest increase" and confirm with numbers.

Worked examples

Example 1

Exports and imports of a firm (₹ crore), 2019–2023: exports 40, 45, 50, 60, 72; imports 30, 42, 45, 48, 54. Find the trade surplus each year and the year with the highest surplus.

Solution

Surplus = exports − imports for each year: 40 − 30 = 10, 45 − 42 = 3, 50 − 45 = 5, 60 − 48 = 12 and 72 − 54 = 18. The highest is ₹18 crore in 2023, where the two lines are furthest apart on the graph.

Example 2

Using the same data, find the percentage growth in exports from 2022 to 2023.

Solution

Increase = 72 − 60 = 12 on a base of 60, so (12/60) × 100 = 20%. Note that the same ₹12 crore rise from a base of 72 would be only 16.67%, which is why the base year matters.

Example 3

Find the average imports over the five years.

Solution

Total = 30 + 42 + 45 + 48 + 54 = 219. Average = 219/5 = ₹43.8 crore.

Example 4

Find the ratio of total exports to total imports over the period.

Solution

Total exports = 267, total imports = 219. Ratio = 267 : 219 = 89 : 73 (dividing by 3).

Example 5

In which year was the surplus the smallest, and why might the graph mislead here?

Solution

The surplus is smallest in 2020 (₹3 crore). Both lines rose that year, but imports jumped by 12 while exports rose only 5, so the lines came close together even though exports grew.

Practice questions

Question 1

Enrolments (hundreds), Jan–Jun: 12, 15, 18, 16, 20, 24. Percentage increase from January to June is:

  1. 100%
  2. 50%
  3. 12%
  4. 200%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 100%

Enrolment doubles from 12 to 24 hundred, and a doubling is a 100 percent increase.

Question 2

Using the same data, the average monthly enrolment is:

  1. 1,700
  2. 1,800
  3. 1,750
  4. 1,650
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 1,750

Total = 105 hundred over 6 months, so the average is 17.5 hundred, i.e. 1,750 students.

Question 3

Using the same data, in which month did enrolment fall compared with the previous month?

  1. March
  2. May
  3. February
  4. April
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. April

Only April shows a decrease, from 18 hundred in March to 16 hundred.

Question 4

Using the same data, the percentage decrease from March to April is about:

  1. 12.5%
  2. 10%
  3. 11.11%
  4. 2%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 11.11%

Decrease = 2 on a base of 18, which is 2/18 = 1/9, about 11.11 percent.

Question 5

Using the same data, the ratio of February to May enrolment is:

  1. 3 : 4
  2. 4 : 5
  3. 3 : 5
  4. 5 : 6
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 3 : 4

February has 15 and May has 20 hundred, and 15 : 20 simplifies to 3 : 4.

FAQs

Can I estimate values that fall between grid lines?

Bank exam line graphs usually mark exact values at each point. If a value is not labelled, estimate carefully from the grid and choose the option closest to your estimate. Avoid questions whose answer hinges on a tiny reading difference.

What is the difference between percentage change and percentage-point change?

If a pass rate moves from 20% to 25%, it rose by 5 percentage points but by 25 percent of its earlier value. DI questions use both phrasings, and options often include both numbers, so read the wording closely.
