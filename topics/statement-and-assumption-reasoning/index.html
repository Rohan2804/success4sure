Statement and Assumption: Spotting Implicit Assumptions

Statement and assumption questions are part of the critical reasoning family tested in IBPS PO and SBI PO mains, RBI Grade B, and MBA entrances such as CMAT and MAT. A short statement — an advertisement, a notice, a policy announcement — is followed by two or three assumptions, and you decide which are implicit, meaning the speaker must have taken them for granted. There is no calculation, but there is a method, and it removes most of the guesswork.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

What 'implicit' means

An assumption is implicit if the statement would not make sense without it. It is not something stated, and it is not a conclusion drawn afterwards. It is the unspoken belief that made the speaker say or do what they did.

The negation test

Reverse the assumption and see if the statement still makes sense. Statement: 'The bank will open on Saturday to help customers update KYC.' Assumption: 'Customers will come on Saturday.' Negated: 'Customers will not come on Saturday.' Then opening would be pointless, so the assumption is implicit.

Extreme and comparative words

Assumptions with 'only', 'all', 'always', 'best' or 'never' are usually not implicit, because the statement rarely needs such a strong belief. 'Use our toothpaste for whiter teeth' assumes people want whiter teeth, not that no other toothpaste whitens teeth.

Advertisements and appeals

Any advertisement, appeal or notice assumes that people will read it and that it will have some effect. Government notices assume the instruction is feasible for those it addresses.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Ask 'Why would the speaker say this?' The answer is usually the implicit assumption.
  • If an assumption brings in new information that the statement does not touch, it is not implicit.
  • Two contradictory assumptions cannot both be implicit, though both can be non-implicit.
  • A suggested action assumes the action is possible and will help.
  • Be careful with 'some' and 'many': a moderate assumption is far more likely to be implicit than an absolute one.

Worked examples

Example 1

Statement: 'Use BrightSmile toothpaste for whiter teeth.' Assumptions: I. People want whiter teeth. II. No other toothpaste can whiten teeth.

Solution

An advertiser promises whiter teeth only because people value them, so I is implicit. II is extreme — the advert does not need other toothpastes to fail. Only I is implicit.

Example 2

Statement: 'The bank has decided to keep its branches open on the second Saturday so that customers can update their KYC before the deadline.' Assumptions: I. Many customers have not yet updated KYC. II. Some customers will visit on that Saturday.

Solution

Opening on a holiday makes sense only if many updates are pending (I) and customers will actually come (II). Negating either makes the decision pointless. Both are implicit.

Example 3

Statement: Exam notice — 'Candidates must carry their Aadhaar card to the examination centre.' Assumptions: I. Candidates generally possess an Aadhaar card. II. Aadhaar is the only valid identity document in India.

Solution

A notice requiring Aadhaar assumes candidates can produce it, so I is implicit. II is an absolute claim the notice does not need; it only sets a rule for this exam. Only I is implicit.

Example 4

Statement: 'If you want to score well in reasoning, practise one puzzle set every day.' Assumptions: I. Regular practice improves performance in puzzles. II. Only candidates who practise daily score well.

Solution

The advice exists because practice helps, so I is implicit. II uses 'only' and is far stronger than the advice requires. Only I is implicit.

Practice questions

Question 1

Statement: 'The city municipal corporation has asked residents to store water as supply will be cut for two days.' Assumptions: I. Residents have some means of storing water. II. The supply cut will last for more than a week.

  1. Only II is implicit
  2. Both are implicit
  3. Only I is implicit
  4. Neither is implicit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Only I is implicit

The request only makes sense if residents can store water, so I is implicit. II contradicts the statement, which mentions two days, so it is not implicit.

Question 2

Statement: 'Apply before 31 March to get the early-bird discount on the course.' Assumptions: I. Some people are influenced by discounts. II. Everyone applies on the last day.

  1. Neither is implicit
  2. Both are implicit
  3. Only II is implicit
  4. Only I is implicit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Only I is implicit

Offering an early discount assumes that discounts affect behaviour, so I is implicit. II is an absolute generalisation that the offer does not depend on.

Question 3

Statement: 'The government has launched a toll-free helpline for farmers to get crop advisory in regional languages.' Assumptions: I. Farmers prefer advice in their own language. II. Some farmers have access to a phone.

  1. Both are implicit
  2. Only I is implicit
  3. Only II is implicit
  4. Neither is implicit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Both are implicit

Offering regional-language advice assumes language preference matters, and a phone helpline assumes farmers can call. Negating either makes the scheme pointless, so both are implicit.

Question 4

Statement: 'Read this newspaper to stay ahead in banking awareness.' Assumptions: I. This is the only newspaper that covers banking news. II. Every reader is preparing for a bank exam.

  1. Only I is implicit
  2. Neither is implicit
  3. Only II is implicit
  4. Both are implicit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Neither is implicit

I uses 'only' and II uses 'every', and the advertisement needs neither absolute claim. It merely assumes some readers value banking awareness.

Question 5

Statement: 'Passengers are requested to keep their tickets until they exit the station.' Assumptions: I. Tickets may be checked at the exit. II. Some passengers throw away tickets early.

  1. Only I is implicit
  2. Only II is implicit
  3. Both are implicit
  4. Neither is implicit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Both are implicit

The request makes sense only if checks can happen at the exit and if some passengers might discard tickets early. Both beliefs underlie the notice.

FAQs

How is an assumption different from a conclusion?

An assumption is what the speaker believed before making the statement; a conclusion is what logically follows after it. For 'Carry an umbrella, it may rain', the assumption is that an umbrella protects from rain, while a conclusion might be that the speaker expects some chance of rain.

Should I use outside knowledge in assumption questions?

Use only common sense and widely accepted facts, not specialised knowledge or personal opinion. The test is whether the statement depends on the assumption, not whether the assumption is true in the real world. A true but unrelated fact is still not an implicit assumption.
