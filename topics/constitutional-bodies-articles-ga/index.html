Constitutional Bodies and Key Articles for GA Sections

Constitutional bodies and important Articles appear in SSC CGL, CHSL and MTS General Awareness almost every year, and in RBI Grade B and bank mains through questions on financial bodies like the Finance Commission and GST Council. The Article numbers and functions of these bodies change only through constitutional amendments, so they are reliable static marks. Pair each body with its Article and one key function, and most questions become simple recall.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Financial bodies

Finance Commission (Article 280): constituted every five years or earlier by the President to recommend how central taxes are shared with states and among states. Comptroller and Auditor General (Article 148): audits the accounts of the Union and states. GST Council (Article 279A, added by the 101st Amendment, 2016): recommends GST rates and rules; chaired by the Union Finance Minister.

Electoral and service bodies

Election Commission (Article 324): superintends elections to Parliament, state legislatures and the offices of President and Vice-President. Union Public Service Commission (Article 315 onwards): conducts recruitment to all-India and central services. State Public Service Commissions are also covered from Article 315.

Law officers

Attorney General of India (Article 76): the highest law officer of the Union government. Advocate General of a state (Article 165): the state's highest law officer.

Commissions for specific groups

National Commission for Scheduled Castes (Article 338), National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (Article 338A) and National Commission for Backward Classes (Article 338B, given constitutional status by the 102nd Amendment).

Financial provisions

Article 110: definition of Money Bill. Article 112: Annual Financial Statement (budget). Article 266: Consolidated Fund. Article 267: Contingency Fund. Article 360: financial emergency. Article 263: Inter-State Council.

Statutory versus constitutional

Constitutional bodies are created by the Constitution itself, such as the Election Commission and the CAG. Statutory bodies are created by Acts of Parliament, such as SEBI and the National Human Rights Commission. Some bodies are neither: NITI Aayog was created in 2015 by an executive resolution of the Union Cabinet. Exams often ask which category a body belongs to.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • 280 = Finance Commission; 148 = CAG; 324 = Election Commission; 315 = UPSC.
  • 279A = GST Council (101st Amendment); 76 = Attorney General.
  • 338, 338A, 338B = SC, ST and Backward Classes commissions.
  • 110 Money Bill, 112 Budget, 266 Consolidated Fund, 267 Contingency Fund, 360 Financial Emergency.
  • NITI Aayog: executive resolution (2015), replacing the Planning Commission.
  • Link each Article with one function; recall improves when facts are paired.

Worked examples

Example 1

Which constitutional body recommends the share of states in central taxes?

Solution

The Finance Commission under Article 280. Its recommendations cover vertical sharing (centre to states) and horizontal sharing (among states).

Example 2

Who chairs the GST Council, and under which Article is it constituted?

Solution

The Union Finance Minister chairs it. It is constituted under Article 279A, inserted by the 101st Constitutional Amendment, 2016.

Example 3

Is NITI Aayog a constitutional body?

Solution

No. It was set up in 2015 by an executive resolution of the Union Cabinet, replacing the Planning Commission. It is neither constitutional nor statutory.

Example 4

Which Article deals with financial emergency?

Solution

Article 360. It allows the President to proclaim a financial emergency if the financial stability or credit of India is threatened. It has never been used so far.

Example 5

Classify as constitutional or statutory: Election Commission, SEBI, UPSC, National Green Tribunal.

Solution

The Election Commission (Article 324) and UPSC (Article 315) are constitutional. SEBI (SEBI Act, 1992) and the National Green Tribunal (NGT Act, 2010) are statutory, created by Acts of Parliament.

Practice questions

Question 1

Under which Article is the Finance Commission constituted?

  1. Article 148
  2. Article 280
  3. Article 324
  4. Article 112
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Article 280

Article 280 provides for the Finance Commission, which recommends the distribution of tax revenues between the centre and the states.

Question 2

Which Article provides for the Comptroller and Auditor General of India?

  1. Article 76
  2. Article 315
  3. Article 148
  4. Article 263
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Article 148

Article 148 establishes the office of the Comptroller and Auditor General, who audits government accounts.

Question 3

The GST Council was created by inserting which Article?

  1. Article 246
  2. Article 269
  3. Article 300A
  4. Article 279A
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Article 279A

The 101st Constitutional Amendment, 2016 inserted Article 279A to create the GST Council, chaired by the Union Finance Minister.

Question 4

Which Article deals with the Election Commission?

  1. Article 324
  2. Article 280
  3. Article 360
  4. Article 338
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Article 324

Article 324 vests the superintendence, direction and control of elections in the Election Commission of India.

Question 5

How was NITI Aayog created?

  1. By a constitutional amendment
  2. By an executive resolution
  3. By an Act of Parliament
  4. By an RBI notification
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. By an executive resolution

NITI Aayog was set up in 2015 through a resolution of the Union Cabinet. It is therefore neither a constitutional nor a statutory body.

FAQs

How many Articles should I memorise for SSC GA?

Focus on 40 to 60 high-frequency Articles: fundamental rights (12–35), directive principles basics, key offices (President, Prime Minister, Parliament), financial provisions and constitutional bodies. Learning them in groups with one function each is more effective than memorising a long numbered list.

Are commission heads and members asked?

Names of current heads, such as the Chief Election Commissioner or the chair of the current Finance Commission, are asked as current affairs. Note them separately from official sources close to your exam date, since they change over time.
