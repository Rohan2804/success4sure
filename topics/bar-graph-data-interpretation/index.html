Bar Graph Data Interpretation: Method and Practice Set

Bar graphs are the most common DI format in IBPS PO, IBPS Clerk, SBI PO and RBI Grade B prelims, where a five-question set is typical, and they appear in SSC CGL Tier 2 and CAT as well. The questions themselves are arithmetic — totals, averages, percentage change and ratios — but they reward a disciplined process: read the axis, note the units, and pull out only the bars each question needs.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Read before you calculate

Check the title, the unit (hundreds, thousands, ₹ crore) and whether bars are simple, grouped or stacked. In a stacked bar, the height of the whole bar is the total and each segment is a part; in a grouped bar, each colour is a separate series. Misreading the unit is the most common silent error.

Core calculations

Most questions ask for one of five things: a total, an average, a percentage change, a ratio, or one value as a percentage of another. Percentage change = (new − old)/old × 100. "A as a percentage of B" = A/B × 100. Ratios should be reduced to lowest terms.

% change = (new − old) / old × 100

Highest growth questions

When asked which year had the highest percentage increase, compare (increase ÷ previous value) for each year, not the raw increase. A jump of 45 on a base of 135 (33%) beats a jump of 30 on a base of 180 (17%).

Stacked and grouped bars

In a stacked bar, find a segment’s value by subtracting the reading at its bottom edge from the reading at its top edge; do not read the top edge alone. In grouped bars, compare bars of the same colour across groups for trend questions and bars within a group for share questions.

Use approximations

Bank DI options are usually far enough apart that two-significant-figure accuracy is enough. Convert to fractions where possible (17.3% ≈ 1/6, 33.3% = 1/3) and compute exactly only when two options are close.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Write the bar values in a small table on rough paper first; it prevents re-reading the graph for every question.
  • For average of n bars, add and divide once; for "average of two years", just halve their sum.
  • If every value grows by the same percentage, ratios between them do not change.
  • A value doubling means a 100% increase, not 200%.
  • For "approximately what percent", round to the nearest option only at the end.

Worked examples

Example 1

Data: accounts opened (in hundreds) by five branches — A 45, B 60, C 35, D 50, E 70. Find the total and the average per branch.

Solution

Total = 45 + 60 + 35 + 50 + 70 = 260 hundred, i.e. 26,000 accounts. Average = 260/5 = 52 hundred = 5,200 accounts per branch.

Example 2

Using the same data, by what percent are E’s accounts more than C’s?

Solution

(70 − 35)/35 × 100 = 100%. E opened twice as many accounts as C.

Example 3

Find the ratio of accounts opened by B and D.

Solution

60 : 50 = 6 : 5.

Example 4

What percentage of all accounts were opened by branch A?

Solution

45/260 × 100 ≈ 17.31%. Approximation: 45/260 is a little more than 1/6 (16.67%), so about 17%.

Example 5

If D’s accounts grow by 20% next year, how many will D open?

Solution

50 × 1.2 = 60 hundred, i.e. 6,000 accounts — the same as B’s current level.

Example 6

Which branch’s figure is closest to the five-branch average?

Solution

The average is 52 hundred. Deviations: A −7, B +8, C −17, D −2, E +18. Branch D, only 2 hundred below the average, is closest. Writing deviations from the average is also a quick way to check the total: −7 + 8 − 17 − 2 + 18 = 0.

Practice questions

Question 1

Production (thousand tonnes): 2019 120, 2020 150, 2021 135, 2022 180, 2023 210. Percentage increase from 2021 to 2022 is:

  1. 33.33%
  2. 25%
  3. 45%
  4. 30%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 33.33%

Increase = 180 − 135 = 45 on a base of 135, which is one-third, i.e. 33.33 percent.

Question 2

Using the same data, the average annual production over the five years is:

  1. 150 thousand tonnes
  2. 162 thousand tonnes
  3. 165 thousand tonnes
  4. 159 thousand tonnes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 159 thousand tonnes

Total = 120 + 150 + 135 + 180 + 210 = 795, and 795 ÷ 5 = 159 thousand tonnes.

Question 3

Using the same data, which year shows the highest percentage increase over the previous year?

  1. 2020
  2. 2023
  3. 2022
  4. 2021
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 2022

Growth rates are 25% (2020), −10% (2021), 33.3% (2022) and 16.7% (2023), so 2022 is highest.

Question 4

Using the same data, the ratio of production in 2019 to 2023 is:

  1. 3 : 5
  2. 4 : 7
  3. 2 : 3
  4. 5 : 7
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 4 : 7

120 : 210 simplifies by dividing both by 30, giving the ratio 4 : 7.

Question 5

Using the same data, production in 2023 is what percent of production in 2020?

  1. 40%
  2. 140%
  3. 120%
  4. 160%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 140%

210 ÷ 150 × 100 = 140 percent, meaning 2023 output is 1.4 times the 2020 output.

FAQs

How many DI sets come in IBPS PO prelims?

Typically one or two DI sets of about five questions each, alongside arithmetic and other question types, though the pattern varies by year. Mains papers contain more and harder DI. Practising one set daily across all chart types is a common approach.

Should I skip a DI set if the first question is hard?

Look at all five questions first. Often two or three are quick (a total or a ratio) even if one needs heavy calculation. Attempt the quick ones, and skip only the questions that require long computations or ambiguous readings.
