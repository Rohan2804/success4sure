Error Spotting in English: Rules That Catch Most Errors

Error spotting (also called error detection or spotting the error) asks you to find the part of a sentence that contains a grammatical or usage mistake. It appears in nearly every SSC CGL, CHSL and MTS English section and in IBPS and SBI prelims, typically three to five questions. Although English grammar is vast, a small set of rules explains most exam errors. Learning to check those rules in a fixed order turns this into a fast-scoring area.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Check the verb first

Most errors involve the verb: agreement with the subject ('The list of items are' → 'is'), tense consistency ('He said that he will come' → 'would come'), or wrong form after modals ('can goes' → 'can go'). Find the subject and main verb of each clause and test them together.

Pronouns

Check agreement in number and gender, and case: 'between you and I' should be 'between you and me', since a preposition takes the object form. 'Each of the students must bring their' is accepted in modern usage, but many exam keys still prefer 'his or her', so read the options carefully.

Prepositions and articles

Common errors: 'discuss about' (discuss takes no preposition), 'married with' (married to), 'senior than' (senior to), 'an university' (a university, because of the 'yu' sound), 'the both' (both the).

Comparatives and degree

Avoid double comparatives ('more better'). Use 'than' after comparatives, 'to' after senior, junior, superior, inferior, prefer. 'One of the best student' should be 'students', since 'one of' takes a plural noun.

Redundancy

Words that repeat meaning are errors: 'return back', 'repeat again', 'cousin brother', 'free gift', 'each and every one of them' in formal writing. Indian English has several such expressions that exam setters target.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Ignore the part marked 'No error' until you have checked the others; it is correct only when every other part passes.
  • With 'neither…nor' and 'either…or', the verb agrees with the nearer subject.
  • 'Scarcely' and 'hardly' pair with 'when', while 'no sooner' pairs with 'than'.
  • 'Lest' is followed by 'should' or the base verb, never by 'not'.
  • After 'It is time', use the past tense: 'It is time we left.'
  • Uncountable nouns (advice, information, furniture, luggage) take no plural form and a singular verb.

Worked examples

Example 1

Find the error: (a) The list of documents / (b) required for the loan / (c) are available / (d) on the website.

Solution

The subject is 'The list', which is singular; 'of documents' is only a modifier. So 'are' should be 'is'. Error in (c).

Example 2

Find the error: (a) No sooner did the train arrive / (b) when the passengers / (c) rushed towards / (d) the doors.

Solution

'No sooner' pairs with 'than', not 'when'. Part (b) should read 'than the passengers'. Error in (b).

Example 3

Find the error: (a) She is senior / (b) than me / (c) in the bank / (d) by three years.

Solution

'Senior' takes 'to', not 'than'. Part (b) should read 'to me'. Error in (b).

Example 4

Find the error: (a) The manager asked us / (b) to discuss about / (c) the new targets / (d) before Friday.

Solution

'Discuss' is a transitive verb and does not take 'about'. Part (b) should read 'to discuss'. Error in (b).

Example 5

Find the error: (a) He gave me / (b) many useful advices / (c) before my interview / (d) No error.

Solution

'Advice' is uncountable and has no plural. It should be 'much useful advice' or 'many useful pieces of advice'. Error in (b).

Practice questions

Question 1

Find the part with an error: 'Neither the manager nor the clerks / was present / at the meeting / yesterday.'

  1. was present
  2. Neither the manager nor the clerks
  3. at the meeting
  4. yesterday
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. was present

With neither…nor, the verb agrees with the nearer subject. 'Clerks' is plural, so 'was' should be 'were'.

Question 2

Find the part with an error: 'He returned back / from Mumbai / after attending / the conference.'

  1. after attending
  2. He returned back
  3. from Mumbai
  4. the conference
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. He returned back

'Return' already means to come back, so 'returned back' is redundant. The correct phrase is simply 'He returned'.

Question 3

Find the part with an error: 'This is one of the / best book / I have read / this year.'

  1. This is one of the
  2. I have read
  3. best book
  4. this year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. best book

'One of the' is followed by a plural noun. The phrase should be 'best books', not 'best book'.

Question 4

Find the part with an error: 'The furniture / in our new office / are very / comfortable.'

  1. The furniture
  2. in our new office
  3. comfortable
  4. are very
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. are very

'Furniture' is an uncountable noun and takes a singular verb. 'Are very' should be 'is very'.

Question 5

Find the part with an error: 'Hardly had I reached / the station / than the train / left.'

  1. than the train
  2. Hardly had I reached
  3. the station
  4. left
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. than the train

'Hardly' pairs with 'when', not 'than'. The part should read 'when the train'.

FAQs

How many grammar rules do I need for error spotting?

Around 40 to 50 core rules — agreement, tense, pronouns, articles, prepositions, comparatives, conjunction pairs and redundancy — explain most exam errors. Learn them with two example sentences each and revise weekly. Pair this with timed practice so you start checking the verb and subject automatically.

Is 'No error' often the correct answer?

It is correct in some questions, but setters usually place a real error in most sentences. Choose 'No error' only after checking each part against your rule list. If one part sounds slightly odd but you cannot name the rule, re-check the verb and preposition before deciding.
