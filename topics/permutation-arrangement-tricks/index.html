Permutation Tricks: Arrangements, Words and Circular Seating

Permutations count arrangements where order matters — seating people in a row, forming numbers from digits or rearranging the letters of a word. IBPS PO, SBI PO and RBI Grade B occasionally ask one question, often on word arrangements, while CAT and CMAT test permutations more deeply. Because answers are exact counts, guessing rarely works; learning a few standard patterns (repeated letters, together, never together, circular) covers most exam questions.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Counting principle and nPr

If one task can be done in m ways and an independent second task in n ways, both together can be done in m × n ways. Choosing and arranging r items from n distinct items gives nPr = n!/(n − r)! arrangements. Three-digit numbers with distinct digits from 1 to 6: 6 × 5 × 4 = 120.

nPr = n! / (n − r)!

Repeated items

When some items are identical, divide by the factorial of each repeat count. BANKING has 7 letters with N twice, so it has 7!/2! = 2,520 distinct arrangements. BALLOON has L twice and O twice, giving 7!/(2! × 2!) = 1,260.

n! / (p! × q! × …)

Together and never together

To keep certain items together, glue them into one block, arrange the blocks, then arrange items inside the block. For "no two together", first arrange the others and place the restricted items in the gaps between and around them.

Circular arrangements

Around a round table, rotations of the same arrangement are identical, so n people can sit in (n − 1)! ways. If clockwise and anticlockwise are also the same (a necklace or garland), divide by 2.

Circular = (n − 1)!

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Digits with 0: the first place cannot be 0, so for 4-digit numbers with distinct digits there are 9 × 9 × 8 × 7 = 4,536.
  • Vowels-together count = (consonants + 1)! × (vowels)! — adjust for repeated letters.
  • Vowels never together = total − vowels together, but "no two vowels together" needs the gap method.
  • For the rank of a word in dictionary order, count words starting with each smaller letter first.
  • If the answer seems huge, check that you have not counted rotations of a circle separately.

Worked examples

Example 1

How many 3-digit numbers with all different digits can be formed using the digits 1 to 6?

Solution

Hundreds place: 6 choices, tens: 5, units: 4. Total = 6 × 5 × 4 = 120 = 6P3.

Example 2

In how many ways can the letters of BANKING be arranged?

Solution

There are 7 letters with N repeated twice. Arrangements = 7!/2! = 5,040/2 = 2,520.

Example 3

In how many arrangements of LEADER do the vowels come together?

Solution

Vowels E, A, E form one block; with L, D, R there are 4 units, arranged in 4! = 24 ways. Inside the block, E, A, E can be arranged in 3!/2! = 3 ways. Total = 24 × 3 = 72.

Example 4

In how many ways can 6 friends sit around a round table?

Solution

Circular arrangement: (6 − 1)! = 5! = 120 ways.

Example 5

In how many ways can 5 boys and 3 girls stand in a row so that no two girls are together?

Solution

Arrange the boys: 5! = 120. They create 6 gaps (including both ends). Place 3 girls in 3 of these gaps in order: 6P3 = 120. Total = 120 × 120 = 14,400.

Example 6

A shelf has room for 4 books. In how many ways can 4 books be chosen and arranged from 7 different books? How many arrangements have a particular book always on the extreme left?

Solution

Ordered selection = 7P4 = 7 × 6 × 5 × 4 = 840. If one particular book is fixed on the extreme left, the remaining 3 places are filled from 6 books in 6 × 5 × 4 = 120 ways. Fixing an item reduces both the number of places and the pool by one.

Practice questions

Question 1

In how many ways can the letters of MOBILE be arranged?

  1. 360
  2. 720
  3. 120
  4. 5,040
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 720

MOBILE has 6 different letters, so there are 6! = 720 possible arrangements.

Question 2

The number of distinct arrangements of the letters of BALLOON is:

  1. 2,520
  2. 5,040
  3. 630
  4. 1,260
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 1,260

There are 7 letters with L and O each repeated twice, so the count is 7!/(2! × 2!) = 1,260.

Question 3

How many 4-digit numbers with all distinct digits can be formed using the digits 0 to 9?

  1. 4,536
  2. 5,040
  3. 4,032
  4. 3,024
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 4,536

The first digit has 9 choices (not 0), then 9, 8 and 7 choices remain, giving 9 × 9 × 8 × 7 = 4,536.

Question 4

In how many ways can 8 people be seated around a circular table?

  1. 40,320
  2. 2,520
  3. 5,040
  4. 720
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 5,040

Circular arrangements of n people number (n − 1)!, so for 8 people the answer is 7! = 5,040.

Question 5

In how many arrangements of ORANGE do all the vowels come together?

  1. 72
  2. 144
  3. 720
  4. 36
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 144

Treat O, A, E as one block with R, N, G: 4! × 3! = 24 × 6 = 144 arrangements.

FAQs

What is the difference between permutation and combination?

Permutation counts arrangements, where order matters: ABC and BAC are different. Combination counts selections, where order does not matter: choosing A, B, C is one committee. If a question involves seating, ranking, numbers or words, think permutation; for committees and teams, think combination.

Why divide by factorials for repeated letters?

Swapping two identical letters does not create a new word, but the n! formula counts each swap as different. Dividing by 2! for a pair of identical letters removes these duplicates. For a letter repeated three times, divide by 3!.
