Work Efficiency, Wages and Alternate-Day Work Problems

Once basic time and work is comfortable, bank and SSC papers add a twist: payment shared among workers, men and women of different efficiency, or people who work on alternate days. These variants regularly appear in IBPS PO, SBI PO, RBI Grade B and SSC CGL, and they are also good quantity-comparison material. All of them reduce to one idea — measure everything in units of work per day.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Wages follow work, not time

When workers are paid jointly for a job, each person’s share is proportional to the work they did. If everyone worked for the same number of days, the share is proportional to efficiency, which is the inverse of their individual time. A (10 days) and B (15 days) working together share in the ratio 3 : 2.

Helper problems

When a third person C helps A and B finish in fewer days, compute what fraction of the job A and B did in those days; the rest was C’s work, and C’s wage is that fraction of the total payment.

Men, women and children

Convert everyone to one unit. "2 men or 3 women can finish a job in 12 days" means 1 man = 1.5 women. Then 4 men + 6 women = 12 women-equivalents, and since 3 women need 12 days (36 woman-days), 12 women need 3 days.

Percentage efficiency

If A is 40% more efficient than B, A’s efficiency : B’s = 140 : 100 = 7 : 5, so time is in the ratio 5 : 7. If B takes 21 days, A takes 15 days.

Time ratio = inverse of efficiency ratio

Alternate days

When A and B work on alternate days starting with A, treat a two-day block as one cycle and count full cycles, then handle the final day carefully: the last person may need only part of a day.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Share of wages = (days worked ÷ individual time) × total wage.
  • For "x% more efficient", time of the efficient worker = original time × 100/(100 + x).
  • In alternate-day questions, stop the cycle count before the work overshoots and finish with a partial day.
  • If the units fit exactly after an odd number of days, the person who started also finishes.

Worked examples

Example 1

A can do a job in 10 days and B in 15 days. Working together they earn ₹5,000. How much does A get?

Solution

Efficiencies are 3 : 2 on 30 units. A’s share = 3/5 × 5,000 = ₹3,000.

Example 2

A can finish a job in 6 days and B in 8 days. With C’s help they finish in 3 days and are paid ₹6,400. Find each person’s share.

Solution

A does 3/6 = 1/2 of the job and B does 3/8. C does 1 − 1/2 − 3/8 = 1/8. Shares: A ₹3,200, B ₹2,400, C ₹800.

Example 3

2 men or 3 women can complete a job in 12 days. How long will 4 men and 6 women take?

Solution

1 man = 1.5 women, so 4 men + 6 women = 12 women. The job is 3 × 12 = 36 woman-days, so 12 women need 3 days.

Example 4

A is 40% more efficient than B. If B takes 21 days to finish a job, how long does A take?

Solution

Efficiency ratio 7 : 5 means time ratio 5 : 7. A’s time = 21 × 5/7 = 15 days.

Example 5

A can do a job in 12 days and B in 18 days. They work on alternate days, starting with A. When will the job be completed?

Solution

Total 36 units; A does 3, B does 2. Each 2-day cycle gives 5 units. After 7 cycles (14 days), 35 units are done. On day 15, A needs only 1 unit, i.e. 1/3 of a day. Total = 14⅓ days.

Practice questions

Question 1

A can do a work in 12 days and B in 24 days. Working together, they are paid ₹1,800. A’s share is:

  1. ₹900
  2. ₹600
  3. ₹1,080
  4. ₹1,200
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹1,200

Efficiencies are 2 : 1 for 12 and 24 days, so A gets 2/3 × 1,800 = ₹1,200 of the payment.

Question 2

A is 50% more efficient than B. If B takes 30 days to finish a job, A alone takes:

  1. 15 days
  2. 20 days
  3. 45 days
  4. 25 days
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 20 days

Efficiency 3 : 2 means time 2 : 3, so A takes 30 × 2/3 = 20 days for the job.

Question 3

3 men or 5 women can finish a job in 30 days. How many days will 6 men and 5 women take?

  1. 10 days
  2. 12 days
  3. 9 days
  4. 15 days
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 10 days

5 women equal 3 men, so the team is 9 men; the job is 90 man-days, so 9 men need 10 days.

Question 4

A can do a job in 6 days and B in 9 days. They work on alternate days, starting with A. The job is finished in:

  1. 7.5 days
  2. 7 days
  3. 8 days
  4. 6.5 days
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 7 days

On 18 units, A does 3 and B does 2; three cycles give 15 units in 6 days and A finishes 3 units on day 7.

Question 5

A and B can finish a job in 10 and 15 days. With C they finish it in 4 days and receive ₹3,000. C’s share is:

  1. ₹800
  2. ₹1,200
  3. ₹1,000
  4. ₹750
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹1,000

A does 4/10 and B does 4/15 of the job, worth ₹1,200 and ₹800, so C earns the remaining ₹1,000.

FAQs

Why are wages not shared by days worked?

Because two people working the same days may produce very different amounts of work. Fair payment is for output. Only when all workers are equally efficient do days worked and work done give the same ratio. Exams always intend payment by work done unless stated otherwise.

Does the starting person matter in alternate-day problems?

Yes, it can change the answer when the job does not finish at the end of a full cycle. If the faster worker starts, the job may finish sooner. Always check which worker is on duty on the final day and use their rate for the partial day.
