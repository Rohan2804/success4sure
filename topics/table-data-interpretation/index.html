Tabular Data Interpretation: Approach and Solved Set

Tables are the most information-dense DI format and the most common in IBPS PO mains, SBI PO and RBI Grade B, and they also appear in prelims and SSC CGL Tier 2. Unlike graphs, there is no reading error — the numbers are exact — so the challenge is choosing the right rows and columns and avoiding unnecessary arithmetic. Row and column totals are the most reused figures, so compute them once.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Map the table first

Identify what rows and columns represent and the unit of each cell. Note whether a column is already a total or a percentage. Many questions combine cells from one row (an item across periods) or one column (all items in a period).

Totals are reusable

Questions like "which model sold the most", "what share of Q2 sales came from S" or "average sales per quarter" all depend on row or column totals. Write totals in the margin as you compute them, so later questions take seconds.

Shares and percentages

Share of an item in a period = cell ÷ column total × 100. Percentage of women in a department = women ÷ department total × 100. Keep track of which total is the base; questions often switch bases between parts.

Share = part / relevant total × 100

Combining rows and columns

Some questions ask for a cell as a share of the grand total, or for one row’s total compared with one column’s total. Compute the grand total once by adding either the row totals or the column totals, and use the other set as a check: both must give the same figure.

Comparing ratios across rows

To find which row has the highest proportion of something, compare fractions such as 60/100, 90/240, 80/200 and 40/100. Cross-multiplication or converting to percentages quickly identifies the largest.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Add columns in pairs that make round numbers (120 + 180, 150 + 150) to speed up totals.
  • If a question asks "approximately", compare fractions to benchmarks like 1/4, 1/3 and 2/5.
  • Growth of a row from first to last period: (last − first)/first × 100.
  • Average of a row = row total ÷ number of periods; avoid averaging percentages from different bases.

Worked examples

Example 1

Phone sales (units) of four models: P 120, 150, 180; Q 200, 180, 220; R 90, 110, 100; S 160, 140, 200 for Q1, Q2, Q3. Find total sales in Q3.

Solution

Add the Q3 column: 180 + 220 + 100 + 200 = 700 units. Pairing 180 + 220 = 400 and 100 + 200 = 300 makes the addition instant.

Example 2

Which model had the highest total sales over the three quarters?

Solution

Row totals: P = 120 + 150 + 180 = 450, Q = 200 + 180 + 220 = 600, R = 90 + 110 + 100 = 300, S = 160 + 140 + 200 = 500. Model Q is highest with 600 units. Keep these totals; later questions on averages or shares reuse them.

Example 3

Find the percentage growth in P’s sales from Q1 to Q3.

Solution

(180 − 120)/120 × 100 = 50%.

Example 4

What is the average quarterly sale of model R?

Solution

(90 + 110 + 100)/3 = 100 units.

Example 5

Model S’s share of total Q2 sales is about what percent?

Solution

Q2 total = 150 + 180 + 110 + 140 = 580. Share = 140/580 × 100 ≈ 24.14%, slightly under one-quarter.

Example 6

Which model showed a decline in sales from Q1 to Q2, and by what percentage?

Solution

Q fell from 200 to 180 (10% decline) and S fell from 160 to 140 (12.5% decline). P and R both rose. So two models declined, and S had the sharper fall in percentage terms even though both lost 20 units.

Practice questions

Question 1

Staff (men, women): HR 40, 60; IT 150, 90; Sales 120, 80; Finance 60, 40. Total employees are:

  1. 640
  2. 600
  3. 620
  4. 660
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 640

Department totals are 100, 240, 200 and 100, which add up to 640 employees.

Question 2

Using the same table, the percentage of women in IT is:

  1. 40%
  2. 37.5%
  3. 35%
  4. 60%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 37.5%

IT has 90 women among 240 staff, and 90/240 = 3/8 = 37.5 percent.

Question 3

Using the same table, the ratio of men to women in the firm is:

  1. 3 : 2
  2. 27 : 37
  3. 37 : 27
  4. 4 : 3
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 37 : 27

Men total 370 and women total 270, and 370 : 270 simplifies to 37 : 27.

Question 4

Using the same table, which department has the highest percentage of women?

  1. HR
  2. IT
  3. Sales
  4. Finance
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. HR

Women shares are HR 60%, IT 37.5%, Sales 40% and Finance 40%, so HR is the highest.

Question 5

Using the same table, Finance employees form what percentage of the total?

  1. 12.5%
  2. 16.67%
  3. 20%
  4. 15.625%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 15.625%

Finance has 100 of 640 employees, and 100/640 × 100 = 15.625 percent of the workforce.

FAQs

Why are table DI sets considered time-consuming?

Because they contain more numbers than graphs and questions often need several cells combined. The cure is to compute and note row and column totals once, and to approximate whenever the options allow. With that habit, table sets are often faster than graph sets.

What if a table has missing cells?

Then it is a missing-data DI set. Use the given relationships, such as totals, percentages or differences, to fill the gaps first, and then answer the questions. These sets are common in IBPS PO mains.
