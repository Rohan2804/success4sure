Paper Folding and Cutting Questions: Unfolding Step by Step

Paper folding and cutting questions show a sheet folded one or more times, then punched or cut, and ask how it looks when opened. They are a standard non-verbal item in SSC CGL, CHSL and MTS, usually one question per paper. Unlike most figure questions, these follow strict rules: every fold acts like a mirror line, and every punch is copied across each fold when the paper opens. Unfolding in reverse order turns a visual guess into a reliable method.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Each fold is a mirror line

When the paper is unfolded, every hole or cut appears again as a mirror image across the fold line. A hole near the fold line appears close to the line on both sides; a hole near the outer edge appears near both outer edges.

Holes double with each fold

One punch through paper folded once makes two holes. Folded twice, the same punch passes through four layers and makes four holes; three folds make eight. If two punches are made, multiply each by the number of layers.

Holes = punches × 2^(number of folds), when every fold covers the punched area

Unfold in reverse order

Open the last fold first. Draw the shape after that fold opens, then open the previous fold, and so on. Trying to open all folds in one mental step is where most mistakes happen.

Diagonal folds

When paper is folded along a diagonal, the mirror line is diagonal too. A hole near one corner reflects to a position near the adjacent side, not straight across. Draw the diagonal on your rough sheet before reflecting.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Count layers at the punch point; that equals the number of holes after opening.
  • A semicircle cut on a fold line opens into a full circle; a half-hole on the fold becomes one hole, not two.
  • Eliminate options with the wrong number of holes first — this often leaves only one or two choices.
  • Check symmetry: the final pattern must be symmetric about every fold line used.
  • Shapes cut at the corner where two folds meet open into a single shape in the centre or at the corner of the full sheet.

Worked examples

Example 1

A square sheet is folded once vertically (left half over right half) and one hole is punched near the top of the folded paper. How many holes appear when opened, and where?

Solution

The punch goes through two layers, so two holes appear. They are mirror images across the vertical fold line: one in the left half and one in the right half, both near the top and at the same distance from the centre line.

Example 2

A square sheet is folded in half vertically and then in half horizontally, and one hole is punched near the centre of the folded square. How many holes appear when opened?

Solution

After two folds the paper has four layers at every point, so one punch makes 2² = 4 holes. They form a symmetric pattern, one in each quarter of the sheet.

Example 3

A sheet is folded in half three times and two holes are punched. How many holes appear when it is fully opened?

Solution

Three folds give 2³ = 8 layers. Each punch makes 8 holes, so two punches make 16 holes in all (assuming both punches pass through all layers).

Example 4

A sheet is folded once and a semicircle is cut out along the folded edge. What appears when opened?

Solution

The semicircle sits on the fold line, so opening it reflects it into the other half and joins the two halves. The result is a single full circle on the fold line, not two semicircles.

Practice questions

Question 1

A sheet is folded once and one hole is punched through both layers. How many holes appear when it is opened?

  1. 1
  2. 4
  3. 2
  4. 3
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 2

Folding once gives two layers at the punch point. The punch goes through both, so the opened sheet shows two holes, one on each side of the fold.

Question 2

A sheet is folded in half twice and one hole is punched. How many holes appear when it is opened?

  1. 2
  2. 6
  3. 8
  4. 4
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 4

Two folds create four layers at every point. A single punch passes through all four, giving 2 × 2 = 4 holes.

Question 3

A sheet is folded in half three times and one hole is punched through all layers. How many holes appear when opened?

  1. 8
  2. 4
  3. 6
  4. 16
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 8

Each fold doubles the layers, so three folds give 2³ = 8 layers. One punch through all of them makes 8 holes.

Question 4

A sheet is folded once and a semicircle is cut along the fold line. What appears on opening?

  1. Two semicircles
  2. One full circle
  3. Two full circles
  4. A quarter circle
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. One full circle

A semicircle on the fold line is reflected into the other half and joins its mirror image. The two halves combine into one complete circle.

Question 5

A sheet is folded in half twice and two holes are punched. How many holes are there after opening?

  1. 4
  2. 6
  3. 8
  4. 16
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 8

Two folds create four layers, so each punch produces four holes. Two punches therefore produce 2 × 4 = 8 holes.

FAQs

What if a fold does not cover the punched area?

Then that part has fewer layers, and the hole is copied only across the folds that actually covered it. Count the layers at the exact punch point instead of using the 2^folds formula blindly. Exam diagrams usually show partial folds clearly, so look at where the punch sits.

How many such questions come in SSC exams?

Typically one paper-folding or paper-cutting question appears in an SSC reasoning section, alongside other non-verbal items like mirror images and embedded figures. Numbers vary by shift. Since the method is rule-based, a few practice sessions are usually enough to answer them confidently.
