Problems on Trains: Crossing Poles, Platforms and Trains

Train questions are a special case of speed and distance where the moving object has length. IBPS Clerk, SBI Clerk and SSC CHSL/CGL nearly always include one, and Indian Railways settings — a Rajdhani crossing a platform, two trains on parallel tracks — make them easy to picture. The trick is to decide what distance is covered (train length alone, or train plus something else) and which speed applies (actual or relative).

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Crossing a point object

When a train passes a pole, a signal post or a standing man, it covers its own length. A 240 m train at 72 km/h (20 m/s) passes a pole in 240 ÷ 20 = 12 seconds.

Time = L / S

Crossing a platform or bridge

The train must cover its own length plus the platform’s length. The same 240 m train crosses a 360 m platform in (240 + 360) ÷ 20 = 30 seconds.

Time = (L + P) / S

Two trains

Two trains crossing each other cover the sum of their lengths. Use the sum of speeds if they move in opposite directions and the difference if they move in the same direction (overtaking).

Time = (L1 + L2) / (S1 ± S2)

Moving man or car

A man walking or a car has negligible length, so the distance is the train’s length, but the speed is relative: train speed minus man’s speed if they move in the same direction, plus if opposite.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Convert to m/s early: 72 km/h = 20 m/s, 54 = 15, 90 = 25, 108 = 30, 36 = 10.
  • If a train passes a pole in t1 and a platform of length P in t2, its speed = P/(t2 − t1) and its length = speed × t1.
  • Two trains of the same length crossing in opposite directions: each covers one length in the crossing time at half the relative speed.
  • A man sitting in a train sees the other train pass in (length of the other train) ÷ relative speed — his own train’s length is irrelevant.

Worked examples

Example 1

A 240 m long train runs at 72 km/h. How long does it take to pass a pole and to cross a 360 m platform?

Solution

72 km/h = 20 m/s. Pole: 240 ÷ 20 = 12 s. Platform: (240 + 360) ÷ 20 = 30 s.

Example 2

Two trains, 150 m and 100 m long, run in opposite directions at 50 km/h and 40 km/h. How long do they take to cross each other?

Solution

Relative speed = 90 km/h = 25 m/s. Distance = 250 m. Time = 10 seconds.

Example 3

A 120 m train at 72 km/h overtakes a 130 m train running at 54 km/h on a parallel track. How long does overtaking take?

Solution

Relative speed = 72 − 54 = 18 km/h = 5 m/s. Distance = 250 m. Time = 50 seconds.

Example 4

A train at 60 km/h passes a man walking at 6 km/h in the same direction in 9 seconds. Find the train’s length.

Solution

Relative speed = 54 km/h = 15 m/s. Length = 15 × 9 = 135 m.

Example 5

A train passes a pole in 15 seconds and a 100 m platform in 25 seconds. Find its speed and length.

Solution

The extra 10 seconds are needed for the 100 m platform, so speed = 10 m/s (36 km/h). Length = 10 × 15 = 150 m.

Example 6

Two trains, each 120 m long, run in opposite directions on parallel tracks and cross each other in 8 seconds. One train runs at 54 km/h. Find the speed of the other.

Solution

Total length 240 m in 8 s gives a relative speed of 30 m/s. One train runs at 15 m/s, so the other also runs at 15 m/s, i.e. 54 km/h. When the lengths and crossing time are given, always find the relative speed first and then split it.

Practice questions

Question 1

A 180 m long train running at 54 km/h passes a pole in:

  1. 10 seconds
  2. 15 seconds
  3. 12 seconds
  4. 9 seconds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 12 seconds

54 km/h equals 15 m/s, so the train covers its 180 m length in 12 seconds.

Question 2

A 300 m train at 90 km/h crosses a 200 m bridge in:

  1. 12 seconds
  2. 18 seconds
  3. 25 seconds
  4. 20 seconds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 20 seconds

Distance is 300 + 200 = 500 m at 25 m/s, which takes 20 seconds to cover.

Question 3

Two trains 140 m and 160 m long run in opposite directions at 60 km/h and 48 km/h. They cross each other in:

  1. 10 seconds
  2. 12 seconds
  3. 9 seconds
  4. 15 seconds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 10 seconds

Relative speed is 108 km/h = 30 m/s, and 300 m ÷ 30 = 10 seconds to cross.

Question 4

A train passes a pole in 12 seconds and a 180 m platform in 24 seconds. Its length is:

  1. 180 m
  2. 150 m
  3. 200 m
  4. 120 m
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 180 m

The platform adds 12 seconds, so speed = 180/12 = 15 m/s and length = 15 × 12 = 180 m.

Question 5

A 200 m train at 90 km/h overtakes a 160 m train at 72 km/h moving in the same direction. Overtaking takes:

  1. 20 seconds
  2. 36 seconds
  3. 72 seconds
  4. 60 seconds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 72 seconds

Relative speed is 18 km/h = 5 m/s, and the total length of 360 m takes 72 seconds.

FAQs

Why is the platform length added when a train crosses it?

Crossing is complete only when the last coach leaves the far end of the platform. From the moment the engine enters to that moment, the engine travels the platform length plus the whole train length. That total is the distance used in the formula.

Do I subtract speeds when a train passes a man walking towards it?

No. If they move towards each other, their speeds add. Subtract only when both move in the same direction. Read the direction carefully because both versions appear and the options usually include the wrong-sign answer.
