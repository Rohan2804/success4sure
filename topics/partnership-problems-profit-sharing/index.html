Partnership Problems: Profit Sharing by Capital and Time

Partnership questions ask how a business profit is shared when partners invest different amounts for different periods. IBPS PO, SBI Clerk and RBI Grade B prelims usually include one, and they are common in DI caselets where several partners join or withdraw during a year. The whole chapter rests on a single rule — profit is shared in the ratio of capital multiplied by time — plus careful book-keeping for changes during the year.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Capital × time

Each partner’s share of profit is proportional to the product of capital and the number of months it was invested. ₹50,000 for 12 months has weight 6,00,000; ₹40,000 for 9 months has weight 3,60,000. The ratio 5 : 3 decides how profit is split.

Profit ratio = C1 × T1 : C2 × T2

Changes during the year

If a partner adds or withdraws capital mid-year, split their investment into periods. A partner who starts with ₹20,000 and withdraws ₹5,000 after 6 months has an effective weight of 20,000 × 6 + 15,000 × 6. Add the pieces for each partner before forming the ratio.

Working partners

Sometimes one partner gets a salary or a fixed percentage of profit for managing the business. Deduct that amount first, then divide the remainder in the capital-time ratio, and finally add the salary back to the working partner’s share.

Finding time or capital

If the profit ratio and capital ratio are given, the time ratio = profit ratio ÷ capital ratio term by term. Capitals 3 : 5 with profits 9 : 10 give times (9/3) : (10/5) = 3 : 2.

Time ratio = (P1/C1) : (P2/C2)

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Drop common zeros: ₹45,000 and ₹30,000 become 45 and 30 before multiplying by months.
  • If everyone invests for the same period, ignore time and use capitals alone.
  • Late joiner: a partner joining after k months invests for (12 − k) months in a one-year business.
  • Check: the shares must add to the total profit — a fast way to catch a slip.
  • If two partners have equal capital × time, they share equally, even with very different capitals.

Worked examples

Example 1

A invests ₹50,000 for 12 months and B invests ₹40,000 for 9 months. The profit is ₹24,000. Find B’s share.

Solution

Ratio = 50 × 12 : 40 × 9 = 600 : 360 = 5 : 3. B’s share = 3/8 × 24,000 = ₹9,000.

Example 2

A starts a business with ₹30,000. After 4 months B joins with ₹45,000. At the end of the year the profit is ₹17,000. How is it shared?

Solution

A: 30 × 12 = 360; B: 45 × 8 = 360. The ratio is 1 : 1, so each gets ₹8,500 despite B’s larger capital.

Example 3

A and B invest in the ratio 2 : 3. A, the working partner, gets 10% of the profit as salary. If the total profit is ₹50,000, find each partner’s total receipt.

Solution

Salary = ₹5,000. Remaining ₹45,000 is divided 2 : 3, giving ₹18,000 and ₹27,000. A receives 5,000 + 18,000 = ₹23,000 and B receives ₹27,000.

Example 4

A invests ₹20,000 and withdraws ₹5,000 after 6 months. B invests ₹15,000 for the whole year. Share a profit of ₹39,000.

Solution

A: 20 × 6 + 15 × 6 = 210; B: 15 × 12 = 180. Ratio 7 : 6, total 13 parts of ₹3,000. A gets ₹21,000 and B gets ₹18,000.

Example 5

A, B and C start a business with ₹20,000, ₹30,000 and ₹40,000. After 6 months, A doubles his capital. Find C’s share of a yearly profit of ₹54,000.

Solution

A: 20 × 6 + 40 × 6 = 360; B: 30 × 12 = 360; C: 40 × 12 = 480. Ratio 360 : 360 : 480 = 3 : 3 : 4, total 10 parts of ₹5,400. C gets 4 × 5,400 = ₹21,600, while A and B get ₹16,200 each.

Practice questions

Question 1

A and B invest ₹15,000 and ₹25,000 for the same period. Out of a profit of ₹16,000, B gets:

  1. ₹6,000
  2. ₹10,000
  3. ₹9,600
  4. ₹8,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹10,000

The ratio is 15 : 25 = 3 : 5, so B receives 5/8 × 16,000 = ₹10,000 of the profit.

Question 2

A invests ₹40,000 for 12 months and B invests ₹60,000 for 6 months. Out of a profit of ₹14,000, A gets:

  1. ₹6,000
  2. ₹7,000
  3. ₹5,600
  4. ₹8,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹8,000

The ratio is 40 × 12 : 60 × 6 = 480 : 360 = 4 : 3, so A receives 4/7 × 14,000 = ₹8,000.

Question 3

Capitals of A and B are in the ratio 3 : 5 and their profits are in the ratio 9 : 10. The ratio of their investment periods is:

  1. 3 : 2
  2. 2 : 3
  3. 27 : 50
  4. 5 : 3
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 3 : 2

Time ratio = (9/3) : (10/5) = 3 : 2, since profit equals capital multiplied by time.

Question 4

A starts with ₹24,000. B joins after 3 months with ₹32,000. Out of a yearly profit of ₹13,200, B gets:

  1. ₹7,040
  2. ₹6,600
  3. ₹5,940
  4. ₹7,200
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹6,600

A has 24 × 12 = 288 and B has 32 × 9 = 288, so they share equally: ₹6,600 each.

Question 5

A, B and C invest in the ratio 2 : 3 : 5 for the same period. Out of a profit of ₹45,000, C gets:

  1. ₹15,000
  2. ₹18,000
  3. ₹13,500
  4. ₹22,500
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹22,500

C’s share is 5/10 of the total profit, which is half of ₹45,000, or ₹22,500.

FAQs

Do partnership questions use months or years?

Either works as long as all partners use the same unit. Months are more convenient because partners often join or leave mid-year. If the business runs for more than a year, count the total months each capital was in use.

What if interest on capital is also given?

Treat interest on capital like a salary: calculate it for each partner first, deduct the total from profit, and then share the remainder in the agreed ratio. Each partner finally receives interest plus their share of the remaining profit.
