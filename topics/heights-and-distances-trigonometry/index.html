Heights and Distances: Angle of Elevation and Depression

Heights and distances is the applied side of trigonometry and a steady one- or two-question topic in SSC CGL and CHSL. The setting is always a right triangle formed by a vertical object, the ground and a line of sight, and almost every question uses 30°, 45° or 60°. Drawing a quick sketch and labelling the known side is more important than any formula.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Elevation and depression

The angle of elevation is measured upward from the horizontal to the line of sight; the angle of depression is measured downward from the horizontal at the observer’s eye. Because the horizontal lines at the top and bottom are parallel, the angle of depression from the top equals the angle of elevation from the bottom.

Choosing the ratio

Use tan when height and horizontal distance are involved (most questions), sin when the slant length (a ladder or a kite string) and height are involved, and cos for slant length and horizontal distance. tan 30° = 1/√3, tan 45° = 1, tan 60° = √3.

height = distance × tan θ

Two-angle questions

When an observer walks d metres towards a tower and the elevation changes from α to β, write the two horizontal distances as h cot α and h cot β. Their difference is d. For 30° and 60°, h(√3 − 1/√3) = d, so h = d√3/2.

h = d / (cot α − cot β)

Shadows

The shadow is the horizontal side and the object is the vertical side; the sun’s elevation is the angle at the tip of the shadow. When the shadow equals the height, the elevation is 45°. A shadow √3 times the height means 30°.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • 30°–60° triangle sides are in the ratio 1 : √3 : 2; 45° triangle sides are 1 : 1 : √2.
  • Elevation 30° → 60° walking d towards a tower: height = (√3/2)d and the final distance = d/2.
  • Use √3 ≈ 1.732 only at the end; keep answers in surd form if options are in surds.
  • If two objects are on opposite sides of a tower, add the horizontal distances; on the same side, subtract.

Worked examples

Example 1

The angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point 20 m from its foot is 60°. Find the height.

Solution

h = 20 × tan 60° = 20√3 ≈ 34.64 m.

Example 2

A 10 m ladder leans against a wall, making 60° with the ground. How high up the wall does it reach?

Solution

Ladder is the hypotenuse, height is opposite: h = 10 × sin 60° = 5√3 ≈ 8.66 m.

Example 3

From the top of a 50 m building, the angle of depression of a car is 30°. How far is the car from the building?

Solution

Angle of elevation from the car is also 30°. Distance = 50 ÷ tan 30° = 50√3 ≈ 86.6 m.

Example 4

The elevation of a tower’s top changes from 30° to 60° as an observer walks 40 m towards it. Find the height.

Solution

h cot 30° − h cot 60° = 40 → h(√3 − 1/√3) = 40 → h × 2/√3 = 40 → h = 20√3 ≈ 34.64 m. The observer’s final distance from the tower is h/√3 = 20 m.

Example 5

A pole’s shadow is equal to its height. What is the sun’s angle of elevation?

Solution

tan θ = height/shadow = 1, so θ = 45°. Later in the afternoon, when the shadow becomes √3 times the height, tan θ = 1/√3 and the sun’s elevation has dropped to 30°.

Practice questions

Question 1

A pole casts a shadow of 10√3 m when the sun’s elevation is 30°. The pole’s height is:

  1. 10√3 m
  2. 30 m
  3. 5√3 m
  4. 10 m
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 10 m

Height = shadow × tan 30° = 10√3 × 1/√3 = 10 m, as the shadow is the horizontal side.

Question 2

A kite string 100 m long makes 30° with the ground. The kite’s height is:

  1. 50 m
  2. 50√3 m
  3. 100√3 m
  4. 25 m
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 50 m

The string is the hypotenuse, so height = 100 × sin 30° = 100 × 1/2 = 50 m.

Question 3

From a point 30√3 m from a 30 m tower, the angle of elevation of the top is:

  1. 45°
  2. 30°
  3. 60°
  4. 15°
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 30°

tan θ = 30/(30√3) = 1/√3, which corresponds to an angle of elevation of 30 degrees.

Question 4

Walking 20 m towards a tower changes the elevation from 30° to 60°. The tower’s height is:

  1. 20√3 m
  2. 10 m
  3. 20 m
  4. 10√3 m
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 10√3 m

Using h = d√3/2 for the 30°–60° case with d = 20 gives h = 10√3 m, about 17.32 m.

Question 5

From the top of a 75 m lighthouse, the angle of depression of a boat is 45°. The boat’s distance from the lighthouse is:

  1. 75√3 m
  2. 37.5 m
  3. 75 m
  4. 75√2 m
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 75 m

At 45°, tan θ = 1, so the horizontal distance equals the height of the lighthouse, 75 m.

FAQs

Is the angle of depression measured from the vertical?

No. Both elevation and depression are measured from the horizontal line through the observer’s eye. Measuring from the vertical gives the complementary angle and a wrong answer. Draw the horizontal line explicitly in your sketch.

Should I use √3 = 1.732 or keep surds?

Look at the options. If they contain √3, keep the surd form throughout. If they are decimals, substitute 1.732 at the very end to avoid rounding errors building up during the calculation.
