Circular Seating Arrangement: Facing Centre and Outside

Circular seating puzzles are a staple of IBPS PO, SBI PO and RBI Grade B prelims, and simpler versions with five or six people appear in clerk exams and SSC. One puzzle generally carries five questions. The single fact that unlocks the topic is which way 'left' runs around the table. Once that is fixed, the method is the same as linear seating: anchor definite clues, branch when needed, and use negative clues to eliminate cases.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Facing the centre

When people face the centre, a person's left is the clockwise direction around the table and their right is anticlockwise. Draw the circle, fix the first person at the bottom, and move clockwise for 'to the left'.

Facing outside

When people face away from the centre, the directions reverse: left is anticlockwise and right is clockwise. In mixed puzzles, draw a small inward or outward arrow at each seat before applying relative clues.

Opposite seats

At a table with an even number n of people, the person opposite sits n/2 places away in either direction. With eight people, opposite means four places away. With an odd number of people there is no exact opposite, so such clues appear only in even-sized puzzles.

No fixed start

A circle has no ends, so you may place the first definite person anywhere. Choose the person mentioned most often in the clues as your anchor; it reduces redrawing.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • With n people facing the centre, 'k to the right' is the same seat as 'n − k to the left'. In a group of eight, third to the right = fifth to the left.
  • 'Second to the left of A' and 'second to the right of A' point to the same seat only when n = 4.
  • Neighbours are always the seats immediately on both sides; 'neighbour' does not depend on facing direction.
  • For square or rectangular tables, people at the corners and the middles of sides usually face the centre unless stated; treat the table like a circle of seats.
  • Use negative clues ('is not a neighbour of') at the very end to choose between surviving cases.

Worked examples

Example 1

Eight people A–H sit around a circular table facing the centre. B sits fourth to the right of A. C sits second to the right of A. D sits immediately to the right of B. E sits immediately to the left of C. F sits second to the left of D. G is an immediate neighbour of A. Find the arrangement and say who sits fourth to the right of F.

Solution

Fix A at seat 0 and number seats 0–7 going to the right (anticlockwise). B = 4 and C = 2. D = 5 (right of B). E = 1 (left of C). F = 5 − 2 = 3. Seats 6 and 7 remain for G and H. A's neighbours are seats 1 and 7; seat 1 is E, so G = 7 and H = 6. Going right from A: A, E, C, F, B, D, H, G. Fourth to the right of F (seat 3) is seat 7: G. Note that B and A are opposite each other, as 'fourth to the right' always gives the opposite seat in a group of eight.

Example 2

Six people R, P, Q, S, T and U sit around a circular table facing the centre. P sits second to the right of R. Q sits third to the right of P. S sits immediately to the left of Q. T is not an immediate neighbour of R. Who sits opposite R?

Solution

Place R at seat 0, numbering to the right. P = 2. Q = 5 (opposite P). S is immediately to the left of Q, so S = 4. Seats 1 and 3 remain. T cannot neighbour R, so T is not at seat 1; T = 3 and U = 1. Opposite R (three seats away) is seat 3: T. U sits between R and P.

Example 3

Five friends sit around a table facing the centre. Kunal sits second to the left of Isha. How many seats to the right of Isha is Kunal?

Solution

With five seats, two to the left equals 5 − 2 = 3 to the right. Kunal is third to the right of Isha.

Practice questions

Question 1

Five people A, B, C, D and E sit around a circular table facing the centre. B sits immediately to the right of A. C sits immediately to the left of A. D is an immediate neighbour of B. Who sits immediately to the left of C?

  1. D
  2. B
  3. E
  4. A
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. E

Going right from A: A, B, then D next to B, and C is just left of A. The order to the right is A, B, D, E, C, so E sits immediately to the left of C.

Question 2

Eight people sit around a circular table facing the centre. Q sits fourth to the right of P. R sits immediately to the right of P. S sits immediately to the right of Q. T sits second to the right of R. U sits immediately to the left of P. Who sits fourth to the right of S?

  1. P
  2. U
  3. T
  4. R
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. R

Numbering seats to the right from P = 0 gives R = 1, T = 3, Q = 4, S = 5 and U = 7. Fourth to the right of S is seat (5 + 4) − 8 = 1, which is R.

Question 3

Six people sit around a circular table facing the centre. How many places to the left of a person is the seat that is second to the right?

  1. Fourth
  2. Second
  3. Third
  4. Fifth
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Fourth

In a circle of six, moving two seats to the right reaches the same seat as moving 6 − 2 = 4 seats to the left.

Question 4

Six people sit around a circular table facing away from the centre. Which direction around the table is a person's left?

  1. Clockwise
  2. Anticlockwise
  3. Towards the centre
  4. It depends on the person
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Anticlockwise

For people facing the centre, left is clockwise. Facing outward reverses that, so a person's left becomes the anticlockwise direction around the table.

Question 5

At a circular table with eight people facing the centre, how many seats away is the person sitting directly opposite you?

  1. Three
  2. Two
  3. Four
  4. Five
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Four

Opposite means halfway round the table. With eight seats that is 8 ÷ 2 = 4 places away in either direction.

FAQs

Why is left clockwise when people face the centre?

Imagine standing at the bottom of the circle facing up towards the centre. Your left hand points towards the 9 o'clock side, and moving from 6 to 9 o'clock is clockwise. That is why, for centre-facing people, 'to the left' means moving clockwise around the table.

How do I handle puzzles where some people face outside?

Mark each person with an arrow pointing in or out. For an inward-facing person use left = clockwise; for outward-facing, left = anticlockwise. Apply each clue using the direction of the person named in that clue, not the person being placed.
