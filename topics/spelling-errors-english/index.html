Spelling Error Questions: Rules and Commonly Misspelt Words

Spelling questions ask you to find the misspelt word among four options, or the correctly spelt one. SSC CGL, CHSL and MTS include them in most English sections, and bank exams occasionally hide a misspelt word inside error-detection or word-usage questions. Spelling looks like pure memory, but a handful of rules explain many of the tested words, and the rest come from a fairly stable list of frequently misspelt words that you can learn in a few weeks.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Doubling the final consonant

In words where the stress falls on the last syllable and the word ends in one vowel plus one consonant, double the consonant before adding -ed or -ing: occur → occurred, begin → beginning, commit → committed. If the stress is not on the last syllable, do not double: benefit → benefited, offer → offered.

ie and ei

The rhyme 'i before e except after c' works for many words: believe, achieve, receive, ceiling. Exceptions are tested often: seize, weird, foreign, height, neighbour, their.

Silent letters

Many errors involve dropped silent letters: government (n), February (r), Wednesday (d), environment (n), knowledge (k), psychology (p), receipt (p), debt (b).

-ance/-ence and -able/-ible

No simple rule covers all words, so learn the frequent ones: maintenance, guidance, existence, independence, occurrence, perseverance; responsible, accessible, eligible, available, reliable, noticeable.

Double letters in the middle

Commonly tested: accommodation (double c, double m), committee (double m, double t, double e), embarrass (double r, double s), occasion (double c, single s), necessary (one c, double s), millennium (double l, double n), questionnaire (double n).

British versus American spelling

Indian exams follow British spelling: colour, favour, programme (for a plan or show), centre, licence (noun) and license (verb), cheque, organise or organize (both accepted). Do not mark British forms as errors. Similarly, 'practice' is the noun and 'practise' the verb in British usage, while 'advice' (noun) and 'advise' (verb) differ in both spelling and pronunciation; exam sentences sometimes test these pairs inside error-spotting questions.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • 'Necessary': one collar (c), two sleeves (s).
  • 'Separate' has 'a rat' in it: sep-a-rate.
  • 'Accommodate' has room for two c's and two m's.
  • 'Definitely' comes from 'finite': defin-ite-ly, not 'definately'.
  • 'Privilege' has no 'd': privi-lege.
  • Read each option letter by letter; spelling questions punish skimming.
  • Words ending in silent e usually drop it before -ing (write → writing) but keep it before -ment (manage → management, except argument and judgment).
  • 'Leisure', 'seize', 'weird', 'foreign' and 'height' break the 'i before e except after c' rule; memorise them as a set.

Worked examples

Example 1

Find the misspelt word: (a) accommodation (b) committee (c) occassion (d) necessary.

Solution

'Occasion' has double c and a single s. 'Occassion' is wrong. Answer: (c).

Example 2

Find the correctly spelt word: (a) recieve (b) receive (c) receeve (d) riceive.

Solution

After 'c', the order is 'ei': receive. Answer: (b).

Example 3

Find the misspelt word: (a) beginning (b) occurred (c) comitted (d) referred.

Solution

'Commit' has a double m, and because the stress falls on the last syllable, the t doubles before -ed: 'committed'. 'Comitted' drops one m. Answer: (c).

Example 4

Find the misspelt word: (a) government (b) enviroment (c) February (d) knowledge.

Solution

'Environment' contains a silent n before -ment. 'Enviroment' is wrong. Answer: (b).

Example 5

Find the correctly spelt word: (a) maintainance (b) maintenence (c) maintenance (d) maintanance.

Solution

The noun drops the 'ai' of 'maintain': maint-e-nance. Answer: (c).

Example 6

Find the misspelt word: (a) independence (b) perseverence (c) existence (d) occurrence.

Solution

The noun from 'persevere' is 'perseverance', with -ance. 'Perseverence' is wrong. The other three correctly end in -ence. Answer: (b).

FAQs

Are American spellings marked wrong in Indian exams?

Indian exams generally follow British spelling, so 'colour' and 'programme' are standard. American forms are rarely the only options. If a question offers both 'colour' and 'color' as separate choices, look for the clearly misspelt word instead, because setters avoid testing regional variants.

How can I improve spelling quickly?

Collect the 150 to 200 most frequently misspelt words from SSC papers and practice books, write each correctly three times, and test yourself weekly. Pay attention to double letters and silent letters, which cause most errors. Reading carefully also helps, since you start noticing correct forms.
