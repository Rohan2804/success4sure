Indian Economy Basics: GDP, GVA, Inflation, CPI and WPI

Basic economy concepts are tested across RBI Grade B, IBPS and SBI mains, SSC CGL and CHSL, CMAT and bank interviews. The questions are rarely about this year's figures alone; they ask what GDP measures, why the RBI tracks CPI rather than WPI, or how real growth differs from nominal growth. These concepts are stable, so they are worth learning properly. Base years and data series are revised from time to time, so check MoSPI and the Office of the Economic Adviser for the current base years.

GDP, GNP and NNP

Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is the value of all final goods and services produced within a country's borders in a period. Gross National Product (GNP) adds net factor income from abroad (earnings of residents abroad minus earnings of foreigners in India). Net National Product (NNP) subtracts depreciation from GNP.

GVA

Gross Value Added (GVA) measures output by sector — agriculture, industry, services — at basic prices. GDP equals GVA plus product taxes minus product subsidies. GVA is useful for seeing which sectors drive growth.

Nominal and real

Nominal GDP uses current prices; real GDP uses constant prices of a base year, removing inflation. If nominal GDP grows 10% and inflation (measured by the GDP deflator) is 4%, real growth is roughly 6%.

CPI and WPI

The Consumer Price Index measures retail price changes for households and is compiled by the National Statistics Office under MoSPI. The Wholesale Price Index measures wholesale prices and is compiled by the Office of the Economic Adviser in the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade. The RBI's inflation target is based on headline CPI.

Types of inflation

Demand-pull inflation comes from demand exceeding supply; cost-push from rising input costs like fuel. Stagflation combines high inflation with low growth. Deflation is a sustained fall in prices; disinflation is a fall in the rate of inflation while prices still rise.

Fiscal and monetary policy

Fiscal policy is the government's use of taxes and spending, presented through the budget. Monetary policy is the RBI's management of interest rates and liquidity. Both influence growth and inflation, and they work best when aligned.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • GDP = inside the borders; GNP = GDP + net factor income from abroad.
  • NNP = GNP − depreciation.
  • Real growth ≈ nominal growth − inflation (approximation).
  • CPI → NSO (MoSPI); WPI → Office of the Economic Adviser (DPIIT).
  • Disinflation: inflation slows but stays positive; deflation: prices actually fall.
  • Check current base years with MoSPI before your exam; they get revised.

Worked examples

Example 1

Nominal GDP grew from ₹220 lakh crore to ₹242 lakh crore. What was nominal growth? If inflation was 4%, what was approximate real growth?

Solution

Nominal growth = (242 − 220) ÷ 220 × 100 = 10%. Approximate real growth = 10 − 4 = 6%.

Example 2

Inflation falls from 7.5% to 5% in a year. Is this deflation?

Solution

No. Prices are still rising, only more slowly. This is disinflation of 2.5 percentage points. Deflation would mean inflation below zero.

Example 3

A software engineer from India works in the US and sends home earnings. Does this add to India's GDP or GNP?

Solution

It is earned abroad, so it is not part of India's GDP. It is factor income from abroad and counts towards India's GNP.

Example 4

Interview: 'Why does the RBI target CPI and not WPI?'

Solution

CPI reflects the prices households actually pay, including services and food, so it better represents the cost of living and inflation expectations. WPI covers wholesale goods and excludes most services, making it less relevant for consumers.

Practice questions

Question 1

Which index is used for the RBI's inflation target?

  1. Wholesale Price Index
  2. GDP deflator
  3. Index of Industrial Production
  4. Consumer Price Index
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Consumer Price Index

The flexible inflation targeting framework uses headline CPI inflation, which reflects retail prices paid by households.

Question 2

GNP equals GDP plus:

  1. Net factor income from abroad
  2. Depreciation
  3. Indirect taxes
  4. Subsidies
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Net factor income from abroad

GNP adds net factor income from abroad to GDP, capturing income earned by residents outside the country minus income paid to foreigners.

Question 3

Inflation falls from 6% to 4%. What is this called?

  1. Deflation
  2. Disinflation
  3. Stagflation
  4. Hyperinflation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Disinflation

Prices are still rising, but more slowly, which is disinflation. Deflation would require a negative inflation rate.

Question 4

Which office compiles the Wholesale Price Index in India?

  1. National Statistics Office
  2. Reserve Bank of India
  3. Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT
  4. NITI Aayog
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT

WPI is compiled by the Office of the Economic Adviser under the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade.

Question 5

Nominal GDP grew 10% and inflation was 4%. What is the approximate real growth?

  1. 14%
  2. 2.5%
  3. 40%
  4. 6%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 6%

Real growth is approximately nominal growth minus inflation, so 10% − 4% gives about 6% real growth.

FAQs

Why do GDP numbers change after release?

Initial GDP estimates are based on partial data and are revised as fuller data arrives, through advance, provisional and revised estimates. Base-year revisions also change the series. For exams, note which estimate a figure refers to and take the latest numbers from MoSPI releases.

Is GVA or GDP better for measuring growth?

Both are used. GVA is better for understanding which sectors are growing, since it measures output at basic prices by industry. GDP includes net product taxes and is the headline measure used internationally. India's official releases report both, and exam questions may ask about either.
