Sentence Improvement Questions: Method and Common Fixes

Sentence improvement questions underline or bold a part of a sentence and offer replacements, usually with an option for 'No improvement'. SSC CGL, CHSL and MTS use them regularly, and bank exams use a related format called phrase replacement. The skill combines error spotting with choosing the best fix: you must notice what is wrong and also recognise which option corrects it without creating a new error.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Find the problem before reading options

Read the sentence and decide what is wrong with the underlined part — tense, agreement, preposition, idiom or word order. Then look for an option that fixes exactly that. Reading options first makes several of them sound acceptable.

Keep the meaning

The right replacement corrects the grammar without changing what the sentence means. An option that is grammatical but alters the meaning (for example, changing 'must' to 'may') is wrong.

Idioms and fixed phrases

Many questions test fixed expressions: 'by and large', 'in lieu of', 'with regard to' (not 'with regards to'), 'as soon as', 'in spite of' (not 'in spite'). Knowing the standard form often answers the question instantly.

When 'No improvement' is right

Choose it only when the original is grammatically correct and natural. Test it with the same checklist you use for errors. Setters include it as the answer in some questions, so do not avoid it out of habit.

Conditional and tense patterns

'If I were you, I would…', 'If he had studied, he would have passed', 'If it rains, we will stay home'. Mixing these patterns ('If he would have studied') is a frequent error in Indian English that examiners test.

Parallel structure

Items joined by 'and', 'or', 'not only… but also' or 'both… and' should share the same grammatical form. 'She likes reading, writing and to paint' should become 'reading, writing and painting'. 'He is not only intelligent but also works hard' is better as 'He is not only intelligent but also hard-working'. Options that restore this balance are frequently the answer.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Compare options side by side; they usually differ in just one or two words, which tells you the rule being tested.
  • Prefer the concise option when two are grammatical; wordy alternatives are often distractors.
  • 'Unless' already carries 'not', so 'unless you do not' is wrong.
  • 'Since' with time points (since 2020), 'for' with durations (for five years).
  • 'Prefer X to Y', not 'prefer X than Y'.
  • 'Lest' takes 'should' or a plain verb: 'Work hard lest you should fail', never 'lest you do not fail'.

Worked examples

Example 1

'If I was the manager, I would approve the leave.' Improve the underlined part 'If I was'.

Solution

For an imaginary or unreal condition, standard grammar uses 'were' for all subjects: 'If I were the manager'. Answer: If I were.

Example 2

'He has been working here since five years.' Improve 'since five years'.

Solution

'Five years' is a duration, so it takes 'for'. Answer: for five years.

Example 3

'Unless you do not submit the form, you cannot apply.' Improve 'Unless you do not submit'.

Solution

'Unless' already means 'if not', so adding 'do not' creates a double negative. Answer: Unless you submit.

Example 4

'If she would have left earlier, she would have caught the bus.' Improve 'If she would have left'.

Solution

In the third conditional, the 'if' clause uses past perfect: 'If she had left earlier'. Answer: If she had left.

Example 5

'I prefer tea than coffee.' Improve 'than coffee'.

Solution

'Prefer' is followed by 'to'. Answer: to coffee.

Example 6

'The new app is fast, secure and it is easy to use.' Improve 'and it is easy to use'.

Solution

The list should keep the same form: three adjectives. Replace with 'and easy to use', giving 'fast, secure and easy to use'.

Practice questions

Question 1

Improve the bold part: 'She **is knowing** the answer to that question.'

  1. has knowing
  2. knows
  3. is known
  4. No improvement
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. knows

'Know' is a stative verb and is not normally used in the continuous form. The simple present 'knows' is correct.

Question 2

Improve the bold part: 'Each of the players **have received** a medal.'

  1. No improvement
  2. were receiving
  3. has received
  4. have been received
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. has received

'Each' is singular, so the verb must also be singular. 'Has received' agrees with the subject and keeps the meaning.

Question 3

Improve the bold part: 'The meeting was cancelled **due to the reason that** the chairman was ill.'

  1. due to reason
  2. for reason that
  3. No improvement
  4. because
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. because

'Due to the reason that' is wordy and awkward. 'Because' expresses the same cause concisely and correctly.

Question 4

Improve the bold part: 'Hardly had she sat down **than the phone rang**.'

  1. when the phone rang
  2. then the phone rang
  3. and the phone rang
  4. No improvement
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. when the phone rang

'Hardly' is paired with 'when' in standard English. The correct form is 'Hardly had she sat down when the phone rang'.

Question 5

Improve the bold part: 'He insisted **to pay** the bill himself.'

  1. for paying
  2. on paying
  3. to paying
  4. No improvement
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. on paying

'Insist' takes the preposition 'on' followed by a gerund. The correct phrase is 'insisted on paying'.

FAQs

How is sentence improvement different from error spotting?

Error spotting only asks where the mistake is. Sentence improvement also asks you to choose the correct fix, so you need to know the right form, not just recognise the wrong one. Practising both together helps, since the underlying rules are the same.

What if two options are grammatically correct?

Choose the one that keeps the original meaning and sounds most natural and concise. Setters often add a grammatical but wordy or meaning-changing option as a distractor. Re-read the full sentence with each option before deciding.
