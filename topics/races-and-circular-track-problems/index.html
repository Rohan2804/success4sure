Races and Circular Track Problems: Relative Speed Tricks

Races and circular-track problems apply relative speed to sport-like settings. SSC CGL and CAT ask them most, while IBPS PO and RBI Grade B use them occasionally as a harder speed question. Phrases like "A beats B by 40 m" or "they meet at the starting point" have precise meanings that are easy to misread, so learning the standard interpretations is more valuable here than learning new formulas.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Reading "beats by"

"In a 1 km race A beats B by 100 m" means that when A crosses the finish line, B has run 900 m. Since they ran for the same time, their speeds are in the ratio 1000 : 900 = 10 : 9. "Beats by t seconds" means B reaches the finish t seconds after A.

Chaining races

If A beats B and B beats C over the same distance, combine the speed ratios. When A finishes, B has run a known distance; at that moment C has covered B’s distance multiplied by the C : B ratio. This avoids comparing times.

Circular track meetings

Two runners starting together on a track of length L meet for the first time after L ÷ (sum of speeds) if they run in opposite directions, and L ÷ (difference of speeds) if they run in the same direction, because the faster runner must gain a full lap.

First meeting: L / (S1 ± S2)

Meeting at the start

Both runners are back at the starting point together after the LCM of their individual lap times. For lap times of 120 s and 600/7 s, the LCM is 600 s, when one has run 5 laps and the other 7.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Head start for a dead heat = the distance by which A beats B.
  • If A beats B by x m in a race of d m, B runs (d − x) m in A’s time; B’s speed = A’s speed × (d − x)/d.
  • LCM of fractions = LCM of numerators ÷ HCF of denominators — handy for lap times.
  • On a circular track, the number of distinct meeting points (opposite directions) equals the sum of the speeds in lowest ratio terms; (same direction) equals their difference.

Worked examples

Example 1

In a 1 km race, A beats B by 100 m. Find the ratio of their speeds and the start A can give B for a dead heat.

Solution

When A runs 1,000 m, B runs 900 m, so speeds are 10 : 9. If B starts 100 m ahead, B needs only 900 m and both finish together, so the start is 100 m.

Example 2

In a 500 m race, A beats B by 40 m and B beats C by 50 m. By how much does A beat C?

Solution

When A finishes, B has run 460 m. B : C = 500 : 450, so C has run 460 × 450/500 = 414 m. A beats C by 86 m, not 90 m.

Example 3

Two runners on a 600 m circular track run at 5 m/s and 7 m/s. When do they first meet if they run in opposite directions, and in the same direction?

Solution

Opposite: 600 ÷ 12 = 50 s. Same direction: the faster one must gain 600 m at 2 m/s, which takes 300 s.

Example 4

For the runners above, when do they first meet again at the starting point?

Solution

Lap times are 120 s and 600/7 s. The LCM is 600 s: the first runner completes 5 laps and the second 7 laps.

Example 5

A runs 200 m in 40 seconds and beats B by 10 seconds. By how many metres does A beat B?

Solution

B takes 50 s for 200 m, so B’s speed is 4 m/s. In A’s 40 s, B runs 160 m. A beats B by 40 m.

Practice questions

Question 1

In a 100 m race, A beats B by 20 m. The ratio of their speeds is:

  1. 4 : 5
  2. 6 : 5
  3. 5 : 4
  4. 5 : 3
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 5 : 4

When A runs 100 m, B runs 80 m in the same time, so speeds are 100 : 80 = 5 : 4.

Question 2

In a 1,000 m race, A beats B by 100 m and B beats C by 100 m. By how much does A beat C?

  1. 200 m
  2. 180 m
  3. 210 m
  4. 190 m
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 190 m

When A finishes, B has run 900 m and C has run 900 × 0.9 = 810 m, so A beats C by 190 m.

Question 3

Two runners on a 400 m circular track run at 6 m/s and 4 m/s in opposite directions. They first meet after:

  1. 200 seconds
  2. 40 seconds
  3. 50 seconds
  4. 100 seconds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 40 seconds

Their relative speed is 10 m/s when running towards each other, so they meet after 400 ÷ 10 = 40 seconds.

Question 4

The same two runners (6 m/s and 4 m/s, 400 m track) run in the same direction. They first meet after:

  1. 200 seconds
  2. 40 seconds
  3. 100 seconds
  4. 160 seconds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 200 seconds

The faster runner must gain one full lap of 400 m at 2 m/s, which takes 200 seconds.

Question 5

In a 400 m race, A finishes in 50 seconds and B in 64 seconds. By how many metres does A beat B?

  1. 56 m
  2. 80 m
  3. 87.5 m
  4. 90 m
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 87.5 m

B runs at 400/64 = 6.25 m/s, covering 312.5 m in 50 seconds, so A wins by 87.5 m.

FAQs

Is "A beats B by 40 m" the same as "A gives B a 40 m start"?

They describe the same speed ratio but different races. In the first, both start together and B is 40 m short at the end. In the second, B starts 40 m ahead; if B’s speed ratio matches, the race ends in a dead heat. Questions mix these to test careful reading.

Why does the meeting time on a circular track use relative speed?

Because the meeting depends only on how fast the gap between the runners changes. Running towards each other, the gap of one lap closes at the sum of speeds. Running the same way, the faster runner must lap the slower one, gaining at the difference of speeds.
