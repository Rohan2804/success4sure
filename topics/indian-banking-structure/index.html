Indian Banking Structure: Scheduled, Commercial, Co-op

Understanding how Indian banks are classified is basic banking awareness for RBI Grade B, IBPS and SBI mains, JAIIB, and bank interviews. Questions ask which schedule makes a bank 'scheduled', who owns regional rural banks, what payments banks cannot do, and which year banks were nationalised. These facts are stable and appear year after year, so learning the structure once pays off across exams.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Scheduled and non-scheduled banks

Scheduled banks are those listed in the Second Schedule of the RBI Act, 1934. They must meet RBI criteria and can access RBI facilities such as refinance. Non-scheduled banks are not in the schedule. Most commercial banks in India are scheduled.

Commercial banks

Public sector banks are majority-owned by the government, such as SBI and Bank of Baroda. Private sector banks are majority-owned by private shareholders. Foreign banks operate in India through branches or wholly owned subsidiaries. Regional rural banks, small finance banks and payments banks are also commercial banks with special licences.

Regional rural banks

RRBs were set up under the Regional Rural Banks Act, 1976 to serve rural areas, with the first RRBs established in 1975 under an ordinance. Their share capital is held by the central government (50%), the sponsor bank (35%) and the state government (15%). Several rounds of amalgamation have reduced their number.

Small finance and payments banks

Small finance banks provide basic banking and lending mainly to small businesses, farmers and the unorganised sector, with priority sector lending requirements higher than those for universal banks. Payments banks accept deposits up to a limit set by the RBI, offer remittances and issue debit cards, but cannot lend or issue credit cards. Check the RBI for the current deposit limit.

Cooperative banks

Cooperative banks are owned by their members. Urban cooperative banks serve towns and cities. The rural short-term structure has three tiers: State Cooperative Banks, District Central Cooperative Banks and Primary Agricultural Credit Societies. Since the 2020 amendment to the Banking Regulation Act, the RBI's supervisory powers over cooperative banks have been strengthened.

Nationalisation milestones

The Imperial Bank of India became the State Bank of India in 1955. Fourteen major banks were nationalised in July 1969 and six more in April 1980. Later decades saw mergers that reduced the number of public sector banks.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Second Schedule of the RBI Act = scheduled bank.
  • RRB ownership: 50 : 35 : 15 (Centre : sponsor bank : state).
  • Payments banks: deposits yes, lending no, credit cards no.
  • Nationalisation: 14 banks in 1969, 6 banks in 1980.
  • SBI was formed in 1955 from the Imperial Bank of India.
  • Rural cooperative short-term structure: StCB → DCCB → PACS.

Worked examples

Example 1

A new payments bank wants to offer personal loans to its customers. Is this allowed?

Solution

No. Payments banks may accept deposits up to the RBI's limit, provide remittance services and issue debit cards, but they are not permitted to lend. They could distribute loans of other institutions as an agent, but not lend from their own books.

Example 2

An RRB has total share capital of ₹100 crore. How much would the sponsor bank hold?

Solution

Under the standard pattern, the sponsor bank holds 35%, so ₹35 crore. The central government holds ₹50 crore and the state government ₹15 crore.

Example 3

What makes a bank a 'scheduled bank'?

Solution

Inclusion in the Second Schedule of the RBI Act, 1934. The RBI includes banks that meet criteria such as minimum paid-up capital and reserves, and conduct their affairs in a way not detrimental to depositors.

Example 4

Interview question: 'Why were banks nationalised in 1969?'

Solution

A balanced answer: to extend banking to rural areas and neglected sectors such as agriculture and small industry, to direct credit according to national priorities, and to reduce the concentration of banking in the hands of a few business groups.

Practice questions

Question 1

Banks listed in which schedule of the RBI Act are called scheduled banks?

  1. First Schedule
  2. Third Schedule
  3. Fifth Schedule
  4. Second Schedule
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Second Schedule

Scheduled banks are those included in the Second Schedule of the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934, which gives them access to RBI facilities.

Question 2

What share of an RRB's capital is held by the sponsor bank?

  1. 35%
  2. 50%
  3. 15%
  4. 25%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 35%

Under the RRB Act, the share capital is split as 50% central government, 35% sponsor bank and 15% state government.

Question 3

Which of these can a payments bank NOT do?

  1. Accept deposits
  2. Give loans
  3. Issue debit cards
  4. Offer remittances
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Give loans

Payments banks are licensed for deposits within the RBI limit, remittances and debit cards, but they are not permitted to lend money.

Question 4

How many banks were nationalised in 1969?

  1. 6
  2. 20
  3. 14
  4. 10
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 14

Fourteen major commercial banks were nationalised in July 1969. A further six banks were nationalised in April 1980.

Question 5

The State Bank of India was formed in 1955 from which bank?

  1. Reserve Bank of India
  2. Bank of Bengal
  3. Punjab National Bank
  4. Imperial Bank of India
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Imperial Bank of India

The Imperial Bank of India was converted into the State Bank of India in 1955. The Imperial Bank itself had been formed by merging three presidency banks.

FAQs

Are small finance banks the same as payments banks?

No. Both are niche licences introduced around 2015, but small finance banks can lend and accept deposits like regular banks with a focus on underserved customers, while payments banks cannot lend at all. Some small finance banks have applied to convert into universal banks.

How many public sector banks are there now?

The number has fallen through mergers, especially the major consolidation that took effect in April 2020. Since further changes are possible, verify the current count and list from the Department of Financial Services or RBI before your exam rather than relying on older study material.
