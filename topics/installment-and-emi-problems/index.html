Installment and EMI Problems: CI and SI Methods

Installment questions ask what equal yearly payment clears a loan, or how much was borrowed if fixed payments are made. They appear in IBPS PO mains, SBI PO and RBI Grade B as harder interest questions, and in SSC CGL Tier 2. The idea is also practical: home, car and education loans in India are repaid through EMIs. The key concept is present value — each future payment is worth less today.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Present worth

Money due in the future is worth less today because it could earn interest meanwhile. Under compound interest at r% a year, ₹A due after n years has a present worth of A ÷ (1 + r/100)ⁿ. ₹4,840 due in 2 years at 10% is worth 4,840 ÷ 1.21 = ₹4,000 today.

PW = A / (1 + r/100)ⁿ

CI installments

With equal annual installments x under compound interest, the loan equals the sum of the present worths of all installments: P = x/(1 + r) + x/(1 + r)² + … (r as a decimal). At 10%, picking x as a multiple of 121 or 1,331 makes the arithmetic clean.

P = Σ x / (1 + r)ᵏ

SI installments

Under simple interest, compare everything at the final date. Each installment earns simple interest from its payment date to the end; the total must equal the debt due at the end. For a debt due in 3 years at 12% with annual payments x: x(1 + 0.24) + x(1 + 0.12) + x = 3.36x.

Monthly EMI

Bank EMIs use the same present-value idea with a monthly rate. EMI = P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ / [(1 + r)ⁿ − 1], where r is the monthly rate and n the number of months. Actual loan rates and terms vary by lender; this formula only illustrates the method.

EMI = P r (1 + r)ⁿ / ((1 + r)ⁿ − 1)

Shortcuts and tricks

  • At 10% CI, two installments of ₹1,210 repay ₹2,100 (1,100 + 1,000).
  • At 10% CI, three installments of ₹1,331 repay ₹3,310 (1,210 + 1,100 + 1,000).
  • Total interest paid = (number of installments × installment) − principal.
  • For SI installments, the last installment earns no interest; the first earns interest for the longest time.

Worked examples

Example 1

A loan of ₹2,100 is repaid in 2 equal annual installments at 10% compound interest. Find each installment.

Solution

x/1.1 + x/1.21 = 2,100. Try x = 1,210: 1,210/1.1 = 1,100 and 1,210/1.21 = 1,000, which add to 2,100. Each installment is ₹1,210.

Example 2

What equal annual installment will discharge a debt of ₹1,092 due in 3 years at 12% simple interest?

Solution

Value at the end of 3 years: first installment grows to 1.24x, second to 1.12x, third stays x. Total 3.36x = 1,092, so x = ₹325.

Example 3

A person borrows ₹3,310 at 10% compound interest and repays in 3 equal annual installments. Find the installment.

Solution

Present worths of ₹1,331 due in 1, 2 and 3 years are 1,210, 1,100 and 1,000, totalling 3,310. So each installment is ₹1,331.

Example 4

Find the present worth of ₹4,840 due in 2 years at 10% compound interest.

Solution

PW = 4,840 ÷ 1.21 = ₹4,000. In other words, ₹4,000 invested today at 10% compound interest grows to ₹4,840 in two years, so the two amounts are equivalent.

Example 5

Estimate the monthly EMI on a ₹1,00,000 loan for 12 months at 1% per month.

Solution

EMI = 1,00,000 × 0.01 × 1.01¹² ÷ (1.01¹² − 1) ≈ ₹8,885. Over 12 months the borrower pays about ₹1,06,619, so the interest is roughly ₹6,619, much less than simple interest of 12% on the full amount, because the principal reduces every month.

Practice questions

Question 1

A sum is repaid in two equal annual installments of ₹441 at 5% compound interest. The sum borrowed is:

  1. ₹840
  2. ₹882
  3. ₹820
  4. ₹800
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹820

Present worths are 441/1.05 = 420 and 441/1.1025 = 400, which add up to ₹820.

Question 2

₹5,500 is borrowed at 20% CI and repaid in two equal annual installments. Each installment is:

  1. ₹3,300
  2. ₹3,600
  3. ₹3,960
  4. ₹2,750
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹3,600

x/1.2 + x/1.44 = 5,500 gives x × 2.2/1.44 = 5,500, so x = ₹3,600.

Question 3

A debt of ₹848 due in 4 years at 4% simple interest is cleared by 4 equal annual installments. Each installment is:

  1. ₹212
  2. ₹190
  3. ₹200
  4. ₹180
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹200

Values at the end are x(1.12 + 1.08 + 1.04 + 1) = 4.24x = 848, so each installment is ₹200.

Question 4

The present worth of ₹6,655 due in 3 years at 10% compound interest is:

  1. ₹5,000
  2. ₹5,500
  3. ₹4,500
  4. ₹6,050
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹5,000

PW = 6,655 ÷ 1.331 = ₹5,000, discounting three years of 10% growth.

Question 5

In the ₹5,500 loan repaid by two installments of ₹3,600 at 20% CI, the total interest paid is:

  1. ₹1,100
  2. ₹2,200
  3. ₹1,500
  4. ₹1,700
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹1,700

Total paid is 2 × 3,600 = ₹7,200, so the interest is 7,200 − 5,500 = ₹1,700.

FAQs

Why is the installment more than the loan divided by the number of years?

Because the lender charges interest on the outstanding balance throughout the loan. Each installment covers part of the principal plus interest, so the total repaid exceeds the amount borrowed. The longer the term, the larger the total interest.

Do banks use these exact formulas for home loans?

Banks use the reducing-balance EMI formula, usually with monthly compounding, which matches the present-value approach here. Actual rates, fees and terms vary by lender and change over time, so always check the lender’s own schedule. Exam questions simply test the method.
