Arithmetic and Geometric Progressions: Formulas and Tricks

Progressions appear in CAT, CMAT and MAT every year and in SSC CGL as a regular algebra topic. Bank exams use them indirectly: number series often hide an AP or GP, and data sufficiency statements may describe equally spaced values. The formulas are compact, and many questions — counting multiples of 7 between two numbers, totalling a monthly savings plan — are simple once you recognise the progression.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Arithmetic progression (AP)

Each term increases by a constant difference d. The n-th term is a + (n − 1)d and the sum of n terms is n/2 × [2a + (n − 1)d], or n/2 × (first + last). The average of an AP equals the average of its first and last terms.

Sₙ = n/2 × (first + last)

Counting terms

The number of terms in an AP from a to l with difference d is (l − a)/d + 1. Two-digit multiples of 7 run from 14 to 98, giving (98 − 14)/7 + 1 = 13 numbers. This formula avoids listing.

n = (l − a)/d + 1

Geometric progression (GP)

Each term is multiplied by a constant ratio r. The n-th term is a·rⁿ⁻¹ and the sum of n terms is a(rⁿ − 1)/(r − 1) for r ≠ 1. For |r| < 1, the infinite sum converges to a/(1 − r).

S∞ = a / (1 − r)

Means

The arithmetic mean of a and b is (a + b)/2; the geometric mean is √(ab). For positive numbers, AM ≥ GM, with equality only when a = b. This inequality is used in CAT to find minimum or maximum values.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Sum of first n natural numbers = n(n + 1)/2; of first n even numbers = n(n + 1); of first n odd numbers = n².
  • If three numbers are in AP, write them as a − d, a, a + d; in GP, as a/r, a, ar — the algebra simplifies.
  • Middle term of an AP with an odd number of terms equals the average of all terms.
  • A GP with ratio 2 doubles: its n-th term is larger than the sum of all previous terms.
  • Increase-by-fixed-amount plans (salary, savings) are APs; increase-by-percentage plans are GPs.

Worked examples

Example 1

Find the 20th term of 7, 11, 15, …

Solution

a = 7, d = 4. T₂₀ = 7 + 19 × 4 = 83.

Example 2

Find the sum of the first 25 terms of 3, 8, 13, …

Solution

a = 3, d = 5. S = 25/2 × (6 + 24 × 5) = 25/2 × 126 = 1,575.

Example 3

How many two-digit numbers are divisible by 7?

Solution

The smallest two-digit multiple of 7 is 14 and the largest is 98. These form an AP with d = 7. Count = (98 − 14)/7 + 1 = 12 + 1 = 13. Forgetting the "+ 1" is the most common error and gives the trap option 12.

Example 4

Find the 8th term of the GP 3, 6, 12, …

Solution

a = 3, r = 2. T₈ = 3 × 2⁷ = 384.

Example 5

Find the sum of the infinite GP 18, 6, 2, …

Solution

a = 18, r = 1/3. S∞ = 18 ÷ (2/3) = 27.

Example 6

Priya saves ₹500 in the first month and increases her saving by ₹100 every month. How much does she save in a year?

Solution

This is an AP with a = 500, d = 100, n = 12. Last term = 500 + 11 × 100 = 1,600. Sum = 12/2 × (500 + 1,600) = 6 × 2,100 = ₹12,600.

Practice questions

Question 1

The 15th term of the AP 5, 9, 13, … is:

  1. 61
  2. 57
  3. 65
  4. 60
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 61

T₁₅ = 5 + 14 × 4 = 5 + 56 = 61, using a + (n − 1)d.

Question 2

The sum 2 + 4 + 6 + … + 60 equals:

  1. 900
  2. 960
  3. 930
  4. 1,830
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 930

There are 30 even numbers, and their sum is n(n + 1) = 30 × 31 = 930.

Question 3

How many three-digit numbers are divisible by 9?

  1. 99
  2. 100
  3. 101
  4. 111
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 100

They run from 108 to 999, so the count is (999 − 108)/9 + 1 = 99 + 1 = 100.

Question 4

The sum of the first 6 terms of the GP 2, 6, 18, … is:

  1. 486
  2. 1,458
  3. 242
  4. 728
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 728

S = 2(3⁶ − 1)/(3 − 1) = 729 − 1 = 728, using a(rⁿ − 1)/(r − 1).

Question 5

The sum of the infinite GP 1 + 1/3 + 1/9 + … is:

  1. 3/2
  2. 4/3
  3. 2
  4. 3
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 3/2

S∞ = a/(1 − r) = 1 ÷ (2/3) = 3/2, since the ratio 1/3 is less than 1.

FAQs

How do I identify whether a series is an AP or a GP?

Subtract consecutive terms: a constant difference means AP. Divide consecutive terms: a constant ratio means GP. If neither is constant, it may be a mixed series, such as differences forming an AP, which is common in bank number-series questions.

When does an infinite GP have a finite sum?

Only when the common ratio lies strictly between −1 and 1. Then the terms shrink towards zero and the total approaches a/(1 − r). For a ratio of 1 or more in size, the terms do not shrink and the sum grows without limit.
