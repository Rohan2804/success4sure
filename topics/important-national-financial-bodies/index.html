Important Financial Regulators and Bodies of India

Questions on regulators and development financial institutions are a staple of RBI Grade B, IBPS and SBI mains, JAIIB, CAIIB, SSC General Awareness and bank interviews. Typical questions ask which body regulates what, where it is headquartered and when it was set up. These facts change rarely, so a well-organised note covers many marks. Leadership names do change, so treat those as current affairs and check them separately.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Financial sector regulators

RBI (Mumbai) regulates banks, NBFCs and payment systems. SEBI (Mumbai), set up in 1988 and made statutory by the SEBI Act, 1992, regulates securities markets. IRDAI (Hyderabad), under the IRDA Act, 1999, regulates insurance. PFRDA (New Delhi) regulates pension funds and the National Pension System and became statutory under the PFRDA Act, 2013.

Development financial institutions

NABARD (Mumbai, 1982) supports agriculture and rural development and refinances rural lending. SIDBI (Lucknow, 1990) supports micro, small and medium enterprises. EXIM Bank (Mumbai, 1982) finances and promotes exports and imports. NHB (New Delhi, 1988) supports housing finance; its regulation of housing finance companies was transferred to the RBI in 2019.

Payments and deposit protection

NPCI (Mumbai, 2008) is an umbrella organisation for retail payments, operating UPI, IMPS, RuPay, NACH and others; it was promoted by the RBI and the Indian Banks' Association. DICGC (Mumbai, 1978) is an RBI subsidiary that insures bank deposits up to a limit per depositor per bank — ₹5 lakh since 2020; confirm the current limit with DICGC.

Other key bodies

The Competition Commission of India (New Delhi) enforces the Competition Act, 2002. The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (New Delhi) regulates insolvency professionals under the IBC, 2016. The Indian Banks' Association (Mumbai) represents banks. IIBF (Mumbai) conducts JAIIB and CAIIB.

Ministry and departments

The Department of Financial Services in the Ministry of Finance handles banks, insurance and pension policy, and the Department of Economic Affairs handles the budget and capital-market policy. Knowing which department owns which policy helps in interview questions.

How to remember who regulates what

Follow the product. A bank deposit, loan or UPI payment points to the RBI; a share, mutual fund or bond issue points to SEBI; a life, health or motor policy points to IRDAI; an NPS or APY account points to PFRDA. Interviewers often give a customer situation and ask which regulator a complaint should finally go to, so practise mapping everyday products to their regulator rather than memorising the list alone.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Mumbai: RBI, SEBI, NABARD, EXIM Bank, NPCI, DICGC, IBA, IIBF.
  • Hyderabad: IRDAI. Lucknow: SIDBI. New Delhi: PFRDA, NHB, CCI, IBBI.
  • 1982 twins: NABARD and EXIM Bank.
  • SEBI: set up 1988, statutory 1992.
  • DICGC is a wholly owned subsidiary of the RBI.
  • Heads of these bodies change; check names separately as current affairs.

Worked examples

Example 1

A complaint about an insurance company's claim rejection — which regulator oversees insurers?

Solution

IRDAI, headquartered in Hyderabad. The customer can also approach the Insurance Ombudsman after first complaining to the insurer.

Example 2

Which institution would refinance a cooperative bank's crop loans?

Solution

NABARD, which provides refinance to rural financial institutions for agricultural and rural lending.

Example 3

A customer has ₹7 lakh in a savings account and ₹3 lakh in a fixed deposit at the same bank. How much is insured by DICGC, assuming a ₹5 lakh limit?

Solution

The limit applies per depositor per bank, combining principal and interest across accounts in the same capacity. Total ₹10 lakh, so ₹5 lakh is insured (under the ₹5 lakh limit in force since 2020).

Example 4

Which body operates UPI?

Solution

The National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI), headquartered in Mumbai.

Example 5

A customer's mutual fund distributor mis-sold a scheme. Which regulator oversees mutual funds?

Solution

SEBI regulates mutual funds and their distribution rules, while AMFI, the industry body, registers distributors. The customer should first complain to the fund house and can then use SEBI's SCORES complaint system.

Practice questions

Question 1

Where is the headquarters of IRDAI?

  1. Mumbai
  2. New Delhi
  3. Hyderabad
  4. Chennai
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Hyderabad

The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India is headquartered in Hyderabad. Most other financial regulators are in Mumbai or New Delhi.

Question 2

Which institution primarily supports micro, small and medium enterprises?

  1. NABARD
  2. EXIM Bank
  3. NHB
  4. SIDBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. SIDBI

The Small Industries Development Bank of India, headquartered in Lucknow, is the principal financial institution for the MSME sector.

Question 3

Which organisation operates UPI, IMPS and RuPay?

  1. NPCI
  2. RBI
  3. SEBI
  4. SIDBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. NPCI

The National Payments Corporation of India runs retail payment systems including UPI, IMPS, RuPay and NACH.

Question 4

When did SEBI get statutory powers?

  1. 1988
  2. 1992
  3. 1999
  4. 2002
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 1992

SEBI was set up in 1988 as a non-statutory body and gained statutory powers through the SEBI Act, 1992.

Question 5

DICGC, which insures bank deposits, is a subsidiary of which body?

  1. Ministry of Finance
  2. SEBI
  3. Reserve Bank of India
  4. State Bank of India
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Reserve Bank of India

The Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation is a wholly owned subsidiary of the Reserve Bank of India.

FAQs

Do I need to know the heads of these bodies?

Yes for RBI Grade B, bank mains and interviews, but names change, so treat them as current affairs. Note the current RBI Governor, SEBI Chairperson, IRDAI Chairperson and similar from official websites shortly before your exam, separately from these static facts.

Is NHB still the regulator of housing finance companies?

No. Since 2019, housing finance companies are regulated by the RBI, while NHB continues to supervise them and provide refinance. Older study material may still describe NHB as the regulator, which is outdated.
