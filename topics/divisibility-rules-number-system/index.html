Divisibility Rules and Number System Basics

The number system chapter is a staple of SSC CGL, CHSL and MTS, where three or four questions on divisibility, factors and remainders are common. Bank exams test it indirectly through simplification and data sufficiency, and CAT uses it heavily in harder forms. Divisibility rules are the foundation: they let you fill missing digits, check answers and factorise quickly without long division.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Core divisibility rules

By 2: last digit even. By 3: digit sum divisible by 3. By 4: last two digits divisible by 4. By 5: last digit 0 or 5. By 8: last three digits divisible by 8. By 9: digit sum divisible by 9. By 11: difference between sums of digits in odd and even places is 0 or a multiple of 11.

Composite divisors

For 6, 12, 15, 18 and similar numbers, test the co-prime factors separately: divisible by 12 means divisible by both 3 and 4; by 18 means by both 2 and 9. Do not use 2 and 6 for 12, because they are not co-prime.

Number of factors

Write n = pᵃ × qᵇ × rᶜ in prime factors. The number of factors is (a + 1)(b + 1)(c + 1). For 360 = 2³ × 3² × 5, it is 4 × 3 × 2 = 24 factors. The number of odd factors ignores the power of 2: 3 × 2 = 6.

Factors = (a + 1)(b + 1)(c + 1)

Useful sums

Sum of the first n natural numbers = n(n + 1)/2; of the first n odd numbers = n²; of the first n even numbers = n(n + 1); of squares = n(n + 1)(2n + 1)/6. These appear directly in SSC and inside series questions.

Σn = n(n + 1)/2

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Missing digit for 9: add the known digits and choose the digit that takes the sum to the next multiple of 9.
  • For 8, only the last three digits matter — ignore everything before them.
  • A number divisible by 7, 11 and 13 is divisible by 1,001; abcabc-type numbers always are.
  • Every prime above 3 is of the form 6k ± 1 — a fast primality filter.
  • Product of any n consecutive integers is divisible by n!.

Worked examples

Example 1

Is 7,35,568 divisible by 8?

Solution

Check the last three digits: 568 ÷ 8 = 71 exactly. So 7,35,568 is divisible by 8.

Example 2

Find the digit * so that 63*472 is divisible by 9.

Solution

Known digit sum = 6 + 3 + 4 + 7 + 2 = 22. The next multiple of 9 is 27, so * = 5. (Using 36 would need * = 14, impossible.)

Example 3

Is 9,48,321 divisible by 11?

Solution

Digits from the right: 1, 2, 3, 8, 4, 9. Odd places (1st, 3rd, 5th) sum to 1 + 3 + 4 = 8; even places sum to 2 + 8 + 9 = 19. Difference = −11, a multiple of 11, so yes (9,48,321 = 11 × 86,211).

Example 4

How many factors does 360 have?

Solution

360 = 2³ × 3² × 5¹. Number of factors = (3 + 1)(2 + 1)(1 + 1) = 24.

Example 5

Find the sum of the first 50 odd natural numbers.

Solution

Sum of the first n odd numbers is n², so the sum is 50² = 2,500.

Example 6

Find the largest 4-digit number that is exactly divisible by 88.

Solution

Divide the largest 4-digit number by 88: 9,999 = 88 × 113 + 55. Subtract the remainder: 9,999 − 55 = 9,944. For the smallest 4-digit multiple, you would instead add (88 − remainder of 1,000) to 1,000. These two templates cover most "largest/smallest n-digit number divisible by k" questions.

Practice questions

Question 1

What is the smallest digit that can replace * in 4*57 to make it divisible by 3?

  1. 2
  2. 1
  3. 3
  4. 5
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 2

The known digits sum to 16; adding 2 gives 18, a multiple of 3, and smaller digits do not work.

Question 2

How many factors does 72 have?

  1. 10
  2. 12
  3. 8
  4. 9
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 12

72 = 2³ × 3², so the number of factors is (3 + 1)(2 + 1) = 12, from 1 up to 72.

Question 3

What is the smallest digit * that makes 3524*6 divisible by 8?

  1. 0
  2. 3
  3. 1
  4. 5
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 1

Only the last three digits matter: 406 is not divisible by 8 but 416 = 8 × 52, so * = 1.

Question 4

Which of these numbers is divisible by 11?

  1. 2,46,862
  2. 2,46,826
  3. 3,14,159
  4. 5,28,416
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 2,46,862

For 246862 the alternate digit sums are 2 + 8 + 4 = 14 and 6 + 6 + 2 = 14; the difference is 0, so it is divisible by 11.

Question 5

The sum of the natural numbers from 1 to 100 is:

  1. 5,000
  2. 5,100
  3. 5,050
  4. 10,100
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 5,050

Using n(n + 1)/2 with n = 100 gives 100 × 101/2 = 5,050, as in the well-known Gauss example.

FAQs

Why does the rule for 11 use alternating sums?

Because 10 leaves a remainder of −1 when divided by 11, so each digit contributes alternately + and − to the remainder. If the alternating sum is a multiple of 11 (including 0), the whole number is divisible by 11.

Is 1 a prime number?

No. A prime has exactly two distinct factors, 1 and itself. The number 1 has only one factor, so it is neither prime nor composite. The smallest prime is 2, which is also the only even prime.
