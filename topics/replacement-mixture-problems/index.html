Replacement Problems in Mixtures: Repeated Dilution Formula

Replacement questions — where some liquid is drawn out and replaced with water again and again — are a distinct sub-type of mixtures that IBPS PO, SBI PO and SSC CGL setters like because the naive approach (subtracting the same amount each time) gives a wrong answer that always appears among the options. One compact formula handles every version, and the reverse form helps find how much was drawn each time.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Why repeated removal is multiplicative

Each time x litres are drawn from a vessel of V litres, the same fraction x/V of every component leaves. So the pure liquid remaining is multiplied by (1 − x/V) every round. After three rounds of removing 4 L from 40 L, milk = 40 × 0.9³ = 29.16 L, not 40 − 12 = 28 L.

Pure left = V × (1 − x/V)ⁿ

Ratio after replacement

Once the remaining pure liquid is known, the rest of the vessel is water (or the replacing liquid), because the volume stays at V. With 80 L of wine and 8 L replaced twice, wine = 64.8 L and water = 15.2 L, giving a ratio 81 : 19.

Finding the quantity drawn

If the final amount is given, take the n-th root: (1 − x/V)ⁿ = final/initial. From 50 L of pure milk, two rounds leaving 32 L give (1 − x/50)² = 0.64, so 1 − x/50 = 0.8 and x = 10 L.

Replacing with another mixture

If part of a 30% solution is replaced by a 15% solution to get 25%, use alligation on concentrations: replaced part : kept part = (30 − 25) : (25 − 15) = 1 : 2, so one-third was replaced.

Fraction replaced = (original − final) / (original − new)

Shortcuts and tricks

  • Fraction left after n rounds is (1 − x/V)ⁿ; for x/V = 1/10, 1/5, 1/4 use 0.9, 0.8, 0.75 powers.
  • Exam numbers are chosen so that the root is clean: 0.64 → 0.8, 0.729 → 0.9, 0.5625 → 0.75.
  • If the replacing liquid is water, the ratio of milk to water after n rounds is (V − x)ⁿ : Vⁿ − (V − x)ⁿ.
  • The option equal to V − n × x is almost always the trap answer.

Worked examples

Example 1

A vessel has 40 litres of milk. 4 litres are taken out and replaced with water. This is done three times. How much milk is left?

Solution

Milk = 40 × (1 − 4/40)³ = 40 × 0.729 = 29.16 litres.

Example 2

From 80 litres of pure wine, 8 litres are drawn and replaced with water. The process is repeated once more. Find the ratio of wine to water.

Solution

Wine = 80 × 0.9² = 64.8 L; water = 80 − 64.8 = 15.2 L. Ratio = 648 : 152 = 81 : 19.

Example 3

A container has 50 litres of pure milk. A certain quantity is removed and replaced with water twice, leaving 32 litres of milk. How much was removed each time?

Solution

(1 − x/50)² = 32/50 = 0.64, so 1 − x/50 = 0.8 and x = 10 litres.

Example 4

A jar is full of a 30% acid solution. Part of it is replaced by a 15% solution, and the strength becomes 25%. What fraction was replaced?

Solution

Replaced : kept = (30 − 25) : (25 − 15) = 5 : 10 = 1 : 2. So 1/3 of the solution was replaced.

Example 5

A 50-litre can holds milk and water in the ratio 4 : 1. Ten litres of the mixture are taken out and replaced with water. Find the new ratio.

Solution

Milk at the start = 40 L. Removing 10 of 50 litres takes away one-fifth of each component, so milk left = 40 × 0.8 = 32 L. Total volume is back to 50 L, so water = 18 L. New ratio = 32 : 18 = 16 : 9. The formula works on the milk component even when the starting liquid is already a mixture.

Practice questions

Question 1

A tank has 60 litres of milk. 6 litres are removed and replaced with water, twice. Milk left is:

  1. 48 litres
  2. 48.6 litres
  3. 49.2 litres
  4. 50.4 litres
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 48.6 litres

Milk = 60 × 0.9² = 60 × 0.81 = 48.6 litres; subtracting 12 litres directly would be wrong.

Question 2

From 100 litres of milk, 20 litres are replaced with water twice. The milk-to-water ratio becomes:

  1. 4 : 1
  2. 3 : 2
  3. 16 : 9
  4. 16 : 5
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 16 : 9

Milk = 100 × 0.8² = 64 L and water = 36 L, so the ratio is 64 : 36 = 16 : 9.

Question 3

A 40-litre vessel of milk has x litres replaced with water twice, leaving 22.5 litres of milk. Find x.

  1. 10 litres
  2. 8 litres
  3. 12 litres
  4. 15 litres
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 10 litres

(1 − x/40)² = 22.5/40 = 0.5625, so 1 − x/40 = 0.75 and x = 10 litres.

Question 4

Part of a 50% solution is replaced by a 20% solution, giving 30%. The fraction replaced is:

  1. 1/3
  2. 1/2
  3. 3/5
  4. 2/3
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 2/3

Replaced : kept = (50 − 30) : (30 − 20) = 2 : 1, so two-thirds of the solution was replaced.

Question 5

From 25 litres of pure juice, 5 litres are replaced with water three times. Juice left is:

  1. 12.8 litres
  2. 10 litres
  3. 13.5 litres
  4. 12 litres
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 12.8 litres

Juice = 25 × (1 − 5/25)³ = 25 × 0.512 = 12.8 litres after three rounds.

FAQs

Why can’t I just subtract the quantity removed each time?

Because after the first round the vessel contains a mixture, so each later draw removes some water too. Only part of the drawn quantity is milk. The multiplicative formula accounts for this; simple subtraction overstates the milk removed.

Does the formula work if the vessel is not full?

It works as long as the total volume stays the same before each draw, because the fraction removed is x/V each time. If the volume changes between rounds, compute each round separately using that round’s volume.
