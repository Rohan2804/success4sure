Day, Week and Month Scheduling Puzzles Explained

Scheduling puzzles place people or events on days of a week, months of a year, or dates of a month. IBPS PO, SBI PO, IBPS Clerk and RBI Grade B prelims often include one such puzzle with five questions, and they appear in mains with a second attribute. They behave like linear arrangements, but the 'row' has a fixed natural order, and clues may use calendar facts such as the number of days in a month.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Treat time as a numbered row

Monday to Sunday become 1 to 7; January to December become 1 to 12. 'Before' means a smaller number, 'after' a larger one, and 'immediately before' means the previous number. Write the row vertically with the earliest at the top so it reads like a timetable.

Calendar facts examiners use

Months with 31 days: January, March, May, July, August, October and December. February has 28 or 29 days; April, June, September and November have 30. A clue such as 'R's exam is in a month with 31 days' among January–June leaves only January, March or May.

Weeks that do not start on Monday

Some puzzles say the week starts on Sunday or that events happen only on weekdays. Fix the numbering accordingly before reading other clues. A wrong starting day shifts every 'first' and 'last' clue.

Two events per slot

When two people share each day (morning and evening sessions), draw a grid of days by sessions. 'Same day' means the same row; 'immediately after' may mean the next session, so read how the puzzle defines order.

Shortcuts and tricks

  • 'Only one person between J and L' on a five-day schedule means their day numbers differ by two.
  • Neither the first nor the last day can be 'immediately after' or 'immediately before' in both directions; use this to cut cases.
  • List the 31-day months on your rough sheet once if a puzzle mentions month lengths.
  • Pair clues like 'M is immediately after K' into a block of two; there are only a few positions such a block can occupy.
  • When a puzzle mentions the year, remember a leap year changes February only.

Worked examples

Example 1

J, K, L, M and O attend a training session, one on each day from Monday to Friday. J attends on Wednesday. Only one person attends between J and L. M attends on the day immediately after K. O attends before L. Find the schedule.

Solution

Number Monday–Friday as 1–5. J = 3. L is 1 or 5. If L = 1, O must be before Monday — impossible, so L = 5. The K–M block needs two consecutive free days: only Monday–Tuesday remain together, so K = 1, M = 2, and O = 4. Schedule: K, M, J, O, L.

Example 2

Six people P, Q, R, S, T and U have exams in different months from January to June. R's exam is in a month with 31 days. R's exam is two months after P's. T's exam is in June. S's exam is before Q's, and U's exam is in the month immediately after Q's. Find the order.

Solution

R must be in January, March or May, and P is two months earlier, so R = March with P = January, or R = May with P = March. T = June. Case R = May: January, February and April are free; the Q–U pair needs two consecutive months, so Q = January and U = February, leaving no month for S before Q. This case fails. Case R = March: February, April and May are free; Q = April and U = May form the pair, and S = February is before Q. Final order: P (Jan), S (Feb), R (Mar), Q (Apr), U (May), T (Jun).

Example 3

From the second schedule, how many people have exams between S and T?

Solution

S is in February and T in June. March, April and May lie between, held by R, Q and U. So three people have exams between them.

Practice questions

Question 1

Using the Monday–Friday schedule K, M, J, O, L, who attends the training immediately before L?

  1. J
  2. O
  3. M
  4. K
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. O

L attends on Friday. The day immediately before is Thursday, and the schedule places O on Thursday.

Question 2

Which of these months has 31 days?

  1. September
  2. June
  3. August
  4. November
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. August

The 31-day months are January, March, May, July, August, October and December. September, June and November each have 30 days, so August is the answer.

Question 3

Five people attend sessions Monday to Friday, one per day. A attends on Tuesday, and only one person attends between A and B. Which day is B's session?

  1. Monday
  2. Wednesday
  3. Friday
  4. Thursday
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Thursday

With Monday–Friday as 1–5, A is day 2 and B must be day 2 + 2 = 4, because day 0 does not exist. Day 4 is Thursday.

Question 4

Events are held on seven consecutive days of a week that starts on Sunday. An event held on the fourth day is held on which day?

  1. Wednesday
  2. Thursday
  3. Tuesday
  4. Monday
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Wednesday

With Sunday as day 1, Monday is day 2, Tuesday is day 3 and Wednesday is day 4.

Question 5

In the January–June exam schedule P, S, R, Q, U, T, whose exam is in a 30-day month other than June?

  1. R
  2. Q
  3. U
  4. P
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Q

Among January to May, only April has 30 days (February has 28 or 29). Q's exam is in April, so Q is the answer.

FAQs

Do I need to memorise the number of days in every month?

Yes, it takes a few minutes and is used in both scheduling puzzles and calendar questions. The knuckle method works well: count months on your knuckles and the gaps between them; months on knuckles have 31 days, while months in the gaps have 30, except February.

How are scheduling puzzles different from linear seating?

The structure is the same, but the order is fixed by time, so there is no 'facing direction' trap. In return, examiners add calendar facts (month lengths, leap years, weekdays only) that you must apply correctly. Treat each day or month as a numbered seat and the method carries over directly.
