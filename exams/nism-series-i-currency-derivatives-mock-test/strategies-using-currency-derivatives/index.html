Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter applies futures and options to real views and exposures. It covers directional trades, hedging for importers and exporters, spreads (bull and bear, call and put, calendar), volatility strategies such as straddles and strangles, collars, protective positions and arbitrage between spot, futures and exchanges. Focus on matching each strategy to a market view, and on calculating net premium, maximum profit, maximum loss and break-even levels. Also understand how option-based hedges give protection while keeping some upside, unlike futures hedges, which lock in a single rate.

Strategies Using Currency Derivatives questions

Question 1

Writing an 84.00 USD/INR call while holding a long 85.00 call, both expiring together, creates which spread?

  1. Bull call spread
  2. Bear call spread
  3. Long strangle
  4. Short straddle
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Bear call spread

Selling the lower-strike call and buying the higher-strike call earns a net credit and profits if USD/INR stays below the lower strike. That is a bear call spread; a bull call spread is the reverse.

Question 2

With USD/INR spot at 84.00, a trader buys an 85.00 call and an 83.00 put of the same expiry. This strategy is a:

  1. Long strangle
  2. Long straddle
  3. Bull put spread
  4. Covered call
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Long strangle

Buying an out-of-the-money call and an out-of-the-money put is a long strangle. It is cheaper than a straddle, which uses the same strike for both, but needs a bigger move to become profitable.

Question 3

A trader expects USD/INR to remain in a narrow range until expiry, with low volatility. Which strategy fits this view?

  1. Long straddle
  2. Long strangle
  3. Buying a call and a put at different strikes
  4. Short straddle
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Short straddle

A short straddle collects premium on both a call and a put at the same strike and profits if the rate stays close to that strike. Long straddles and strangles need a large move, which the trader does not expect.

Question 4

An exporter buys a USD/INR 83.50 put at Rs 0.30 and sells an 85.00 call at Rs 0.30, same expiry. Ignoring costs, the effective rate he gets for his dollars at expiry will lie:

  1. Between 83.50 and 85.00
  2. At exactly 84.25 whatever happens
  3. Anywhere below 83.50 with no floor
  4. Above 85.00 with no cap
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Between 83.50 and 85.00

This is a zero-cost collar since premiums offset. The put sets a floor at 83.50 and the short call caps gains at 85.00, so the realised rate stays within that band.

Question 5

An importer wants protection against rupee depreciation but wishes to benefit if the rupee strengthens before his payment date. Which exchange-traded position suits him?

  1. Sell USD/INR futures
  2. Sell a USD/INR put option
  3. Buy a USD/INR call option
  4. Buy a USD/INR put option
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Buy a USD/INR call option

A long call caps the rupee cost of dollars at the strike plus premium, yet if USD/INR falls he lets it lapse and buys cheaper in the market. Selling a put gives no protection against a rising rate.

Question 6

A trader buys a USD/INR 84.50 put at Rs 0.60 and sells an 84.00 put at Rs 0.30 (lot USD 1,000). What is his maximum loss on one lot?

  1. Rs 200
  2. Rs 300
  3. Rs 500
  4. Rs 900
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Rs 300

This bear put spread costs a net 0.60 - 0.30 = Rs 0.30 per USD, which is lost if USD/INR ends above 84.50. Maximum loss = 0.30 x 1,000 = Rs 300; maximum profit would be (0.50 - 0.30) x 1,000 = Rs 200.

Question 7

A position of long USD/INR futures combined with a long USD/INR put option behaves most like:

  1. A long call option
  2. A short call option
  3. A long put option
  4. A short futures position
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A long call option

Long futures gains as the rate rises; the put limits losses if it falls. The combined payoff has limited downside and open upside, the same shape as a long call. This is the protective put strategy.

Question 8

A trader expects the rupee to appreciate sharply against the US dollar over the next month. The simplest futures position for this view is to:

  1. Buy USD/INR futures
  2. Buy EUR/USD futures
  3. Sell USD/INR futures
  4. Buy USD/INR futures and sell an equal number of USD/INR futures
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Sell USD/INR futures

Rupee appreciation means USD/INR falls, so a short USD/INR futures position gains. Buying and selling equal contracts of the same future leaves no net exposure, and EUR/USD does not involve the rupee.
