Codes of Conduct & Investor Protection: NISM Series I practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Codes of Conduct & Investor Protection, from the NISM Series I (Currency Derivatives) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the conduct expected of trading members and their staff and the mechanisms that protect investors. It includes the code of conduct for stock brokers under SEBI regulations, KYC and client registration through KYC registration agencies, the rights and obligations document, handling of client funds and running account settlement, and avoiding unauthorised or excessive trading. It also covers the grievance redressal ladder, from the broker to SCORES and the online dispute resolution mechanism, and investor protection funds. Focus on the sequence of grievance steps and the duties a broker owes to every client.

Codes of Conduct & Investor Protection questions

Question 1

An investor has a complaint about trades done by his broker in currency derivatives. What should normally be his first step?

  1. File a civil suit in the Supreme Court
  2. Complain directly to the Reserve Bank of India
  3. Write to the broker's grievance redressal desk
  4. Stop paying margin on all open positions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Write to the broker's grievance redressal desk

Investors should first approach the intermediary itself. If not resolved, they can escalate to SEBI's SCORES and then to online dispute resolution. Courts and RBI are not the first point of contact.

Question 2

If an investor is not satisfied with the resolution of a complaint against a stock broker, SEBI's framework allows him to seek conciliation and arbitration through:

  1. The Online Dispute Resolution (ODR) platform
  2. The RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme
  3. The Insurance Ombudsman
  4. The Lok Sabha Petitions Committee
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The Online Dispute Resolution (ODR) platform

SEBI's ODR mechanism connects investors and intermediaries with independent conciliators and arbitrators online. The RBI ombudsman handles banking complaints, and the insurance ombudsman handles insurance cases.

Question 3

The Investor Protection Fund maintained by stock exchanges is mainly used to:

  1. Compensate genuine investor claims against a broker declared a defaulter
  2. Guarantee a minimum return on derivatives trades
  3. Reimburse trading losses of clients during periods of high volatility, so that no investor ever loses capital
  4. Fund marketing campaigns of member brokers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Compensate genuine investor claims against a broker declared a defaulter

The IPF pays eligible investor claims, within limits, when a member broker is declared a defaulter. It does not cover normal trading losses or guarantee any returns.

Question 4

Client KYC records collected by one SEBI intermediary can be accessed by other intermediaries through:

  1. The Credit Information Bureau
  2. A KYC Registration Agency (KRA)
  3. The Registrar of Companies
  4. The Income-tax e-filing portal
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A KYC Registration Agency (KRA)

KRAs registered with SEBI hold the KYC records of investors, so once KYC is done with one intermediary others can fetch it. This avoids repeated documentation by the client.

Question 5

Which action by a trading member's staff would breach the code of conduct?

  1. Explaining the risks of currency derivatives to a client in detail before the account is activated for trading
  2. Sending a contract note within 24 hours of a trade
  3. Promising a client assured monthly returns from currency futures trading
  4. Recording client orders before placing them
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Promising a client assured monthly returns from currency futures trading

Brokers must not guarantee returns or make misleading statements. Explaining risks, issuing timely contract notes and keeping order records are all expected practices under the regulations.

Question 6

A broker repeatedly places frequent trades in a client's account mainly to earn brokerage, without regard to the client's interest. This practice is known as:

  1. Hedging
  2. Arbitrage
  3. Churning
  4. Netting
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Churning

Churning is excessive trading done to generate commissions, and it breaches the duty to act in the client's interest. Hedging, arbitrage and netting are legitimate market activities.

Question 7

Under SEBI's running account rules, unused client funds lying with a broker must be:

  1. Retained by the broker until the client closes the account
  2. Invested by the broker in its own name for extra return
  3. Transferred to the exchange's investor protection fund
  4. Settled back to the client periodically, as per the client's chosen monthly or quarterly cycle
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Settled back to the client periodically, as per the client's chosen monthly or quarterly cycle

Brokers must periodically settle client running accounts and return unused funds, subject to permitted retention for obligations. Keeping funds indefinitely or using them for the broker's benefit is not allowed.

Question 8

The document that sets out the rights and obligations of the stock broker and the client, as prescribed by SEBI and the exchanges, is:

  1. The Memorandum of Association
  2. The Rights and Obligations document
  3. The Offer Document
  4. The Contract Note
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The Rights and Obligations document

The Rights and Obligations document, given at account opening, lays out mandatory terms between broker and client. A contract note records individual trades, and an offer document relates to public issues.
