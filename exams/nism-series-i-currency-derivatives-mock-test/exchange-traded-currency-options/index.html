Exchange Traded Currency Options: NISM Series I practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Exchange Traded Currency Options, from the NISM Series I (Currency Derivatives) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers options on currency pairs traded on Indian exchanges: calls and puts, European exercise, premium quoted in rupees, cash settlement against the reference rate and automatic exercise of in-the-money contracts. It explains moneyness, intrinsic and time value, payoffs for buyers and writers, factors that drive premiums, and the option Greeks such as delta, gamma, theta and vega. When revising, practise payoff and break-even calculations for both long and short positions and understand how spot, strike, volatility, time and interest rates affect call and put premiums.

Exchange Traded Currency Options questions

Question 1

What is the maximum profit that the writer (seller) of a USD/INR put option can earn?

  1. The strike price minus the premium
  2. The premium received
  3. Unlimited, if the rupee appreciates sharply
  4. The strike price multiplied by the lot size
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The premium received

A writer's best case is that the option expires worthless, so he keeps the premium received. His potential loss is large if the rate falls well below the strike, but his profit is capped at the premium.

Question 2

USD/INR spot is 84.10. What is the intrinsic value of a USD/INR put option with a strike of 83.75?

  1. Rs 0.35
  2. Zero
  3. Rs 83.75
  4. Rs 0.35 plus the premium
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Zero

A put has intrinsic value only when the strike is above spot. Here the strike 83.75 is below spot 84.10, so the put is out of the money and its intrinsic value is zero; its whole premium is time value.

Question 3

Which option Greek measures the fall in an option's premium as time passes, other things remaining equal?

  1. Delta
  2. Gamma
  3. Theta
  4. Rho
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Theta

Theta measures time decay. Delta measures sensitivity to the underlying rate, gamma measures how fast delta changes, and rho measures sensitivity to interest rates.

Question 4

If expected volatility of USD/INR rises sharply, other things unchanged, the premiums of USD/INR calls and puts will generally:

  1. Rise for calls and fall for puts
  2. Rise for both calls and puts
  3. Fall for both calls and puts
  4. Remain unchanged for both
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Rise for both calls and puts

Higher volatility increases the chance that any option finishes deep in the money, while the buyer's loss is still capped. So both call and put premiums rise; this sensitivity is measured by vega.

Question 5

A trader writes 2 lots of USD/INR 84.00 puts (lot USD 1,000) at a premium of Rs 0.25. The final settlement price is 83.60. His net result is:

  1. Profit of Rs 500
  2. Loss of Rs 800
  3. Loss of Rs 300
  4. Profit of Rs 300
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Loss of Rs 300

The put is in the money by 84.00 - 83.60 = 0.40. Net loss per USD is 0.40 - 0.25 = 0.15, so for 2 lots: 0.15 x 1,000 x 2 = Rs 300. Rs 800 ignores the premium received.

Question 6

Other things remaining the same, which change will increase the premium of a USD/INR call option?

  1. A fall in expected volatility
  2. A rise in the USD/INR spot rate
  3. A reduction in the time left to expiry
  4. A higher strike price for the same expiry
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A rise in the USD/INR spot rate

A call gains value when the underlying rises because it moves closer to, or deeper into, the money. Lower volatility, less time to expiry and a higher strike all reduce a call's premium.

Question 7

On expiry, what happens to an in-the-money USD/INR option held by a buyer who takes no action?

  1. It lapses because the buyer did not submit an exercise request
  2. It is settled by delivery of US dollars to the buyer
  3. It is rolled over to the next month's contract
  4. It is automatically exercised and settled in cash in rupees
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. It is automatically exercised and settled in cash in rupees

Exchange-traded currency options in India are European, cash-settled and automatically exercised at expiry if in the money. The buyer receives the difference between settlement price and strike, in rupees.

Question 8

Premium for a USD/INR option traded on an Indian exchange is quoted in:

  1. US dollars per rupee
  2. Percentage of the strike price
  3. Rupees per lot of USD 100,000
  4. Rupees per US dollar
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Rupees per US dollar

Like the futures price, the option premium is quoted in rupees per unit of the base currency, i.e. per USD. Total premium payable is the quoted premium multiplied by the lot size of USD 1,000.
