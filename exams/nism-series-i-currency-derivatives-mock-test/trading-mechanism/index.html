Trading Mechanism: NISM Series I practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Trading Mechanism, from the NISM Series I (Currency Derivatives) syllabus.

What this chapter covers

This chapter describes how currency derivatives are traded on Indian exchanges: the fully automated, screen-based, anonymous order-matching system, types of members and their roles, client registration and unique client codes, and order types such as market, limit, stop-loss, immediate-or-cancel and day orders. It also covers order matching on price-time priority, contract notes and the basic duties of trading members towards clients. When revising, be clear about how each order type behaves, who may trade or clear for whom, and the documents and timelines involved in client dealings.

Trading Mechanism questions

Question 1

An immediate-or-cancel (IOC) order in currency futures is one that:

  1. Stays in the order book until the end of the trading day
  2. Is executed only when a trigger price is reached
  3. Remains valid in the order book until the contract expires unless the client cancels it earlier
  4. Is executed at once to the extent possible, and any unexecuted part is cancelled
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Is executed at once to the extent possible, and any unexecuted part is cancelled

An IOC order must match immediately as soon as it reaches the market; whatever is not matched at once is cancelled. A day order stays in the book until the close, and a stop-loss order waits for a trigger.

Question 2

Two buy orders for USD/INR futures are in the book at the same price of 84.10. Which will be matched first against an incoming sell order at 84.10?

  1. The order with the larger quantity
  2. The order placed by a clearing member
  3. The order that was entered earlier
  4. The orders are matched in proportion to their size
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The order that was entered earlier

Exchanges match orders on price-time priority. The best price gets priority, and at the same price the order that reached the system first is executed first. Order size or member type does not change priority.

Question 3

A Trading-cum-Clearing Member (TCM) in the currency derivatives segment can:

  1. Only clear trades and never execute trades on the exchange
  2. Only trade on its own behalf, with clearing done by the exchange
  3. Trade on its own and clients' behalf and also clear trades of itself and other trading members
  4. Trade only for institutional and corporate clients, with no right to accept retail individuals as clients
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Trade on its own and clients' behalf and also clear trades of itself and other trading members

A TCM has both trading and clearing rights: it executes its own and clients' trades and can clear and settle for other trading members too. A professional clearing member only clears and does not trade.

Question 4

A Self-Clearing Member (SCM) differs from a TCM in that an SCM:

  1. Cannot place orders on the exchange at all
  2. Clears trades for all trading members of the exchange
  3. Clears and settles only its own trades, including those of its clients
  4. Is not required to deposit any margin with the clearing corporation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Clears and settles only its own trades, including those of its clients

An SCM has trading rights and clears only the trades it executes, for itself and its own clients. It cannot clear for other trading members, which a TCM may do, and it must still deposit margins.

Question 5

A client places a market order to buy 5 USD/INR futures contracts. This order will be executed:

  1. Only at the previous day's settlement price
  2. At the best price available in the order book at that time
  3. Only if the price falls below the last traded price within the next few minutes of entry
  4. At the FBIL reference rate for that day
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. At the best price available in the order book at that time

A market order has no price limit and matches immediately against the best available counter orders. It guarantees execution if liquidity exists, but not the price, unlike a limit order.

Question 6

A client wants to buy a large number of contracts but show only a small part of the quantity in the market at a time. He should use:

  1. An immediate-or-cancel order
  2. A stop-loss order
  3. A disclosed quantity order
  4. A market-on-close order
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A disclosed quantity order

A disclosed quantity order reveals only part of the total order to the market; as that part is filled, the next part is shown. This reduces market impact. IOC and stop-loss orders do not hide quantity.

Question 7

Within what time must a trading member issue a contract note to a client after executing a trade on the client's behalf?

  1. Within 7 working days of the trade
  2. At the end of each calendar month
  3. Within 24 hours of the trade
  4. Only when the client asks for it
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Within 24 hours of the trade

Trading members must issue a contract note, physical or electronic, within 24 hours of execution. It records the trade price, quantity, time and charges and is the client's legal evidence of the trade.

Question 8

A day order for EUR/INR futures that is not executed during the session will:

  1. Be carried forward automatically to the next trading day at the same limit price
  2. Be converted into a market order at the close
  3. Remain valid until the contract expires
  4. Be cancelled automatically at the end of the trading day
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Be cancelled automatically at the end of the trading day

A day order is valid only for the trading day on which it is placed. Any unexecuted quantity is removed from the book at the close, and the client must re-enter it the next day if still required.
