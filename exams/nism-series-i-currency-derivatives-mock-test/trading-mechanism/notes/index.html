Trading Mechanism: NISM Series I revision notes

This chapter explains how currency derivatives are traded on Indian exchanges: the participants, the electronic trading system, order types and the rules for client identification and positions. Questions are factual, often about how a specific order type behaves or which member does what.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Trading platforms

NSE, BSE and MSE run currency derivatives segments with anonymous, screen-based order matching on price-time priority. Trading hours for currency derivatives typically run from 9:00 am to 5:00 pm on weekdays, with earlier cut-offs on expiry for maturing contracts. Check the exchange for current timings.

2. Member types

Trading members execute trades for themselves and clients. Clearing members clear and settle trades. Self-clearing members clear only their own trades. Professional Clearing Members, typically banks or custodians, clear and settle trades executed by other trading members but do not trade.

3. Limit and market orders

A limit order to buy USD/INR futures at 83.50 executes only at 83.50 or lower. A market order executes immediately at the best available price, with the risk of slippage in thin markets. A limit order gives price certainty but may not execute at all, while a market order gives execution certainty but not price certainty.

4. Stop-loss orders

A stop-loss sell order with a trigger of 83.20 becomes active only when the market trades at or below 83.20. It then becomes a limit or market order. Stop-losses help traders cap losses without watching screens continuously. A gap in prices can cause execution below the trigger.

5. Order conditions

Day orders lapse at the end of the trading day if unexecuted. Immediate or Cancel orders execute at once, fully or partly, and cancel the rest. Orders must stay within the exchange's operating price range to prevent erroneous trades. Unexecuted orders do not carry over to the next day.

6. Client registration

Before placing orders for a client, a trading member must complete KYC, register the client with the exchange under a Unique Client Code with PAN, and provide the Risk Disclosure Document and the rights and obligations document. Orders without a valid UCC are rejected.

7. Position limits

Exchanges and SEBI set position limits at client, trading member and bank level, typically linked to a share of total open interest or a fixed dollar amount, whichever is higher. These limits prevent concentration and manipulation. Limits differ for each currency pair.

8. RBI's exposure requirement

RBI's rules on exchange-traded currency derivatives require users to have an underlying exposure to currency risk, whether contracted or anticipated. Users may take positions up to a specified limit without producing documents, but must still have the exposure. Check the current RBI directions.

Common traps in the exam

  • Thinking a Professional Clearing Member can trade for its own book - it only clears for trading members.
  • Believing a stop-loss order executes at once; it waits for the trigger price.
  • Assuming anyone can trade currency derivatives purely to speculate under RBI's current rules - an underlying exposure is expected.
  • Forgetting that orders without a registered UCC are rejected.

Quick recap

Currency derivatives trade on NSE, BSE and MSE with price-time priority. Trading members trade; clearing members settle; PCMs only clear for others. Limit, market, stop-loss, day and IOC orders behave differently. UCC with PAN and the RDD are required before trading. Position limits apply; RBI expects users to have underlying exposure.
