What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the laws and regulators that govern currency derivatives in India. RBI regulates the foreign exchange market under the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999, deciding which contracts and participants are permitted, while SEBI regulates exchanges, clearing corporations and intermediaries under the SEBI Act, 1992 and the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956. It also covers the RBI-SEBI coordination mechanism, the requirement for underlying exposure for users, rules for bank membership and certification of associated persons. Focus on which authority handles which aspect and the main laws behind each.

Regulatory Framework questions

Question 1

Which law defines derivatives as securities and governs recognised stock exchanges on which currency derivatives trade?

  1. Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999
  2. Banking Regulation Act, 1949
  3. Companies Act, 2013
  4. Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956

The SCRA, 1956 includes derivatives in the definition of securities and provides for recognition and regulation of stock exchanges. FEMA governs foreign exchange transactions, while the Banking Regulation Act covers banks.

Question 2

Exchange-traded currency futures were first introduced in India in:

  1. 2008
  2. 1993
  3. 2000
  4. 2015
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 2008

Trading in USD/INR futures began on Indian exchanges in August 2008. Options on USD/INR followed in 2010. The years 1993 and 2000 relate to exchange rate unification and FEMA coming into force.

Question 3

Coordination between RBI and SEBI on exchange-traded currency and interest rate derivatives is carried out mainly through:

  1. The RBI-SEBI Standing Technical Committee
  2. The Monetary Policy Committee
  3. The GST Council
  4. The Board for Financial Supervision
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The RBI-SEBI Standing Technical Committee

The RBI-SEBI Standing Technical Committee was set up to coordinate on product design and regulation of exchange-traded currency and interest rate derivatives. The MPC sets the policy repo rate and has no such role.

Question 4

Under RBI's framework for exchange-traded currency derivatives involving the rupee, a user taking positions for hedging is expected to:

  1. Hold a banking licence before taking any position
  2. Trade only in cross-currency pairs that do not involve the rupee
  3. Obtain SEBI's prior written approval for each order and every modification of an existing order or position
  4. Have an underlying exposure, contracted or anticipated, to the currency risk being hedged
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Have an underlying exposure, contracted or anticipated, to the currency risk being hedged

RBI permits users to participate in rupee-linked exchange-traded contracts to hedge genuine currency risk. Up to a threshold users need not show documents, but the underlying exposure must exist. No per-order approval is required.

Question 5

FEMA, 1999, which governs foreign exchange transactions in India, replaced which earlier law?

  1. Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956
  2. Foreign Exchange Regulation Act, 1973
  3. Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934
  4. Sale of Goods Act, 1930
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Foreign Exchange Regulation Act, 1973

FEMA replaced FERA, 1973, which treated violations as criminal offences. FEMA shifted to a facilitation approach with civil penalties. The RBI Act, 1934 continues in force and was not replaced.

Question 6

Banks that wish to become trading or clearing members of the currency derivatives segment of an exchange must follow the conditions prescribed by:

  1. The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India
  2. The Ministry of Corporate Affairs
  3. The Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority
  4. The Reserve Bank of India
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The Reserve Bank of India

Banks are regulated by RBI, and RBI lays down prudential conditions such as net worth and capital adequacy for banks seeking membership. They must also meet the exchange's own membership rules.

Question 7

Which authority regulates the over-the-counter foreign exchange market and authorised dealer banks in India?

  1. The Securities and Exchange Board of India
  2. The Reserve Bank of India
  3. The Forward Markets Commission
  4. The National Stock Exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The Reserve Bank of India

RBI regulates OTC forex dealing and authorised dealers under FEMA. SEBI's role is limited to exchange-traded products and market intermediaries. The Forward Markets Commission was merged with SEBI in 2015.

Question 8

The requirement that associated persons of currency derivatives brokers hold the NISM Series I certificate arises from:

  1. Foreign Exchange Management (Current Account Transactions) Rules, 2000
  2. The Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934
  3. SEBI (Certification of Associated Persons in the Securities Markets) Regulations, 2007
  4. The Prevention of Money-laundering Act, 2002
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. SEBI (Certification of Associated Persons in the Securities Markets) Regulations, 2007

SEBI's certification regulations of 2007 empower it to require NISM certification for persons dealing with investors or handling trades. FEMA rules and the RBI Act do not deal with intermediary staff certification.
