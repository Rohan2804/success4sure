Accounting and Taxation: NISM Series I practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Accounting and Taxation, from the NISM Series I (Currency Derivatives) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how currency derivatives transactions are accounted for and taxed. It explains accounting entries for initial margin, daily mark-to-market and final settlement, the fair value approach under Ind AS 109 for companies, and the income-tax treatment of exchange-traded derivatives as non-speculative business income. It also covers set-off and carry-forward of losses, turnover computation for tax audit, the applicable return form and indirect charges such as GST and stamp duty. Focus on the logic behind classification of income and on which charges apply to currency derivatives compared with equity derivatives.

Accounting and Taxation questions

Question 1

Which provision of the Income-tax Act excludes eligible derivatives transactions on a recognised stock exchange from being treated as speculative transactions?

  1. Section 43(5)
  2. Section 80C
  3. Section 10(38)
  4. Section 194A
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Section 43(5)

Section 43(5) defines speculative transactions and its proviso excludes eligible trades in derivatives on recognised exchanges. Section 80C covers investment deductions and 194A deals with TDS on interest.

Question 2

In the books of a trader, the initial margin paid in cash to the broker for currency futures is generally shown as:

  1. An expense in the profit and loss account
  2. A current asset, such as margin deposit receivable
  3. Share capital of the business
  4. A long-term liability
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A current asset, such as margin deposit receivable

Initial margin is a deposit that will be returned when positions are closed, so it is an asset, not an expense. Only realised MTM losses and charges are taken to the profit and loss account.

Question 3

A trader has a non-speculative business loss from currency futures that he cannot set off this year. For how many assessment years can it be carried forward?

  1. 4 assessment years
  2. 2 assessment years
  3. It cannot be carried forward
  4. 8 assessment years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 8 assessment years

A non-speculative business loss can be carried forward for up to 8 assessment years and set off against business income. Speculative losses can be carried forward for only 4 years.

Question 4

A salaried individual makes a loss from trading currency futures in a year. Under income-tax rules, this business loss:

  1. Can be fully set off against his salary income
  2. Can be set off against salary up to Rs 2 lakh
  3. Must be set off only against capital gains
  4. Cannot be set off against his salary income
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Cannot be set off against his salary income

Section 71(2A) bars setting off a business loss against income under the head 'Salaries'. It can be set off against other heads such as house property or capital gains in the same year, and the balance carried forward.

Question 5

On currency derivatives trades, GST is charged on:

  1. Brokerage and exchange transaction charges
  2. The full notional value of each contract
  3. The premium amount received by option writers
  4. The mark-to-market profit of the client
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Brokerage and exchange transaction charges

GST applies to services, so it is charged on brokerage, exchange transaction charges and similar fees. It is not levied on the contract value, the premium or the client's trading profit.

Question 6

For tax audit purposes, turnover from derivatives trading is generally computed as:

  1. The total notional value of all contracts traded
  2. Only the net profit for the year
  3. The total initial margin deposited during the year
  4. The aggregate of favourable and unfavourable differences on settled trades
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The aggregate of favourable and unfavourable differences on settled trades

As per the ICAI guidance on tax audit, turnover in derivatives is the absolute total of favourable and unfavourable differences on settled trades. Using notional contract value would hugely overstate it.

Question 7

An individual whose main income from currency futures is taxed as non-speculative business income will usually file which income-tax return form?

  1. ITR-3
  2. ITR-1
  3. ITR-2
  4. ITR-7
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ITR-3

ITR-3 is meant for individuals and HUFs with income from business or profession maintained under regular books. ITR-1 and ITR-2 do not cover business income, and ITR-7 is for trusts and similar entities.

Question 8

A listed company following Ind AS that holds currency futures not designated in a hedge relationship will generally measure them at:

  1. Historical cost until the contract expires
  2. The lower of cost or market value
  3. Notional value, disclosed only in the notes to accounts without any entry in the balance sheet
  4. Fair value, with changes recognised in profit or loss
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Fair value, with changes recognised in profit or loss

Under Ind AS 109, derivatives are measured at fair value. Unless hedge accounting applies, gains and losses go through profit or loss. Cost-based measurement would hide the derivative's actual value.
