Exchange Traded Currency Futures: NISM Series I revision notes

This is the heaviest chapter in Series I. You must know contract specifications for USD/INR and other rupee pairs, how futures are settled, how to compute MTM and basis, and how importers and exporters hedge. Specifications are revised occasionally, so confirm current details on NSE and BSE websites as of 2026.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Currency pairs traded

Indian exchanges offer futures on USD/INR, EUR/INR, GBP/INR and JPY/INR, plus cross-currency pairs such as EUR/USD, GBP/USD and USD/JPY. USD/INR is by far the most liquid contract, and volumes are concentrated in the near-month expiry. Cross-currency contracts are quoted in the second currency of the pair, such as US dollars per euro for EUR/USD.

2. Contract size and quotation

The USD/INR futures lot is USD 1,000, and EUR/INR and GBP/INR lots are 1,000 units of the foreign currency. JPY/INR is quoted in rupees per 100 yen, with a lot of 1,00,000 yen. Prices are quoted to four decimal places in rupees.

3. Tick size

The minimum price movement for rupee-pair futures is ₹0.0025, or a quarter of a paisa. On one USD/INR lot of USD 1,000, one tick is worth ₹2.50. Traders compute gains as ticks moved x tick value x number of lots.

4. Expiry and last trading day

Monthly contracts are available for up to 12 months. The last trading day is two working days before the last business day of the expiry month, and final settlement happens on the last business day. Trading on expiry day stops earlier than normal hours.

5. Cash settlement in rupees

USD/INR futures are cash settled in Indian rupees - no dollars change hands. The final settlement price is the RBI reference rate (published through FBIL) on the last trading day. Profits and losses are paid in rupees through the clearing corporation.

6. Daily mark-to-market

A trader buys 10 USD/INR lots at 83.20 and the settlement price that day is 83.45. MTM profit = (83.45 - 83.20) x 1,000 x 10 = ₹2,500, credited the next day. The next day's MTM is computed from 83.45.

7. Basis

Basis = spot - futures. With spot at 83.50 and near-month futures at 83.80, basis is -0.30. The futures premium reflects the interest rate differential and shrinks as expiry approaches, converging to the spot rate at final settlement. A wider gap means higher carry.

8. Importer hedge

An importer who must pay USD 50,000 in two months fears rupee depreciation. Buying 50 lots of USD/INR futures locks in the rate: if the dollar rises, futures gains offset the higher rupee cost of the payment. If the dollar falls, the futures lose but the payment becomes cheaper.

9. Exporter hedge outcome

An exporter sells USD/INR futures at 84.00. At expiry they settle at 85.00 and he sells his dollars in the spot market at 85.00. He loses ₹1 per dollar on futures but gains ₹1 versus 84.00 in spot, so his effective rate stays 84.00, ignoring basis and costs.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Futures P&L = (Exit price - Entry price) x Lot size x Number of lots (long)
  • Tick value = Tick size x Lot size = 0.0025 x 1,000 = ₹2.50 for USD/INR
  • Basis = Spot - Futures

Common traps in the exam

  • Quoting JPY/INR per one yen - it is quoted per 100 yen.
  • Assuming USD/INR futures are settled by delivery of dollars; they are cash settled in rupees.
  • Taking the last trading day as the last business day of the month - it is two working days earlier.
  • Hedging an importer's payable by selling futures; importers buy USD/INR futures.

Quick recap

USD/INR lot USD 1,000, tick ₹0.0025, monthly expiries up to 12 months, last trading day two working days before month-end, cash settled in rupees at the RBI/FBIL reference rate. JPY/INR is per 100 yen. MTM daily. Basis = spot - futures, converging at expiry. Importers buy, exporters sell.
