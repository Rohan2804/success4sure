Exchange Traded Currency Futures: NISM Series I practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Exchange Traded Currency Futures, from the NISM Series I (Currency Derivatives) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the design of currency futures traded on Indian exchanges: the INR pairs (USD/INR, EUR/INR, GBP/INR, JPY/INR) and cross-currency pairs (EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY), their lot sizes, quotation, tick size, expiry cycle, daily and final settlement prices and cash settlement in rupees. It also explains futures pricing, convergence with spot, basis and open interest, and how importers and exporters use futures to hedge. When revising, memorise contract specifications and practise mark-to-market and hedge-size calculations for each pair, not only USD/INR.

Exchange Traded Currency Futures questions

Question 1

What is the contract (lot) size of a GBP/INR futures contract on Indian exchanges?

  1. GBP 1,000
  2. GBP 10,000
  3. GBP 100
  4. GBP 100,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. GBP 1,000

GBP/INR futures, like USD/INR and EUR/INR, have a lot size of 1,000 units of the base currency. Only JPY/INR uses a much larger lot of JPY 100,000 because of the yen's low unit value.

Question 2

Which of the following is a cross-currency pair, not involving the rupee, on which futures are available on Indian exchanges?

  1. EUR/USD
  2. SGD/INR
  3. AUD/NZD
  4. CHF/JPY
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. EUR/USD

Indian exchanges offer futures on EUR/USD, GBP/USD and USD/JPY as cross-currency pairs. AUD/NZD and CHF/JPY are not listed, and SGD/INR involves the rupee and is not a listed pair either.

Question 3

As a USD/INR futures contract approaches its expiry, its price relative to the spot rate typically:

  1. Moves further away from the spot rate
  2. Becomes fixed at the price on the first trading day
  3. Converges towards the spot rate
  4. Doubles the spot premium seen at launch
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Converges towards the spot rate

The futures-spot gap reflects the interest cost of carry for the remaining period. As time to expiry shrinks, carry approaches zero, so the futures price converges to spot and the basis narrows.

Question 4

A trader sells 20 EUR/INR futures contracts (lot EUR 1,000) at 91.50. The day's settlement price is 91.80. His mark-to-market result for the day is:

  1. Profit of Rs 6,000
  2. Loss of Rs 6,000
  3. Loss of Rs 600
  4. Profit of Rs 3,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Loss of Rs 6,000

A short position loses when the price rises. Loss = (91.80 - 91.50) x 1,000 x 20 = Rs 6,000. The Rs 600 option ignores the number of contracts.

Question 5

A client holds 5 long GBP/INR futures (lot GBP 1,000). The previous day's settlement price was 106.40 and the final settlement price on expiry is 106.10. The final day's settlement amount is:

  1. Rs 1,500 receivable by the client
  2. Rs 1,500 payable by the client
  3. Rs 300 payable by the client
  4. Rs 4,500 payable by the client
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Rs 1,500 payable by the client

Earlier days' gains and losses were already settled through daily MTM. On expiry only the move from the last settlement price counts: (106.10 - 106.40) x 1,000 x 5 = -Rs 1,500, a payment by the long holder.

Question 6

An Indian importer must pay JPY 5,000,000 in two months and fears the rupee will weaken against the yen. To hedge with JPY/INR futures, he should:

  1. Sell 50 contracts
  2. Buy 50 contracts
  3. Buy 5,000 contracts
  4. Sell 5 contracts
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Buy 50 contracts

The importer loses if JPY/INR rises, so he must buy futures. With a lot size of JPY 100,000, he needs 5,000,000 / 100,000 = 50 contracts. Selling would add to his risk, not reduce it.

Question 7

Open interest in a currency futures contract refers to:

  1. The total number of contracts traded during the day
  2. The interest earned by the exchange on client margins
  3. The difference between the INR and USD interest rates
  4. The total number of contracts outstanding that have not yet been closed or settled
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The total number of contracts outstanding that have not yet been closed or settled

Open interest counts positions still open at the end of the day. Volume counts all trades done during the day, including those that opened and closed positions, so the two measures differ.

Question 8

For currency futures on Indian exchanges, the daily settlement price is generally based on:

  1. The first traded price of the day
  2. The weighted average price of trades in the last half hour of trading
  3. The FBIL reference rate published every day
  4. The highest price traded during the day
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The weighted average price of trades in the last half hour of trading

Daily MTM uses the volume-weighted average price of the last 30 minutes, or a theoretical price if there are no trades. The FBIL reference rate is used for final settlement on expiry, not for daily settlement.
