About the NISM Series I exam

NISM Series I tests how exchange-traded currency futures and options work in India — USD-INR and cross-currency contracts, pricing through interest rate parity, hedging for importers and exporters, trading and clearing mechanics, and the RBI and SEBI rules that govern the segment.

Who should take it: Dealers, sales staff and other associated persons of brokers who trade in the currency derivatives segment, and treasury professionals who want a structured grounding in currency hedging.

Sample 1 · Introduction to Currency Markets

In the quotation USD/INR = 84.10, which currency is the base currency?

  1. INR
  2. Both USD and INR equally
  3. USD
  4. Neither, because it is a cross rate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. USD

The first currency in a pair is the base currency; the quote tells how many units of INR (quote currency) buy one USD.

Sample 2 · Foreign Exchange Derivatives

Which feature is typical of an OTC currency forward but NOT of an exchange-traded currency future?

  1. Contract terms are customised bilaterally between the two parties
  2. Daily mark-to-market through a clearing corporation
  3. Standardised lot size and expiry date
  4. Novation by a clearing corporation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Contract terms are customised bilaterally between the two parties

Forwards are privately negotiated and tailor-made, whereas futures are standardised, cleared and marked to market daily.

Sample 3 · Exchange Traded Currency Futures

The standard contract size of a USD/INR futures contract on Indian exchanges is:

  1. USD 500
  2. USD 10,000
  3. USD 1,00,000
  4. USD 1,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. USD 1,000

USD/INR futures are traded in lots of USD 1,000, keeping the contract accessible to small hedgers.

Sample 4 · Exchange Traded Currency Options

Exchange-traded USD/INR options in India are of which exercise style?

  1. American
  2. European
  3. Bermudan
  4. Asian
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. European

They can be exercised only on the expiry date, which is the definition of a European option.

Sample 5 · Strategies Using Currency Derivatives

A trader expecting USD/INR to rise moderately buys an 84.00 call and sells an 85.00 call of the same expiry. This position is a:

  1. Bear put spread
  2. Long straddle
  3. Short strangle
  4. Bull call spread
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Bull call spread

Buying a lower-strike call and selling a higher-strike call creates a limited-risk, limited-reward bullish spread.

NISM Series I mock test FAQs

How many questions are there in the NISM Series I exam?

The NISM Series I paper has 100 multiple-choice questions for 100 marks, to be completed in 120 minutes. This is the indicative pattern — always confirm it in the latest official notification.

Is there negative marking in NISM Series I?

Yes. −0.25 mark per wrong answer (25% of the marks for that question). Our mock tests apply the same rule.

What is the pass mark for NISM Series I?

You need 60% to pass. Our mock tests show pass or fail against the same mark.

Are the NISM Series I mock tests free?

Yes. All 30 full-length NISM Series I mock tests are free, with no payment or plan. Create a free account (one click with Google) to start; your results are saved across devices. You can retake any test as often as you like.

How should I prepare for NISM Series I in two weeks?

Spend the first week reading the workbook chapter by chapter, focusing on USD-INR contract specifications, pricing through interest rate parity and hedging examples. In the second week, take a timed mock test every other day, review every wrong answer and revisit the clearing, margining and regulatory chapters, which carry many factual questions.

Is NISM Series I mandatory for currency derivatives dealers?

SEBI requires associated persons of brokers who deal in the currency derivatives segment, such as dealers and sales staff, to hold a valid NISM Series I certificate. Treasury professionals often take it voluntarily. Your compliance team can confirm whether your role needs it; Confirm the current rules on nism.ac.in.

Which chapters of NISM Series I need the most practice?

Learners usually find pricing of currency futures, option payoffs and hedging strategies the hardest because they involve calculations. Practise these with worked examples until you can solve them quickly. Trading, clearing and settlement chapters are mostly factual, so short revision and repeated question practice work well for them.
