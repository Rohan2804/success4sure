About the NISM Series I exam

NISM Series I tests how exchange-traded currency futures and options work in India — USD-INR and cross-currency contracts, pricing through interest rate parity, hedging for importers and exporters, trading and clearing mechanics, and the RBI and SEBI rules that govern the segment.

Who should take it: Dealers, sales staff and other associated persons of brokers who trade in the currency derivatives segment, and treasury professionals who want a structured grounding in currency hedging.

Sample 1 · Introduction to Currency Markets

In the quotation USD/INR = 84.10, which currency is the base currency?

  1. INR
  2. Both USD and INR equally
  3. USD
  4. Neither, because it is a cross rate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. USD

The first currency in a pair is the base currency; the quote tells how many units of INR (quote currency) buy one USD.

Sample 2 · Foreign Exchange Derivatives

Which feature is typical of an OTC currency forward but NOT of an exchange-traded currency future?

  1. Contract terms are customised bilaterally between the two parties
  2. Daily mark-to-market through a clearing corporation
  3. Standardised lot size and expiry date
  4. Novation by a clearing corporation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Contract terms are customised bilaterally between the two parties

Forwards are privately negotiated and tailor-made, whereas futures are standardised, cleared and marked to market daily.

Sample 3 · Exchange Traded Currency Futures

The standard contract size of a USD/INR futures contract on Indian exchanges is:

  1. USD 500
  2. USD 10,000
  3. USD 1,00,000
  4. USD 1,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. USD 1,000

USD/INR futures are traded in lots of USD 1,000, keeping the contract accessible to small hedgers.

Sample 4 · Exchange Traded Currency Options

Exchange-traded USD/INR options in India are of which exercise style?

  1. American
  2. European
  3. Bermudan
  4. Asian
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. European

They can be exercised only on the expiry date, which is the definition of a European option.

Sample 5 · Strategies Using Currency Derivatives

A trader expecting USD/INR to rise moderately buys an 84.00 call and sells an 85.00 call of the same expiry. This position is a:

  1. Bear put spread
  2. Long straddle
  3. Short strangle
  4. Bull call spread
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Bull call spread

Buying a lower-strike call and selling a higher-strike call creates a limited-risk, limited-reward bullish spread.
