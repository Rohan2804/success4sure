What this chapter covers

This chapter builds the vocabulary of the foreign exchange market: how currency pairs are written, base and quote currencies, direct and indirect quotes, bid-ask spreads, pips and cross rates. It also covers value dates such as cash, tom and spot, the main market participants such as authorised dealer banks, and the evolution of India's exchange rate regime from a fixed peg to a market-determined, managed float with current account convertibility. When revising, practise reading quotes from both sides, converting appreciation and depreciation into percentages correctly, and working out cross rates with bid and ask prices.

Introduction to Currency Markets questions

Question 1

USD/INR moves from 83.00 to 84.50 over a month. Which statement correctly describes this move?

  1. The rupee has appreciated against the US dollar
  2. The US dollar has appreciated and the rupee has depreciated
  3. Both currencies have appreciated by the same amount
  4. The rupee has depreciated only if Indian inflation has also risen in that month
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The US dollar has appreciated and the rupee has depreciated

A higher USD/INR rate means more rupees are needed to buy one dollar. So the dollar has strengthened (appreciated) and the rupee has weakened (depreciated). Inflation is not needed to describe the move.

Question 2

A foreign exchange deal with a 'tom' value date is settled on:

  1. The next business day after the trade date
  2. The same day as the trade date
  3. The second business day after the trade date
  4. Any date both parties agree, beyond one month
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The next business day after the trade date

'Tom' is short for tomorrow: settlement happens on the next business day. 'Cash' (or value today) settles the same day, and spot settles on the second business day after the trade.

Question 3

A quote that shows how many units of foreign currency can be bought with one unit (or 100 units) of the home currency is called:

  1. A direct quote
  2. A cross rate
  3. A forward outright quote
  4. An indirect quote
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. An indirect quote

An indirect quote expresses the home currency as the base, for example USD 1.19 per INR 100 for an Indian resident. A direct quote shows home currency per unit of foreign currency, like USD/INR = 84.00.

Question 4

USD/INR rises from 80.00 to 84.00. By roughly what percentage has the rupee depreciated against the dollar?

  1. 5.00%
  2. 4.00%
  3. 5.25%
  4. 4.76%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 4.76%

Rupee value in dollars falls from 1/80 to 1/84, a change of 80/84 - 1 = -4.76%. The 5% figure is the dollar's appreciation, which is measured from the dollar's side of the quote.

Question 5

Since the unification of exchange rates in 1993, India's exchange rate regime is best described as:

  1. A fixed peg to the US dollar revised once a year by the government
  2. A basket peg with a fixed band announced by the Finance Ministry
  3. A completely free float in which RBI never buys or sells foreign currency
  4. A market-determined rate with RBI intervention to curb excess volatility
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A market-determined rate with RBI intervention to curb excess volatility

After the LERMS experiment, rates were unified in March 1993 and the rupee became market-determined. RBI intervenes to smooth undue volatility but does not target a fixed level, so it is a managed float.

Question 6

Which statement about rupee convertibility is correct today?

  1. It is fully convertible on both current account and capital account transactions
  2. It is convertible on capital account but not on current account
  3. Convertible on current account; capital account still regulated
  4. It is not convertible for any type of transaction
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Convertible on current account; capital account still regulated

India accepted current account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF in 1994. Capital account transactions remain subject to limits and conditions under FEMA, so convertibility there is only partial.

Question 7

A dealer quotes EUR/USD at 1.0850 / 1.0853. What is the bid-ask spread in pips?

  1. 3 pips
  2. 0.3 pips
  3. 30 pips
  4. 53 pips
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 3 pips

For EUR/USD a pip is the fourth decimal place, 0.0001. The spread is 1.0853 - 1.0850 = 0.0003, which equals 3 pips. Thirty pips would be a difference of 0.0030.

Question 8

GBP/USD is quoted at 1.2700 / 1.2702 and USD/INR at 84.00 / 84.02. The derived two-way GBP/INR quote is approximately:

  1. 106.66 / 106.74
  2. 106.70 / 106.70
  3. 106.68 / 106.72
  4. 106.72 / 106.68
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 106.68 / 106.72

To buy GBP with INR you buy GBP at 1.2702 and buy USD at 84.02: 1.2702 x 84.02 = 106.72 (ask). The bid uses both bids: 1.2700 x 84.00 = 106.68. Bid must always be below ask.
