Clearing, Settlement & Risk Management: NISM Series I practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Clearing, Settlement & Risk Management, from the NISM Series I (Currency Derivatives) syllabus.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how the clearing corporation stands between buyers and sellers and manages risk in the currency derivatives segment. It covers the types of clearing members, clearing banks, daily and final settlement, and the margin system: SPAN-based initial margin, extreme loss margin, calendar spread treatment and mark-to-market settlement. It also covers position limits, collection and segregation of client margins, close-out of defaulting positions and settlement guarantee funds. Focus on the purpose of each margin, the timing of fund settlement and practise multi-day mark-to-market calculations for long and short positions.

Clearing, Settlement & Risk Management questions

Question 1

In the currency derivatives segment, the extreme loss margin is collected mainly to cover:

  1. The brokerage payable by the client to the trading member
  2. Losses in unusual market conditions that go beyond what initial margin is designed to cover
  3. The premium paid by option buyers
  4. Delays in issuing contract notes to clients
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Losses in unusual market conditions that go beyond what initial margin is designed to cover

Initial margin covers a large one-day loss at a high confidence level. Extreme loss margin is an extra buffer for rarer, larger moves outside that range. It has nothing to do with brokerage or option premium.

Question 2

SPAN, the system used by clearing corporations to compute initial margin, stands for:

  1. Systematic Position Allocation and Netting system
  2. Settlement Price Adjustment Norm
  3. Standard Portfolio Analysis of Risk
  4. Standard Price Average Number
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Standard Portfolio Analysis of Risk

SPAN (Standard Portfolio Analysis of Risk) simulates many price and volatility scenarios on a whole portfolio and sets margin at the worst likely loss, allowing offsets between related positions.

Question 3

On which day are the funds for a trading day's MTM obligations in currency futures paid in and paid out?

  1. Only on the expiry date of the contract, after the final settlement price is known
  2. On the next working day (T+1)
  3. Once a week, on Friday
  4. Within 30 days of the trade
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. On the next working day (T+1)

MTM obligations arising on a trading day are paid in and paid out on the following working day through the clearing members' accounts with clearing banks. Waiting until expiry would allow losses to build up.

Question 4

The funds settlement between clearing members and the clearing corporation is carried out through:

  1. Cash deposited at exchange counters
  2. Cheques sent by clients directly to the exchange
  3. Accounts held with the Reserve Bank of India by each client
  4. Designated clearing bank accounts of the clearing members
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Designated clearing bank accounts of the clearing members

Each clearing member keeps a clearing account with an approved clearing bank. Pay-in amounts are debited and pay-out amounts credited to these accounts electronically on the settlement day.

Question 5

If a clearing member fails to meet its funds pay-in obligation, the clearing corporation may:

  1. Cancel all trades done on that day by every member
  2. Close out the member's open positions and use its margins and deposits to recover dues
  3. Ask the counterparties to bear the loss in proportion
  4. Postpone settlement for all members until the dues are paid
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Close out the member's open positions and use its margins and deposits to recover dues

The clearing corporation guarantees settlement to non-defaulting members. On a default it can stop the member's trading, close out positions and use its margin and deposits, then other resources, to cover the shortfall.

Question 6

A trader sells 4 USD/INR futures contracts (lot USD 1,000) at 84.00. Settlement prices are 84.15 on day 1 and 83.90 on day 2. His MTM for day 2 alone is:

  1. Loss of Rs 600
  2. Profit of Rs 400
  3. Profit of Rs 1,000
  4. Loss of Rs 1,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Profit of Rs 1,000

Day 2 MTM is measured from day 1 settlement price: (84.15 - 83.90) x 1,000 x 4 = Rs 1,000 profit for the short. Day 1 had a loss of Rs 600, which was already settled.

Question 7

A trading member collects margin from clients for currency derivatives. Which practice is correct?

  1. Client margins must be kept separate and not used for the member's own positions
  2. Client margins may be used to meet the member's proprietary losses
  3. Margin needs to be collected only if the client makes a loss
  4. Margin can be waived for clients who trade frequently
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Client margins must be kept separate and not used for the member's own positions

Regulations require upfront margin collection from clients and segregation of client funds. Using one client's money for the member's own trades or other clients' dues is not allowed.

Question 8

Calendar spread positions in currency futures attract lower margins mainly because:

  1. They can be held only intraday and must be squared off before the close of that day's trading session
  2. The exchange does not guarantee their settlement
  3. The two legs are in the same currency pair, so their price moves largely offset
  4. They are settled by physical delivery of currency
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The two legs are in the same currency pair, so their price moves largely offset

In a calendar spread, a long and short position in different expiries of the same pair move closely together, so net risk is smaller than two outright positions. The clearing corporation still guarantees them.
