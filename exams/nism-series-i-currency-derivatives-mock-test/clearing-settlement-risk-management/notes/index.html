Clearing, Settlement & Risk Management: NISM Series I revision notes

This chapter explains how the clearing corporation guarantees currency derivative trades and contains risk. Questions test novation, margin types and their purpose, the timing of MTM settlement, the Settlement Guarantee Fund and why spreads get lower margins.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Novation

When a trade is matched, the clearing corporation becomes the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer. This replaces counterparty risk between members with the credit of the clearing corporation, which is backed by margins and default funds.

2. Initial margin

Initial margin is collected upfront to cover the potential loss on a position over a day under adverse conditions. It is computed using SPAN with a high confidence level. For currency futures it is a small percentage of contract value, so leverage is high.

3. Extreme loss margin

Extreme loss margin is charged in addition to initial margin to cover tail risks beyond SPAN's scenarios, usually as a percentage of the gross open position's value. Both margins must be in place before trading. It protects against sudden, unusually large swings in the exchange rate.

4. Calendar spread margin

A long near-month and short far-month position in the same pair carries much less risk because both legs move together with the spot rate. Clearing corporations therefore charge a reduced calendar spread margin instead of full margins on both legs, as long as the near leg is not about to expire.

5. Daily MTM settlement

Futures positions are marked to the daily settlement price, and profits and losses are settled in cash on T+1. Members collect MTM losses from clients and pass on gains. Failure to pay MTM can lead to positions being closed out.

6. Final settlement

On expiry, open futures are settled in rupees against the final settlement price (the reference rate). In-the-money options are automatically exercised and cash settled. There is no physical delivery of foreign currency in rupee-pair contracts. Positions closed before expiry settle only through MTM and need no final settlement.

7. Settlement Guarantee Fund

The Core Settlement Guarantee Fund ensures that settlement is completed even if a clearing member defaults. It is funded by contributions from the clearing corporation, the exchange and clearing members, and is used only after the defaulter's own margins are exhausted.

8. Other risk controls

Clearing corporations monitor positions and margins in real time, enforce position limits, apply penalties for margin shortfalls and require members to segregate client collateral. Members that breach limits may have their trading terminals disabled. Clients are also expected to keep adequate margins at all times to avoid forced square-offs.

Common traps in the exam

  • Thinking novation transfers default risk to the other trading member - it moves it to the clearing corporation.
  • Assuming MTM is settled only at expiry; it is settled daily.
  • Confusing the Settlement Guarantee Fund (backs settlement) with the Investor Protection Fund (compensates clients of defaulting brokers).
  • Believing calendar spreads need full margin on both legs; they get a reduced spread margin.

Quick recap

Novation makes the clearing corporation the counterparty. Initial margin via SPAN plus extreme loss margin, collected upfront. MTM in cash on T+1. Final settlement in rupees at the reference rate; no delivery. Spreads get lower margin. The Core SGF completes settlement if a member defaults.
