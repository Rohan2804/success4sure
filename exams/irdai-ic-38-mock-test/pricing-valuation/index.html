Pricing & Valuation: IRDAI IC-38 practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Pricing & Valuation, from the IRDAI IC-38 (Insurance Agents Pre-Recruitment Exam) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how life insurance premiums are calculated and how insurers value their liabilities. The net premium depends on mortality and interest, while the office premium adds loadings for expenses, contingencies and bonus. Learners should understand the level premium system and why it builds reserves, the effect of age, sum assured and payment mode on premium, and rebates. Valuation by actuaries compares liabilities with assets to find surplus, which in participating business is distributed as bonus. Focus on the direction in which each factor moves the premium, and on simple premium calculations per thousand sum assured.

Pricing & Valuation questions

Question 1

The net premium of a life insurance policy is calculated mainly on the basis of:

  1. Mortality, interest and agent's commission
  2. Expenses and expected bonus
  3. Mortality and interest
  4. Taxes and the insurer's profit target
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Mortality and interest

Net (pure) premium reflects only expected mortality cost discounted at an assumed rate of interest. Loadings for expenses, commission, contingencies and bonus are added later to arrive at the office premium.

Question 2

Other things being equal, the premium for a term plan for a 45-year-old is higher than for a 25-year-old because:

  1. The probability of death rises with age
  2. Older people are always charged a penalty by IRDAI
  3. The interest rate assumed is lower for older people
  4. Commission rates are higher for older proposers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The probability of death rises with age

Mortality tables show the probability of death increasing with age. Since the cost of providing cover depends on this probability, the premium for an older life is higher.

Question 3

Under the level premium system, the premiums paid in the early years of a policy are:

  1. Less than the actual cost of risk, with the resulting deficit being recovered from the insurer's shareholders in later years
  2. Exactly equal to the cost of risk in each year
  3. Refunded to the policyholder at the end of each year
  4. More than the actual cost of risk, the excess building a reserve for later years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. More than the actual cost of risk, the excess building a reserve for later years

With a level premium, the policyholder pays the same amount every year. In early years, this exceeds the cost of mortality; the surplus builds a reserve that meets the higher risk of later years.

Question 4

Actuarial valuation of a life insurer is carried out mainly to:

  1. Fix the commission of each agent
  2. Compare its liabilities with its assets and determine surplus or deficit
  3. Decide the premium for a single new policy
  4. Calculate the income tax payable by each policyholder on the maturity proceeds of his policy
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Compare its liabilities with its assets and determine surplus or deficit

Valuation estimates the present value of future policy liabilities and compares it with assets. The resulting surplus, if any, helps decide bonus to participating policyholders and confirms solvency.

Question 5

Life insurers often give a rebate in premium for a higher sum assured because:

  1. Larger policies have no mortality risk
  2. Administrative costs per policy do not rise in proportion to the sum assured
  3. IRDAI requires every insurer to give a fixed discount on all policies with a sum assured above ₹1 lakh
  4. Agents are not paid any commission on large policies
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Administrative costs per policy do not rise in proportion to the sum assured

Many policy costs, such as issuing documents and maintaining records, are similar for small and large policies. Spread over a bigger sum assured, the expense per thousand falls, and the saving is passed on as a rebate.

Question 6

Over a year, the total premium under the annual mode is usually lower than under the monthly mode because:

  1. Monthly-mode policyholders have higher mortality
  2. Monthly premiums attract goods and services tax, whereas annual premiums are fully exempt from any such tax levy
  3. Annual-mode policies do not carry any insurance cover
  4. The insurer saves on collection costs and earns interest on the money for a longer period
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The insurer saves on collection costs and earns interest on the money for a longer period

Collecting premium once a year costs less, and the insurer receives the full year's money in advance, earning interest on it. Hence annual mode is cheaper, and monthly or quarterly modes carry a loading.

Question 7

The tabular premium for a plan is ₹48 per thousand sum assured, and a rebate of ₹2 per thousand applies for the sum assured chosen. What is the annual premium for a sum assured of ₹5,00,000?

  1. ₹23,000
  2. ₹24,000
  3. ₹22,000
  4. ₹23,500
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹23,000

Net rate = 48 − 2 = ₹46 per thousand. Premium = 46 × 500 = ₹23,000. ₹24,000 ignores the rebate, while ₹22,000 deducts it twice.

Question 8

In a life insurer's participating fund, surplus arises mainly when:

  1. More policyholders lapse their policies after one year
  2. The insurer raises its premium rates retrospectively
  3. Actual experience of mortality, interest and expenses is better than assumed
  4. Claims are delayed beyond the stipulated time
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Actual experience of mortality, interest and expenses is better than assumed

Premiums are based on cautious assumptions. If fewer people die, investments earn more, or expenses are lower than assumed, a surplus emerges, part of which is distributed to participating policyholders as bonus.
