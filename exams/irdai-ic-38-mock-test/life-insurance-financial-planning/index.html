Life Insurance & Financial Planning: IRDAI IC-38 practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Life Insurance & Financial Planning, from the IRDAI IC-38 (Insurance Agents Pre-Recruitment Exam) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter links life insurance with personal financial planning. It explains life stages and how needs change from a young single earner to retirement, the need-based approach to finding the right amount of cover, and the role of life insurance as a unique asset that creates an immediate estate. It also covers basic financial concepts: the time value of money, compounding, inflation, the risk-return trade-off among asset classes, liquidity and emergency funds. Practise simple calculations such as future value under inflation and the cover gap, and always start from the customer's goals before suggesting any product.

Life Insurance & Financial Planning questions

Question 1

A couple whose children are now financially independent, and who have about ten years left until retirement, should usually give priority to:

  1. Buying the largest possible child education plan
  2. Taking a new long-tenure home loan to buy a second house for investment
  3. Investing all savings in a single high-risk small-cap stock
  4. Building a retirement corpus and ensuring adequate health cover
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Building a retirement corpus and ensuring adequate health cover

In the 'empty nest' stage, family protection needs reduce while retirement and health needs grow. Building a retirement corpus and securing health cover are the main goals for this life stage.

Question 2

If inflation averages 6% per annum, an annual household expense of ₹50,000 today will cost approximately how much after 12 years?

  1. ₹86,000
  2. ₹89,542
  3. ₹1,00,610
  4. ₹1,06,646
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹1,00,610

Future cost = 50,000 × (1.06)^12 ≈ ₹1,00,610. ₹86,000 wrongly applies simple growth (6% × 12 years), while ₹89,542 and ₹1,06,646 compound for 10 and 13 years respectively.

Question 3

Before recommending any insurance product, an agent should first:

  1. Show the product with the highest commission
  2. Understand the customer's goals, income, liabilities and existing cover
  3. Collect the first premium so that the customer does not change his mind
  4. Compare competitor products only on the basis of premium
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Understand the customer's goals, income, liabilities and existing cover

Need-based selling begins with fact-finding: goals, income, dependants, loans and current cover. Only after this analysis can a suitable product and sum assured be recommended.

Question 4

Over a long period, which of these asset classes typically carries the highest risk along with the highest expected return?

  1. Equity shares
  2. Bank savings account
  3. Bank fixed deposit
  4. Government of India bonds held to maturity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Equity shares

Equity offers higher expected long-term returns but with high volatility. Savings accounts, fixed deposits and government bonds held to maturity provide lower but more stable and predictable returns.

Question 5

Life insurance is often called a unique asset because:

  1. It always gives the highest return among all available investments, including equity shares and property
  2. Its value can never be affected by lapse or surrender
  3. It creates an immediate estate for the family as soon as the policy is in force
  4. It can be bought only by people with very high incomes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. It creates an immediate estate for the family as soon as the policy is in force

Ordinary savings build up slowly, but a life policy guarantees the full sum assured to the family from the first premium if death occurs. This 'instant estate' is what makes life insurance unique.

Question 6

An emergency fund for meeting sudden expenses should preferably be kept in:

  1. Liquid, low-risk instruments such as a savings account or a liquid fund
  2. Real estate, because property prices generally rise
  3. Long-term deposits that carry a heavy penalty for premature withdrawal before maturity
  4. Small-cap equity shares to maximise returns
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Liquid, low-risk instruments such as a savings account or a liquid fund

An emergency fund must be available quickly without loss of value, so liquidity and safety matter most. Property, locked-in deposits and volatile equities are unsuitable for this purpose.

Question 7

Rakesh has a home loan outstanding of ₹20 lakh, expects children's education costs of ₹15 lakh, and needs a family income corpus of ₹40 lakh. He already has life cover of ₹25 lakh and savings of ₹10 lakh. Using the needs approach, his additional life cover requirement is:

  1. ₹75 lakh
  2. ₹50 lakh
  3. ₹65 lakh
  4. ₹40 lakh
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹40 lakh

Total needs = 20 + 15 + 40 = ₹75 lakh. Less existing cover (₹25 lakh) and savings (₹10 lakh) gives a gap of ₹40 lakh. ₹75 lakh ignores existing resources; ₹50 lakh ignores savings.

Question 8

₹1,00,000 invested at 8% per annum compounded annually will grow in 3 years to approximately:

  1. ₹1,24,000
  2. ₹1,25,971
  3. ₹1,16,640
  4. ₹1,27,450
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹1,25,971

Maturity value = 1,00,000 × (1.08)^3 = ₹1,25,971. ₹1,24,000 is the simple-interest figure, and ₹1,16,640 is the value after only two years of compounding.
