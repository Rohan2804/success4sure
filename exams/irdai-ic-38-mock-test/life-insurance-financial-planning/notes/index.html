Life Insurance & Financial Planning: IRDAI IC-38 revision notes

This chapter links life insurance to the broader task of financial planning. It explains why families need protection, how to estimate the right cover using human life value and needs-based methods, how needs change across life stages, and where insurance fits among savings and investments. Questions include a simple HLV calculation and matching life stages with suitable products.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. The core need

Life insurance protects dependants from the financial loss caused by the early death of an earning member. Without it, a family may have to sell assets, take loans or cut children's education. It also offers savings and retirement options, but protection is the primary purpose.

2. Human life value

HLV estimates the economic value of a person to the family as the present value of future income used for the family. A simple method: annual income contributed to the family divided by a suitable interest rate. If a person contributes ₹6 lakh a year and the rate is 8%, HLV is about ₹75 lakh.

3. Needs-based approach

Add up the family's needs on death: outstanding loans, an emergency fund, children's education and marriage, and an income stream for the spouse. Subtract existing assets and cover. The gap is the additional insurance needed. This is often more practical than HLV.

4. Life stages

A young single person has low need for life cover but should start saving. A young married couple with a home loan needs high term cover. Parents with school-going children need protection and education funding. Empty nesters shift focus to retirement. Retirees need annuities and health cover.

5. Financial planning process

Establish the relationship, gather data, analyse the current position, develop recommendations, implement and monitor. Goals should be specific, measurable and time-bound. Insurance comes first in the plan because protection must be in place before investing for growth.

6. Risks across life

People face the risk of dying too early (family left without income), living too long (outliving savings) and disability or illness (loss of income plus extra costs). Term insurance, annuities and health or disability covers respectively address these.

7. Savings and investment

Insurance-linked savings such as endowment or ULIPs combine protection and saving but usually give lower cover per rupee than term plans. Financial planning compares them with alternatives such as PPF, mutual funds and deposits based on goals and risk.

8. Agent's role

An agent should first understand the family's situation and recommend adequate cover, not just sell the product with the best commission. A ₹10 lakh endowment policy for a 30-year-old breadwinner with a ₹40 lakh home loan leaves the family exposed.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Simple HLV = annual income contributed to the family / interest rate (for example ₹6 lakh / 0.08 = ₹75 lakh).

Common traps in the exam

  • Protection is the primary purpose of life insurance; savings is secondary.
  • HLV uses income contributed to the family, not total gross income.
  • Retirees mainly need health cover and annuities, not large life cover.
  • Needs-based cover subtracts existing assets and insurance from total needs.

Quick recap

Life insurance protects dependants against loss of income. Estimate cover by human life value (income for family divided by interest rate) or by needs (liabilities plus goals minus assets). Needs change by life stage. Financial planning: gather data, analyse, recommend, implement, monitor. Protection first, then savings. Risks: dying early, living long, disability.
