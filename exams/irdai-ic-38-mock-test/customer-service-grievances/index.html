Customer Service & Grievances: IRDAI IC-38 practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Customer Service & Grievances, from the IRDAI IC-38 (Insurance Agents Pre-Recruitment Exam) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter deals with the agent's role in serving customers before and after the sale. It covers the importance of service for an intangible product like insurance, the dimensions of service quality (reliability, assurance, tangibles, empathy, responsiveness), moments of truth, and communication skills such as active listening and body language. It also explains how grievances are handled: the insurer's own grievance cell, IRDAI's grievance system, the Insurance Ombudsman and consumer commissions under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019. Focus on how an agent should behave in real situations and on the order in which a complaint moves through the redressal forums.

Customer Service & Grievances questions

Question 1

Active listening by an insurance agent mainly involves:

  1. Talking continuously about the product's many features so that there is never any awkward silence in the meeting
  2. Preparing the next sales pitch while the customer is still speaking
  3. Agreeing with every statement of the customer to avoid any disagreement
  4. Paying full attention to the customer, clarifying doubts and responding to what is actually said
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Paying full attention to the customer, clarifying doubts and responding to what is actually said

Active listening means concentrating on the speaker, asking clarifying questions and responding to their real concerns. It builds trust and helps the agent understand the customer's needs before suggesting a product.

Question 2

In the language of customer service, a 'moment of truth' refers to:

  1. The day on which the insurer finally settles and pays a claim after completing all its investigations
  2. The time when the agent discloses his commission
  3. Any point of contact at which the customer forms an impression of the service
  4. The final signature on the proposal form
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Any point of contact at which the customer forms an impression of the service

A moment of truth is every interaction — a phone call, a visit, a receipt — where the customer judges the quality of service. A claim payment is one such moment, but not the only one.

Question 3

An agent always delivers policy documents, premium reminders and claim forms exactly when he has promised. Which dimension of service quality does this mainly reflect?

  1. Tangibles
  2. Reliability
  3. Empathy
  4. Assurance
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Reliability

Reliability is the ability to perform the promised service dependably and accurately. Tangibles relate to physical appearance, empathy to individual care, and assurance to knowledge and courtesy that inspire confidence.

Question 4

Under the Insurance Ombudsman mechanism, an award passed by the Ombudsman is:

  1. Binding on the insurer once the complainant accepts it, while the complainant may instead pursue other remedies
  2. Binding on the complainant but only advisory for the insurer
  3. Advisory for both parties, and enforceable against the insurer only after it has been separately approved and notified by IRDAI
  4. Binding on both parties, with no further remedy available to anyone
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Binding on the insurer once the complainant accepts it, while the complainant may instead pursue other remedies

The Ombudsman's award binds the insurer, which must comply within the prescribed time once the complainant accepts it. A complainant who is not satisfied is free to reject it and approach other forums such as a consumer commission.

Question 5

A policyholder who alleges deficiency in service by an insurer can file a complaint before a Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission under:

  1. Section 41 of the Insurance Act, 1938
  2. The Indian Contract Act, 1872
  3. The Consumer Protection Act, 2019
  4. The Life Insurance Corporation Act, 1956
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The Consumer Protection Act, 2019

Insurance is a 'service' under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, so policyholders can approach District, State or National Commissions for deficiency in service. The other Acts do not set up consumer forums.

Question 6

Which of the following is a sign of positive body language when an agent meets a prospect?

  1. Comfortable eye contact and an open, relaxed posture
  2. Arms folded tightly across the chest throughout the meeting
  3. Frequently checking the mobile phone while the prospect speaks
  4. Looking away and tapping fingers on the table
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Comfortable eye contact and an open, relaxed posture

Steady eye contact and an open posture show attention, honesty and confidence. Folded arms, checking the phone or fidgeting send signals of disinterest or defensiveness and weaken trust.

Question 7

A customer telephones the agent, very angry because his claim has been delayed. The agent's best first response is to:

  1. Tell the customer that claim delays are the insurer's fault, not his, and end the call
  2. Advise the customer to file a court case immediately
  3. Argue that the customer must have submitted wrong documents
  4. Listen patiently, acknowledge the problem and promise a specific follow-up
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Listen patiently, acknowledge the problem and promise a specific follow-up

An upset customer first needs to be heard. Acknowledging the problem and committing to a definite follow-up calms the situation and preserves the relationship; blaming others or arguing usually worsens the complaint.

Question 8

Why is good service especially important in selling insurance compared with selling most physical goods?

  1. Insurance products are identical across all insurers, so price is irrelevant
  2. Insurance is an intangible promise, so the service experienced is what the customer actually judges
  3. IRDAI fixes exactly the same premium for every policy of every insurer, so the only thing left to compete on is service
  4. Customers buy insurance only once in their lifetime and never renew it
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Insurance is an intangible promise, so the service experienced is what the customer actually judges

A customer cannot see or touch an insurance policy; he experiences it only through the service he receives and the claim at the end. Strong service builds trust, renewals and referrals.
