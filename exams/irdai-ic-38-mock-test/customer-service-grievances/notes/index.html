Customer Service & Grievances: IRDAI IC-38 revision notes

An insurance agent is the face of the insurer for most customers, so IC-38 tests service standards and the grievance redressal system closely. This chapter covers the importance of customer service, communication, the free-look period, policyholder protection rules and the escalation route from the insurer to the ombudsman and consumer commissions. Limits and timelines are revised by IRDAI from time to time.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Why service matters

Insurance is a promise to pay in the future, so trust built through service decides renewals and referrals. A customer who gets a timely reminder before a premium due date and quick help with a claim is far more likely to renew and recommend. Lifetime value of a satisfied customer is high.

2. Communication skills

Agents should listen actively, explain clearly without jargon, avoid exaggeration, and confirm understanding. Non-verbal cues and punctuality matter. Explaining exclusions honestly at the sale prevents disputes at claim time.

3. Free-look period

After receiving the policy document, the policyholder can review it and return it if not satisfied, during the free-look period. The insurer refunds the premium after deducting proportionate risk premium, medical examination costs and stamp duty (and unit value changes for ULIPs). IRDAI extended the free-look period to 30 days in 2024 for life and health policies; check current rules.

4. Policyholder protection rules

IRDAI regulations require insurers to give clear proposal forms, benefit illustrations and key features documents, respond to service requests within set timelines, and disclose the grievance process. Agents must give prospects correct information about all material terms.

5. Insurer's grievance system

Every insurer has a grievance redressal officer and a grievance cell. A complaint should first be lodged with the insurer, which must acknowledge it promptly and resolve it within the time IRDAI specifies (typically two weeks).

6. Bima Bharosa

IRDAI's Bima Bharosa portal (successor to IGMS) lets policyholders register and track complaints against insurers online. It acts as a central repository and monitors whether insurers resolve complaints on time.

7. Insurance ombudsman

If the insurer rejects the complaint, does not reply within the time allowed, or the policyholder is unhappy with the reply, a complaint can be filed with the insurance ombudsman within one year of the insurer's reply, for claims up to ₹50 lakh. The ombudsman's award is binding on the insurer, which must comply within 30 days; the policyholder can still go to court if unsatisfied.

8. Consumer commissions

Under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, complaints can go to the District Commission (up to ₹50 lakh), State Commission (₹50 lakh to ₹2 crore) and National Commission (above ₹2 crore), based on value of consideration paid, as revised in 2021.

Common traps in the exam

  • The ombudsman route is open only after first approaching the insurer.
  • The ombudsman's award binds the insurer, not the policyholder.
  • Free-look refund is not the full premium; proportionate risk premium, medical and stamp costs are deducted.
  • Consumer commission jurisdiction is based on consideration paid, not the claim amount alone.

Quick recap

Service builds trust and renewals; communicate clearly and disclose exclusions. Free-look lets policyholders return a policy with partial deductions (now 30 days for life and health). Complain first to the insurer's GRO; track on Bima Bharosa; then go to the insurance ombudsman within one year, up to ₹50 lakh, award binding on insurer within 30 days. Consumer commissions are the other route.
