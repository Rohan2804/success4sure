Introduction to Insurance: IRDAI IC-38 practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Introduction to Insurance, from the IRDAI IC-38 (Insurance Agents Pre-Recruitment Exam) syllabus.

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces risk and how insurance handles it. Learners should be clear on the difference between risk, peril and hazard; physical and moral hazard; pure and speculative risk; and the main risk-management techniques of avoidance, retention, reduction and transfer. It also explains how pooling and the law of large numbers allow insurers to predict losses, the primary and secondary burdens of risk, the basic rules for deciding what to insure, and how reinsurance lets insurers spread very large risks. Focus on applying these terms to everyday scenarios rather than memorising definitions.

Introduction to Insurance questions

Question 1

In a fire policy covering a warehouse, the fire itself is best described as the:

  1. Hazard
  2. Moral hazard
  3. Peril
  4. Risk premium
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Peril

A peril is the event that actually causes the loss, such as fire, flood or theft. A hazard is a condition that increases the chance or size of loss, like storing inflammable goods carelessly.

Question 2

Which of the following is an example of a pure risk?

  1. The chance that a factory may be damaged by a flood
  2. Buying gold for trading in the hope of a quick profit
  3. Purchasing a lottery ticket that may win a prize
  4. Starting a new business that may earn a profit or make a loss
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The chance that a factory may be damaged by a flood

A pure risk offers only the possibility of loss or no loss, as with flood damage. The other options are speculative risks, which carry a chance of gain as well, and are normally not insurable.

Question 3

A large company running hundreds of delivery vans decides to pay for minor dents and scratches out of its own funds instead of insuring them. This is:

  1. Risk transfer
  2. Risk avoidance
  3. Risk reduction through loss prevention
  4. Risk retention
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Risk retention

When an organisation consciously bears small, predictable losses itself, it is retaining the risk. Transfer would mean buying insurance, while avoidance would mean not running the vans at all.

Question 4

A restaurant stores cylinders of cooking gas and drums of kerosene right next to its open kitchen fire. This condition is an example of:

  1. Moral hazard
  2. Physical hazard
  3. Speculative risk
  4. Risk retention
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Physical hazard

A physical hazard is a tangible condition of the property or its surroundings that increases the chance of loss. Moral hazard, in contrast, relates to the attitude or dishonesty of the insured person.

Question 5

Insurers can estimate their likely claims for the coming year with reasonable accuracy mainly because of:

  1. The principle of subrogation applied to every claim
  2. The law of large numbers
  3. The fact that every insured person will definitely suffer a loss
  4. Government guarantees on all insurance losses
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The law of large numbers

When a large number of similar, independent exposures are pooled, actual losses tend to come close to expected losses. This law of large numbers lets insurers price risk and fix premiums sensibly.

Question 6

Which of the following is an example of the secondary burden of risk?

  1. The cost of rebuilding a shop that has actually been destroyed by fire
  2. The value of stock stolen in a burglary at a godown
  3. The loss of a car that was written off in an accident
  4. Keeping large sums idle as a reserve to meet a possible future loss
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Keeping large sums idle as a reserve to meet a possible future loss

The primary burden is the actual loss suffered. The secondary burden includes costs borne even if no loss occurs, such as funds locked up as reserves and the fear and anxiety of uncertainty.

Question 7

A homeowner insures his house because its destruction would leave him financially ruined. Which rule of risk management is he following?

  1. Don't risk more than you can afford to lose
  2. Don't risk a lot for a little
  3. Consider the likely outcomes carefully
  4. Avoid every activity that carries any risk at all
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Don't risk more than you can afford to lose

The rule of not risking more than you can afford to lose says that a loss which would cause financial ruin must be transferred. 'Don't risk a lot for a little' is about weighing premium against cover.

Question 8

An insurer that has accepted a very large refinery risk passes a part of it to another insurer under a separate contract, without involving the policyholder. This arrangement is:

  1. Double insurance
  2. Contribution
  3. Reinsurance
  4. Assignment
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Reinsurance

Reinsurance is insurance bought by an insurer to share a part of its own risk. The policyholder deals only with the original insurer. Double insurance is when the insured buys cover from two insurers for the same risk.
