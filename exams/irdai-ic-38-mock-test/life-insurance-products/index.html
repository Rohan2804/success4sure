Life Insurance Products: IRDAI IC-38 practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Life Insurance Products, from the IRDAI IC-38 (Insurance Agents Pre-Recruitment Exam) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter describes the main types of life insurance products sold in India. It covers term plans and their variants (return of premium, decreasing term for loans), whole life, endowment and money-back plans, participating and non-participating policies, bonuses, unit-linked plans, annuities (immediate and deferred), and riders such as accidental death benefit. Learners should be able to match a product to a customer's need, understand how a ULIP allocates premium into units, and calculate simple reversionary bonus. Focus on the differences between traditional and unit-linked plans and between protection and savings products.

Life Insurance Products questions

Question 1

A whole life policy pays the sum assured:

  1. Only if the insured survives a fixed term of 20 years
  2. On the death of the insured whenever it occurs (or at a very high specified age)
  3. Only on death caused by an accident
  4. In equal instalments every five years during the term, with the balance paid on survival to maturity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. On the death of the insured whenever it occurs (or at a very high specified age)

Whole life insurance provides cover for the entire lifetime, so the claim arises on death whenever it happens; many plans also pay at a high age such as 99 or 100.

Question 2

In a participating (with-profits) life policy, the policyholder:

  1. Bears the full investment risk through units
  2. Receives no benefit other than the guaranteed sum assured
  3. Must participate in the insurer's board meetings
  4. Shares in the insurer's surplus through bonuses
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Shares in the insurer's surplus through bonuses

Participating policies share in the surplus of the insurer's par fund, usually through reversionary or terminal bonuses. Non-participating policies offer only the guaranteed benefits, and ULIPs pass investment risk to the policyholder.

Question 3

Term insurance with return of premium (TROP) differs from a pure term plan mainly because:

  1. The premiums paid are returned if the insured survives the policy term
  2. It pays double the sum assured on death due to any cause during the policy term
  3. It does not require any medical underwriting
  4. It has no fixed policy term
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The premiums paid are returned if the insured survives the policy term

Under TROP, the total premiums paid are refunded on survival to maturity, while a pure term plan pays nothing on survival. For this extra benefit, TROP premiums are noticeably higher.

Question 4

A borrower wants life cover that reduces in line with his outstanding home loan. The most suitable product is:

  1. A whole life policy
  2. An immediate annuity
  3. Mortgage redemption (decreasing term) assurance
  4. A money-back policy with survival benefits every five years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Mortgage redemption (decreasing term) assurance

Mortgage redemption assurance is a decreasing term plan whose sum assured falls roughly with the loan balance, so the loan can be cleared if the borrower dies. It is cheaper than a level term plan.

Question 5

In a ULIP, an annual premium of ₹50,000 is paid. The premium allocation charge is 5% and the NAV on the date of allocation is ₹20. How many units are allotted?

  1. 2,500
  2. 2,250
  3. 2,425
  4. 2,375
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 2,375

Amount invested = 50,000 − 5% = ₹47,500. Units = 47,500 ÷ 20 = 2,375. 2,500 ignores the allocation charge, while 2,250 and 2,425 apply the charge incorrectly.

Question 6

An accidental death benefit rider attached to a life policy:

  1. Replaces the base sum assured on natural death
  2. Refunds all premiums if no accident occurs
  3. Pays an additional sum if death results from an accident
  4. Covers hospitalisation expenses for any illness
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Pays an additional sum if death results from an accident

A rider is an add-on to the base policy. The accidental death benefit rider pays an extra amount, over the base sum assured, if death is caused by an accident within the conditions of the rider.

Question 7

In a deferred annuity, the annuity payments:

  1. Begin immediately, one period after the single premium is paid
  2. Begin after a chosen deferment period during which the purchase money accumulates
  3. Are paid only to the nominee after the annuitant's death, together with all the accumulated interest
  4. Are paid only once, as a lump sum on retirement
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Begin after a chosen deferment period during which the purchase money accumulates

A deferred annuity has an accumulation phase, after which income begins at the vesting date. In an immediate annuity, payments start straight away, from the end of the first payment period.

Question 8

A simple reversionary bonus of ₹45 per thousand sum assured is declared on a policy with a sum assured of ₹4,00,000. The bonus added to the policy for that year is:

  1. ₹18,000
  2. ₹1,800
  3. ₹45,000
  4. ₹9,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹18,000

Bonus = 45 × (4,00,000 ÷ 1,000) = 45 × 400 = ₹18,000. Simple reversionary bonus is calculated on the basic sum assured only and is added to the policy, payable with the claim.
