Life Insurance Products: IRDAI IC-38 revision notes

IC-38 expects agents to know each life insurance product's purpose, benefits and limitations. This chapter covers traditional plans (term, endowment, whole life, money back), unit linked plans, pension products, riders and group and government schemes. Questions ask which product suits a customer's need or what a specific feature means, such as a surrender value or a lock-in period.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Term insurance

Term insurance pays the sum assured only if the insured dies during the term; nothing is paid on survival in a pure term plan. It gives the highest cover per rupee of premium. A 30-year-old can often get ₹1 crore of cover for a modest annual premium. Return-of-premium variants cost more.

2. Endowment

An endowment plan pays the sum assured plus bonuses either on death during the term or on survival to maturity. It combines protection and savings but cover per rupee is much lower than term. Suitable for disciplined savers wanting guaranteed or bonus-linked maturity.

3. Money back

Money back plans pay a part of the sum assured at intervals during the term (survival benefits), with the full sum assured payable on death regardless of earlier payouts. Useful for planned expenses like education milestones.

4. Whole life

Whole life plans cover the insured for life (or till an advanced age such as 99 or 100), paying on death whenever it happens. They are used for estate planning or leaving a legacy.

5. ULIPs

Unit linked insurance plans invest the premium, after charges, in funds chosen by the policyholder (equity, debt, balanced). Investment risk lies with the policyholder. ULIPs have a five-year lock-in, allow switches between funds, and must disclose charges. NAV-based value is paid on maturity or death.

6. Pension and annuity plans

Deferred pension plans accumulate money until vesting, after which an annuity is bought. Immediate annuities start paying soon after a lump sum is paid. Options include life annuity, joint life and return of purchase price.

7. Participating and non-participating

Participating (with-profit) policies share in the insurer's surplus through bonuses; non-participating policies have fixed benefits without bonuses. Bonuses, once declared as reversionary bonuses, are added to the policy and paid with the claim.

8. Riders

Riders are add-ons that extend cover for an extra premium: accidental death benefit, critical illness, waiver of premium, and accidental disability. IRDAI caps rider premiums relative to the base policy so the base cover remains the main contract.

9. Group and government schemes

Group term insurance covers employees or members under one master policy. PMJJBY offers ₹2 lakh life cover for an annual premium for people aged 18 to 50 with a bank account; PMSBY offers accident cover for those aged 18 to 70. Premiums are revised periodically.

Common traps in the exam

  • Pure term plans pay nothing on survival.
  • In money back plans, the full sum assured is paid on death even after survival benefits.
  • ULIP investment risk is borne by the policyholder, not the insurer.
  • Non-participating policies do not receive bonuses.

Quick recap

Term: pure protection, cheapest cover. Endowment: death or maturity benefit with savings. Money back: periodic payouts plus full cover on death. Whole life: cover for life. ULIPs: market-linked, five-year lock-in, policyholder bears risk. Pension plans build corpus then annuity. Par policies earn bonuses. Riders add cover. PMJJBY (life, 18 to 50) and PMSBY (accident, 18 to 70).
