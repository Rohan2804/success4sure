Life Insurance Claims: IRDAI IC-38 practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Life Insurance Claims, from the IRDAI IC-38 (Insurance Agents Pre-Recruitment Exam) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how life insurance claims are settled. It distinguishes maturity, survival benefit, surrender and death claims, and lists the documents needed for each, such as the discharge voucher, policy document, death certificate, claimant's statement and, in unnatural deaths, the FIR and post-mortem report. It also covers who receives the money — nominee, assignee or legal heirs — the treatment of loans and unpaid premiums, death during the grace period, and the grounds on which a claim may be repudiated. Practise calculating the net claim amount after deductions and know why early claims are examined more closely.

Life Insurance Claims questions

Question 1

For settling a maturity claim, the insurer normally requires:

  1. A death certificate issued by the local municipal authority
  2. A signed discharge voucher together with the original policy document and bank and KYC details
  3. A First Information Report lodged with the police, along with the post-mortem report and the police inquest report
  4. A fresh medical examination of the policyholder
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A signed discharge voucher together with the original policy document and bank and KYC details

A maturity claim is paid to the policyholder on survival to the end of the term. The insurer needs a discharge form, the policy document and payment details; death documents or medicals are irrelevant here.

Question 2

Under a money-back policy, a survival benefit is paid:

  1. Only when the insured dies during the term
  2. Only when the policy is surrendered
  3. Each year as a fixed percentage of the premium paid
  4. When the insured survives to specified dates during the policy term
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. When the insured survives to specified dates during the policy term

Money-back plans pay a portion of the sum assured at fixed intervals if the life assured is alive on those dates. These are survival benefits and are separate from the maturity and death benefits.

Question 3

A policy has a sum assured of ₹5,00,000 and accrued bonus of ₹60,000. On the policyholder's death, there is an outstanding policy loan of ₹1,20,000 with unpaid interest of ₹8,000. The net claim payable is:

  1. ₹4,32,000
  2. ₹4,40,000
  3. ₹5,60,000
  4. ₹3,72,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹4,32,000

Claim = 5,00,000 + 60,000 − 1,20,000 − 8,000 = ₹4,32,000. ₹4,40,000 ignores the interest, and ₹5,60,000 ignores the loan altogether. The insurer recovers the loan and interest from the claim.

Question 4

Which document is essential for any death claim under a life policy?

  1. The maturity discharge voucher
  2. The original proposal form
  3. The death certificate
  4. The agent's licence copy
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The death certificate

A death claim cannot be settled without proof of death, normally the death certificate issued by the local authority, along with the claim form and the claimant's identity and bank details.

Question 5

For an accidental death benefit claim, in addition to the usual death claim papers, the insurer generally requires:

  1. A certificate from the employer only
  2. The original age proof of the nominee
  3. A fresh proposal form signed by the nominee
  4. The FIR, post-mortem report and police inquest report
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The FIR, post-mortem report and police inquest report

Since the benefit depends on death being accidental, the insurer needs police and medical records — FIR, inquest and post-mortem reports — to establish the cause and circumstances of death.

Question 6

If a policyholder dies without a valid nomination or assignment, the claim amount is generally paid to:

  1. The agent who sold the policy, to be distributed later
  2. The insurer's unclaimed amounts fund, immediately
  3. The legal heirs, on production of evidence of title such as a succession certificate
  4. The policyholder's employer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The legal heirs, on production of evidence of title such as a succession certificate

Without a nominee or assignee, the insurer pays the persons legally entitled to the estate. It asks for evidence of title, such as a succession certificate or legal heir certificate, to obtain a valid discharge.

Question 7

Within three years of the policy, a death claim may be repudiated mainly if the insurer can establish:

  1. That the agent's licence had expired
  2. Fraud or suppression of material facts in the proposal
  3. That the nominee lives in another state
  4. That the claim was submitted within the prescribed time
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Fraud or suppression of material facts in the proposal

Under Section 45, a policy can be questioned within three years on grounds of fraud or misstatement or suppression of material facts. Administrative issues such as the agent's licence or nominee's address are not grounds.

Question 8

If the life assured dies during the grace period without having paid the due premium, the insurer will usually:

  1. Pay the claim after deducting the unpaid premium
  2. Reject the claim because the premium was unpaid
  3. Pay only the surrender value of the policy
  4. Pay only the premiums received so far
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Pay the claim after deducting the unpaid premium

During the grace period the policy remains in force. If death occurs then, the claim is admitted and the unpaid premium due is deducted from the claim amount.
