Life Insurance Claims: IRDAI IC-38 revision notes

Claims are the moment of truth in insurance. This chapter covers the types of life insurance claims, the documents required, how nominees, assignees and legal heirs receive money, how early claims are investigated, and the timelines IRDAI sets. Questions often test who receives the claim, what an early claim is, and the role of nomination versus assignment.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Types of claims

Survival benefit claims are periodic payments in money back plans. Maturity claims are paid when the policyholder survives to the end of the term. Death claims arise when the insured dies during the term. Rider claims, such as accidental death or critical illness, follow their own conditions.

2. Maturity claims

Insurers send intimation in advance of maturity. The policyholder submits the discharge form, policy document (or indemnity if lost), and bank details. Payment is made on or before the maturity date if documents are in order. Assignees, if any, must sign the discharge.

3. Death claim documents

Standard requirements include the claim form, death certificate, policy document, the claimant's identity and bank details, and for early claims, medical records, employer certificate and, for unnatural death, police FIR and post-mortem report. Insurers cannot ask for documents not needed for the claim.

4. Early claims

A death claim within the first three years of the policy (or revival) is often called an early claim and may be investigated for non-disclosure of material facts. After three years, Section 45 prevents the insurer from questioning the policy on any ground.

5. Nomination

Under Section 39, the policyholder can nominate persons to receive the policy money on death. Nomination can be changed any time by endorsement. A 'beneficial nominee' (spouse, children or parents) is entitled to the money absolutely; other nominees receive it on behalf of legal heirs. If there is no nominee, legal heirs need succession documents.

6. Assignment

Under Section 38, the policyholder can transfer rights in the policy to another person by assignment, absolute (all rights) or conditional (rights revert in specified events). Assignment is commonly done to a bank as security for a loan. On absolute assignment, nomination is cancelled.

7. Presumption of death

When a person has been missing for seven years and not heard of by those who would naturally have heard, the law presumes death. Premiums should be kept paid until the presumption can be raised through a court order, after which the claim can be settled.

8. Timelines and interest

IRDAI regulations require insurers to settle death claims within a fixed period after receiving all documents, with a longer period where investigation is needed, and to pay interest for delay. These periods were shortened under the 2024 policyholder protection framework; check current timelines.

Common traps in the exam

  • Assignment transfers ownership rights; nomination only names who receives the money on death.
  • Absolute assignment cancels an existing nomination.
  • Presumption of death needs seven years of being unheard of; premiums should continue meanwhile.
  • Early claim generally means death within three years of commencement or revival.

Quick recap

Claims: survival benefit, maturity, death and riders. Maturity needs discharge form and policy; death needs claim form, death certificate and supporting records, more for unnatural deaths. Early claims (within three years) may be investigated; after three years Section 45 protects. Section 39 nomination, Section 38 assignment. Presumption of death after seven years. IRDAI sets settlement timelines with interest for delay.
