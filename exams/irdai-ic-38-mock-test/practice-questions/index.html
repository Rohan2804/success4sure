Free IRDAI IC-38 practice questions

Question 1 · Introduction to Insurance

Which of the following risks is normally NOT insurable?

  1. Loss from a fall in the price of shares an investor bought
  2. Damage to a factory by fire
  3. Death of the family breadwinner
  4. Theft of goods from a warehouse
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Loss from a fall in the price of shares an investor bought

Insurance covers pure risks (only loss or no loss); a share-price fall is a speculative risk, which also carries a chance of gain and is not insurable.

Question 2 · Customer Service & Grievances

Before a policyholder can file a complaint with the Insurance Ombudsman, he or she must normally:

  1. File a civil suit in the district court
  2. Get written approval from the insurance agent
  3. Obtain a no-objection certificate from IRDAI
  4. First complain in writing to the insurer and be rejected, get no reply in the prescribed time, or be dissatisfied with the reply
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. First complain in writing to the insurer and be rejected, get no reply in the prescribed time, or be dissatisfied with the reply

The Ombudsman is an appellate forum for grievances: the insurer must first be given the chance to resolve the complaint.

Question 3 · Regulatory Aspects of Agency

Under Section 64VB of the Insurance Act, an insurer cannot assume a risk unless:

  1. The agent has visited the proposer's home
  2. The policy has been in force for one year
  3. The proposer has submitted an income-tax return
  4. The premium is received in advance (or guaranteed / deposited in the prescribed manner)
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The premium is received in advance (or guaranteed / deposited in the prescribed manner)

Section 64VB requires premium to be received in advance before the insurer assumes risk, so cover starts only after payment.

Question 4 · Legal Principles of Insurance

In life insurance, insurable interest must exist:

  1. Only at the time of claim
  2. At the time the policy is taken
  3. Both at inception and at the time of claim
  4. At no stage, since life insurance is a gift
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. At the time the policy is taken

For life insurance, insurable interest is required at inception; it need not continue until death (e.g., after divorce).

Question 5 · Life Insurance & Financial Planning

Using the Rule of 72, money invested at 9% per annum will roughly double in:

  1. 6 years
  2. 9 years
  3. 12 years
  4. 8 years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 8 years

Years to double ≈ 72 ÷ rate = 72 ÷ 9 = 8 years.

Question 6 · Life Insurance Products

A pure term insurance plan typically:

  1. Pays the sum assured on survival to maturity
  2. Pays the sum assured only if the insured dies during the policy term
  3. Guarantees bonuses every year
  4. Has a compulsory five-year lock-in
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Pays the sum assured only if the insured dies during the policy term

Term insurance is pure protection; if the insured survives the term, nothing is payable (unless it is a return-of-premium variant).

Question 7 · Pricing & Valuation

The office (gross) premium of a life policy is:

  1. Net premium plus loadings for expenses, contingencies and (for with-profit plans) bonus
  2. Net premium minus the agent's commission
  3. Only the mortality cost of the year
  4. The sum assured divided by the term
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Net premium plus loadings for expenses, contingencies and (for with-profit plans) bonus

Net premium covers mortality and interest; loadings for expenses and other margins are added to arrive at the office premium.

Question 8 · Documentation & Underwriting

The primary document on which a life insurance contract is based is the:

  1. Proposal form
  2. Premium receipt
  3. Agent's report only
  4. Claim form
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Proposal form

The proposal form carries the proposer's declarations and is the foundation of the contract; misstatements in it can affect claims.

Question 9 · Life Insurance Claims

A policy has sum assured ₹5,00,000 with 20 annual premiums. After paying 8 premiums the policyholder stops paying; accrued bonus is ₹40,000. The paid-up value is:

  1. ₹2,00,000
  2. ₹3,40,000
  3. ₹5,40,000
  4. ₹2,40,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹2,40,000

Paid-up SA = 5,00,000 × 8/20 = ₹2,00,000; add accrued bonus ₹40,000 = ₹2,40,000.

Question 10 · Health Insurance

A hospitalisation claim admissible at ₹1,50,000 is subject to a 10% co-payment. The insurer will pay:

  1. ₹1,50,000
  2. ₹1,40,000
  3. ₹1,35,000
  4. ₹15,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹1,35,000

Co-pay of 10% = ₹15,000 is borne by the insured; insurer pays 1,50,000 − 15,000 = ₹1,35,000.

Question 11 · General Insurance

A house worth ₹10 lakh is insured for ₹6 lakh under a policy with an average clause. A fire causes a loss of ₹4 lakh. The claim payable is:

  1. ₹2,40,000
  2. ₹4,00,000
  3. ₹6,00,000
  4. ₹1,60,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹2,40,000

Under-insurance applies: claim = loss × SI/value = 4,00,000 × 6/10 = ₹2,40,000.

Question 12 · Introduction to Insurance

A shop owner stops locking his store properly because he knows the stock is insured. This is an example of:

  1. Physical hazard
  2. Moral hazard
  3. Peril
  4. Speculative risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Moral hazard

Moral hazard is a change in the insured's attitude or behaviour (here, carelessness) that increases the chance of loss because insurance exists.

Question 13 · Customer Service & Grievances

Bima Bharosa is best described as:

  1. A government scheme that guarantees all insurance claims
  2. A crop insurance scheme for small farmers
  3. The licensing portal through which agents pay their fees
  4. IRDAI's online platform for registering and tracking grievances against insurers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. IRDAI's online platform for registering and tracking grievances against insurers

Bima Bharosa (the successor to IGMS) is IRDAI's integrated grievance management system for policyholder complaints.

Question 14 · Regulatory Aspects of Agency

Under the framework after the 2015 amendment to the Insurance Act, an individual insurance agent is appointed by:

  1. IRDAI directly, through a licence issued to the individual
  2. The Ministry of Finance
  3. The insurer, following IRDAI's eligibility, training and examination norms
  4. The Insurance Ombudsman
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The insurer, following IRDAI's eligibility, training and examination norms

Since the 2015 amendment, insurers appoint agents under IRDAI-approved policies; IRDAI sets the qualification and exam requirements.

Question 15 · Legal Principles of Insurance

Which principle does NOT apply to a life insurance policy?

  1. Utmost good faith
  2. Insurable interest
  3. Valid consideration
  4. Indemnity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Indemnity

Human life cannot be valued exactly, so a life policy pays the agreed sum assured; it is a contract of assurance, not indemnity (hence no contribution or subrogation).
