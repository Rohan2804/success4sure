Legal Principles of Insurance: IRDAI IC-38 practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Legal Principles of Insurance, from the IRDAI IC-38 (Insurance Agents Pre-Recruitment Exam) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the legal foundations of an insurance contract. It begins with the elements of a valid contract under the Indian Contract Act — offer and acceptance, consideration, free consent, competent parties and lawful object — and the difference between void and voidable contracts. It then explains the special principles of insurance: utmost good faith and material facts, insurable interest, indemnity, subrogation, contribution and proximate cause. Learners should also understand warranties and representations. Expect scenario questions where you must decide which principle applies and whether a loss is payable.

Legal Principles of Insurance questions

Question 1

A storm blows the roof off a warehouse and rain then enters and spoils the goods inside. The policy covers storm but excludes rain damage. The loss to the goods is:

  1. Not payable, because rain was the last cause in time
  2. Payable only up to half, because two causes were involved
  3. Payable, because the storm is the proximate cause of the damage
  4. Not payable, because a warehouse cannot be insured against storm
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Payable, because the storm is the proximate cause of the damage

Proximate cause is the active, efficient cause that sets the chain of events in motion. The storm caused the rain to enter, so the storm is the proximate cause and the loss is covered.

Question 2

After paying a full own-damage claim, a motor insurer files a suit against the negligent truck owner who caused the accident to recover the amount. This right arises from the principle of:

  1. Subrogation
  2. Contribution
  3. Utmost good faith
  4. Proximate cause
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Subrogation

Subrogation transfers the insured's rights against a third party to the insurer after it has paid the loss, preventing the insured from being paid twice. Contribution concerns sharing a loss between insurers.

Question 3

Which of the following would be a material fact for a life insurance proposal?

  1. The proposer's favourite sport is cricket
  2. The proposer recently changed his mobile handset and telephone service provider
  3. The proposer prefers vegetarian food on weekdays
  4. The proposer underwent heart bypass surgery two years ago
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The proposer underwent heart bypass surgery two years ago

A material fact is one that would influence the underwriter's decision to accept the risk or the premium charged. A past heart surgery clearly affects mortality risk; the other facts are irrelevant.

Question 4

In the formation of an insurance contract, the 'offer' is usually made by:

  1. The insurer, by publishing its sales brochure and advertisements
  2. The proposer, by submitting a signed proposal form
  3. The agent, by giving a premium quotation
  4. IRDAI, by approving the product
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The proposer, by submitting a signed proposal form

A brochure or quotation is only an invitation to offer. The proposer makes the offer through the proposal form, and the insurer accepts it, often with or without modifications, by issuing the policy.

Question 5

A contract entered into because one party's consent was obtained by undue influence is:

  1. Void from the very beginning and therefore unenforceable by either party
  2. Voidable at the option of the party whose consent was so obtained
  3. Valid and fully enforceable against both parties
  4. Illegal and punishable as a criminal offence
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Voidable at the option of the party whose consent was so obtained

Under the Indian Contract Act, consent caused by coercion, undue influence, fraud or misrepresentation is not free, and the contract becomes voidable at the option of the aggrieved party.

Question 6

Who among the following has an insurable interest in the life of a person?

  1. A close neighbour who has lived in the same housing society for many years
  2. A fan who admires that person's public work
  3. A colleague who works in another department
  4. A bank that has lent money to that person, to the extent of the loan
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A bank that has lent money to that person, to the extent of the loan

Insurable interest exists where a person would suffer a financial loss from the death of the life assured. A creditor has such interest up to the debt amount; a neighbour, fan or colleague does not.

Question 7

A term in a policy that must be strictly complied with, whether or not it is material to the risk, and whose breach allows the insurer to avoid the policy, is a:

  1. Warranty
  2. Representation
  3. Endorsement
  4. Recital clause
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Warranty

A warranty is a promise by the insured that must be literally fulfilled. A breach entitles the insurer to avoid the contract even if the breach did not contribute to the loss. A representation only needs to be substantially true.

Question 8

In an insurance contract, the consideration given by the insured is the:

  1. Sum assured
  2. Proposal form
  3. Premium
  4. Policy document
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Premium

Consideration is what each party gives in exchange. The insured pays the premium, and the insurer gives its promise to pay on the happening of the insured event. The sum assured is the insurer's promised payment.
