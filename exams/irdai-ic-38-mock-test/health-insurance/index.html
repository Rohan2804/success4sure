Health Insurance: IRDAI IC-38 practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Health Insurance, from the IRDAI IC-38 (Insurance Agents Pre-Recruitment Exam) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers health insurance products and their key features. It explains indemnity hospitalisation policies, family floaters, top-up and super top-up covers, critical illness and other fixed-benefit plans, and the inclusion of day-care procedures, AYUSH treatment and pre- and post-hospitalisation expenses. Learners should understand terms such as sum insured, sub-limits, room-rent limits with proportionate deduction, co-payment, deductibles, waiting periods and exclusions. Practise calculations on super top-ups and proportionate deduction, and be clear on the difference between indemnity and benefit policies. Avoid memorising limits that may change; focus on the concepts.

Health Insurance questions

Question 1

A super top-up health policy differs from a regular top-up policy because its deductible applies to:

  1. Each separate hospitalisation claim individually
  2. Only claims made in the first year
  3. The total of all admissible claims in a policy year
  4. Only the pre- and post-hospitalisation expenses
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The total of all admissible claims in a policy year

A regular top-up pays only when a single claim exceeds the deductible. A super top-up considers the aggregate of claims in the year, paying once the combined amount crosses the deductible.

Question 2

A super top-up policy has a deductible of ₹3 lakh. During the policy year, the insured has two admissible hospitalisation claims of ₹2 lakh and ₹2.5 lakh. The super top-up insurer will pay:

  1. ₹1.5 lakh
  2. Nil
  3. ₹4.5 lakh
  4. ₹2 lakh
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹1.5 lakh

Aggregate claims = 2 + 2.5 = ₹4.5 lakh. Excess over the ₹3 lakh deductible = ₹1.5 lakh. A regular top-up with a per-claim deductible would pay nil, as neither single claim exceeds ₹3 lakh.

Question 3

A family floater health policy provides:

  1. A separate, equal sum insured for each family member, which cannot be shared with any other member
  2. Cover only for the eldest member of the family
  3. Cover for the family only while travelling abroad
  4. One sum insured that can be used by any one or all of the covered family members
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. One sum insured that can be used by any one or all of the covered family members

Under a floater, the sum insured 'floats' across the family: any member can use it, and total claims by all members cannot exceed it in the year. Individual policies give each person a separate sum insured.

Question 4

A critical illness policy typically pays:

  1. Only the actual hospital bills, up to the sum insured
  2. A lump sum on diagnosis of a listed illness, irrespective of actual expenses
  3. A fixed daily allowance for each day spent in hospital, subject to a maximum number of days
  4. Only the cost of medicines after discharge
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A lump sum on diagnosis of a listed illness, irrespective of actual expenses

Critical illness cover is a benefit policy. On diagnosis of a covered illness such as cancer or a heart attack, and survival of any stated period, it pays a fixed lump sum regardless of the bills incurred.

Question 5

The cost of diagnostic tests done a few days before admission, for the same illness for which the insured is hospitalised, is covered under:

  1. Domiciliary hospitalisation
  2. Pre-hospitalisation expenses
  3. Day-care treatment
  4. Post-hospitalisation expenses
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Pre-hospitalisation expenses

Pre-hospitalisation cover pays for related medical expenses incurred for a specified number of days before admission. Post-hospitalisation cover applies to expenses after discharge.

Question 6

A policy allows room rent of ₹5,000 per day, but the insured chooses a room costing ₹10,000 per day. Associated medical expenses to which proportionate deduction applies are ₹80,000. The amount payable for these expenses is:

  1. ₹80,000
  2. ₹60,000
  3. ₹20,000
  4. ₹40,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹40,000

The eligible room is half the room taken (5,000 ÷ 10,000), so associated expenses are paid proportionately: 80,000 × 0.5 = ₹40,000. Items such as pharmacy and implants are excluded from proportionate deduction.

Question 7

AYUSH treatment covered under many health policies refers to:

  1. Ayurveda, Yoga and Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha and Homoeopathy
  2. Allopathy, Yoga, Unani, Surgery and Homoeopathy
  3. Acupuncture, Yoga, Unani, Siddha and Hydrotherapy
  4. Ayurveda, Yoga, Unani, Sowa-Rigpa and Herbal remedies only
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Ayurveda, Yoga and Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha and Homoeopathy

AYUSH stands for Ayurveda, Yoga and Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha and Homoeopathy. Health policies cover in-patient treatment under these systems in recognised hospitals, subject to policy terms.

Question 8

Day-care procedures in health insurance are:

  1. Routine outpatient consultations with a general physician
  2. Treatments taken only at home on a doctor's advice
  3. Treatments that need less than 24 hours of hospitalisation because of advanced technology, and are covered
  4. Cosmetic procedures done in a clinic in a single day
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Treatments that need less than 24 hours of hospitalisation because of advanced technology, and are covered

Procedures such as cataract surgery or chemotherapy can now be done in under 24 hours. Policies cover listed day-care procedures even though the usual 24-hour hospitalisation condition is not met.
