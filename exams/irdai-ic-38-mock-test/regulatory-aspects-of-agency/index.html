Regulatory Aspects of Agency: IRDAI IC-38 practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Regulatory Aspects of Agency, from the IRDAI IC-38 (Insurance Agents Pre-Recruitment Exam) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the legal framework within which an insurance agent works. It explains the role of IRDAI, set up under the IRDA Act, 1999, and key provisions of the Insurance Act, 1938, such as payment of commission only to authorised persons and the prohibition of rebating. It also covers the agent's code of conduct: disclosing identity and the insurer represented, explaining product features honestly, avoiding mis-selling, handling premiums properly and helping customers with documentation and claims. Learners should know the grounds on which an agency can be cancelled and should be able to recognise ethical and unethical behaviour in practical situations.

Regulatory Aspects of Agency questions

Question 1

The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI) was established under:

  1. The Insurance Act, 1938
  2. The IRDA Act, 1999
  3. The LIC Act, 1956
  4. The General Insurance Business (Nationalisation) Act, 1972
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The IRDA Act, 1999

IRDAI is a statutory body created by the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority Act, 1999. The Insurance Act, 1938 is the main law governing insurance business, which IRDAI administers.

Question 2

Under Section 40 of the Insurance Act, 1938, an insurer is NOT permitted to pay commission or remuneration for procuring business to:

  1. An individual agent appointed by the insurer
  2. A corporate agent registered with IRDAI
  3. An insurance broker licensed by IRDAI and acting on behalf of the client who placed the business
  4. A person who is not an agent or intermediary authorised under the Act
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A person who is not an agent or intermediary authorised under the Act

Section 40 allows commission only to persons authorised to solicit insurance, such as appointed agents and licensed intermediaries. Paying an unlicensed person for bringing in business is prohibited.

Question 3

Which of the following is the most likely ground for cancellation of an individual agent's appointment?

  1. Being found to have suppressed material facts or committed fraud while soliciting business
  2. Selling more policies than the agent's annual target
  3. Recommending a pure term plan instead of a savings-oriented endowment plan to a young customer with dependants and a home loan
  4. Advising a customer to update his nomination after marriage
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Being found to have suppressed material facts or committed fraud while soliciting business

An insurer may cancel an agent's appointment for misconduct such as fraud, misrepresentation or breach of the code of conduct. Meeting targets or giving suitable advice are good practices, not grounds for cancellation.

Question 4

Under the code of conduct, while soliciting business an agent must:

  1. Keep the name of his insurer confidential until the policy is issued
  2. Promise a fixed bonus rate for all future years
  3. Identify himself and disclose the name of the insurer he represents
  4. Collect the proposal without explaining the product features
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Identify himself and disclose the name of the insurer he represents

The code of conduct requires an agent to disclose his identity and licence, the insurer he represents and, on request, his commission. He must explain product features honestly and must not promise future bonuses.

Question 5

An agent describes a market-linked ULIP to a retired person seeking guaranteed monthly income as 'a fixed deposit with free insurance'. This is:

  1. Acceptable, because a ULIP does have an insurance element
  2. Acceptable, if the customer signs the proposal form
  3. Rebating under Section 41 of the Insurance Act
  4. Mis-selling through misrepresentation of the product
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Mis-selling through misrepresentation of the product

Describing a market-linked product as a guaranteed deposit misrepresents its risk and does not suit the customer's need. This is mis-selling. Rebating, in contrast, means sharing commission or premium as an inducement.

Question 6

An agent's commission on a policy is paid by:

  1. The policyholder, as a separate fee over and above the premium
  2. IRDAI, from the fees collected from insurers
  3. The insurer, out of the premium it receives
  4. The Insurance Council, from its annual budget
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The insurer, out of the premium it receives

Agents are remunerated by the insurer, and the cost of commission is built into the premium loading. The policyholder does not pay a separate fee to the agent, and neither IRDAI nor the Council pays commission.

Question 7

A customer hands over the renewal premium in cash to his agent. As a matter of proper conduct, the agent should:

  1. Keep it with him until the end of the grace period to earn interest
  2. Deposit it with the insurer promptly and ensure the customer gets the insurer's official receipt
  3. Give the customer a handwritten personal receipt on his own letterhead in place of the insurer's official premium receipt
  4. Adjust part of it against his own commission before depositing
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Deposit it with the insurer promptly and ensure the customer gets the insurer's official receipt

Premium belongs to the insurer and must be remitted promptly; only the insurer's receipt is valid proof of payment. Holding the money or deducting commission from it is a serious breach of conduct.

Question 8

If a proposer is unable to fill in the proposal form himself, the agent may help by:

  1. Filling it as per the proposer's answers, explaining the contents, and obtaining the proposer's signature with a suitable declaration
  2. Filling and signing the form on the proposer's behalf without telling him the contents
  3. Leaving the medical questions blank so that underwriting is quicker
  4. Writing down the answers that he believes the underwriter is most likely to accept, even where the proposer actually said something different
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Filling it as per the proposer's answers, explaining the contents, and obtaining the proposer's signature with a suitable declaration

The proposal remains the proposer's statement. If someone else fills it, the contents must be explained to the proposer, who signs it, and a declaration to this effect is recorded. Altering or omitting answers is unethical.
