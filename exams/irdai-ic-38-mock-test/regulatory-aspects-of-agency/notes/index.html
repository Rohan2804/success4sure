Regulatory Aspects of Agency: IRDAI IC-38 revision notes

This chapter explains the legal framework that governs insurance agents: who can become one, how they are appointed, what they may and may not do, and the penalties for violations. Questions test eligibility, training, the number of insurers an agent can represent, Section 41 on rebating, and the agent's code of conduct. IRDAI rules here are updated periodically.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Who is an insurance agent

An insurance agent is a person appointed by an insurer to solicit and procure insurance business, including renewals, in return for commission. The agent represents the insurer, so the insurer is responsible for the agent's acts within authority.

2. Eligibility

An individual must be at least 18 years old, have the minimum educational qualification prescribed by IRDAI (at least a 10th standard pass), complete the prescribed training from an approved institution and pass the IRDAI pre-recruitment examination (IC-38). The person must not be disqualified, for example for criminal breach of trust or being of unsound mind.

3. Appointment by insurer

Since 2016 regulations, insurers themselves appoint individual agents after verifying eligibility, rather than IRDAI issuing licences. The insurer issues an appointment letter and identity card. The agent can work for a limited number of insurers across life, general and health as IRDAI permits; check current rules.

4. Section 41: rebating prohibited

No person may offer any rebate of commission or premium as an inducement to take or renew a policy, except rebates allowed in the insurer's published prospectus. An agent offering a customer half of his commission to buy a policy violates Section 41 and faces a penalty.

5. Commission

Commission is paid by the insurer, not by the policyholder directly, from the premium. IRDAI's expense of management regulations allow insurers to set commission through board-approved policies within overall expense limits. Agents must disclose commission if the customer asks.

6. Code of conduct

Agents must identify themselves and the insurer, explain product features fully, help in filling the proposal form correctly, disclose all material facts to the insurer, not induce policyholders to drop existing policies for new ones (twisting), and assist in claims. They must not misrepresent or collect premium beyond authority.

7. Cancellation of appointment

The insurer can cancel an appointment for violating the code of conduct, fraud, or disqualification. IRDAI can impose penalties on insurers and intermediaries for violations. A cancelled agent's details may be shared across the industry to prevent reappointment elsewhere.

8. Other intermediaries

Corporate agents (including banks under bancassurance), insurance brokers (who represent the customer, not the insurer), insurance marketing firms, web aggregators and point-of-sales persons are other channels with their own regulations. Exams often contrast agents and brokers.

Common traps in the exam

  • An agent represents the insurer; a broker represents the customer.
  • Offering part of commission as a rebate violates Section 41 unless allowed in the prospectus.
  • Insurers, not IRDAI, now appoint individual agents after the IC-38 exam and training.
  • Persuading a customer to surrender an old policy to buy a new one (twisting) breaches the code of conduct.

Quick recap

Agents are appointed by insurers to sell and service policies for commission. Eligibility: 18+, minimum 10th pass, prescribed training and IC-38. Section 41 bans rebates except those in the prospectus. Commission comes from the insurer within expense limits. Code: disclose fully, fill proposals honestly, no twisting, help with claims. Brokers represent customers, agents represent insurers.
