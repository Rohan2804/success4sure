General Insurance: IRDAI IC-38 revision notes

General insurance covers property, liability and personal accident risks. For IC-38, you need a working knowledge of the main classes, how sums insured and claims are calculated, and key terms such as average clause, deductible, IDV and no claim bonus. Questions often include a quick under-insurance calculation or ask which policy covers a described loss.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Fire insurance

Property policies cover buildings, plant, stocks and contents against fire and allied perils such as lightning, explosion, storm, flood, riot and malicious damage. For businesses, standard products such as Bharat Sookshma Udyam Suraksha (small units) and Bharat Laghu Udyam Suraksha are used; Bharat Griha Raksha covers homes.

2. Average clause

If property is under-insured, claims are reduced proportionately. A building worth ₹50 lakh insured for ₹40 lakh suffers a ₹10 lakh loss: claim = 10 lakh x 40/50 = ₹8 lakh. This encourages insuring for full value. Some standard home and small business policies waive average within limits.

3. Marine insurance

Marine cargo insurance covers goods in transit by sea, air, rail or road, using Institute Cargo Clauses A (widest, all risks subject to exclusions), B and C (narrowest, named perils). Marine hull insurance covers ships. Insurable interest must exist at the time of loss.

4. Motor insurance

Third-party liability insurance is compulsory under the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988, covering death, injury and property damage to others. Own damage cover protects the insured vehicle. A comprehensive (package) policy combines both. Standalone own damage policies are available for vehicles with separate TP cover.

5. IDV and no claim bonus

Insured Declared Value is the sum insured for own damage, based on the manufacturer's listed price less depreciation by age. No claim bonus is a discount on the own damage premium for claim-free years, rising in steps from 20% to 50%, and belongs to the insured, so it can transfer to a new vehicle.

6. Engineering and liability

Engineering covers include contractor's all risk, erection all risk, machinery breakdown and electronic equipment insurance. Liability covers include public liability, product liability, professional indemnity and directors and officers liability, protecting against legal claims by third parties.

7. Personal accident and others

Personal accident policies pay fixed benefits for accidental death or disability and may reimburse medical expenses. Other covers include burglary, money in transit, fidelity guarantee, travel, crop insurance (PMFBY), shopkeeper's and householder's packages.

8. Deductibles and claims

A deductible (excess) is the first part of each claim borne by the insured, reducing small claims and premium. On a claim, the insured notifies the insurer promptly, takes steps to minimise loss, and a surveyor may assess losses above a threshold. Subrogation and contribution apply in general insurance.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Claim under average clause = loss x (sum insured / value at risk).
  • IDV = manufacturer's listed selling price less age-based depreciation (plus accessories similarly adjusted).

Common traps in the exam

  • Third-party motor cover is compulsory; own damage cover is optional.
  • No claim bonus belongs to the insured, not the vehicle.
  • Institute Cargo Clause A is the widest; C is the narrowest.
  • Average clause applies when sum insured is less than value at risk, reducing the claim proportionately.

Quick recap

General insurance covers property, liability and accident risks. Fire policies (Bharat Sookshma, Laghu, Griha Raksha) cover fire and allied perils; average clause cuts claims for under-insurance. Marine cargo uses ICC A, B, C. Motor third-party is compulsory; own damage is optional; IDV is sum insured; NCB 20% to 50% stays with the insured. Engineering, liability and PA round off products.
