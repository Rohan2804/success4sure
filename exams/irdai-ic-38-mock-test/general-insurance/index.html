General Insurance: IRDAI IC-38 practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on General Insurance, from the IRDAI IC-38 (Insurance Agents Pre-Recruitment Exam) syllabus.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces the main lines of general (non-life) insurance. It covers fire and special perils policies and the basis of claim settlement (market value or reinstatement value), burglary, motor insurance (third-party liability and own damage, deductibles and salvage), marine hull and cargo, liability covers such as public liability, and miscellaneous covers like fidelity guarantee. Learners should know which perils are typically covered or excluded, how deductibles and salvage reduce a claim, and which policy suits a given business need. Focus on applying general insurance principles, especially indemnity, to practical claim situations.

General Insurance questions

Question 1

Which of the following is typically covered under a standard fire and special perils policy?

  1. Gradual wear and tear of machinery
  2. Loss due to war or invasion
  3. Theft without any fire or other insured event
  4. Damage caused by lightning
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Damage caused by lightning

Lightning is a named peril under the standard fire and special perils policy. Wear and tear, war and simple theft are excluded or need separate covers such as burglary insurance.

Question 2

A private car's own-damage loss is assessed at ₹38,000. The damaged parts, retained by the insured, have a salvage value of ₹2,000, and the compulsory deductible is ₹1,000. The claim payable is:

  1. ₹37,000
  2. ₹36,000
  3. ₹35,000
  4. ₹38,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹35,000

Claim = 38,000 − 2,000 (salvage retained by insured) − 1,000 (compulsory deductible) = ₹35,000. Paying ₹36,000 or ₹37,000 would ignore one of the deductions, giving the insured more than an indemnity.

Question 3

A standard burglary policy usually requires, for a claim to be admissible:

  1. That the theft was committed by an employee of the insured
  2. Forcible and violent entry into or exit from the insured premises
  3. That the stolen goods were kept outside the premises
  4. That the loss occurred during a fire at the premises
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Forcible and violent entry into or exit from the insured premises

Burglary insurance covers theft involving forcible and violent entry or exit. Theft by employees is excluded (fidelity guarantee covers it), and goods outside the premises are generally not covered.

Question 4

Marine hull insurance covers:

  1. The ship itself, including its machinery, against perils of the sea
  2. Only the cargo carried on the ship
  3. Only the wages of the ship's crew
  4. Goods carried by road from the port to the warehouse
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The ship itself, including its machinery, against perils of the sea

Marine insurance has two main branches: hull, covering the vessel and its machinery, and cargo, covering goods in transit. Inland road transit is covered under inland transit or cargo policies.

Question 5

A public liability policy protects the insured against:

  1. Damage to the insured's own building from fire
  2. Loss of cash due to dishonesty of employees
  3. Legal liability to third parties for bodily injury or property damage arising from its premises or operations
  4. Fall in the market value of the insured's shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Legal liability to third parties for bodily injury or property damage arising from its premises or operations

Liability insurance covers the insured's legal liability to others. A public liability policy pays compensation to members of the public injured or whose property is damaged due to the insured's premises or activities.

Question 6

Under a reinstatement value fire policy, a claim is settled on the basis of:

  1. The cost of replacing the property with new property of the same kind, without deduction for depreciation
  2. The market value of the property after deducting depreciation
  3. The original purchase price of the property as shown in the books of account, regardless of when it was bought
  4. Half of the sum insured, irrespective of the actual loss
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The cost of replacing the property with new property of the same kind, without deduction for depreciation

Reinstatement value cover pays the cost of rebuilding or replacing with new property of the same kind and capacity, without a deduction for depreciation. A market value policy deducts depreciation.

Question 7

A comprehensive (package) motor policy covers:

  1. Only third-party liability as required by law
  2. Only own damage to the vehicle, without any third-party cover
  3. Only personal accident of passengers
  4. Both own damage to the insured vehicle and third-party liability
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Both own damage to the insured vehicle and third-party liability

A package policy combines own-damage cover for the vehicle with the compulsory third-party liability cover. A 'liability only' policy covers just the third-party portion required by law.

Question 8

A company wants protection against loss of money caused by the dishonesty of its cashier. The most suitable cover is:

  1. Burglary insurance
  2. Fidelity guarantee insurance
  3. Money-in-transit insurance
  4. Public liability insurance
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Fidelity guarantee insurance

Fidelity guarantee insurance indemnifies an employer for financial loss caused by fraud or dishonesty of employees. Burglary and money-in-transit covers deal with outsiders' theft, not employee dishonesty.
