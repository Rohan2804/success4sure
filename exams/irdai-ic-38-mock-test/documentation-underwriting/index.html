Documentation & Underwriting: IRDAI IC-38 practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Documentation & Underwriting, from the IRDAI IC-38 (Insurance Agents Pre-Recruitment Exam) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the documents used at each stage of a life policy and the process of underwriting. Documents include the prospectus, proposal form, age and KYC proof, first premium receipt, the policy document and endorsements. Learners should know standard and non-standard age proofs, the rules on nomination (including appointees for minor nominees) and assignment. Underwriting is the process of assessing and classifying risk: financial underwriting to avoid over-insurance, medical underwriting, and the treatment of standard and substandard lives. Focus on what each document proves and on recognising which factors an underwriter will look at closely.

Documentation & Underwriting questions

Question 1

Among these documents, which one would an insurer normally regard as weaker, non-standard evidence of the proposer's age?

  1. A passport
  2. A birth certificate issued by the municipal registrar of births
  3. A school leaving certificate
  4. A self-declaration or affidavit of age
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A self-declaration or affidavit of age

Non-standard age proofs, such as an affidavit or self-declaration, are less reliable, and insurers may accept them only with restrictions or an extra premium. Birth certificates, passports and school certificates are standard proofs.

Question 2

The prospectus or sales brochure of a life insurance product must contain:

  1. Only the insurer's past bonus rates and claim figures
  2. Only the name and contact details of the agent
  3. The product's features, benefits, exclusions and main terms and conditions
  4. A guarantee that the product will always outperform bank fixed deposits over every policy term
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The product's features, benefits, exclusions and main terms and conditions

The prospectus is meant to help a prospect make an informed decision, so it must describe the scope of benefits, conditions and exclusions honestly. It cannot promise returns that are not guaranteed.

Question 3

The First Premium Receipt (FPR) issued by a life insurer signifies that:

  1. The proposal is still under consideration
  2. The insurance contract has commenced
  3. The policy has been assigned to a bank
  4. The free-look period has expired
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The insurance contract has commenced

The FPR is issued once the proposal is accepted and the first premium is adjusted. It is evidence that the contract has started; subsequent premiums are acknowledged through renewal premium receipts.

Question 4

A change in the policyholder's address on an existing life policy is usually recorded by means of:

  1. An endorsement
  2. A fresh proposal form
  3. A new policy bond
  4. A revival quotation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. An endorsement

Endorsements are written amendments that record changes in the terms or details of a policy after issue, such as address, nomination or plan changes. They form part of the policy document.

Question 5

A proposer with a modest declared income asks for a very large sum assured without any apparent financial need. The underwriter's main concern here is:

  1. The proposer's age proof being non-standard
  2. The premium mode chosen by the proposer
  3. Over-insurance and the possibility of moral hazard
  4. The agent's eligibility for commission
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Over-insurance and the possibility of moral hazard

Financial underwriting checks that the sum assured is reasonable relative to income and need. Excessive cover without justification suggests speculation or moral hazard, so the underwriter may limit the cover.

Question 6

Under Section 39 of the Insurance Act, a nomination can be made by:

  1. A policyholder who has taken a policy on his or her own life
  2. Any person who pays the premium on someone else's life
  3. The nominee, on behalf of the policyholder
  4. The agent, at the time of collecting the proposal
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A policyholder who has taken a policy on his or her own life

Nomination is permitted only where the policyholder has insured his or her own life. It names who will receive the claim money. It can be made at proposal stage or later, by an endorsement.

Question 7

When a policyholder nominates his minor son, he should also name:

  1. An assignee who will become the owner of the policy
  2. A guarantor who will continue paying the premium
  3. A trustee under the Married Women's Property Act, 1874, to hold the policy money for the minor
  4. An appointee to receive the claim money during the nominee's minority
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. An appointee to receive the claim money during the nominee's minority

A minor cannot give a valid discharge for the claim. So the policyholder names an appointee, who receives the policy money on the minor's behalf if the claim arises before the nominee turns 18.

Question 8

Which of the following is most likely to make an underwriter ask for a detailed medical examination, even for a modest sum assured?

  1. The proposer being a salaried employee of a government bank
  2. A history of diabetes and high blood pressure disclosed in the proposal
  3. The proposer choosing the annual mode of premium payment
  4. The proposer having submitted a passport as age proof
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A history of diabetes and high blood pressure disclosed in the proposal

Disclosed medical conditions such as diabetes or hypertension directly affect mortality, so the underwriter needs medical evidence to classify the life as standard or substandard. The other facts do not raise health concerns.
