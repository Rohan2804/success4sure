English Language: IBPS PO practice questions

15 free questions with answers and explanations on English Language, from the IBPS PO (IBPS Probationary Officer (Prelims)) syllabus.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this section covers

IBPS PO English tests reading comprehension, vocabulary in context, grammar and sentence structure. Expect passages on banking, economy and social themes, followed by questions on main idea, inference and word meaning. Grammar appears as error spotting, sentence improvement and fillers, so revise subject–verb agreement, conditionals, tenses, prepositions and paired conjunctions such as 'scarcely…when'. Para jumbles reward spotting linking words like 'however' and 'therefore'. Build vocabulary through regular reading of editorials rather than memorising long lists, and practise timed passages to improve speed.

English Language questions

Question 1

Read the passage and answer the question. Financial literacy campaigns often focus on teaching people to save, but saving alone is not enough. A household that keeps all its surplus as cash at home loses purchasing power every year to inflation. Knowing where to save, and understanding the trade-off between safety, liquidity and return, matters as much as the habit itself. Which of the following best expresses the main idea of the passage?

  1. Inflation harms only those households that save regularly every month
  2. Saving well needs knowledge of where and how to save, not just the habit
  3. Financial literacy campaigns have been of no real use to households
  4. Liquidity is always more important than the safety of one's savings
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Saving well needs knowledge of where and how to save, not just the habit

The passage says saving 'alone is not enough' and that knowing where to save and weighing safety, liquidity and return matters as much. It never calls campaigns useless or ranks liquidity above safety.

Question 2

Read the passage and answer the question. Once considered a novelty, QR-code payments have become ubiquitous in Indian towns; even roadside tea stalls display a code beside the kettle. Yet this convenience brings a new risk: fraudsters paste their own codes over a merchant's, diverting payments that customers believe they are making to the shop. As used in the passage, the word 'ubiquitous' most nearly means:

  1. Very expensive
  2. Hard to trust
  3. Rarely noticed
  4. Found everywhere
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Found everywhere

Ubiquitous means present everywhere. The example of even roadside tea stalls displaying a QR code shows how widespread such payments have become, so 'found everywhere' fits the context.

Question 3

Read the passage and answer the question. Once considered a novelty, QR-code payments have become ubiquitous in Indian towns; even roadside tea stalls display a code beside the kettle. Yet this convenience brings a new risk: fraudsters paste their own codes over a merchant's, diverting payments that customers believe they are making to the shop. According to the passage, how do fraudsters divert payments meant for merchants?

  1. By hacking into the bank accounts of the roadside tea stalls
  2. By asking customers to pay in cash instead of by QR code
  3. By covering the merchant's QR code with a code of their own
  4. By printing fake currency notes and passing them to shopkeepers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. By covering the merchant's QR code with a code of their own

The passage states that fraudsters paste their own codes over a merchant's, so customers unknowingly pay the fraudster. Hacking accounts or fake notes is not mentioned anywhere in the passage.

Question 4

Choose the word that best fills the blank. The committee's report was so ________ that even experts found it difficult to follow.

  1. lucid
  2. convoluted
  3. concise
  4. candid
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. convoluted

Convoluted means extremely complex and hard to follow, which matches 'even experts found it difficult'. Lucid and concise mean clear and brief, which contradict the sentence; candid means frank.

Question 5

Choose the word that best fills the blank. Every bank must ________ with the know-your-customer norms before opening a new account.

  1. comply
  2. compile
  3. compel
  4. complain
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. comply

'Comply with' means to act in accordance with a rule, which is what banks must do with KYC norms. Compile, compel and complain are similar-sounding but do not fit with 'with the norms'.

Question 6

Choose the pair of words that best fills both blanks. The new policy was ________ by the board, but its implementation was ________ due to a shortage of funds.

  1. rejected, hastened
  2. approved, accelerated
  3. opposed, completed
  4. approved, delayed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. approved, delayed

'But' signals contrast: the board approved the policy, yet a fund shortage delayed it. A shortage of funds cannot logically accelerate or complete implementation, so the other pairs fail.

Question 7

Identify the part of the sentence that contains a grammatical error. The number of customers (A) / visiting the branch (B) / have increased sharply (C) / since last year. (D)

  1. (A)
  2. (B)
  3. (C)
  4. (D)
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. (C)

The subject is 'the number', which is singular, so the verb must be 'has increased'. Contrast 'a number of customers have', where the plural verb would be correct. The error is in part (C).

Question 8

Identify the part of the sentence that contains a grammatical error. If I was (A) / in your place, (B) / I would accept (C) / the transfer offer. (D)

  1. (A)
  2. (B)
  3. (C)
  4. (D)
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. (A)

In an imaginary or hypothetical condition, the subjunctive 'were' is used with all subjects: 'If I were in your place'. Hence part (A) contains the error; the rest is correct.

Question 9

Choose the option that best replaces the part in capitals. If no change is needed, choose 'No improvement required'. He insisted ON TO PAY the bill himself.

  1. on paying
  2. to pay
  3. for paying
  4. No improvement required
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. on paying

The verb 'insist' takes the preposition 'on' followed by a gerund: 'insisted on paying'. 'Insisted to pay' and 'insisted for paying' are incorrect usages, so the sentence needs improvement.

Question 10

Choose the option that best replaces the part in capitals. If no change is needed, choose 'No improvement required'. Scarcely had she reached the station WHEN THE TRAIN LEFT.

  1. than the train left
  2. then the train left
  3. when the train had left
  4. No improvement required
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. No improvement required

'Scarcely' pairs with 'when' (just as 'no sooner' pairs with 'than'), and the past perfect belongs in the first clause. So the sentence is already correct; 'than' is a common trap.

Question 11

Rearrange the sentences to form a coherent paragraph. P: With a microloan from a self-help group, she finally bought her first machine. Q: Meena had always wanted to start a small tailoring business at home. R: Within a year, she was earning enough to repay the loan and hire a helper. S: However, she lacked the money to buy even a single sewing machine.

  1. SQPR
  2. QPSR
  3. QSPR
  4. PQSR
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. QSPR

Q introduces Meena's wish, S states the obstacle with 'However', P resolves it through the microloan, and R gives the result a year later. Hence QSPR forms the logical sequence.

Question 12

Select the synonym of the given word: AMELIORATE

  1. Worsen
  2. Improve
  3. Ignore
  4. Complicate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Improve

To ameliorate is to make something bad or unsatisfactory better, so 'improve' is the synonym. 'Worsen' is its opposite, and ignore or complicate do not carry the sense of making better.

Question 13

Pick the antonym of OBSCURE from the options given below.

  1. Evident
  2. Hidden
  3. Vague
  4. Murky
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Evident

Obscure means not clear or hard to understand. 'Evident' means plainly visible or clear, making it the antonym. Hidden, vague and murky are close in meaning to obscure, not opposite.

Question 14

Choose the correct meaning of the idiom: 'To bite the bullet'.

  1. To speak rudely to a senior colleague
  2. To make a hasty decision without thinking
  3. To avoid a problem for as long as possible
  4. To face a painful situation with courage
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. To face a painful situation with courage

'Bite the bullet' means to accept and face something unpleasant or difficult bravely, for example accepting a tough cost-cutting decision. Avoiding a problem is the opposite of its meaning.

Question 15

Choose the word that is INCORRECTLY spelt.

  1. Embarrass
  2. Occurrence
  3. Millenium
  4. Questionnaire
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Millenium

The correct spelling is 'Millennium', with double l and double n. Embarrass (double r, double s), occurrence (double c, double r) and questionnaire (double n) are all spelt correctly.
