Taxation: NISM Series V-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Taxation, from the NISM Series V-D (Mutual Fund – Specialized Investment Fund (SIF) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers taxation of mutual fund and SIF investments in India. Revise how a scheme is classified for tax (equity-oriented with at least 65% domestic equity, specified debt-oriented schemes, and others), holding periods, current capital gains rates, IDCW taxation and TDS, set-off and carry-forward of losses, and the treatment of switches. For long-short SIF strategies, note that classification depends on the actual equity exposure as defined by tax law. Rates change with Budgets, so confirm current figures before the exam.

Taxation questions

Question 1

An SIF strategy keeps at least 65% of its assets in domestic listed equity, including hedged positions as permitted by tax rules. For tax purposes it would be treated as:

  1. Equity-oriented
  2. A specified debt-oriented fund taxed at slab rates, irrespective of holding period
  3. A business trust
  4. An exempt instrument
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Equity-oriented

Tax treatment follows the portfolio composition. A fund meeting the 65% domestic equity threshold is equity-oriented, so equity capital gains rates and the 12-month holding period apply.

Question 2

Equity-oriented fund units held for 10 months are sold at a gain of ₹80,000. Ignoring cess, the tax on this gain is:

  1. ₹10,000, since gains below ₹1.25 lakh are taxed at the long-term rate
  2. ₹12,000
  3. ₹16,000
  4. Nil
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹16,000

Units held for 12 months or less give short-term gains, taxed at 20% for equity-oriented funds. 20% of ₹80,000 = ₹16,000. The ₹1.25 lakh exemption applies only to long-term gains.

Question 3

A long-term capital loss on mutual fund units that cannot be set off this year can be carried forward for up to:

  1. 4 assessment years, and only against business income of those years
  2. 1 year
  3. Indefinitely
  4. 8 assessment years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 8 assessment years

Capital losses can be carried forward for eight assessment years, provided the return is filed on time. Long-term losses can only be set off against long-term gains.

Question 4

A resident investor receives IDCW from a mutual fund. Which statement is correct?

  1. It is exempt because the fund has already paid dividend distribution tax on it
  2. It is taxed at the investor's slab rate
  3. It is taxed at 12.5% flat
  4. It is taxed only on redemption
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. It is taxed at the investor's slab rate

Dividend distribution tax was abolished in 2020. IDCW is now taxed in the investor's hands at slab rates, with TDS above the prescribed threshold.

Question 5

An investor switches units from an equity long-short SIF strategy to a debt strategy of the same AMC. For tax purposes, this is:

  1. Exempt, since the AMC and the investor remain the same
  2. Taxable only if exit load is charged
  3. A taxable transfer
  4. Treated as a gift
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A taxable transfer

A switch is a redemption from one scheme or strategy and a purchase in another, so any capital gain on the units switched out is taxable.

Question 6

Securities Transaction Tax applies on redemption of units of:

  1. Debt-oriented schemes and liquid funds, at the same rate as equity schemes
  2. All schemes equally
  3. Gold ETFs only
  4. Equity-oriented schemes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Equity-oriented schemes

STT is levied on redemption of units of equity-oriented funds, at a small rate. Redemption of debt schemes does not attract STT.

Question 7

For equity units acquired before 1 February 2018, grandfathering means that:

  1. All gains on those units are fully exempt from tax forever, whenever they are sold
  2. Gains till 31 Jan 2018 are protected
  3. Indexation applies from 2001
  4. The units are taxed at slab rates
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Gains till 31 Jan 2018 are protected

Cost is stepped up to the fair market value on 31 January 2018 (subject to limits), so gains accrued up to that date are not taxed under the long-term equity regime.

Question 8

A non-resident investor redeems equity fund units at a gain. Tax on the gain is generally:

  1. Deducted at source by the fund
  2. Collected only when the investor files a return in the country of residence
  3. Never payable in India
  4. Paid by the distributor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Deducted at source by the fund

For non-residents, the mutual fund deducts tax at source on capital gains at the applicable rates before paying redemption proceeds, subject to any treaty benefit claimed.
