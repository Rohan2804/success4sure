Mutual Fund Scheme Performance: NISM Series V-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Mutual Fund Scheme Performance, from the NISM Series V-D (Mutual Fund – Specialized Investment Fund (SIF) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how to evaluate scheme and strategy performance. Revise two-tier benchmarking, the use of total return indices, rolling versus point-to-point returns, CAGR and XIRR, tracking error and information ratio, and why peer comparison must stay within the same category or strategy type. For long-short SIFs, consider performance relative to an appropriate benchmark and on a risk-adjusted basis, including behaviour in falling markets. Practise CAGR and information ratio calculations.

Mutual Fund Scheme Performance questions

Question 1

For investments made at different dates and amounts, such as a SIP, the most appropriate return measure is:

  1. Simple absolute return calculated from the first instalment date to today
  2. Point-to-point CAGR
  3. Average NAV
  4. XIRR
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. XIRR

XIRR accounts for the timing and size of each cash flow, giving an annualised return for irregular or periodic investments like SIPs.

Question 2

A scheme beat its benchmark by an average 1.5% a year with a tracking error of 5%. Its information ratio is:

  1. 3.33, being the tracking error divided by the excess return earned
  2. 0.30
  3. 7.5
  4. 1.50
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 0.30

Information ratio = excess return / tracking error = 1.5 / 5 = 0.30. It measures how consistently a manager adds value relative to the benchmark.

Question 3

Why should a long-short SIF be judged partly on its behaviour in falling markets?

  1. Because SEBI requires SIFs to show positive returns in every quarter
  2. Because SIF NAVs do not fall
  3. Its aim is to limit downside
  4. Because falling markets have no effect on long-only funds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Its aim is to limit downside

A key promise of long-short strategies is better downside management through short positions. Checking drawdowns in falling markets tests whether this works.

Question 4

₹10 lakh invested grew to ₹16 lakh in 4 years. The CAGR is closest to:

  1. 12.5%
  2. 15.0%, being the 60% gain spread equally over the four years
  3. 16.0%
  4. 10.0%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 12.5%

CAGR = (16/10)^(1/4) − 1 ≈ 12.47%. Dividing the 60% absolute gain by four years gives 15%, which ignores compounding.

Question 5

Under two-tier benchmarking, the second-tier benchmark of a scheme:

  1. Must always be the Nifty 50 Price Return Index, for every category
  2. Reflects the scheme's own style
  3. Is chosen by the investor
  4. Applies only to debt schemes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Reflects the scheme's own style

The first tier reflects the category; the second tier is chosen by the AMC to better match the specific scheme's investment style or strategy.

Question 6

Equity scheme returns are compared with a Total Return Index rather than a price index because:

  1. TRI excludes dividends, making the comparison stricter for the fund
  2. Price indices are not published
  3. TRI includes dividends
  4. TRI is calculated by the AMC
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. TRI includes dividends

Funds receive dividends and include them in NAV, so the fair comparison is with a TRI, which assumes dividends are reinvested.

Question 7

When comparing SIF strategies, a fair comparison would be between:

  1. Strategies of the same type
  2. An equity long-short SIF and a liquid fund, using one-year returns only
  3. An SIF and a bank FD
  4. Any two SIFs regardless of type
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Strategies of the same type

Strategy types differ in risk and objective. Like-for-like comparison within the same strategy type, and against an appropriate benchmark, is meaningful.

Question 8

A high tracking error in an index fund indicates that:

  1. The fund has beaten the index by a large margin in every single period
  2. The fund has low expenses
  3. The index is volatile
  4. Returns deviate a lot from the index
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Returns deviate a lot from the index

Tracking error is the variability of the gap between fund and index returns. A passive fund should keep it low; a high figure points to poor replication.
