Exchange Traded Interest Rate Options: NISM Series V-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Exchange Traded Interest Rate Options, from the NISM Series V-D (Mutual Fund – Specialized Investment Fund (SIF) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers options on interest rate instruments traded on exchanges, along with related OTC products such as caps, floors and collars. Revise how option values respond to rate changes (calls on bond prices gain when rates fall, puts gain when rates rise), intrinsic and time value, break-even levels, and the risk profile of buyers and writers. Understand how option-based protection can be used by debt strategies to limit losses while keeping upside.

Exchange Traded Interest Rate Options questions

Question 1

A call option on a bond futures contract will gain value if:

  1. Interest rates rise, reducing the bond futures price at expiry
  2. Interest rates fall
  3. The option nears expiry
  4. Volatility falls
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Interest rates fall

Lower rates increase bond prices, so a call on bond futures moves into the money. Rising rates benefit puts instead.

Question 2

A put option on bond futures has a strike of 98.00. The futures price at expiry is 96.75. The intrinsic value is:

  1. 0.00, as the put is out of the money at this futures price
  2. 96.75
  3. 2.50
  4. 1.25
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 1.25

Put intrinsic value = strike − underlying = 98.00 − 96.75 = 1.25. The put is in the money because the futures price is below the strike.

Question 3

A buyer pays a premium of 0.80 for a put on bond futures with strike 99.00. The break-even futures price at expiry is:

  1. 98.20
  2. 99.80, adding the premium to the strike price
  3. 99.00
  4. 97.40
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 98.20

A put buyer breaks even when the underlying is at strike minus premium: 99.00 − 0.80 = 98.20. Below that level the position is profitable.

Question 4

A debt fund wants protection against rising yields but wishes to keep gains if yields fall. The suitable option position is to:

  1. Sell call options on bond futures to earn premium and fully remove the risk
  2. Buy calls
  3. Buy puts on bond futures
  4. Sell puts
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Buy puts on bond futures

Long puts gain when bond prices fall (yields rise), protecting the portfolio, while upside from falling yields is retained, less the premium paid.

Question 5

An interest rate floor is most useful for:

  1. Borrowers with floating-rate loans who fear that rates may rise sharply
  2. Equity investors
  3. Exporters
  4. Floating-rate lenders
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Floating-rate lenders

A floor pays out when rates fall below the floor rate, protecting an investor or lender who earns floating-rate interest against falling income.

Question 6

Time value of an option is greatest when the option is:

  1. At the money
  2. Deep in the money, where it behaves almost exactly like the underlying itself
  3. Expired
  4. Deep out of the money
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. At the money

At-the-money options have the most uncertainty about finishing in the money, so they carry the highest time value. Deep in or out of the money options have less.

Question 7

Why must writers of exchange-traded interest rate options post margin?

  1. Because they pay the premium to the buyer at the start of the contract
  2. Because they cannot close positions
  3. Their potential losses are large
  4. Margins are optional
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Their potential losses are large

Writers receive a premium but face potentially large losses if rates move against them. Margins protect the clearing corporation against default.

Question 8

A borrower buys a cap at 8% and sells a floor at 6%. This combination is called a:

  1. Straddle, which profits from large rate moves in either direction
  2. Collar
  3. Swaption
  4. Calendar spread
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Collar

A collar limits the borrowing cost between 6% and 8%. Selling the floor reduces the cost of the cap but gives up benefits below 6%.
