Interest Rate Derivatives: NISM Series V-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Interest Rate Derivatives, from the NISM Series V-D (Mutual Fund – Specialized Investment Fund (SIF) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces interest rate derivatives used to manage or take views on rates: forward rate agreements, interest rate swaps, overnight indexed swaps, interest rate futures and options. Revise who uses each product, how payoffs depend on rate movements, and how these instruments allow debt long-short SIF strategies to take short duration exposure. Understand the difference between paying and receiving fixed in a swap and how an FRA settlement is computed.

Interest Rate Derivatives questions

Question 1

A bank expects to lend money for 6 months starting 3 months from now and fears rates will fall. To lock in its lending rate, it should:

  1. Buy an FRA, which gains when interest rates rise above the agreed rate
  2. Pay fixed in a swap
  3. Sell bond futures
  4. Sell an FRA
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Sell an FRA

A lender gains from higher rates. Selling an FRA (receiving the fixed contract rate) protects against falling rates, as the FRA pays out if the reference rate falls below the contract rate.

Question 2

In an interest rate swap, the party that pays fixed and receives floating benefits when:

  1. Interest rates fall, reducing the floating amount received under the swap
  2. Interest rates rise
  3. Rates stay fixed
  4. The notional is repaid
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Interest rates rise

The fixed payer receives a floating rate. When rates rise, floating receipts increase while fixed payments stay the same, so the swap gains value.

Question 3

A debt long-short SIF wants to benefit if yields rise. Using interest rate futures, it would:

  1. Buy interest rate futures, which rise in price when yields go up
  2. Buy long-duration bonds
  3. Sell interest rate futures
  4. Hold cash only
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Sell interest rate futures

Bond futures prices fall when yields rise, so a short futures position gains. This lets a debt long-short strategy express a view on rising rates.

Question 4

The overnight MIBOR in India is mainly used as the benchmark for:

  1. Overnight indexed swaps
  2. Equity index futures traded on the National Stock Exchange
  3. Home loan EMIs
  4. Gold ETFs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Overnight indexed swaps

OIS contracts in India are commonly benchmarked to overnight MIBOR. They let participants exchange fixed rates for compounded overnight rates.

Question 5

An FRA has a contract rate of 7% on ₹10 crore for 6 months (0.5 year). The reference rate is set at 7.4%. Ignoring discounting, the payment to the FRA buyer is:

  1. ₹4,00,000, using the full year instead of half a year
  2. ₹2,00,000
  3. ₹20,000
  4. ₹74,00,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹2,00,000

Payment = 10 crore × (7.4% − 7%) × 0.5 = ₹2,00,000. The buyer gains when the reference rate exceeds the contract rate.

Question 6

Which of these is NOT an interest rate derivative?

  1. An overnight indexed swap linked to the overnight MIBOR benchmark
  2. A forward rate agreement
  3. Nifty 50 futures
  4. A 10-year G-sec future
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Nifty 50 futures

Nifty futures derive value from an equity index. OIS, FRAs and bond futures all derive value from interest rates or interest-bearing securities.

Question 7

The main purpose of an interest rate cap for a floating-rate borrower is to:

  1. Set a maximum borrowing rate
  2. Guarantee that the borrower always pays exactly the floating market rate
  3. Remove principal risk
  4. Increase the loan size
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Set a maximum borrowing rate

A cap pays the borrower when the reference rate exceeds the cap rate, effectively placing a ceiling on interest cost while keeping the benefit of falling rates.

Question 8

In a plain vanilla interest rate swap, the payments exchanged on each date are normally:

  1. Exchanged in full, including the notional principal, on every payment date
  2. Paid only by the fixed payer
  3. Deferred until maturity
  4. Netted
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Netted

Both legs are in the same currency, so only the net difference between fixed and floating interest is paid by the party owing more.
