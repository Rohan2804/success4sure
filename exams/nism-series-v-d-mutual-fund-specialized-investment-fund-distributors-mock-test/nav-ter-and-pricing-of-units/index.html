NAV, TER and Pricing of Units: NISM Series V-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on NAV, TER and Pricing of Units, from the NISM Series V-D (Mutual Fund – Specialized Investment Fund (SIF) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how NAV is calculated, how expenses are charged through the Total Expense Ratio, and the price at which units are bought and sold. Revise the NAV formula, daily expense accrual, slab-wise TER limits, exit loads, valuation of securities including derivatives used in long-short strategies, segregated portfolios after a credit event, and how IDCW affects NAV. For SIFs, understand that short derivative positions are marked to market daily and flow into NAV. Practise NAV and redemption calculations.

NAV, TER and Pricing of Units questions

Question 1

A long-short strategy has long holdings worth ₹520 crore, a short futures position with a mark-to-market loss of ₹8 crore, cash of ₹15 crore and other liabilities of ₹7 crore. With 50 crore units, the NAV is:

  1. ₹10.70, since futures losses are booked only when the position is finally closed
  2. ₹10.56
  3. ₹10.24
  4. ₹10.40
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹10.40

Net assets = 520 − 8 + 15 − 7 = ₹520 crore. NAV = 520 / 50 = ₹10.40. Daily mark-to-market losses on derivatives reduce NAV immediately; ignoring the ₹8 crore loss gives ₹10.56.

Question 2

Which expense must be borne by the AMC rather than charged to the scheme?

  1. Custodian fees charged for safekeeping of the scheme's securities within the limit
  2. Expenses above the TER limit
  3. Registrar fees
  4. Audit fees
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Expenses above the TER limit

Only permitted expenses up to SEBI's TER limits can be charged to a scheme. Anything beyond the limit is borne by the AMC, its sponsor or trustees.

Question 3

An investor redeems 2,000 units at an NAV of ₹45 with an exit load of 0.5%. The amount paid before tax is:

  1. ₹90,450, because the exit load is added to the NAV paid to the investor
  2. ₹90,000
  3. ₹89,550
  4. ₹89,100
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹89,550

Redemption price = 45 × (1 − 0.005) = ₹44.775. Proceeds = 2,000 × 44.775 = ₹89,550. ₹89,100 applies a 1% load instead.

Question 4

A segregated portfolio is created in a debt scheme when:

  1. A held security suffers a credit event
  2. The scheme's AUM falls below ₹100 crore during any quarter of the financial year
  3. The fund manager resigns
  4. Interest rates rise sharply
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A held security suffers a credit event

Segregation (side-pocketing) separates a downgraded or defaulted security from the main portfolio so new and redeeming investors are treated fairly, and existing investors keep any later recovery.

Question 5

Daily accrual of expenses means that:

  1. Expenses are charged to investors once a year as a separate bill payable by cheque
  2. NAV is published net of expenses
  3. Expenses are deducted from SIP instalments only
  4. Expenses are recovered from distributors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. NAV is published net of expenses

Expenses are accrued every day within the TER, so each published NAV already reflects them. Investors do not pay a separate bill.

Question 6

A scheme declares an IDCW of ₹2 per unit when its cum-IDCW NAV is ₹32. Ignoring market movement, the ex-IDCW NAV is:

  1. ₹32, as IDCW is paid out of the AMC's own profits and not from the NAV
  2. ₹34
  3. ₹30
  4. ₹31
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹30

IDCW is paid out of the scheme's assets, so NAV falls by the amount paid: 32 − 2 = ₹30. The investor's total value (NAV plus cash) is unchanged.

Question 7

Exit load collected by a scheme is:

  1. Credited back to the scheme
  2. Retained by the distributor as an additional commission for the investor's account
  3. Paid to SEBI
  4. Kept by the AMC as profit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Credited back to the scheme

Exit load, net of GST, is credited to the scheme, so it benefits remaining investors and discourages short-term flows that impose costs on them.

Question 8

How are exchange-traded futures held by a scheme valued for NAV purposes?

  1. At the original contract price until expiry, without any daily adjustment
  2. At the face value of the underlying
  3. At the fund manager's estimate
  4. At the exchange settlement price
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. At the exchange settlement price

Exchange-traded derivatives are valued at the daily settlement or closing price published by the exchange, so gains and losses are reflected in NAV every day.
