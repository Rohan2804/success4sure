Interest Rates and Fixed Income Market: NISM Series V-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Interest Rates and Fixed Income Market, from the NISM Series V-D (Mutual Fund – Specialized Investment Fund (SIF) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the fixed income basics needed for debt long-short strategies: types of interest rates, compounding conventions, bond pricing, yield to maturity, the yield curve, and price sensitivity measured by duration and convexity. Revise money market instruments, government securities, and the inverse link between yields and prices. Practise effective annual rate, bond price and duration calculations, and understand why long-maturity, low-coupon bonds are most sensitive to rate changes.

Interest Rates and Fixed Income Market questions

Question 1

A nominal rate of 8% a year compounded quarterly is equivalent to an effective annual rate of about:

  1. 8.00%, as compounding frequency does not change the annual rate
  2. 8.16%
  3. 8.24%
  4. 8.30%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 8.24%

Effective rate = (1 + 0.08/4)^4 − 1 = 1.02^4 − 1 = 8.24%. More frequent compounding raises the effective rate above the nominal rate.

Question 2

A 2-year bond with face value ₹100 pays a 10% annual coupon. If the yield is 8%, its price is closest to:

  1. ₹103.57
  2. ₹100.00, because the bond always trades at face value until it matures
  3. ₹96.53
  4. ₹104.00
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹103.57

Price = 10/1.08 + 110/1.08² = 9.26 + 94.31 = ₹103.57. The bond trades at a premium because its coupon exceeds the market yield.

Question 3

For the same bond (2 years, 10% annual coupon, 8% yield), the Macaulay duration is about:

  1. 2.00 years, because duration always equals the remaining maturity
  2. 1.91 years
  3. 1.50 years
  4. 1.09 years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 1.91 years

Duration = (1 × 9.26 + 2 × 94.31) / 103.57 ≈ 1.91 years. For coupon bonds, duration is below maturity; it equals maturity only for zero-coupon bonds.

Question 4

Which bond's price is most sensitive to a change in yields?

  1. A 1-year Treasury bill purchased at the latest weekly auction
  2. 3-year high-coupon bond
  3. Floating rate bond
  4. 30-year low-coupon bond
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 30-year low-coupon bond

Long maturity and low coupons both increase duration. A 30-year low-coupon bond therefore has the largest price change for a given yield change.

Question 5

A normal (upward-sloping) yield curve indicates that:

  1. Long-term yields exceed short-term yields
  2. Short-term yields are higher than long-term yields across all maturities
  3. All yields are equal
  4. Bonds have no credit risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Long-term yields exceed short-term yields

Investors usually demand higher yields for lending longer, so the normal curve slopes upward. An inverted curve shows the opposite.

Question 6

Positive convexity of a bond means that:

  1. Price falls faster when yields fall than it rises when yields rise
  2. Price gains exceed losses for equal yield moves
  3. The bond has no duration
  4. The bond pays no coupon
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Price gains exceed losses for equal yield moves

Convexity makes the price–yield curve bowed. For an equal fall and rise in yield, the gain is larger than the loss, which benefits holders.

Question 7

Government of India dated securities are issued by:

  1. SEBI, through the stock exchanges' primary market bidding platforms
  2. Commercial banks
  3. AMFI
  4. RBI on behalf of the Government
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. RBI on behalf of the Government

RBI manages public debt and issues G-secs through auctions on behalf of the Government of India.

Question 8

When market interest rates fall, the price of an existing fixed-coupon bond:

  1. Falls, because its coupon now looks unattractive compared with new bonds
  2. Stays the same
  3. Rises
  4. Becomes zero
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Rises

Existing bonds with higher coupons become more valuable when new yields fall, so their prices rise. Prices and yields move inversely.
