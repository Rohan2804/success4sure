Legal and Regulatory Framework: NISM Series V-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Legal and Regulatory Framework, from the NISM Series V-D (Mutual Fund – Specialized Investment Fund (SIF) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers regulations that apply to mutual funds and SIFs: investment restrictions, borrowing limits, advertisement rules, SIF-specific norms on minimum investment and distributor eligibility, and investor grievance redressal. Revise how SIFs must be branded separately, who may distribute them (NISM V-D certified distributors), the accredited investor exemption, the role of AMFI codes, and how complaints move from the AMC to SCORES and online dispute resolution. Expect scenario questions on what a distributor may or may not do.

Legal and Regulatory Framework questions

Question 1

An investor's SIF holding falls below ₹10 lakh only because the market fell. Under SEBI's framework, this is treated as:

  1. A passive breach, not requiring exit
  2. A violation that requires the AMC to redeem all units immediately at the prevailing NAV
  3. A reason to charge a penalty
  4. Grounds to freeze the folio
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A passive breach, not requiring exit

The minimum investment is tested on investment amount. A fall in value due to market movement is a passive breach and does not force redemption.

Question 2

Which body issues the certification that a distributor must pass before selling SIF strategies?

  1. AMFI, through a separate SIF distributor licence that replaces the ARN
  2. RBI
  3. NISM
  4. CDSL
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. NISM

NISM conducts the Series V-D certification for SIF distributors. Distributors also hold an ARN from AMFI, but the specific certification comes from NISM.

Question 3

An AMC's SIF advertisement uses the same brand and look as its regular mutual fund schemes. Under SEBI norms this is:

  1. Allowed, as long as the SIF has a separate fund manager
  2. Allowed for accredited investors only
  3. Required for brand consistency
  4. Not allowed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Not allowed

SIFs must carry distinct branding so investors do not confuse them with regular mutual fund schemes that have lower minimums and different risks.

Question 4

SEBI's power to regulate mutual funds and SIFs flows from the:

  1. Banking Regulation Act, 1949
  2. SEBI Act, 1992
  3. Indian Contract Act, 1872, and the Indian Trusts Act read together
  4. RBI Act, 1934
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. SEBI Act, 1992

The SEBI Act empowers SEBI to regulate collective investment vehicles. The SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996 and SIF circulars are issued under it.

Question 5

A mutual fund scheme may borrow only to meet temporary liquidity needs. The maximum borrowing is:

  1. 50% of net assets, for up to one year, to buy additional securities
  2. Unlimited, with trustee approval
  3. 20% of net assets, up to 6 months
  4. 10% of net assets for any purpose
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 20% of net assets, up to 6 months

Borrowing is allowed only for redemptions or IDCW payouts, up to 20% of net assets and for a maximum of six months. It cannot be used to leverage the portfolio.

Question 6

A distributor tells a client that an SIF's long-short approach 'makes losses impossible'. This statement is:

  1. Correct, since shorts offset all long positions under every market condition
  2. Correct for hybrid strategies only
  3. Acceptable if the client is wealthy
  4. Misleading and a breach of conduct
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Misleading and a breach of conduct

Long-short strategies can still lose money, for example when both long and short positions move against the manager. Promising no losses is misrepresentation.

Question 7

If an investor's grievance is not resolved by the AMC, the next escalation is usually:

  1. Filing a police complaint against the distributor's branch office
  2. SEBI's SCORES platform
  3. The Banking Ombudsman
  4. AMFI's board of directors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. SEBI's SCORES platform

Investors first approach the AMC; if unresolved, they can file on SCORES. Disputes still pending can be taken to the online dispute resolution mechanism.

Question 8

All schemes of a mutual fund together may not hold more than what share of the voting capital of any single company?

  1. 10%
  2. 25% of the paid-up capital of each investee company
  3. 5%
  4. 51%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 10%

The 10% fund-level cap on voting rights prevents mutual funds from gaining control of investee companies and limits concentration.
