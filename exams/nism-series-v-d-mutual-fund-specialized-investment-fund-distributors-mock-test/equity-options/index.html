Equity Options: NISM Series V-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Equity Options, from the NISM Series V-D (Mutual Fund – Specialized Investment Fund (SIF) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers equity options: calls and puts, buyers and writers, moneyness, intrinsic and time value, payoff at expiry, and factors that influence premiums (underlying price, strike, time, volatility, interest rates and dividends). Revise the option Greeks at a basic level (delta, gamma, theta, vega), put-call parity, and the difference between European and American styles. Practise payoff and break-even calculations, since they appear frequently.

Equity Options questions

Question 1

A put option with strike ₹800 is bought for a premium of ₹25. At expiry the stock is at ₹740. The buyer's net profit per share is:

  1. ₹35
  2. ₹60, since the premium was already paid earlier and is a sunk cost
  3. ₹25
  4. ₹85
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹35

Payoff = 800 − 740 = ₹60. Net profit = 60 − 25 = ₹35 per share. ₹85 wrongly adds the premium.

Question 2

Which factor increases the premium of both call and put options, other things equal?

  1. A rise in the underlying stock price, which raises both calls and puts together
  2. Shorter time to expiry
  3. Higher volatility
  4. Lower interest rates
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Higher volatility

Higher volatility raises the chance of large moves, which benefits option holders on both sides. A price rise increases calls but reduces puts.

Question 3

An option's delta of 0.6 means that its premium will change by about:

  1. ₹6 for every ₹1 move in the underlying, as delta is quoted in tens
  2. ₹1 for every ₹0.6 move in time
  3. Nothing
  4. ₹0.60 for a ₹1 move
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹0.60 for a ₹1 move

Delta measures sensitivity to the underlying price. A delta of 0.6 implies about ₹0.60 change in premium for each ₹1 change in the underlying.

Question 4

Theta of an option measures the change in premium due to:

  1. Changes in implied volatility of the underlying over the life of the option
  2. Passage of time
  3. Changes in interest rates
  4. Changes in strike price
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Passage of time

Theta measures time decay. Options lose time value as expiry approaches, which hurts buyers and benefits writers, all else equal.

Question 5

A stock trades at ₹1,000. A call with strike ₹1,000 costs ₹30. Over the option's life, the risk-free return is 2%. By put-call parity, a put with the same strike should cost about:

  1. ₹30.00, because ATM calls and puts always have the same price
  2. ₹49.61
  3. ₹10.39
  4. ₹19.61
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹10.39

Parity: C + PV(K) = P + S. PV(K) = 1,000 / 1.02 = 980.39. P = 30 + 980.39 − 1,000 = ₹10.39.

Question 6

Exercise of a Nifty option bought on an Indian exchange is possible:

  1. On any trading day up to and including the expiry date, at the holder's choice
  2. Only on the purchase date
  3. Never
  4. Only at expiry
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Only at expiry

European options may be exercised only at expiry. All exchange-traded equity options in India are European style.

Question 7

A call with strike ₹500 when the stock is at ₹470 is:

  1. In the money, because the stock is close to the strike price of the option
  2. Out of the money
  3. At the money
  4. Deep in the money
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Out of the money

A call is out of the money when the underlying price is below the strike. Exercising it now would mean paying ₹500 for a stock worth ₹470.

Question 8

The maximum loss for the writer of a naked call option is:

  1. Unlimited
  2. Limited to the premium received at the time of writing the call
  3. Equal to the strike price
  4. Zero
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Unlimited

If the stock rises without limit, the call writer must deliver at the strike, facing theoretically unlimited loss. Only the premium is the maximum gain.
