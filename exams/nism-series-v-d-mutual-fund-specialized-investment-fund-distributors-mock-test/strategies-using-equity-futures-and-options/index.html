Strategies Using Equity Futures and Options: NISM Series V-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Strategies Using Equity Futures and Options, from the NISM Series V-D (Mutual Fund – Specialized Investment Fund (SIF) Distributors) syllabus.

What this chapter covers

This chapter applies futures and options to hedging, speculation and arbitrage, which mirrors what long-short SIF strategies do. Revise beta-based hedging with index futures, protective puts, covered calls, bull and bear spreads, straddles and strangles, and calendar spreads. For each strategy, know the market view, maximum profit, maximum loss and break-even. Practise computing the number of futures contracts to hedge a portfolio and the payoff of spread strategies.

Strategies Using Equity Futures and Options questions

Question 1

A ₹1.5 crore portfolio has a beta of 1.1. Each index futures contract is worth ₹15 lakh. To fully hedge the portfolio, the manager should sell:

  1. 10 contracts, ignoring beta, since the portfolio is diversified
  2. 11 contracts
  3. 15 contracts
  4. 9 contracts
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 11 contracts

Contracts = portfolio value × beta / contract value = 1.5 crore × 1.1 / 15 lakh = 11. Ignoring beta gives 10, leaving the higher-beta portfolio under-hedged.

Question 2

A trader buys a ₹100 strike call for ₹6 and sells a ₹110 strike call for ₹2 on the same stock and expiry. The maximum profit is:

  1. ₹10, the full difference between the two strike prices
  2. ₹4
  3. ₹8
  4. ₹6
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹6

Net cost = 6 − 2 = ₹4. Maximum profit = strike difference − net cost = 10 − 4 = ₹6, reached when the stock is at or above ₹110 at expiry.

Question 3

An investor holds shares and sells call options on them to earn extra income in a flat market. This strategy is a:

  1. Covered call
  2. Protective put, which limits downside by buying the right to sell
  3. Long straddle
  4. Bear put spread
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Covered call

A covered call earns premium against existing shares. It caps upside above the strike but adds income when prices stay range-bound.

Question 4

An investor wants to keep a stock but protect against a sharp fall, while retaining upside. The suitable strategy is to:

  1. Sell a call on the stock, which earns premium and fully removes downside risk
  2. Sell a put
  3. Buy a put
  4. Buy more stock
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Buy a put

A protective put acts like insurance: losses below the strike are offset by the put, while gains above remain, net of the premium paid.

Question 5

A trader expects a stock to stay in a narrow range until expiry and is willing to take large risk if it moves sharply. Which strategy profits most from this view?

  1. Long strangle, buying an out-of-the-money call and an out-of-the-money put
  2. Long call
  3. Long futures
  4. Short straddle
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Short straddle

A short straddle (selling an ATM call and put) earns maximum premium if the stock stays near the strike, but has large losses if it moves sharply either way.

Question 6

A long-short manager wants to profit from expected outperformance of bank stocks over IT stocks, with little exposure to the overall market. A suitable approach is to:

  1. Buy bank futures and sell IT futures
  2. Buy both bank and IT futures in equal value to double the exposure to the market
  3. Sell Nifty futures only
  4. Buy put options on both sectors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Buy bank futures and sell IT futures

A pair trade goes long the expected outperformer and short the expected underperformer in similar amounts, so market moves largely cancel and relative performance drives returns.

Question 7

In a bear put spread, the trader:

  1. Buys a lower-strike call and sells a higher-strike call to profit from a rising market
  2. Sells two puts
  3. Buys a higher-strike put and sells a lower-strike put
  4. Buys a call and a put at the same strike
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Buys a higher-strike put and sells a lower-strike put

A bear put spread profits from a moderate fall. Selling the lower-strike put reduces cost but caps the gain at the strike difference minus net premium.

Question 8

A trader buys an ₹800 call for ₹15 and an ₹800 put for ₹12. At expiry, the stock must be beyond which levels for the trade to make a profit?

  1. Above ₹815 or below ₹788, using only each option's own premium
  2. Above ₹827 or below ₹773
  3. Between ₹773 and ₹827
  4. Exactly at ₹800
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Above ₹827 or below ₹773

A long straddle costs ₹27 in total. It breaks even at 800 + 27 = ₹827 and 800 − 27 = ₹773, and profits only if the price moves beyond these levels.
