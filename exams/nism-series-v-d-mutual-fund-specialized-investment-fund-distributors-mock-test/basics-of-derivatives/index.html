Basics of Derivatives: NISM Series V-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Basics of Derivatives, from the NISM Series V-D (Mutual Fund – Specialized Investment Fund (SIF) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces derivatives as contracts whose value depends on an underlying asset, index or rate. Revise the main types (forwards, futures, options and swaps), differences between OTC and exchange-traded contracts, the role of hedgers, speculators and arbitrageurs, leverage, and counterparty risk. Since SIF strategies use derivatives for hedging and short exposure, understand how a short futures position profits from a fall and how leverage magnifies both gains and losses.

Basics of Derivatives questions

Question 1

A trader who takes a futures position to profit from an expected price move, without any underlying exposure, is a:

  1. Hedger, because every futures position automatically reduces existing risk
  2. Speculator
  3. Arbitrageur
  4. Market regulator
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Speculator

Speculators take on risk to profit from price views. Hedgers reduce existing risk, and arbitrageurs exploit price differences across markets.

Question 2

Leverage in futures arises because:

  1. The full contract value must be paid on the trade date in cash
  2. Futures never lose value
  3. Futures are guaranteed by SEBI
  4. Only a margin is paid
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Only a margin is paid

A futures position needs only an initial margin, a fraction of the contract value. Small price moves therefore create large percentage gains or losses on the margin.

Question 3

A forward contract differs from a futures contract mainly because a forward is:

  1. Customised and traded OTC
  2. Standardised, exchange-traded and settled daily through the clearing corporation
  3. Always cash-settled
  4. Free from all risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Customised and traded OTC

Forwards are private bilateral agreements with terms set by the parties and carry counterparty risk. Futures are standardised, exchange-traded and marked to market daily.

Question 4

Counterparty risk in exchange-traded futures is largely removed by:

  1. Allowing each buyer to pick a seller they personally know and trust
  2. Avoiding margins
  3. The clearing corporation acting as central counterparty
  4. Daily changes in lot size
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The clearing corporation acting as central counterparty

Through novation, the clearing corporation becomes buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer, backed by margins and default funds.

Question 5

Which of the following is an example of an arbitrage?

  1. Buying call options because one expects a sharp rally in a stock
  2. Holding shares for ten years
  3. Selling futures to hedge a portfolio
  4. Buying shares in cash and selling overpriced futures
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Buying shares in cash and selling overpriced futures

Arbitrage locks in a nearly riskless profit from mispricing, such as buying in the cash market and selling relatively overpriced futures of the same stock.

Question 6

An SIF fund manager holds a stock and sells futures on the same stock in equal quantity. The combined position has:

  1. Near-zero net exposure to that stock
  2. Double the exposure to the stock's price movements, both up and down
  3. Unlimited upside
  4. No margin requirement
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Near-zero net exposure to that stock

The short futures offset the long stock, so price moves largely cancel out. Such hedged positions do not count towards the unhedged short limit.

Question 7

Which is TRUE of an option buyer compared with an option seller?

  1. The buyer must post large margins because the buyer's loss is unlimited
  2. The seller has limited loss
  3. The buyer's loss is limited to the premium
  4. Both have identical risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The buyer's loss is limited to the premium

An option buyer pays a premium and can lose at most that amount. The seller receives the premium but faces potentially large losses, hence margins.

Question 8

A swap is best described as an agreement to:

  1. Buy a share at a fixed price on a stock exchange at the end of the trading day
  2. Exchange cash flows in future
  3. Insure a portfolio against all losses
  4. Lend money without interest
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Exchange cash flows in future

In a swap, two parties exchange streams of cash flows, such as fixed for floating interest, according to agreed terms over a period.
