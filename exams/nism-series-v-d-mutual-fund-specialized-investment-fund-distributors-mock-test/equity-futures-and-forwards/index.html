Equity Futures and Forwards: NISM Series V-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Equity Futures and Forwards, from the NISM Series V-D (Mutual Fund – Specialized Investment Fund (SIF) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers equity futures and forwards: contract features, pricing by cost of carry, basis and convergence, daily mark-to-market, margins, and settlement. Revise the fair value formula with dividends, how basis behaves as expiry approaches, contango and backwardation, and calculation of MTM profit or loss. These ideas underpin hedging and short positions in long-short SIF strategies, so practise numerical examples carefully.

Equity Futures and Forwards questions

Question 1

A stock trades at ₹2,000. The risk-free rate is 7% a year and a dividend of ₹10 is expected before expiry in 3 months. Using simple interest, the fair futures price is:

  1. ₹2,035, since dividends do not affect futures prices
  2. ₹2,140
  3. ₹1,965
  4. ₹2,025
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹2,025

Fair value = 2,000 × (1 + 0.07 × 0.25) − 10 = 2,035 − 10 = ₹2,025. Expected dividends reduce the cost of carry because the futures holder does not receive them.

Question 2

Basis is defined as spot price minus futures price. As expiry approaches, basis generally:

  1. Widens steadily, reaching its maximum on the expiry day itself
  2. Converges to zero
  3. Stays constant
  4. Becomes equal to the dividend
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Converges to zero

Futures and spot prices converge at expiry because the futures then represent immediate delivery. Any gap would be arbitraged away.

Question 3

When futures prices are above spot prices, the market is said to be in:

  1. Backwardation, which reflects a positive cost of carry in the futures
  2. Equilibrium
  3. Contango
  4. Default
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Contango

Contango means futures trade above spot, typical when financing cost exceeds income from the asset. Backwardation is futures below spot.

Question 4

A trader sells 2 lots of stock futures (lot size 500) at ₹1,200. The settlement price that day is ₹1,185. The MTM result is:

  1. Gain of ₹15,000
  2. Loss of ₹15,000, because the price has moved down
  3. Gain of ₹7,500
  4. Loss of ₹7,500
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Gain of ₹15,000

Short position gains when price falls: 2 × 500 × (1,200 − 1,185) = ₹15,000. ₹7,500 counts only one lot.

Question 5

Daily mark-to-market settlement in futures ensures that:

  1. Profits are paid only at expiry, along with the final settlement
  2. Gains and losses are settled daily
  3. The futures price never changes
  4. No margin is required
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Gains and losses are settled daily

MTM settles each day's gain or loss in cash, preventing large unpaid losses from building up and reducing default risk.

Question 6

If the futures price is well above its fair value, an arbitrageur can earn a profit by:

  1. Selling spot and buying futures, then waiting for the next dividend
  2. Buying both spot and futures
  3. Buying spot and selling futures
  4. Selling both
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Buying spot and selling futures

Cash-and-carry: buy the underlying, sell the overpriced futures and hold till expiry; convergence locks in the excess over carry cost.

Question 7

Which statement about stock futures in India is correct?

  1. They are physically settled at expiry
  2. They are always cash-settled with no delivery of shares in any case
  3. They have no lot size
  4. They trade only OTC
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. They are physically settled at expiry

Stock futures and stock options in India are physically settled at expiry, so open positions result in delivery of shares. Index derivatives are cash-settled.

Question 8

An investor fears a short-term fall in a stock he holds but does not want to sell it. Using futures, he can:

  1. Buy more futures on the same stock to average his cost
  2. Buy index call options
  3. Do nothing
  4. Sell futures on that stock
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Sell futures on that stock

Selling futures creates an offsetting short position: if the stock falls, gains on the futures offset losses on the holding.
