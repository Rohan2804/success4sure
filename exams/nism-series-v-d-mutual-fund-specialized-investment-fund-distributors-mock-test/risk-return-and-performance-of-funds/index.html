Risk, Return and Performance of Funds: NISM Series V-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Risk, Return and Performance of Funds, from the NISM Series V-D (Mutual Fund – Specialized Investment Fund (SIF) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how to measure risk and return for mutual funds and long-short strategies. Revise standard deviation, beta, downside deviation and drawdown, and risk-adjusted measures such as Sharpe, Treynor, Sortino and alpha. For long-short strategies, understand net and gross exposure, how a lower net exposure reduces market sensitivity, and the risk that both long and short sides lose together. Also cover interest rate, credit and liquidity risk in debt portfolios. Expect calculations on Sharpe, Treynor and exposure.

Risk, Return and Performance of Funds questions

Question 1

A long-short strategy is 90% long and 30% short. Its net market exposure is:

  1. 120%, being the total of the long and the short positions added together
  2. 90%
  3. 60%
  4. 30%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 60%

Net exposure = long − short = 90% − 30% = 60%. Gross exposure (long + short) is 120%, which indicates total positions rather than market sensitivity.

Question 2

Compared with a fully invested long-only equity fund, a long-short fund with lower net exposure will typically have:

  1. Lower sensitivity to market moves
  2. Higher beta and larger swings than the market in both directions
  3. No risk of any loss
  4. Zero tracking error
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Lower sensitivity to market moves

Lower net exposure means less market (beta) risk. Losses are still possible if stock selection on either side goes wrong.

Question 3

Fund P returned 16% with standard deviation 20%; Fund Q returned 12% with standard deviation 10%. The risk-free rate is 6%. Which has the better Sharpe ratio?

  1. Fund P, because it delivered the higher absolute return over the year
  2. Fund Q
  3. Both are equal
  4. Cannot be computed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Fund Q

Sharpe P = (16 − 6) / 20 = 0.50. Sharpe Q = (12 − 6) / 10 = 0.60. Fund Q earns more excess return per unit of total risk.

Question 4

A long-short manager buys stock A and shorts stock B. The market rises, A falls and B rises. The likely result is:

  1. Gains on both sides, since a long-short strategy is hedged against all market moves
  2. Gain on A only
  3. No change in NAV
  4. Losses on both sides
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Losses on both sides

The long position in A loses as A falls, and the short in B loses as B rises. Long-short strategies can lose on both legs when stock selection is wrong.

Question 5

Which risk measure focuses only on returns below a minimum acceptable level?

  1. Downside deviation
  2. Standard deviation, which treats upside and downside moves in the same way
  3. Beta
  4. R-squared
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Downside deviation

Downside deviation measures volatility of returns below a target. It is used in the Sortino ratio, which penalises only harmful volatility.

Question 6

A debt portfolio has a modified duration of 3. If yields rise by 0.5%, its value will change by approximately:

  1. +1.5%, as rising yields increase the coupon income of the portfolio
  2. −1.5%
  3. −3.0%
  4. −0.5%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. −1.5%

Price change ≈ −modified duration × change in yield = −3 × 0.5% = −1.5%. Prices fall when yields rise.

Question 7

A fund holding thinly traded securities faces heavy redemptions. The danger it faces is best described as:

  1. Sponsor risk, i.e. the sponsor being unable to support the AMC financially
  2. Interest rate risk
  3. Tracking risk
  4. Liquidity risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Liquidity risk

Illiquid holdings may have to be sold at a discount to meet redemptions, which hurts remaining investors. Notice periods in SIFs help manage this.

Question 8

A fund's beta is 0.6 relative to Nifty. If Nifty falls 10%, the fund is expected to fall about:

  1. 16%, since beta adds to the market fall instead of scaling it
  2. 10%
  3. 6%
  4. 0.6%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 6%

Expected move = beta × market move = 0.6 × −10% = −6%. A beta below 1 indicates lower sensitivity to market movements.
