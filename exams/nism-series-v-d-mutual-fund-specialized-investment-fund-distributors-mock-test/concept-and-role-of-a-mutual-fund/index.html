Concept and Role of a Mutual Fund: NISM Series V-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Concept and Role of a Mutual Fund, from the NISM Series V-D (Mutual Fund – Specialized Investment Fund (SIF) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the mutual fund concept and SEBI's scheme categories, and introduces the Specialized Investment Fund (SIF) as a separate product within the mutual fund framework. Revise the SIF investment strategies (equity long-short, equity ex-top 100 long-short, sector rotation long-short, debt long-short, sectoral debt long-short, active asset allocator long-short and hybrid long-short) with their key allocation rules, the minimum investment threshold, short exposure limits and the five-level risk band. Also revise regular mutual fund categories that SIFs are compared with.

Concept and Role of a Mutual Fund questions

Question 1

An SIF's Equity Ex-Top 100 Long-Short strategy must invest at least 65% of its assets in:

  1. Large cap stocks ranked 1 to 100 by full market capitalisation
  2. Government securities
  3. Equity outside the top 100 stocks
  4. Units of other SIFs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Equity outside the top 100 stocks

As its name suggests, this strategy focuses on companies outside the top 100 by market capitalisation, with at least 65% in such equities, while using limited short positions through derivatives.

Question 2

Under the SIF framework, a Sector Rotation Long-Short strategy invests at least 80% of its assets in:

  1. Equity of at most four sectors
  2. Equity spread across at least ten sectors in equal weights
  3. Debt of a single sector
  4. Any asset class freely
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Equity of at most four sectors

Sector rotation SIFs concentrate at least 80% in equity and equity-related instruments of a maximum of four sectors, rotating among them, with limited short exposure.

Question 3

A Hybrid Long-Short SIF must hold at least:

  1. 65% in equity and the rest in gold
  2. 25% each in equity and in debt
  3. 80% in debt and money market instruments, with no equity at all
  4. 50% in REITs and InvITs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 25% each in equity and in debt

The hybrid long-short strategy invests at least 25% in equity and at least 25% in debt, with flexibility for the rest and limited unhedged shorts.

Question 4

How many risk levels does the SIF risk-band use?

  1. Six, the same as the mutual fund riskometer used for regular schemes
  2. Three
  3. Ten
  4. Five
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Five

SIF strategies carry a risk-band with five levels (Level 1 lowest to Level 5 highest), distinct from the six-level riskometer used for regular mutual fund schemes.

Question 5

Which statement about short positions in an SIF is correct?

  1. Unhedged shorts via derivatives are capped
  2. SIFs may short-sell any amount of stock without limits through the cash market
  3. SIFs cannot use derivatives at all
  4. Short positions are allowed only in debt SIFs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Unhedged shorts via derivatives are capped

SIFs may take unhedged short exposure through derivatives within a set cap (25% of net assets). Derivatives for hedging and rebalancing are permitted in addition.

Question 6

An AMC already offers one Equity Long-Short SIF strategy. It wants to launch a second Equity Long-Short strategy. Under SEBI norms, this is:

  1. Permitted, provided both strategies have a different fund manager and benchmark
  2. Not permitted
  3. Permitted only for accredited investors
  4. Mandatory once AUM crosses ₹1,000 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Not permitted

As with mutual fund categories, an AMC may offer only one strategy in each SIF strategy type, so investors are not confused by near-identical offerings.

Question 7

The key feature that distinguishes an SIF from a regular equity mutual fund is that an SIF:

  1. Guarantees a minimum return to investors who stay invested for three years or more
  2. Has no minimum investment
  3. Is not regulated by SEBI
  4. Can take limited short positions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Can take limited short positions

SIFs bridge mutual funds and PMS/AIFs by allowing long-short strategies with a higher minimum ticket. Regular mutual funds cannot take unhedged short positions.

Question 8

A Debt Long-Short SIF is most likely to use which instruments for its short exposure?

  1. Short sale of equity shares in the cash market
  2. Gold futures only
  3. Exchange-traded interest rate derivatives
  4. Bank fixed deposits placed with the AMC's sponsor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Exchange-traded interest rate derivatives

Debt long-short strategies take long positions in debt instruments and short exposure through interest rate derivatives, aiming to benefit from views on rates and spreads.
