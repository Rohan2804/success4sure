Investment Landscape: NISM Series V-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Investment Landscape, from the NISM Series V-D (Mutual Fund – Specialized Investment Fund (SIF) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter sets the context for advising investors on sophisticated products. It covers financial goals, risk capacity versus risk appetite, asset classes and their behaviour, asset allocation and rebalancing, and behavioural biases. For SIF distribution, focus on judging whether an investor has the wealth, horizon and understanding to handle long-short strategies. Practise calculations on rebalancing, real returns and compounding, and know how correlation between assets affects portfolio risk.

Investment Landscape questions

Question 1

Two assets have a correlation of −0.3. Combining them in a portfolio will most likely:

  1. Double the portfolio's expected return
  2. Reduce overall portfolio volatility
  3. Remove all market risk from the portfolio, whatever the weights chosen
  4. Increase volatility compared with each asset alone
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Reduce overall portfolio volatility

Assets that do not move together offset part of each other's fluctuations, so portfolio volatility falls below the weighted average. Negative correlation does not remove all risk or double returns.

Question 2

An investor's risk profile questionnaire shows high willingness to take risk, but she has little savings and needs the money in a year. A distributor should give more weight to her:

  1. High stated risk appetite and her strong wish to earn equity-like returns
  2. Preference for recent top performers
  3. Interest in new fund offers
  4. Limited risk capacity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Limited risk capacity

Risk capacity, the ability to absorb losses, limits what is suitable regardless of willingness. A short horizon and small savings mean she cannot afford large losses.

Question 3

A portfolio drifts from its target 50:50 equity–debt mix to 65:35 after a rally. Restoring the 50:50 mix by selling equity is called:

  1. Rebalancing
  2. Market timing based on a forecast of the next rally
  3. Averaging down
  4. Tactical overweighting
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Rebalancing

Rebalancing brings the portfolio back to its strategic mix, which controls risk and enforces 'sell high, buy low' discipline. It is rule-based rather than forecast-based.

Question 4

An investor feels the pain of a ₹1 lakh loss far more than the pleasure of a ₹1 lakh gain and therefore refuses to sell losing positions. This reflects:

  1. Overconfidence in one's own stock-picking ability and research
  2. Anchoring
  3. Loss aversion
  4. Home bias
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Loss aversion

Loss aversion means losses hurt more than equal gains please. It often leads investors to hold losers too long in the hope of breaking even.

Question 5

Which investor is most likely to need a high allocation to liquid, low-risk assets?

  1. A 30-year-old saving for retirement through a monthly SIP over thirty years
  2. One with a 15-year goal and stable income
  3. One with surplus wealth and no near-term needs
  4. One with a large expense due in three months
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. One with a large expense due in three months

Money needed soon should not be exposed to market swings. Long horizons and stable incomes allow more equity; near-term needs call for liquidity and safety.

Question 6

₹10,00,000 grows at 12% a year for 3 years, compounded annually. The ending value is closest to:

  1. ₹14,04,928
  2. ₹13,60,000
  3. ₹14,40,000
  4. ₹13,12,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹14,04,928

Value = 10,00,000 × 1.12³ = ₹14,04,928. ₹13,60,000 is simple interest for three years, which ignores compounding.

Question 7

Inflation is 6% and a debt investment yields 7% before tax. For an investor in the 30% tax slab, the approximate real post-tax return is:

  1. +1.0%
  2. +4.9%, since tax applies only to the real part of the return
  3. −1.1%
  4. +0.1%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. −1.1%

Post-tax yield = 7% × (1 − 0.30) = 4.9%. Subtracting 6% inflation gives about −1.1%. Taxes apply to the full nominal return, which is why high-slab investors can lose purchasing power.

Question 8

Which asset class typically provides regular income with lower volatility than equity?

  1. Small cap equity
  2. High-quality debt
  3. Sectoral equity funds investing in a single cyclical sector such as metals
  4. Equity derivatives
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. High-quality debt

High-quality bonds pay regular interest and their prices move far less than equities. Small caps, sector funds and derivatives carry much higher volatility.
