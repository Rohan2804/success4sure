Investor Services: NISM Series V-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Investor Services, from the NISM Series V-D (Mutual Fund – Specialized Investment Fund (SIF) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the investor's journey: KYC, applying for units, cut-off times and applicable NAV, systematic plans, redemptions, statements, nomination and transmission. For SIFs, revise how the minimum investment is checked at PAN level across strategies of an AMC, how systematic plans work once the threshold is met, and how redemption frequency and notice periods apply. For regular schemes, know cut-off rules, payment timelines and processes for minors, NRIs and change of details.

Investor Services questions

Question 1

An investor already holds ₹12 lakh across SIF strategies of an AMC and wants to invest ₹1 lakh in another strategy of the same AMC. Under the minimum investment rule:

  1. Not allowed, since every new strategy needs a separate ₹10 lakh investment
  2. This is allowed
  3. Allowed only through a lumpsum of ₹5 lakh
  4. Allowed only for accredited investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. This is allowed

The ₹10 lakh minimum is checked at the investor's PAN level across all SIF strategies of the AMC. Having met it, the investor can add smaller amounts.

Question 2

For an equity scheme, a purchase application received at 2:45 pm on Wednesday, with funds reaching the scheme's account at 4 pm the same day, gets:

  1. Wednesday's NAV, because the application itself was received before the 3 pm cut-off
  2. Tuesday's NAV
  3. Friday's NAV
  4. Thursday's NAV
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Thursday's NAV

For equity schemes, the applicable NAV depends on when funds are available for use. Funds realised after the 3 pm cut-off get the next business day's NAV.

Question 3

Which document is mandatory for a resident individual's KYC for mutual fund investments above micro limits?

  1. PAN
  2. Passport, as the only acceptable identity and address proof for KYC
  3. Voter ID only
  4. Driving licence only
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. PAN

PAN is mandatory for all mutual fund investments except PAN-exempt micro investments. Other documents serve as proof of address.

Question 4

SIP, STP and SWP facilities in SIF strategies can be used by an investor:

  1. Freely from the first instalment, without any minimum amount at all
  2. Only in debt strategies
  3. After the minimum is met
  4. Never
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. After the minimum is met

SIFs allow systematic plans, but the investor must first meet the ₹10 lakh threshold at PAN level (unless exempt as an accredited investor).

Question 5

When the sole holder of a folio dies without a nominee, the legal heir will typically need to submit:

  1. Only a self-attested copy of the heir's PAN and nothing else
  2. A fresh application for allotment
  3. A redemption request signed by a distributor
  4. Legal documents proving succession
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Legal documents proving succession

Without a nominee, the AMC needs documents such as a succession certificate, probate, or indemnity and NOCs (based on value) to establish the claimant's right.

Question 6

A Consolidated Account Statement for a demat account holder who also holds mutual fund units in statement form is generated by:

  1. The depositories
  2. The investor's bank, along with the monthly savings bank statement
  3. SEBI
  4. Each distributor separately
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The depositories

Where the investor has a demat account, the depositories send a single CAS covering demat holdings and mutual fund folios linked through PAN.

Question 7

An NRI wants returns on a mutual fund investment to be fully repatriable. The investment should be funded from:

  1. An NRO account, which is always fully repatriable without limits
  2. A resident savings account
  3. An NRE account
  4. Cash
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. An NRE account

NRE account funds and proceeds are freely repatriable. NRO funds are generally non-repatriable, subject to permitted remittance limits.

Question 8

Redemption proceeds of most open-ended schemes must be transferred to investors within:

  1. 10 working days from the end of the month in which the request is made
  2. 3 working days
  3. 1 month
  4. 1 working day
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 3 working days

SEBI requires redemption proceeds to be paid within three working days for most schemes, with specified exceptions for schemes facing special circumstances.
