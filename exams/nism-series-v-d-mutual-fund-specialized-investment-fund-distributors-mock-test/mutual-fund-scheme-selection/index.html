Mutual Fund Scheme Selection: NISM Series V-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Mutual Fund Scheme Selection, from the NISM Series V-D (Mutual Fund – Specialized Investment Fund (SIF) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter is about matching products to investors. Revise how goals, horizon, risk profile, liquidity needs and tax status guide the choice among mutual fund categories and SIF strategies. For SIFs, suitability is central: they need a ₹10 lakh minimum, may have restricted redemption frequency, and use derivatives and short positions. Distributors must judge whether the investor has adequate wealth, experience and understanding. Also cover choosing between active and passive, and growth versus IDCW options.

Mutual Fund Scheme Selection questions

Question 1

Which investor is the most suitable for an equity long-short SIF?

  1. A wealthy, experienced investor with a long horizon
  2. A first-time investor who needs all of the money back within six months
  3. A retiree relying on the corpus for monthly expenses
  4. A student investing pocket money
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A wealthy, experienced investor with a long horizon

SIFs suit investors who can meet the minimum, understand derivative-based strategies, can bear losses and do not need quick access to the money.

Question 2

An investor who wants daily liquidity with very low risk should prefer:

  1. A sector rotation long-short SIF with a weekly redemption window and notice period
  2. A small cap fund
  3. A liquid or overnight fund
  4. A credit risk fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A liquid or overnight fund

Liquid and overnight funds offer daily redemption and low volatility. SIFs may restrict redemptions and carry higher risk.

Question 3

An investor wants equity exposure with lower downside than a long-only fund, can invest ₹25 lakh, and has a 5-year horizon. A reasonable option to consider is:

  1. An overnight fund, as it gives equity returns with no downside
  2. A gilt fund with 10-year maturity
  3. A sectoral long-only fund
  4. An equity long-short SIF
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. An equity long-short SIF

Equity long-short strategies aim to participate in equity markets while using shorts to soften drawdowns. The investor's ticket size and horizon fit this product.

Question 4

Before recommending an SIF, a distributor should particularly confirm that the investor:

  1. Has invested in at least three new fund offers in the last year
  2. Understands derivatives risks
  3. Has a demat account with CDSL
  4. Is below 40 years of age
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Understands derivatives risks

SIFs use derivatives and short positions, which can behave unexpectedly. Understanding and accepting these risks is essential to suitability.

Question 5

For an investor who needs ₹30,000 a month from a ₹50 lakh corpus with moderate risk, a better structure than IDCW is:

  1. The IDCW option of a small cap fund, since IDCW is always fixed
  2. A single lumpsum redemption
  3. An SWP from a hybrid scheme
  4. An ELSS with lock-in
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. An SWP from a hybrid scheme

An SWP gives a fixed, predictable cash flow, and only the gain portion of each withdrawal is taxed. IDCW amounts are not assured and are fully taxable.

Question 6

Why may an index fund be preferred by some investors over an actively managed large cap fund?

  1. Index funds guarantee to beat the market by a fixed margin each year
  2. Index funds have no market risk
  3. Index funds carry a lock-in
  4. Lower cost and no manager risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Lower cost and no manager risk

Index funds track the market at low cost and avoid the risk of active underperformance. They still carry full market risk.

Question 7

Holding several long-short SIFs with similar strategies mainly creates:

  1. Automatic hedging that removes all risk from the overall portfolio
  2. Overlap with little diversification
  3. Lower total minimum investment
  4. A tax exemption
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Overlap with little diversification

Similar strategies tend to hold similar positions, so adding more of them increases cost and complexity without much diversification benefit.

Question 8

An investor with a 2-year goal and low risk tolerance asks to invest in a hybrid long-short SIF. The most suitable response is:

  1. Recommend a lower-risk debt option
  2. Accept immediately, since hybrid strategies are always low risk
  3. Ask the investor to borrow to meet the minimum
  4. Suggest an equity long-short strategy instead
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Recommend a lower-risk debt option

A short horizon and low risk tolerance point towards low-duration debt or similar products. SIF strategies, even hybrid ones, may be too risky and less liquid for this goal.
