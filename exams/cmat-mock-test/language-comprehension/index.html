Language Comprehension: CMAT practice questions

15 free questions with answers and explanations on Language Comprehension, from the CMAT (Common Management Admission Test) syllabus.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this section covers

Language Comprehension tests vocabulary, grammar and reading. Expect synonyms and antonyms, idioms, one-word substitution, error spotting, fill-in-the-blanks, sentence improvement, para jumbles and short reading passages with questions on the main idea, details and inference. Revise subject–verb agreement, conditionals, prepositions and comparatives such as 'senior to'. Build vocabulary through regular reading. For passages, read the questions first, then identify the author's central claim, and reject options that are too extreme or contradict the text.

Language Comprehension questions

Question 1

Her candid feedback, though uncomfortable, helped the team improve. Which word is closest in meaning to CANDID as used here?

  1. Secretive
  2. Cautious
  3. Cunning
  4. Frank
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Frank

Candid means honest and straightforward in speech, which is what frank means. Secretive and cunning suggest concealment, the opposite idea.

Question 2

The firm hired temporary staff to augment its sales force before the festive season. Which word is the antonym of AUGMENT?

  1. Enlarge
  2. Supplement
  3. Diminish
  4. Amplify
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Diminish

To augment is to increase or add to something. Diminish means to reduce, so it is the antonym; the other three are near-synonyms of augment.

Question 3

The auditor was meticulous, checking every voucher twice. Choose the synonym of METICULOUS.

  1. Careless
  2. Painstaking
  3. Hurried
  4. Ambitious
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Painstaking

A meticulous person pays very careful attention to every detail, which is the sense of painstaking. Careless and hurried are opposites.

Question 4

Choose the one word for: a person who does not believe in the existence of God.

  1. Atheist
  2. Agnostic
  3. Theist
  4. Heretic
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Atheist

An atheist denies the existence of God. An agnostic holds that it cannot be known, a theist believes, and a heretic holds views opposed to accepted doctrine.

Question 5

Select the most appropriate meaning of the idiom 'a blessing in disguise'.

  1. A gift that is given secretly to someone in need
  2. Something that seems bad at first but turns out well
  3. A favour that later causes serious trouble
  4. A hidden talent that nobody has noticed yet
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Something that seems bad at first but turns out well

A blessing in disguise is an apparent misfortune that later proves beneficial, for example losing a job that leads to a better one.

Question 6

Select the most appropriate meaning of the idiom 'to bell the cat'.

  1. To raise a false alarm in order to scare people
  2. To reveal a secret that one promised to keep
  3. To keep a constant watch over a dishonest person
  4. To take on a risky task for the benefit of others
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. To take on a risky task for the benefit of others

From the fable of the mice who wanted a bell on the cat but found no volunteer, to bell the cat means to undertake a dangerous job on everyone's behalf.

Question 7

Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error. Neither the manager nor his assistants / was present / at the meeting / yesterday.

  1. was present
  2. Neither the manager nor his assistants
  3. at the meeting
  4. yesterday
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. was present

With 'neither…nor', the verb agrees with the nearer subject. 'Assistants' is plural, so the sentence needs 'were present'.

Question 8

Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error. He is senior than me / in the organisation, / but he never / behaves arrogantly.

  1. in the organisation,
  2. but he never
  3. He is senior than me
  4. behaves arrogantly.
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. He is senior than me

Latin comparatives such as senior, junior, superior and prior take 'to', not 'than'. The correct form is 'He is senior to me'.

Question 9

Fill in the blank with the most appropriate word. Despite repeated reminders, the vendor remained ______ about the delivery date and gave no clear answer.

  1. punctual
  2. transparent
  3. evasive
  4. grateful
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. evasive

'Despite repeated reminders' and 'gave no clear answer' signal avoidance, so evasive fits. Transparent would mean the opposite, being open and clear.

Question 10

Fill in the blank with the correct preposition. She has been working with this firm ______ 2019.

  1. since
  2. for
  3. from
  4. by
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. since

'Since' is used with a point in time in the present perfect continuous. 'For' is used with a period, such as 'for six years'.

Question 11

Select the best replacement for the underlined part. If no improvement is needed, choose 'No improvement'. [If I would have known] about the delay, I would have taken the earlier train.

  1. If I knew
  2. If I would know
  3. No improvement
  4. Had I known
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Had I known

A third conditional uses the past perfect in the if-clause: 'If I had known' or the inverted 'Had I known'. 'Would have' never appears in the if-clause.

Question 12

Arrange the sentences into a coherent paragraph. P. As a result, even small shops now accept payments through a phone. Q. Over the past decade, digital payments in India have grown rapidly. R. This growth has been driven largely by the Unified Payments Interface. S. UPI allows instant transfers between bank accounts at little or no cost.

  1. QSRP
  2. QRSP
  3. RQSP
  4. QRPS
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. QRSP

Q introduces the topic, R names the driver of growth, S explains what UPI does, and P gives the consequence ('As a result'). So the order is QRSP.

Question 13

Read the passage and answer the question. When companies first moved to remote work, many managers feared that productivity would collapse. For routine, well-defined tasks, the fear proved largely unfounded: output held steady and employees saved hours of commuting. The harder problems were subtler. New hires, who learn much by watching experienced colleagues, found it difficult to absorb the informal knowledge that never appears in a manual. Spontaneous conversations that spark ideas also became rarer. Firms that succeeded did not simply return everyone to the office; they deliberately created occasions for mentoring and unstructured interaction, whether in person or online. Which of the following best states the main idea of the passage?

  1. Remote work's real cost was lost informal learning, which firms had to address deliberately
  2. Remote work sharply reduced the output of employees doing routine and well-defined tasks
  3. Companies should bring all employees back to the office to restore their productivity
  4. Saving commuting time is the most important benefit that remote work offers to staff
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Remote work's real cost was lost informal learning, which firms had to address deliberately

The passage says routine output held steady; the subtler problem was weaker informal learning and fewer spontaneous ideas, which successful firms tackled on purpose. It rejects a simple return to office.

Question 14

Read the passage and answer the question. When companies first moved to remote work, many managers feared that productivity would collapse. For routine, well-defined tasks, the fear proved largely unfounded: output held steady and employees saved hours of commuting. The harder problems were subtler. New hires, who learn much by watching experienced colleagues, found it difficult to absorb the informal knowledge that never appears in a manual. Spontaneous conversations that spark ideas also became rarer. Firms that succeeded did not simply return everyone to the office; they deliberately created occasions for mentoring and unstructured interaction, whether in person or online. According to the passage, which group found remote work especially difficult?

  1. Senior managers
  2. New hires
  3. Employees doing routine tasks
  4. Workers with long commutes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. New hires

The passage states that new hires, who learn by watching experienced colleagues, struggled to absorb informal knowledge. Routine-task employees, by contrast, kept output steady.

Question 15

Read the passage and answer the question. When companies first moved to remote work, many managers feared that productivity would collapse. For routine, well-defined tasks, the fear proved largely unfounded: output held steady and employees saved hours of commuting. The harder problems were subtler. New hires, who learn much by watching experienced colleagues, found it difficult to absorb the informal knowledge that never appears in a manual. Spontaneous conversations that spark ideas also became rarer. Firms that succeeded did not simply return everyone to the office; they deliberately created occasions for mentoring and unstructured interaction, whether in person or online. With which statement would the author most likely agree?

  1. Productivity always falls when employees stop meeting in person
  2. Formal manuals capture nearly everything a new employee needs
  3. Online interaction cannot support any kind of mentoring at all
  4. Returning all staff to the office is not, by itself, a complete solution
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Returning all staff to the office is not, by itself, a complete solution

The author says successful firms 'did not simply return everyone to the office' and created mentoring 'in person or online'. Hence an office return alone is not enough.
