Innovation & Entrepreneurship: CMAT practice questions

15 free questions with answers and explanations on Innovation & Entrepreneurship, from the CMAT (Common Management Admission Test) syllabus.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this section covers

Innovation & Entrepreneurship tests the ideas and vocabulary of starting and growing ventures: types of innovation, lean startup concepts such as the MVP and pivot, business models and the Business Model Canvas, funding stages, burn rate and dilution, intellectual property (patents, copyright, designs, trademarks, trade secrets, GIs), and Indian startup-support initiatives. Learn standard definitions with examples, know the key thinkers such as Schumpeter, Christensen and Osterwalder, and practise short numerical questions on runway and equity stakes.

Innovation & Entrepreneurship questions

Question 1

In the Lean Startup approach, a Minimum Viable Product (MVP) is best described as:

  1. the cheapest product a firm can sell at a profit in the mass market
  2. a fully featured product launched only after detailed market research
  3. a prototype that is shown only to investors before any funding round
  4. the simplest version of a product that lets the team learn from real customers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. the simplest version of a product that lets the team learn from real customers

Eric Ries defines the MVP as the version that allows maximum validated learning about customers with the least effort. Its purpose is learning, not a polished or cheapest launch.

Question 2

A food-delivery startup finds that offices, not households, are its most loyal customers, and reshapes its model to serve corporate canteens. In startup terminology, this is a:

  1. Spin-off
  2. Merger
  3. Pivot
  4. Bootstrap
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Pivot

A pivot is a structured change of strategy to test a new hypothesis while keeping what has been learned. A spin-off creates a separate company; bootstrapping concerns self-funding.

Question 3

Under the Copyright Act, 1957, copyright in a published literary work generally lasts for:

  1. 20 years from publication
  2. the author's lifetime plus 70 years
  3. 10 years, renewable every 10 years
  4. the author's lifetime plus 60 years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. the author's lifetime plus 60 years

In India, copyright in literary, dramatic, musical and artistic works lasts for the author's life plus 60 years. Life plus 70 years is the rule in the EU and USA, not India.

Question 4

In India, registration of an industrial design under the Designs Act, 2000 gives protection for a maximum of:

  1. 10 years
  2. 20 years
  3. 15 years
  4. 60 years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 15 years

Design registration is valid for 10 years and can be extended once by 5 years, giving 15 years in all. 20 years is the patent term.

Question 5

The phrase 'creative destruction', describing how innovation replaces old industries, is most closely associated with:

  1. Peter Drucker
  2. Joseph Schumpeter
  3. Adam Smith
  4. Michael Porter
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Joseph Schumpeter

Schumpeter argued that entrepreneurs drive economic change through innovations that destroy existing products and firms. Drucker wrote on systematic innovation, and Porter on competitive strategy.

Question 6

How many building blocks does the Business Model Canvas developed by Alexander Osterwalder contain?

  1. Nine
  2. Five
  3. Seven
  4. Twelve
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Nine

The canvas has nine blocks: customer segments, value propositions, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partnerships and cost structure.

Question 7

Which sequence shows the usual order of startup funding stages?

  1. Seed → Pre-seed → Series A → Series B
  2. Pre-seed → Seed → Series A → Series B
  3. Series A → Seed → Pre-seed → Series B
  4. Pre-seed → Series A → Seed → Series B
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Pre-seed → Seed → Series A → Series B

Founders typically raise pre-seed money (often from themselves, friends or angels), then a seed round, followed by institutional Series A and later Series B rounds to scale.

Question 8

A startup has ₹1.8 crore in the bank. Its monthly expenses are ₹45 lakh and monthly revenue is ₹15 lakh. Assuming these stay constant, what is its cash runway?

  1. 4 months
  2. 8 months
  3. 12 months
  4. 6 months
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 6 months

Net burn = ₹45 lakh − ₹15 lakh = ₹30 lakh per month. Runway = ₹180 lakh ÷ ₹30 lakh = 6 months. Using gross burn alone gives the wrong answer of 4 months.

Question 9

An enterprise sells low-cost sanitary pads made by rural women's groups and reinvests most of its surplus to expand access in villages. This is best described as:

  1. Social entrepreneurship
  2. Corporate social responsibility
  3. Franchising
  4. Lifestyle entrepreneurship
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Social entrepreneurship

Social entrepreneurship uses a business model whose primary aim is social impact, reinvesting surplus towards that mission. CSR is spending by an existing company, not the core business itself.

Question 10

An employee of a large bank develops and launches a new digital lending product within the bank, using its resources. Such behaviour is called:

  1. Outsourcing
  2. Franchising
  3. Intrapreneurship
  4. Disintermediation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Intrapreneurship

Intrapreneurship means acting like an entrepreneur inside an existing organisation, taking initiative and risk to create new products while using the employer's resources.

Question 11

In a SWOT analysis of a startup, a large competitor announcing entry into the same market would be classified as a:

  1. Weakness
  2. Strength
  3. Threat
  4. Opportunity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Threat

Strengths and weaknesses are internal; opportunities and threats are external. A new competitor is an external factor that could harm the firm, so it is a threat.

Question 12

The 'Blue Ocean Strategy' proposed by W. Chan Kim and Renée Mauborgne recommends that firms:

  1. create uncontested market space that makes competition irrelevant
  2. cut prices aggressively to win share from existing competitors
  3. focus only on the largest and most crowded existing markets
  4. copy successful rivals quickly and improve on their products
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. create uncontested market space that makes competition irrelevant

Blue oceans are new, uncontested markets created through value innovation; red oceans are crowded markets where firms fight over existing demand, often through price cuts.

Question 13

A restaurant chain keeps its spice-blend recipe confidential among a few staff bound by non-disclosure agreements instead of applying for a patent. The recipe is protected as a:

  1. Trademark
  2. Copyright
  3. Geographical Indication
  4. Trade secret
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Trade secret

A trade secret is valuable confidential information protected by secrecy and contracts. Unlike a patent, it never requires public disclosure and can last indefinitely if kept secret.

Question 14

A founder owns 60% of a company's 1,00,000 shares. The company then issues 25,000 new shares to an investor. What is the founder's stake after this issue?

  1. 45%
  2. 48%
  3. 50%
  4. 60%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 48%

The founder still holds 60,000 shares, but the total rises to 1,25,000. Stake = 60,000 ÷ 1,25,000 = 48%. This reduction in ownership is called dilution.

Question 15

In the design thinking process (Empathise, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test), which stage comes immediately after defining the problem?

  1. Ideate
  2. Empathise
  3. Prototype
  4. Test
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Ideate

After empathising with users and defining the problem, teams ideate by generating many possible solutions, then build prototypes and test them. The process is iterative.
