Taxation: NISM Series XIX-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Taxation, from the NISM Series XIX-C (Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the tax treatment of AIFs and their investors in India. It explains pass-through status for Category I and II AIFs (except business income), TDS on distributions, treatment of losses, taxation of Category III AIFs at fund level under trust rules, characterisation of income as capital gains or business income, non-resident investors and treaty access, GST on management fees and the tax issues around carried interest. Since the new Income-tax Act has renumbered provisions, focus on principles rather than section numbers or rates.

Taxation questions

Question 1

Dividend income received by a Category II AIF is taxed:

  1. At the fund level only, with investors receiving it tax-free
  2. As capital gains
  3. Exempt in all cases
  4. In investors' hands as dividend income
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. In investors' hands as dividend income

Under pass-through, dividend income retains its character and is taxed in investors' hands.

Question 2

Business income of a Category II AIF is taxed at fund level. When that income is later distributed, investors are generally:

  1. Taxed again at slab rates
  2. Taxed at double the fund rate
  3. Required to pay GST
  4. Not taxed again on it
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Not taxed again on it

Business income taxed at the fund level is exempt in investors' hands on distribution, avoiding double tax.

Question 3

Losses of a Category I or II AIF (other than business losses) can be passed to investors only if:

  1. They are non-residents
  2. The fund is open-ended
  3. The loss exceeds ₹1 crore
  4. They have held units for the prescribed minimum period
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. They have held units for the prescribed minimum period

Loss pass-through requires investors to have held units for at least the prescribed period, preventing loss trading.

Question 4

Carried interest received by managers is a contested tax issue because:

  1. It is always exempt
  2. It may be treated as capital gains or fee income
  3. It is taxed as agricultural income
  4. It is outside Indian tax law
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. It may be treated as capital gains or fee income

Characterisation depends on structure and facts; tax authorities may treat it as service income rather than capital gains.

Question 5

A Category III AIF trust with indeterminate shares may be taxed:

  1. At zero
  2. Only on distributions
  3. At the maximum marginal rate
  4. At each investor's own slab rate on the share distributed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. At the maximum marginal rate

When beneficiaries' shares are not determinable, trust income is generally taxed at the maximum marginal rate.

Question 6

GST on management fees is a cost to investors because:

  1. SEBI levies it
  2. The fund usually cannot claim input credit
  3. It is refunded to the manager after the fund's annual audit
  4. It applies only to carry
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The fund usually cannot claim input credit

AIFs typically cannot set off GST paid on fees, so it raises fund costs.

Question 7

A non-resident investor in a Category II AIF benefits from a tax treaty by:

  1. Opening a fixed deposit
  2. Registering as an FPI only
  3. Doing nothing
  4. Providing a tax residency certificate and forms
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Providing a tax residency certificate and forms

Treaty rates generally require a tax residency certificate and related forms from the non-resident investor.

Question 8

The main purpose of TDS on AIF distributions to residents is to:

  1. Replace investors' final tax liability on the income distributed
  2. Collect tax early and track income
  3. Exempt investors from filing
  4. Fund SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Collect tax early and track income

TDS secures tax revenue and creates an audit trail; investors still declare income and claim credit.
