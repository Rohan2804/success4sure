Fee Structure and Fund Performance: NISM Series XIX-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Fee Structure and Fund Performance, from the NISM Series XIX-C (Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how AIF managers are paid and how fund performance is measured. It explains management fees on committed or invested capital, fund expenses, carried interest, hurdle or preferred return, GP catch-up, European (whole-of-fund) and American (deal-by-deal) waterfalls, clawback and escrow, and the effect of fees on net returns. Performance topics include IRR, gross and net returns, TVPI, DPI, RVPI and MOIC. Practise waterfall and multiple calculations step by step, and understand how fee bases shift after the investment period.

Fee Structure and Fund Performance questions

Question 1

Fund capital is ₹200 crore and total proceeds are ₹300 crore. The preferred return is ₹16 crore and carry is 20% with a full (100%) GP catch-up. The manager's total carry is:

  1. ₹16.8 crore
  2. ₹16 crore
  3. ₹60 crore
  4. ₹20 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹20 crore

Total profit = ₹100 crore. With full catch-up and enough profit, the manager ends up with 20% of all profit: ₹20 crore. Without catch-up it would be 20% × (100 − 16) = ₹16.8 crore.

Question 2

Using the same fund (capital ₹200 crore, proceeds ₹300 crore, preferred return ₹16 crore, 20% carry) but with no catch-up, the carry is:

  1. ₹20 crore
  2. ₹16.8 crore
  3. ₹3.2 crore
  4. ₹13.6 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹16.8 crore

Profit above the hurdle = 100 − 16 = ₹84 crore. Carry = 20% × 84 = ₹16.8 crore.

Question 3

After the investment period, a fund charges 1.5% a year on invested capital of ₹400 crore. The annual management fee is:

  1. ₹4 crore
  2. ₹8 crore
  3. ₹6 crore
  4. ₹60 lakh
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹6 crore

Fee = 1.5% × 400 = ₹6 crore. Fee bases often shift from commitments to invested capital after the investment period.

Question 4

A fund has paid-in capital of ₹150 crore, distributions of ₹90 crore and NAV of ₹120 crore. Its TVPI and DPI are:

  1. 0.6x and 1.4x
  2. 1.4x and 0.8x
  3. 1.4x and 0.6x
  4. 2.1x and 0.6x
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 1.4x and 0.6x

TVPI = (90 + 120) ÷ 150 = 1.4x. DPI = 90 ÷ 150 = 0.6x.

Question 5

An investor puts ₹100 crore into a fund and receives ₹144 crore exactly two years later, with no other cash flows. The IRR is:

  1. 22%
  2. 44%
  3. 18%
  4. 20%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 20%

IRR solves 100 × (1 + r)² = 144, so r = 20%. 22% is the simple annual average of 44%.

Question 6

Why do investors prefer carry placed in escrow until they have received back their contributed capital?

  1. It reduces the risk of needing a clawback
  2. It raises the manager's carry
  3. It removes the hurdle so that carry is earned from the first rupee
  4. It increases management fees
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. It reduces the risk of needing a clawback

Holding carry in escrow protects against later losses that would otherwise require the manager to return excess carry.

Question 7

Under a deal-by-deal (American) waterfall, the manager:

  1. Earns carry only after all capital is returned
  2. Never earns carry
  3. Earns carry only on unrealised gains
  4. Can earn carry on each profitable exit early
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Can earn carry on each profitable exit early

American waterfalls pay carry deal by deal, so carry can be paid before all capital is returned; a clawback is therefore important.

Question 8

Fund expenses such as audit and legal fees charged to the fund mainly affect:

  1. Net returns to investors
  2. Only the manager's income
  3. Gross IRR only
  4. The fund's tax status
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Net returns to investors

Fund expenses are borne by investors and reduce net returns, widening the gap between gross and net IRR.
