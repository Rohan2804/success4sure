Sample 1 · Investments Landscape

An investor earns a nominal return of 10% when inflation is 6%. Using the exact Fisher relationship, the real return is approximately:

  1. 4.00%
  2. 16.00%
  3. 6.00%
  4. 3.77%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 3.77%

Real return = (1.10 / 1.06) − 1 ≈ 3.77%.

Sample 2 · Types of Investments

Which of these is a hybrid security combining features of debt and equity?

  1. Treasury bill
  2. Equity share
  3. Compulsorily convertible debenture
  4. Commercial paper
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Compulsorily convertible debenture

A CCD pays interest like debt and later converts into equity, which is why it is widely used in PE deals.

Sample 3 · Concept of Informational Efficiency

If prices fully reflect all publicly available information, so that fundamental analysis of published data cannot consistently earn excess returns, the market is:

  1. Weak form efficient only
  2. Strong form efficient
  3. Inefficient
  4. Semi-strong form efficient
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Semi-strong form efficient

Semi-strong efficiency means public information (including financial statements) is already in prices.

Sample 4 · Introduction to Modern Portfolio Theory

Two assets each have standard deviation of 20% and correlation of +1. A 50:50 portfolio of them will have a standard deviation of:

  1. 10%
  2. 20%
  3. 14.1%
  4. 0%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 20%

With perfect positive correlation there is no diversification benefit; portfolio σ is the weighted average, 20%.

Sample 5 · Introduction to Capital Market Theory

Using CAPM, if the risk-free rate is 6%, the expected market return is 12% and a fund's beta is 1.2, its required return is:

  1. 14.4%
  2. 12.0%
  3. 13.2%
  4. 7.2%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 13.2%

Required return = 6% + 1.2 × (12% − 6%) = 6% + 7.2% = 13.2%.

NISM Series XIX-C mock test FAQs

How many questions are there in the NISM Series XIX-C exam?

The NISM Series XIX-C paper has 100 multiple-choice questions for 100 marks, to be completed in 180 minutes. This is the indicative pattern — always confirm it in the latest official notification.

Is there negative marking in NISM Series XIX-C?

Yes. −0.25 mark per wrong answer (25% of the marks for that question). Our mock tests apply the same rule.

What is the pass mark for NISM Series XIX-C?

You need 60% to pass. Our mock tests show pass or fail against the same mark.

Are the NISM Series XIX-C mock tests free?

Yes. All 30 full-length NISM Series XIX-C mock tests are free, with no payment or plan. Create a free account (one click with Google) to start; your results are saved across devices. You can retake any test as often as you like.

Should I take NISM XIX-C, XIX-D or XIX-E?

Series XIX-C covers AIF managers across categories, while XIX-D focuses on Category I and II AIF managers and XIX-E on Category III. Which one satisfies the requirement depends on your fund's category and current SEBI rules, so confirm with your compliance team and on nism.ac.in.

Is NISM Series XIX-C difficult?

It is one of the broader NISM papers, combining portfolio theory with private markets, fund structuring, valuation and regulation. Many learners find valuation, fee waterfalls and benchmarking the hardest. Plan for steady practice with worked examples rather than relying on reading alone.

How long should I study for NISM Series XIX-C?

Most professionals need three to four weeks. Cover portfolio theory and the AIF ecosystem first, then fees, investment process and valuation, and finally taxation and regulation. Reserve the last week for timed mock tests and revision of weak chapters.
