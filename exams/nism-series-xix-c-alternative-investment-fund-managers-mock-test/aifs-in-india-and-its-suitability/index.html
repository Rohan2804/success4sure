AIFs in India and Its Suitability: NISM Series XIX-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on AIFs in India and Its Suitability, from the NISM Series XIX-C (Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains the AIF categories under SEBI's regulations and how a manager or adviser judges suitability. It covers Category I sub-categories (venture capital and angel funds, SME, social impact, infrastructure, special situation funds), Category II (private equity, private debt, funds of funds) and Category III (hedge-fund style, PIPE and long-short), along with the characteristics that make AIFs suitable only for sophisticated investors with surplus wealth and long horizons. Focus on classifying a proposed fund correctly and on recognising when an AIF is unsuitable for an investor.

AIFs in India and Its Suitability questions

Question 1

A fund that invests in listed shares through preferential allotments and uses derivatives and short positions would most likely be a:

  1. Category III AIF
  2. Category I SME fund
  3. Category II fund of funds
  4. Category I social impact fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Category III AIF

Strategies involving listed securities with derivatives and shorting fall under Category III.

Question 2

An AIF set up to invest in unlisted small and medium enterprises would register as:

  1. Category III hedge fund
  2. Category I SME fund
  3. Category II debt fund
  4. A mutual fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Category I SME fund

SME funds are a Category I sub-category, reflecting policy support for smaller businesses.

Question 3

Which investor is LEAST suitable for a Category II PE fund?

  1. One needing the money within two years
  2. An HNI with long horizon and surplus wealth
  3. A pension fund with long-term liabilities
  4. A family office seeking diversification
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. One needing the money within two years

PE funds lock in capital for many years. An investor needing funds soon cannot accommodate the illiquidity.

Question 4

A fund focusing on companies creating measurable social benefits, such as affordable healthcare, would be a:

  1. Category III long-short fund
  2. Social impact fund under Category I
  3. Category II real estate fund investing in completed office buildings
  4. Portfolio management service
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Social impact fund under Category I

Social impact funds, part of Category I, invest in entities with social objectives and may raise money through the social stock exchange route.

Question 5

Why are Category I and II AIFs required to be close-ended?

  1. SEBI wants them to trade on stock exchanges like closed-end funds
  2. Their illiquid assets cannot meet redemptions
  3. They hold only cash
  4. Investors prefer daily NAV
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Their illiquid assets cannot meet redemptions

Unlisted holdings cannot be sold quickly, so allowing redemptions would harm remaining investors.

Question 6

A fund of funds investing in multiple Category II AIFs offers an investor:

  1. Lower total fees than investing directly in the underlying funds
  2. Diversification across managers, with an extra fee layer
  3. Daily liquidity
  4. A guaranteed return
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Diversification across managers, with an extra fee layer

FoFs diversify across managers and vintages but add a second layer of fees.

Question 7

An angel fund primarily pools money from:

  1. Retail investors through SIPs
  2. Banks for infrastructure lending
  3. Mutual funds seeking exposure to listed shares of large companies
  4. Angel investors backing early-stage start-ups
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Angel investors backing early-stage start-ups

Angel funds, a Category I VCF sub-category, pool capital from angel investors to fund early-stage start-ups.

Question 8

Which question is most important when judging whether an AIF suits a client?

  1. Can she lock in money and bear a total loss?
  2. Does she prefer to bank with the sponsor's group bank for convenience?
  3. How many credit cards does she hold?
  4. What is her favourite sector?
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Can she lock in money and bear a total loss?

Suitability depends on ability to tolerate illiquidity and losses, plus understanding of the product.
