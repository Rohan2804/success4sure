What this chapter covers

This chapter builds on portfolio theory to cover capital market theory: the risk-free asset, the capital market line, the market portfolio, the security market line and the Capital Asset Pricing Model. It explains beta as a measure of systematic risk, required return, and performance measures derived from these ideas — Sharpe, Treynor and Jensen's alpha — together with multi-factor models. Practise CAPM and performance-measure calculations, and understand when each measure is appropriate, for example Treynor for diversified portfolios and Sharpe for total-risk comparison.

Introduction to Capital Market Theory questions

Question 1

Risk-free rate is 7%, expected market return is 13% and a portfolio's beta is 0.8. Its CAPM required return is:

  1. 10.4%
  2. 13.0%
  3. 17.4%
  4. 11.8%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 11.8%

Required return = 7 + 0.8 × (13 − 7) = 7 + 4.8 = 11.8%.

Question 2

A fund returned 15% with beta 1.5 when the risk-free rate was 6%. Its Treynor ratio is:

  1. 10.0
  2. 9.0
  3. 1.5
  4. 6.0
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 6.0

Treynor = (15 − 6) ÷ 1.5 = 6.0. It measures excess return per unit of systematic risk.

Question 3

A fund returned 16% with beta 1.2. The risk-free rate was 6% and the market returned 12%. Its Jensen's alpha is:

  1. +4.0%
  2. +2.8%
  3. +10.0%
  4. −2.8%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. +2.8%

CAPM return = 6 + 1.2 × 6 = 13.2%. Alpha = 16 − 13.2 = +2.8%. +4% compares with the market return without adjusting for beta.

Question 4

The capital market line (CML) plots:

  1. Individual securities' expected returns plotted against their betas
  2. Bond yields against maturity
  3. Company earnings over time
  4. Efficient portfolios of the risk-free and market portfolio
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Efficient portfolios of the risk-free and market portfolio

The CML shows combinations of the risk-free asset and the market portfolio, with total risk on the x-axis. The SML uses beta.

Question 5

A stock plots above the security market line. This suggests it is:

  1. Overvalued, since it offers less than its required return
  2. Undervalued, offering more than its required return
  3. Risk-free
  4. Perfectly priced
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Undervalued, offering more than its required return

A security above the SML offers a higher expected return than CAPM requires for its beta, implying undervaluation.

Question 6

Beta of a portfolio with 50% in a stock of beta 1.2 and 50% in a stock of beta 0.8 is:

  1. 2.0
  2. 1.0
  3. 0.96
  4. 0.4
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 1.0

Portfolio beta is the weighted average: 0.5 × 1.2 + 0.5 × 0.8 = 1.0.

Question 7

Multi-factor models extend CAPM by:

  1. Removing the risk-free rate
  2. Using several risk factors such as size and value
  3. Assuming that all assets have exactly the same beta as the market
  4. Ignoring market risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Using several risk factors such as size and value

Factor models explain returns with several systematic factors beyond the market, such as size, value and momentum.

Question 8

Which measure is most appropriate to compare a fund that is an investor's entire portfolio?

  1. Treynor ratio
  2. Sharpe ratio
  3. Beta alone
  4. Dividend yield
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Sharpe ratio

When a fund is the whole portfolio, total risk matters, so the Sharpe ratio (using standard deviation) is appropriate. Treynor suits well-diversified holdings within a larger portfolio.
