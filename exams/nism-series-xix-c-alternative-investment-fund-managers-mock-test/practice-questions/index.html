Free NISM Series XIX-C practice questions

Question 1 · Investments Landscape

Which of the following is a real asset rather than a financial asset?

  1. A corporate bond
  2. An equity share
  3. A fixed deposit receipt
  4. A commercial office building
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A commercial office building

Real assets are physical or tangible; financial assets are claims on them or on issuers.

Question 2 · Types of Investments

Mezzanine financing in private markets typically ranks:

  1. Above senior secured debt
  2. Equal to common equity
  3. Below common equity
  4. Below senior secured debt but above common equity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Below senior secured debt but above common equity

Mezzanine is subordinated debt or preferred capital that sits between senior loans and equity in the capital structure.

Question 3 · Concept of Informational Efficiency

Private equity managers argue that they can earn excess returns partly because:

  1. Private company prices are published daily
  2. Insider trading laws do not apply to anyone
  3. Private companies have no information asymmetry
  4. Private markets are less informationally efficient than listed markets
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Private markets are less informationally efficient than listed markets

Limited disclosure and fewer participants create information gaps that skilled managers can exploit.

Question 4 · Introduction to Modern Portfolio Theory

The efficient frontier represents portfolios that:

  1. Have zero risk
  2. Contain only one asset
  3. Offer the highest expected return for each level of risk
  4. Have the lowest return for each level of risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Offer the highest expected return for each level of risk

Portfolios on the frontier dominate all others with the same risk.

Question 5 · Introduction to Capital Market Theory

According to capital market theory, which risk is compensated by a higher expected return?

  1. Systematic (market) risk
  2. Company-specific risk
  3. Total risk including diversifiable risk
  4. Operational risk at the investee level
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Systematic (market) risk

Diversifiable risk can be removed at no cost, so only systematic risk (beta) earns a premium.

Question 6 · AIFs in India and Its Suitability

An AIF that invests in unlisted equity of growth companies, cannot use leverage for investments, and receives no specific government incentives fits:

  1. Category I
  2. Category II
  3. Category III
  4. None; it must be a mutual fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Category II

Category II is the residual category for PE and debt funds without leverage or specific incentives.

Question 7 · AIF Ecosystem

Which ecosystem participant is responsible for ensuring the AIF's assets are kept safely and separately from the manager?

  1. Placement agent
  2. Investment committee
  3. Fund's legal counsel
  4. Custodian
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Custodian

A custodian holds securities independently, reducing misuse risk; SEBI mandates custodians for AIFs.

Question 8 · AIF Structuring

The main advantage of using a trust structure for an Indian Category II AIF is:

  1. Statutory tax pass-through for non-business income and operational flexibility
  2. Limited liability for the trustee under the Companies Act
  3. Ability to list units on day one
  4. Exemption from SEBI regulation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Statutory tax pass-through for non-business income and operational flexibility

Trusts are flexible and Category I/II trusts get pass-through status under tax law.

Question 9 · Fee Structure and Fund Performance

Fund size ₹100 crore; total proceeds ₹150 crore; preferred return equals ₹8 crore; carry 20% with 100% GP catch-up. What is the manager's total carry?

  1. ₹10 crore
  2. ₹8.4 crore
  3. ₹8 crore
  4. ₹12 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹10 crore

With full catch-up the manager ends up with 20% of total profit: 20% × (150 − 100) = ₹10 crore.

Question 10 · Indices and Benchmarking

For comparing a Category I or II AIF's performance with public markets, the Public Market Equivalent (PME) approach:

  1. Compares the fund's IRR with the index's one-year return
  2. Invests the fund's cash flows hypothetically in a public index to compare outcomes
  3. Uses only the fund's final NAV
  4. Ignores the timing of cash flows
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Invests the fund's cash flows hypothetically in a public index to compare outcomes

PME mirrors the fund's contributions and distributions in an index so that timing is matched.

Question 11 · Investment Strategies, Investment Process and Governance of Funds

An AIF's investment committee includes external members. Under SEBI's rules, these external members:

  1. Are responsible for investment decisions they approve, subject to the conditions in the regulations
  2. Bear no responsibility for decisions
  3. Must be SEBI officers
  4. Can be anonymous to investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Are responsible for investment decisions they approve, subject to the conditions in the regulations

Investment committee members, including external ones, are accountable for decisions and must comply with the PPM and regulations.

Question 12 · Fund Due Diligence – Investor Perspective

During due diligence, an investor finds the manager's reported gross IRR is 25% but net IRR is 16%. The difference mainly reflects:

  1. Management fees, fund expenses and carried interest
  2. Investor's personal tax
  3. Inflation
  4. Currency conversion only
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Management fees, fund expenses and carried interest

Net IRR is after fees, expenses and carry, which investors actually receive.

Question 13 · Legal Documentation and Negotiations

A 'no-fault divorce' clause in fund documents allows investors to:

  1. Exit the fund individually at any time
  2. Avoid paying any fees from day one
  3. Remove the manager or terminate the investment period by a specified supermajority vote, even without cause
  4. Transfer units without consent
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Remove the manager or terminate the investment period by a specified supermajority vote, even without cause

It gives investors collective power to replace the manager without having to prove misconduct.

Question 14 · Valuation

IPEV-style valuation guidelines require unlisted investments to be measured at:

  1. Fair value at the reporting date
  2. Historical cost until exit
  3. Face value
  4. The lowest price ever paid
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Fair value at the reporting date

Fair value reflects the price that would be received in an orderly transaction at the measurement date.

Question 15 · Fund Monitoring, Reporting and Exit

A secondary sale of an LP interest allows an investor in a close-ended AIF to:

  1. Force the fund to redeem its units
  2. Exit before fund maturity by selling its interest to another investor
  3. Increase its commitment without approval
  4. Avoid paying carry
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Exit before fund maturity by selling its interest to another investor

The secondaries market provides liquidity for otherwise illiquid fund interests.
