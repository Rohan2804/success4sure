Valuation: NISM Series XIX-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Valuation, from the NISM Series XIX-C (Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers fair valuation of AIF portfolio investments. It explains market approaches (trading comparables and precedent transactions), income approaches (DCF and its terminal value), the price of recent investment, net assets, and adjustments for control, marketability and preference rights. It also covers SEBI's requirements on independent valuers, valuation frequency and the use of IPEV or industry-endorsed guidelines, and the governance of valuation policies. Practise EV-to-equity bridges and perpetuity calculations, and understand when to recalibrate valuations after new information.

Valuation questions

Question 1

A company has EBITDA of ₹25 crore. Peers trade at 12x EV/EBITDA and the company's net debt is ₹50 crore. Its equity value is:

  1. ₹300 crore
  2. ₹350 crore
  3. ₹250 crore
  4. ₹200 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹250 crore

EV = 25 × 12 = ₹300 crore. Equity = 300 − 50 = ₹250 crore. ₹350 crore wrongly adds net debt.

Question 2

A company's free cash flow next year is ₹8 crore, growing 5% forever. At a 13% discount rate, the enterprise value is:

  1. ₹100 crore
  2. ₹61.5 crore
  3. ₹160 crore
  4. ₹105 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹100 crore

EV = 8 ÷ (0.13 − 0.05) = ₹100 crore. ₹61.5 crore ignores growth.

Question 3

Calibration in private company valuation means:

  1. Using the same value every year
  2. Taking the average of all analyst targets
  3. Using book value only
  4. Aligning model inputs with the entry price, then updating
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Aligning model inputs with the entry price, then updating

Calibration checks that the method reproduces the transaction price at entry, and then updates inputs as conditions change.

Question 4

A valuer applies a discount for lack of marketability to a stake because:

  1. It cannot be sold quickly in an open market
  2. It carries more rights than listed shares and therefore commands a premium
  3. SEBI mandates a fixed 30% discount
  4. It is listed on an exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. It cannot be sold quickly in an open market

An illiquid holding is worth less than an otherwise identical freely tradable one; the discount is judgement-based, not fixed.

Question 5

The 'price of recent investment' method is most reliable when:

  1. The round was two years ago
  2. The price was set by insiders only
  3. The company has since failed to meet its milestones and is raising a down round
  4. The round was recent and at arm's length
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The round was recent and at arm's length

A recent arm's-length round is good evidence of fair value; older or insider-only rounds are weaker evidence.

Question 6

SEBI requires AIF valuations to be carried out by an independent valuer mainly to:

  1. Increase reported returns so that the fund can raise its next fund faster
  2. Avoid audits
  3. Set tax rates
  4. Reduce bias in NAV and fee computation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Reduce bias in NAV and fee computation

Independence limits the manager's influence on valuations that affect performance and fees.

Question 7

In a DCF, a higher discount rate, other things equal, will:

  1. Raise the valuation
  2. Have no effect
  3. Double the terminal value
  4. Lower the valuation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Lower the valuation

A higher discount rate reduces the present value of future cash flows.

Question 8

Precedent transaction multiples often exceed trading multiples because they include:

  1. Lower growth assumptions
  2. A marketability discount
  3. A control premium
  4. Tax refunds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A control premium

Acquirers pay a premium for control, so deal multiples are typically higher than minority trading multiples.
