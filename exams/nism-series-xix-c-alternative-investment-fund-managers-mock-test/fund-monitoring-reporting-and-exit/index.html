Fund Monitoring, Reporting and Exit: NISM Series XIX-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Fund Monitoring, Reporting and Exit, from the NISM Series XIX-C (Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers ongoing management of an AIF after investment. It explains portfolio monitoring, reporting to investors (capital account statements, quarterly reports, valuations), reporting to SEBI and the trustee, the annual PPM compliance audit, exit routes for portfolio companies (IPO, strategic sale, secondary sale, buyback, recapitalisation) and liquidity options for investors (secondaries, continuation funds), as well as fund wind-up, tenure extension, dissolution and in-specie distribution. Focus on choosing exits and on the investor-protection rules around end-of-life options.

Fund Monitoring, Reporting and Exit questions

Question 1

A recapitalisation as an exit route means the portfolio company:

  1. Lists on a stock exchange
  2. Raises debt to pay a dividend to shareholders
  3. Is liquidated
  4. Merges with the fund so that its shares are cancelled
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Raises debt to pay a dividend to shareholders

In a dividend recap, the company borrows and distributes the proceeds, allowing a partial exit without selling shares.

Question 2

Extension of a close-ended Category I or II AIF's tenure beyond its stated life requires:

  1. Only the manager's decision
  2. Approval of two-thirds of unit holders by value, within limits
  3. SEBI's prior approval for any extension
  4. No approval at all
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Approval of two-thirds of unit holders by value, within limits

Tenure may be extended by up to two years with approval of two-thirds of investors by value; further options follow SEBI's framework.

Question 3

An investor in a close-ended AIF wants to exit early. The most realistic route is:

  1. Selling its interest in the secondary market
  2. Redeeming its units at NAV from the fund on giving notice
  3. Asking SEBI to refund
  4. Defaulting on capital calls
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Selling its interest in the secondary market

Close-ended AIFs do not offer redemptions; secondary sales provide liquidity, usually at a discount.

Question 4

A buyback exit involves:

  1. The company or promoters repurchasing the fund's shares
  2. An IPO
  3. A strategic merger
  4. A write-off
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The company or promoters repurchasing the fund's shares

In a buyback, the investee or promoters buy back the investor's stake, often under negotiated put rights.

Question 5

Which report helps an investor see its share of fund NAV and cash flows?

  1. Private placement memorandum
  2. Trust deed
  3. Term sheet
  4. Capital account statement
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Capital account statement

The capital account statement shows contributions, distributions and the investor's share of NAV.

Question 6

In-specie distribution at the end of a fund's tenure means:

  1. Paying cash only
  2. Distributing unsold investments to investors directly
  3. Converting the fund to a mutual fund
  4. Selling all assets to the sponsor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Distributing unsold investments to investors directly

Investors receive the remaining securities themselves instead of cash, subject to approval.

Question 7

A strategic sale is often favoured over an IPO because it:

  1. Gives a complete exit at once, often with a premium
  2. Always gives a higher price than an IPO
  3. Needs no negotiations
  4. Avoids taxes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Gives a complete exit at once, often with a premium

Strategic buyers can acquire the whole stake at once and may pay a synergy premium.

Question 8

Fund monitoring of portfolio companies typically includes:

  1. Daily trading of their shares to capture short-term price moves
  2. Running their payroll
  3. Board participation and KPI tracking
  4. Setting their tax rates
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Board participation and KPI tracking

Funds monitor portfolio companies through board seats, management reporting and tracking of key performance indicators.
