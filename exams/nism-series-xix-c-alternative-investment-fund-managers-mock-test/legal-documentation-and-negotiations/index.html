Legal Documentation and Negotiations: NISM Series XIX-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Legal Documentation and Negotiations, from the NISM Series XIX-C (Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the legal documents of an AIF and negotiation of terms between managers and investors, and between funds and investee companies. Fund documents include the PPM, trust deed, contribution agreement, investment management agreement and side letters, with terms such as key-person, removal, clawback, MFN, defaults, transfers and advisory committees. Deal documents include term sheets, share subscription and shareholders' agreements, with protections such as anti-dilution, tag-along, drag-along, ROFR, board seats and information rights. Focus on the purpose of each clause.

Legal Documentation and Negotiations questions

Question 1

A right of first refusal (ROFR) in a shareholders' agreement gives the investor:

  1. The right to match an offer before shares are sold to a third party
  2. The right to force all shareholders to sell
  3. A guaranteed dividend
  4. Automatic board control
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The right to match an offer before shares are sold to a third party

ROFR lets an existing holder buy shares on the same terms before they go to an outsider.

Question 2

Full-ratchet anti-dilution protection means that if new shares are issued at a lower price:

  1. The investor's conversion price drops to the new price
  2. The investor loses its preference rights and becomes an ordinary shareholder
  3. The investor must invest more money
  4. Nothing changes for the investor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The investor's conversion price drops to the new price

Full ratchet resets the conversion price to the lower issue price, strongly protecting the investor; weighted-average methods are milder.

Question 3

An MFN clause in a side letter entitles an investor to:

  1. Manage the fund
  2. Better terms later given to comparable investors
  3. Exit at NAV at any time, irrespective of the fund's lock-in terms
  4. Waive all fees
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Better terms later given to comparable investors

MFN lets the investor elect to receive more favourable terms offered to other similar investors.

Question 4

Information rights in a PE investment allow the fund to:

  1. Vote on all employee hirings
  2. Receive regular financial and operating reports
  3. Sell shares without restriction
  4. Fix product prices
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Receive regular financial and operating reports

Information rights ensure periodic reporting so the investor can monitor its investment.

Question 5

A 'key-person event' typically triggers:

  1. An increase in carry
  2. Automatic winding up of the fund without any vote by investors
  3. Suspension of the investment period
  4. Higher management fees
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Suspension of the investment period

When named key people leave, the investment period is often suspended until investors approve a solution.

Question 6

A put option negotiated by a PE investor against promoters allows the investor to:

  1. Sell its shares to the promoters on specified terms
  2. Buy more shares at a discount
  3. Force a merger
  4. Avoid all taxes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Sell its shares to the promoters on specified terms

A put gives an exit by requiring promoters to buy the investor's shares, subject to legal limits on assured returns for foreign investors.

Question 7

Side letters must be handled so that they:

  1. Are hidden from SEBI
  2. Override the PPM without disclosure
  3. Comply with SEBI's rules on investor rights
  4. Guarantee returns
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Comply with SEBI's rules on investor rights

Side letters must fit SEBI's framework on pro-rata and differential rights and be disclosed as required.

Question 8

A term sheet is generally:

  1. A fully binding share purchase agreement
  2. A SEBI filing
  3. Non-binding on commercial terms, except clauses like confidentiality
  4. A valuation report
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Non-binding on commercial terms, except clauses like confidentiality

Term sheets summarise key terms before definitive documents; most commercial terms are non-binding except confidentiality and exclusivity.
