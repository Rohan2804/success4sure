Indices and Benchmarking: NISM Series XIX-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Indices and Benchmarking, from the NISM Series XIX-C (Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how AIF performance is benchmarked. It covers public market indices and their limits for private funds, peer-group benchmarks by strategy and vintage, quartile rankings, the public market equivalent method, and SEBI's framework under which benchmarking agencies publish industry benchmarks using data reported by AIFs. It also covers hedge-fund style benchmarks and risk-adjusted comparison for Category III. Focus on choosing appropriate benchmarks, comparing funds of the same vintage and strategy, and the pitfalls of comparing IRRs with index returns.

Indices and Benchmarking questions

Question 1

A 2020-vintage PE fund ranks in the top quartile. This means its performance is:

  1. Above the Nifty 50 total return index over the same period
  2. Guaranteed for future funds
  3. In the best 25% of comparable 2020 funds
  4. In the top 25% of all AIFs ever
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. In the best 25% of comparable 2020 funds

Quartile rankings compare funds with peers of the same strategy and vintage; top quartile means the best 25% of that peer group.

Question 2

Why is comparing a PE fund's IRR directly with an index's annual return problematic?

  1. IRR is cash-flow weighted while index returns are time-weighted
  2. Index returns include fees
  3. IRR always equals index return
  4. Indices cannot be measured
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. IRR is cash-flow weighted while index returns are time-weighted

IRR reflects the timing and size of fund cash flows, while index returns assume a buy-and-hold investment, so a like-for-like comparison needs methods such as PME.

Question 3

Under SEBI's framework, performance benchmarks for AIFs are published by:

  1. Benchmarking agencies using data reported by AIFs
  2. Each fund manager for its own fund, using its chosen index
  3. The stock exchanges
  4. The Reserve Bank of India
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Benchmarking agencies using data reported by AIFs

SEBI's framework uses benchmarking agencies that compile data reported by AIFs to publish industry and vintage benchmarks.

Question 4

A long-short Category III fund should ideally be benchmarked against:

  1. The Sensex alone, since it is India's oldest equity benchmark
  2. Bank fixed deposit rates only
  3. A benchmark reflecting its net exposure and strategy
  4. A gold price index
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A benchmark reflecting its net exposure and strategy

A long-short fund carries lower net market exposure, so its benchmark should reflect that, or a blend of market and cash.

Question 5

Peer-group benchmarks for private funds can suffer from:

  1. Too much daily data
  2. Exchange-traded pricing that makes every fund's NAV observable daily
  3. Survivorship and self-reporting bias
  4. Mandatory government audit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Survivorship and self-reporting bias

If poorly performing funds stop reporting or disappear, peer data can be biased upward.

Question 6

The Kaplan–Schoar PME ratio is greater than 1. This indicates that the fund:

  1. Underperformed the public index on a cash-flow-matched basis
  2. Had zero returns
  3. Outperformed the public index
  4. Is a public fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Outperformed the public index

A PME ratio above 1 means the fund's distributions and value exceeded what the same cash flows would have earned in the index.

Question 7

Vintage-year benchmarking is important because:

  1. Funds of the same year faced similar conditions
  2. All vintages perform identically
  3. SEBI requires one vintage per manager
  4. It removes fee effects
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Funds of the same year faced similar conditions

Market conditions at entry strongly affect returns, so comparing funds of the same vintage is fairer.

Question 8

Which statistic best measures a Category III fund's consistency of outperformance over its benchmark?

  1. Dividend yield
  2. Information ratio
  3. Distributions to paid-in capital (DPI)
  4. Vintage year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Information ratio

The information ratio divides active return by tracking error, measuring consistency of outperformance.
