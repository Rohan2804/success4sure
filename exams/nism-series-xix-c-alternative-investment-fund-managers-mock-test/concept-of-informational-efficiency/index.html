Concept of Informational Efficiency: NISM Series XIX-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Concept of Informational Efficiency, from the NISM Series XIX-C (Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the efficient market hypothesis and its forms — weak, semi-strong and strong — and what each implies for technical analysis, fundamental analysis and insider information. It discusses market anomalies, behavioural biases, limits to arbitrage, and why private and less-researched markets may be less efficient, giving skilled managers room to add value. When revising, focus on matching evidence or strategies to the form of efficiency they test, and on why informational inefficiency is a core argument for active management in alternative assets.

Concept of Informational Efficiency questions

Question 1

If a market is weak-form efficient, which strategy should NOT consistently earn excess returns?

  1. Using inside information illegally
  2. Analysing unpublished field research
  3. Trading on past price patterns
  4. Investing in private companies
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Trading on past price patterns

Weak-form efficiency means prices reflect all past trading data, so technical analysis cannot consistently beat the market. Other information may still add value.

Question 2

Strong-form efficiency implies that:

  1. Only past prices and trading volumes are reflected in current prices
  2. Fundamental analysis always works
  3. Even private information cannot earn excess returns
  4. Markets are always mispriced
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Even private information cannot earn excess returns

Under strong-form efficiency, prices reflect all public and private information. Evidence of insider profits suggests real markets are not strong-form efficient.

Question 3

Which is a behavioural bias that may cause prices to deviate from fundamentals?

  1. Perfect information disclosure
  2. Herding by investors
  3. Zero transaction costs
  4. Rational expectations by all investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Herding by investors

Herding — following the crowd — can push prices away from fair value. The other options describe idealised efficient conditions.

Question 4

'Limits to arbitrage' explain why mispricing can persist because:

  1. Arbitrage is always riskless and free
  2. Regulators ban all arbitrage
  3. Arbitrage can be costly and risky
  4. Prices cannot move once set
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Arbitrage can be costly and risky

Short-selling constraints, funding costs and the risk that mispricing widens can deter arbitrageurs, so anomalies may persist.

Question 5

Why are small unlisted companies often considered less informationally efficient?

  1. They are required to publish daily accounts
  2. Their share prices are fixed by SEBI
  3. Few analysts cover them and information is scarce
  4. They are traded on stock exchanges continuously with many market makers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Few analysts cover them and information is scarce

Limited disclosure and coverage mean prices may not reflect all information, giving diligent investors an edge.

Question 6

A share price rises sharply on the day of a positive earnings announcement and then drifts upward for weeks. The drift is evidence against:

  1. Weak-form efficiency only
  2. Strong-form inefficiency
  3. The existence of stock markets
  4. Semi-strong form efficiency
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Semi-strong form efficiency

Prices in a semi-strong efficient market adjust quickly to public news. A slow drift after announcements is a known anomaly.

Question 7

For an AIF manager, the main practical implication of market inefficiency in private markets is that:

  1. Passive indexing is the only possible approach
  2. All managers will earn the same returns
  3. Due diligence is unnecessary
  4. Skill in sourcing and diligence can add value
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Skill in sourcing and diligence can add value

Inefficiency rewards skill in finding and analysing deals, which is why manager selection matters greatly in private markets.

Question 8

An investor consistently earns excess returns using only published financial statements. This would contradict:

  1. Weak-form efficiency only
  2. Semi-strong form efficiency
  3. The random walk of past prices only
  4. No form of efficiency
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Semi-strong form efficiency

Published statements are public information; persistent excess returns from analysing them contradict semi-strong efficiency.
