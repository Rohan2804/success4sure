Regulatory Framework: NISM Series XIX-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Regulatory Framework, from the NISM Series XIX-C (Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers SEBI's AIF Regulations and circulars from a manager's perspective: registration and eligibility, sponsor and manager obligations, continuing interest, minimum corpus and investment, investor limits, accredited investors and large value funds, investment conditions and concentration limits by category, leverage, PPM filing and changes, valuation, reporting, KMP certification, stewardship, PMLA obligations, direct plans and distribution rules, conflicts and penalties. Focus on the stable thresholds and the principles behind rules that SEBI revises frequently.

Regulatory Framework questions

Question 1

A Category II AIF has a corpus of ₹500 crore. The sponsor or manager's minimum continuing interest is:

  1. ₹12.5 crore
  2. ₹10 crore
  3. ₹5 crore
  4. ₹25 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹5 crore

For Category I and II, it is the lower of 2.5% of corpus or ₹5 crore. 2.5% of 500 = 12.5, so the requirement is ₹5 crore.

Question 2

A Category II AIF with investable funds of ₹320 crore can invest at most how much in one investee company under the general limit?

  1. ₹32 crore
  2. ₹64 crore
  3. ₹160 crore
  4. ₹80 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹80 crore

Category I and II AIFs may invest up to 25% of investable funds in one investee: 25% × 320 = ₹80 crore.

Question 3

The maximum number of investors in an AIF scheme (other than angel funds) is:

  1. 1,000
  2. 200
  3. 49
  4. No limit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 1,000

SEBI caps an AIF scheme at 1,000 investors, except angel funds, which keeps AIFs as private pools rather than public offerings.

Question 4

Which of the following must an AIF manager do under the PMLA framework?

  1. Accept cash freely
  2. Skip KYC for HNIs
  3. Share data publicly
  4. Verify investor identity and report suspicious transactions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Verify investor identity and report suspicious transactions

AIFs must perform KYC, maintain records and report suspicious transactions as reporting entities under the PMLA framework.

Question 5

Large value funds for accredited investors enjoy:

  1. Exemption from SEBI registration
  2. Certain relaxations, such as higher concentration limits
  3. Guaranteed returns
  4. Unlimited leverage for Category II schemes investing in unlisted debt
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Certain relaxations, such as higher concentration limits

SEBI provides relaxations such as higher concentration limits for large value funds of accredited investors.

Question 6

A Category II AIF wants to borrow to make investments. This is:

  1. Not allowed, except temporary borrowing within limits
  2. Allowed up to 2x NAV
  3. Allowed without limit
  4. Mandatory
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Not allowed, except temporary borrowing within limits

Category I and II AIFs may borrow only temporarily for operational shortfalls, within limits.

Question 7

Material changes to the PPM generally require:

  1. Investor consent or an exit option for dissenters
  2. Only a notice from the manager, with no exit option for investors
  3. Approval by the investee
  4. Nothing
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Investor consent or an exit option for dissenters

Material changes require investor approval as prescribed and an exit option for dissenting investors.

Question 8

The minimum investment per investor (other than accredited investors and certain employees) in an AIF is:

  1. ₹1 crore
  2. ₹25 lakh
  3. ₹50 lakh
  4. ₹10 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹1 crore

SEBI sets ₹1 crore as the minimum investment per investor, with exemptions for accredited investors and certain employees or directors.
