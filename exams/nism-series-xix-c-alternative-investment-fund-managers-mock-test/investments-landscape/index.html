Investments Landscape: NISM Series XIX-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Investments Landscape, from the NISM Series XIX-C (Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter sets the context for AIF management by surveying the investment landscape: real versus financial assets, the main asset classes, the role of financial intermediaries, the savings–investment link, nominal and real returns, and the range of investors from retail savers to institutions and family offices. It also introduces the growth of private markets alongside public markets. When revising, focus on classifying assets, computing real returns with the Fisher relationship, and understanding why different investors — with different horizons, liabilities and liquidity needs — choose different mixes of traditional and alternative assets.

Investments Landscape questions

Question 1

A fund earns a nominal return of 12% when inflation is 5%. Using the exact Fisher relationship, the real return is about:

  1. 7.00%
  2. 17.60%
  3. 5.71%
  4. 6.67%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 6.67%

Real return = 1.12 ÷ 1.05 − 1 ≈ 6.67%. Subtracting inflation directly gives the 7% approximation.

Question 2

Which investor is typically best suited to commit capital to a 10-year closed-end fund?

  1. An endowment with perpetual horizon
  2. A household saving for a car to be bought next year
  3. A corporate treasury managing its weekly payroll cash
  4. A retiree drawing monthly living expenses
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. An endowment with perpetual horizon

Endowments and similar long-horizon institutions can tolerate long lock-ups. Short-term or income-dependent investors need liquidity.

Question 3

Financial intermediaries such as banks and funds mainly help the economy by:

  1. Fixing the prices of all listed securities on the exchange every day
  2. Eliminating all investment risk for savers
  3. Replacing the need for capital markets
  4. Channelling savings to productive uses
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Channelling savings to productive uses

Intermediaries pool savings and allocate them to borrowers and businesses, transforming maturity, size and risk. They cannot remove risk entirely.

Question 4

An AIF holds the following. Which one is a financial asset (a claim on another party) and not a real asset?

  1. A warehouse used for storing grain
  2. Farmland leased to tenants
  3. A debenture of a listed company
  4. A toll road operated by a concessionaire
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A debenture of a listed company

A debenture is a claim on the issuer's cash flows, making it a financial asset. Warehouses, farmland and roads are real assets.

Question 5

Private markets have grown relative to public markets partly because:

  1. Stock exchanges have stopped approving new listings of small and mid-sized companies
  2. Companies stay private longer, funded by private capital
  3. Private investments are guaranteed by SEBI
  4. Public markets are closed to institutions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Companies stay private longer, funded by private capital

Abundant private capital lets companies scale before listing, shifting value creation into private markets. Private investments carry no regulatory guarantee.

Question 6

A family office wants to preserve wealth across generations. Compared with a young salaried saver, it can usually:

  1. Hold only cash and deposits because its size makes market investing unnecessary
  2. Avoid all diversification
  3. Take more illiquidity risk for higher returns
  4. Ignore inflation completely
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Take more illiquidity risk for higher returns

With a long horizon and large asset base, a family office can lock up part of its capital to earn the illiquidity premium. It still needs diversification and inflation protection.

Question 7

The main difference between primary and secondary markets is that in primary markets:

  1. Existing securities change hands between investors
  2. Only derivatives are traded
  3. Prices are fixed by the regulator
  4. Issuers raise fresh capital from investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Issuers raise fresh capital from investors

Primary markets involve new issues where money goes to the issuer. Secondary markets provide trading among investors.

Question 8

An investor's required return on a private fund should be higher than on a comparable listed fund mainly because of:

  1. Lower fees in private funds
  2. Illiquidity and higher uncertainty
  3. Daily liquidity in private funds
  4. Government guarantees on private funds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Illiquidity and higher uncertainty

Private funds are illiquid, opaque and costly, so investors demand a premium over comparable public investments.
