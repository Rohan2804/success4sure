Investment Strategies, Investment Process and Governance of Funds: NISM Series XIX-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Investment Strategies, Investment Process and Governance of Funds, from the NISM Series XIX-C (Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the strategies AIF managers pursue — venture capital, growth, buyouts, private credit, special situations, real estate, infrastructure and Category III trading strategies — and the investment process from sourcing and screening through due diligence, IC approval, structuring, monitoring and exit. Governance topics include the investment committee, conflict-of-interest policies, related-party approvals, allocation policy, compliance with the PPM, and the responsibilities of the manager, trustee and key personnel. Focus on strategy-specific risks and on governance practices SEBI expects.

Investment Strategies, Investment Process and Governance of Funds questions

Question 1

A leveraged buyout typically creates value through:

  1. Holding minority stakes passively and relying on the promoter to create value
  2. Investing only in listed shares
  3. Operational improvement, debt paydown and multiple expansion
  4. Avoiding any use of debt
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Operational improvement, debt paydown and multiple expansion

Buyouts acquire control, improve operations, use cash flows to repay acquisition debt and may exit at higher multiples.

Question 2

Growth equity differs from venture capital in that growth equity usually invests in:

  1. Idea-stage start-ups that are yet to generate any revenue
  2. Distressed companies
  3. Proven, revenue-generating businesses
  4. Government bonds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Proven, revenue-generating businesses

Growth equity backs established businesses needing capital to expand, typically with lower risk than early-stage VC.

Question 3

A manager's two funds both want to invest in the same deal. Good governance requires:

  1. Giving it to the fund paying more carry
  2. Letting the deal team decide informally
  3. Splitting it without any documentation
  4. Allocating per a disclosed allocation policy
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Allocating per a disclosed allocation policy

Detailed due diligence findings are presented to the investment committee for final approval, after which the deal moves to documentation and closing.

Question 4

Which step usually comes immediately before investment committee final approval?

  1. Exit
  2. Detailed due diligence
  3. Monitoring
  4. Distribution to investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Detailed due diligence

Detailed due diligence findings are presented to the IC for final approval, followed by closing.

Question 5

A private credit fund's key risk control is:

  1. Covenants and security on loans
  2. Daily share price tracking of the borrower's listed competitors
  3. Index replication
  4. Investing only in cash
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Covenants and security on loans

Covenants, collateral and regular monitoring protect a private credit fund's lenders against deterioration in the borrower's business.

Question 6

What responsibility do members of an AIF's investment committee who are not employees of the manager carry for decisions they approve?

  1. Have no responsibility at all, as they are only advisers to the manager
  2. Must be SEBI employees
  3. Share responsibility for decisions they approve
  4. Cannot vote
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Share responsibility for decisions they approve

SEBI holds IC members, including external ones, responsible for decisions they take, subject to the regulations.

Question 7

A manager plans to invest in a company where a sponsor affiliate is a promoter. This should be:

  1. Disclosed and approved by investors as required
  2. Done quietly to save time
  3. Prohibited for all AIFs in every case
  4. Approved only by the affiliate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Disclosed and approved by investors as required

Investments involving the sponsor's associates are related-party transactions requiring disclosure and investor approval under SEBI's rules.

Question 8

An event-driven strategy in Category III typically profits from:

  1. Corporate actions such as mergers and demergers
  2. Long-term holding of rent-yielding real estate in metro cities
  3. Collecting coupons on government bonds
  4. Index tracking
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Corporate actions such as mergers and demergers

Event-driven funds seek gains from price effects of corporate events such as mergers, demergers, buybacks and restructurings.
