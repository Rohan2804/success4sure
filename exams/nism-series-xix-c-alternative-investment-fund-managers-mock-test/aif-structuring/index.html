AIF Structuring: NISM Series XIX-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on AIF Structuring, from the NISM Series XIX-C (Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the legal and commercial structuring of AIFs. It explains trust, company and LLP forms, documents such as the trust deed, contribution agreement and investment management agreement, unit classes and differential rights, onshore and offshore structures, feeder and co-investment vehicles, FEMA treatment of downstream investment, and the tax and regulatory considerations that drive structuring decisions. Focus on why a particular structure is chosen, how foreign capital is brought in, and how unit classes or co-investment vehicles must be disclosed and kept within SEBI's framework.

AIF Structuring questions

Question 1

An AIF wants to admit foreign investors while keeping its downstream investments classified as domestic under FEMA. The key requirement is that:

  1. Foreign investors together hold less than 10% of the scheme's units
  2. It invests only in listed securities
  3. Its sponsor and manager are Indian-owned and controlled
  4. It registers as an FPI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Its sponsor and manager are Indian-owned and controlled

Downstream investment is treated as domestic when the sponsor and manager are owned and controlled by resident Indian citizens, regardless of foreign LP share.

Question 2

A co-investment vehicle allows investors to:

  1. Avoid all fund fees permanently on their main fund commitment
  2. Manage the fund
  3. Redeem units early
  4. Invest more in specific deals alongside the fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Invest more in specific deals alongside the fund

Co-investment vehicles let selected investors take extra exposure in particular investee companies, under terms set within SEBI's framework.

Question 3

Why might a fund choose a company structure despite trusts being more common?

  1. Companies pay no tax
  2. Companies need no SEBI registration
  3. Companies can raise from retail investors freely
  4. Some investors prefer corporate governance
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Some investors prefer corporate governance

Some investors prefer board governance and familiarity of corporate forms, though tax treatment and flexibility differ.

Question 4

The contribution agreement in a trust-based AIF mainly governs:

  1. The custodian's fees
  2. The investor's commitment and drawdowns
  3. The SEBI registration process
  4. The investee company's shareholding pattern after each investment
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The investor's commitment and drawdowns

It records each investor's commitment, drawdown mechanics, default consequences and other terms.

Question 5

Differential rights for certain investors in an AIF are allowed when:

  1. They follow SEBI's framework and do not harm others
  2. They are kept secret from SEBI
  3. They guarantee returns to those investors
  4. They are approved only by the manager
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. They follow SEBI's framework and do not harm others

SEBI permits differential rights within its framework, with disclosure, provided other investors' rights are not adversely affected.

Question 6

An offshore feeder fund investing into an Indian AIF helps foreign investors mainly by:

  1. Avoiding Indian KYC norms
  2. Pooling them in a familiar vehicle
  3. Guaranteeing a currency hedge on all rupee-denominated investments
  4. Allowing daily redemptions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Pooling them in a familiar vehicle

Feeders pool foreign investors in a jurisdiction and vehicle they are comfortable with, simplifying participation.

Question 7

In a trust-based AIF, the trust deed is executed between:

  1. The investor and the custodian
  2. The manager and the investee
  3. The settlor and the trustee
  4. SEBI and the sponsor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The settlor and the trustee

The trust deed is executed by the settlor and trustee, creating the trust and setting out its objects.

Question 8

Separate unit classes for the manager team typically exist to:

  1. Avoid reporting the manager team's holdings to SEBI and the trustee
  2. Increase leverage
  3. Allocate carry or fee waivers
  4. Allow early redemption
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Allocate carry or fee waivers

Team classes usually carry carry entitlements and fee waivers, disclosed in the PPM.
