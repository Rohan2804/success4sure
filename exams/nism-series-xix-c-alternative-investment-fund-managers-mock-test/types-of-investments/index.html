Types of Investments: NISM Series XIX-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Types of Investments, from the NISM Series XIX-C (Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter describes the instruments and asset types that AIF managers use: equity and preference shares, convertible instruments, debt and structured credit, mezzanine and venture debt, real estate and infrastructure, commodities and derivatives. It explains the capital structure of a company and how claims rank, the features of convertible and hybrid instruments, and how instrument design affects risk and return. Focus on where each instrument sits in the capital structure, how conversion and liquidation rights work, and which instruments suit different strategies such as venture, buyout, credit and special situations.

Types of Investments questions

Question 1

In a liquidation, which claim is paid first among these?

  1. Equity shares
  2. Senior secured debt
  3. Preference shares
  4. Mezzanine debt that is subordinated to senior lenders
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Senior secured debt

Senior secured lenders rank ahead of subordinated debt, preference and equity holders, which is why their returns are lower.

Question 2

A compulsorily convertible preference share (CCPS) is popular with Indian PE investors mainly because it:

  1. Offers preference rights and later converts to equity
  2. Pays a fixed coupon that must be paid in cash even in years when the company makes losses
  3. Can never be converted into equity shares
  4. Carries no rights in a liquidation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Offers preference rights and later converts to equity

CCPS offers preferential rights, such as liquidation preference, and converts into equity, which is often favoured under FEMA for foreign investors.

Question 3

Venture debt is typically provided to:

  1. Mature companies with strong cash flows only
  2. Governments for infrastructure
  3. Retail investors for personal loans
  4. VC-backed start-ups to extend runway
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. VC-backed start-ups to extend runway

Venture debt supplements equity in VC-funded companies, often with warrants, helping them extend runway without immediate dilution.

Question 4

Which instrument gives its holder the right but not the obligation to buy shares at a fixed price in future?

  1. A warrant
  2. A non-convertible debenture
  3. A commercial paper
  4. A fixed deposit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A warrant

A warrant is an option to buy shares at a set price. NCDs, CP and deposits are plain debt instruments.

Question 5

Infrastructure investments are attractive to long-term investors mainly because they often offer:

  1. Very short holding periods with quick exits through the stock market
  2. Daily liquidity on stock exchanges
  3. Stable, long-dated, sometimes inflation-linked cash flows
  4. No exposure to regulatory risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Stable, long-dated, sometimes inflation-linked cash flows

Infrastructure assets such as roads and power often have long concessions and contracted revenues, sometimes indexed to inflation. They carry regulatory and construction risk.

Question 6

A 'payment-in-kind' (PIK) feature in a mezzanine loan means that:

  1. Interest is paid in commodities
  2. The loan is repaid in shares at once
  3. Interest is added to principal instead of paid in cash
  4. No interest is ever charged, since the lender is repaid only through equity warrants
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Interest is added to principal instead of paid in cash

PIK interest accrues to the principal, conserving the borrower's cash but increasing the lender's risk and final payoff.

Question 7

Which investment type is most associated with special situation funds?

  1. Seed-stage start-ups that are still developing their first product
  2. Listed large-cap blue chips
  3. Government treasury bills
  4. Stressed and distressed assets
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Stressed and distressed assets

Special situation funds invest in stressed loans and securities of companies under distress or restructuring.

Question 8

A real estate debt fund lending to developers faces which key risk?

  1. Project delays and developer default
  2. Exchange-rate risk on rupee loans made to Indian developers
  3. Dividend cut by listed companies
  4. Index rebalancing risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Project delays and developer default

Real estate lenders depend on project completion and sales; delays, approvals and developer default are the key risks.
