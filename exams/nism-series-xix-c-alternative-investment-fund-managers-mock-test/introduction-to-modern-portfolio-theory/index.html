Introduction to Modern Portfolio Theory: NISM Series XIX-C practice questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces Markowitz's modern portfolio theory. It covers expected return and risk of a portfolio, variance and standard deviation, covariance and correlation, the benefit of diversification, the efficient frontier, and how investor preferences determine the optimal portfolio. It also discusses the limits of MPT for alternative assets, such as smoothed returns and non-normal distributions. Practise computing portfolio return and standard deviation for two assets, and understand how correlation changes diversification benefits.

Introduction to Modern Portfolio Theory questions

Question 1

A portfolio has 60% in an asset with expected return 14% and 40% in one with 8%. The expected portfolio return is:

  1. 11.0%
  2. 10.4%
  3. 11.6%
  4. 22.0%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 11.6%

Expected return = 0.6 × 14 + 0.4 × 8 = 8.4 + 3.2 = 11.6%. 11% is a simple average ignoring weights.

Question 2

Using the same weights (60:40), if the assets' standard deviations are 20% and 10% and correlation is zero, the portfolio standard deviation is about:

  1. 16.0%
  2. 12.6%
  3. 14.0%
  4. 10.0%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 12.6%

σ = √(0.6²×0.2² + 0.4²×0.1²) = √(0.0144 + 0.0016) ≈ 12.6%. 16% is the weighted average, which applies only when correlation is +1.

Question 3

Two assets with standard deviations of 20% and 10% have correlation of −1. A 50:50 portfolio has standard deviation of:

  1. 15%
  2. 5%
  3. 0%
  4. 10%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 5%

With perfect negative correlation, σ = |0.5×20 − 0.5×10| = 5%. A different weighting (1/3 : 2/3) could reduce it to zero.

Question 4

Diversification is most effective when assets have:

  1. Perfect positive correlation
  2. Identical returns at all times
  3. Low or negative correlation
  4. The same issuer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Low or negative correlation

Lower correlation means assets do not move together, so combining them reduces portfolio risk.

Question 5

Which risk can be reduced by diversification?

  1. Systematic market risk
  2. Risk from economy-wide recessions
  3. Unsystematic (company-specific) risk
  4. Changes in the general level of interest rates
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Unsystematic (company-specific) risk

Company-specific risk can be diversified away. Market-wide risks affect all assets and remain.

Question 6

A portfolio lies below the efficient frontier. This means:

  1. It is the optimal portfolio for all investors, whatever their risk tolerance
  2. Another portfolio offers more return for the same risk
  3. It has zero risk
  4. It cannot be improved
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Another portfolio offers more return for the same risk

Portfolios below the frontier are inefficient; one with higher return at the same risk exists.

Question 7

A key limitation of MPT when applied to private equity is that:

  1. Private equity returns are reported daily
  2. Private equity has no correlation data at all, so no optimisation is possible
  3. MPT works only for bonds
  4. Reported returns are smoothed, understating risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Reported returns are smoothed, understating risk

Appraisal-based valuations smooth returns, lowering measured volatility and correlation, so optimisers may over-allocate to private assets.

Question 8

The optimal portfolio for an investor on the efficient frontier depends on:

  1. The number of stocks listed on the exchange
  2. The custodian of the portfolio
  3. The fund's legal structure
  4. Her risk preference
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Her risk preference

The investor's risk tolerance determines which efficient portfolio she chooses — the tangency with her indifference curve.
