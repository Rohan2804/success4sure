Fund Due Diligence – Investor Perspective: NISM Series XIX-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Fund Due Diligence – Investor Perspective, from the NISM Series XIX-C (Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how investors assess AIF managers before committing. It explains investment due diligence (team, strategy, track record and attribution, portfolio construction, pipeline), operational due diligence (valuation, custody, compliance, cyber security, service providers), alignment of interest, terms and fees, ESG and stewardship considerations, and reference checks. It highlights red flags such as unexplained team departures, selective track records and weak controls. Focus on the evidence investors should seek and how to interpret gross versus net returns and realised versus unrealised performance.

Fund Due Diligence – Investor Perspective questions

Question 1

When reviewing a track record, an investor should prefer evidence from:

  1. Unrealised marks set by the manager
  2. Projected returns in marketing material
  3. Industry averages
  4. Realised exits with cash returned
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Realised exits with cash returned

Realised returns are confirmed by cash, while unrealised marks depend on valuation judgement.

Question 2

Which finding in operational due diligence is a serious concern?

  1. Use of a reputed auditor
  2. A documented valuation policy
  3. A dedicated compliance officer who reports to the board of the manager
  4. No independent reconciliation of positions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. No independent reconciliation of positions

Without independent reconciliation of positions with the custodian and brokers, errors or misappropriation may go undetected.

Question 3

An investor asks for deal-level attribution of a manager's past funds. The aim is to learn:

  1. The location and size of the manager's offices across cities
  2. The fund's legal form
  3. Which professionals generated the returns
  4. The manager's marketing costs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Which professionals generated the returns

Attribution shows who led successful deals and whether they remain with the team.

Question 4

A manager's previous fund shows a high IRR but low DPI after eight years. The investor should:

  1. Assume the fund is top quartile
  2. Ignore DPI as irrelevant
  3. Commit immediately
  4. Probe why little cash has been returned
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Probe why little cash has been returned

Low DPI late in a fund's life means value is mostly unrealised, so exit prospects and valuations need scrutiny.

Question 5

Which is the strongest sign of alignment in a fund?

  1. High management fees charged on total commitments for the fund's full life
  2. A significant GP commitment from personal funds
  3. Guaranteed carry
  4. No clawback provision
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A significant GP commitment from personal funds

Personal capital at risk aligns the manager's outcome with investors'; high fees and guaranteed carry weaken alignment.

Question 6

ESG due diligence on a manager mainly examines:

  1. How environmental, social and governance risks are handled
  2. The manager's personal tax returns
  3. Daily share prices
  4. The fund's NAV only
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. How environmental, social and governance risks are handled

ESG diligence checks how the manager identifies and manages ESG risks in portfolio companies.

Question 7

A manager shows only its three best deals in a pitch. This is a concern about:

  1. Over-diversification across too many sectors in the past funds
  2. High DPI
  3. Low fees
  4. Selective presentation of track record
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Selective presentation of track record

Cherry-picking deals misrepresents overall performance; investors should ask for the full record.

Question 8

Why do investors check a manager's key-person and succession plans?

  1. SEBI fixes the salaries of key persons named in the PPM
  2. Results may depend on a few individuals
  3. It affects tax rates
  4. It sets the hurdle rate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Results may depend on a few individuals

Departure of key people can disrupt the fund, so succession planning and key-person clauses are important.
