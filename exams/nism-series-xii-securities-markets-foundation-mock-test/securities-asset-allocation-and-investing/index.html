Securities, Asset Allocation and Investing: NISM Series XII practice questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the types of securities available to investors, including equity, preference shares, bonds, hybrids and mutual fund units, along with the risks and returns of each. It introduces asset allocation, diversification and the basics of investing, such as return measures, the power of compounding, inflation and investment approaches like fundamental and technical analysis. When revising, compare securities by risk and return, and understand why asset allocation matters more than picking individual securities.

Securities, Asset Allocation and Investing questions

Question 1

An investor buys a share at ₹200 and sells it after a year at ₹230, receiving no dividend. What is her return?

  1. 15%
  2. 30%
  3. 13%
  4. 23%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 15%

Return = (230 − 200) ÷ 200 = 15%. 13% wrongly divides the gain by the selling price, and 30% is the rupee gain, not a percentage.

Question 2

Which security typically offers the highest potential return along with the highest risk?

  1. Government treasury bills
  2. Bank fixed deposits
  3. Equity shares
  4. AAA-rated corporate bonds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Equity shares

Equity holders are residual claimants, so returns are uncertain but potentially high. Treasury bills, deposits and top-rated bonds pay fixed amounts with lower risk.

Question 3

A company has a P/E ratio of 25 and EPS of ₹16. What is its share price?

  1. ₹41
  2. ₹1.56
  3. ₹250
  4. ₹400
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹400

Price = P/E × EPS = 25 × 16 = ₹400. The other options add the numbers or divide them, which misapplies the definition of the P/E ratio.

Question 4

Why does inflation matter to investors?

  1. It increases the face value of bonds
  2. It reduces the real value of returns
  3. It guarantees higher share prices
  4. It has no effect on savings
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. It reduces the real value of returns

Inflation erodes purchasing power, so returns must be judged after inflation. A deposit earning less than inflation loses real value.

Question 5

Technical analysis mainly studies:

  1. A company's balance sheet
  2. Past prices and volumes
  3. Management quality
  4. Industry growth prospects
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Past prices and volumes

Technical analysts study price and volume charts to identify trends and patterns. Fundamental analysts examine financial statements, management and industry prospects.

Question 6

An investor with a 3-year goal and low risk appetite should give most weight to:

  1. Debt instruments
  2. Small-cap shares
  3. Equity derivatives
  4. Sector funds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Debt instruments

Short horizons and low risk tolerance call for stable instruments such as deposits and debt funds. Small caps, derivatives and sector funds are volatile.

Question 7

₹1 lakh invested at 10% compounded annually becomes how much after 3 years?

  1. ₹1,30,000
  2. ₹1,10,000
  3. ₹1,33,100
  4. ₹1,33,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹1,33,100

FV = 1,00,000 × 1.1³ = ₹1,33,100. ₹1,30,000 is the simple-interest value, which ignores interest earned on interest.

Question 8

A bond's coupon rate is:

  1. The price at which it trades
  2. Its yield to maturity
  3. The rating given by an agency
  4. The interest rate on its face value
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The interest rate on its face value

The coupon rate is the annual interest paid as a percentage of face value. YTM depends on the market price, and ratings measure credit quality.
