Mutual Funds: NISM Series XII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Mutual Funds, from the NISM Series XII (Securities Markets Foundation) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces mutual funds for securities market learners: the concept of pooling, the trust structure, NAV, types of schemes (equity, debt, hybrid, index and ETFs), open and closed-ended schemes, SIPs, costs such as expense ratio and exit loads, and investor rights. When revising, compute NAV and units, understand how passive and active funds differ, and learn how to choose a scheme based on goals, horizon and risk shown in the riskometer.

Mutual Funds questions

Question 1

An investor invests ₹10,000 in a scheme at a NAV of ₹25. How many units does she get, ignoring charges?

  1. 250 units
  2. 400 units
  3. 2,500 units
  4. 40 units
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 400 units

Units = amount ÷ NAV = 10,000 ÷ 25 = 400. The other options misplace the decimal or divide the wrong way.

Question 2

An exchange traded fund differs from a regular open-ended index fund because ETF units:

  1. Are bought only from the AMC at day-end NAV
  2. Carry guaranteed returns
  3. Have no expense ratio at all
  4. Trade on a stock exchange during market hours
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Trade on a stock exchange during market hours

ETF units are listed and traded on exchanges at market prices throughout the day. Open-ended index fund units are bought and redeemed with the AMC at NAV.

Question 3

Who appoints the asset management company of a mutual fund and oversees it on behalf of unitholders?

  1. The trustees
  2. The custodian
  3. The registrar
  4. The distributor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The trustees

Trustees protect unitholders' interests, appoint the AMC with SEBI approval and monitor its compliance. Custodians hold assets and registrars maintain records.

Question 4

A closed-ended scheme:

  1. Allows purchases and redemptions daily with the AMC
  2. Has no maturity date at all
  3. Has a fixed maturity and lists units
  4. Can be bought only by companies
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Has a fixed maturity and lists units

Closed-ended schemes accept money only during the NFO, have a fixed term and list units on exchanges for liquidity. Open-ended schemes allow continuous transactions.

Question 5

Which fund type aims to match an index's return rather than beat it?

  1. Small-cap fund
  2. Index fund
  3. Focused fund
  4. Value fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Index fund

Index funds are passively managed and replicate an index, aiming for returns close to it at low cost. Active funds try to outperform their benchmarks.

Question 6

A balanced advantage fund mainly aims to:

  1. Hold only government bonds
  2. Invest only in gold
  3. Track a single sector index
  4. Shift between equity and debt dynamically
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Shift between equity and debt dynamically

Balanced advantage (dynamic asset allocation) funds change their equity and debt mix based on market conditions or valuation models.

Question 7

Which statement about mutual fund returns is correct?

  1. They are not guaranteed
  2. They are guaranteed by SEBI
  3. They are fixed at the time of NFO
  4. They are insured by DICGC
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. They are not guaranteed

Mutual fund returns depend on the performance of underlying investments and are not guaranteed. DICGC insurance covers bank deposits, not fund units.

Question 8

A scheme's NAV fell from ₹40 to ₹36 over a year with no payouts. The return is:

  1. −4%
  2. −11.1%
  3. −10%
  4. +10%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. −10%

Return = (36 − 40) ÷ 40 = −10%. −11.1% divides by the ending NAV, and −4% is the rupee change per unit.
