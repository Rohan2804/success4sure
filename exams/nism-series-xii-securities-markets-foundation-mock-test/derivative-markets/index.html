Derivative Markets: NISM Series XII practice questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces derivatives traded in Indian markets: forwards, futures and options, their underlying assets (equity, indices, currencies, commodities and interest rates), key terms such as lot size, expiry, premium, strike and open interest, and how they are used for hedging, speculation and arbitrage. It also covers margins and the risks of leverage. When revising, compute simple futures and option payoffs, understand settlement types, and learn why derivatives can magnify both gains and losses.

Derivative Markets questions

Question 1

A trader buys a call with a strike of ₹200 for a premium of ₹8. At expiry the stock is at ₹215. What is the net profit per share?

  1. ₹15
  2. ₹23
  3. ₹7
  4. ₹8
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹7

Payoff = 215 − 200 = ₹15; net profit = 15 − 8 = ₹7. ₹15 ignores the premium paid.

Question 2

An investor buys one futures lot of 500 shares at ₹300 and sells it at ₹310. Ignoring costs, her profit is:

  1. ₹5,000
  2. ₹10
  3. ₹1,500
  4. ₹50,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹5,000

Profit = (310 − 300) × 500 = ₹5,000. ₹10 is the profit per share; multiplying by lot size gives the total.

Question 3

The maximum loss for an option buyer is:

  1. Unlimited
  2. The premium paid
  3. The strike price
  4. Twice the premium
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The premium paid

An option buyer can let the option lapse, so the most he can lose is the premium. Option sellers face larger potential losses.

Question 4

A put option buyer profits when the underlying price:

  1. Rises above the strike price
  2. Stays exactly at the strike
  3. Rises above strike plus premium
  4. Falls below strike minus premium
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Falls below strike minus premium

A put gains when the underlying falls; net profit starts below strike minus premium. Rising prices hurt a put buyer.

Question 5

Index futures in India are settled at expiry by:

  1. Cash settlement
  2. Physical delivery of all index stocks
  3. Delivery of an ETF
  4. Rolling over automatically
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Cash settlement

An index cannot be delivered, so index futures and options are cash settled based on the final settlement price.

Question 6

A rise in open interest along with rising prices usually suggests:

  1. Short sellers closing positions
  2. No trading activity
  3. New long positions being added
  4. The contract has expired
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. New long positions being added

Rising open interest with rising prices typically indicates fresh buying. Short covering usually reduces open interest while prices rise.

Question 7

Why do derivatives carry high risk for small investors?

  1. They are not regulated by SEBI
  2. Their prices never change
  3. They cannot be sold before expiry
  4. Leverage magnifies losses
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Leverage magnifies losses

Derivatives require only margin for large exposure, so small price moves cause large losses relative to capital. They are regulated and can be closed before expiry.

Question 8

A company that will import goods in three months buys currency futures to fix its rupee cost. It is:

  1. Speculating
  2. Hedging
  3. Arbitraging
  4. Market making
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Hedging

Using derivatives to offset an existing business exposure is hedging. Speculation takes positions without an underlying exposure.
