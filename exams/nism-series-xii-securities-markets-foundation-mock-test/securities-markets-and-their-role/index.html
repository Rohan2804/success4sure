What this chapter covers

This chapter explains why securities markets exist and how they work: channelling savings into investment, price discovery, liquidity and risk sharing. It introduces the main participants and intermediaries, including issuers, investors, stock exchanges, clearing corporations, depositories, brokers and depository participants, and the regulators SEBI and RBI. When revising, be clear about who does what in the market, the difference between primary and secondary markets, and how market infrastructure protects investors.

Securities Markets and Their Role questions

Question 1

Which of the following is a market infrastructure institution in the Indian securities market?

  1. A mutual fund distributor
  2. An investment adviser
  3. A listed company
  4. A stock exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A stock exchange

Stock exchanges, clearing corporations and depositories are market infrastructure institutions that provide core trading, clearing and holding systems. Distributors, advisers and issuers use this infrastructure.

Question 2

How do securities markets help the economy grow?

  1. By moving savings to productive businesses
  2. By fixing the prices of all goods and services
  3. By printing money for companies
  4. By guaranteeing returns to all savers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. By moving savings to productive businesses

Securities markets let companies and governments raise funds from savers for productive investment. They do not fix goods prices, print money or guarantee returns.

Question 3

Liquidity in a securities market means that investors can:

  1. Get guaranteed profits on every trade
  2. Buy or sell quickly at fair prices
  3. Hold securities only in physical form
  4. Avoid paying any transaction costs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Buy or sell quickly at fair prices

A liquid market has many buyers and sellers, so trades can be executed quickly without large price impact. Liquidity does not guarantee profits or remove costs.

Question 4

A depository participant (DP) acts as:

  1. The regulator of stock exchanges
  2. The issuer of new shares
  3. The investor's link to the depository
  4. A bank that lends to listed companies
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The investor's link to the depository

DPs, such as banks and brokers, open demat accounts and offer depository services as agents of NSDL or CDSL. SEBI is the regulator and companies are issuers.

Question 5

Which regulator oversees stock brokers and stock exchanges in India?

  1. RBI
  2. SEBI
  3. IRDAI
  4. PFRDA
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. SEBI

SEBI regulates stock exchanges, brokers, depositories and other securities market intermediaries. RBI regulates banks and money markets, IRDAI insurance and PFRDA pensions.

Question 6

Investors in the securities market include:

  1. Only banks and insurance companies
  2. Only the central government
  3. Only listed companies
  4. Individuals, institutions and foreign investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Individuals, institutions and foreign investors

Investors range from retail individuals and HUFs to mutual funds, insurers, pension funds and foreign portfolio investors. Diversity of participants adds depth to the market.

Question 7

Price discovery in the secondary market happens through:

  1. A price set by SEBI every morning
  2. The issuer announcing a daily price
  3. Interaction of buy and sell orders
  4. The depository fixing a price
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Interaction of buy and sell orders

Prices emerge from the matching of many buy and sell orders on the exchange, reflecting available information and demand and supply. No authority fixes daily prices.

Question 8

Which is an example of a financial security?

  1. A corporate bond
  2. A residential flat
  3. A tonne of wheat
  4. A painting
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A corporate bond

Securities are tradable financial instruments such as shares, bonds and units. Flats, commodities and art are physical assets, not securities.
