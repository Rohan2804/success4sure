Free NISM Series XII practice questions

Question 1 · Securities Markets and Their Role

Which regulator is primarily responsible for the government securities and money markets?

  1. RBI
  2. SEBI
  3. IRDAI
  4. PFRDA
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. RBI

The RBI regulates government securities, money markets and related instruments; SEBI regulates the broader securities market.

Question 2 · Securities, Asset Allocation and Investing

One advantage of debt financing over equity financing for an issuer is that

  1. Interest need not be paid in losses
  2. Debt never has to be repaid
  3. Interest is tax-deductible and ownership is not diluted
  4. It requires no credit rating ever
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Interest is tax-deductible and ownership is not diluted

Debt keeps ownership intact and interest reduces taxable profit, though it creates fixed payment obligations.

Question 3 · Primary Markets

A Follow-on Public Offer (FPO) is made by

  1. A company that is already listed
  2. A company making its first public issue
  3. A mutual fund launching a scheme
  4. The RBI selling treasury bills
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A company that is already listed

An FPO is a further public issue by a company whose shares are already listed.

Question 4 · Secondary Markets

An order to buy a share at ₹500 or lower is a

  1. Market order
  2. Limit order
  3. Stop-loss order
  4. Immediate-or-cancel market order
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Limit order

A limit order specifies the worst price the trader will accept; a market order executes at the best available price.

Question 5 · Mutual Funds

A passively managed mutual fund

  1. Picks stocks to beat the market
  2. Guarantees returns
  3. Mirrors an index instead of selecting securities on the manager's judgement
  4. Invests only in unlisted firms
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Mirrors an index instead of selecting securities on the manager's judgement

Passive funds such as index funds and ETFs replicate an index, usually at lower cost.

Question 6 · Derivative Markets

How do futures differ from forwards?

  1. Futures are customised; forwards are standardised
  2. Futures carry no margin; forwards do
  3. Only forwards are marked to market
  4. Futures are standardised and exchange-traded; forwards are customised OTC contracts
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Futures are standardised and exchange-traded; forwards are customised OTC contracts

Futures have standard terms, trade on exchanges and are cleared, while forwards are bilateral and tailor-made.

Question 7 · Securities Markets and Their Role

Which entity holds investors' securities in electronic (dematerialised) form?

  1. Stock exchange
  2. Merchant banker
  3. Credit rating agency
  4. Depository
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Depository

Depositories (NSDL and CDSL) maintain securities in electronic form, accessed through depository participants.

Question 8 · Securities, Asset Allocation and Investing

A debenture that can be converted into equity shares at a later date is an example of a

  1. Pure equity instrument
  2. Money market instrument
  3. Hybrid instrument
  4. Derivative contract
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Hybrid instrument

Convertible debentures combine features of debt and equity, making them hybrid instruments.

Question 9 · Primary Markets

In a book-built issue, the cap of the price band can be at most what percentage of the floor price?

  1. 120%
  2. 105%
  3. 110%
  4. 150%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 120%

SEBI requires the cap to be at least 105% and at most 120% of the floor price.

Question 10 · Secondary Markets

An investor holding a share bought at ₹300 wants to cap his loss if it falls to ₹270. Which order should he place?

  1. A limit buy order at ₹270
  2. A market buy order
  3. A limit sell order at ₹330
  4. A stop-loss sell order with a trigger at ₹270
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A stop-loss sell order with a trigger at ₹270

A stop-loss order activates when the trigger price is hit, limiting further losses.

Question 11 · Mutual Funds

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) involves

  1. Investing a fixed amount at regular intervals
  2. Investing a lump sum only once
  3. Withdrawing a fixed amount regularly
  4. Investing only when markets fall
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Investing a fixed amount at regular intervals

SIPs invest fixed amounts periodically, bringing discipline and rupee cost averaging.

Question 12 · Derivative Markets

A call option gives the buyer the

  1. Obligation to buy the underlying
  2. Right, but not the obligation, to buy the underlying at the strike price
  3. Right to sell the underlying
  4. Obligation to sell the underlying
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Right, but not the obligation, to buy the underlying at the strike price

A call buyer pays a premium for the right to buy; the seller bears the obligation if exercised.

Question 13 · Securities Markets and Their Role

By acting as counterparty to every trade through novation, a clearing corporation

  1. Sets the prices of securities
  2. Approves IPO prospectuses
  3. Guarantees settlement and removes counterparty risk between trading members
  4. Provides loans to investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Guarantees settlement and removes counterparty risk between trading members

Novation makes the clearing corporation the buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer, guaranteeing settlement.

Question 14 · Securities, Asset Allocation and Investing

Compared with equity shareholders, preference shareholders

  1. Have priority in receiving dividends and capital repayment
  2. Always have full voting rights
  3. Receive interest that is tax-deductible for the company
  4. Rank after equity shareholders in liquidation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Have priority in receiving dividends and capital repayment

Preference shares rank ahead of equity for dividends and repayment of capital on winding up.

Question 15 · Primary Markets

In a public issue, a retail individual investor is one who applies for shares worth up to

  1. ₹50,000
  2. ₹5 lakh
  3. ₹10 lakh
  4. ₹2 lakh
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹2 lakh

Applications by individuals up to ₹2 lakh fall in the retail individual investor category.
