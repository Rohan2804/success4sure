Secondary Markets: NISM Series XII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Secondary Markets, from the NISM Series XII (Securities Markets Foundation) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how listed securities are traded: stock exchanges, trading members, order types, the trading session, market and limit orders, circuit filters, clearing and settlement, margins, contract notes and investor protection. When revising, learn the trading cycle from order to settlement, how different orders behave, why margins are collected, and the protections available to investors, including grievance redressal and investor protection funds. Settlement timelines and charges change over time, so focus on the process.

Secondary Markets questions

Question 1

An investor places a market order to buy 100 shares. This order will:

  1. Execute only at a price he specifies
  2. Execute at the best available price
  3. Remain pending until the next day
  4. Be cancelled if the price moves
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Execute at the best available price

A market order executes immediately at the best price available in the order book. A limit order executes only at the specified price or better.

Question 2

Pay-out in the settlement process means:

  1. Investors pay money to the exchange
  2. Brokers collect margins from clients
  3. The exchange cancels unsettled trades
  4. Buyers get securities and sellers get funds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Buyers get securities and sellers get funds

Pay-out is when the clearing corporation delivers securities to buyers and funds to sellers. Pay-in is when buyers pay funds and sellers deliver securities.

Question 3

A price band on an individual stock limits:

  1. How far its price can move in a day
  2. The number of shares that can be bought
  3. The brokerage that can be charged
  4. The dividend the company can pay
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. How far its price can move in a day

Price bands or circuit filters restrict the daily price range of a stock to curb excessive volatility. They do not limit quantities, brokerage or dividends.

Question 4

An investor buys 50 shares at ₹400 and sells them at ₹380. Ignoring charges, his loss is:

  1. ₹20
  2. ₹2,000
  3. ₹1,000
  4. ₹500
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹1,000

Loss = (400 − 380) × 50 = ₹1,000. ₹20 is the loss per share, not the total.

Question 5

Why should an investor check the contract note received from his broker?

  1. To verify trade details and charges
  2. To find the next day's market trend
  3. To claim dividends from the company
  4. To change his demat account number
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. To verify trade details and charges

The contract note confirms price, quantity, time and charges for each trade, helping investors detect errors or unauthorised trades quickly.

Question 6

Which service lets investors file and track complaints against listed companies and intermediaries online?

  1. RBI Retail Direct
  2. NSDL e-voting
  3. SEBI SCORES
  4. AMFI's NAV page
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. SEBI SCORES

SCORES is SEBI's online complaint redress system, linked to market-wide dispute resolution. Retail Direct is for G-secs, e-voting for shareholder resolutions and AMFI's page for NAVs.

Question 7

A stop-loss sell order is typically used by an investor who:

  1. Wants to buy only when prices fall
  2. Wants to limit losses if prices fall
  3. Wants to ensure the highest selling price
  4. Wants to avoid any execution at all
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Wants to limit losses if prices fall

A stop-loss sell order becomes active when the price falls to a trigger, helping cap losses on a holding. It does not guarantee the best price.

Question 8

The role of the clearing corporation in the secondary market is to:

  1. List new companies on the exchange
  2. Hold investors' securities in demat form
  3. Fix brokerage rates for all brokers
  4. Guarantee settlement of trades
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Guarantee settlement of trades

The clearing corporation clears and settles trades and guarantees settlement through novation, removing counterparty risk. Depositories hold securities.
