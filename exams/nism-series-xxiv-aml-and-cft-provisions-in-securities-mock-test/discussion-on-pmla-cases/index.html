Discussion on PMLA Cases: NISM Series XXIV practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Discussion on PMLA Cases, from the NISM Series XXIV (AML and CFT Provisions in Securities Markets) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter discusses important judicial decisions and enforcement cases under the PMLA and their lessons for compliance. It includes the Supreme Court's decision in Vijay Madanlal Choudhary v. Union of India (2022) on the scope of money laundering, ECIR, Section 50 statements, burden of proof and bail, later rulings on arrest procedure and the link with scheduled offences, and illustrative cases involving market manipulation, shell companies and accommodation entries. Focus on what the rulings mean for reporting entities and investigators.

Discussion on PMLA Cases questions

Question 1

In Pankaj Bansal v. Union of India (2023), the Supreme Court held that at the time of arrest under the PMLA, the ED must:

  1. Obtain written consent of the accused person before the arrest
  2. Publish the arrest on its website before informing the person
  3. Wait until the scheduled offence trial is completely over
  4. Furnish the grounds of arrest to the arrested person in writing
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Furnish the grounds of arrest to the arrested person in writing

The Court held that grounds of arrest must be supplied in writing to give effect to the constitutional right to be informed, enabling the arrested person to seek bail or legal remedy.

Question 2

In Vijay Madanlal Choudhary (2022), the Supreme Court held that money laundering under Section 3:

  1. Applies only if the person is caught depositing cash at a bank
  2. Covers any process or activity connected with proceeds of crime
  3. Requires all of placement, layering and integration to be proved
  4. Does not cover concealment or possession of proceeds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Covers any process or activity connected with proceeds of crime

The Court read Section 3 broadly: concealment, possession, acquisition, use or projecting proceeds as untainted are each independent activities that can constitute the offence.

Question 3

A key lesson from cases involving shell companies used for accommodation entries is that intermediaries should:

  1. Accept any company that has a valid PAN and incorporation certificate
  2. Look beyond paperwork to the company's real activity and owners
  3. Treat all newly incorporated companies as low risk
  4. Avoid checking directors, since that is the RoC's job
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Look beyond paperwork to the company's real activity and owners

Shell entities often have valid documents but no real business. Checking business substance, beneficial owners and fund flows helps detect accommodation entries and laundering.

Question 4

Following Vijay Madanlal Choudhary, the provisional attachment of property under Section 5 is:

  1. Unconstitutional and no longer permitted
  2. Allowed only after the accused's conviction
  3. Valid, with safeguards such as recorded reasons
  4. Possible only with consent of the property owner
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Valid, with safeguards such as recorded reasons

The Court upheld Section 5, noting built-in safeguards such as recording reasons in writing, a time limit and confirmation by the Adjudicating Authority.

Question 5

Market manipulation cases involving synchronised trades in illiquid shares to create bogus long-term gains show that:

  1. Long-term capital gains are always genuine
  2. Illiquid stocks cannot be used for laundering
  3. Brokers have no role in identifying such trades, as surveillance is entirely the exchange's job
  4. Exchange alerts and broker monitoring are vital red-flag sources
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Exchange alerts and broker monitoring are vital red-flag sources

Schemes that ramp up penny stocks to generate fake gains were detected through exchange surveillance and broker alerts. Intermediaries must review such patterns and report STRs.

Question 6

A compliance lesson from PMLA case law for reporting entities is that:

  1. Good records and timely STRs support investigations
  2. Records can be destroyed once the client relationship ends
  3. Reporting suspicious transactions is optional if the client is valuable
  4. Tipping off clients is acceptable after filing an STR
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Good records and timely STRs support investigations

Investigations rely heavily on reporting entities' records and reports. Firms must retain records for the prescribed period and never tip off clients.

Question 7

In Vijay Madanlal Choudhary, the Court held that the twin conditions for bail under Section 45 are:

  1. Applicable only to offences under the NDPS Act
  2. Reporting suspicious transactions is optional if the client is valuable and has a long relationship
  3. Constitutional and reasonable given the gravity of the offence
  4. Replaced by ordinary bail provisions of the CrPC
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Constitutional and reasonable given the gravity of the offence

The Court upheld the twin conditions, finding them reasonable for the serious offence of money laundering, after Parliament's 2018 amendment addressed earlier defects.

Question 8

A broker files an STR on a client who later is not charged with any offence. The broker:

  1. Must pay compensation to the client in every case
  2. Loses its registration automatically
  3. Must disclose the STR to the client afterwards
  4. Is protected if the STR was made in good faith
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Is protected if the STR was made in good faith

The PMLA protects reporting entities and their officers from civil or criminal proceedings for reports made in good faith. Confidentiality of the STR continues.
