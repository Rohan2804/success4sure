Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002: NISM Series XXIV practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002, from the NISM Series XXIV (AML and CFT Provisions in Securities Markets) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the key provisions of the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002: definitions of money laundering, proceeds of crime and reporting entity; obligations of reporting entities to maintain records, verify clients and report to FIU-IND; attachment, adjudication and confiscation of property; powers of search, seizure, arrest and summons; Special Courts and the Appellate Tribunal; bail conditions; and penalties for reporting entities. Focus on the obligations of securities intermediaries as reporting entities and on the main enforcement stages.

Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 questions

Question 1

Under the PMLA, records of transactions must be maintained by a reporting entity for at least:

  1. One year from the date of the last transaction
  2. Three months after the account is closed
  3. Five years from the date of transaction
  4. Permanently, without any time limit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Five years from the date of transaction

The PMLA requires records of transactions to be kept for five years from the date of transaction, and identity records for five years after the business relationship ends.

Question 2

A provisional attachment order under the PMLA must be confirmed by which authority to continue?

  1. The stock exchange where the shares are listed
  2. The Adjudicating Authority
  3. The Principal Officer of the reporting entity
  4. The Registrar of Companies
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The Adjudicating Authority

After the Enforcement Directorate provisionally attaches property, it files a complaint before the Adjudicating Authority, which decides whether to confirm the attachment.

Question 3

Appeals against orders of the Adjudicating Authority under the PMLA lie to:

  1. The Appellate Tribunal
  2. The Securities Appellate Tribunal in all cases
  3. The FIU-IND Director directly
  4. The Income-tax Appellate Tribunal
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The Appellate Tribunal

The PMLA provides for an Appellate Tribunal to hear appeals against Adjudicating Authority orders, with a further appeal to the High Court on questions of law.

Question 4

Statements recorded by an ED officer under Section 50 of the PMLA are significant because:

  1. They can only be recorded in the presence of the accused person's lawyer and a magistrate
  2. They are made to the police and so are never admissible in any proceedings
  3. The officer can summon persons and record statements that are admissible as evidence
  4. They can be recorded only after the person has been convicted of the scheduled offence
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The officer can summon persons and record statements that are admissible as evidence

Section 50 gives ED officers civil-court-like powers to summon persons and documents. Persons must state the truth, and such statements can be used as evidence.

Question 5

A reporting entity fails to report suspicious transactions as required. Under the PMLA, the Director, FIU-IND may:

  1. Imprison the reporting entity's clients for seven years
  2. Impose a monetary penalty for each failure
  3. Cancel the reporting entity's SEBI registration directly
  4. Order the closure of the stock exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Impose a monetary penalty for each failure

The Director, FIU-IND can impose fines on reporting entities and their officers for each failure to comply with reporting and record-keeping obligations. Registration is a matter for SEBI.

Question 6

Under the PMLA, the term 'reporting entity' includes:

  1. Only public sector banks regulated by the RBI
  2. Only listed companies with a market capitalisation above ₹1,000 crore and their promoters and directors
  3. Banks, financial institutions, intermediaries and designated businesses
  4. Only individuals with income above ₹50 lakh a year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Banks, financial institutions, intermediaries and designated businesses

Reporting entities include banking companies, financial institutions, intermediaries such as brokers and depository participants, and persons carrying on designated businesses or professions.

Question 7

Bail for an accused under the PMLA is subject to 'twin conditions', which require the court to be satisfied that:

  1. There are reasonable grounds to believe the accused is not guilty and is unlikely to offend on bail
  2. The accused has paid all income tax dues for the last ten years and has never been arrested before
  3. The accused has surrendered his passport to the stock exchange
  4. The scheduled offence has been completely tried and decided
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. There are reasonable grounds to believe the accused is not guilty and is unlikely to offend on bail

Section 45 imposes twin conditions for bail after giving the public prosecutor an opportunity to oppose. The Supreme Court upheld these conditions in Vijay Madanlal Choudhary (2022).

Question 8

Property confiscated to the Central Government under the PMLA may be restored to a claimant with a legitimate interest if:

  1. The Special Court is satisfied the claimant suffered loss and acted in good faith
  2. The claimant files a request with the stock exchange where the confiscated shares are listed
  3. The claimant pays a fee to FIU-IND
  4. The accused agrees to the restoration in writing
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The Special Court is satisfied the claimant suffered loss and acted in good faith

The PMLA allows the Special Court to order restoration of confiscated property to persons with a legitimate interest who suffered a quantifiable loss, such as victims or bona fide lenders.
