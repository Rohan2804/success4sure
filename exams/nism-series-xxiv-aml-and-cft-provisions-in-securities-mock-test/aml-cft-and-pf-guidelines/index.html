AML, CFT and PF Guidelines: NISM Series XXIV practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on AML, CFT and PF Guidelines, from the NISM Series XXIV (AML and CFT Provisions in Securities Markets) syllabus.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the international and national standards that shape AML/CFT/PF programmes: FATF's 40 Recommendations, the risk-based approach, customer due diligence and enhanced due diligence, politically exposed persons, correspondent relationships, new technologies, wire transfers, targeted financial sanctions under UN Security Council resolutions, India's UAPA Section 51A order and the WMD Act Section 12A order. Focus on how a securities intermediary should apply these standards in onboarding, monitoring and sanctions screening.

AML, CFT and PF Guidelines questions

Question 1

Under the FATF Recommendations, enhanced due diligence for a foreign politically exposed person typically includes:

  1. Opening the account without any documents to save time
  2. Reducing monitoring of the relationship, because PEPs are well-known and trusted public figures
  3. Senior management approval and establishing the source of wealth and funds
  4. Refusing all transactions above ₹1,000 permanently
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Senior management approval and establishing the source of wealth and funds

PEPs carry higher corruption risk. Firms must obtain senior management approval, establish source of wealth and funds, and conduct enhanced ongoing monitoring of the relationship.

Question 2

Targeted financial sanctions require an intermediary that identifies a designated person's account to:

  1. Allow the person to withdraw all funds before freezing
  2. Freeze the funds without delay and report to the authorities
  3. Inform the client first and seek permission to freeze
  4. Wait until the next annual audit to take any action
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Freeze the funds without delay and report to the authorities

Under UN sanctions and India's UAPA and WMD Act orders, funds of designated persons must be frozen immediately without prior notice, and the authorities informed as prescribed.

Question 3

A 'risk-based approach' allows an intermediary to:

  1. Ignore all low-value clients for AML purposes completely and focus only on institutional clients
  2. Apply the same checks to every client regardless of risk
  3. Apply simplified measures to lower-risk clients and stronger measures to higher-risk ones
  4. Avoid reporting suspicious transactions of low-risk clients
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Apply simplified measures to lower-risk clients and stronger measures to higher-risk ones

Resources are focused where risks are higher, with simplified CDD only where risk is demonstrably low. Reporting obligations apply to all clients regardless of risk rating.

Question 4

FATF's recommendations on new technologies require firms to:

  1. Assess ML/TF risks before launching new products or technologies
  2. Avoid using any digital onboarding method at all
  3. Allow anonymous accounts for online investors
  4. Stop all KYC once a client uses a mobile app
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Assess ML/TF risks before launching new products or technologies

Before launching new products, delivery channels or technologies, firms should identify and mitigate the related risks. Digital onboarding is allowed with appropriate controls.

Question 5

Under India's framework, freezing of assets of persons linked to WMD proliferation is implemented through an order under:

  1. Section 2 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872
  2. Section 80C of the Income-tax Act
  3. The SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations
  4. Section 12A of the WMD Act, 2005
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Section 12A of the WMD Act, 2005

The 2022 amendment to the Weapons of Mass Destruction Act introduced Section 12A, empowering the government to freeze funds of persons involved in proliferation financing.

Question 6

An intermediary should screen its client base against sanctions lists:

  1. At onboarding and whenever lists are updated
  2. Only once, at the time of account opening
  3. Only when the client asks for a statement
  4. Only if the client is a foreign national
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. At onboarding and whenever lists are updated

Sanctions lists change frequently. Screening at onboarding and on every list update ensures newly designated persons are identified and their assets frozen without delay.

Question 7

Which client characteristic is most likely to require enhanced due diligence?

  1. A salaried employee investing through a monthly SIP
  2. A pensioner holding a small portfolio of blue-chip shares
  3. Complex ownership through layers of offshore companies
  4. A student opening an account with complete KYC and a small balance
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Complex ownership through layers of offshore companies

Opaque structures can hide beneficial owners and are a classic risk indicator. Simple, transparent profiles with small or regular transactions are generally lower risk.

Question 8

Correspondent relationships and 'nested' accounts raise AML concern because:

  1. They always involve cash deposits at bank branches
  2. They are prohibited under all Indian laws
  3. They reduce transaction volumes in the market
  4. Underlying customers of the respondent may not be known
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Underlying customers of the respondent may not be known

When a foreign institution uses an account to serve its own clients, the intermediary may not see the ultimate customers. FATF requires extra checks on respondent institutions' controls.
