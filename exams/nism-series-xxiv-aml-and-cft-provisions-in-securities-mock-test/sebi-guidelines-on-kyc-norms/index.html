SEBI Guidelines on KYC Norms: NISM Series XXIV practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SEBI Guidelines on KYC Norms, from the NISM Series XXIV (AML and CFT Provisions in Securities Markets) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers KYC norms applicable to the securities market: the KYC process and documents, PAN requirements and exemptions, officially valid documents, in-person verification and video-based KYC, KYC Registration Agencies, the Central KYC Records Registry, KYC for non-individuals, FPIs and NRIs, periodic updation, and the KYC status categories (registered, validated, on hold). Focus on what an intermediary must collect and verify, how KRAs and CKYCR work, and what happens when KYC records cannot be validated.

SEBI Guidelines on KYC Norms questions

Question 1

Under SEBI's KRA framework, a KYC record that has been verified with the issuing source, such as the Income Tax database or Aadhaar, is marked as:

  1. Rejected
  2. Validated
  3. On hold
  4. Registered only
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Validated

SEBI requires KRAs to verify key attributes like PAN, name, address and mobile/e-mail with official sources. Records verified in this way are marked 'validated' and can be used across intermediaries.

Question 2

If a client's KYC record at the KRA is marked 'on hold' because the e-mail or mobile number could not be verified, the intermediary should:

  1. Get the details corrected before allowing transactions
  2. Allow unlimited trading until the next annual review
  3. Close the account and transfer the securities to itself
  4. Ignore the status since it is not important
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Get the details corrected before allowing transactions

An 'on hold' status signals incomplete or unverified details. Clients cannot transact until the missing or incorrect information is corrected and re-verified.

Question 3

Video-based customer identification (V-CIP) for securities market KYC is acceptable if:

  1. The client sends a pre-recorded video by e-mail
  2. It is live, recorded, secure and done by trained staff
  3. The client is identified only through a voice call
  4. A friend of the client confirms the identity on video
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. It is live, recorded, secure and done by trained staff

SEBI permits video-based IPV and KYC where the interaction is live, recorded, geotagged and conducted with security safeguards by authorised officials.

Question 4

For an FPI client, KYC requirements are generally:

  1. Waived completely because FPIs are foreign
  2. Risk-based, with categories determining documentation
  3. The same as for a domestic retail investor
  4. Limited to collecting a photograph only
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Risk-based, with categories determining documentation

SEBI applies a risk-based KYC approach for FPIs, with documentation varying by FPI category and beneficial owners identified as required under the PML Rules.

Question 5

The Central KYC Records Registry (CKYCR) helps the financial sector by:

  1. Providing a common KYC repository across regulators
  2. Rating clients' creditworthiness for loans
  3. Approving IPO allotments for investors
  4. Fixing brokerage rates for trades
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Providing a common KYC repository across regulators

CKYCR, operated by CERSAI, holds KYC records that banks, insurers and securities intermediaries can access, reducing repeated KYC and improving consistency.

Question 6

A client's KYC documents show a different name from his PAN. The intermediary should:

  1. Open the account using whichever name the client prefers
  2. Resolve the mismatch before opening the account
  3. Open the account but block only withdrawals
  4. Ignore minor differences in name in all cases
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Resolve the mismatch before opening the account

KYC requires consistent identity information. Name mismatches must be resolved with supporting documents before onboarding, to avoid fictitious or benami accounts.

Question 7

Which client is exempted from the PAN requirement for transacting in the securities market, subject to verification?

  1. Any client investing less than ₹10 crore
  2. All NRIs, irrespective of the amount invested
  3. All clients aged above 60 years, irrespective of residence or the amount they invest
  4. Investors residing in Sikkim, subject to residence verification
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Investors residing in Sikkim, subject to residence verification

SEBI allows limited exemptions, such as investors residing in Sikkim, government officials in an official capacity and certain others, subject to verification of identity and residence.

Question 8

For a company client, KYC requires identification of:

  1. Beneficial owners and authorised signatories
  2. Only the company's statutory auditor
  3. Only the company's largest supplier
  4. Only its registered office landlord
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Beneficial owners and authorised signatories

Intermediaries must verify the company's constitution, identify beneficial owners who own or control it, and verify persons authorised to act on its behalf.
