SEBI Guidelines on AML/CFT/PF Standards: NISM Series XXIV practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SEBI Guidelines on AML/CFT/PF Standards, from the NISM Series XXIV (AML and CFT Provisions in Securities Markets) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers SEBI's Master Circular on AML/CFT/PF obligations of registered intermediaries. Topics include the written AML policy, client acceptance and due diligence, risk categorisation, Clients of Special Category, identification of beneficial owners, reliance on third parties, ongoing monitoring and alert generation, record keeping, reporting to FIU-IND, appointment of Principal Officer and Designated Director, employee training and hiring, and sanctions screening. Focus on practical alert scenarios and on the intermediary's responsibilities when red flags appear.

SEBI Guidelines on AML/CFT/PF Standards questions

Question 1

Under SEBI's AML master circular, an intermediary's client acceptance policy should ensure that:

  1. No account is opened in a fictitious or benami name
  2. Accounts are opened even if clients refuse to give KYC documents
  3. Clients with sanctioned names can trade below a certain value
  4. Accounts are opened only for clients introduced by employees
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. No account is opened in a fictitious or benami name

SEBI requires that accounts are not opened in anonymous, fictitious or benami names and that clients are identified and verified before the relationship begins.

Question 2

An intermediary should conduct training on AML for:

  1. Front-line, compliance and back-office staff
  2. Only the board of directors once in five years
  3. Only staff who have been with the firm for over ten years
  4. Only external auditors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Front-line, compliance and back-office staff

SEBI expects ongoing employee training so staff understand AML policies, red flags and reporting procedures. Front-line staff are often the first to notice unusual activity.

Question 3

A client's trading suddenly shifts from small trades in large caps to heavy daily trading in one illiquid small-cap, matched with the same counterparties. The intermediary should:

  1. Ignore it, since trading volumes are the client's personal choice
  2. Inform the client that an STR is being considered
  3. Increase the client's credit limit to support more trades
  4. Review the activity and consider filing an STR
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Review the activity and consider filing an STR

Abrupt changes and matched trades in illiquid scrips can indicate manipulation or laundering. The firm should examine the activity and report if suspicious, without tipping off the client.

Question 4

SEBI's 'Clients of Special Category' include:

  1. All salaried resident individuals below 30 years of age
  2. Every client who uses a mobile trading app
  3. Clients who hold only mutual fund units
  4. Non-resident clients and high-net-worth clients
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Non-resident clients and high-net-worth clients

CSCs include NRIs, HNIs, trusts and charities, PEPs, companies with close family shareholding, clients from high-risk jurisdictions and others needing more scrutiny.

Question 5

An intermediary must ensure its foreign branches and subsidiaries follow AML standards that are:

  1. Only those of the host country, whatever they are
  2. Optional, as decided by the local branch head
  3. The stricter of Indian and host-country requirements
  4. Lower than Indian standards to reduce costs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The stricter of Indian and host-country requirements

SEBI requires that, where requirements differ, branches and subsidiaries abroad apply the higher standard, to the extent the host country's laws permit.

Question 6

Hiring procedures under SEBI's AML guidelines require intermediaries to:

  1. Hire only relatives of existing directors
  2. Screen employees for integrity and suitability
  3. Avoid any background checks to protect privacy
  4. Let clients choose which employees handle their accounts
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Screen employees for integrity and suitability

Adequate screening when hiring, especially for sensitive roles, reduces insider risk. Staff with access to client data and transactions must be trustworthy.

Question 7

When an intermediary relies on a KYC Registration Agency's records for a client, it should:

  1. Treat the client as low risk without any further review
  2. Still perform its own risk assessment and ongoing monitoring
  3. Delete its own copy of the client's documents, since the KRA already maintains the official record
  4. Stop all transaction monitoring for that client
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Still perform its own risk assessment and ongoing monitoring

KRA records help with identification, but the intermediary remains responsible for its risk categorisation, ongoing due diligence and reporting obligations.

Question 8

The Principal Officer under SEBI's AML framework should be:

  1. A junior trainee working only on client calls
  2. An external consultant with no access to records
  3. The client's chartered accountant
  4. A sufficiently senior officer able to act independently
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A sufficiently senior officer able to act independently

SEBI expects the Principal Officer to be senior enough to discharge duties independently, with access to all records, and to report to senior management or the board.
