Scheduled Offences: NISM Series XXIV practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Scheduled Offences, from the NISM Series XXIV (AML and CFT Provisions in Securities Markets) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains the Schedule to the PMLA, which lists predicate offences whose proceeds can give rise to money laundering. It covers Parts A, B and C of the Schedule, examples of offences under the Indian Penal Code (now Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita), NDPS Act, Prevention of Corruption Act, SEBI Act, Customs Act, Companies Act and others, and the link between a scheduled offence and a PMLA case. Focus on why the existence of a scheduled offence matters and on examples relevant to securities markets.

Scheduled Offences questions

Question 1

Which of the following is an example of a scheduled offence relevant to securities markets?

  1. Late filing of an income tax return by a salaried person
  2. Failure to update a mobile number with the depository
  3. Fraudulent and unfair trade practices under the SEBI Act
  4. Delay in paying a mobile phone bill
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Fraudulent and unfair trade practices under the SEBI Act

Offences under the SEBI Act, such as manipulative and deceptive practices or insider trading, are scheduled offences. Proceeds from them can therefore be the subject of money laundering cases.

Question 2

Part B of the PMLA Schedule mainly covers:

  1. All offences under the Motor Vehicles Act
  2. Disputes between shareholders and companies over dividends, bonus issues and rights entitlements
  3. Civil cases for recovery of debts
  4. Offences under the Customs Act involving a value above a threshold
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Offences under the Customs Act involving a value above a threshold

Part B covers offences under the Customs Act involving a total value of ₹1 crore or more (the specified threshold), such as false declarations. Part A covers a wide list of offences with no value threshold.

Question 3

A broker's client generates large profits through insider trading and then moves them into property. The PMLA is relevant because:

  1. Property purchases are always illegal under the PMLA when made out of trading profits in shares
  2. Insider trading is only a civil matter with no criminal angle
  3. Insider trading is a scheduled offence and the profits are proceeds of crime
  4. The PMLA applies only to cash deposits in banks
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Insider trading is a scheduled offence and the profits are proceeds of crime

Proceeds derived from a scheduled offence are proceeds of crime, and dealing with them — such as projecting them as untainted property — can amount to money laundering.

Question 4

Offences under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 are included in the PMLA Schedule. This means that:

  1. Public servants can never be investigated under any law
  2. Corruption is no longer an offence under other laws
  3. Only the CBI can investigate any PMLA case
  4. Bribe money can be pursued through money laundering proceedings
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Bribe money can be pursued through money laundering proceedings

Since corruption offences are scheduled, the bribe amount and assets derived from it can be attached and prosecuted under the PMLA, in addition to proceedings under the PC Act.

Question 5

Under the Supreme Court's interpretation, the PMLA offence depends on the scheduled offence in that:

  1. Proceeds of crime must arise from criminal activity relating to a scheduled offence
  2. The ED may proceed even if no scheduled offence is alleged anywhere
  3. The scheduled offence must first be tried by the ED itself and result in conviction before any PMLA action
  4. Any civil breach of contract is sufficient
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Proceeds of crime must arise from criminal activity relating to a scheduled offence

Money laundering is linked to proceeds generated from a scheduled offence. If there is no scheduled offence or it is finally quashed or ends in acquittal, there can be no proceeds of crime.

Question 6

Which of these offences is included in the PMLA Schedule?

  1. Breach of a rental agreement between landlord and tenant
  2. Failure to file a GST return on time for one month
  3. Offences relating to narcotic drugs under the NDPS Act
  4. Non-payment of a credit card bill by an individual
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Offences relating to narcotic drugs under the NDPS Act

Drug trafficking offences under the NDPS Act are classic predicate offences for money laundering. Civil defaults and minor procedural lapses are not scheduled offences.

Question 7

A person commits an offence abroad which, if committed in India, would be a scheduled offence, and the proceeds are moved into Indian shares. How does the PMLA treat this?

  1. The PMLA never applies to any offence committed outside India
  2. Only the foreign country can take action, and Indian assets cannot be attached
  3. It can be a scheduled offence with cross-border implications
  4. It is treated only as a tax matter under the Income-tax Act
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. It can be a scheduled offence with cross-border implications

The PMLA Schedule includes offences with cross-border implications, so proceeds of such crimes brought into India can be attached and laundering prosecuted here, often with international cooperation.

Question 8

Fraud under the Companies Act, 2013 (Section 447) being a scheduled offence means that:

  1. All companies must register with FIU-IND as NPOs
  2. Proceeds of corporate fraud can be targeted under PMLA
  3. Directors are exempt from PMLA proceedings
  4. The Companies Act no longer applies to fraud
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Proceeds of corporate fraud can be targeted under PMLA

Corporate fraud under Section 447 is a scheduled offence, so assets derived from it can be attached and laundering prosecuted, alongside action by the SFIO or other agencies.
