About the NISM Series XXIV exam

NISM Series XXIV covers anti-money laundering and countering the financing of terrorism in the securities market — PMLA, record-keeping rules, scheduled offences, SEBI AML/CFT guidelines, KYC norms and case studies.

Who should take it: Compliance, KYC and principal-officer teams at intermediaries who handle AML/CFT obligations and suspicious transaction reporting.

Sample 1 · Introduction to AML, CFT and PF

Which sequence correctly describes the three classic stages of money laundering?

  1. Placement, layering, integration
  2. Layering, placement, integration
  3. Integration, placement, layering
  4. Placement, integration, layering
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Placement, layering, integration

Illicit cash is first placed in the financial system, then layered through complex transactions, and finally integrated into the legitimate economy.

Sample 2 · Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002

Under the PMLA, 2002, the offence of money laundering is defined in which section?

  1. Section 12
  2. Section 45
  3. Section 24
  4. Section 3
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Section 3

Section 3 defines the offence of money laundering as being involved in any process or activity connected with proceeds of crime.

Sample 3 · PML (Maintenance of Records) Rules, 2005

Under the PML Rules, cash transactions exceeding what value (or integrally connected cash transactions aggregating above it in a month) must be reported to FIU-IND?

  1. ₹50,000
  2. ₹10 lakh
  3. ₹2 lakh
  4. ₹1 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹10 lakh

Cash transactions above ₹10 lakh, or a series of integrally connected cash transactions aggregating above ₹10 lakh within a month, are reported in the CTR.

Sample 4 · Scheduled Offences

Under the PMLA framework, a 'scheduled offence' is significant because:

  1. It is the only offence that can be tried by a magistrate
  2. It is exempt from investigation by the Enforcement Directorate
  3. It is an offence that is punishable only by fine
  4. Money laundering can arise only in relation to proceeds of crime from a scheduled offence
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Money laundering can arise only in relation to proceeds of crime from a scheduled offence

Proceeds of crime under PMLA must be linked to a scheduled offence, so the Schedule defines the predicate crimes for money laundering.

Sample 5 · AML, CFT and PF Guidelines

FATF's 'risk-based approach' requires countries and reporting entities to:

  1. Apply the same level of due diligence to every customer regardless of risk
  2. Avoid serving all customers from high-risk sectors
  3. Identify and assess ML/TF risks and apply measures proportionate to those risks
  4. Rely solely on government lists to identify risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Identify and assess ML/TF risks and apply measures proportionate to those risks

Under a risk-based approach, enhanced measures apply to higher risks and simplified measures may apply to lower risks.
