Introduction to AML, CFT and PF: NISM Series XXIV practice questions

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces money laundering, terrorist financing and proliferation financing, and why the securities market is vulnerable to them. It covers the stages of laundering (placement, layering, integration), common typologies, the differences between ML, TF and PF, the role of international bodies such as FATF, the UN Security Council and the Egmont Group, and India's institutional framework including FIU-IND, the Enforcement Directorate and sectoral regulators. Focus on recognising typologies in market scenarios and on knowing which agency does what.

Introduction to AML, CFT and PF questions

Question 1

A client buys and sells the same illiquid shares repeatedly across several related accounts to obscure the source of funds. This activity mainly represents which stage of money laundering?

  1. Placement of cash into the banking system for the first time
  2. Integration of clean-looking funds into the legitimate economy
  3. Layering
  4. Structuring of cash deposits below reporting thresholds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Layering

Layering uses complex, often circular transactions to separate funds from their criminal origin. Placement introduces illicit cash into the system, and integration brings laundered money back as apparently legitimate wealth.

Question 2

The Egmont Group is best described as:

  1. A network of national Financial Intelligence Units
  2. A United Nations committee that freezes terrorist assets worldwide
  3. An association of stock exchanges that sets listing standards
  4. A global body of central banks that sets capital rules for banks
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A network of national Financial Intelligence Units

The Egmont Group connects FIUs across countries to share financial intelligence securely. FIU-IND is a member. Asset freezing lists come from the UN Security Council, and capital rules from the Basel Committee.

Question 3

Why is terrorist financing sometimes harder to detect than money laundering?

  1. Terrorist financing always involves very large cash deposits
  2. Terrorist funds are always routed through stock exchanges
  3. Terrorist financing is not a crime under Indian law
  4. Funds may be small and come from legitimate sources
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Funds may be small and come from legitimate sources

TF can use modest amounts from legitimate income or donations, so the warning signs are subtle. The focus is on where money is going, while ML focuses on where it came from.

Question 4

A country placed by FATF under 'increased monitoring' is commonly said to be on the:

  1. Black list with a call for counter-measures
  2. Grey list
  3. White list of fully compliant jurisdictions
  4. Watch list maintained by FIU-IND for clients
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Grey list

Jurisdictions under increased monitoring (grey list) have strategic AML/CFT deficiencies but are working with FATF on an action plan. The 'call for action' list is the more severe black list.

Question 5

Proliferation financing risk for a securities intermediary arises mainly when:

  1. A client may be linked to WMD programmes or sanctioned persons
  2. A client invests regularly through a monthly SIP in equity schemes
  3. A client changes his registered mobile number and e-mail address
  4. A client asks for a consolidated account statement once a year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A client may be linked to WMD programmes or sanctioned persons

PF involves financing weapons of mass destruction and their delivery systems, often via front companies tied to sanctioned states or persons. Routine investing or KYC updates are not indicators.

Question 6

Which agency is responsible for investigating money laundering offences and attaching proceeds of crime under the PMLA?

  1. The Financial Intelligence Unit – India, which analyses reports
  2. The Securities and Exchange Board of India
  3. The Central Board of Direct Taxes
  4. The Directorate of Enforcement
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The Directorate of Enforcement

The Directorate of Enforcement investigates PMLA offences, attaches property and prosecutes. FIU-IND receives and analyses reports, SEBI regulates intermediaries and CBDT administers direct taxes.

Question 7

Trade-based money laundering typically involves:

  1. Mis-invoicing goods to move value across borders
  2. Buying units of liquid mutual funds and holding them for years
  3. Paying income tax in advance through challans
  4. Opening a demat account with complete KYC
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Mis-invoicing goods to move value across borders

Over- or under-invoicing imports and exports lets criminals transfer value disguised as trade. Securities intermediaries should note trade-linked companies whose flows do not fit their profile.

Question 8

FIU-IND functions under the administrative control of the:

  1. Ministry of Home Affairs (Internal Security Division)
  2. Ministry of Finance (Department of Revenue)
  3. Reserve Bank of India's Department of Supervision
  4. Ministry of External Affairs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Ministry of Finance (Department of Revenue)

FIU-IND is the central national agency under the Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance, reporting to the Economic Intelligence Council. It receives, analyses and disseminates financial intelligence.
