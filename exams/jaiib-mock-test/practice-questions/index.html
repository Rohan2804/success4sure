Question 1 · Indian Economy & Indian Financial System

The Monetary Policy Committee of RBI consists of:

  1. Five members, all from RBI
  2. Seven members appointed by Parliament
  3. Four members, with the Finance Secretary as chair
  4. Six members, with the Governor having a casting vote in case of a tie
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Six members, with the Governor having a casting vote in case of a tie

The MPC has three RBI members (including the Governor, who chairs) and three external members; the Governor has a casting vote.

Question 2 · Principles & Practices of Banking

Under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, after a cheque is dishonoured for insufficient funds, the payee must send a written demand notice to the drawer within:

  1. 30 days of receiving information of dishonour
  2. 7 days of the cheque date
  3. 3 months of dishonour
  4. 1 year of dishonour
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 30 days of receiving information of dishonour

The payee must issue notice within 30 days; the drawer then gets 15 days to pay before a complaint can be filed.

Question 3 · Accounting & Financial Management for Bankers

The effective annual rate of a 12% nominal rate compounded quarterly is approximately:

  1. 12.00%
  2. 12.36%
  3. 12.55%
  4. 12.68%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 12.55%

EAR = (1 + 0.12/4)⁴ − 1 = (1.03)⁴ − 1 = 0.1255, i.e., 12.55%.

Question 4 · Retail Banking & Wealth Management

In a standard reducing-balance EMI loan, the early instalments consist of:

  1. A larger interest component and a smaller principal component
  2. Only principal and no interest
  3. Equal principal and interest every month
  4. A smaller interest component than later instalments
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A larger interest component and a smaller principal component

Interest is charged on the outstanding balance, which is highest early, so early EMIs are mostly interest.

Question 5 · Indian Economy & Indian Financial System

Under India's flexible inflation targeting framework, the CPI inflation target is:

  1. 2% with a band of ±1%
  2. 5% with a band of ±3%
  3. 4% with a tolerance band of ±2%
  4. 6% with no band
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 4% with a tolerance band of ±2%

The government has set the target at 4% CPI inflation, with an upper limit of 6% and lower limit of 2%.

Question 6 · Principles & Practices of Banking

As per RBI instructions, banks should not pay cheques presented after:

  1. One month from the date of the cheque
  2. Three months from the date of the cheque
  3. Six months from the date of the cheque
  4. One year from the date of the cheque
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Three months from the date of the cheque

Cheques become stale after three months from their date and are returned unpaid.

Question 7 · Accounting & Financial Management for Bankers

A machine costs ₹1,00,000, has a scrap value of ₹10,000 and a useful life of 5 years. Annual depreciation under the straight-line method is:

  1. ₹20,000
  2. ₹18,000
  3. ₹22,000
  4. ₹10,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹18,000

SLM depreciation = (1,00,000 − 10,000) ÷ 5 = ₹18,000.

Question 8 · Retail Banking & Wealth Management

Credit Information Companies (such as those producing credit scores) in India are governed by the:

  1. Banking Ombudsman Scheme
  2. SARFAESI Act, 2002
  3. Insurance Act, 1938
  4. Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act, 2005
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act, 2005

CICRA 2005 regulates credit bureaus, which collect credit data and generate credit reports and scores.

Question 9 · Indian Economy & Indian Financial System

In RBI's liquidity adjustment framework, if the policy repo rate is 6.00%, the Marginal Standing Facility rate is:

  1. 5.75%
  2. 6.50%
  3. 7.00%
  4. 6.25%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 6.25%

MSF is set 25 basis points above the repo rate (and SDF 25 bps below), so MSF = 6.25%.

Question 10 · Principles & Practices of Banking

A cheque crossed 'Account Payee' should be:

  1. Collected only for credit to the account of the named payee
  2. Paid in cash across the counter
  3. Endorsed to any third party freely
  4. Paid only after six months
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Collected only for credit to the account of the named payee

The account payee crossing directs the collecting banker to credit the proceeds only to the payee's account.

Question 11 · Accounting & Financial Management for Bankers

An asset costing ₹1,00,000 is depreciated at 20% on the written-down value method. Depreciation for the second year is:

  1. ₹20,000
  2. ₹16,000
  3. ₹12,800
  4. ₹18,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹16,000

Year 1: 20,000, WDV = 80,000; Year 2: 20% × 80,000 = ₹16,000.

Question 12 · Retail Banking & Wealth Management

For a 28-year-old investor with a long investment horizon and high risk tolerance, an adviser would ordinarily recommend:

  1. A relatively higher allocation to equity
  2. Investing everything in savings bank deposits
  3. Only short-term debt funds
  4. Only gold
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A relatively higher allocation to equity

Long horizons allow equity volatility to average out, so higher equity exposure suits young, risk-tolerant investors.

Question 13 · Indian Economy & Indian Financial System

Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) balances are:

  1. Maintained by banks as cash balances with RBI, on which no interest is paid
  2. Held by banks in gold in their own vaults
  3. Invested in corporate bonds
  4. Lent to NBFCs at the repo rate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Maintained by banks as cash balances with RBI, on which no interest is paid

CRR is a share of net demand and time liabilities kept as balances with RBI and earns no interest.

Question 14 · Principles & Practices of Banking

A banker's general lien is often described as:

  1. An implied pledge, as the bank can sell the goods/securities after reasonable notice
  2. A mortgage of immovable property
  3. A guarantee by a third party
  4. An unsecured claim
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. An implied pledge, as the bank can sell the goods/securities after reasonable notice

Unlike an ordinary lien (only retention), a banker's lien carries an implied power of sale, so it is called an implied pledge.

Question 15 · Accounting & Financial Management for Bankers

A firm has current assets of ₹6 lakh and current liabilities of ₹4 lakh. Its current ratio is:

  1. 0.67 : 1
  2. 2 : 1
  3. 1.5 : 1
  4. 1.33 : 1
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 1.5 : 1

Current ratio = 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5.
