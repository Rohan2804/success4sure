Accounting & Financial Management for Bankers: JAIIB revision notes

Accounting and Financial Management for Bankers is the most numerical JAIIB paper. It covers accounting principles and final accounts, depreciation, bank-specific accounts, ratio analysis, time value of money, capital budgeting, costing and working capital, and taxation basics. Expect around half the questions to involve calculations, so practise formulas with small numbers until you can do each in a minute.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Accounting basics

Accounting follows concepts such as going concern, accrual, consistency, prudence and matching. Under double entry every transaction has equal debit and credit. Golden rules: personal accounts, debit the receiver and credit the giver; real accounts, debit what comes in and credit what goes out; nominal accounts, debit expenses and losses, credit incomes and gains.

2. From journal to final accounts

Transactions are recorded in the journal, posted to ledgers, summarised in a trial balance, and adjusted for accruals, prepayments and depreciation to prepare the profit and loss account and balance sheet. A trial balance that agrees does not prove absence of errors, such as errors of omission or compensating errors.

3. Depreciation

Straight line method charges equal depreciation each year: (cost - residual value) / useful life. Written down value method applies a fixed rate to the opening book value, giving higher charges in early years. A ₹10 lakh asset at 20% WDV is depreciated by ₹2 lakh in year one and ₹1.6 lakh in year two.

4. Bank financial statements

Banks prepare accounts in formats prescribed under the Third Schedule of the Banking Regulation Act: Form A for the balance sheet and Form B for the profit and loss account, with schedules for capital, deposits, advances and investments. Interest income on NPAs is recognised only on realisation.

5. Ratio analysis

Liquidity: current ratio (current assets / current liabilities), quick ratio. Leverage: debt-equity ratio, interest coverage. Profitability: net profit margin, return on assets, return on equity. Turnover: inventory turnover, debtors turnover. Bankers use these to judge borrowers' credit quality.

6. Time value of money

Money today is worth more than the same money tomorrow. FV = PV x (1 + r)^n; PV = FV / (1 + r)^n. Annuity formulas value equal periodic flows. ₹1 lakh at 8% for 3 years grows to about ₹1.26 lakh. Effective annual rate rises with more frequent compounding.

7. EMI calculation

EMI = P x r x (1 + r)^n / [(1 + r)^n - 1], where r is the monthly rate and n the number of months. In early EMIs, interest forms a larger part; principal share rises over time. Banks use this for retail loans.

8. Capital budgeting

Payback period counts years to recover the investment. NPV discounts all cash flows at the cost of capital; accept if NPV is positive. IRR is the rate at which NPV is zero; accept if IRR exceeds cost of capital. Profitability index = PV of inflows / initial outlay. NPV is preferred when methods conflict.

9. Costing and break-even

Costs are fixed or variable. Contribution = sales - variable cost. Break-even point in units = fixed costs / contribution per unit. With fixed cost ₹6 lakh, price ₹100 and variable cost ₹60, break-even is 15,000 units. Margin of safety = actual sales - break-even sales.

10. Working capital

Working capital = current assets - current liabilities. The operating cycle runs from cash to raw materials, work in progress, finished goods, debtors and back to cash. Banks finance part of the working capital gap, with the borrower bringing a margin.

11. Taxation basics

Bankers deduct TDS on interest above thresholds, report high-value transactions and handle GST on fees and charges. Banks must understand the tax treatment of interest income, provisions and depreciation in their own accounts.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • SLM depreciation = (cost - residual value) / life; WDV depreciation = rate x opening book value.
  • EMI = P r (1+r)^n / [(1+r)^n - 1].
  • Break-even units = fixed cost / (price - variable cost per unit).
  • NPV = sum of CF_t / (1+k)^t - initial investment.

Common traps in the exam

  • A balanced trial balance does not guarantee error-free accounts.
  • WDV gives higher depreciation in early years than SLM for the same asset.
  • Accept a project if IRR exceeds the cost of capital, not if IRR is merely positive.
  • Break-even uses contribution per unit, not selling price per unit.

Quick recap

Double entry and golden rules; journal to ledger to trial balance to final accounts. Depreciation: SLM equal, WDV declining. Bank accounts in Third Schedule Forms A and B. Ratios: liquidity, leverage, profitability, turnover. TVM: FV and PV formulas, EMI. Capital budgeting: payback, NPV, IRR, PI. Break-even = fixed cost / contribution per unit. Working capital and operating cycle.
