What this chapter covers

This paper covers accounting basics and financial calculations bankers use. Revise double entry and the treatment of non-cash items, final accounts, ratio analysis (liquidity, leverage, profitability), working capital, time value of money (simple and compound interest, present value, annuities), depreciation and foreign exchange basics. Many questions are numerical, so practise calculations carefully and check units such as days in a year and compounding periods.

Accounting & Financial Management for Bankers questions

Question 1

A firm has total debt of ₹60 lakh and shareholders' equity of ₹40 lakh. Its debt–equity ratio is:

  1. 0.67 : 1
  2. 0.6 : 1
  3. 2.5 : 1
  4. 1.5 : 1
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 1.5 : 1

Debt–equity = debt / equity = 60 / 40 = 1.5. 0.6 is debt divided by total capital, a different ratio.

Question 2

Current assets are ₹8 lakh, of which inventory is ₹3 lakh. Current liabilities are ₹4 lakh. The quick ratio is:

  1. 2.00
  2. 1.25
  3. 0.75
  4. 2.75
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 1.25

Quick ratio = (current assets − inventory) / current liabilities = (8 − 3) / 4 = 1.25. The current ratio would be 2.0.

Question 3

Simple interest on ₹50,000 at 9% per annum for 146 days (365-day year) is:

  1. ₹4,500
  2. ₹1,825
  3. ₹1,800
  4. ₹1,314
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹1,800

SI = 50,000 × 9% × 146 / 365 = ₹1,800. ₹4,500 is interest for a full year.

Question 4

The present value of ₹1,00,000 receivable after 3 years at 10% compounded annually is closest to:

  1. ₹75,131
  2. ₹70,000
  3. ₹76,923
  4. ₹73,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹75,131

PV = 1,00,000 / (1.10)^3 = 1,00,000 / 1.331 = ₹75,131. Subtracting 30% simple interest gives ₹70,000, which is wrong.

Question 5

₹10,000 is deposited at the end of each year for 3 years at 10% compounded annually. The value at the end of year 3 is:

  1. ₹30,000
  2. ₹33,100
  3. ₹33,000
  4. ₹36,410
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹33,100

FV = 10,000 × (1.1² + 1.1 + 1) = 10,000 × 3.31 = ₹33,100. ₹36,410 assumes deposits at the start of each year (annuity due).

Question 6

Why is depreciation added back to net profit when estimating cash generated from operations?

  1. It is a cash receipt from customers
  2. It is a tax refund
  3. It is a non-cash expense
  4. It is a capital receipt
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. It is a non-cash expense

Depreciation reduces profit but involves no cash outflow in the year, so it is added back to profit to arrive at operating cash flow.

Question 7

Net working capital is defined as:

  1. Current assets minus current liabilities
  2. Fixed assets minus long-term loans
  3. Total assets minus total liabilities
  4. Share capital plus reserves
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Current assets minus current liabilities

Net working capital measures short-term liquidity: the excess of current assets over current liabilities that is financed from long-term sources.

Question 8

A quote 'EUR 1 = ₹95.20' in India is a direct quote. The equivalent indirect quote is about:

  1. ₹1 = EUR 95.20
  2. ₹100 = EUR 1.52
  3. ₹1 = EUR 0.952
  4. ₹1 = EUR 0.0105
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹1 = EUR 0.0105

An indirect quote states foreign currency per unit of home currency: 1 / 95.20 ≈ 0.0105 EUR per rupee.
