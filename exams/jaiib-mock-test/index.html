About the JAIIB exam

JAIIB is the Indian Institute of Banking & Finance's associate examination for bank employees. Its papers cover the Indian economy and financial system, principles and practices of banking, accounting and financial management for bankers, and retail banking and wealth management.

Who should take it: Bank employees who are IIBF members and want the JAIIB qualification for career progression and increments.

Sample 1 · Indian Economy & Indian Financial System

Fiscal deficit of the government is:

  1. Revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts
  2. Total expenditure minus total receipts including borrowings
  3. Interest payments minus tax revenue
  4. Total expenditure minus (revenue receipts plus non-debt capital receipts)
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Total expenditure minus (revenue receipts plus non-debt capital receipts)

Fiscal deficit measures the total borrowing requirement: expenditure not covered by revenue and non-debt capital receipts.

Sample 2 · Principles & Practices of Banking

With respect to a savings bank deposit, the relationship between the bank and the customer is primarily that of:

  1. Creditor (bank) and debtor (customer)
  2. Trustee and beneficiary
  3. Agent and principal
  4. Debtor (bank) and creditor (customer)
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Debtor (bank) and creditor (customer)

The bank owes the deposited money to the customer, so the bank is the debtor and the depositor the creditor.

Sample 3 · Accounting & Financial Management for Bankers

₹1,00,000 is invested at 10% per annum compounded annually. The amount after 2 years is:

  1. ₹1,20,000
  2. ₹1,21,000
  3. ₹1,10,000
  4. ₹1,22,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹1,21,000

A = 1,00,000 × (1.10)² = 1,00,000 × 1.21 = ₹1,21,000.

Sample 4 · Retail Banking & Wealth Management

Under RBI norms, the maximum loan-to-value ratio for an individual housing loan up to ₹30 lakh is:

  1. 75%
  2. 90%
  3. 80%
  4. 100%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 90%

LTV caps are 90% for loans up to ₹30 lakh, 80% for above ₹30 lakh up to ₹75 lakh, and 75% above ₹75 lakh.

Sample 5 · Indian Economy & Indian Financial System

GDP at market prices is ₹250 lakh crore, indirect taxes are ₹30 lakh crore and subsidies ₹10 lakh crore. GDP at factor cost is:

  1. ₹210 lakh crore
  2. ₹230 lakh crore
  3. ₹270 lakh crore
  4. ₹290 lakh crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹230 lakh crore

Factor cost = market price − net indirect taxes = 250 − (30 − 10) = ₹230 lakh crore.
