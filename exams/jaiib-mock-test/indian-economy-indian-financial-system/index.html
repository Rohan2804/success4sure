What this chapter covers

This paper covers the structure of the Indian economy and the financial system that supports it. Revise national income concepts (nominal and real GDP, deflator), fiscal indicators (fiscal, revenue and primary deficits), monetary policy tools and the liquidity adjustment framework including the SDF and MSF, the balance of payments, financial inclusion schemes, and the regulators of banking, securities, insurance, pensions and commodity markets. Practise simple calculations on growth, deficits and money multipliers.

Indian Economy & Indian Financial System questions

Question 1

Nominal GDP grew by 11% in a year while the GDP deflator rose by 4%. Real GDP growth is closest to:

  1. 7.0%
  2. 6.7%
  3. 15.4%
  4. 2.8%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 6.7%

Real growth = (1.11 / 1.04) − 1 ≈ 6.7%. Simply subtracting gives 7%, a rough approximation. The deflator strips out price changes from nominal GDP.

Question 2

Under RBI's liquidity adjustment framework, the floor of the policy rate corridor is the:

  1. Marginal Standing Facility rate
  2. Bank Rate
  3. Cash Reserve Ratio
  4. Standing Deposit Facility rate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Standing Deposit Facility rate

Since 2022 the SDF, which absorbs surplus liquidity without collateral, forms the floor of the corridor, while the MSF rate forms the ceiling around the repo rate.

Question 3

The government's fiscal deficit is ₹16 lakh crore and interest payments are ₹11 lakh crore. The primary deficit is:

  1. ₹5 lakh crore
  2. ₹27 lakh crore
  3. ₹11 lakh crore
  4. ₹16 lakh crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹5 lakh crore

Primary deficit = fiscal deficit − interest payments = 16 − 11 = ₹5 lakh crore. It shows borrowing needed for spending other than past debt servicing.

Question 4

Remittances sent home by Indians working abroad are recorded in India's balance of payments under the:

  1. Capital account, as foreign direct investment
  2. Financial account, as portfolio flows
  3. Current account
  4. Errors and omissions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Current account

Private transfers such as remittances are part of invisibles in the current account. FDI and portfolio flows belong to the capital/financial account.

Question 5

If RBI lowers the policy repo rate, the most likely immediate effect is:

  1. A rise in banks' cost of funds
  2. An increase in the CRR
  3. Higher yields on government bonds
  4. Lower borrowing costs in the economy
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Lower borrowing costs in the economy

A repo cut lowers the cost at which banks borrow from RBI, which typically passes into lower lending and deposit rates and lower bond yields.

Question 6

Which regulator oversees commodity derivatives exchanges in India?

  1. SEBI
  2. Forward Markets Commission
  3. Reserve Bank of India
  4. Ministry of Consumer Affairs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. SEBI

The Forward Markets Commission was merged with SEBI in 2015, and SEBI now regulates commodity derivatives exchanges and brokers.

Question 7

Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana is primarily aimed at:

  1. Providing crop insurance to farmers
  2. Funding large infrastructure projects
  3. Universal access to basic bank accounts
  4. Recapitalising public sector banks
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Universal access to basic bank accounts

PMJDY aims at financial inclusion by giving every household a basic savings account with RuPay card and access to insurance and credit facilities.

Question 8

In a simplified model where banks keep only the CRR of 4% as reserves, the maximum money multiplier is:

  1. 4
  2. 25
  3. 96
  4. 0.04
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 25

Money multiplier = 1 / reserve ratio = 1 / 0.04 = 25. In practice it is lower because of currency held by the public and excess reserves.
