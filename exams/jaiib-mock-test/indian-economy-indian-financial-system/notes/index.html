Indian Economy & Indian Financial System: JAIIB revision notes

Paper 1 of JAIIB, Indian Economy and Indian Financial System, tests whether a banker understands the economic environment in which banks work. It spans macroeconomics, monetary and fiscal policy, the structure of financial markets, regulators and recent reforms. Questions are mostly conceptual with some current-affairs flavour, so learn the mechanisms and check current policy rates from RBI before the exam.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. National income concepts

GDP measures the value of final goods and services produced within India in a year; GNP adds net factor income from abroad. Nominal GDP uses current prices; real GDP uses constant base-year prices, removing inflation. GDP at factor cost plus net indirect taxes gives GDP at market prices. India's GDP base year is periodically revised by MoSPI.

2. Sectors of the economy

The primary sector covers agriculture and allied activities, secondary covers manufacturing, construction and utilities, and tertiary covers services. Services contribute the largest share of India's GDP, while agriculture still employs a large share of the workforce, a contrast examiners like.

3. Inflation and its measures

Inflation is a sustained rise in the general price level. CPI (Combined) measures retail prices and is the target for RBI's monetary policy under flexible inflation targeting: 4% with a band of 2% to 6%. WPI measures wholesale prices. Demand-pull and cost-push are the two broad causes.

4. Monetary policy framework

The six-member Monetary Policy Committee sets the repo rate. The liquidity adjustment facility corridor has the Standing Deposit Facility at the floor and the Marginal Standing Facility at the ceiling, with the repo rate in between. RBI also uses CRR, SLR, open market operations and variable rate repos and reverse repos.

5. Quantitative tools

CRR is the share of net demand and time liabilities banks keep as cash balances with RBI, earning no interest. SLR is the share kept in liquid assets such as government securities. Raising CRR or SLR reduces lendable funds; OMO purchases inject liquidity and sales absorb it.

6. Fiscal policy

Fiscal policy covers government taxation, spending and borrowing. Fiscal deficit = total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings. Revenue deficit = revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts. Primary deficit = fiscal deficit minus interest payments. The FRBM framework sets deficit targets.

7. Balance of payments

The current account records trade in goods and services, income and transfers (including remittances, where India is the world's largest recipient). The capital account records FDI, FPI, loans and banking capital. India usually runs a trade deficit partly offset by services exports and remittances. RBI manages forex reserves.

8. Structure of the financial system

The system comprises institutions (banks, NBFCs, insurers, mutual funds, pension funds), markets (money, capital, forex, government securities, derivatives), instruments and services. Regulators include RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, PFRDA and IFSCA for GIFT City.

9. RBI's functions

RBI is the monetary authority, issuer of currency, banker to the government, banker's bank and lender of last resort, regulator and supervisor of banks and NBFCs, manager of foreign exchange under FEMA, and overseer of payment systems.

10. Money market

The money market deals in funds up to one year: call money (overnight), notice money (2 to 14 days), term money, treasury bills, commercial paper, certificates of deposit and repo/TREPS. It helps banks manage daily liquidity and transmits policy rate changes.

11. Banking sector reforms

Key milestones: bank nationalisation in 1969 and 1980; Narasimham Committee reforms in 1991 and 1998 (prudential norms, deregulation of interest rates); entry of new private banks; payments banks and small finance banks from 2015; recapitalisation and mergers of public sector banks around 2019-20.

12. Financial inclusion and digital payments

PMJDY opened accounts for the unbanked with RuPay cards and overdraft facility; DBT routes subsidies directly. UPI, operated by NPCI, has made India a leader in real-time retail payments. Business correspondents extend banking to villages without branches.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Fiscal deficit = total expenditure - (revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts).
  • Primary deficit = fiscal deficit - interest payments.
  • Real GDP growth is approximately nominal GDP growth - inflation (GDP deflator).

Common traps in the exam

  • SDF is the floor and MSF the ceiling of the LAF corridor; the old fixed reverse repo is no longer the floor.
  • RBI targets CPI inflation, not WPI.
  • CRR earns no interest; SLR assets do.
  • Primary deficit excludes interest payments; revenue deficit compares revenue items only.

Quick recap

GDP vs GNP, nominal vs real. CPI targeting at 4% (2% to 6%) by the six-member MPC. LAF corridor: SDF floor, repo, MSF ceiling; CRR and SLR as quantitative tools; OMOs manage liquidity. Fiscal, revenue and primary deficits. BoP: current and capital accounts. RBI: monetary authority, currency, government banker, regulator, forex manager. Reforms and inclusion through PMJDY and UPI.
