Retail Banking & Wealth Management: JAIIB practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Retail Banking & Wealth Management, from the JAIIB (Junior Associate of the Indian Institute of Bankers (per paper)) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This paper covers retail banking products, delivery channels and wealth management. Revise retail loan products and appraisal (FOIR, LTV, EMI), credit cards and credit scores, RBI's digital lending and Key Fact Statement norms, payment systems, bancassurance and third-party products, and wealth management basics such as risk profiling, asset allocation and the financial planning process. Practise EMI and eligibility calculations and know the customer-protection rules for retail lending.

Retail Banking & Wealth Management questions

Question 1

A home loan of ₹10 lakh at 9% per annum is repayable in 120 monthly instalments. The EMI is closest to:

  1. ₹12,668
  2. ₹9,000
  3. ₹15,833
  4. ₹11,250
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹12,668

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n / [(1 + r)^n − 1] with r = 0.75% and n = 120, giving about ₹12,668. ₹15,833 assumes flat interest on the full principal.

Question 2

Under RBI's digital lending guidelines, loan disbursals by regulated entities must generally be made:

  1. Into the lending app's pool account
  2. In cash through agents
  3. Directly into the borrower's bank account
  4. Into the account of the loan service provider
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Directly into the borrower's bank account

RBI requires disbursals and repayments to flow directly between the borrower's and the lender's accounts, without pass-through or pool accounts of third parties.

Question 3

A Key Fact Statement (KFS) for retail and MSME loans mainly helps the borrower by:

  1. Guaranteeing loan approval within a day
  2. Removing the need for KYC
  3. Fixing the interest rate for life
  4. Disclosing the all-in cost (APR) and key terms before signing
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Disclosing the all-in cost (APR) and key terms before signing

RBI mandates a KFS in a standard format showing the annual percentage rate, fees and charges and other key terms, so borrowers can compare loans before committing.

Question 4

A credit card holder pays only the 'minimum amount due' each month. The likely result is:

  1. No interest charged at all
  2. Interest charged on the unpaid balance and loss of the interest-free period
  3. Automatic closure of the card
  4. A higher credit limit the next month
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Interest charged on the unpaid balance and loss of the interest-free period

Paying only the minimum avoids a late fee but interest is charged on the outstanding balance, and new purchases lose the interest-free period.

Question 5

A borrower earns ₹80,000 a month and existing EMIs are ₹12,000. The bank caps total EMIs at 50% of income. The maximum new EMI is:

  1. ₹40,000
  2. ₹52,000
  3. ₹28,000
  4. ₹34,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹28,000

Allowed EMIs = 50% × 80,000 = ₹40,000. Less existing EMIs of ₹12,000 leaves ₹28,000 for the new loan.

Question 6

Before recommending investment products to a customer, a bank's wealth manager should first:

  1. Recommend the product with the highest commission
  2. Ask for the customer's net banking password
  3. Sell insurance to cover all risks
  4. Assess the customer's risk profile and goals
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Assess the customer's risk profile and goals

Suitability starts with understanding goals, horizon and risk appetite. Commission-led selling causes mis-selling, and passwords must never be asked for.

Question 7

Credit scores issued by credit information companies in India typically range from:

  1. 0 to 100
  2. 300 to 900
  3. 1 to 10
  4. 500 to 1,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 300 to 900

Most CICs in India use a range of about 300 to 900, where higher scores indicate better credit history and lower risk.

Question 8

A bank sells mutual fund units to its customers as a distributor. In this role, the bank:

  1. Earns commission and must follow distribution norms
  2. Guarantees the returns of the scheme
  3. Becomes the trustee of the mutual fund
  4. Holds the scheme's securities as custodian
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Earns commission and must follow distribution norms

As a distributor the bank earns trail commission and must follow SEBI/AMFI norms, including suitability and disclosure. Returns are not guaranteed by the bank.
