Principles & Practices of Banking: JAIIB practice questions

What this chapter covers

This paper covers the legal and operational foundations of banking. Revise banker–customer relationships for different services, negotiable instruments (crossings, paying and collecting banker protections, payment in due course), KYC and AML norms, types of accounts and their treatment (including inoperative accounts), asset classification and provisioning under RBI's prudential norms, and digital banking. Questions combine legal concepts with practical scenarios, so focus on applying rules to cases.

Principles & Practices of Banking questions

Question 1

In a safe deposit locker facility, the relationship between the bank and the customer is that of:

  1. Debtor and creditor
  2. Trustee and beneficiary
  3. Lessor and lessee
  4. Agent and principal
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Lessor and lessee

The bank lets out the locker space to the customer, creating a lessor–lessee relationship. The bank does not know or hold the contents as a debtor would hold deposits.

Question 2

A customer has given a mandate to her son to operate her account. The mandate is automatically terminated when:

  1. The customer dies
  2. The son changes his job
  3. The account balance falls below ₹10,000
  4. The bank changes the branch manager
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The customer dies

A mandate is an agency; it ends on the death, insanity or insolvency of the principal (the customer). Changes in the agent's job or the bank's staff do not end it.

Question 3

A savings account with no customer-induced transactions for over two years is classified as:

  1. Closed
  2. Inoperative
  3. NPA
  4. Frozen by court order
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Inoperative

RBI treats accounts with no customer-induced transactions for more than two years as inoperative (dormant). Balances remain the customer's and can be reactivated after KYC.

Question 4

A cheque bearing the words 'Not Negotiable' between two parallel lines:

  1. Cannot be transferred to anyone at all
  2. Must be paid in cash at the counter
  3. Can be collected only for a bank's own account
  4. Can be transferred, but the transferee gets no better title than the transferor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Can be transferred, but the transferee gets no better title than the transferor

'Not negotiable' crossing removes the negotiability feature: the cheque can still be transferred, but a transferee cannot get a better title than the person who transferred it.

Question 5

A cash credit account is treated as an NPA when it remains 'out of order' for:

  1. More than 90 days
  2. More than 30 days
  3. More than 180 days
  4. More than one year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. More than 90 days

Under RBI's IRAC norms, an overdraft or cash credit account that is out of order (for example, balance above limit or no credits to cover interest) for over 90 days becomes an NPA.

Question 6

A loan classified as doubtful (up to one year in that category) has a secured portion of ₹40 lakh and an unsecured portion of ₹10 lakh. The provision required under IRAC norms is:

  1. ₹10 lakh
  2. ₹20 lakh
  3. ₹14 lakh
  4. ₹50 lakh
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹20 lakh

For D1, the unsecured portion needs 100% provision (₹10 lakh) and the secured portion 25% (₹10 lakh), giving ₹20 lakh in total.

Question 7

Under the Negotiable Instruments Act, 'payment in due course' requires payment to be made:

  1. Only to the drawer of the cheque in person
  2. Within one week of the date of the cheque
  3. In cash, irrespective of the crossing
  4. As per the apparent tenor of the instrument, in good faith and without negligence
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. As per the apparent tenor of the instrument, in good faith and without negligence

Payment in due course protects the paying banker when it pays according to the apparent tenor, in good faith, without negligence, to the person in possession entitled to payment.

Question 8

Which RBI-permitted method allows banks to complete customer identification remotely using a live, consent-based video interaction?

  1. Offline Aadhaar e-sign
  2. NEFT verification
  3. V-CIP
  4. Positive Pay
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. V-CIP

Video-based Customer Identification Process lets regulated entities verify customers through a secure live video session, with consent, as an alternative to in-person verification.
