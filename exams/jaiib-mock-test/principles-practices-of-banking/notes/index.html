Principles & Practices of Banking: JAIIB revision notes

Principles and Practices of Banking is the core operational paper of JAIIB. It covers the banker-customer relationship, accounts and KYC, negotiable instruments, payment systems, lending principles, securities and charges, asset classification and customer service. Many questions are section-based, especially from the Negotiable Instruments Act. Limits and thresholds change, so check current RBI circulars.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Banker-customer relationship

The primary relationship is debtor-creditor: for deposits the bank is the debtor, for loans the creditor. Other relationships arise by function: trustee (for funds given for a specific purpose), agent (collecting cheques, paying bills), bailee (safe custody) and lessor-lessee (safe deposit lockers).

2. Types of accounts and KYC

Savings, current, recurring and fixed deposit accounts serve different needs. Special customers include minors, joint holders, partnerships, companies, trusts and HUFs, each needing specific documents and mandates. KYC under the RBI Master Direction requires identification, address verification, beneficial ownership checks and risk categorisation with periodic updation.

3. Negotiable instruments

The NI Act, 1881 covers promissory notes, bills of exchange and cheques. A cheque is a bill drawn on a specified banker payable on demand. A holder in due course takes the instrument for value, in good faith, before maturity, and gets a better title than the transferor.

4. Crossing and endorsement

General crossing (two parallel lines) means payment only through a bank account. Special crossing names a bank. 'Account payee' crossing directs collection only into the payee's account. Endorsements can be in blank, special, restrictive or conditional.

5. Section 138: cheque dishonour

Dishonour for insufficient funds is a criminal offence if the cheque was presented within its validity (three months), the payee sends a demand notice within 30 days of learning of dishonour, and the drawer fails to pay within 15 days of receiving notice. The complaint must be filed within one month of the cause of action. Punishment can be up to two years or a fine up to twice the cheque amount, or both.

6. Payment systems

NEFT settles in half-hourly batches round the clock; RTGS is real-time gross settlement for high-value payments (minimum ₹2 lakh); IMPS and UPI are instant. CTS clears cheques using images. NACH handles bulk debits and credits like EMIs and salaries. RBI's Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 governs them.

7. Lending principles

Safety, liquidity, profitability, purpose, diversification and security guide lending. Credit appraisal looks at the borrower's character, capacity, capital, collateral and conditions (the five Cs). Loan documentation must be complete and enforceable.

8. Modes of creating charge

Pledge transfers possession of movable goods to the lender. Hypothecation creates a charge without possession, common for vehicles and stocks. Mortgage creates an interest in immovable property; equitable mortgage by deposit of title deeds is common. Lien is a right to retain goods until dues are paid; assignment transfers actionable claims such as book debts or policies.

9. Asset classification

A loan becomes a non-performing asset when interest or instalment remains overdue for more than 90 days. Sub-standard assets have been NPAs for up to 12 months; doubtful assets beyond that; loss assets are identified as uncollectible. Provisioning increases with each category and the age of doubtful assets.

10. Priority sector lending

Domestic scheduled commercial banks must lend 40% of adjusted net bank credit or credit equivalent of off-balance sheet exposure, whichever is higher, to priority sectors such as agriculture, MSMEs, export credit, education, housing, social infrastructure and renewable energy, with sub-targets for agriculture and weaker sections.

11. Deposit insurance and grievance

DICGC insures deposits up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank, covering principal and interest. Customers with complaints first approach the bank, then the RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2021, which provides a single window for complaints against banks, NBFCs and payment system participants.

12. Banker's obligations

Banks must honour cheques when funds are sufficient and the cheque is in order, maintain secrecy of customer accounts except as permitted by law, and act on stop payment instructions. Wrongful dishonour can make the bank liable for damages to the customer's reputation.

Common traps in the exam

  • In a deposit, the bank is the debtor and the customer the creditor.
  • Hypothecation involves no transfer of possession; pledge does.
  • An NPA arises after more than 90 days overdue, not 90 days from disbursement.
  • Section 138 notice must be sent within 30 days of learning of dishonour; the drawer then gets 15 days to pay.
  • DICGC cover is ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank, combining principal and interest.

Quick recap

Relationship: debtor-creditor plus trustee, agent, bailee, lessor. KYC with risk categorisation. NI Act: promissory note, bill, cheque; holder in due course; crossings. Section 138: 3-month validity, 30-day notice, 15 days to pay, complaint within a month. Payment systems: NEFT, RTGS, IMPS, UPI, CTS, NACH. Charges: pledge, hypothecation, mortgage, lien, assignment. NPA after 90 days. PSL 40%. DICGC ₹5 lakh.
