English Language: SBI Clerk practice questions

15 free questions with answers and explanations on English Language, from the SBI Clerk (SBI Junior Associate (Prelims)) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this section covers

The English Language section of SBI Clerk (Junior Associate) checks basic grammar, vocabulary and reading skills. Expect short comprehension passages, fillers, error spotting, sentence improvement, para jumbles, synonyms and antonyms, idioms, spellings and one-word substitution. Revise subject–verb agreement, tenses, prepositions of time, and common errors such as redundancy ('return back') and 'one of' with plural nouns. Read passages carefully and pick answers stated in the text rather than from general knowledge. Accuracy matters because of negative marking.

English Language questions

Question 1

Read the passage and answer the question that follows. Lakshmi runs a small tailoring shop in her village. For years she kept her earnings in a tin box at home. Last year a bank correspondent visited the village and helped her open a savings account using her Aadhaar card. Now she deposits money every week and has also started a small recurring deposit for her daughter's education. She says the account has given her both safety and discipline. Where did Lakshmi keep her earnings before she opened the account?

  1. With a local moneylender
  2. In a tin box at home
  3. In her daughter's school
  4. In a post office account
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. In a tin box at home

The passage clearly says that for years she kept her earnings in a tin box at home. A moneylender, school or post office is never mentioned, so those options are unsupported.

Question 2

Read the passage and answer the question that follows. Lakshmi runs a small tailoring shop in her village. For years she kept her earnings in a tin box at home. Last year a bank correspondent visited the village and helped her open a savings account using her Aadhaar card. Now she deposits money every week and has also started a small recurring deposit for her daughter's education. She says the account has given her both safety and discipline. Why has Lakshmi started a recurring deposit?

  1. To buy new machines for her tailoring shop
  2. To repay a loan taken from the bank
  3. To pay the bank correspondent's fee
  4. To save for her daughter's education
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. To save for her daughter's education

The passage states that she started a small recurring deposit for her daughter's education. Shop machines, loan repayment and fees are not mentioned anywhere, so these options are distractors.

Question 3

Read the passage and answer the question that follows. Lakshmi runs a small tailoring shop in her village. For years she kept her earnings in a tin box at home. Last year a bank correspondent visited the village and helped her open a savings account using her Aadhaar card. Now she deposits money every week and has also started a small recurring deposit for her daughter's education. She says the account has given her both safety and discipline. Which word is closest in meaning to 'discipline' as used in the passage?

  1. Punishment
  2. Freedom
  3. Regularity
  4. Confusion
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Regularity

Here 'discipline' refers to the habit of saving money every week in a regular, controlled way. 'Punishment' is another meaning of the word but does not fit this context; 'freedom' and 'confusion' do not fit at all.

Question 4

Fill in the blank with the correct word. Mr. Sharma has been working at this branch ________ five years.

  1. since
  2. for
  3. from
  4. by
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. for

'For' is used with a period of time (five years), while 'since' is used with a point of time (since 2020). 'From' and 'by' do not fit with the present perfect continuous here.

Question 5

Fill in the blank with the correct verb form. Neither the manager nor the clerks ________ present at yesterday's meeting.

  1. was
  2. is
  3. has been
  4. were
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. were

With 'neither…nor', the verb agrees with the nearer subject. The nearer subject 'clerks' is plural, and 'yesterday' needs the past tense, so 'were' is correct, not 'was'.

Question 6

Which part of the sentence contains an error? She don't know (A) / how to fill (B) / the deposit slip (C) / correctly. (D)

  1. (A)
  2. (B)
  3. (C)
  4. (D)
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. (A)

The subject 'She' is third-person singular, so the correct form is 'She doesn't know', not 'She don't know'. The rest of the sentence is grammatically correct.

Question 7

Which part of the sentence contains an error? The cashier (A) / gave me (B) / a ten-rupees note (C) / as change. (D)

  1. (A)
  2. (B)
  3. (C)
  4. (D)
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. (C)

When a number and a noun together act as an adjective before another noun, the noun stays singular: 'a ten-rupee note'. Hence 'ten-rupees' in part (C) is wrong.

Question 8

Which part of the sentence contains an error? One of my friend (A) / has opened (B) / a savings account (C) / in this bank. (D)

  1. (A)
  2. (B)
  3. (C)
  4. (D)
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. (A)

'One of' is always followed by a plural noun, so it should be 'One of my friends'. The verb 'has opened' is correct because the subject 'One' is singular.

Question 9

Improve the part in capitals, if needed. After signing the form, he RETURNED BACK it to the officer.

  1. returned
  2. returned again
  3. had return back
  4. No improvement required
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. returned

'Return' already means to give or go back, so 'returned back' is redundant. The correct sentence is 'he returned it to the officer'. 'Had return back' is also ungrammatical.

Question 10

Arrange the sentences P, Q, R and S in a logical order. P: He filled in the withdrawal form carefully. Q: Amit went to the bank to withdraw some cash. R: Finally, the cashier handed him the money. S: He then submitted the form at the counter.

  1. QSPR
  2. PQSR
  3. QPRS
  4. QPSR
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. QPSR

Q introduces Amit going to the bank, P describes filling the form, S ('then') shows him submitting it and R ('Finally') ends with receiving the cash. This is the only natural sequence.

Question 11

Select the word that means the same as ABUNDANT.

  1. Scarce
  2. Expensive
  3. Plentiful
  4. Hidden
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Plentiful

'Abundant' means available in large quantities, which matches 'plentiful'. 'Scarce' is its opposite, while 'expensive' and 'hidden' are unrelated to quantity.

Question 12

Select the word that is the opposite of ANCIENT.

  1. Old
  2. Modern
  3. Historic
  4. Antique
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Modern

'Ancient' means belonging to the very distant past. Its opposite is 'modern'. 'Old', 'historic' and 'antique' all carry a sense of age, so they are similar in meaning, not opposite.

Question 13

What does the idiom 'to break the ice' mean?

  1. To start a conversation in an awkward situation
  2. To cause serious damage to something
  3. To end a long friendship suddenly
  4. To complete a difficult task quickly
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. To start a conversation in an awkward situation

'To break the ice' means to say or do something that eases tension and starts a conversation, for example among strangers. It has nothing to do with damage, ending friendships or finishing tasks.

Question 14

Which of the following words is spelt correctly?

  1. Comittee
  2. Commitee
  3. Comitee
  4. Committee
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Committee

The correct spelling has double 'm', double 't' and double 'e': C-O-M-M-I-T-T-E-E. Each wrong option drops one of these doubled letters, a frequent spelling error in exams.

Question 15

Give one word for: 'A person who travels on foot along a road'.

  1. Passenger
  2. Pilgrim
  3. Pedestrian
  4. Commuter
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Pedestrian

A pedestrian is a person walking on a road. A passenger travels in a vehicle, a pilgrim travels to a holy place, and a commuter travels regularly between home and work, usually by vehicle.
