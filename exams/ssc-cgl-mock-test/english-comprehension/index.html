English Comprehension: SSC CGL practice questions

15 free questions with answers and explanations on English Comprehension, from the SSC CGL (Combined Graduate Level (Tier 1)) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this section covers

English Comprehension checks vocabulary, grammar and reading skills. Vocabulary items include synonyms, antonyms, idioms and phrases, one-word substitutions and spelling. Grammar items include error spotting, fill-in-the-blanks, sentence improvement, active-passive voice and direct-indirect speech, testing rules such as subject-verb agreement, fixed prepositions and correlative conjunctions. Reading comprehension and cloze passages test understanding of the main idea and word meanings in context. Revise grammar rules with examples, learn commonly confused words, and read short editorials regularly to build speed and contextual understanding.

English Comprehension questions

Question 1

Choose the word closest in meaning to the word in capitals. The minister gave a CANDID reply when reporters asked about the delay.

  1. Cautious
  2. Rigid
  3. Frank
  4. Secretive
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Frank

'Candid' means honest and straightforward in speech, which matches 'frank'. 'Secretive' is nearly its opposite, while 'cautious' and 'rigid' describe unrelated qualities.

Question 2

Choose the word opposite in meaning to the word in capitals. My grandfather led a FRUGAL life even after he became rich.

  1. Thrifty
  2. Extravagant
  3. Careful
  4. Modest
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Extravagant

'Frugal' means careful and sparing with money. Its opposite is 'extravagant', meaning spending too much. 'Thrifty' and 'careful' are synonyms, not antonyms.

Question 3

Select the most appropriate meaning of the idiom 'to burn the midnight oil'.

  1. To work or study late into the night
  2. To waste money on useless things
  3. To lose one's temper suddenly
  4. To destroy evidence of a mistake
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. To work or study late into the night

'To burn the midnight oil' refers to staying up late to work or study, originally by the light of an oil lamp. It has nothing to do with waste or anger.

Question 4

Select the one word for: 'A person who is unable to pay his or her debts'.

  1. Indolent
  2. Inveterate
  3. Insolent
  4. Insolvent
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Insolvent

'Insolvent' describes a person or firm unable to pay debts. 'Insolent' means rude, 'indolent' means lazy and 'inveterate' means habitual; they look similar but differ in meaning.

Question 5

Which of the following words is spelt correctly?

  1. Embarass
  2. Embarrass
  3. Emberrass
  4. Embarras
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Embarrass

The correct spelling is 'embarrass', with double 'r' and double 's'. The other options drop one of the doubled letters or change the vowel.

Question 6

Identify the segment that contains a grammatical error. Neither the manager nor his assistants / was present / at the meeting / held yesterday.

  1. Neither the manager nor his assistants
  2. held yesterday
  3. at the meeting
  4. was present
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. was present

With 'neither…nor', the verb agrees with the nearer subject. Here the nearer subject 'assistants' is plural, so 'was present' should be 'were present'.

Question 7

Identify the segment that contains a grammatical error. He is senior than me / in the office / by three years / and earns more.

  1. and earns more
  2. in the office
  3. He is senior than me
  4. by three years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. He is senior than me

Latin comparatives such as senior, junior, superior and inferior take 'to', not 'than'. The correct form is 'He is senior to me'.

Question 8

Fill in the blank with the most appropriate word. The thief was caught ______ the act of breaking the lock.

  1. on
  2. by
  3. in
  4. at
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. in

The fixed expression is 'caught in the act', meaning caught while doing something wrong. The other prepositions do not form this idiom.

Question 9

Fill in the blank with the most appropriate word. Hardly had I reached the station ______ the train left.

  1. when
  2. then
  3. but
  4. than
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. when

'Hardly' and 'scarcely' are followed by 'when' (or 'before'). 'Than' is used with 'no sooner', which is the usual source of confusion.

Question 10

Select the most appropriate improvement for the underlined part. The number of students in the class 'are increasing' every year.

  1. is increasing
  2. were increasing
  3. are increased
  4. have been increasing
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. is increasing

The subject is 'the number', which is singular, so the verb must be singular: 'is increasing'. 'A number of students' would take a plural verb, but 'the number of' does not.

Question 11

Select the correct passive form of: Who wrote this letter?

  1. By whom has this letter been written?
  2. Who was written this letter by?
  3. By whom this letter was written?
  4. By whom was this letter written?
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. By whom was this letter written?

An interrogative with 'who' becomes 'by whom' in the passive, keeping question word order: By whom + was + object + past participle. The present perfect option changes the tense.

Question 12

Select the correct indirect form of: She said to me, "Please help me."

  1. She told to me that please help her.
  2. She requested me to help her.
  3. She requested me that I help me.
  4. She said me to please help her.
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. She requested me to help her.

A polite request with 'please' is reported with 'requested', and the imperative becomes a to-infinitive. The pronoun 'me' changes to 'her' to refer back to the speaker.

Question 13

Read the passage and answer the question. Rainwater harvesting is an old practice that has found new relevance in Indian cities. As groundwater levels fall, many housing societies now collect rooftop runoff and channel it into recharge pits instead of letting it flow into drains. The method is inexpensive and needs little maintenance, yet its benefits depend on cleaning the filters regularly before every monsoon. According to the passage, what do the benefits of rainwater harvesting depend on?

  1. The number of drains available in the city
  2. Regular cleaning of filters before the monsoon
  3. The total cost of building large recharge pits
  4. Housing societies recruiting full-time staff
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Regular cleaning of filters before the monsoon

The last sentence says the benefits 'depend on cleaning the filters regularly before every monsoon'. The passage calls the method inexpensive and low-maintenance, so cost and staffing are not the limiting factors.

Question 14

Read the passage and answer the question. Rainwater harvesting is an old practice that has found new relevance in Indian cities. As groundwater levels fall, many housing societies now collect rooftop runoff and channel it into recharge pits instead of letting it flow into drains. The method is inexpensive and needs little maintenance, yet its benefits depend on cleaning the filters regularly before every monsoon. Which word is closest in meaning to 'relevance' as used in the passage?

  1. Expense
  2. Importance
  3. Difficulty
  4. Antiquity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Importance

The passage says an old practice has found new relevance because groundwater is falling, i.e. it has become important or applicable again. 'Antiquity' refers to oldness, the opposite idea.

Question 15

Choose the word opposite in meaning to the word in capitals. The fame of most viral videos is EPHEMERAL.

  1. Permanent
  2. Transient
  3. Fleeting
  4. Momentary
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Permanent

'Ephemeral' means lasting a very short time. 'Fleeting', 'transient' and 'momentary' are all synonyms, so the only antonym is 'permanent'.
