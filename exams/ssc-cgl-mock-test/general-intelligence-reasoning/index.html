General Intelligence & Reasoning: SSC CGL practice questions

15 free questions with answers and explanations on General Intelligence & Reasoning, from the SSC CGL (Combined Graduate Level (Tier 1)) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this section covers

This section tests verbal and non-verbal reasoning: analogies, classification (odd one out), number and letter series, coding-decoding, blood relations, directions, syllogisms, ranking, calendars, clocks, mathematical operations, seating arrangement and inequalities, along with figure-based items such as mirror images and paper folding. Most questions are short and rule-based, so speed comes from recognising common patterns: squares and cubes, alphabet positions, odd-day counting and the clock-angle formula. When revising, practise drawing quick diagrams for directions and family trees, use Venn diagrams for syllogisms, and always verify a found rule against every given pair before choosing an option.

General Intelligence & Reasoning questions

Question 1

Select the number that completes the analogy. 11 : 120 :: 13 : ?

  1. 168
  2. 169
  3. 156
  4. 170
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 168

Each first term is squared and then 1 is subtracted: 11² − 1 = 120. Applying the same rule, 13² − 1 = 169 − 1 = 168. The option 169 forgets the subtraction step.

Question 2

Select the number that does NOT belong to the group. 27, 64, 100, 125

  1. 27
  2. 100
  3. 125
  4. 64
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 100

27, 64 and 125 are perfect cubes (3³, 4³ and 5³). 100 is a perfect square (10²) but not a perfect cube, so it is the odd one out.

Question 3

Find the next term of the series. 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ?

  1. 44
  2. 36
  3. 42
  4. 40
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 42

The terms are 1×2, 2×3, 3×4, 4×5 and 5×6, so the next term is 6×7 = 42. Equivalently the differences 4, 6, 8, 10 increase by 2, giving 30 + 12.

Question 4

Find the next pair in the series. AZ, BY, CX, ?

  1. DW
  2. EW
  3. DV
  4. DX
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. DW

The first letters move forward one step (A, B, C, D) while the second letters move backward one step (Z, Y, X, W). Hence the next pair is DW.

Question 5

In a certain code language, CHAIR is written as AFYGP. How will SOFA be written in that language?

  1. RMDY
  2. UQHC
  3. QMDY
  4. QMDZ
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. QMDY

Every letter is moved two places back in the alphabet: C→A, H→F, A→Y, I→G, R→P. So S→Q, O→M, F→D and A→Y, giving QMDY. UQHC wrongly shifts letters forward.

Question 6

If ROSE is coded as 6821, CHAIR as 73456 and PREACH as 961473, what is the code for SEARCH?

  1. 216473
  2. 214673
  3. 214763
  4. 241673
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 214673

From the three codes: S = 2, E = 1, A = 4, R = 6, C = 7 and H = 3. Writing them in the order S-E-A-R-C-H gives 214673.

Question 7

If 'A + B' means A is the mother of B and 'A × B' means A is the brother of B, then what does 'P × Q + R' mean about P with respect to R?

  1. P is the father of R
  2. P is the paternal uncle of R
  3. P is the maternal uncle of R
  4. P is the brother of R
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. P is the maternal uncle of R

P × Q means P is Q's brother, and Q + R means Q is R's mother. The brother of one's mother is one's maternal uncle, so P is R's maternal uncle.

Question 8

A person is facing North. He turns 90° clockwise, then 135° anticlockwise, and then 180° clockwise. Which direction is he facing now?

  1. South-East
  2. North-East
  3. North-West
  4. South-West
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. South-East

Measuring clockwise from North: 0° + 90° = 90° (East), 90° − 135° = −45° (North-West), then −45° + 180° = 135°, which is South-East. North-West is the position before the last turn.

Question 9

Statements: Some pens are books. No book is a bag. Conclusions: I. Some pens are not bags. II. Some bags are pens.

  1. Only conclusion I follows
  2. Neither I nor II follows
  3. Both I and II follow
  4. Only conclusion II follows
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Only conclusion I follows

The pens that are books cannot be bags because no book is a bag, so 'some pens are not bags' is definite. Nothing tells us whether any bag is a pen, so II is only a possibility.

Question 10

In a class, Neha is 12th from the top and 28th from the bottom. How many students are there in the class?

  1. 41
  2. 38
  3. 40
  4. 39
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 39

Total = (rank from top) + (rank from bottom) − 1 = 12 + 28 − 1 = 39. Adding without subtracting 1 counts Neha twice and gives the wrong answer 40.

Question 11

On which day of the week did 15 August 1947 fall?

  1. Friday
  2. Sunday
  3. Saturday
  4. Thursday
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Friday

1600 years give 0 odd days, 300 years give 1 and 46 years give 4, so 1946 years give 5. 1 January–15 August 1947 has 227 days = 3 odd days. Total 8 ≡ 1 odd day, so the date fell on a Friday.

Question 12

What is the angle between the hour hand and the minute hand of a clock at 4:20?

  1. 5°
  2. 0°
  3. 20°
  4. 10°
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 10°

Angle = |30H − 5.5M| = |30 × 4 − 5.5 × 20| = |120 − 110| = 10°. The hour hand moves 10° beyond the 4 in 20 minutes, which is why the hands do not overlap.

Question 13

If 3 # 4 = 25 and 5 # 12 = 169, then what is 6 # 8?

  1. 100
  2. 196
  3. 48
  4. 14
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 100

The rule is a # b = a² + b²: 9 + 16 = 25 and 25 + 144 = 169. So 6 # 8 = 36 + 64 = 100. The option 196 is (6 + 8)², a common slip.

Question 14

Five friends A, B, C, D and E sit in a row facing North. C sits exactly in the middle and A sits to the immediate right of C. B sits at one of the extreme ends, and D does not sit next to C. Who sits to the immediate left of C?

  1. B
  2. E
  3. D
  4. A
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. E

C is 3rd and A is 4th from the left. B and D must take the two ends (D cannot be 2nd, as that is next to C), so the only seat left beside C, the 2nd one, goes to E.

Question 15

Statement: P > Q ≥ R = S < T Conclusions: I. P > S II. Q > T

  1. Only conclusion II follows
  2. Neither I nor II follows
  3. Both I and II follow
  4. Only conclusion I follows
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Only conclusion I follows

P > Q ≥ R = S gives P > S, so I is true. Q and T are both linked to S in opposite directions (Q ≥ S < T), so no definite relation exists between Q and T; II does not follow.
