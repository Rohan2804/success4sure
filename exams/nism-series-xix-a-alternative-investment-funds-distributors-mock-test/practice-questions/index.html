Free NISM Series XIX-A practice questions

Question 1 · Overview of Alternative Investments

An HNI says she wants to add private equity mainly because it is 'uncorrelated with everything'. What is the most accurate response?

  1. Private equity has zero correlation with listed markets by regulation
  2. Private equity always moves opposite to listed equity
  3. Private assets can diversify a portfolio, but reported low correlation is partly due to infrequent, appraisal-based valuations
  4. Correlation is irrelevant for illiquid assets
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Private assets can diversify a portfolio, but reported low correlation is partly due to infrequent, appraisal-based valuations

Smoothed, periodic valuations understate measured volatility and correlation, so the diversification benefit is real but overstated by reported numbers.

Question 2 · Alternative Investment Funds in India

A fund that provides risk capital to early-stage start-ups would ordinarily register under which AIF category?

  1. Category I
  2. Category II
  3. Category III
  4. It cannot register as an AIF
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Category I

Venture capital funds, including angel funds, are a sub-category of Category I AIFs, which invest in sectors seen as socially or economically desirable.

Question 3 · Concepts in the AIF Industry

An investor committed ₹2 crore to a Category II AIF. The manager has so far called 40% of commitments. How much commitment remains uncalled?

  1. ₹80 lakh
  2. ₹1.2 crore
  3. ₹2 crore
  4. ₹1.6 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹1.2 crore

Called = 40% × ₹2 crore = ₹80 lakh; uncalled = ₹2 crore − ₹80 lakh = ₹1.2 crore.

Question 4 · AIF Regulatory Framework

Ignoring accredited investors and employees/directors of the manager, the minimum investment an individual must make in an AIF is:

  1. ₹10 lakh
  2. ₹25 lakh
  3. ₹50 lakh
  4. ₹1 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹1 crore

The general minimum ticket is ₹1 crore; a lower ₹25 lakh limit applies to employees or directors of the AIF/manager, and accredited investors are exempt.

Question 5 · Alternative Investment Fund Structuring

In a trust-structured AIF, the document that governs the investor's commitment and drawdown obligations is the:

  1. Contribution agreement
  2. Memorandum of association
  3. Certificate of incorporation
  4. Scheme information document
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Contribution agreement

The contribution agreement between investor, trustee and manager records commitment, drawdowns, fees and other rights.

Question 6 · Risk and Return – Fund and Investor Perspective

For a Category I or II AIF, the most appropriate return measure that captures the timing of capital calls and distributions is:

  1. Simple average annual return
  2. Time-weighted daily NAV return
  3. Dividend yield
  4. Internal rate of return (IRR)
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Internal rate of return (IRR)

Because the manager controls the timing of cash flows, money-weighted IRR is the standard measure for closed-ended private funds.

Question 7 · Investment Process and Governance of Funds

In an AIF, the investment committee's principal role is to:

  1. Market the fund to new investors
  2. Approve investment and exit decisions in line with the fund's strategy and PPM
  3. Act as custodian of the fund's securities
  4. Calculate tax payable by each investor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Approve investment and exit decisions in line with the fund's strategy and PPM

The investment committee scrutinises and approves deals; its members are responsible for decisions taken within the PPM framework.

Question 8 · Fund Due Diligence – Investor Perspective

When doing due diligence on a first-time fund manager, an investor should place the greatest weight on:

  1. The fund's brochure design
  2. The team's attributable track record, stability and alignment of interest
  3. Projected IRRs in the pitch deck alone
  4. The number of distributors selling the fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The team's attributable track record, stability and alignment of interest

Without a fund-level history, the team's past deals, cohesion and skin in the game are the best evidence of capability.

Question 9 · Legal Documentation and Negotiation – Investor Perspective

The Private Placement Memorandum (PPM) of an AIF is mainly meant to:

  1. Serve as the trust's registration certificate
  2. Act as a contract note for each drawdown
  3. Replace KYC documents
  4. Disclose the fund's strategy, risks, fees, terms and the manager's background to prospective investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Disclose the fund's strategy, risks, fees, terms and the manager's background to prospective investors

The PPM is the primary offer document giving investors the information needed to make an informed decision.

Question 10 · Fund Monitoring, Reporting and Exit

During the life of a close-ended AIF, which of the following is a common exit route for the fund's portfolio investments?

  1. Initial public offering or strategic sale of the investee company
  2. Redemption of units by the investee company
  3. Daily repurchase by the AIF
  4. Conversion into bank deposits
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Initial public offering or strategic sale of the investee company

PE funds realise value by taking companies public, selling to strategic buyers or other funds, or by buybacks.

Question 11 · Valuation

Valuation of unlisted investments held by Category I and II AIFs is required to be carried out by:

  1. The distributor who sold the fund
  2. The investee company's own management only
  3. An independent valuer, following a disclosed valuation methodology
  4. Any investor holding the most units
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. An independent valuer, following a disclosed valuation methodology

Independence avoids conflicts, since manager fees and reported performance depend on valuations.

Question 12 · Taxation – India Specific

For Category I and II AIFs, income other than business income is generally taxed:

  1. Only at the fund level at the maximum marginal rate
  2. Neither in the fund's nor the investors' hands
  3. In the hands of investors, as if they had invested directly (pass-through)
  4. Only when the fund is wound up
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. In the hands of investors, as if they had invested directly (pass-through)

Indian tax law gives Category I and II AIFs pass-through status for non-business income.

Question 13 · Good Practices

An AIF distributor is approached by a retired client with ₹1.2 crore of total savings who needs regular income. Recommending that she commit ₹1 crore to a 10-year PE fund is:

  1. Suitable, because she meets the minimum ticket size
  2. Suitable, because PE returns are higher
  3. Unsuitable, because it concentrates her savings in an illiquid product that does not match her income need
  4. Acceptable if she signs a risk disclaimer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Unsuitable, because it concentrates her savings in an illiquid product that does not match her income need

Meeting the minimum investment does not make a product suitable; liquidity needs and concentration matter.

Question 14 · Overview of Alternative Investments

Which of the following is generally treated as an alternative asset class?

  1. Listed large-cap shares
  2. Government treasury bills
  3. Bank fixed deposits
  4. Venture capital
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Venture capital

Venture capital invests in unlisted early-stage companies and falls outside traditional stocks, bonds and cash.

Question 15 · Alternative Investment Funds in India

A private equity fund investing in growth-stage unlisted companies, with no special incentives and no leverage, would typically be registered as:

  1. Category I AIF
  2. Category II AIF
  3. Category III AIF
  4. A mutual fund scheme
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Category II AIF

Category II is the residual category for funds such as PE and debt funds that are neither Category I nor Category III and do not use leverage.
