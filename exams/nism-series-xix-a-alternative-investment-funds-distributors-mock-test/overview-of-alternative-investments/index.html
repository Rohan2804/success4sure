Overview of Alternative Investments: NISM Series XIX-A practice questions

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces alternative investments and how they differ from traditional listed equity, bonds and cash. It covers private equity, venture capital, private credit, real estate, infrastructure, hedge-fund strategies and commodities, along with their common features: illiquidity, long horizons, higher minimum investments, complex fees, limited transparency and appraisal-based pricing. Learners should understand why investors use alternatives — diversification, access to the illiquidity premium and specialist skill — and the risks, including valuation uncertainty and manager dispersion. Focus on comparing alternatives with traditional assets and identifying which investors they suit.

Overview of Alternative Investments questions

Question 1

Which characteristic is most typical of alternative investments compared with listed large-cap shares?

  1. Higher minimum ticket sizes
  2. Daily pricing on a recognised stock exchange with continuous liquidity
  3. Lower fees because managers are passive
  4. Full transparency of holdings every day
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Higher minimum ticket sizes

Alternatives usually require large minimum investments, are illiquid and charge higher, more complex fees. Daily exchange pricing and full transparency are features of listed securities.

Question 2

The extra return investors expect for locking up capital in long-dated private funds is known as the:

  1. Illiquidity premium
  2. Equity risk premium on large-cap listed shares
  3. Term premium on government bonds
  4. Currency premium
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Illiquidity premium

Investors demand compensation for being unable to sell easily; this is the illiquidity premium. The equity risk premium relates to listed equity versus risk-free assets.

Question 3

Why is the dispersion of returns between top and bottom managers usually wider in private equity than in large-cap equity mutual funds?

  1. Private equity managers are not allowed to diversify across companies at all
  2. All private equity funds track the same index
  3. Outcomes depend heavily on manager skill in sourcing and value creation
  4. Regulators fix the returns of private equity funds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Outcomes depend heavily on manager skill in sourcing and value creation

Private equity returns depend on deal sourcing, negotiation and active value creation, so manager selection matters greatly. Listed large-cap funds hold similar stocks and their returns cluster more closely.

Question 4

Which of the following is a real asset rather than a financial claim?

  1. A corporate bond issued by a toll road company
  2. A toll road owned by an infrastructure fund
  3. A listed share of an infrastructure company
  4. Units of a liquid mutual fund that holds treasury bills
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A toll road owned by an infrastructure fund

A toll road is a tangible, real asset. Bonds, shares and fund units are financial claims, even if they relate to infrastructure companies.

Question 5

An investor sees that a private equity fund's quarterly reported returns have a very low standard deviation. A key caution is that:

  1. Smoothed, appraisal-based valuations can understate true volatility
  2. Low volatility proves the fund carries no market risk at all and can replace debt holdings
  3. Private equity is always less risky than government bonds
  4. Reported volatility of private funds is audited daily
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Smoothed, appraisal-based valuations can understate true volatility

Private fund valuations are periodic and judgement-based, which smooths returns and understates risk. Low reported volatility does not mean the absence of market risk.

Question 6

Private credit funds typically earn returns by:

  1. Buying listed shares and holding them for dividends
  2. Investing only in gold and silver
  3. Lending to companies and earning interest and fees
  4. Holding cash in bank deposits
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Lending to companies and earning interest and fees

Private credit funds lend directly to companies, earning interest, fees and sometimes equity kickers. They are a form of debt investment, not listed equity or commodities.

Question 7

For which investor is a meaningful allocation to alternative investments most suitable?

  1. A wealthy family with long horizon and surplus liquidity
  2. A retiree who needs monthly income from a small corpus and may need cash for medical bills
  3. A young saver building an emergency fund
  4. A trader who needs daily access to all funds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A wealthy family with long horizon and surplus liquidity

Alternatives suit investors with long horizons, large wealth and no need for near-term liquidity. Those needing regular income or liquidity are poorly matched to locked-in products.

Question 8

Venture capital differs from buyout private equity mainly because venture capital:

  1. Acquires controlling stakes in mature companies using significant debt financing
  2. Invests only in listed companies
  3. Backs early-stage firms with minority stakes
  4. Avoids technology businesses
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Backs early-stage firms with minority stakes

Venture capital funds early-stage, often loss-making companies and usually takes minority stakes. Buyouts acquire control of mature businesses, typically with leverage.
