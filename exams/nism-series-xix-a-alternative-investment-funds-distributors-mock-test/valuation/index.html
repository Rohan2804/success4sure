Valuation: NISM Series XIX-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Valuation, from the NISM Series XIX-A (Alternative Investment Funds (Category I and II) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how AIFs value their portfolio investments. It introduces fair value and the main approaches — market (comparable companies and transactions), income (discounted cash flow) and cost-based approaches such as the price of a recent investment — along with adjustments for control, marketability and liquidation preferences. It covers SEBI's requirements on independent valuers, valuation frequency and the use of IPEV or industry-endorsed guidelines, and the importance of a consistent valuation policy. Focus on choosing the right method for a company's stage and on the risks of stale or biased valuations.

Valuation questions

Question 1

A company has EBITDA of ₹30 crore and comparable companies trade at 8x EV/EBITDA. If net debt is ₹60 crore, the equity value is:

  1. ₹240 crore
  2. ₹180 crore
  3. ₹300 crore
  4. ₹210 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹180 crore

Enterprise value = 30 × 8 = ₹240 crore. Equity value = EV − net debt = 240 − 60 = ₹180 crore. ₹240 crore is the enterprise value, not equity.

Question 2

Which valuation approach is most suitable for an infrastructure asset with long, predictable contracted cash flows?

  1. Price of the most recent funding round
  2. Discounted cash flow
  3. Liquidation value
  4. Book value of share capital
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Discounted cash flow

Predictable long-term cash flows suit DCF valuation. Recent round prices fit early-stage companies, and liquidation value suits distressed situations.

Question 3

Under SEBI's framework, the valuation of AIF investments must be done by an independent valuer. 'Independent' mainly means the valuer:

  1. Is a large investor in the fund
  2. Is the fund's own investment committee
  3. Is appointed by the investee company
  4. Is not an associate of the manager or sponsor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Is not an associate of the manager or sponsor

Independence requires the valuer to be free from relationships with the manager, sponsor and trustee that could bias valuations. Investors or investees cannot provide unbiased values.

Question 4

A PE fund holds preference shares with a 1x liquidation preference in a company that may be sold at a modest valuation. The valuation should:

  1. Reflect the preference's effect on proceeds
  2. Ignore the preference and value all shares equally
  3. Assume the preference will never be used
  4. Use only the par value of preference shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Reflect the preference's effect on proceeds

Liquidation preferences change how sale proceeds are distributed, especially at lower exit values, so they must be reflected in fair value.

Question 5

Why might an AIF apply a discount for lack of control to a minority stake?

  1. A minority holder cannot direct company decisions
  2. Minority stakes are always worth more than controlling stakes
  3. SEBI requires a fixed 50% discount on all minority stakes
  4. Minority stakes cannot be valued at all
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A minority holder cannot direct company decisions

Without control, the investor cannot set strategy, dividends or a sale, which can reduce the value per share compared with a controlling stake. There is no fixed regulatory discount.

Question 6

A fund continues to value a start-up at its last round price two years after investment, though the company has missed every milestone. This is a concern because:

  1. Last-round price is never allowed in any valuation
  2. The valuer is required to use book value
  3. Missed milestones always increase value
  4. Stale values overstate NAV and performance
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Stale values overstate NAV and performance

Valuations must be updated for developments; keeping an outdated round price after poor performance overstates value and can mislead investors.

Question 7

The 'comparable transactions' method values a company using:

  1. Book value of the company's fixed assets
  2. Prices paid in recent acquisitions of similar firms
  3. The company's share capital
  4. The fund's original cost
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Prices paid in recent acquisitions of similar firms

Precedent transaction multiples reflect what buyers have paid for similar businesses, often including a control premium, unlike trading multiples of listed peers.

Question 8

A company is expected to produce free cash flow of ₹12 crore next year, growing at 4% forever. At a 14% discount rate, its enterprise value is:

  1. ₹85.7 crore
  2. ₹120 crore
  3. ₹300 crore
  4. ₹66.7 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹120 crore

Using the growing perpetuity formula, EV = 12 ÷ (0.14 − 0.04) = ₹120 crore. ₹85.7 crore ignores growth (12 ÷ 0.14).
