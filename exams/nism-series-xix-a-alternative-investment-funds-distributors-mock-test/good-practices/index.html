Good Practices: NISM Series XIX-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Good Practices, from the NISM Series XIX-A (Alternative Investment Funds (Category I and II) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers ethical and regulatory standards for AIF distributors. It explains SEBI's rules on distribution — NISM certification, the direct plan option, disclosure and payment of distribution fees (with limits on upfront commission for Category I and II), suitability and appropriateness assessment, KYC and AML obligations — and good conduct such as fair disclosure of risks, fees and lock-ins, avoiding mis-selling and conflicts, and maintaining records. Learners should be able to judge whether a recommendation is suitable, identify mis-selling and know what must be disclosed to an investor before they commit.

Good Practices questions

Question 1

Before recommending a Category II AIF, a distributor should explain to the client that:

  1. The client can redeem at NAV any time
  2. Returns are assured by the sponsor
  3. The money will be locked in for years
  4. The fund is regulated as a mutual fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The money will be locked in for years

Category II AIFs are close-ended and illiquid; investors must understand the lock-in and drawdown commitments. Returns are not assured and AIFs are not mutual funds.

Question 2

A distributor tells a client that an AIF is 'SEBI-approved, so it cannot lose money'. This is:

  1. Misleading, as registration is not a guarantee
  2. Accurate, as SEBI guarantees AIF returns
  3. Acceptable if the client is an HNI
  4. Accurate for Category I funds only
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Misleading, as registration is not a guarantee

SEBI registration means the fund is regulated, not that its returns are protected. Claiming otherwise is mis-selling.

Question 3

Which of the following must a distributor disclose to the investor?

  1. Personal details of other clients
  2. The distribution fee earned from the AIF
  3. The manager's internal deal pipeline
  4. The SEBI inspection reports of other distributors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The distribution fee earned from the AIF

Distributors must disclose commissions and fees they receive from the AIF so the investor understands potential conflicts. Other clients' information must remain confidential.

Question 4

An AIF distributor's KYC and AML obligations include:

  1. Accepting cash in large amounts to speed onboarding
  2. Ignoring politically exposed persons
  3. Skipping KYC for accredited investors
  4. Verifying identity and source of funds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Verifying identity and source of funds

AML rules require verifying identity, beneficial ownership and source of funds, with enhanced checks for higher-risk clients like PEPs. Accredited investors still need KYC.

Question 5

A client wants to invest in an AIF through the direct plan after receiving advice from a SEBI-registered investment adviser. The distributor should:

  1. Insist that the client invests through the regular plan
  2. Charge the full distribution fee anyway
  3. Refuse to process the client's KYC
  4. Not claim distribution fee on that investment
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Not claim distribution fee on that investment

Direct plans carry no distribution fee; investors advised by registered investment advisers may use them. Pushing a regular plan for a fee would be improper.

Question 6

Which situation suggests possible mis-selling of an AIF?

  1. A family office with large surplus invests 5% of its portfolio
  2. An accredited investor invests after reading the PPM
  3. A client nearing retirement invests most of her savings in one illiquid fund
  4. A business owner commits after independent advice
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A client nearing retirement invests most of her savings in one illiquid fund

Putting most of a near-retiree's savings into one illiquid fund ignores liquidity needs and diversification, suggesting unsuitable advice.

Question 7

Why does SEBI restrict upfront distribution commissions for Category I and II AIFs?

  1. To increase the distributor's total income
  2. To guarantee higher returns to investors
  3. To reduce incentives for churning and mis-selling
  4. To allow early redemption
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. To reduce incentives for churning and mis-selling

Spreading commission as trail over the fund's life aligns the distributor's income with the investor's holding and reduces incentives to push products for upfront fees.

Question 8

A distributor keeps records of risk-profiling and suitability assessments for each AIF client. This mainly helps to:

  1. Avoid paying GST on distribution fees
  2. Increase the fund's IRR
  3. Bypass the minimum investment
  4. Show the advice was appropriate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Show the advice was appropriate

Records demonstrate that the distributor assessed suitability before recommending and are required for compliance and dispute resolution.
