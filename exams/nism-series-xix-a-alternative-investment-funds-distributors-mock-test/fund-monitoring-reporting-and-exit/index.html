Fund Monitoring, Reporting and Exit: NISM Series XIX-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Fund Monitoring, Reporting and Exit, from the NISM Series XIX-A (Alternative Investment Funds (Category I and II) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers what happens after investors commit. It explains periodic reporting to investors (capital account statements, valuation reports, portfolio updates), reporting to SEBI and the trustee, PPM compliance audits, monitoring of the manager, and exit routes for both the fund's portfolio investments and the investors themselves: IPO, strategic sale, secondary sale, buyback, in-specie distribution, secondaries and continuation funds. It also covers the end of a fund's tenure, extension and dissolution or liquidation options. Focus on which exits suit which situations and how investors keep track of a fund's progress.

Fund Monitoring, Reporting and Exit questions

Question 1

Which document shows an individual investor's contributions, distributions and share of NAV in an AIF?

  1. Capital account statement
  2. Private placement memorandum
  3. Trust deed
  4. Investment management agreement
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Capital account statement

A capital account statement tracks each investor's commitment, amounts called, distributions and current value. The PPM and trust deed are constitutional documents.

Question 2

A PE fund sells its stake in a portfolio company to another PE fund. This exit route is called a:

  1. Initial public offering
  2. Buyback by the promoter
  3. Secondary sale
  4. Write-off
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Secondary sale

Selling to another financial investor is a secondary sale. An IPO lists the shares, and a buyback involves the company or promoters repurchasing them.

Question 3

A close-ended AIF's tenure has ended but some unlisted holdings remain unsold. One option within SEBI's framework is to:

  1. Convert the scheme into an open-ended mutual fund
  2. Extend the tenure indefinitely without investor approval
  3. Distribute the holdings in specie to investors who agree
  4. Sell the holdings to the sponsor at book value without consent
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Distribute the holdings in specie to investors who agree

SEBI permits in-specie distribution of unliquidated investments with investor approval, or use of the dissolution route. Indefinite extension or forced sales to the sponsor are not allowed.

Question 4

Why is an IPO often an attractive exit for a PE fund?

  1. It guarantees the fund can sell its entire stake on listing day
  2. It eliminates lock-in requirements on pre-IPO shareholders
  3. It may achieve a high valuation and liquidity
  4. It avoids SEBI disclosure rules
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. It may achieve a high valuation and liquidity

Listing can deliver a strong valuation and a liquid market, but pre-IPO shareholders face lock-ins and full sale is rarely immediate.

Question 5

An investor in a 10-year AIF needs liquidity in year five. A realistic option is to:

  1. Redeem units from the fund at NAV on request
  2. Ask SEBI to cancel its commitment
  3. Stop paying capital calls without consequences
  4. Sell its interest on the secondary market
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Sell its interest on the secondary market

Close-ended AIFs do not offer redemption; investors needing liquidity can sell their interests to secondary buyers, typically at a discount and with manager consent.

Question 6

An AIF must report periodically to SEBI. A key purpose of such reporting is to allow SEBI to:

  1. Approve each individual investment in advance
  2. Calculate investors' income tax
  3. Set the fund's management fee
  4. Monitor the industry's investments and risks
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Monitor the industry's investments and risks

Periodic reporting enables SEBI to oversee activity, leverage, concentration and compliance across AIFs. SEBI does not pre-approve investments or set fees.

Question 7

A GP-led continuation vehicle typically gives existing investors:

  1. A compulsory transfer of all their units to the new vehicle
  2. A choice to sell or roll over into the new vehicle
  3. A guaranteed premium over NAV
  4. The right to manage the continuation vehicle
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A choice to sell or roll over into the new vehicle

In a continuation fund, selected assets move to a new vehicle; existing investors can usually sell for cash or roll their interest. Fairness requires an independent price process.

Question 8

The annual audit of compliance with the terms of the PPM is meant to:

  1. Verify the scheme's adherence to its PPM
  2. Replace the statutory audit of accounts
  3. Value each portfolio company
  4. Calculate carried interest
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Verify the scheme's adherence to its PPM

The PPM audit checks whether the scheme has followed its stated terms and disclosures. It complements, but does not replace, the statutory financial audit or valuations.
