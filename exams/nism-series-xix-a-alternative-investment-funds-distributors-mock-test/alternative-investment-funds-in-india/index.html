Alternative Investment Funds in India: NISM Series XIX-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Alternative Investment Funds in India, from the NISM Series XIX-A (Alternative Investment Funds (Category I and II) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains the AIF framework in India under the SEBI (Alternative Investment Funds) Regulations, 2012. It covers the definition of an AIF, what is excluded from it, and the three categories: Category I (venture capital including angel funds, SME, social impact, infrastructure and special situation funds), Category II (private equity, private debt and funds of funds without leverage) and Category III (hedge-fund style funds using complex strategies and leverage). It also reviews the growth of the AIF industry. Focus on classifying a fund by its strategy and on the key differences among the three categories.

Alternative Investment Funds in India questions

Question 1

A fund that invests in units of other Category I and II AIFs, without leverage, would normally be registered as:

  1. Category II AIF (fund of funds)
  2. Category I AIF as a social impact fund
  3. Category III AIF
  4. A mutual fund under SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Category II AIF (fund of funds)

Funds of AIFs are generally registered as Category II AIFs. Category III is for funds using complex strategies and leverage, and mutual funds have a separate framework.

Question 2

Which fund is NOT covered by the SEBI AIF Regulations?

  1. A private equity fund pooling money from HNIs
  2. A venture debt fund raising ₹200 crore privately
  3. A mutual fund registered under SEBI's mutual fund regulations
  4. A long-short fund for sophisticated investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A mutual fund registered under SEBI's mutual fund regulations

Mutual funds, collective investment schemes and certain other regulated vehicles are excluded from the AIF definition. Privately pooled PE, debt and hedge-style funds fall within it.

Question 3

Angel funds in India are a sub-category of:

  1. Category II AIF private equity funds investing in late-stage companies
  2. Category III AIF
  3. Category I AIF (venture capital funds)
  4. Portfolio management services
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Category I AIF (venture capital funds)

Angel funds are a sub-category of venture capital funds under Category I, pooling money from angel investors for start-ups. They are not PMS or Category III.

Question 4

A fund that invests in listed and unlisted securities of stressed companies, including through acquisition of stressed loans, falls in which sub-category?

  1. Special situation fund under Category I
  2. Infrastructure fund under Category I
  3. Hedge fund under Category III
  4. Fund of funds under Category II
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Special situation fund under Category I

Special situation funds invest in special situation assets such as stressed loans and securities of stressed companies and are a Category I sub-category.

Question 5

Which feature is allowed for Category III AIFs but not for Category I and II AIFs?

  1. Investment in unlisted companies of any kind, including early-stage start-ups
  2. Charging a management fee
  3. Leverage for investment purposes
  4. Raising money from accredited investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Leverage for investment purposes

Category III AIFs can use leverage, within SEBI limits, to make investments. Category I and II AIFs may borrow only temporarily for operational needs, and all categories can charge fees.

Question 6

A debt fund that lends to unlisted mid-sized companies without leverage would typically register as:

  1. Category I AIF as an SME fund
  2. Category III AIF
  3. Category II AIF
  4. A non-banking financial company
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Category II AIF

A private debt fund without leverage or special incentives is a Category II AIF. Category III is for complex trading strategies, and NBFCs are regulated by the RBI under a different framework.

Question 7

Category I AIFs are distinguished mainly because they invest in sectors that:

  1. Have the highest expected returns
  2. Are listed on recognised stock exchanges
  3. Government or regulators view as socially or economically desirable
  4. Require leverage to generate returns
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Government or regulators view as socially or economically desirable

Category I covers funds investing in start-ups, SMEs, social ventures, infrastructure and special situations, which policymakers consider desirable and may support with incentives.

Question 8

The rapid growth of commitments raised by AIFs in India over the last decade is best explained by:

  1. A legal requirement for banks to invest a fixed share of deposits in AIFs
  2. Rising HNI wealth and demand for private-market access
  3. AIFs guaranteeing higher returns than fixed deposits
  4. Mutual funds being banned from equity investing
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Rising HNI wealth and demand for private-market access

AIF growth reflects growing wealth, demand for private-market exposure and institutional interest. AIFs do not guarantee returns, and there is no deposit-investment mandate for banks.
