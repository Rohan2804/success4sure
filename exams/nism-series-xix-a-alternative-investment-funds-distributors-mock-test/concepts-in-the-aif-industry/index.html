Concepts in the AIF Industry: NISM Series XIX-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Concepts in the AIF Industry, from the NISM Series XIX-A (Alternative Investment Funds (Category I and II) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces core private fund concepts. It covers commitments and drawdowns, first and final close, investment period and fund tenure, the J-curve, vintage year, management fee and carried interest, hurdle rate, catch-up, waterfall structures, sponsor commitment, co-investment and the difference between gross and net returns. When revising, practise numerical questions on uncalled commitment, hurdle amounts and simple carry calculations, and be able to explain how these terms affect an investor's cash flows and returns over the life of a close-ended fund.

Concepts in the AIF Industry questions

Question 1

An investor committed ₹5 crore to a Category II AIF and 35% has been drawn down so far. The uncalled commitment is:

  1. ₹1.75 crore
  2. ₹3.25 crore
  3. ₹5.00 crore
  4. ₹3.50 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹3.25 crore

Drawn amount = 35% × 5 crore = ₹1.75 crore. Uncalled = 5 − 1.75 = ₹3.25 crore. ₹1.75 crore is the amount already called.

Question 2

The 'vintage year' of a private equity fund usually refers to:

  1. The year the fund manager was incorporated as a company
  2. The year the fund made its first investment or capital call
  3. The year the fund is expected to wind up
  4. The year the fund's last investment is exited
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The year the fund made its first investment or capital call

Vintage year marks the start of a fund's investing activity and is used to compare funds that faced similar market conditions. It is not the manager's founding or the fund's end.

Question 3

A fund's investors must receive their capital plus an 8% annual compounded return before carry is paid. On ₹100 crore held for three years, the amount investors must receive first is about:

  1. ₹124.00 crore
  2. ₹108.00 crore
  3. ₹133.10 crore
  4. ₹125.97 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹125.97 crore

With compounding, 100 × 1.08³ ≈ ₹125.97 crore. ₹124 crore uses simple interest, and ₹133.10 crore uses 10% instead of 8%.

Question 4

The 'investment period' of a close-ended AIF is the period during which:

  1. Investors can redeem their units at NAV on demand
  2. Only exits are allowed and no new investments are made
  3. The manager can make new portfolio investments
  4. The fund raises capital from new investors every month without a final close
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The manager can make new portfolio investments

During the investment period the manager deploys commitments into new deals. After it ends, the fund mainly manages and exits existing investments.

Question 5

A fund generates proceeds of ₹160 crore on ₹100 crore of capital. The hurdle amount is ₹30 crore and carry is 20% of profits above the hurdle with no catch-up. The manager's carry is:

  1. ₹12 crore
  2. ₹8 crore
  3. ₹30 crore
  4. ₹6 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹6 crore

Profit = ₹60 crore. Profit above hurdle = 60 − 30 = ₹30 crore. Carry = 20% × 30 = ₹6 crore. ₹12 crore applies 20% to the whole profit, as a full catch-up would.

Question 6

'Gross IRR' and 'net IRR' of an AIF differ mainly because net IRR:

  1. Includes only realised investments and ignores unrealised holdings
  2. Deducts fees, expenses and carried interest
  3. Is calculated before management fees
  4. Uses the hurdle rate instead of actual cash flows
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Deducts fees, expenses and carried interest

Net IRR reflects the investor's actual experience after fees, fund expenses and carry. Gross IRR measures portfolio performance before these costs.

Question 7

A co-investment opportunity in a private fund context means:

  1. The fund invests in another AIF of the same manager
  2. Two managers jointly manage a single scheme
  3. An investor transfers its commitment to another investor at a discount
  4. Investors invest directly alongside the fund in a specific deal
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Investors invest directly alongside the fund in a specific deal

Co-investment lets fund investors take an additional direct stake in a particular portfolio company alongside the AIF, often at lower or no fees. Transferring a commitment is a secondary sale.

Question 8

A 'first close' in fundraising means that:

  1. The fund has stopped accepting all new investors permanently
  2. The fund has accepted initial commitments and can begin operating
  3. All capital has been drawn down
  4. The fund's first investment has been exited
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The fund has accepted initial commitments and can begin operating

At first close, the fund admits its initial investors and can start investing, while it may continue raising money until the final close.
