Fund Due Diligence – Investor Perspective: NISM Series XIX-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Fund Due Diligence – Investor Perspective, from the NISM Series XIX-A (Alternative Investment Funds (Category I and II) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how investors evaluate an AIF before committing. It covers investment due diligence (strategy, team, track record, attribution, pipeline, portfolio construction), operational due diligence (back office, valuation, custody, compliance, cyber security, service providers), alignment of interest (sponsor commitment, fee terms, carry structure), and assessment of terms and legal documents. It also covers red flags and the use of due diligence questionnaires and reference checks. Focus on what evidence an investor should seek, how to verify track record and how to spot weaknesses.

Fund Due Diligence – Investor Perspective questions

Question 1

Which question best tests whether a manager's track record is attributable to the current team?

  1. What were the overall market returns in those years?
  2. How many offices does the manager have?
  3. Which current team members led the past deals?
  4. What is the manager's brand recognition among retail investors?
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Which current team members led the past deals?

Attribution analysis checks whether the people who generated past returns are still with the firm. Market returns and branding do not show who created value.

Question 2

A manager refuses to share the names of the independent valuer and the fund's auditor during due diligence. This is:

  1. A red flag warranting further enquiry
  2. Normal practice to protect business secrets of the fund manager from competitors
  3. Irrelevant to operational risk
  4. Required by SEBI confidentiality rules
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A red flag warranting further enquiry

Transparency about key service providers is basic; refusal to disclose them raises concerns about valuation and controls. No regulation requires hiding them from investors.

Question 3

Reference checks with founders of companies previously backed by the manager help an investor assess:

  1. The manager's behaviour as a partner
  2. The fund's tax status
  3. The legal validity of the trust deed
  4. The SEBI registration number
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The manager's behaviour as a partner

Speaking with founders and co-investors reveals how the manager behaves, adds value and handles difficulties — information that documents cannot provide.

Question 4

A due diligence questionnaire (DDQ) is mainly used to:

  1. Replace the PPM as the legal offer document
  2. Calculate the fund's NAV
  3. Collect standard information about the manager
  4. Register the investor as an accredited investor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Collect standard information about the manager

A DDQ gathers consistent information on strategy, team, operations, compliance and terms, making comparison across managers easier. It does not replace the PPM.

Question 5

An investor learns that the fund manager's chief financial officer also acts as the fund's sole valuation decision-maker without independent review. The main concern is:

  1. Excessive diversification of portfolio holdings
  2. Lack of independence in valuation
  3. Too many service providers
  4. High distribution fees
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Lack of independence in valuation

Valuations drive reported performance and fees. Concentrating valuation decisions in management without independent review creates conflicts and the risk of inflated values.

Question 6

Which of these best indicates alignment of interest between a manager and investors?

  1. High management fees charged on committed capital throughout the fund's entire life
  2. A guaranteed minimum carry for the manager
  3. Meaningful team investment and carry paid after capital is returned
  4. Carry paid deal by deal with no clawback
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Meaningful team investment and carry paid after capital is returned

Personal capital at risk and a whole-of-fund waterfall tie the manager's rewards to investors' outcomes. High fees and guaranteed carry weaken alignment.

Question 7

Why do investors look at a manager's portfolio construction plan during due diligence?

  1. To calculate the investor's own tax liability
  2. To verify the custodian's net worth
  3. To set the investor's KYC risk category
  4. To check if diversification and sizing fit the strategy
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. To check if diversification and sizing fit the strategy

The plan shows how many companies the fund will hold, typical ticket sizes and reserves for follow-ons, which determine risk and consistency with the stated strategy.

Question 8

A first-time fund is led by professionals who spun out of a large established PE firm. The investor's key diligence focus should be:

  1. Whether the old firm's brand is mentioned in the PPM and on the new manager's website
  2. The size of the new office
  3. The number of LinkedIn followers of the partners
  4. Whether they can operate their own firm
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Whether they can operate their own firm

Spin-out teams may have good deal records but must show they can run a firm — operations, compliance, fundraising and team stability. Branding and office size are not meaningful.
