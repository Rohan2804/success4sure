AIF Regulatory Framework: NISM Series XIX-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on AIF Regulatory Framework, from the NISM Series XIX-A (Alternative Investment Funds (Category I and II) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the main requirements of the SEBI AIF Regulations and circulars. Topics include registration, minimum corpus and minimum investment, the limit on number of investors, sponsor or manager continuing interest, accredited investors and large value funds, tenure and its extension, investment conditions and concentration limits for each category, leverage rules, the PPM and its filing through a merchant banker, the direct plan option, investor charter and grievance redressal, and reporting. Revise the key thresholds and the reasons behind them; where limits are frequently revised, understand the principle.

AIF Regulatory Framework questions

Question 1

A Category I AIF has a corpus of ₹80 crore. The minimum continuing interest required from the sponsor or manager is:

  1. ₹5 crore
  2. ₹4 crore
  3. ₹80 lakh
  4. ₹2 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹2 crore

For Category I and II AIFs, continuing interest is the lower of 2.5% of corpus or ₹5 crore. 2.5% of 80 crore = ₹2 crore, which is lower than ₹5 crore.

Question 2

A Category II AIF has investable funds of ₹240 crore. Under the general concentration limit, the maximum it can invest in one investee company is:

  1. ₹24 crore
  2. ₹60 crore
  3. ₹48 crore
  4. ₹120 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹60 crore

Category I and II AIFs may invest up to 25% of investable funds in a single investee company (large value funds have a higher limit). 25% × 240 = ₹60 crore.

Question 3

Which statement about accredited investors in AIFs is correct?

  1. They can redeem units of close-ended Category II AIFs on demand at NAV
  2. They may invest below the ₹1 crore minimum
  3. They must invest at least ₹10 crore in every scheme
  4. They are exempt from KYC requirements
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. They may invest below the ₹1 crore minimum

Accredited investors are exempt from the ₹1 crore minimum investment requirement and can access accredited-investor-only schemes. They are still subject to KYC and fund lock-ins.

Question 4

The minimum tenure of a Category I or II AIF is:

  1. Three years
  2. One year
  3. Five years
  4. Ten years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Three years

Category I and II AIFs must be close-ended with a minimum tenure of three years from the final close. Category III may be open-ended or close-ended.

Question 5

A new AIF scheme must file its placement memorandum with SEBI through:

  1. The fund's statutory auditor
  2. A SEBI-registered merchant banker
  3. A stock exchange
  4. The custodian of the AIF
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A SEBI-registered merchant banker

SEBI requires the PPM of a new scheme to be filed through a SEBI-registered merchant banker, who carries out due diligence on the disclosures.

Question 6

Which grievance redressal route is available to an investor with an unresolved complaint against an AIF manager?

  1. The Insurance Ombudsman
  2. The NPS Central Grievance Management System
  3. SEBI SCORES, followed by online dispute resolution if needed
  4. The Banking Ombudsman of RBI for all investment complaints
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. SEBI SCORES, followed by online dispute resolution if needed

AIF investors can lodge complaints on SEBI's SCORES and, if unresolved, use the online dispute resolution mechanism for securities market disputes.

Question 7

Category I and II AIFs may borrow only for temporary funding needs. Which condition is part of this framework?

  1. Borrowing is capped as a share of investable funds and for a short period
  2. Borrowing is unlimited if investors approve
  3. Borrowing is permitted only to buy listed shares
  4. Borrowings must be at least 50% of the corpus
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Borrowing is capped as a share of investable funds and for a short period

SEBI permits such AIFs to borrow only to meet temporary shortfalls, within limits on amount, duration and frequency. Leverage for investment is not allowed for these categories.

Question 8

SEBI requires every AIF scheme to offer a 'direct plan'. Its main feature is:

  1. A guaranteed minimum return
  2. Shorter lock-in than the regular plan
  3. A lower minimum investment of ₹10 lakh
  4. No distribution or placement fee is charged
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. No distribution or placement fee is charged

A direct plan is for investors coming directly or through SEBI-registered investment advisers or PMS and carries no distribution fee. It has the same lock-in and minimums.
