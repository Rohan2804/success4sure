Risk and Return – Fund and Investor Perspective: NISM Series XIX-A practice questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how risk and return are measured for private funds and from an investor's viewpoint. It explains IRR and its limitations, multiples such as TVPI, DPI and RVPI, the public market equivalent approach, the J-curve, and the main risks: illiquidity, valuation, leverage within portfolio companies, concentration, manager and key-person risk, currency and regulatory risk. Revise the calculation and meaning of the multiples, why IRR can be flattered by early exits or subscription lines, and how investors diversify across vintages, managers and strategies.

Risk and Return – Fund and Investor Perspective questions

Question 1

A fund has paid-in capital of ₹80 crore, distributions of ₹60 crore and residual NAV of ₹52 crore. Its TVPI is:

  1. 1.40x
  2. 0.75x
  3. 0.65x
  4. 1.75x
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 1.40x

TVPI = (distributions + NAV) ÷ paid-in = (60 + 52) ÷ 80 = 1.40x. 0.75x is the DPI (60 ÷ 80) and 0.65x is the RVPI (52 ÷ 80).

Question 2

Using the same fund (paid-in ₹80 crore, distributions ₹60 crore, NAV ₹52 crore), the RVPI is:

  1. 0.75x
  2. 1.40x
  3. 0.87x
  4. 0.65x
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 0.65x

RVPI = residual value ÷ paid-in = 52 ÷ 80 = 0.65x. It shows how much value remains unrealised in the portfolio.

Question 3

Why can IRR alone be misleading when comparing private funds?

  1. IRR ignores the timing of cash flows and so is unaffected by early exits
  2. IRR cannot be calculated for close-ended funds
  3. A quick exit with small gains can show a high IRR
  4. IRR is always lower than the money multiple
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A quick exit with small gains can show a high IRR

IRR is sensitive to timing: a quick exit with modest gains can produce a high IRR but little absolute wealth. Investors therefore look at multiples alongside IRR.

Question 4

An investor commits to private equity funds of five different vintage years. The main benefit is:

  1. Reducing exposure to one year's entry valuations
  2. Avoiding management fees in later years
  3. Guaranteeing a positive IRR in each fund
  4. Shortening the lock-in of each fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Reducing exposure to one year's entry valuations

Vintage diversification spreads capital across different market and valuation conditions, reducing the risk of investing all at a market peak.

Question 5

A fund uses a subscription credit line to fund deals before calling capital from investors. This tends to:

  1. Reduce the fund's total profits automatically
  2. Raise reported IRR by delaying capital calls
  3. Lower the reported IRR
  4. Eliminate the J-curve and all fees
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Raise reported IRR by delaying capital calls

Delaying capital calls shortens the period investor money is at work, boosting IRR, though interest costs may reduce the absolute multiple. Investors should look at both measures.

Question 6

The public market equivalent (PME) method helps an investor answer:

  1. What the fund's management fee should be
  2. Whether the fund is legally registered
  3. Whether the fund beat a comparable public index
  4. How many investors the fund has
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Whether the fund beat a comparable public index

PME compares a private fund's cash flows with a hypothetical investment in a public index made at the same times, showing relative outperformance.

Question 7

Which risk is most significant for an Indian investor in an AIF that invests in a few large unlisted companies?

  1. Interest-rate risk on government bonds held as cash management
  2. Inflation-indexation risk
  3. Exchange-rate risk on rupee-denominated holdings
  4. Concentration risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Concentration risk

A small number of large holdings makes outcomes depend on a few companies. Rupee-denominated holdings carry no exchange-rate risk for a domestic investor.

Question 8

The J-curve suggests that an investor reviewing a fund in year two should:

  1. Immediately sell the interest in the secondary market at any price
  2. Expect the highest returns in the first year
  3. Ignore fees because they do not affect early returns
  4. Not conclude that early negative returns mean failure
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Not conclude that early negative returns mean failure

Early returns are depressed by fees and costs before investments mature, so negative early IRRs are normal. Fees are a key driver of the J-curve's dip.
