Alternative Investment Fund Structuring: NISM Series XIX-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Alternative Investment Fund Structuring, from the NISM Series XIX-A (Alternative Investment Funds (Category I and II) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how AIFs are legally organised. It explains the choice between trust, company, limited liability partnership and body corporate structures, and why most Indian AIFs are trusts. It describes the documents that create and govern the fund — trust deed, contribution agreement, investment management agreement and PPM — and the roles of sponsor, trustee, manager, custodian and other parties. It also covers structures for foreign investors, such as offshore feeders and unified structures, and the FEMA treatment of downstream investment. Focus on who does what in each structure and the reasons for choosing a structure.

Alternative Investment Fund Structuring questions

Question 1

Which document appoints the investment manager of a trust-based AIF and sets out its duties and fees?

  1. The trust deed alone without any separate agreement with the manager
  2. The contribution agreement
  3. The custodian's agreement
  4. The investment management agreement
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The investment management agreement

The trustee appoints the manager through an investment management agreement, which specifies powers, duties and fees. The contribution agreement governs investors' commitments.

Question 2

A key reason most Indian AIFs are structured as trusts is that trusts:

  1. Are the only structure permitted by SEBI for AIFs
  2. Pay no tax under any circumstances
  3. Are flexible to set up and wind up
  4. Allow unlimited leverage
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Are flexible to set up and wind up

Trusts are flexible to establish and dissolve and fit the statutory pass-through regime for Category I and II AIFs. Companies and LLPs are also permitted, and trusts are not tax-free in all cases.

Question 3

In a trust-based AIF, who is the 'settlor'?

  1. The person who creates the trust with an initial settlement
  2. The investor with the largest commitment
  3. The custodian holding the assets
  4. The merchant banker filing the PPM
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The person who creates the trust with an initial settlement

The settlor creates the trust by settling an initial sum and executing the trust deed, usually the sponsor. Investors become contributors or beneficiaries through contribution agreements.

Question 4

Why might an AIF choose an LLP structure instead of a trust?

  1. LLPs need no registration with SEBI to operate as AIFs
  2. Partners' liability is limited
  3. LLPs are exempt from FEMA rules
  4. LLPs are the only structure for Category III funds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Partners' liability is limited

An LLP gives partners limited liability and a contractual framework familiar to global investors. It must still register with SEBI as an AIF and follow FEMA.

Question 5

If an Indian AIF's sponsor and manager are owned and controlled by resident Indian citizens, its downstream investment is treated under FEMA as:

  1. Foreign direct investment in all cases
  2. Foreign portfolio investment
  3. Domestic investment, even if some investors are foreign
  4. Not permitted at all
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Domestic investment, even if some investors are foreign

Under FEMA, downstream investments by an AIF are treated as foreign investment only if the sponsor or manager is not owned and controlled by resident Indian citizens. Foreign LP money alone does not change this.

Question 6

In a unified structure, foreign investors invest:

  1. Only through an offshore fund that invests via the FPI route
  2. Only in listed Indian shares through a mutual fund
  3. Through an Indian bank fixed deposit
  4. Directly into the Indian AIF alongside domestic investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Directly into the Indian AIF alongside domestic investors

In a unified structure, offshore and domestic investors invest directly into the same Indian AIF, sometimes using an offshore pooling vehicle. In contrast, a pure offshore structure uses FPI or FDI routes.

Question 7

Which party in a trust-based AIF is primarily responsible for ensuring that the manager acts in accordance with the trust deed and PPM?

  1. The trustee
  2. The placement agent
  3. The investee company
  4. The tax consultant
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The trustee

The trustee oversees the fund on behalf of beneficiaries and ensures the manager follows the trust deed and PPM. Placement agents only help raise capital.

Question 8

An AIF offers two classes of units: Class A for investors and Class B for the manager team, which carries entitlement to carried interest. Class B units are used mainly to:

  1. Channel carried interest to the manager team
  2. Give the manager team voting control over all investor decisions
  3. Avoid the minimum investment requirement for investors
  4. Allow the manager to redeem units at any time
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Channel carried interest to the manager team

Separate unit classes allow carried interest and different fee terms to be allocated to the manager team, as disclosed in the PPM. They do not give overriding control or waive investor requirements.
