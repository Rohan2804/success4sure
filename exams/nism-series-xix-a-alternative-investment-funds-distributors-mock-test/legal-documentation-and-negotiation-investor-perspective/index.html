Legal Documentation and Negotiation – Investor Perspective: NISM Series XIX-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Legal Documentation and Negotiation – Investor Perspective, from the NISM Series XIX-A (Alternative Investment Funds (Category I and II) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the main legal documents of an AIF and the terms investors negotiate. Documents include the PPM, trust deed, contribution agreement, investment management agreement and side letters. Key terms include commitment and drawdown mechanics, default provisions, fees and expenses, the waterfall, clawback, key-person and removal clauses, the investor advisory committee, transfer restrictions, MFN rights and confidentiality, along with SEBI's rules on pro-rata rights and differential rights. Focus on the purpose of each clause and how it protects investors or the manager.

Legal Documentation and Negotiation – Investor Perspective questions

Question 1

An investor fails to fund a drawdown notice. The contribution agreement typically allows the manager to:

  1. Raise the commitments of all other investors without consent
  2. Cancel the fund automatically
  3. Waive the default without any consequences in all cases
  4. Apply default penalties to the defaulting investor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Apply default penalties to the defaulting investor

Default clauses permit penalties such as interest, forfeiture of a portion of the interest or forced transfer, protecting non-defaulting investors from funding gaps.

Question 2

A 'for-cause removal' clause allows investors to:

  1. Remove the manager for events like fraud or breach
  2. Redeem their units at NAV whenever they wish
  3. Appoint a new auditor without any reason
  4. Veto every individual investment
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Remove the manager for events like fraud or breach

For-cause removal lets investors replace the manager following defined events such as fraud, gross negligence or material breach, usually by a specified majority.

Question 3

Which clause limits an investor's ability to sell its fund interest to a third party?

  1. Most favoured nation clause
  2. Key-person clause
  3. Hurdle rate clause
  4. Transfer restrictions requiring manager consent
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Transfer restrictions requiring manager consent

Transfer restriction clauses require the manager's consent before an investor sells its interest, protecting the fund from unsuitable investors.

Question 4

Under SEBI's framework on investor rights in AIFs, differential rights granted to select investors must:

  1. Be kept secret from other investors
  2. Not harm the rights of other investors
  3. Always include higher returns guaranteed by the sponsor
  4. Be approved by the stock exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Not harm the rights of other investors

SEBI requires pro-rata rights generally, permitting differential rights only within its framework and without affecting other investors adversely. Guaranteed returns are not permitted.

Question 5

Which expense is most likely to be negotiated as borne by the manager rather than the fund?

  1. Fund audit fees
  2. Custodian charges
  3. Legal costs of closing portfolio deals
  4. Manager's own office rent and salaries
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Manager's own office rent and salaries

Overheads such as the manager's office and staff are usually paid out of the management fee. Fund-level expenses like audit, custody and deal costs are commonly charged to the fund.

Question 6

An investor advisory committee (IAC) of an AIF commonly:

  1. Makes all investment decisions instead of the manager
  2. Sets the investors' tax rates
  3. Appoints the SEBI inspection team
  4. Reviews conflicts and valuation issues
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Reviews conflicts and valuation issues

The IAC, made up of investor representatives, advises on conflicts of interest, valuation questions and certain consents. Investment decisions remain with the manager.

Question 7

A 'GP giveback' provision allows the manager to:

  1. Return carried interest to investors each year
  2. Recall distributions to meet fund indemnities
  3. Give back unused commitments after the first close
  4. Refund management fees on demand
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Recall distributions to meet fund indemnities

A giveback lets the fund recall part of past distributions from investors to meet indemnity obligations or liabilities. It differs from a clawback, which recovers excess carry from the manager.

Question 8

Why do investors often negotiate a cap on organisational (set-up) expenses?

  1. To prevent the fund from being registered with SEBI
  2. To limit fund costs borne by investors
  3. To ensure the fund invests only in listed shares
  4. To avoid paying any management fee at all during the fund's life
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. To limit fund costs borne by investors

Set-up costs such as legal and registration fees are charged to the fund; a cap prevents excessive costs eroding investor returns.
