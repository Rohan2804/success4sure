Taxation – India Specific: NISM Series XIX-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Taxation – India Specific, from the NISM Series XIX-A (Alternative Investment Funds (Category I and II) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the Indian tax treatment of AIFs and their investors. It explains the statutory pass-through regime for Category I and II AIFs, under which non-business income is taxed in investors' hands with the same character, while business income is taxed at fund level; TDS on income distributed to investors; the treatment of losses; the taxation of Category III AIFs at fund level; the position of non-resident investors and treaty benefits; and GST on management fees. Since tax provisions are being renumbered under the new Income-tax Act, focus on principles rather than section numbers.

Taxation – India Specific questions

Question 1

Capital gains earned by a Category II AIF on selling unlisted shares are taxed in investors' hands as:

  1. Business income in all cases
  2. Income from other sources
  3. Capital gains, with the same character
  4. Exempt income
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Capital gains, with the same character

Under the pass-through regime, income other than business income keeps its character in investors' hands, so capital gains remain capital gains.

Question 2

When a Category I or II AIF distributes income (other than business income) to a resident investor, the fund generally:

  1. Pays no attention to tax because income is exempt
  2. Pays the final tax on behalf of all investors at the maximum marginal rate
  3. Deducts GST from the distribution
  4. Deducts tax at source as prescribed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Deducts tax at source as prescribed

The AIF deducts TDS on income credited or paid to investors, as prescribed by law; investors then pay tax on their share, claiming credit for TDS. GST does not apply to distributions.

Question 3

A Category II AIF incurs a capital loss on an investment. The loss is generally:

  1. Retained at fund level forever
  2. Passed through to investors who meet holding conditions
  3. Converted into business loss
  4. Ignored for tax purposes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Passed through to investors who meet holding conditions

Losses other than business losses are passed to investors, provided they have held units for the prescribed minimum period. Business losses stay at fund level.

Question 4

Business income earned by a Category I AIF trust is taxed:

  1. At the fund level
  2. Only in investors' hands as exempt income
  3. As capital gains in investors' hands
  4. At a concessional rate of 1%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. At the fund level

Business income is excluded from pass-through and is taxed at the fund level; distributions of that income to investors are then exempt in their hands.

Question 5

A non-resident investor in a Category II AIF wants to claim lower tax under a tax treaty. She typically needs to provide:

  1. Only her passport copy
  2. A tax residency certificate and documents
  3. Nothing, since non-residents are exempt
  4. Her Indian bank fixed deposit receipts
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A tax residency certificate and documents

Treaty benefits require a tax residency certificate and related documentation; the AIF may then apply the treaty rate on income allocable to her.

Question 6

Management fees charged by an AIF manager attract:

  1. Goods and Services Tax
  2. Securities Transaction Tax
  3. Commodity Transaction Tax
  4. No indirect tax
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Goods and Services Tax

Management fees are a supply of services and attract GST, which increases the cost borne by the fund. STT and CTT apply to securities and commodity transactions.

Question 7

Why do Category I and II AIFs issue investors an annual statement of income allocated, by nature of income?

  1. Because investors cannot see the fund's NAV otherwise
  2. So investors can report each income type
  3. To claim GST credits
  4. Because SEBI fixes investors' tax rates
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. So investors can report each income type

With pass-through, investors are taxed on their share of each income type — capital gains, interest, dividends — so the AIF must report the breakdown for correct filing.

Question 8

Unlike Category I and II AIFs, a Category III AIF set up as a trust is generally taxed:

  1. Only in investors' hands with full pass-through for all types of income
  2. At zero rate because it invests in listed securities
  3. Only on unrealised gains
  4. At the fund level, as no statutory pass-through applies
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. At the fund level, as no statutory pass-through applies

Category III AIFs do not enjoy statutory pass-through, so income is generally taxed at the trust level under trust-taxation rules.
