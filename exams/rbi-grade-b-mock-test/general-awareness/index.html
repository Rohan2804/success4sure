General Awareness: RBI Grade B practice questions

15 free questions with answers and explanations on General Awareness, from the RBI Grade B (RBI Grade B Officer (Phase I)) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this section covers

This section of RBI Grade B Phase I tests general awareness with a strong tilt towards banking and the economy. Expect questions on the RBI's history, functions and monetary policy tools, the liquidity adjustment framework, CRR and SLR, Basel III capital and liquidity norms, deposit insurance, development institutions such as NABARD, key constitutional provisions on public finance, and core macroeconomic ideas like the balance of payments, exchange rates and inequality. While revising, focus on stable concepts and the reasoning behind each tool, and cross-check any number-based fact against the latest RBI publications.

General Awareness questions

Question 1

In which year was the Reserve Bank of India nationalised, ending its status as a shareholders' bank?

  1. 1935
  2. 1969
  3. 1949
  4. 1955
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 1949

The RBI began operations in 1935 as a privately held shareholders' bank and was nationalised with effect from 1 January 1949. The year 1969 is associated with the nationalisation of 14 major commercial banks, not the RBI.

Question 2

When the RBI began operations in April 1935, where was its Central Office located before it moved permanently to Mumbai in 1937?

  1. New Delhi
  2. Calcutta (Kolkata)
  3. Madras (Chennai)
  4. Nagpur
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Calcutta (Kolkata)

The RBI's Central Office was first set up in Calcutta and shifted permanently to Bombay (now Mumbai) in 1937, where it remains, housing the Governor's office and policy departments.

Question 3

The Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) that banks must maintain is prescribed under which law?

  1. Section 42 of the RBI Act, 1934
  2. Section 17 of the RBI Act, 1934
  3. Section 24 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949
  4. Section 35A of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Section 24 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949

SLR is mandated by Section 24 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949. Section 42 of the RBI Act, 1934 is the tempting distractor because it governs the Cash Reserve Ratio, not SLR.

Question 4

Which liquidity tool lets the RBI absorb surplus funds from banks without having to give them government securities as collateral in return?

  1. Standing Deposit Facility
  2. Marginal Standing Facility
  3. Fixed-rate reverse repo
  4. Term repo auction
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Standing Deposit Facility

The Standing Deposit Facility absorbs liquidity without collateral, removing the constraint of the RBI's securities holdings. Reverse repo needs collateral, while MSF and term repo inject liquidity rather than absorb it.

Question 5

A bank is checking which of its holdings count towards its SLR requirement. Which of the following would NOT qualify?

  1. Rated bonds issued by a private manufacturing company
  2. Cash held in its own vaults
  3. Unencumbered Central Government securities
  4. Gold valued as per RBI norms
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Rated bonds issued by a private manufacturing company

SLR can be held in cash, gold and unencumbered approved securities such as Government securities. Corporate bonds, however safe or well rated, are not SLR-eligible assets.

Question 6

Under Basel III norms, the Capital Conservation Buffer must be held in the form of:

  1. Additional Tier 1 perpetual bonds
  2. Common Equity Tier 1 capital
  3. Tier 2 subordinated debt
  4. General provisions and loss reserves
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Common Equity Tier 1 capital

The Capital Conservation Buffer is meant to absorb losses in stress without breaching minimum requirements, so it must consist of the highest-quality capital, Common Equity Tier 1. Bonds and provisions do not qualify.

Question 7

The Liquidity Coverage Ratio under Basel III requires banks to hold high-quality liquid assets sufficient to cover total net cash outflows over which period of stress?

  1. 7 days
  2. 90 days
  3. 30 days
  4. One year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 30 days

LCR measures short-term resilience: the stock of high-quality liquid assets should cover net cash outflows over a 30-day stress period. The one-year horizon relates to the Net Stable Funding Ratio instead.

Question 8

Which of the following is an instrument of fiscal policy rather than monetary policy?

  1. A reduction in the policy repo rate
  2. An increase in the Cash Reserve Ratio
  3. Open market sale of government securities
  4. A cut in personal income tax rates
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A cut in personal income tax rates

Fiscal policy is the government's use of taxation and public expenditure. Changing income tax rates is a fiscal measure, whereas repo rate, CRR and open market operations are monetary tools used by the RBI.

Question 9

Which Article of the Constitution of India provides for the constitution of a Finance Commission?

  1. Article 280
  2. Article 112
  3. Article 266
  4. Article 148
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Article 280

Article 280 requires the President to constitute a Finance Commission every five years or earlier, mainly to recommend the sharing of taxes between the Union and the States. Article 148 deals with the CAG.

Question 10

The Union Budget is presented to Parliament as the 'Annual Financial Statement' under which Article of the Constitution?

  1. Article 110
  2. Article 112
  3. Article 280
  4. Article 265
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Article 112

Article 112 requires a statement of estimated receipts and expenditure of the Government of India for each financial year, called the Annual Financial Statement. Article 110 defines a Money Bill.

Question 11

The Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC), which insures bank deposits in India, is:

  1. A department of the Ministry of Finance
  2. A subsidiary of the State Bank of India
  3. A wholly owned subsidiary of the Reserve Bank of India
  4. A public sector general insurance company
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A wholly owned subsidiary of the Reserve Bank of India

DICGC is a wholly owned subsidiary of the RBI, set up under the DICGC Act, 1961. It insures deposits of eligible banks, including commercial banks and co-operative banks, up to a specified limit.

Question 12

If the Indian rupee depreciates sharply against the US dollar, other things remaining equal, what is the most likely immediate effect?

  1. Imported crude oil becomes cheaper in rupees
  2. India's external debt falls in rupee terms
  3. Foreign travel becomes cheaper for Indians
  4. Indian exports become cheaper for buyers abroad
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Indian exports become cheaper for buyers abroad

A weaker rupee means a foreign buyer needs fewer dollars to buy the same Indian goods, so exports become more competitive. Imports, foreign travel and dollar-denominated debt all become costlier in rupee terms.

Question 13

In India's balance of payments, a Foreign Direct Investment inflow is recorded in which account?

  1. Capital (financial) account
  2. Current account
  3. Merchandise trade account
  4. Invisibles account
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Capital (financial) account

FDI creates ownership claims on assets, so it is recorded in the capital (financial) account. The current account covers trade in goods and services, primary income and transfers; invisibles are a part of it.

Question 14

A Gini coefficient of zero for a country would indicate:

  1. Perfect inequality, with one person earning all income
  2. Perfect equality of income distribution
  3. Zero growth in national income
  4. An equal number of rich and poor households
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Perfect equality of income distribution

The Gini coefficient ranges from 0 to 1 (or 0 to 100). A value of 0 means everyone has the same income, while a value of 1 means a single person receives all the income.

Question 15

The National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development (NABARD) was set up in 1982 on the recommendation of which committee?

  1. Narasimham Committee
  2. M. Narasimham Working Group on Lead Banks
  3. B. Sivaraman Committee (CRAFICARD)
  4. Raghuram Rajan Committee
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. B. Sivaraman Committee (CRAFICARD)

NABARD was established on 12 July 1982 based on the recommendations of CRAFICARD, chaired by B. Sivaraman. The Narasimham Committees dealt with financial and banking sector reforms in the 1990s.
