Reasoning: RBI Grade B practice questions

15 free questions with answers and explanations on Reasoning, from the RBI Grade B (RBI Grade B Officer (Phase I)) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this section covers

Reasoning in RBI Grade B Phase I is the most time-intensive section, dominated by puzzles and seating arrangements in linear, circular and floor-based formats. Alongside these, expect syllogisms, inequalities, blood relations, direction sense, coding-decoding, ranking and order, alphanumeric series and occasional clock or word-based questions. Practise drawing quick diagrams, fix definite placements first, then test the remaining cases. For syllogisms use Venn diagrams and accept a conclusion only if it is true in every possible case. Speed comes from regular timed practice of mixed sets.

Reasoning questions

Question 1

Seven persons P, Q, R, S, T, U and V sit in a straight row facing north. • S sits third from the left end. • Only two persons sit between S and V. • T sits immediately to the right of V. • Q sits at one of the extreme ends. • R is an immediate neighbour of S. • U sits somewhere to the left of R. Who sits exactly in the middle of the row?

  1. R
  2. P
  3. S
  4. U
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. R

S is third from left, so V is sixth and T seventh; Q must take the left end. R neighbours S at fourth, U fills second, P fifth: Q U S R P V T. The middle (fourth) seat is R's.

Question 2

Seven persons P, Q, R, S, T, U and V sit in a straight row facing north. • S sits third from the left end. • Only two persons sit between S and V. • T sits immediately to the right of V. • Q sits at one of the extreme ends. • R is an immediate neighbour of S. • U sits somewhere to the left of R. How many persons sit between U and V?

  1. Two
  2. Three
  3. Four
  4. One
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Three

The final order from left is Q, U, S, R, P, V, T. U is second and V is sixth, so S, R and P, three persons, sit between them.

Question 3

Statements: All banks are lenders. Some lenders are brokers. No broker is an insurer. Conclusions: I. Some banks are brokers. II. Some lenders are not insurers. Which conclusion(s) follow?

  1. Only conclusion I follows
  2. Only conclusion II follows
  3. Either I or II follows
  4. Both I and II follow
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Only conclusion II follows

The lenders that are brokers can never be insurers, so some lenders are not insurers, and II follows. Banks need not overlap with brokers at all, so I does not follow.

Question 4

Statements: Some coins are notes. All notes are cheques. Some cheques are drafts. Conclusions: I. Some coins are cheques. II. Some drafts are coins. Which conclusion(s) follow?

  1. Only conclusion I follows
  2. Only conclusion II follows
  3. Either I or II follows
  4. Neither I nor II follows
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Only conclusion I follows

Coins that are notes are also cheques, so some coins are cheques and I follows. Drafts are linked only to some cheques, which may not be the coin-notes, so II is not certain.

Question 5

Statements: P > Q ≥ R = S, S < T ≤ U Conclusions: I. P > S II. U > R Which conclusion(s) are definitely true?

  1. Only I is true
  2. Only II is true
  3. Either I or II is true
  4. Both I and II are true
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Both I and II are true

From P > Q ≥ R = S we get P > S, so I is true. From U ≥ T > S = R we get U > R, so II is also true. Both conclusions hold.

Question 6

M is the sister of N. N is the son of P. P is the husband of Q. R is the mother of P. How is M related to R?

  1. Daughter
  2. Niece
  3. Granddaughter
  4. Daughter-in-law
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Granddaughter

N is P's son and M is N's sister, so M is P's daughter. R is P's mother, which makes M the daughter of R's son, that is, R's granddaughter.

Question 7

Riya walks 12 m towards the south, turns right and walks 5 m, then turns right again and walks 24 m. How far and in which direction is she now from her starting point?

  1. 13 m North-East
  2. 17 m North-West
  3. 12 m South-West
  4. 13 m North-West
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 13 m North-West

Facing south, a right turn is west and the next right is north. She ends 5 m west and 24 − 12 = 12 m north of the start. Distance = √(25 + 144) = 13 m, towards the North-West.

Question 8

In a certain code, CREDIT is written as XIVWRG. How is DEPOSIT written in that code?

  1. WVKLHRG
  2. WVKLHGR
  3. WVLKHRG
  4. XVKLHRG
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. WVKLHRG

Each letter is replaced by its opposite in the alphabet (A↔Z, B↔Y, C↔X and so on). Applying this, D→W, E→V, P→K, O→L, S→H, I→R, T→G gives WVKLHRG.

Question 9

If in a certain code CAT = 24 and DOG = 26, what is the code for BANK?

  1. 26
  2. 28
  3. 30
  4. 32
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 28

Each word's code is the sum of the alphabetical positions of its letters: C(3) + A(1) + T(20) = 24 and D(4) + O(15) + G(7) = 26. BANK = 2 + 1 + 14 + 11 = 28.

Question 10

In a class of 45 students, Rohit ranks 14th from the top. Sunil is nine ranks below Rohit. What is Sunil's rank from the bottom?

  1. 22nd
  2. 24th
  3. 23rd
  4. 21st
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 23rd

Sunil is 14 + 9 = 23rd from the top. His rank from the bottom is 45 − 23 + 1 = 23rd. Forgetting to add 1 gives the tempting wrong answer 22nd.

Question 11

Study the arrangement: R 4 M # 7 K 2 @ T 9 P % 3 B & 6 H How many symbols in the arrangement are immediately followed by a number?

  1. Two
  2. Four
  3. One
  4. Three
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Three

The symbols are #, @, % and &. # is followed by 7, % by 3 and & by 6, while @ is followed by the letter T. So three symbols qualify.

Question 12

Five friends A, B, C, D and E have different heights. • B is taller than C but shorter than A. • E is shorter than only one person. • D is taller than C. • A is not the tallest. Who is the tallest?

  1. D
  2. E
  3. A
  4. B
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. D

E is second tallest and A is not tallest, so the tallest is D (B and C are shorter than A). Then A is third, B fourth and C shortest: D > E > A > B > C.

Question 13

Five friends A, B, C, D and E have different heights. • B is taller than C but shorter than A. • E is shorter than only one person. • D is taller than C. • A is not the tallest. Who is the third tallest?

  1. B
  2. A
  3. E
  4. C
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A

The clues give the order D > E > A > B > C. E is second, as it is shorter than only one person, so A is third tallest.

Question 14

How many pairs of letters are there in the word PRODUCE, each of which has as many letters between them in the word as in the English alphabet (in either direction)?

  1. Two
  2. Three
  3. One
  4. None
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. One

Only R–U qualifies: two letters (O, D) lie between them in the word and two (S, T) in the alphabet. Pairs such as D–C or D–E are alphabet neighbours but not word neighbours, so they fail.

Question 15

What is the angle between the hour hand and the minute hand of a clock at 3:40?

  1. 140°
  2. 120°
  3. 150°
  4. 130°
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 130°

Angle = |30H − 5.5M| = |30 × 3 − 5.5 × 40| = |90 − 220| = 130°. The minute hand moves 6° per minute and the hour hand 0.5° per minute.
