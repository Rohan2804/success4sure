English Language: RBI Grade B practice questions

15 free questions with answers and explanations on English Language, from the RBI Grade B (RBI Grade B Officer (Phase I)) syllabus.

What this section covers

The English Language section checks reading comprehension, grammar and vocabulary under time pressure. Passages are usually on economics, banking, policy or social issues, followed by questions on the main idea, inference, tone and the meaning of words in context. Grammar appears through error spotting, sentence improvement and fill-in-the-blanks, testing subject-verb agreement, tenses, prepositions, conditionals and comparatives. Vocabulary items cover synonyms, antonyms, idioms and one-word substitutions. Revise core grammar rules, read editorials daily, and practise eliminating options quickly.

English Language questions

Question 1

Read the passage and answer the question. Digital lending has widened access to credit for borrowers who were once ignored by formal lenders. A salaried worker in a small town can now obtain a small loan within minutes, using data from her phone instead of a long credit history. Yet speed has a cost. Some apps have concealed fees, harassed borrowers over delays and shared personal data without consent. Regulators have therefore insisted that loans be disbursed directly into the borrower's bank account and that the true annual cost be disclosed upfront. The aim is not to slow innovation but to make sure convenience does not come at the price of fairness. Which of the following best expresses the main idea of the passage?

  1. Digital lending apps should be banned because some of them harass borrowers
  2. Regulators want to slow down innovation in lending to protect banks
  3. Small-town borrowers prefer lenders who rely on long credit histories
  4. Digital lending widens access to credit but needs rules to keep it fair
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Digital lending widens access to credit but needs rules to keep it fair

The passage praises wider access, lists abuses, and says rules aim to keep convenience fair without slowing innovation. It never calls for a ban, and it explicitly denies that regulators want to slow innovation.

Question 2

Read the passage and answer the question. Digital lending has widened access to credit for borrowers who were once ignored by formal lenders. A salaried worker in a small town can now obtain a small loan within minutes, using data from her phone instead of a long credit history. Yet speed has a cost. Some apps have concealed fees, harassed borrowers over delays and shared personal data without consent. Regulators have therefore insisted that loans be disbursed directly into the borrower's bank account and that the true annual cost be disclosed upfront. The aim is not to slow innovation but to make sure convenience does not come at the price of fairness. Which of the following is NOT mentioned in the passage as a problem with some lending apps?

  1. Charging interest above a legal ceiling
  2. Concealing fees from borrowers
  3. Harassing borrowers over delays
  4. Sharing personal data without consent
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Charging interest above a legal ceiling

The passage lists concealed fees, harassment over delays and sharing data without consent. It mentions disclosure of the true annual cost but says nothing about any legal ceiling on interest rates.

Question 3

Read the passage and answer the question. Banks once treated climate change as a distant concern for environmental groups. That view is changing. A loan to a coastal factory may turn sour if rising seas flood its premises, and a coal plant may lose value long before its machinery wears out if cleaner energy becomes cheaper. Such risks are hard to price because they unfold over decades, while most loans are judged over a few years. Supervisors now ask banks to test how their portfolios would fare under different climate scenarios. As used in the passage, the word 'sour' most nearly means:

  1. taste unpleasantly acidic
  2. become difficult to recover
  3. grow larger in size
  4. be signed again on new terms
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. become difficult to recover

In the passage a loan 'turns sour' when floods damage the borrower's premises, so the loan is unlikely to be repaid. The literal sense of an acidic taste does not fit a loan.

Question 4

Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error. Each of the branch managers (A) / who attended the review meeting (B) / have been asked (C) / to submit a revised plan. (D)

  1. (A)
  2. (B)
  3. (C)
  4. (D)
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. (C)

The subject is 'Each', which is singular, so the verb must be 'has been asked'. The plural noun 'managers' inside the phrase 'of the branch managers' does not change the number of the subject.

Question 5

Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error. Neither the chairman nor the directors (A) / was present at the meeting (B) / when the auditors (C) / presented their findings. (D)

  1. (A)
  2. (B)
  3. (C)
  4. (D)
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. (B)

With 'neither ... nor', the verb agrees with the nearer subject. Here the nearer subject is the plural 'directors', so 'was present' should be 'were present'.

Question 6

Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error. The new manager, along with his team, (A) / has been working hard (B) / to clear the backlog (C) / since the last two months. (D)

  1. (A)
  2. (B)
  3. (C)
  4. (D)
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. (D)

'Since' is used with a point of time, while 'for' is used with a period of time. 'Two months' is a period, so the phrase should read 'for the last two months'.

Question 7

Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error. The regional head insisted for (A) / an immediate review (B) / of all the pending (C) / loan applications. (D)

  1. (A)
  2. (B)
  3. (C)
  4. (D)
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. (A)

The verb 'insist' takes the preposition 'on', not 'for'. The correct phrase is 'insisted on an immediate review'. The remaining parts are grammatically correct.

Question 8

Choose the word that best fills the blank. The central bank's decision to leave rates unchanged was widely ________ by economists, most of whom had predicted a pause.

  1. deterred
  2. exempted
  3. anticipated
  4. retracted
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. anticipated

The economists had predicted a pause, so the decision was expected, that is, 'anticipated'. 'Deterred', 'exempted' and 'retracted' do not make sense with a decision that economists had forecast.

Question 9

Choose the pair of words that best fills the blanks. Ever since the company ________ its distribution network, its rural sales ________ steadily.

  1. has expanded / grew
  2. expands / had grown
  3. had expanded / will grow
  4. expanded / have grown
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. expanded / have grown

'Ever since' with a past event (simple past 'expanded') is followed by the present perfect for a continuing result, 'have grown'. The other pairs mix tenses that do not fit this structure.

Question 10

Choose the option that best replaces the part in capitals. If no change is needed, choose 'No improvement required'. IF I WOULD HAVE KNOWN about the deadline, I would have applied much earlier.

  1. If I had known
  2. If I have known
  3. Had I would know
  4. No improvement required
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. If I had known

In a third conditional about an unreal past, the if-clause takes the past perfect: 'If I had known'. 'Would have' should never appear in the if-clause itself.

Question 11

Choose the option that best replaces the part in capitals. If no change is needed, choose 'No improvement required'. Although she joined later, she is SENIOR THAN ME in the officers' cadre because of her rank.

  1. more senior than me
  2. senior to me
  3. seniorer to me
  4. No improvement required
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. senior to me

Latin comparatives such as senior, junior, superior and inferior take 'to', not 'than'. So the correct phrase is 'senior to me'; 'more senior than' is an unnecessary double form here.

Question 12

Which of the following words is closest in meaning to PRUDENT, as in a prudent lender?

  1. Reckless
  2. Generous
  3. Judicious
  4. Hesitant
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Judicious

Prudent means acting with care and good judgement, especially about the future, which matches 'judicious'. 'Reckless' is its opposite, and 'hesitant' implies indecision rather than sound judgement.

Question 13

Choose the word most nearly OPPOSITE in meaning to AUSTERITY.

  1. Severity
  2. Thrift
  3. Discipline
  4. Extravagance
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Extravagance

Austerity means strict economy and the avoidance of luxury. Its opposite is extravagance, meaning lavish spending. 'Thrift' and 'discipline' are close in meaning to austerity, not opposite.

Question 14

Choose the correct meaning of the idiom: 'To take something with a pinch of salt'.

  1. To regard it with some doubt
  2. To accept it eagerly and fully
  3. To add a little extra detail to it
  4. To deal with it very harshly
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. To regard it with some doubt

Taking something with a pinch of salt means not believing it completely and treating it with scepticism. It has nothing to do with eager acceptance or harsh treatment.

Question 15

Choose the one word for: 'A system of government in which the wealthy class rules'.

  1. Oligarchy
  2. Plutocracy
  3. Autocracy
  4. Bureaucracy
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Plutocracy

Plutocracy is rule by the wealthy. Oligarchy, the tempting option, means rule by a small group, which need not be defined by wealth. Autocracy is rule by one person.
