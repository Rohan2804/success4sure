Quantitative Aptitude: RBI Grade B practice questions

15 free questions with answers and explanations on Quantitative Aptitude, from the RBI Grade B (RBI Grade B Officer (Phase I)) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this section covers

Quantitative Aptitude in RBI Grade B Phase I mixes speed-based calculation with problem solving. Common question types are simplification and approximation, number series (missing and wrong term), quadratic equations comparing x and y, and data interpretation from tables or charts. Arithmetic word problems cover simple and compound interest, profit and discount, partnership, time and work, pipes and cisterns, boats and streams, trains, ages, averages and probability. Learn shortcuts such as the two-year CI − SI formula, practise mental calculation, and attempt DI sets carefully since several questions share one table.

Quantitative Aptitude questions

Question 1

What will come in place of the question mark (?) 25% of 640 + 3/4 of 288 − 12² = ?

  1. 222
  2. 242
  3. 232
  4. 212
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 232

25% of 640 = 160 and 3/4 of 288 = 216. So the value is 160 + 216 − 144 = 232. Doing percentage and fraction parts first avoids errors.

Question 2

What will come in place of the question mark (?) 7, 8, 12, 21, 37, ?

  1. 61
  2. 58
  3. 64
  4. 62
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 62

The differences are 1, 4, 9 and 16, which are the squares 1², 2², 3² and 4². The next difference is 5² = 25, so the answer is 37 + 25 = 62.

Question 3

Find the wrong number in the series. 4, 6, 12, 32, 90, 315, 1260

  1. 32
  2. 90
  3. 12
  4. 315
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 32

Each term is multiplied by 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 3.5 and 4 in turn: 4, 6, 12, 30, 90, 315, 1260. The fourth term should be 12 × 2.5 = 30, so 32 is wrong.

Question 4

Two equations I and II are given. Solve both and choose the correct relationship between x and y. I. x² − 11x + 30 = 0 II. y² − 13y + 42 = 0

  1. x > y
  2. x ≥ y
  3. x < y
  4. x ≤ y
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. x ≤ y

I factorises as (x − 5)(x − 6) = 0, so x = 5 or 6. II gives (y − 6)(y − 7) = 0, so y = 6 or 7. Since x = 6 = y is possible, the relation is x ≤ y.

Question 5

The difference between compound interest (compounded annually) and simple interest on a certain sum for 2 years at 8% per annum is ₹128. What is the sum?

  1. ₹16,000
  2. ₹20,000
  3. ₹25,000
  4. ₹18,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹20,000

For 2 years, CI − SI = P × (r/100)². So 128 = P × 0.0064, giving P = ₹20,000. This shortcut works only for two-year periods with annual compounding.

Question 6

A trader marks an item 40% above its cost price and then allows two successive discounts of 10% and 5%. What is his profit percentage?

  1. 25%
  2. 21.4%
  3. 19.7%
  4. 20%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 19.7%

Taking cost as 100, the marked price is 140. After 10% off it is 126, and after a further 5% off it is 119.7. Profit is therefore 19.7%, not simply 40 − 15 = 25%.

Question 7

A starts a business with ₹40,000. Five months later B joins with ₹60,000. At the end of the year the total profit is ₹45,000. What is B's share?

  1. ₹24,000
  2. ₹22,500
  3. ₹27,000
  4. ₹21,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹21,000

Capital-time products are A = 40,000 × 12 = 4,80,000 and B = 60,000 × 7 = 4,20,000, a ratio of 8 : 7. B's share = 45,000 × 7/15 = ₹21,000.

Question 8

Pipes A and B can fill a tank in 12 hours and 15 hours respectively, while pipe C can empty the full tank in 20 hours. If all three are opened together on an empty tank, in how many hours will it be full?

  1. 10 hours
  2. 8 hours
  3. 12 hours
  4. 9 hours
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 10 hours

Net filling per hour = 1/12 + 1/15 − 1/20 = (5 + 4 − 3)/60 = 1/10. So the tank fills in 10 hours. Forgetting to subtract the outlet gives a much shorter time.

Question 9

A boat covers 48 km downstream in 3 hours and 32 km upstream in 4 hours. What is the speed of the stream?

  1. 12 km/h
  2. 4 km/h
  3. 6 km/h
  4. 8 km/h
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 4 km/h

Downstream speed = 16 km/h and upstream speed = 8 km/h. Stream speed is half the difference, (16 − 8)/2 = 4 km/h. The 12 km/h figure is the boat's speed in still water.

Question 10

A train 240 m long crosses a platform 360 m long in 30 seconds. What is the speed of the train?

  1. 64 km/h
  2. 80 km/h
  3. 72 km/h
  4. 54 km/h
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 72 km/h

The train must cover its own length plus the platform: 600 m in 30 s, which is 20 m/s. Converting, 20 × 18/5 = 72 km/h.

Question 11

The present ages of A and B are in the ratio 5 : 7. After 6 years, the ratio will be 7 : 9. What is A's present age?

  1. 18 years
  2. 21 years
  3. 12 years
  4. 15 years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 15 years

Let the ages be 5k and 7k. Then (5k + 6)/(7k + 6) = 7/9, so 45k + 54 = 49k + 42, giving k = 3. A's present age is 5 × 3 = 15 years.

Question 12

The average of 11 results is 50. The average of the first six results is 49 and that of the last six is 52. What is the sixth result?

  1. 56
  2. 54
  3. 58
  4. 52
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 56

The sixth result is counted in both groups. Sixth = (6 × 49) + (6 × 52) − (11 × 50) = 294 + 312 − 550 = 56.

Question 13

Two fair dice are thrown together. What is the probability that the sum of the numbers shown is a prime number?

  1. 1/3
  2. 5/12
  3. 7/18
  4. 4/9
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 5/12

Prime sums are 2, 3, 5, 7 and 11, occurring in 1, 2, 4, 6 and 2 ways respectively, a total of 15 out of 36 outcomes. So the probability is 15/36 = 5/12.

Question 14

The table shows loans disbursed (₹ crore) by a bank. Year 2021: Housing 400, Education 100 Year 2022: Housing 450, Education 130 Year 2023: Housing 530, Education 130 By what percentage did the bank's total loans (housing + education) increase from 2021 to 2023?

  1. 30%
  2. 28%
  3. 32%
  4. 35%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 32%

Total loans were 400 + 100 = 500 in 2021 and 530 + 130 = 660 in 2023. The increase is 160, which is 160/500 × 100 = 32%.

Question 15

The table shows loans disbursed (₹ crore) by a bank. Year 2021: Housing 400, Education 100 Year 2022: Housing 450, Education 130 Year 2023: Housing 530, Education 130 What is the average annual housing loan disbursement over the three years?

  1. ₹450 crore
  2. ₹475 crore
  3. ₹445 crore
  4. ₹460 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹460 crore

Housing loans total 400 + 450 + 530 = 1,380 crore over three years. The average is 1,380 ÷ 3 = ₹460 crore.
