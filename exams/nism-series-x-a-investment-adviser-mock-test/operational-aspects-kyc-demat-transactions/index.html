Operational Aspects (KYC, Demat, Transactions): NISM Series X-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Operational Aspects (KYC, Demat, Transactions), from the NISM Series X-A (Investment Adviser (Level 1)) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the operational side of investing: KYC requirements, KYC Registration Agencies and the Central KYC registry, in-person and video verification, PAN and Aadhaar, nomination, demat accounts and depository participants, basic services demat accounts, transaction processes in mutual funds and securities, and change of details. When revising, know what documents are needed, who maintains KYC records, how nomination works, and the steps and timelines for common transactions.

Operational Aspects (KYC, Demat, Transactions) questions

Question 1

The Central KYC Records Registry (CKYCR) is maintained by:

  1. SEBI
  2. CERSAI
  3. NSDL
  4. AMFI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. CERSAI

CKYCR is operated by the Central Registry of Securitisation Asset Reconstruction and Security Interest of India (CERSAI), allowing one KYC to be used across financial sectors. NSDL is a depository and AMFI is an industry body.

Question 2

A basic services demat account (BSDA) is meant for:

  1. Institutional investors only
  2. Foreign portfolio investors
  3. Companies issuing shares
  4. Small investors with low holdings
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Small investors with low holdings

BSDA offers lower or nil annual maintenance charges to individuals with small holdings below a threshold, encouraging financial inclusion. Other accounts carry normal charges.

Question 3

Why should an investor add a nominee to a demat account?

  1. To allow the nominee to trade freely now
  2. To reduce tax on dividends
  3. To ease transfer of holdings on death
  4. To increase the account's credit limit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. To ease transfer of holdings on death

Nomination lets securities be transmitted smoothly to the nominee after the holder's death. It does not give the nominee trading rights during the holder's lifetime or affect tax.

Question 4

A client changes his address. Updating it once with the KRA ensures that:

  1. All linked intermediaries get the update
  2. Only his bank account is updated
  3. His PAN is cancelled and reissued
  4. His existing investments are redeemed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. All linked intermediaries get the update

KRAs share KYC data with registered intermediaries, so one update flows to mutual funds, brokers and others using that KYC. PAN and investments are unaffected.

Question 5

Which document is mandatory for most securities market transactions in India?

  1. Passport
  2. Voter ID
  3. PAN
  4. Driving licence
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. PAN

PAN is the key identifier for securities market transactions, with limited exemptions. Other documents may serve as address proof but are not mandatory identifiers.

Question 6

A client wants to convert physical share certificates into electronic form. This process is:

  1. Dematerialisation
  2. Rematerialisation
  3. Transmission
  4. Pledging
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Dematerialisation

Dematerialisation converts physical certificates into electronic holdings through a DP. Rematerialisation is the reverse, transmission is transfer on death, and pledging creates a charge.

Question 7

In-person verification during KYC can now be done through:

  1. A phone call without video
  2. An email with a scanned signature only
  3. A newspaper advertisement
  4. Video-based verification
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Video-based verification

SEBI permits video-based in-person verification by intermediaries, using secure processes, as an alternative to physical meetings. Phone calls or emails alone do not meet IPV norms.

Question 8

A mutual fund unitholder submits a redemption request. The AMC must normally pay the money within:

  1. Ten working days
  2. Three working days
  3. One calendar month
  4. Fifteen working days
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Three working days

SEBI and AMFI norms require redemption proceeds to be paid within three working days for most schemes, with interest for delays. Some exceptional cases allow longer timelines.
