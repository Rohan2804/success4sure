Investment Advisory & Financial Planning Basics: NISM Series X-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Investment Advisory & Financial Planning Basics, from the NISM Series X-A (Investment Adviser (Level 1)) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces investment advisory and financial planning: the difference between advice and product distribution, the steps of the financial planning process, client life-cycle stages, goal setting and the importance of risk profiling and suitability. It also covers the adviser's role as a fiduciary who puts the client's interest first. When revising, learn the sequence of planning steps, how goals are prioritised and quantified, and why understanding the client's full financial situation must come before any product recommendation.

Investment Advisory & Financial Planning Basics questions

Question 1

A client says his goal is 'to be rich some day'. What should the adviser do first to make this goal usable in a plan?

  1. Recommend a small-cap equity fund straight away
  2. Help him define it in amount and time terms
  3. Ignore it and focus only on his tax-saving needs for the current year
  4. Ask him to choose a product from a list of popular schemes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Help him define it in amount and time terms

A planning goal must be specific, measurable and time-bound, so the adviser converts a vague wish into a target amount and date. Only then can the required savings and asset allocation be worked out.

Question 2

Which statement best distinguishes an investment adviser from a mutual fund distributor?

  1. The adviser can sell any product and earn commission from each fund house
  2. The distributor must charge a fixed fee directly to the client
  3. The adviser is paid by the client for advice
  4. Both are paid in the same way and have identical obligations
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The adviser is paid by the client for advice

A SEBI-registered investment adviser is remunerated only by the client and must act in the client's interest. A distributor is paid commissions by product manufacturers, which creates a different set of incentives.

Question 3

A 52-year-old client with grown-up children and a paid-off home is most likely in which life-cycle stage?

  1. Consolidation, nearing retirement
  2. Early accumulation, just starting a career
  3. Distribution, already drawing a pension for many years
  4. Dependence on parents for financial support
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Consolidation, nearing retirement

In the consolidation stage, income is near its peak, major liabilities are largely settled and the focus shifts to building and protecting the retirement corpus. Distribution begins after retirement.

Question 4

Why is a periodic review an essential step of the financial planning process?

  1. SEBI requires advisers to change the portfolio every month
  2. Reviews allow the adviser to earn additional commissions
  3. A plan once made can never be modified, so reviews only confirm it
  4. Client circumstances and markets change over time
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Client circumstances and markets change over time

Income, goals, family situation, tax laws and market conditions change, so a plan must be monitored and updated. Reviews are not about churning the portfolio or earning commissions.

Question 5

Two clients have the same income and age. One is anxious about any fall in value; the other is comfortable with volatility. This difference relates mainly to their:

  1. Risk capacity
  2. Investment horizon
  3. Liquidity needs
  4. Risk tolerance
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Risk tolerance

Risk tolerance is the psychological willingness to accept volatility. Risk capacity is the financial ability to bear losses, which here appears similar because income and age are the same.

Question 6

An adviser recommends a product mainly because it pays her a higher fee from the issuer, though a cheaper suitable option exists. This breaches the principle of:

  1. Acting in the client's best interest
  2. Diversification across asset classes
  3. Rupee cost averaging
  4. Tax efficiency of investment products
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Acting in the client's best interest

A fiduciary must place the client's interest ahead of her own. Choosing a product for personal gain when a better suitable option exists is a conflict of interest and a breach of fiduciary duty.

Question 7

Which piece of information is most important to collect before preparing a client's asset allocation?

  1. The brand of car he plans to buy next year
  2. His goals, time horizon and risk profile
  3. The names of funds his friends have bought recently
  4. His preferred stock exchange for trading shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. His goals, time horizon and risk profile

Asset allocation depends on what the money is for, when it is needed and how much risk the client can and will take. Peer choices and trivial preferences do not drive allocation.

Question 8

A client has three goals: emergency fund, child's education in 12 years and a foreign holiday next year. Which is generally prioritised first?

  1. The foreign holiday next year
  2. The child's education in 12 years
  3. The emergency fund
  4. All goals should be funded equally from the start
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The emergency fund

An emergency fund protects all other goals from being derailed by sudden expenses or income loss, so it is usually the first priority. Essential long-term goals come next, and discretionary goals after that.
