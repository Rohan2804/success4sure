Equity Investment: NISM Series X-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Equity Investment, from the NISM Series X-A (Investment Adviser (Level 1)) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers equity as an asset class: features of equity shares, sources of equity returns, risks, corporate actions such as bonus issues, splits, rights and dividends, and basic valuation measures like EPS, P/E, P/B and dividend yield. It also covers market-cap categories and investment styles such as growth and value. When revising, practise computing simple ratios, understand how corporate actions change share count and price, and know why equity suits long horizons.

Equity Investment questions

Question 1

A company's net profit is ₹50 crore and it has 10 crore shares outstanding. What is its earnings per share?

  1. ₹5
  2. ₹50
  3. ₹0.50
  4. ₹10
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹5

EPS = net profit available to equity shareholders ÷ number of shares = 50 crore ÷ 10 crore = ₹5. The other figures misplace the decimal or confuse profit with share count.

Question 2

A share trades at ₹300 and its book value per share is ₹120. What is its price-to-book ratio?

  1. 0.4 times
  2. 3.0 times
  3. 2.5 times
  4. 1.8 times
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 2.5 times

P/B = market price ÷ book value per share = 300 ÷ 120 = 2.5. A ratio of 0.4 would result from dividing book value by price, which inverts the formula.

Question 3

An investor prefers companies trading at low P/E and P/B ratios relative to their fundamentals. Her investment style is:

  1. Growth investing
  2. Value investing
  3. Momentum investing
  4. Index investing
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Value investing

Value investors seek stocks priced below their intrinsic worth, often indicated by low valuation multiples. Growth investors pay higher multiples for fast earnings growth, and momentum investors follow price trends.

Question 4

A company splits its shares from face value ₹10 to ₹5. An investor held 200 shares at ₹800. Ignoring market movement, after the split she holds:

  1. 200 shares at about ₹400
  2. 400 shares at about ₹800
  3. 100 shares at about ₹1,600
  4. 400 shares at about ₹400
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 400 shares at about ₹400

A 2-for-1 split doubles the share count and halves the price, leaving total value unchanged at ₹1,60,000. It does not change her proportionate ownership.

Question 5

Which of the following is the main source of long-term return for equity investors?

  1. Fixed interest paid by the company every quarter
  2. Guaranteed repayment of capital on a maturity date
  3. Growth in company earnings and dividends
  4. Interest credited by the stock exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Growth in company earnings and dividends

Equity returns come from dividends and price appreciation driven by earnings growth. Equity has no fixed interest or maturity, and exchanges do not pay interest to shareholders.

Question 6

Which risk of equity investing cannot be removed by holding many stocks across sectors?

  1. Company-specific management risk
  2. Market risk
  3. Risk of a single factory fire
  4. Risk of one firm losing a key contract
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Market risk

Systematic or market risk affects all stocks, for example from interest rates or economic slowdown, and cannot be diversified away. Events specific to one company are unsystematic and can be diversified.

Question 7

A shareholder bought a share at ₹400, received a dividend of ₹10 and sold it after one year at ₹440. What was his total return?

  1. 12.5%
  2. 10.0%
  3. 2.5%
  4. 11.4%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 12.5%

Total return = (price gain + dividend) ÷ purchase price = (40 + 10) ÷ 400 = 12.5%. 10% counts only the price gain and 2.5% only the dividend yield.

Question 8

Under SEBI's categorisation, mid-cap companies are those ranked:

  1. 1st to 100th by full market capitalisation
  2. 251st onwards by full market capitalisation
  3. 1st to 50th by free-float market capitalisation
  4. 101st to 250th by full market capitalisation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 101st to 250th by full market capitalisation

SEBI defines large caps as the top 100 companies, mid caps as 101st to 250th and small caps as 251st onwards, ranked by full market capitalisation using AMFI's periodic list.
