Client Financial Position & Debt Management: NISM Series X-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Client Financial Position & Debt Management, from the NISM Series X-A (Investment Adviser (Level 1)) syllabus.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter teaches how to assess a client's financial position using the personal balance sheet (net worth statement) and cash flow statement, and key ratios such as savings ratio, liquidity ratio, solvency ratio and debt-to-income. It also covers emergency funds, good versus bad debt, loan prioritisation, prepayment decisions and credit scores. When revising, practise computing these ratios from data and recommending sensible actions, such as repaying expensive debt first or building a liquid buffer.

Client Financial Position & Debt Management questions

Question 1

A family's essential monthly expenses are ₹60,000. If the adviser recommends an emergency fund of six months' expenses, how much should it be?

  1. ₹60,000
  2. ₹7,20,000
  3. ₹1,80,000
  4. ₹3,60,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹3,60,000

Emergency fund = monthly essential expenses × months of cover = 60,000 × 6 = ₹3,60,000. ₹7,20,000 would be twelve months and ₹1,80,000 only three months.

Question 2

A client's monthly take-home income is ₹1,50,000 and her monthly expenses including EMIs are ₹1,05,000. What is her savings ratio?

  1. 30%
  2. 70%
  3. 45%
  4. 35%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 30%

Savings = 1,50,000 − 1,05,000 = ₹45,000. Savings ratio = 45,000 ÷ 1,50,000 = 30%. 70% is the share of income spent, not saved.

Question 3

A client has total assets of ₹1 crore and total liabilities of ₹40 lakh. What is the solvency ratio (net worth ÷ total assets)?

  1. 40%
  2. 60%
  3. 250%
  4. 167%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 60%

Net worth = ₹1 crore − ₹40 lakh = ₹60 lakh. Solvency ratio = 60 lakh ÷ 1 crore = 60%. 40% is the liabilities-to-assets ratio, the complement of solvency.

Question 4

Which of the following is generally considered 'good debt'?

  1. A credit card balance used to fund a holiday
  2. A personal loan taken to buy the latest smartphone
  3. An education loan for a skill that raises income
  4. A loan taken to speculate in futures and options
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. An education loan for a skill that raises income

Good debt finances assets or capabilities that are likely to grow in value or raise earning power. Consumption debt and borrowing to speculate do not create such value and often carry high cost.

Question 5

A client has ₹2 lakh surplus. She has a car loan at 9%, a personal loan at 15% and a home loan at 8.5% with tax benefits. Which should she prepay first?

  1. The home loan at 8.5%
  2. The car loan at 9%
  3. The personal loan at 15%
  4. Split the money equally across all three loans
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The personal loan at 15%

Prepaying the highest-cost debt gives the greatest guaranteed saving. The personal loan at 15% is costliest; the home loan is cheapest after tax benefits, so it should be prepaid last.

Question 6

A personal cash flow statement differs from a net worth statement because it shows:

  1. Assets and liabilities at a point in time
  2. Inflows and outflows over a period
  3. Only the market value of investments held
  4. Only the tax payable by the client
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Inflows and outflows over a period

A cash flow statement records income and expenses over a period, showing the surplus available. The net worth statement is a snapshot of assets minus liabilities on a given date.

Question 7

A client's liquid assets are ₹2,40,000 and monthly expenses are ₹80,000. His liquidity ratio (in months of expenses) is:

  1. 3 months
  2. 2 months
  3. 6 months
  4. 0.33 months
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 3 months

Liquidity ratio = liquid assets ÷ monthly expenses = 2,40,000 ÷ 80,000 = 3 months. It shows how long the client can meet expenses from liquid funds if income stops.

Question 8

Which action is most likely to improve a client's credit score over time?

  1. Applying for several new loans in the same month
  2. Using the full credit card limit every month
  3. Closing the oldest credit card and missing a payment
  4. Paying all EMIs and card dues on time
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Paying all EMIs and card dues on time

Timely repayment is the biggest factor in credit scores. Multiple applications, high credit utilisation and missed payments all lower the score.
