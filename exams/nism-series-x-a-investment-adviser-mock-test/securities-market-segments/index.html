Securities Market Segments: NISM Series X-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Securities Market Segments, from the NISM Series X-A (Investment Adviser (Level 1)) syllabus.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter describes the segments of the Indian securities market: the primary market for new issues, the secondary market for trading, and the equity cash, equity derivatives, currency, commodity and debt segments. It also covers SME platforms, government securities access for retail investors, and the roles of exchanges, clearing corporations and depositories. When revising, focus on what trades in each segment, how retail investors can access bonds and G-secs, and the basic process of an IPO and secondary trade.

Securities Market Segments questions

Question 1

A small and medium enterprise wants to list with lighter eligibility norms than the main board. Where can it list?

  1. The currency derivatives segment
  2. The commodity derivatives segment
  3. The negotiated dealing system of the RBI
  4. The SME platform of a stock exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The SME platform of a stock exchange

Exchanges run dedicated SME platforms (such as NSE Emerge and BSE SME) with separate eligibility and disclosure norms for smaller companies. Derivatives segments and NDS are for trading other instruments.

Question 2

Through RBI Retail Direct, an individual investor can:

  1. Trade equity options directly with the RBI
  2. Buy unlisted shares of private companies
  3. Open a gilt account and buy government securities
  4. Invest in foreign stocks without any limit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Open a gilt account and buy government securities

RBI Retail Direct lets individuals open a Retail Direct Gilt account with the RBI to buy G-secs in primary auctions and trade on NDS-OM. It does not cover equity options, unlisted shares or foreign stocks.

Question 3

In which segment would a trader buy USD/INR futures?

  1. Equity cash segment
  2. Currency derivatives segment
  3. Debt segment
  4. SME platform
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Currency derivatives segment

USD/INR futures and options trade in the currency derivatives segment of recognised exchanges. The equity cash segment is for shares, the debt segment for bonds and the SME platform for small company shares.

Question 4

An investor buys shares of a listed company from another investor on the exchange. This trade takes place in the:

  1. Secondary market
  2. Primary market
  3. Money market
  4. Over-the-counter forward market
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Secondary market

Trading in already-issued securities between investors happens in the secondary market. The primary market is where issuers sell new securities to raise capital.

Question 5

Which entity guarantees settlement of trades executed on a stock exchange?

  1. The depository participant
  2. The clearing corporation
  3. The registrar and transfer agent
  4. The issuing company
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The clearing corporation

The clearing corporation acts as central counterparty through novation and guarantees settlement. Depository participants hold demat accounts, RTAs maintain issuer records and the issuer has no role in trade settlement.

Question 6

Online bond platform providers that sell listed debt securities to retail investors are regulated by:

  1. IRDAI
  2. PFRDA
  3. SEBI
  4. The Ministry of Corporate Affairs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. SEBI

SEBI regulates online bond platform providers, which must be registered as stock brokers in the debt segment and offer only listed or to-be-listed debt securities. IRDAI and PFRDA regulate insurance and pensions.

Question 7

Why do exchanges maintain separate trading segments for equity, derivatives, currency and debt?

  1. To ensure that investors can trade only one product type in their life
  2. Because each segment is owned by a different government ministry
  3. So that prices in one segment are never linked to another
  4. Each has its own contracts and risk rules
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Each has its own contracts and risk rules

Different instruments need different contract specifications, margins and settlement processes, so exchanges organise them into segments. Investors can trade in several segments, and prices across segments are closely linked.

Question 8

A company's first-ever offer of shares to the public, followed by listing, is called:

  1. An initial public offer
  2. A follow-on public offer
  3. A rights issue
  4. A buyback offer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. An initial public offer

An IPO is the first sale of shares to the public by an unlisted company, after which it lists on an exchange. An FPO is by a listed company, and rights issues go only to existing holders.
