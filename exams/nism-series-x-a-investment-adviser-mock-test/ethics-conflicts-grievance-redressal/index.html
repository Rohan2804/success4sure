Ethics, Conflicts & Grievance Redressal: NISM Series X-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Ethics, Conflicts & Grievance Redressal, from the NISM Series X-A (Investment Adviser (Level 1)) syllabus.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the ethical duties of an investment adviser: acting in the client's best interest, avoiding or disclosing conflicts of interest, confidentiality, fair dealing, avoiding misrepresentation and maintaining professional competence. It also covers the investor grievance mechanism, including handling complaints within set timelines, SEBI SCORES and the online dispute resolution platform. When revising, apply principles to scenarios: identify the conflict, decide what must be disclosed or avoided, and know the correct path for escalating complaints.

Ethics, Conflicts & Grievance Redressal questions

Question 1

An adviser receives a gift voucher from a fund house whose schemes she recommends. What should she do?

  1. Accept it quietly as a normal business gift
  2. Recommend that fund house's schemes more often
  3. Decline it or disclose it as a conflict
  4. Share the voucher with the client to balance it
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Decline it or disclose it as a conflict

Benefits from product providers create conflicts that can bias advice. The adviser should avoid such benefits or at least disclose them; increasing recommendations would worsen the breach.

Question 2

An adviser shares a client's portfolio details with a property developer without consent. This breaches:

  1. Diversification norms
  2. Client confidentiality
  3. Asset allocation policy
  4. Rupee cost averaging
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Client confidentiality

Client information must be kept confidential and used only for advisory purposes unless consent is given or law requires disclosure. Sharing it with a developer is a serious breach.

Question 3

A client complains to an adviser. Within what broad expectation should the adviser respond?

  1. Promptly, within prescribed timelines
  2. Only when SEBI asks the adviser to respond
  3. After the client's next annual review
  4. Never, if the adviser thinks the complaint is wrong
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Promptly, within prescribed timelines

Advisers must have a grievance redressal mechanism and resolve complaints within SEBI's prescribed timelines. Unresolved complaints can be escalated by the client to SCORES and ODR.

Question 4

An adviser holds shares of a company she is about to recommend to clients. The ethical approach is to:

  1. Recommend it without mentioning her holding
  2. Buy more shares before recommending it
  3. Sell her shares just after clients buy
  4. Disclose her holding to clients
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Disclose her holding to clients

Personal holdings in recommended securities are a conflict that must be disclosed, and trading around recommendations is improper. Clients need to know of any interest that may affect advice.

Question 5

An adviser realises she lacks expertise in a client's complex cross-border tax issue. She should:

  1. Give a confident answer to keep the client
  2. Ignore the issue in the plan
  3. Copy advice from an online forum
  4. Refer the client to a qualified expert
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Refer the client to a qualified expert

Professional competence means knowing one's limits and seeking expert help where needed. Guessing or ignoring material issues can harm the client.

Question 6

If an investor's complaint is not resolved through SCORES, a further option is:

  1. The Banking Ombudsman
  2. SEBI's online dispute resolution platform
  3. The Income Tax department
  4. The Competition Commission of India
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. SEBI's online dispute resolution platform

SEBI's ODR platform offers online conciliation and arbitration for unresolved securities market disputes. The banking ombudsman, tax department and CCI do not handle such disputes.

Question 7

Showing a client only the best-performing period of a recommended fund, while hiding weaker periods, is:

  1. Acceptable marketing practice
  2. Required by SEBI for clarity
  3. Misleading and unethical
  4. Fine if the client is a high-net-worth investor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Misleading and unethical

Selective presentation of performance misrepresents the product. Advisers must present balanced, fair information covering appropriate periods, risks and benchmarks.

Question 8

Fair treatment of clients requires that an adviser:

  1. Does not favour some clients over others
  2. Gives the best ideas only to the largest clients
  3. Charges different fees without disclosure
  4. Allocates scarce opportunities to family members first
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Does not favour some clients over others

Fair dealing means treating all clients equitably in advice and allocation, with transparent fees. Favouring large clients or family members breaches this duty.
