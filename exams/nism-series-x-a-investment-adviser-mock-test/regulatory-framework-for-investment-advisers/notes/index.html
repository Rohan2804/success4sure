Regulatory Framework for Investment Advisers: NISM Series X-A revision notes

This chapter covers the SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013 and their amendments - who must register, qualification and certification requirements, fee rules, and the obligations on suitability and documentation. It is heavily tested. Several limits have been revised recently, so verify current numbers with SEBI as of 2026.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Who must register

Anyone giving investment advice for consideration, directly or indirectly, must register with SEBI as an investment adviser unless exempt. This includes individuals, partnerships and companies. Using titles like 'financial planner' or 'wealth adviser' while advising for a fee without registration is a violation.

2. Exemptions

Exempt categories include persons giving incidental advice in their main profession - such as lawyers, chartered accountants and company secretaries - and mutual fund distributors or brokers giving incidental advice on products they sell. Insurance agents under IRDAI and pension advisers under PFRDA are also exempt for those products.

3. Qualification and certification

Individual advisers and principal officers need educational qualifications and NISM certifications (Series X-A and X-B, or the consolidated requirement prescribed by SEBI), and must keep them current through renewal. Persons associated with advice need the same certification. Lapsed certification can bar an adviser from practising.

4. Supervision by BASL

SEBI has recognised BSE Administration and Supervision Ltd (BASL) as the body that administers and supervises investment advisers, including enrolment, fee collection and periodic inspection. Advisers must be members of BASL in addition to being registered with SEBI. Its inspections complement SEBI's own oversight.

5. Remuneration

An adviser may receive fees only from the client, never commissions from product providers on the same client. SEBI caps fees for individual and HUF clients through a fixed-fee limit per family or a percentage of assets under advice, and limits how far in advance fees can be collected. Check current limits on SEBI's website.

6. Segregation from distribution

Advisory and distribution activities must be kept separate at the client level: a client who takes advice cannot be sold products for commission by the same adviser or its group. Individual advisers cannot act as distributors at all. Family-level checks apply to related accounts.

7. Risk profiling and suitability

Before advising, the adviser must profile the client's risk, document it and ensure every recommendation is suitable for that profile and the client's objectives. Where the client wants a product inconsistent with the profile, the adviser must record the deviation and the client's acknowledgement.

8. Agreement and records

A written agreement covering scope, fees and terms must be signed before advice begins. Advisers must maintain records of client data, risk profiles, advice given and its rationale for at least five years, and conduct an annual compliance audit. Advice given over phone must be recorded.

Common traps in the exam

  • Assuming an IA may take commission as long as it is disclosed - advisers may receive fees only from clients.
  • Thinking all mutual fund distributors must register as IAs; incidental advice on their own products is exempt.
  • Forgetting the risk profiling and suitability documentation required before advice.
  • Quoting older fee caps as current - SEBI revised limits; check the latest circulars.

Quick recap

Advice for consideration requires SEBI IA registration unless exempt (lawyers, CAs, distributors giving incidental advice). Advisers need NISM certification and BASL membership. Fees come only from clients, within SEBI caps. Advice and distribution are segregated at client level. Risk profiling, suitability, written agreements and five-year records are mandatory.
