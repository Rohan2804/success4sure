Mutual Funds: NISM Series X-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Mutual Funds, from the NISM Series X-A (Investment Adviser (Level 1)) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers mutual funds as investment vehicles: structure (sponsor, trustee, AMC, custodian), scheme types under SEBI's categorisation, NAV, expense ratio, loads, direct and regular plans, SIPs, STPs and SWPs, risk-o-meter and taxation basics. Advisers must match schemes to client goals. When revising, compute redemption amounts and units, understand rupee cost averaging, know the main features of equity, debt, hybrid, index and solution-oriented schemes, and learn how to read a scheme's risk and cost information.

Mutual Funds questions

Question 1

A scheme's NAV is ₹50 and it has an exit load of 1% on redemptions within a year. An investor redeems within the year. What is the redemption price per unit?

  1. ₹50.50
  2. ₹50.00
  3. ₹49.00
  4. ₹49.50
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹49.50

Exit load is deducted from NAV: 50 × (1 − 0.01) = ₹49.50. ₹50.50 wrongly adds the load, which would apply to an entry load, now not charged by mutual funds.

Question 2

An investor invests ₹6,000 each month for three months at NAVs of ₹20, ₹25 and ₹30. What is his average cost per unit?

  1. ₹25.00
  2. About ₹26.10
  3. About ₹24.32
  4. ₹20.00
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. About ₹24.32

Units bought = 300 + 240 + 200 = 740. Average cost = 18,000 ÷ 740 ≈ ₹24.32. This is below the simple average NAV of ₹25, illustrating rupee cost averaging.

Question 3

Under SEBI's scheme categorisation, a multi cap fund must invest at least:

  1. 65% in large-cap stocks only
  2. 25% each in large, mid and small caps
  3. 80% in a single sector
  4. 50% in debt and 50% in equity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 25% each in large, mid and small caps

SEBI requires multi cap funds to hold at least 25% each in large-, mid- and small-cap stocks, ensuring true diversification across market caps. Flexi cap funds have no such minimums.

Question 4

A client wants to move money from a liquid fund into an equity fund in equal monthly instalments. Which facility suits this?

  1. Systematic transfer plan
  2. Systematic withdrawal plan
  3. Dividend reinvestment option
  4. New fund offer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Systematic transfer plan

An STP periodically transfers a fixed amount from one scheme to another of the same fund house, spreading equity entry over time. An SWP pays money out to the investor.

Question 5

Who holds the assets of a mutual fund scheme in safe custody?

  1. The custodian
  2. The asset management company
  3. The sponsor
  4. The registrar and transfer agent
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The custodian

The custodian, registered with SEBI, holds the scheme's securities. The AMC manages the money, the trustees oversee the AMC, and the RTA maintains unitholder records.

Question 6

Equity linked savings schemes have a lock-in period of:

  1. 1 year
  2. 5 years
  3. No lock-in at all
  4. 3 years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 3 years

ELSS units are locked in for three years from the date of each investment, including each SIP instalment. They carry the shortest lock-in among tax-saving investment options under the old regime.

Question 7

An arbitrage fund earns returns mainly from:

  1. Long-term bets on small-cap stocks
  2. Price gaps between cash and futures markets
  3. Investing in foreign real estate
  4. Lending to unrated companies
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Price gaps between cash and futures markets

Arbitrage funds buy in the cash market and sell in futures (or vice versa) to capture the spread, giving low-risk, money-market-like returns while being treated as equity-oriented for tax.

Question 8

The riskometer on a mutual fund scheme is meant to show:

  1. The guaranteed return of the scheme
  2. The fund manager's personal rating
  3. The scheme's level of risk to investors
  4. The number of investors in the scheme
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The scheme's level of risk to investors

The riskometer grades a scheme's risk from low to very high based on its portfolio and is reviewed monthly. It does not indicate returns or fund manager quality.
