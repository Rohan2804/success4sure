Indian Financial Markets: NISM Series X-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Indian Financial Markets, from the NISM Series X-A (Investment Adviser (Level 1)) syllabus.

What this chapter covers

This chapter gives an overview of the Indian financial system: money markets and capital markets, their instruments, participants and regulators. Money market instruments include treasury bills, commercial paper, certificates of deposit, call money and repo, while capital markets cover equity, bonds and related products. Regulators include the RBI, SEBI, IRDAI and PFRDA. Focus on which regulator oversees which market or product, the typical tenor and issuer of each instrument, and the role of financial intermediaries in channelling savings into investment.

Indian Financial Markets questions

Question 1

Which instrument is issued by banks to raise short-term funds and is a money market instrument?

  1. Commercial paper
  2. Certificate of deposit
  3. Treasury bill
  4. Equity share
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Certificate of deposit

Certificates of deposit are issued by banks and certain financial institutions. Commercial paper is issued by companies, treasury bills by the government through the RBI, and equity shares are capital market instruments.

Question 2

Treasury bills in India are currently issued with which maturities?

  1. 30, 60 and 90 days
  2. 1, 3 and 5 years
  3. 7, 14 and 28 days
  4. 91, 182 and 364 days
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 91, 182 and 364 days

The Government of India issues treasury bills of 91, 182 and 364 days through RBI auctions. They are issued at a discount and redeemed at face value.

Question 3

Which regulator oversees the National Pension System?

  1. IRDAI
  2. SEBI
  3. PFRDA
  4. NABARD
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. PFRDA

The Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority regulates NPS and pension funds. IRDAI regulates insurance, SEBI regulates securities markets and NABARD is a development bank for agriculture.

Question 4

In a repo transaction, a bank:

  1. Sells securities and agrees to buy them back later
  2. Buys shares permanently from the RBI
  3. Issues new equity to the public
  4. Lends money without any collateral
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Sells securities and agrees to buy them back later

In a repo, the borrower sells securities with an agreement to repurchase them at a set price, making it a collateralised loan. The difference in prices is the interest, called the repo rate.

Question 5

Which of these is a function of financial intermediaries such as banks and mutual funds?

  1. Printing currency notes for circulation
  2. Setting the policy repo rate
  3. Pooling savings and channelling them into investment
  4. Passing laws on taxation of income
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Pooling savings and channelling them into investment

Intermediaries mobilise savings from many households and allocate them to borrowers and issuers, offering diversification and liquidity. Currency issue and policy rates are RBI functions; tax laws are made by Parliament.

Question 6

Commercial paper is typically issued by:

  1. The central government to fund long-term deficits
  2. Highly rated corporates for short-term funds
  3. Retail investors to borrow from banks
  4. Stock exchanges to finance trading members
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Highly rated corporates for short-term funds

Commercial paper is an unsecured short-term promissory note issued by creditworthy companies and certain financial institutions to meet working capital needs. Governments use T-bills and dated securities.

Question 7

Which statement about the capital market is correct?

  1. It deals in long-term instruments like shares and bonds
  2. It deals only in instruments maturing within one year
  3. It is regulated mainly by IRDAI
  4. It does not include any secondary market trading
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. It deals in long-term instruments like shares and bonds

The capital market provides long-term funds through equity and debt securities and includes both primary issues and secondary trading. Instruments under one year belong to the money market.

Question 8

Since the merger of the Forward Markets Commission with SEBI in 2015, commodity derivatives exchanges are regulated by:

  1. RBI
  2. Ministry of Agriculture
  3. Competition Commission of India
  4. SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. SEBI

The FMC was merged into SEBI in September 2015, bringing commodity derivatives under SEBI's regulation alongside securities markets. RBI regulates money and forex markets, not commodity exchanges.
