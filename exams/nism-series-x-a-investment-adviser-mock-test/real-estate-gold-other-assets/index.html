Real Estate, Gold & Other Assets: NISM Series X-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Real Estate, Gold & Other Assets, from the NISM Series X-A (Investment Adviser (Level 1)) syllabus.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers alternative and real assets for individual investors: residential and commercial real estate, REITs and InvITs, gold in physical and financial forms (ETFs, funds, sovereign gold bonds), silver ETFs and other assets. Key themes are liquidity, costs, valuation, rental yields and diversification benefits. When revising, compare direct property with REITs, physical gold with gold ETFs, and think about how much of a client's portfolio should go into such assets and why.

Real Estate, Gold & Other Assets questions

Question 1

A flat bought for ₹80 lakh earns annual rent of ₹2.4 lakh. What is its gross rental yield?

  1. 3%
  2. 30%
  3. 0.3%
  4. 6%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 3%

Gross rental yield = annual rent ÷ property value = 2.4 lakh ÷ 80 lakh = 3%. Residential yields in Indian cities are often low, so returns depend heavily on capital appreciation.

Question 2

Compared with buying commercial property directly, investing in a listed REIT offers:

  1. Guaranteed rental income every month
  2. Smaller ticket size and better liquidity
  3. Complete control over the building's management
  4. No exposure to real estate market risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Smaller ticket size and better liquidity

REIT units can be bought in small amounts and traded on exchanges, giving diversification across properties. Income is not guaranteed and unitholders still bear real estate risk.

Question 3

An InvIT mainly holds:

  1. Residential flats rented to individuals
  2. Gold bars held in bank vaults
  3. Infrastructure assets such as roads and power lines
  4. Shares of listed IT companies
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Infrastructure assets such as roads and power lines

Infrastructure Investment Trusts own revenue-generating infrastructure assets like toll roads, transmission lines and pipelines. REITs hold real estate, and gold or shares are held by other vehicles.

Question 4

Why is gold often included in a diversified portfolio?

  1. It pays a high regular dividend
  2. It is guaranteed to beat inflation every year
  3. Its price never falls in rupee terms
  4. It tends to have low correlation with equities
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. It tends to have low correlation with equities

Gold often behaves differently from equities, especially in crises, so a modest allocation can reduce portfolio volatility. It pays no income on its own and its price can fall.

Question 5

A key drawback of direct real estate as an investment is:

  1. Its price is quoted every second on exchanges
  2. Illiquidity and high transaction costs
  3. It cannot be used as collateral for loans
  4. It always gives lower returns than bank deposits
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Illiquidity and high transaction costs

Property takes time to sell, needs large amounts and attracts stamp duty, registration and brokerage costs. These features reduce flexibility compared with financial assets.

Question 6

Gold ETFs in India are backed by:

  1. Shares of gold mining companies only
  2. Bank fixed deposits
  3. Government securities
  4. Physical gold of specified purity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Physical gold of specified purity

Gold ETFs hold physical gold of prescribed purity, and their NAV tracks domestic gold prices. Funds investing in mining companies are equity funds, not gold ETFs.

Question 7

A client wants gold exposure without storage worries and with exchange liquidity. Which product fits best?

  1. A gold exchange traded fund
  2. Gold jewellery from a local shop
  3. Gold coins kept in a home locker
  4. A gold loan from an NBFC
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A gold exchange traded fund

Gold ETFs are held in demat form, avoid making charges and storage risk, and can be traded on exchanges. A gold loan is borrowing against gold, not an investment.

Question 8

SEBI requires REITs to invest most of their assets in:

  1. Undeveloped agricultural land
  2. Listed equity shares of any sector
  3. Completed, revenue-generating properties
  4. Foreign currency deposits
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Completed, revenue-generating properties

SEBI regulations require the bulk of a REIT's value (at least 80%) to be in completed and rent-generating properties, limiting development risk. This supports stable distributions.
