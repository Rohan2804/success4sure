PMS, AIF & Other Managed Portfolios: NISM Series X-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on PMS, AIF & Other Managed Portfolios, from the NISM Series X-A (Investment Adviser (Level 1)) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers managed investment products beyond mutual funds: portfolio management services (discretionary, non-discretionary and advisory), alternative investment funds (Categories I, II and III), and newer options such as specialised investment funds. Learn their regulatory framework, minimum investment, structure, fees, taxation basics, transparency and suitability for high-net-worth investors. When revising, compare these products with mutual funds on cost, flexibility, risk, and liquidity, and know which AIF categories may use leverage.

PMS, AIF & Other Managed Portfolios questions

Question 1

In a portfolio management service, the client's securities are held:

  1. In the portfolio manager's proprietary account
  2. In a pooled account like a mutual fund
  3. In the client's own demat account
  4. With the stock exchange as collateral
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. In the client's own demat account

PMS portfolios are held individually in the client's name, giving full transparency and individual ownership. Mutual funds pool money and investors hold units, not the underlying securities.

Question 2

Which AIF category can use leverage and employ complex trading strategies, including hedge funds?

  1. Category I
  2. Category III
  3. Category II
  4. None, AIFs cannot use leverage
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Category III

Category III AIFs, such as hedge funds, may use leverage and complex strategies subject to limits. Category I invests in socially or economically desirable areas, and Category II includes private equity and debt funds without leverage for investment.

Question 3

In a non-discretionary PMS, investment decisions are made:

  1. By the manager only with client approval
  2. Entirely by the portfolio manager without consulting the client
  3. By SEBI on behalf of the client
  4. Automatically by an index-tracking algorithm
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. By the manager only with client approval

In a non-discretionary PMS, the manager recommends and executes trades only after the client approves them. In a discretionary PMS, the manager decides on the client's behalf.

Question 4

Category I AIFs include funds that invest in:

  1. Listed derivatives with high leverage
  2. Only government securities
  3. Only gold and silver
  4. Start-ups, SMEs and infrastructure
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Start-ups, SMEs and infrastructure

Category I AIFs include venture capital, angel, SME and infrastructure funds that the government considers economically or socially desirable. Leveraged trading strategies fall under Category III.

Question 5

Compared with a mutual fund, a PMS generally offers:

  1. More customisation at a higher minimum
  2. Lower minimum investment and pooled ownership
  3. Guaranteed returns approved by SEBI
  4. Daily NAV published on AMFI's website
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. More customisation at a higher minimum

PMS offers a personalised portfolio with individual holdings, but has a high minimum ticket and often performance-linked fees. Mutual funds are pooled, have low minimums and publish daily NAVs.

Question 6

A Category II AIF that invests in unlisted companies is best described as:

  1. A liquid mutual fund
  2. A private equity fund
  3. A government bond fund
  4. An index exchange traded fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A private equity fund

Private equity and private debt funds are typical Category II AIFs investing in unlisted companies or instruments. Liquid funds, G-sec funds and ETFs are mutual fund products.

Question 7

Why are PMS and AIF products generally suitable only for wealthier, experienced investors?

  1. They are banned for residents of India
  2. They do not allow any investment in equity
  3. They need large tickets and carry higher risk
  4. They are guaranteed by the government
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. They need large tickets and carry higher risk

High minimum investments, concentrated or complex strategies and limited liquidity make these products suitable mainly for investors with large portfolios and the ability to bear losses.

Question 8

A portfolio manager reports performance to clients. SEBI requires PMS performance to be reported using:

  1. Only the return in the best month
  2. The return before any fees and expenses
  3. A simple average of yearly returns
  4. A time-weighted rate of return
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A time-weighted rate of return

SEBI requires PMS returns to be computed on a time-weighted basis, net of fees and expenses, and compared with a benchmark, allowing fair comparison across managers.
