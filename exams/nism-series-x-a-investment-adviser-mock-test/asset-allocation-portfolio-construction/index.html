Asset Allocation & Portfolio Construction: NISM Series X-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Asset Allocation & Portfolio Construction, from the NISM Series X-A (Investment Adviser (Level 1)) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers asset allocation as the main driver of portfolio outcomes: strategic, tactical and dynamic allocation, risk and return of portfolios, correlation and diversification, core-satellite construction, rebalancing, and the investment policy statement. When revising, practise calculating portfolio return and risk, understand how correlation affects diversification, and be able to propose an allocation from a client's goals, horizon and risk profile and explain when and how to rebalance.

Asset Allocation & Portfolio Construction questions

Question 1

A portfolio has 60% in asset A (standard deviation 20%) and 40% in asset B (standard deviation 8%), with a correlation of 0.2. Its standard deviation is closest to:

  1. 15.2%
  2. 10.9%
  3. 13.0%
  4. 17.0%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 13.0%

σp = √(0.6²×0.2² + 0.4²×0.08² + 2×0.6×0.4×0.2×0.08×0.2) ≈ 13.0%. 15.2% is the weighted average, which applies only with perfect correlation.

Question 2

In a core-satellite approach, the 'core' is usually invested in:

  1. Low-cost, diversified funds
  2. Highly concentrated small-cap bets
  3. Frequent trading in options
  4. A single sector expected to boom
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Low-cost, diversified funds

The core holds broad, low-cost investments that capture market returns reliably. Satellites are smaller positions in active or thematic ideas that aim for extra returns.

Question 3

An investment policy statement (IPS) mainly records:

  1. Daily market tips given to the client
  2. Objectives, constraints and allocation policy
  3. Only the adviser's fee invoice
  4. The client's tax return for the year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Objectives, constraints and allocation policy

The IPS documents the client's goals, risk tolerance, time horizon, liquidity and other constraints, target allocation and review rules. It guides decisions and keeps both parties disciplined.

Question 4

Which client is most suited to a higher equity allocation?

  1. A 75-year-old retiree living off portfolio income
  2. A client needing the money for a house in one year
  3. A client with no emergency fund and unstable income
  4. A 30-year-old with stable income and long goals
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A 30-year-old with stable income and long goals

A long horizon and stable income increase capacity to bear equity volatility. Short horizons, reliance on portfolio income and lack of buffers argue for lower equity.

Question 5

Dynamic asset allocation differs from strategic allocation because it:

  1. Never changes once set at the start
  2. Invests only in fixed deposits
  3. Is used only by government pension funds
  4. Changes the mix based on market conditions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Changes the mix based on market conditions

Dynamic allocation shifts between equity and debt in response to valuations or other signals. Strategic allocation is a long-term target mix maintained by periodic rebalancing.

Question 6

Calendar rebalancing means a portfolio is rebalanced:

  1. At fixed intervals, such as yearly
  2. Only when the client asks for a new product
  3. Whenever the market moves by 1% in a day
  4. Never, to save transaction costs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. At fixed intervals, such as yearly

Calendar rebalancing resets the portfolio to target weights at regular intervals. Threshold rebalancing instead triggers when weights drift beyond set bands.

Question 7

Adding an asset with negative correlation to an existing portfolio is likely to:

  1. Increase overall portfolio volatility sharply
  2. Reduce overall portfolio volatility
  3. Have no effect on portfolio risk
  4. Guarantee a higher return
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Reduce overall portfolio volatility

Negatively correlated assets tend to move in opposite directions, smoothing portfolio returns and lowering volatility. Diversification does not guarantee higher returns.

Question 8

A client's risk profile suggests 50% equity, but she wants 90% equity after hearing friends' stories. The adviser should:

  1. Immediately raise equity to 90% to keep her happy
  2. Refuse to advise her any further
  3. Explain the risks and stick to a suitable allocation
  4. Move everything into a single hot stock
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Explain the risks and stick to a suitable allocation

Advisers must recommend suitable allocations and educate clients on risks. Changing the allocation purely due to peer influence would breach suitability; refusing to engage helps no one.
