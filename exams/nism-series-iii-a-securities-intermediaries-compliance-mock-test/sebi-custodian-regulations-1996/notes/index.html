SEBI (Custodian) Regulations, 1996: NISM Series III-A revision notes

Custodians safeguard securities and related assets for institutional clients such as mutual funds, FPIs, insurers and portfolio managers. The Custodian Regulations, 1996 define custodial services, set eligibility and capital norms and require strict segregation and controls. Exams test the scope of services, the net worth requirement, conflicts of interest and the custodian's duties on corporate actions.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Custodial services

Custodial services mean safekeeping of securities, gold and other assets of a client, along with related services such as collecting dividends and interest, tracking and acting on corporate actions, maintaining accounts of holdings, and settling trades by receiving or delivering securities and funds. A mutual fund cannot operate without a custodian.

2. Eligibility

Only an entity registered with SEBI may act as custodian. It must have a minimum net worth of ₹50 crore, adequate infrastructure, trained staff and systems for safekeeping and record keeping. Most custodians in India are banks or bank subsidiaries.

3. Segregation of assets

Client assets must be held separately from the custodian's own assets and from those of other clients. Each client's holdings must be identifiable at all times. If the custodian fails, client assets are not available to its creditors.

4. Internal controls

The custodian must have controls to prevent fraud, errors and unauthorised transfers, conduct regular reconciliation with depositories and clients, and undergo periodic audit. Instructions must come from authorised signatories under agreed procedures.

5. Corporate actions

The custodian informs the client of rights issues, bonuses, buybacks, mergers and voting events, acts on instructions within deadlines, and collects entitlements. Missing a rights issue deadline for an FPI client due to delay in passing on information would be a service failure.

6. Conflicts of interest

A custodian should not act for a mutual fund whose sponsor or AMC is its associate unless specified conditions on independence (such as minimum directors who are independent) are met. It must avoid conflicts between its own business and its role as custodian, and disclose any that arise.

7. Agreement and reporting

Each client relationship is governed by a written agreement setting out duties, fees, liability and reporting. Custodians report holdings and transactions to clients and file periodic reports with SEBI. They also often act as DDPs for FPI registration.

8. Records and inspection

Records of client holdings, instructions and correspondence must be maintained for the period specified and are open to SEBI inspection. A compliance officer oversees adherence.

Common traps in the exam

  • The custodian's minimum net worth is ₹50 crore, higher than many other intermediaries.
  • Custodians safekeep and service assets; they do not decide what to buy or sell for the client.
  • Client assets are not part of the custodian's estate on insolvency.
  • Associate relationships with an AMC need independence safeguards; they are not automatically allowed.

Quick recap

Custodians safekeep securities and other assets for institutional clients and service them: settlements, dividends, corporate actions and records. SEBI registration and ₹50 crore net worth are required. Client assets are segregated and protected from the custodian's creditors. Strong internal controls, reconciliation and written agreements are mandatory. Conflicts with associated AMCs need independence safeguards.
