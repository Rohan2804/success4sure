SEBI (Custodian) Regulations, 1996: NISM Series III-A practice questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the SEBI (Custodian) Regulations, 1996, which govern entities that provide custodial services for securities and other assets. It explains what custodial services are, eligibility and net worth, separation of custodial business, obligations such as safekeeping, segregation, reconciliation and collection of corporate benefits, internal controls and audit, record keeping, and the code of conduct. It also covers custodians' roles for mutual funds and FPIs. Focus on the core services, the duty to keep client assets separate and reconciled, and restrictions on custodians that are associated with the entities they serve.

SEBI (Custodian) Regulations, 1996 questions

Question 1

A custodian must reconcile client holdings with depository records regularly in order to:

  1. Increase its fee income from clients
  2. Reduce the net worth it needs to maintain
  3. Avoid informing clients of corporate actions
  4. Detect and correct any mismatch in clients' securities promptly
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Detect and correct any mismatch in clients' securities promptly

Regular reconciliation ensures that the custodian's books match depository records and that client assets are accounted for correctly. Breaks must be investigated and reported promptly.

Question 2

Which client typically must appoint a SEBI-registered custodian before investing in India?

  1. A salaried employee investing via SIP
  2. A resident individual buying shares through a broker
  3. A foreign portfolio investor
  4. A retail investor applying in an IPO
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A foreign portfolio investor

FPIs are required to appoint a custodian for safekeeping, settlement and regulatory reporting. Retail investors use DPs instead. DPs serve resident retail investors instead.

Question 3

Under the Custodian Regulations, a custodian must have adequate internal controls. A key reason is to:

  1. Allow the custodian to lend client securities without consent
  2. Prevent loss, misuse or unauthorised use of client assets
  3. Enable the custodian to trade on clients' behalf
  4. Replace the need for audits
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Prevent loss, misuse or unauthorised use of client assets

Internal controls, audits and checks protect client assets from errors, fraud and misuse, which is the custodian's core responsibility. Internal and external audits test these controls regularly.

Question 4

A custodian receives notice of a rights issue by a company whose shares it holds for a client. It should:

  1. Sell the client's shares to avoid the rights issue
  2. Ignore the notice, as rights issues are optional
  3. Inform the client promptly and act on the client's instructions
  4. Subscribe to the rights on its own without asking, using the client's cash
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Inform the client promptly and act on the client's instructions

Custodians must keep clients informed of corporate actions and act on their instructions, while collecting benefits such as dividends and rights.

Question 5

For a mutual fund, SEBI rules restrict the appointment of a custodian that is:

  1. Registered with SEBI for over five years
  2. An associate in which the sponsor or its associates hold a controlling stake
  3. Based in a different city from the asset management company's registered head office
  4. A scheduled commercial bank
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. An associate in which the sponsor or its associates hold a controlling stake

To protect independence, a custodian in which the sponsor or its associates hold 50% or more of voting rights generally cannot be the fund's custodian.

Question 6

A custodian provides custodial services for gold held by a gold ETF. This is:

  1. Prohibited, as custodians can hold only shares
  2. Allowed only if the gold is held abroad
  3. Permitted, as custodial services can extend to goods such as gold held by mutual funds
  4. Allowed only for foreign investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Permitted, as custodial services can extend to goods such as gold held by mutual funds

Custodians may provide safekeeping for goods such as gold or silver held by mutual fund schemes, in addition to securities.

Question 7

Under the Custodian Regulations, a custodian must maintain records of its clients' securities and transactions so that:

  1. Only the custodian's directors can see them, and not even SEBI inspectors
  2. SEBI can inspect them and clients can be given accurate statements
  3. The records can be deleted after each quarter
  4. The custodian can sell the records to data vendors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. SEBI can inspect them and clients can be given accurate statements

Custodians must keep accurate and complete records for the prescribed period and make them available to SEBI and clients as required.

Question 8

Which of the following is NOT ordinarily a custodial service?

  1. Safekeeping of securities
  2. Deciding which securities the client should buy
  3. Informing clients of corporate actions
  4. Collecting dividends and interest for the client
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Deciding which securities the client should buy

Custodians safeguard assets, settle trades and collect benefits. Investment decisions are made by the client or its fund manager, not the custodian.
