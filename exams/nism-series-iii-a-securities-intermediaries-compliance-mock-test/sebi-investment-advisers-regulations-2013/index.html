SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013: NISM Series III-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013, from the NISM Series III-A (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Non-Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013, which regulate persons giving investment advice for a fee. It explains registration and exemptions, qualification and certification, net worth, the separation of advice from distribution, the investment advisory agreement, risk profiling and suitability, fee modes and limits, implementation services, disclosure of conflicts, record keeping, annual compliance audit and grievance redressal. It also covers the administration and supervisory body for advisers. Revise who must register, the duties owed to clients, rules on fees and agreements and the restrictions on earning commissions from products recommended.

SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013 questions

Question 1

An investment adviser wants to help a client invest in mutual funds recommended in its advice. Under the regulations, the adviser may provide implementation services:

  1. Through regular plans to earn trail commission
  2. Only in schemes of the adviser's associate AMC
  3. Only through direct plans, without earning any commission
  4. Only if the client separately pays an extra commission to the adviser
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Only through direct plans, without earning any commission

Investment advisers may offer implementation through direct plans of mutual funds, and cannot receive commission or consideration from product providers.

Question 2

Before providing investment advice, a registered investment adviser must:

  1. Enter into an investment advisory agreement with the client
  2. Collect fees for five years in advance
  3. Obtain the client's demat account password
  4. Get the client's employer to approve the advice
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Enter into an investment advisory agreement with the client

The regulations require an advisory agreement setting out terms, fees and the scope of services before advice is given. The agreement also covers fees and termination terms.

Question 3

A mutual fund distributor describes himself as an 'independent financial adviser' on his visiting card. This is:

  1. Permitted as long as he holds a valid ARN and has passed the relevant NISM distributor exam
  2. Permitted after informing his AMC
  3. Permitted if he advises fewer than 100 clients
  4. Not permitted, as the term 'adviser' is restricted to registered investment advisers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Not permitted, as the term 'adviser' is restricted to registered investment advisers

SEBI prohibits distributors who are not registered as investment advisers from using terms like 'adviser' or 'IFA', to avoid misleading investors.

Question 4

Who among the following is generally exempt from registration as an investment adviser?

  1. An individual charging fees for stock-specific advice via an app
  2. A firm giving portfolio advice to the public for a fixed fee
  3. A person giving advice exclusively on insurance products regulated by IRDAI
  4. A company providing comprehensive financial planning services to families for a fee
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A person giving advice exclusively on insurance products regulated by IRDAI

Persons advising only on products regulated by another regulator, like insurance agents on IRDAI products, are exempt. Paid securities advice generally needs registration.

Question 5

A registered investment adviser must get an annual compliance audit conducted. The audit is meant to:

  1. Certify the adviser's investment returns for marketing
  2. Approve the adviser's personal investments
  3. Check the adviser's compliance with the IA Regulations and SEBI circulars
  4. Calculate the adviser's income-tax liability
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Check the adviser's compliance with the IA Regulations and SEBI circulars

The annual audit, conducted by a qualified auditor, verifies adherence to the regulations and circulars, and its findings must be acted upon.

Question 6

An investment adviser recommends a high-risk small-cap fund to a retired client whose risk profile shows low risk tolerance. This is a breach of:

  1. No rule at all, since the client was always free to refuse the advice given
  2. Only the client's own responsibility to read documents
  3. The suitability obligation, as advice must match the client's risk profile
  4. The adviser's duty to maximise returns at any cost
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The suitability obligation, as advice must match the client's risk profile

Advisers must ensure that advice is suitable for the client based on risk profiling, and must not recommend products beyond the client's risk capacity.

Question 7

The administration and supervisory body for investment advisers, recognised by SEBI, is responsible for:

  1. Fixing the fees that advisers can charge
  2. Registering new mutual fund schemes before they are launched to investors
  3. Paying compensation to all clients of advisers
  4. Supervising registered advisers, including inspection and monitoring
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Supervising registered advisers, including inspection and monitoring

SEBI recognised a stock exchange subsidiary as the administration and supervisory body for IAs, which handles supervision, inspection and related functions.

Question 8

A registered investment adviser should disclose to clients:

  1. Any conflict of interest, including any consideration received from associates
  2. Its competitors' fee structures
  3. The names of its other clients
  4. Only the positive outcomes of past advice
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Any conflict of interest, including any consideration received from associates

Advisers must disclose material conflicts, such as associate relationships or interests in recommended products, so clients can evaluate the advice.
