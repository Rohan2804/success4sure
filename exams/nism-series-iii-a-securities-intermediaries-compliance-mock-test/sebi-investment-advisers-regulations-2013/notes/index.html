SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013: NISM Series III-A revision notes

The Investment Advisers Regulations, 2013 bring fee-based investment advice under SEBI registration and separate it from commission-based distribution. Questions test who needs registration, exemptions, qualification and certification rules, segregation of advisory and distribution, risk profiling and suitability, and fee limits. Several provisions were amended in 2024 and 2025, so treat limits as 'as of 2026 — check SEBI'.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Who must register

Anyone giving investment advice for consideration, directly or indirectly, and holding out as an adviser must register. Exemptions include advice given incidentally by professionals like chartered accountants and lawyers, insurance agents advising only on insurance, and distributors giving incidental advice on products they distribute without charging an advisory fee.

2. Qualifications and certification

Individual advisers and principal officers need prescribed education and experience and must pass NISM Series X-A and X-B certifications, renewing them periodically. Persons associated with advice in a non-individual IA need the same certifications. SEBI relaxed some educational criteria in recent amendments.

3. Segregation from distribution

An IA cannot provide distribution services to its advisory clients, and the separation applies at the client's family level. A body corporate must keep advisory and distribution in separate subsidiaries or divisions with an arm's-length relationship. An individual IA cannot offer distribution services at all.

4. Risk profiling

Before advising, the IA must collect information on the client's age, income, liabilities, investment objective, horizon, risk appetite and capacity to absorb loss, and assign a risk profile. The profile must be communicated to the client and updated periodically. Advice must follow the profile.

5. Suitability

Recommendations must be suitable to the client's risk profile and needs. Example: suggesting small-cap equity funds and options strategies to a 68-year-old with a conservative profile and short horizon would breach suitability even if the client agreed. The IA should document the reason for each recommendation.

6. Fees

Fees are charged under one of two modes per family: as a percentage of assets under advice or as a fixed fee, within limits SEBI specifies (for example, the AUA mode cap of 2.5% a year; as of 2026 — check the latest SEBI circular). Limits apply to individual and HUF clients. Fees should be paid through banking channels, not cash.

7. Agreement and disclosures

An IA must sign a client agreement containing mandatory terms, disclose conflicts of interest, its registration details, and any consideration received from product manufacturers. Disclaimers cannot hide material risks. Advertisements must follow SEBI's advertisement code and avoid promising returns.

8. Records and audit

IAs keep records of risk profiles, suitability, advice given with rationale, client agreements, fees and complaints for five years, and undergo annual compliance audits. Grievances must be addressed promptly and can be escalated through SCORES and ODR.

Common traps in the exam

  • An individual IA cannot act as a distributor for anyone, not just for advisory clients.
  • Segregation applies at family level: advising a husband and distributing to his wife counts as the same client family.
  • A client's signed agreement to unsuitable advice does not cure a suitability failure.
  • Mutual fund distributors giving incidental advice without a fee are exempt; charging an advisory fee needs registration.

Quick recap

Fee-based investment advice needs SEBI IA registration with NISM X-A and X-B. Advisory and distribution must be separated, at family level; individuals cannot distribute at all. Do risk profiling, give only suitable advice, document rationale, sign a client agreement with disclosures. Fees by AUA or fixed mode within SEBI limits. Keep records five years and do annual compliance audit.
