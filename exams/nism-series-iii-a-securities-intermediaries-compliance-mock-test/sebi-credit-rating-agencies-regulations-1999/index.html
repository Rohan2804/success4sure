SEBI (Credit Rating Agencies) Regulations, 1999: NISM Series III-A practice questions

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the SEBI (Credit Rating Agencies) Regulations, 1999, which regulate entities that rate debt instruments and other securities. It covers eligibility of promoters, net worth, the rating process, rating committees, surveillance and review, rating symbols, disclosures such as rating rationales, non-cooperation by issuers and default recognition, restrictions on rating related entities, separation of non-rating businesses and the code of conduct. Revise the nature of a credit rating, the safeguards against conflicts of interest, the obligation to keep ratings under continuous surveillance and the disclosures a CRA must make to investors and the market.

SEBI (Credit Rating Agencies) Regulations, 1999 questions

Question 1

A credit rating agency must keep a rating under surveillance during the life of the instrument. This means it must:

  1. Review the rating at least once a year and whenever significant developments occur
  2. Review the rating only when the issuer requests it and pays a separate additional review fee
  3. Withdraw the rating after the first interest payment
  4. Never change a rating once assigned
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Review the rating at least once a year and whenever significant developments occur

CRAs must continuously monitor ratings, conduct at least annual reviews and act promptly on material events that may affect creditworthiness.

Question 2

An issuer stops giving information to its credit rating agency. The CRA should:

  1. Upgrade the rating to encourage cooperation
  2. Delete the rating from its records without disclosure
  3. Keep the rating unchanged indefinitely until the issuer resumes sharing information with it
  4. Flag the issuer as 'not cooperating' and review the rating based on available information
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Flag the issuer as 'not cooperating' and review the rating based on available information

Where an issuer is non-cooperative, the CRA must disclose this, flag the rating as 'issuer not cooperating' and rate based on available information.

Question 3

To discourage 'rating shopping', CRAs must disclose:

  1. Ratings that were not accepted by the issuer
  2. The names of their own shareholders' clients
  3. The personal holdings of the issuer's directors
  4. The fees charged to each issuer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Ratings that were not accepted by the issuer

Disclosure of non-accepted ratings prevents issuers from seeking multiple ratings and publishing only the most favourable one. Investors can then see the full rating history of the issuer.

Question 4

A credit rating decision at a CRA is taken by:

  1. The issuer's board of directors
  2. A rating committee, based on analysis by the rating team
  3. The stock exchange where the debt is listed
  4. The marketing team that obtained the mandate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A rating committee, based on analysis by the rating team

Ratings are decided by a rating committee to ensure objectivity, separating rating decisions from business development and commercial considerations. Business teams cannot influence rating outcomes.

Question 5

A CRA publishes a press release explaining the key factors behind a rating. This is called a:

  1. Rating mandate
  2. Rating agreement
  3. Rating symbol
  4. Rating rationale
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Rating rationale

CRAs must publish rating rationales covering the key drivers, strengths, weaknesses and outlook, so investors understand the basis of the rating.

Question 6

Under SEBI norms, an issuer's failure to pay principal or interest even by one day beyond the due date is generally recognised by CRAs as:

  1. A reason to upgrade the rating
  2. A default
  3. A minor delay with no rating impact
  4. A technical error that can be ignored
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A default

CRAs follow a uniform default recognition policy in which even a one-day delay in servicing debt is treated as a default unless specific conditions apply.

Question 7

Which of the following is a correct statement about a credit rating?

  1. It reflects the market price of the security
  2. It is not a recommendation to buy, sell or hold the instrument
  3. It is binding on the issuer's lenders when they price their loans
  4. It guarantees repayment of the debt
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. It is not a recommendation to buy, sell or hold the instrument

A rating is an opinion on credit risk only. It is not investment advice, it does not cover market price risk and it is not a guarantee.

Question 8

A CRA also offers non-rating services such as consulting. Under the regulations, it must:

  1. Rate only companies that also buy its consulting services
  2. Let consultants sit on rating committees
  3. Offer discounted rating fees to all those issuers that also take its consulting services
  4. Keep these activities separate from rating activities to avoid conflicts of interest
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Keep these activities separate from rating activities to avoid conflicts of interest

CRAs must segregate non-rating activities and manage conflicts so that commercial relationships do not influence ratings. Such separation protects the credibility of ratings.
