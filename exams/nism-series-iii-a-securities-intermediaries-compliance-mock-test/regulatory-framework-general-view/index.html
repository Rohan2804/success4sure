Regulatory Framework – General View: NISM Series III-A practice questions

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter maps out who regulates what in Indian finance. It covers SEBI for the securities market, RBI for banks, NBFCs, money, foreign exchange and government securities markets, IRDAI for insurance, PFRDA for pensions, IFSCA for GIFT IFSC, and the Ministry of Finance and Ministry of Corporate Affairs. It also explains co-ordination bodies such as the Financial Stability and Development Council and the key laws behind each regulator. While revising, learn to match an activity to its regulator and statute, and note areas of shared jurisdiction, such as listed company matters under the Companies Act.

Regulatory Framework – General View questions

Question 1

A company wants to set up a finance business in GIFT City's International Financial Services Centre. Which regulator will primarily supervise it?

  1. Securities and Exchange Board of India
  2. International Financial Services Centres Authority (IFSCA)
  3. Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority
  4. Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. International Financial Services Centres Authority (IFSCA)

IFSCA is the unified regulator for financial products, services and institutions in an IFSC such as GIFT City. It took over the powers that SEBI, RBI, IRDAI and PFRDA would otherwise exercise there.

Question 2

Which regulator issues directions on foreign exchange transactions under FEMA, 1999?

  1. Competition Commission of India
  2. Reserve Bank of India
  3. SEBI
  4. Ministry of Corporate Affairs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Reserve Bank of India

FEMA is administered mainly by the RBI, which frames regulations and issues directions on foreign exchange transactions, in consultation with the Central Government.

Question 3

The Financial Stability and Development Council (FSDC) is chaired by the:

  1. Prime Minister of India
  2. Chairman of SEBI
  3. Governor of the Reserve Bank of India
  4. Union Finance Minister
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Union Finance Minister

The FSDC is an apex co-ordination body for financial sector regulators. It is chaired by the Union Finance Minister and includes heads of RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, PFRDA and IFSCA.

Question 4

Which regulator supervises the government securities market and the call money market?

  1. Reserve Bank of India
  2. SEBI
  3. Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority
  4. Ministry of Corporate Affairs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Reserve Bank of India

The RBI regulates the money market, the government securities market and the forex market. SEBI regulates trading in listed securities on stock exchanges.

Question 5

For listed companies, provisions of the Companies Act, 2013 relating to issue and transfer of securities and non-payment of dividend are administered by:

  1. SEBI
  2. The Reserve Bank of India
  3. The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India
  4. The Registrar of Companies alone
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. SEBI

Section 24 of the Companies Act, 2013 gives SEBI power to administer provisions on issue and transfer of securities and non-payment of dividend for listed companies and those intending to list.

Question 6

An NBFC that is also registered as a stock broker would be supervised by:

  1. RBI alone, since it is primarily a finance company
  2. The Ministry of Corporate Affairs, since it supervises all companies registered in India
  3. SEBI alone, since securities activities override other licences
  4. RBI for its NBFC activities and SEBI for its stock broking activities
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. RBI for its NBFC activities and SEBI for its stock broking activities

India follows activity-based regulation, so an entity with multiple activities answers to each relevant regulator for that activity. Each regulator inspects the activity under its law.

Question 7

Insurance companies and insurance intermediaries in India are regulated by:

  1. IFSCA
  2. PFRDA
  3. IRDAI
  4. SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. IRDAI

The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India, set up under the IRDA Act, 1999, regulates insurers, insurance brokers, agents and other insurance intermediaries.

Question 8

Which body under the Ministry of Finance is responsible for capital market policy matters and legislation such as the SEBI Act and the SCRA?

  1. Department of Economic Affairs
  2. Department of Revenue
  3. Department of Expenditure
  4. Department of Financial Services
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Department of Economic Affairs

The Department of Economic Affairs, through its capital markets division, handles policy and legislation relating to the securities market, while SEBI is the market regulator.
