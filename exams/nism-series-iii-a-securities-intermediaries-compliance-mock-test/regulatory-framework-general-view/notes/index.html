Regulatory Framework – General View: NISM Series III-A revision notes

This chapter maps the legal and regulatory architecture of Indian securities markets: which regulator controls which activity, which Act gives that power, and how disputes travel from an order to an appeal. Examiners like matching questions (law to regulator, activity to authority) and the appeal route from SEBI to the Securities Appellate Tribunal and then the Supreme Court.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. The main regulators

SEBI regulates securities markets and intermediaries; RBI regulates banks, NBFCs, money, forex and government securities markets; IRDAI regulates insurance; PFRDA regulates the National Pension System; the Ministry of Corporate Affairs administers the Companies Act; the Department of Economic Affairs in the Finance Ministry frames policy and administers SCRA rules.

2. Core securities laws

SEBI Act, 1992 (creates SEBI and its powers); Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956 (stock exchanges, contracts, listing); Depositories Act, 1996 (demat holding); Companies Act, 2013 (issue and transfer of securities, prospectus, disclosures for unlisted companies). FEMA, 1999 and PMLA, 2002 also bind market intermediaries.

3. Shared jurisdiction

Some areas overlap. Issue and transfer of securities of listed companies or companies proposing to list is administered by SEBI, while unlisted companies stay with MCA. Government securities trading is regulated by RBI even when it happens on stock exchanges. Interest rate and currency derivatives on exchanges are jointly governed by RBI and SEBI.

4. Delegated legislation

Acts give the framework; regulations, circulars and master circulars fill in the detail. SEBI frames regulations under Section 30 of the SEBI Act with Parliament's approval process, and issues circulars under Section 11. Exchanges and depositories add bye-laws and business rules that members must follow. All layers are binding on intermediaries.

5. Self-regulatory role of MIIs

Stock exchanges, clearing corporations and depositories act as first-level regulators of their members: they inspect brokers and DPs, impose penalties, and suspend or expel members. SEBI oversees the MIIs themselves. This is why compliance officers report to both the exchange or depository and to SEBI.

6. Appeals and the SAT

A person aggrieved by an order of SEBI, an adjudicating officer, or certain RBI, IRDAI and PFRDA orders can appeal to the Securities Appellate Tribunal within 45 days of receiving the order. A SAT decision can be challenged before the Supreme Court within 60 days on a question of law. Civil courts cannot entertain matters within SAT's jurisdiction.

7. Financial Stability and Development Council

The FSDC, chaired by the Union Finance Minister, brings together the heads of RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, PFRDA and others to monitor systemic risk and coordinate inter-regulatory issues. It is not a super-regulator and does not replace any individual regulator's powers; exams test this distinction.

8. Why a framework matters to an intermediary

A broker who is also a mutual fund distributor and an insurance agent answers to SEBI, the exchange, AMFI rules and IRDAI simultaneously. Knowing which rulebook applies to which product avoids mis-selling, wrong disclosures and duplicate violations. The compliance function exists to keep this map current.

Common traps in the exam

  • SAT hears appeals against SEBI; the Supreme Court hears appeals against SAT. An appeal does not go from SEBI directly to the High Court as a matter of routine.
  • Government securities are RBI territory even when traded on NSE or BSE platforms.
  • FSDC coordinates regulators; it does not issue registrations or penalise intermediaries.
  • Unlisted company securities are mostly MCA's domain, not SEBI's, unless the company proposes to list.

Quick recap

SEBI for securities, RBI for banks, money and forex, IRDAI for insurance, PFRDA for pensions, MCA for company law. Four core laws: SEBI Act, SCRA, Depositories Act, Companies Act. Exchanges and depositories are frontline regulators of their members. Appeal against SEBI goes to SAT within 45 days and then to the Supreme Court within 60 days.
