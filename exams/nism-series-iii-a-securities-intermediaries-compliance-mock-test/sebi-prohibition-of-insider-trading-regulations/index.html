SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations: NISM Series III-A practice questions

What this chapter covers

The SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015 prohibit trading by insiders when in possession of unpublished price sensitive information (UPSI). The chapter covers definitions of insider, connected person and UPSI, the ban on communicating UPSI except for legitimate purposes, trading windows, pre-clearance and contra trade restrictions, trading plans, initial and continual disclosures, the code of fair disclosure, the code of conduct for listed companies and intermediaries, and the structured digital database. Focus on who is an insider, what counts as UPSI, timelines for disclosures and the responsibilities of the compliance officer.

SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations questions

Question 1

Which of the following would generally be treated as unpublished price sensitive information (UPSI)?

  1. Draft quarterly results of a listed company before they are announced
  2. A press release already posted on the company's website
  3. An audited annual report already filed with the exchange
  4. The company's closing share price as published on the stock exchange website
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Draft quarterly results of a listed company before they are announced

UPSI is information not generally available that is likely to materially affect the price, such as financial results, dividends or mergers before public disclosure.

Question 2

A person who was a statutory auditor of a company four months ago, and had access to its UPSI, trades in its shares. Under the regulations he is:

  1. A connected person, since the association was within six months before the act
  2. Not an insider, because his engagement has ended
  3. Exempt, as auditors are outside the scope of these rules
  4. An insider only if he currently holds over 10% of the shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A connected person, since the association was within six months before the act

A connected person includes anyone associated with the company in a way giving access to UPSI during the six months prior to the act concerned.

Question 3

The trading window for designated persons of a listed company is ordinarily closed from the end of every quarter until:

  1. Seven days before the board meeting that approves the results
  2. The first day of the next quarter
  3. The annual general meeting of the company
  4. 48 hours after the declaration of financial results
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 48 hours after the declaration of financial results

Trading restriction for designated persons generally runs from the end of the quarter until 48 hours after the financial results are made public.

Question 4

A company shares UPSI with its bankers for evaluating a loan. Under the regulations, such sharing is:

  1. Allowed only if the bankers first obtain SEBI's specific written approval for each instance of sharing
  2. Allowed as a 'legitimate purpose', provided the recipient is given notice to keep it confidential
  3. Always prohibited, regardless of purpose
  4. Allowed only after the information is published
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Allowed as a 'legitimate purpose', provided the recipient is given notice to keep it confidential

UPSI may be shared for legitimate purposes, such as with lenders, auditors or advisers, in the ordinary course of business, with confidentiality obligations and database entries.

Question 5

A listed company must maintain a structured digital database (SDD) to record:

  1. The voting results of all general meetings
  2. The salary details of all employees
  3. The dividend paid to every shareholder in the last ten years
  4. The names and PAN of persons with whom UPSI is shared, with time stamps
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The names and PAN of persons with whom UPSI is shared, with time stamps

The SDD records the nature of UPSI and details of persons sharing and receiving it, with time stamps and audit trails, so that leaks can be traced.

Question 6

A promoter wants to trade in his company's shares despite regularly holding UPSI. The regulations allow this through:

  1. A trading plan approved by the compliance officer and disclosed to the stock exchanges in advance
  2. Trading through a relative's account
  3. Trading only on days when the market is closed
  4. Verbal permission from the managing director, recorded later in the minutes of the next board meeting
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A trading plan approved by the compliance officer and disclosed to the stock exchanges in advance

A trading plan is a pre-scheduled, publicly disclosed plan that lets persons who perpetually hold UPSI trade without breaching the prohibition, subject to conditions.

Question 7

Under the Code of Fair Disclosure, a listed company should designate a senior officer to deal with dissemination of information. This officer is called the:

  1. Chief Risk Officer
  2. Principal Officer
  3. Designated Director
  4. Chief Investor Relations Officer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Chief Investor Relations Officer

The code requires a senior officer, the Chief Investor Relations Officer, to oversee uniform and prompt disclosure of UPSI to avoid selective disclosure.

Question 8

Once a designated person makes a continual disclosure of trades to the company, the company must inform the stock exchanges within:

  1. The next annual general meeting
  2. Two trading days of receiving the disclosure
  3. Thirty days of receiving the disclosure
  4. Seven calendar days after the end of the quarter
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Two trading days of receiving the disclosure

The company must notify the stock exchanges of the details within two trading days of receiving the continual disclosure or becoming aware of it.
