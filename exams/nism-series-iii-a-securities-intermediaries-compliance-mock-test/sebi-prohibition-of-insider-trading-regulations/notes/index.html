SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations: NISM Series III-A revision notes

The SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015 stop people from trading on unpublished price sensitive information. They apply to listed companies, intermediaries and fiduciaries, and to anyone holding UPSI. Expect questions on who is an insider, what counts as UPSI, the trading window, trading plans, contra trades, disclosure triggers and the informant reward mechanism.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. UPSI

Unpublished price sensitive information is information about a company or its securities, not generally available, which on becoming available is likely to materially affect the price. Typical examples: financial results, dividends, changes in capital structure, mergers and acquisitions, delistings, and changes in key managerial personnel. Information on the exchange website is 'generally available'.

2. Who is an insider

An insider is a connected person or anyone in possession of or having access to UPSI. A connected person is someone associated with the company in the six months before the act, in a way that gives access to UPSI: directors, employees, auditors, bankers, consultants and their relatives. A stranger who overhears a director in a lift and trades can also be an insider.

3. Communication and trading prohibited

Insiders must not communicate UPSI except for legitimate purposes, performance of duties or legal obligations, and no one may procure UPSI. Trading while in possession of UPSI is prohibited. Defences exist, such as off-market inter-se transfers between promoters who both had the same UPSI, block deals and trades under an approved trading plan.

4. Trading window

Listed companies close a trading window for designated persons and their relatives during which they cannot trade. For financial results it usually runs from the end of each quarter until 48 hours after the results are made public. The compliance officer can also close it when other UPSI exists.

5. Trading plans

An insider who perpetually holds UPSI may submit a trading plan to the compliance officer for approval, which is then disclosed to the exchanges. The plan must start only after a cool-off period, cannot cover the period around results, and once approved must be executed irrevocably. Cool-off and plan-length rules were revised in 2024; check the latest SEBI text.

6. Pre-clearance and contra trade

Designated persons need pre-clearance from the compliance officer above thresholds set in the company's code, and must execute within seven trading days of approval. They must not enter an opposite (contra) trade within six months of the earlier trade; profits from a contra trade are disgorged to SEBI's Investor Protection and Education Fund.

7. Disclosures

Promoters, KMP and directors disclose holdings on appointment within 7 days. Continual disclosure applies when the value traded in a calendar quarter exceeds ₹10 lakh (single or a series of transactions): the person informs the company within 2 trading days, and the company informs the exchanges within 2 trading days of receipt.

8. Codes, database and informants

Every listed company and intermediary must have a code of conduct to regulate trading and a code of fair disclosure. A structured digital database records who shared UPSI with whom. Informants can report violations to SEBI's Office of Informant Protection and may receive a reward, subject to a cap set by SEBI.

Common traps in the exam

  • A connected person includes anyone connected in the six months before the act, not only current employees.
  • The ₹10 lakh continual disclosure threshold is per calendar quarter, not per financial year.
  • The trading window reopens 48 hours after results are made public, not on the day of the board meeting.
  • Contra trade profits go to the IPEF administered by SEBI, not to the company.
  • Once approved, a trading plan cannot be withdrawn or deviated from.

Quick recap

UPSI is non-public, price-moving information. Insiders include connected persons (six-month look-back) and anyone holding UPSI. No trading or communication while in possession of UPSI. Trading window closes from quarter end till 48 hours after results. Trading plans are irrevocable after approval. Contra trade bar: six months. Continual disclosure above ₹10 lakh per quarter within 2 trading days.
