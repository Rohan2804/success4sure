SCRA, 1956 and SCRR, 1957: NISM Series III-A revision notes

The Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956 and the rules made under it in 1957 control stock exchanges, the contracts traded on them and the listing of securities. The Act is older than SEBI and is administered jointly with the Central Government. Expect questions on the definition of securities, recognition of exchanges, listing and delisting, and the minimum public shareholding rule in Rule 19A.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Definition of securities

Section 2(h) defines securities widely: shares, scrips, stocks, bonds, debentures and other marketable securities of a company or body corporate; derivatives; units of collective investment schemes and mutual funds; government securities; security receipts; and rights or interests in securities. Because derivatives are included, exchange-traded F&O falls within SCRA.

2. Recognised stock exchanges

An exchange must be recognised under Section 4 to operate. Recognition is granted by the Central Government (powers delegated to SEBI) after checking rules, bye-laws and investor protection. Contracts in securities outside a recognised exchange in a notified area are restricted, which channels trading into regulated platforms.

3. Corporatisation and demutualisation

Exchanges were once broker-owned mutual bodies. Section 4B required corporatisation and demutualisation so that ownership, management and trading rights are separated. Brokers can no longer control the board of the exchange whose rules they must obey. SEBI's SECC Regulations now govern exchange and clearing corporation ownership.

4. Clearing corporations

SCRA recognises clearing corporations, which clear and settle trades executed on exchanges. Separation of trading (exchange) and settlement (clearing corporation) strengthens risk management. Clearing corporations act as central counterparty to every trade.

5. Listing under Section 21

Where securities are listed, the company must comply with the listing conditions of the exchange, now largely contained in the SEBI LODR Regulations. Rule 19 of SCRR sets the requirements to be met for listing, including the minimum offer to the public at the time of an IPO, which varies with post-issue market capitalisation.

6. Delisting under Section 21A

A recognised exchange may delist securities on grounds specified in the rules, for example repeated non-compliance or failure to pay listing fees, after giving the company a reasonable opportunity of being heard. The company can appeal to SAT. Voluntary delisting by promoters follows the SEBI Delisting Regulations with a reverse book-building or other prescribed price discovery.

7. Minimum public shareholding

Rule 19A requires every listed company, other than certain public sector and specified companies, to maintain public shareholding of at least 25%. A company that falls below must restore it within the period allowed, using methods such as offer for sale, QIP or rights issue. Example: if promoters hold 82%, at least 7% must be diluted.

8. Penalties and powers

SCRA lets SEBI call for periodic returns, direct exchanges to amend rules, supersede an exchange's governing board in extreme cases, and impose penalties for failures like not furnishing information or violating listing conditions. Appeals lie to SAT, like SEBI Act matters.

Common traps in the exam

  • Rule 19A is minimum public shareholding (25%); Rule 19(2)(b) is about the minimum offer size at IPO. Do not mix them.
  • Derivatives are 'securities' under SCRA Section 2(h), so derivative contracts are regulated under it.
  • Demutualisation separates ownership and trading rights; it does not mean exchanges stop having broker members.
  • Compulsory delisting by an exchange is different from voluntary delisting by promoters; both are covered but under different procedures.

Quick recap

SCRA 1956 governs securities contracts, exchanges, clearing corporations and listing; SCRR 1957 adds the detail. Section 2(h) defines securities including derivatives and MF units. Section 4 recognition, 4B demutualisation, 21 listing, 21A delisting with SAT appeal. Rule 19A: listed companies must keep at least 25% public shareholding and restore shortfalls within the permitted time.
