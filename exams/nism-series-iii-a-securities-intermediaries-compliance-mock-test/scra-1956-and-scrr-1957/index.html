SCRA, 1956 and SCRR, 1957: NISM Series III-A practice questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter deals with the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956 and the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Rules, 1957, which govern recognition of stock exchanges and clearing corporations, contracts in securities and listing. Learners should know the wide definition of 'securities', recognition and corporatisation and demutualisation of exchanges, the power to regulate contracts and options, conditions for listing, appeals against refusal to list, and penalties. The Rules set norms on minimum public offer and minimum public shareholding. When revising, focus on definitions, the role of recognised exchanges and the separation of ownership from trading rights.

SCRA, 1956 and SCRR, 1957 questions

Question 1

Under the SCRA, 1956, which of the following is included in the definition of 'securities'?

  1. A residential property title deed
  2. A life insurance policy
  3. Units of a mutual fund scheme
  4. A savings bank deposit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Units of a mutual fund scheme

Section 2(h) of the SCRA includes shares, bonds, derivatives, government securities, units of mutual funds and collective investment schemes, and security receipts. Bank deposits and insurance policies are not securities.

Question 2

Corporatisation and demutualisation of a stock exchange means:

  1. The exchange is converted into a government department
  2. The exchange stops trading in equity and moves to derivatives
  3. Ownership and management are separated from the trading rights of members
  4. Only brokers may own shares in the exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Ownership and management are separated from the trading rights of members

Demutualisation separates owners from traders so that brokers' trading interests do not control the exchange, improving governance and reducing conflicts of interest.

Question 3

A stock exchange refuses to list a company's securities. Under the SCRA, the company can appeal against this decision to:

  1. The National Company Law Tribunal
  2. The Registrar of Companies
  3. The Securities Appellate Tribunal
  4. The Reserve Bank of India
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The Securities Appellate Tribunal

Section 22 of the SCRA lets a company appeal to SAT when a recognised stock exchange refuses, omits or fails to list its securities.

Question 4

A stock exchange in India can operate legally only if it:

  1. Is registered as a non-banking finance company
  2. Is approved by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs
  3. Has at least ten listed companies
  4. Is recognised under the SCRA
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Is recognised under the SCRA

Recognition under Section 4 of the SCRA is mandatory for a stock exchange. The Central Government's power to grant recognition is exercised by SEBI.

Question 5

Under the SCRR, 1957, minimum public shareholding norms require a listed company to:

  1. Bring its public shareholding up to the required level within the time allowed if it falls short
  2. Keep all public shareholders' holdings in physical form
  3. Delist automatically from all stock exchanges as soon as its public shareholding falls below 50% of capital
  4. Ensure promoters always hold at least 75% of the shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Bring its public shareholding up to the required level within the time allowed if it falls short

Rule 19A requires listed companies to maintain minimum public shareholding and to restore it within the specified period if it falls below, failing which regulatory action follows.

Question 6

Derivatives are treated as 'securities' under the SCRA. As a result, trading in derivatives is legal when it is:

  1. Approved in writing by the company whose shares are the underlying of the derivative
  2. Settled only in cash through a bank
  3. Traded on a recognised stock exchange under its rules and bye-laws
  4. Agreed between any two persons, with no further conditions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Traded on a recognised stock exchange under its rules and bye-laws

Section 18A makes derivative contracts legal and valid if traded on a recognised stock exchange and settled on its clearing house as per its rules and bye-laws.

Question 7

Which body may, under the SCRA, supersede the governing board of a recognised stock exchange in public interest?

  1. The company with the largest market capitalisation on the exchange
  2. The Registrar of Companies
  3. The stock exchange's largest broker
  4. SEBI, exercising powers delegated by the Central Government
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. SEBI, exercising powers delegated by the Central Government

Section 11 of the SCRA allows supersession of a stock exchange's governing board in the interest of the trade or the public. This power is exercised by SEBI.

Question 8

A clearing corporation in the securities market mainly:

  1. Sets interest rates for margin funding
  2. Issues contract notes to retail clients
  3. Clears and settles trades and acts as the central counterparty
  4. Lists securities and approves offer documents
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Clears and settles trades and acts as the central counterparty

A recognised clearing corporation clears and settles exchange trades, guarantees settlement as central counterparty and manages the settlement guarantee fund.
