SEBI (PFUTP) Regulations, 2003: NISM Series III-A practice questions

What this chapter covers

The SEBI (Prohibition of Fraudulent and Unfair Trade Practices relating to Securities Market) Regulations, 2003 prohibit fraud, manipulation and unfair practices in dealing in securities. This chapter explains the wide definition of 'fraud', the general prohibition in Regulation 3, and the illustrative list of manipulative practices in Regulation 4, such as circular and wash trades, price rigging, spreading false information, front running, mis-selling and misuse of client accounts. It also covers SEBI's powers of investigation and action. When revising, learn to recognise the practices from scenarios and note that intermediaries and their employees are specifically covered.

SEBI (PFUTP) Regulations, 2003 questions

Question 1

Two connected parties repeatedly buy and sell the same shares between themselves with no real change of ownership. This is called:

  1. Wash or circular trading
  2. Arbitrage trading
  3. Hedging
  4. Block deal trading
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Wash or circular trading

Trades with no genuine change in beneficial ownership create a false appearance of activity and are prohibited as manipulative under the PFUTP Regulations.

Question 2

A person posts false news on social media about a large order win by a small company to push up its share price and then sells his holdings. This is:

  1. A fraudulent practice of spreading false information to manipulate prices
  2. Legitimate investment research
  3. Permitted marketing by an investor
  4. A routine block deal
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A fraudulent practice of spreading false information to manipulate prices

Disseminating misleading information to influence prices and then profiting, often called pump and dump, is expressly prohibited under PFUTP. SEBI can also order disgorgement of the profits made.

Question 3

A distributor sells a high-risk product to an elderly client by describing it as 'capital protected'. Under PFUTP this is:

  1. Only a breach of the distributor's internal policy
  2. Permitted if the product is listed on an exchange
  3. Mis-selling, which is a fraudulent and unfair trade practice
  4. Acceptable as long as the client has signed the application form and risk disclosure
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Mis-selling, which is a fraudulent and unfair trade practice

Mis-selling, including selling unsuitable products or making false or misleading statements, is specifically listed as an unfair trade practice. A signed form does not cure a misleading sales pitch.

Question 4

A trader places large buy orders far from the market price and cancels them repeatedly to create a false impression of demand. This practice is known as:

  1. Spoofing or layering
  2. Portfolio rebalancing
  3. Short covering
  4. Market making
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Spoofing or layering

Placing orders without intent to execute, to mislead other participants about demand or supply, is a manipulative practice under PFUTP.

Question 5

Under the PFUTP Regulations, 'fraud' includes:

  1. Only acts committed by listed companies
  2. Only acts that cause a combined loss of more than ₹1 crore to investors and are proved by a court of law
  3. Only acts that are also offences under the Indian Penal Code
  4. Any act or concealment that induces another person to deal in securities, even without wrongful gain
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Any act or concealment that induces another person to deal in securities, even without wrongful gain

The definition of fraud is deliberately wide and covers acts, omissions and concealment that induce dealing, even without proof of wrongful gain or loss.

Question 6

A broker executes excessive trades in a client's account mainly to earn more brokerage, without regard to the client's interest. This is called:

  1. Algorithmic trading
  2. Proprietary trading
  3. Margin trading facility
  4. Churning, an unfair trade practice
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Churning, an unfair trade practice

Excessive trading in a client account to generate commissions harms the client and is treated as an unfair trade practice by an intermediary.

Question 7

An employee of a mutual fund buys a stock just before the fund places a large order in it, using his knowledge of the order. Under PFUTP this is:

  1. Legitimate personal investment
  2. Front running
  3. Insider trading of UPSI of a listed company
  4. Index rebalancing
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Front running

Trading ahead of a large non-public order with knowledge of it is front running, which is covered under PFUTP regardless of whether the person is an intermediary.

Question 8

A company issues a misleading advertisement about its financial performance to attract investors to its share issue. This falls under PFUTP because:

  1. Only stock brokers and their employees can be held liable under PFUTP, not listed companies
  2. PFUTP applies only to derivative trades
  3. Publishing misleading information to induce dealing in securities is a prohibited practice
  4. Advertisements are never regulated by SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Publishing misleading information to induce dealing in securities is a prohibited practice

Making or publishing false or misleading statements to induce investors to deal in securities is a fraudulent practice under PFUTP.
