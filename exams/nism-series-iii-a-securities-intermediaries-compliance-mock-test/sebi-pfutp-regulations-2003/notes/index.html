SEBI (PFUTP) Regulations, 2003: NISM Series III-A revision notes

The Prohibition of Fraudulent and Unfair Trade Practices relating to Securities Market Regulations, 2003 target manipulation, fraud and deception in the securities market. They are broad and principles-based, so questions describe a behaviour and ask whether it is a violation and of what kind. Learn the definition of fraud, the deemed manipulative practices listed in Regulation 4 and how intermediaries are covered.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Definition of fraud

Fraud is defined very widely: any act, expression, omission or concealment, whether in a deceitful manner or not, by a person or by anyone else with his connivance, to induce another person to deal in securities. It need not involve proof that anyone actually lost money or that the person intended to deceive in the criminal sense.

2. General prohibition

No person may buy, sell or deal in securities in a fraudulent manner, use any manipulative or deceptive device, employ any scheme to defraud, or engage in any act that operates as fraud or deceit on any person in connection with dealing in securities. The rule applies to everyone, not only registered intermediaries.

3. Market manipulation examples

Regulation 4 deems certain acts manipulative: creating a false appearance of trading, dealing in a security without intention of change in ownership (wash trades), circular trading among a group, inflating or depressing prices through artificial volume, and advancing or delaying the settlement price. Example: three related accounts trading a thinly traded scrip back and forth to raise its price.

4. Misleading information

Publishing or spreading false information that is likely to influence investors is prohibited, including through social media, tips groups and paid stock recommendations. Planting bullish news before selling (pump and dump) is a classic violation. Companies that publish misleading financial statements are also covered.

5. Front running

An intermediary or its employee who trades ahead of a large client order, using knowledge of that order, commits a fraudulent practice. Example: a dealer at a broking firm buys shares minutes before executing a mutual fund's large buy order and sells after the price rises. Front running by any person with access to such information is covered.

6. Mis-selling

Selling securities or services by making false or misleading statements, concealing material facts or risks, or ignoring suitability is mis-selling. Selling a complex structured product to a retiree who asked for safe income, without explaining risks, is an example. Mutual fund unit mis-selling is explicitly covered.

7. Intermediary-specific practices

Intermediaries must not churn client accounts to earn brokerage, issue false contract notes, misuse client funds or securities, or deliberately misreport prices. Knowingly assisting a manipulation, for example lending accounts to an operator, can make the intermediary liable.

8. Consequences

SEBI may suspend trading, restrain persons from the market, impound gains, order disgorgement with interest, impose penalties under Section 15HA of the SEBI Act and launch prosecution. Investigations commonly use trade logs, phone records and connections between accounts to establish the pattern.

Common traps in the exam

  • Fraud under PFUTP does not require proof of an actual loss to investors.
  • Wash trades are about no real change in ownership; circular trades are rotation within a group. Both are manipulative.
  • PFUTP applies to any person, not only SEBI-registered intermediaries.
  • Front running uses knowledge of a pending order; insider trading uses UPSI about the company. Do not confuse the two.

Quick recap

PFUTP 2003 bans fraud, manipulation and unfair trade practices in securities by anyone. Fraud is defined broadly and needs no proof of loss. Deemed manipulative acts include wash and circular trades, false volume, price rigging and spreading false information. Front running, churning and mis-selling are unfair practices. Penalties include restraint, disgorgement and Section 15HA penalties.
