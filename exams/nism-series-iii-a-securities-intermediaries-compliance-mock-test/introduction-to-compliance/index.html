Introduction to Compliance: NISM Series III-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Introduction to Compliance, from the NISM Series III-A (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Non-Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains what compliance means for a SEBI-registered intermediary and why it matters. It covers compliance risk, the role of the board and senior management, the position and duties of the compliance officer, compliance policies and manuals, monitoring and testing, regulatory reporting and record keeping. It also contrasts compliance with internal audit and risk management, and discusses building a compliance culture. Focus on the independence and responsibilities of the compliance officer, the consequences of non-compliance such as penalties and reputational loss, and practical tools like checklists, maker-checker controls and escalation of breaches.

Introduction to Compliance questions

Question 1

Compliance risk for an intermediary is best described as the risk of:

  1. Legal or regulatory sanctions, financial loss or reputational damage from failing to follow laws, rules and codes
  2. A breakdown of computer systems at the stock exchange
  3. Losing money because market prices move against the firm's own positions
  4. A counterparty failing to pay what it owes on settlement day
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Legal or regulatory sanctions, financial loss or reputational damage from failing to follow laws, rules and codes

Compliance risk arises from not complying with laws, regulations, codes of conduct and internal standards. Market, credit and operational risk are separate categories.

Question 2

Who carries ultimate responsibility for an intermediary's compliance with regulations?

  1. The statutory auditor
  2. The compliance officer alone
  3. The stock exchange or depository where it is a member
  4. The board of directors and senior management
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The board of directors and senior management

The compliance officer advises, monitors and reports, but the board and senior management are ultimately accountable for ensuring the firm complies.

Question 3

A compliance officer finds a serious breach of client fund rules and senior management asks him to keep it quiet. What should he do?

  1. Wait for the next inspection to see if it is noticed
  2. Escalate it to the board and report to the regulator as required
  3. Resolve it informally with the affected clients and close the matter without recording it
  4. Keep it confidential to protect the firm's reputation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Escalate it to the board and report to the regulator as required

Compliance officers must report non-compliance to the board and to the regulator where rules require it. Hiding a breach exposes the firm and the officer to action.

Question 4

How does the compliance function differ from internal audit?

  1. Compliance only deals with tax laws, while internal audit deals with SEBI rules
  2. Compliance monitors regulatory adherence on an ongoing basis, while internal audit gives periodic independent assurance on controls
  3. There is no difference; the two terms mean exactly the same thing
  4. Compliance work is always outsourced to outside consulting firms, while internal audit can be performed only by the board of directors itself
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Compliance monitors regulatory adherence on an ongoing basis, while internal audit gives periodic independent assurance on controls

Compliance is an ongoing second-line function, while internal audit provides independent periodic assurance on controls, including on the compliance function itself.

Question 5

A 'maker-checker' control in an intermediary's back office means:

  1. The same person enters and approves a transaction to save time
  2. Transactions are checked only after the year-end audit
  3. Clients check their own transactions and confirm them online
  4. One person enters a transaction and a different person verifies and approves it
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. One person enters a transaction and a different person verifies and approves it

Maker-checker separates entry and approval between two people, reducing errors and fraud. It is a basic preventive control. It is widely used for payments and client fund transfers.

Question 6

Which document typically sets out an intermediary's compliance responsibilities, procedures and escalation paths?

  1. The annual income-tax return
  2. A board-approved compliance policy or manual
  3. The memorandum and articles of association of the firm
  4. The client registration form
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A board-approved compliance policy or manual

A compliance policy or manual approved by the board lays out roles, procedures, monitoring and reporting, guiding staff on how to meet regulatory requirements.

Question 7

An intermediary keeps a regulatory calendar listing every report and filing due date. This mainly helps to:

  1. Increase the firm's brokerage income
  2. Reduce the minimum net worth the firm must maintain
  3. Replace the need for a compliance officer
  4. Avoid missed or delayed regulatory submissions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Avoid missed or delayed regulatory submissions

A compliance calendar tracks due dates for reports, returns and renewals so that filings are made on time, a common area of lapses and penalties.

Question 8

A firm with an effective compliance culture is most likely to show which feature?

  1. Compliance is discussed only when an inspection is announced
  2. Business targets override compliance checks during busy periods
  3. Breaches are fixed quietly without any record being kept
  4. Staff report concerns freely and senior management acts on them
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Staff report concerns freely and senior management acts on them

A healthy compliance culture is driven from the top, encourages escalation of issues without fear and treats compliance as part of everyday business.
