SEBI (Intermediaries) Regulations, 2008: NISM Series III-A revision notes

The Intermediaries Regulations, 2008 give a common procedure for registering, supervising and acting against all SEBI intermediaries, so that individual regulations only add activity-specific rules. Examiners focus on the fit and proper person test, the validity of registration, general obligations and the range of actions SEBI can take after an enquiry.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Why a common regulation

Before 2008, each intermediary regulation had its own registration and enforcement procedure. The 2008 regulations standardised these, covering application, grant of certificate, conditions, inspection and enforcement for brokers, DPs, merchant bankers, RTAs, investment advisers and others. Activity-specific regulations then add capital, qualification and conduct requirements.

2. Application and grant

An applicant files the prescribed form with fees and documents through the route specified (for brokers and DPs, through the exchange or depository). SEBI checks eligibility and fit and proper status, may seek clarifications, and grants a certificate. Rejection must be communicated with reasons, giving the applicant a chance to be heard.

3. Validity of registration

Registration granted under these regulations is generally permanent, subject to payment of fees and continued compliance, unless suspended, cancelled or surrendered. Earlier, many certificates had to be renewed every few years. Any material change in control requires SEBI's prior approval.

4. Fit and proper person criteria

Schedule II assesses integrity, reputation, character, absence of convictions and restraint orders, and financial soundness of the applicant and its principal officers, directors, promoters and key managerial persons. A person against whom a criminal complaint involving economic offences has been filed by SEBI, or who is declared a wilful defaulter, is generally not fit and proper.

5. Continuous obligations

Intermediaries must abide by the code of conduct, keep books and records, submit information SEBI asks for, inform SEBI of material changes, and redress investor grievances promptly through SCORES and other channels. Fit and proper status must continue throughout the life of the registration.

6. Code of conduct essentials

Act with integrity, due skill, care and diligence; avoid conflicts of interest or disclose and manage them; do not make exaggerated statements about services; keep client information confidential; maintain adequate infrastructure and trained staff. These generic principles appear in Schedule III and in each specific regulation.

7. Enquiry and actions

After an inspection or complaint, a designated authority may conduct an enquiry and recommend action. SEBI can then pass orders such as warning, suspension of registration for a period, cancellation of registration, prohibition from taking new clients, or debarment of principal officers. The intermediary must be given a show-cause notice and a hearing.

8. Effect of suspension or cancellation

On suspension or cancellation the intermediary must stop the activity, allow clients to withdraw or transfer funds and securities, and hand over records as directed. Client protection continues even after the business stops. Cancellation does not wipe out liability for past violations.

Common traps in the exam

  • Registration is now largely permanent, but fit and proper status must be maintained continuously, not just at the time of application.
  • A warning is the mildest action; cancellation is the most severe. Options sometimes rank them in the wrong order.
  • A change in control needs SEBI's prior approval, not merely intimation afterwards.
  • Fit and proper criteria apply to promoters, directors and key managerial persons, not just the entity.

Quick recap

Intermediaries Regulations 2008 give one procedure for registration, inspection and enforcement across SEBI intermediaries. Registration is generally permanent but fit and proper status (Schedule II) must continue. Duties include records, information to SEBI, grievance redressal and code of conduct. Enforcement range: warning, suspension, cancellation, prohibition, debarment, always after notice and hearing. Change in control needs prior approval.
