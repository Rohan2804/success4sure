SEBI (Intermediaries) Regulations, 2008: NISM Series III-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SEBI (Intermediaries) Regulations, 2008, from the NISM Series III-A (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Non-Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

These regulations set common rules for all SEBI-registered intermediaries, alongside the activity-specific regulations. The chapter covers the application and registration process, the fit and proper person criteria, general obligations such as appointing a compliance officer and redressing investor grievances, the general code of conduct, prior approval for change in control, and the procedure for action against an intermediary, including the role of the designated authority, show cause notices and orders. Revise the types of action SEBI can take, the obligations that continue after registration and the circumstances that trigger prior approval or fresh registration.

SEBI (Intermediaries) Regulations, 2008 questions

Question 1

An intermediary proposes a change in control. Under the Intermediaries Regulations, it must:

  1. Surrender its existing registration and apply afresh as a new entity in every case
  2. Simply update its website, as no approval is needed
  3. Obtain SEBI's prior approval before the change in control takes effect
  4. Inform SEBI within one year after the change is completed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Obtain SEBI's prior approval before the change in control takes effect

A change in control of a registered intermediary requires SEBI's prior approval, so that the incoming controllers can be assessed for the fit and proper criteria.

Question 2

Which of the following would most likely make an applicant NOT 'fit and proper' for registration?

  1. Its promoters have earlier run another registered intermediary without any regulatory action
  2. A pending charge sheet for an economic offence against its director
  3. Its directors hold postgraduate degrees
  4. It has filed audited accounts every year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A pending charge sheet for an economic offence against its director

Criminal complaints or charge sheets for economic offences, debarment orders and similar matters are disqualifications under the fit and proper criteria.

Question 3

Under the Intermediaries Regulations, every intermediary must appoint a compliance officer who is responsible for:

  1. Fixing the brokerage rates charged to all clients
  2. Monitoring compliance with the Act, rules and regulations and redressing investor grievances
  3. Preparing the firm's income-tax returns
  4. Representing the firm before courts in civil suits
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Monitoring compliance with the Act, rules and regulations and redressing investor grievances

The compliance officer monitors compliance with securities laws, reports non-compliance to SEBI where observed and handles investor grievances. The officer is also the point of contact for SEBI.

Question 4

When SEBI proposes action against an intermediary, the intermediary receives a show cause notice. Its main purpose is to:

  1. Ask the intermediary to pay the penalty immediately
  2. Inform the public and the stock exchanges that the intermediary has already been found guilty
  3. Give the intermediary an opportunity to respond before an order is passed
  4. Transfer the intermediary's clients to another firm
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Give the intermediary an opportunity to respond before an order is passed

A show cause notice follows principles of natural justice, letting the intermediary reply to the allegations before the designated authority's recommendation and final order.

Question 5

Which of the following is NOT one of the actions SEBI may take against an intermediary under these regulations?

  1. Cancellation of the certificate of registration
  2. Imprisonment of the intermediary's directors by SEBI
  3. Suspension of the certificate of registration
  4. Issue of a warning or prohibition from taking new clients
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Imprisonment of the intermediary's directors by SEBI

SEBI can suspend or cancel registration, prohibit taking new clients, issue warnings and other directions. Imprisonment can be ordered only by a court in criminal proceedings.

Question 6

An investor complains against an intermediary on SEBI's SCORES platform. The intermediary is expected to:

  1. Wait until SEBI imposes a penalty before responding
  2. Resolve the complaint and upload an action taken report within the prescribed time
  3. Ignore it, since complaints on SCORES can be lodged only against listed companies and their RTAs
  4. Forward it to the stock exchange and take no further action
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Resolve the complaint and upload an action taken report within the prescribed time

Registered intermediaries must redress complaints received through SCORES within the timelines set by SEBI and upload action taken reports, with escalation if unresolved.

Question 7

After SEBI cancels an intermediary's certificate of registration, the intermediary must generally:

  1. Change its name and resume business without any new approval
  2. Stop the registered activity and protect the interests of existing clients as directed
  3. Continue taking new clients until the appeal is decided
  4. Transfer client funds to its own accounts for safekeeping
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Stop the registered activity and protect the interests of existing clients as directed

On cancellation, the intermediary must cease the activity and allow clients to move, for example transferring records and assets, in line with SEBI's directions.

Question 8

An intermediary must ensure its key managerial personnel and employees meet the requirements of SEBI. This includes ensuring that:

  1. Persons dealing with clients hold the NISM certifications prescribed for their roles
  2. All employees are relatives of the promoters
  3. Employees trade only through personal accounts held with competing intermediaries, never with the firm
  4. Only employees with ten years' experience deal with clients
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Persons dealing with clients hold the NISM certifications prescribed for their roles

SEBI requires associated persons in specified functions, such as compliance, operations and client dealing, to obtain the relevant NISM certification.
