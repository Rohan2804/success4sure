SEBI (RTA) Regulations, 1993: NISM Series III-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SEBI (RTA) Regulations, 1993, from the NISM Series III-A (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Non-Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the SEBI (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents) Regulations, 1993. It explains the two categories of RTAs, registration and capital requirements, and the functions of registrars to an issue, such as processing applications and finalising allotment, and of share transfer agents, such as maintaining registers of members and handling investor service requests. It also covers the code of conduct, record keeping, inspection, grievance handling and the shift to dematerialised transfers. Focus on the difference between the two roles, the RTA's duties in issue processes and its obligations in investor servicing.

SEBI (RTA) Regulations, 1993 questions

Question 1

Which of the following is a function of a registrar to an issue?

  1. Finalising the basis of allotment along with the lead manager and stock exchange
  2. Acting as the custodian of FPI investments
  3. Rating the debt instruments issued by the company and publishing the rating rationale
  4. Underwriting the unsubscribed portion of the issue
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Finalising the basis of allotment along with the lead manager and stock exchange

The registrar processes applications, finalises the basis of allotment with the lead manager and exchange, and handles allotment and refund instructions.

Question 2

An RTA registered under Category I can:

  1. Act both as a registrar to an issue and as a share transfer agent
  2. Act only as a registrar to an issue
  3. Act as a stock broker and depository
  4. Act only as a share transfer agent
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Act both as a registrar to an issue and as a share transfer agent

Category I RTAs may carry on both activities, while Category II RTAs may act as either a registrar to an issue or a share transfer agent.

Question 3

An investor holding physical shares of a listed company wants to transfer them to a buyer. Under current rules:

  1. The company must issue new physical certificates to the buyer
  2. The RTA will register the physical transfer in the same way as before
  3. The buyer must apply to SEBI for permission
  4. Transfers of listed securities can be effected only in demat form
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Transfers of listed securities can be effected only in demat form

Since April 2019, transfers of listed securities other than transmission or transposition are processed only in demat form, so physical holders must dematerialise first.

Question 4

When an RTA processes a request for issue of duplicate securities, it now issues a:

  1. Letter of confirmation, which the investor uses to get the securities dematerialised
  2. Fresh offer document to the investor
  3. New physical share certificate in all cases
  4. Cash refund equal to the market value of the lost securities on the date of the request
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Letter of confirmation, which the investor uses to get the securities dematerialised

For service requests like duplicates or transmission, RTAs issue a letter of confirmation, and the investor must submit it for dematerialisation within the validity period.

Question 5

Which of the following best describes the RTA's role in investor grievance redressal?

  1. Fixing the dividend rate for the company
  2. Resolving complaints on transfers, transmission, dividends and records within set timelines
  3. Deciding appeals against SEBI orders
  4. Approving changes in the company's articles
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Resolving complaints on transfers, transmission, dividends and records within set timelines

RTAs handle investor service requests and complaints on behalf of issuers and must address them within set timelines, including those received via SCORES.

Question 6

An RTA must maintain proper books and records for its clients and keep them:

  1. Accessible to any member of the public on request
  2. Only in the issuer's registered office and never at the RTA's own premises
  3. Destroyed once the issue is complete
  4. Available for inspection by SEBI and preserved for the specified period
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Available for inspection by SEBI and preserved for the specified period

RTAs must maintain records such as applications, allotment, transfers and grievances, preserve them for prescribed periods and produce them during SEBI inspections.

Question 7

In a public issue, the registrar to the issue is appointed by:

  1. The issuer company, usually in consultation with the lead manager
  2. The stock exchange where the shares are proposed to be listed after the issue
  3. SEBI, through a lottery among registered RTAs
  4. The depository that will hold the shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The issuer company, usually in consultation with the lead manager

The issuer appoints the registrar to its issue, normally with input from the lead manager, and enters into an agreement setting out the registrar's duties.

Question 8

Investor service requests such as change of address for physical holders are submitted to the RTA using:

  1. Standardised forms prescribed by SEBI for investor service requests
  2. A request to the depository participant only
  3. An application to the Registrar of Companies
  4. A plain letter to the stock exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Standardised forms prescribed by SEBI for investor service requests

SEBI has standardised ISR forms for physical holders to update PAN, KYC, nomination and other details with RTAs, simplifying the process.
