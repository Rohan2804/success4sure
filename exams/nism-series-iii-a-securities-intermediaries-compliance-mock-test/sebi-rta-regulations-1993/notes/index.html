SEBI (RTA) Regulations, 1993: NISM Series III-A revision notes

Registrars to an issue and share transfer agents keep the records that connect investors with the companies they own. This chapter covers who they are, how they are registered, what they do during an issue and afterwards, and the conduct and record-keeping obligations SEBI imposes. Questions often test the categories, the activities that belong to each, and grievance timelines.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Two activities, one regulation

A registrar to an issue collects applications, keeps records of applicants and money received, finalises the basis of allotment with the exchange, and arranges credit of securities or refunds. A share transfer agent maintains the register of members after listing and handles transfers, transmission, dividend payments and corporate actions on behalf of the company.

2. Categories

Category I RTAs can act as both registrar to an issue and share transfer agent. Category II can act as only one of them. Capital requirements differ between the two categories, with Category I needing more. Companies may also handle transfers in-house, but then cannot act as STA for others without registration.

3. Role during an IPO

The registrar receives bid data from exchanges and SCSBs, rejects technically invalid applications, works out the basis of allotment in consultation with the designated stock exchange, issues allotment advice, and instructs depositories to credit shares and SCSBs to unblock funds for unsuccessful bids. Speed and accuracy matter under T+3 listing.

4. Investor servicing

After listing, the STA processes transmission on death, change of address and bank details, issue of duplicate certificates for remaining physical holders, dematerialisation and rematerialisation requests and dividend or interest payments. Requests need the documents prescribed by SEBI, such as standard forms for KYC updates of physical holders.

5. Records and inspection

RTAs keep records of applications, allotments, transfers, refunds and complaints for the period SEBI specifies, and permit inspection by SEBI. They must have adequate systems, a disaster recovery site and periodic audit, since they hold sensitive investor data for many issuers.

6. Grievance redressal

RTAs must resolve investor complaints quickly, maintain a register of complaints and report status to the issuer and SEBI. Unresolved complaints can be escalated through SCORES and the online dispute resolution mechanism. Investors can also approach the company, which remains responsible to its shareholders.

7. Code of conduct

An RTA must act fairly, not favour any issuer or investor, maintain confidentiality, not misuse information about allotments, and avoid conflicts such as acting for an associate's issue without disclosure. It must not delay transfers or refunds without valid reasons.

8. Relationship with the issuer

The RTA acts under an agreement with the company, which defines duties, fees and data handling. The company cannot escape its own obligations under LODR by outsourcing them; both can be held responsible for delays in investor servicing.

Common traps in the exam

  • Category I can do both activities; Category II only one. Options sometimes reverse this.
  • The basis of allotment is finalised by the registrar with the designated stock exchange, not by the merchant banker alone.
  • Outsourcing to an RTA does not remove the listed company's own responsibility to shareholders.
  • Transmission (on death) is not a transfer and remains possible for physical shares.

Quick recap

RTAs handle two jobs: registrar to an issue (applications, allotment, refunds) and share transfer agent (register of members, transmission, corporate actions). Category I does both, Category II one. They keep records, permit SEBI inspection, resolve grievances with SCORES escalation and follow a code of fairness and confidentiality. The issuer remains responsible alongside the RTA.
