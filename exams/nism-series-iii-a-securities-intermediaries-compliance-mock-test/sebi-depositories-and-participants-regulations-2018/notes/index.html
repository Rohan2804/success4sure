SEBI (Depositories and Participants) Regulations, 2018: NISM Series III-A revision notes

While the Depositories Act creates the legal framework, the SEBI (Depositories and Participants) Regulations, 2018 contain the working rules: who can become a depository or DP, governance of depositories, and how DPs must open accounts, handle instructions and protect investors. Exams favour operational scenarios such as DIS misuse, transmission, nomination and reconciliation.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Depository eligibility

A depository must be a company with a minimum net worth of ₹100 crore, sponsored by eligible institutions, and must meet ownership limits applicable to market infrastructure institutions. It is a first-line regulator of DPs and must have governing board independence and a separate regulatory department.

2. Who can be a DP

Banks, financial institutions, custodians, clearing corporations, registered stock brokers, NBFCs and RTAs meeting net worth criteria can be DPs. Each DP must be registered with SEBI through the depository and comply with depository bye-laws and operating instructions.

3. Account opening

The DP does KYC (through a KRA where possible), executes the account opening form, gives the rights and obligations and tariff sheet, and collects nomination or an explicit opt-out declaration. A Basic Services Demat Account is a low-cost account for individuals holding small values of securities, with reduced or nil maintenance charges below SEBI's thresholds.

4. Delivery instruction slips

DIS booklets are issued only on request and serially numbered. Pre-signed blank DIS, loose slips and slips with alterations not countersigned should not be accepted. Many transfers now happen through electronic instructions with OTP or through the DDPI given to a broker. Misuse of DIS is a classic DP compliance failure.

5. Nomination and transmission

Individual account holders can nominate up to the number of nominees SEBI permits with percentage shares. On death of a sole holder, securities are transmitted to the nominee after documents such as death certificate and KYC. In joint accounts, the surviving holders get the securities first; nominees come in only if all holders die.

6. Freezing and closure

An investor can freeze the account against debits, credits or both, useful when travelling abroad. Accounts with no securities and no transactions can be closed on request; balances must first be transferred. Regulator or court orders can also freeze accounts.

7. Reconciliation and audit

DPs reconcile records with the depository daily, and depositories reconcile with issuers and RTAs. Internal audit and concurrent audit of DP operations are required at intervals specified by SEBI and depositories. Mismatches must be resolved promptly to prevent unauthorised transfers.

8. Investor protection

Depositories maintain an investor protection fund, monitor grievances against DPs and conduct inspections. DPs send transaction statements periodically and holding statements at least annually, and alert investors by SMS or email on debits. Complaints can be escalated to the depository and then SCORES.

Common traps in the exam

  • In a joint account the surviving joint holders inherit first; the nominee receives securities only when no joint holder survives.
  • BSDA is a reduced-cost demat account for small holdings, not a separate category of depository.
  • Blank pre-signed DIS must not be accepted from clients or retained by the DP.
  • Depositories, not SEBI directly, are the first-level regulators that inspect DPs.

Quick recap

D&P Regulations 2018: depositories need ₹100 crore net worth and act as frontline regulators of DPs. Eligible DPs include banks, brokers, custodians and NBFCs. DPs open accounts with KYC, nomination, BSDA option; control DIS strictly; transmit to nominee or surviving joint holders; reconcile daily; send statements and debit alerts; handle grievances with escalation to depository and SCORES.
