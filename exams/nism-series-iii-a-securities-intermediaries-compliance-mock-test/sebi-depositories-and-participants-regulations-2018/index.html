SEBI (Depositories and Participants) Regulations, 2018: NISM Series III-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SEBI (Depositories and Participants) Regulations, 2018, from the NISM Series III-A (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Non-Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

These regulations govern the registration, governance and operations of depositories and their participants. The chapter covers eligibility and net worth for depositories and DPs, ownership limits and governance norms, bye-laws, the agreement between DP and client, account opening, transfer and pledge operations, nomination and transmission, statements of account, internal controls, audits, record keeping, inspection and grievance redressal. Focus on the operational duties of DPs towards clients, safeguards such as freezing and authentication of instructions, and the role of the depository in supervising its participants.

SEBI (Depositories and Participants) Regulations, 2018 questions

Question 1

Which of the following entities is eligible to apply for registration as a depository participant?

  1. A listed manufacturing company
  2. A proprietary concern with no financial business
  3. A scheduled commercial bank
  4. An individual retail investor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A scheduled commercial bank

Banks, financial institutions, custodians, clearing corporations, stock brokers and NBFCs meeting criteria can become DPs. Ordinary companies and individuals are not eligible.

Question 2

Depositories send investors a Consolidated Account Statement (CAS) mainly to:

  1. Show only the balance in the investor's linked savings bank account along with interest credited
  2. Calculate the investor's income-tax payable
  3. Give a single view of the investor's securities holdings and transactions across accounts
  4. Replace the need for contract notes from brokers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Give a single view of the investor's securities holdings and transactions across accounts

CAS consolidates holdings and transactions across demat accounts and mutual fund folios linked by PAN, helping investors detect unauthorised activity.

Question 3

A client wants to prevent any debit from his demat account while he is abroad. He can request his DP to:

  1. Transfer all shares to the DP's account
  2. Convert all holdings into physical form
  3. Close the account permanently
  4. Freeze the account for debits
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Freeze the account for debits

Clients can freeze their demat accounts for debits, credits or both, which protects against unauthorised transfers during the period. The client can revoke the freeze when he returns.

Question 4

When a sole holder of a demat account dies and has registered a nominee, the securities are:

  1. Frozen permanently until a court order is obtained
  2. Transmitted to the nominee's account after the DP verifies the required documents
  3. Forfeited to the depository
  4. Sold and the proceeds paid to the DP
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Transmitted to the nominee's account after the DP verifies the required documents

On death of a sole holder, securities are transmitted to the registered nominee on submission of the death certificate and transmission request.

Question 5

Before executing a delivery instruction slip (DIS) submitted in paper form, a DP should:

  1. Verify the client's signature and that the slip was issued to that client
  2. Execute it immediately without any verification
  3. Accept it only if signed by the stock broker
  4. Ask SEBI to approve the transfer before it is processed in the depository system
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Verify the client's signature and that the slip was issued to that client

DPs must verify signatures and DIS serial numbers to prevent misuse of slips, and must keep records of instructions received.

Question 6

A Basic Services Demat Account (BSDA) is designed for:

  1. Companies holding treasury shares
  2. Small investors with low holdings, offering reduced or nil annual maintenance charges
  3. Foreign portfolio investors only, who need a low-cost account for their custodial holdings
  4. Clearing members holding pool securities
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Small investors with low holdings, offering reduced or nil annual maintenance charges

BSDA aims to reduce the cost of holding securities for small investors, with lower or zero annual maintenance charges up to specified holding values.

Question 7

Under the D&P Regulations, a depository is required to:

  1. Leave supervision of DPs entirely to SEBI
  2. Allow DPs to operate without any agreement
  3. Guarantee all trades executed on stock exchanges
  4. Conduct periodic inspection of its participants and act on deficiencies found
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Conduct periodic inspection of its participants and act on deficiencies found

Depositories act as first-level regulators of DPs, inspecting them, monitoring compliance and taking action under their bye-laws. SEBI also inspects depositories and DPs directly.

Question 8

A DP's books show a client holding of 500 shares, while the depository's records show 450. The DP should:

  1. Identify and resolve the difference through reconciliation immediately
  2. Treat its own books as correct and ignore the depository
  3. Credit 50 shares from another client's account
  4. Wait until the client complains and only then check the depository's records
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Identify and resolve the difference through reconciliation immediately

Daily reconciliation between DP records and depository records ensures accuracy, and any difference must be investigated and corrected promptly. Unresolved differences can put client assets at risk.
