Proxy Advisors: NISM Series III-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Proxy Advisors, from the NISM Series III-A (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Non-Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers proxy advisors, who advise institutional investors and shareholders on how to vote at company meetings. Proxy advisors register with SEBI as research analysts and follow additional norms set by SEBI. The chapter covers their role in corporate governance, the code of conduct, disclosure of voting recommendation policies and methodology, management of conflicts of interest, sharing of reports with clients and the company, procedures for company comments and grievance redressal, and the link with stewardship responsibilities of institutional investors. Revise the transparency obligations and conflict-management rules, since these are the main focus of SEBI's framework for proxy advisors.

Proxy Advisors questions

Question 1

A proxy advisor must share its voting recommendation report with the company concerned:

  1. At the same time as it shares the report with its clients
  2. Only after the shareholder meeting is over
  3. Never, as the report is confidential to clients
  4. Only if the company agrees to pay a fee for receiving the report
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. At the same time as it shares the report with its clients

SEBI requires proxy advisors to share reports with the company at the same time as with clients, and to include the company's comments, if any, as an addendum.

Question 2

To ensure transparency, a proxy advisor must publicly disclose:

  1. The final votes cast by each client
  2. Its voting recommendation policies and methodology
  3. The salaries of its analysts
  4. The personal portfolios of its clients
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Its voting recommendation policies and methodology

Proxy advisors must disclose their policies, procedures and methodologies for voting recommendations and review them periodically. This lets clients and companies understand how recommendations are reached.

Question 3

A proxy advisor provides consulting services to a company and also issues voting recommendations on that company's resolutions. This raises a concern of:

  1. Failure to file an offer document with SEBI before advising
  2. Insider trading by the company's shareholders
  3. Conflict of interest, which must be disclosed and managed
  4. Market manipulation under PFUTP
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Conflict of interest, which must be disclosed and managed

Providing consulting to a company while advising investors on its resolutions can bias recommendations, so such conflicts must be disclosed and managed.

Question 4

Institutional investors such as mutual funds use proxy advisors mainly to help them:

  1. Analyse resolutions and decide their votes as part of stewardship responsibilities
  2. Fix the company's dividend policy and recommend the payout ratio to its board each year
  3. Execute trades in the shares of the company
  4. Prepare the company's annual accounts
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Analyse resolutions and decide their votes as part of stewardship responsibilities

Proxy advisors support institutional investors in meeting stewardship obligations by analysing resolutions, though the final voting decision rests with the investor.

Question 5

A company disagrees with a proxy advisor's recommendation and sends its comments. The proxy advisor should:

  1. Report the company to SEBI for interference
  2. Withdraw its recommendation automatically
  3. Share the company's comments with its clients along with the report
  4. Delete the comments to avoid confusing clients
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Share the company's comments with its clients along with the report

Proxy advisors must have a process to receive company comments and communicate them to clients, so that investors see both views.

Question 6

A proxy advisor's voting recommendation is:

  1. A legal opinion approved by SEBI
  2. Advisory in nature, and the institutional investor decides how to vote
  3. Binding on the institutional investor that subscribes to the proxy advisory service
  4. Binding on the company's board of directors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Advisory in nature, and the institutional investor decides how to vote

Recommendations help investors decide but are not binding. Institutional investors remain responsible for their own voting decisions and must disclose them.

Question 7

If a proxy advisor finds a factual error in a report already sent to clients, it should:

  1. Ignore the error, since the report has already been sent and is final
  2. Delete the report without informing clients
  3. Issue a correction or clarification to clients promptly
  4. Wait for the company to point it out publicly
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Issue a correction or clarification to clients promptly

Proxy advisors are expected to have procedures for correcting errors and informing clients promptly, maintaining the reliability of their recommendations.

Question 8

Which of the following best explains why SEBI regulates proxy advisors?

  1. They act as stock exchanges for voting rights
  2. They hold investors' money in escrow
  3. Their recommendations can significantly influence voting outcomes and corporate governance
  4. They decide the listing of companies
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Their recommendations can significantly influence voting outcomes and corporate governance

Because institutional investors often follow proxy advice, proxy advisors affect governance outcomes, which is why SEBI requires transparency and conflict management.
