Proxy Advisors: NISM Series III-A revision notes

Proxy advisers analyse shareholder resolutions and recommend how institutional investors should vote. Their influence on corporate governance outcomes led SEBI to regulate them through a dedicated chapter in the Research Analysts Regulations and later procedural guidelines. Questions test their role, registration route, conflict management, disclosure of methodology and the process for sharing reports with companies.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Who is a proxy adviser

A proxy adviser provides advice to institutional investors or shareholders, through any means, on exercising their rights in a listed company, including recommendations on public offers or voting on agenda items. In India, firms such as IiAS, InGovern and SES are well-known proxy advisers.

2. Why they matter

Mutual funds, insurers and FPIs vote on thousands of resolutions each year and often rely on proxy research. A negative recommendation on a related-party transaction or director appointment can sway enough votes to defeat a resolution, so accuracy and independence matter.

3. Registration route

Proxy advisers must register with SEBI under the Research Analysts Regulations, 2014, which contain a separate chapter on proxy advisers. They must meet eligibility conditions, employ qualified staff and comply with the code of conduct applicable to research analysts and the specific proxy adviser provisions.

4. Conflict of interest

Proxy advisers must disclose conflicts such as consulting relationships with the company being evaluated, or holdings of the firm or its clients in the company. Where a conflict exists, they must manage it through policies and disclosure, and may need to refrain from recommending.

5. Voting recommendation methodology

They must publish their voting policies and methodologies, review them at least annually and disclose material changes. Recommendations should be based on these policies and publicly available information, with reasons stated so that investors can judge rather than follow blindly.

6. Sharing reports with companies

Under SEBI's procedural guidelines, proxy advisers share their reports with clients and the company at the same time, and the company may send comments. If received, the comments are circulated to clients. Factual errors must be corrected promptly and the correction communicated.

7. Not a decision-maker

Final voting decisions rest with the institutional investor, which must follow its own stewardship code and disclose votes and rationale. Proxy advice is an input; investors cannot transfer their fiduciary responsibility to the adviser.

8. Records and grievance

Proxy advisers keep records of reports, recommendations, policies and communications, and must address complaints from clients and companies. Annual compliance audits are required as for other research analysts.

Common traps in the exam

  • Proxy advisers register under the Research Analysts Regulations, not under a separate standalone regulation.
  • The report goes to clients and the company at the same time; the company does not get a pre-approval right.
  • Institutional investors remain responsible for their votes even if they follow proxy advice.
  • Voting policies must be reviewed at least annually and published.

Quick recap

Proxy advisers recommend how institutions should vote at shareholder meetings. They register with SEBI under the RA Regulations, disclose conflicts, publish and annually review voting methodologies, share reports with clients and companies simultaneously, circulate company comments and fix errors fast. Institutions still own their voting decisions under stewardship codes.
