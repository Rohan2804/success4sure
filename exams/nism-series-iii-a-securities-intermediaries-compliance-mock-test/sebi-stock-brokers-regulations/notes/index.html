SEBI (Stock Brokers) Regulations: NISM Series III-A revision notes

The Stock Brokers Regulations and the many SEBI circulars built around them set out how brokers and clearing members are registered and how they must deal with clients. This chapter is heavy in operational rules: client onboarding, handling of client funds and securities, margins, contract notes and grievance redressal. SEBI revises broker regulations often, so focus on principles and check current limits.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Registration and membership

A stock broker must be a member of a recognised stock exchange and registered with SEBI, applying through the exchange. Clearing members must also register. Net worth and deposit requirements vary by segment and are prescribed by SEBI and the exchanges. Brokers must have qualified, NISM-certified staff dealing with clients.

2. Types of clearing members

A self-clearing member clears only its own and its clients' trades. A trading-cum-clearing member clears its own trades and also those of other trading members. A professional clearing member does not trade but clears for others, often a bank or large firm. Know which type clears for whom.

3. Client onboarding

Brokers complete KYC (often through KRAs), collect the account opening form including rights and obligations, risk disclosure document and policies, and allot each client a Unique Client Code. Trades must be placed only under the correct UCC. Nomination or an explicit opt-out is required for new accounts.

4. Segregation of client assets

Client money must be kept in a separate client bank account, and client securities in separate client accounts, never mixed with the broker's own. Using one client's funds for another client's obligations or for proprietary trading is prohibited. Clearing corporations now track client collateral at the individual client level.

5. Margins

Brokers must collect upfront margins (initial margin plus exposure or extreme loss margin) from clients before trades in the derivatives and cash segments and report collections to the clearing corporation. Short collection attracts penalties. Example: if the SPAN plus exposure margin for a Nifty futures lot is ₹1.4 lakh, the client must have that available upfront.

6. Contract notes and statements

A contract note, physical or electronic with a digital signature, must be issued within 24 hours of the trade, showing order and trade numbers, time, quantity, price, brokerage and taxes. Brokers also send periodic statements of funds and securities so that clients can reconcile.

7. Running account settlement

Unless the client asks otherwise, funds and securities left with the broker must be settled by returning them on a periodic basis, monthly or quarterly as the client chooses, after retaining only the amount needed for obligations. This limits the broker's ability to hold idle client money.

8. Authorised persons and grievances

Authorised persons are agents of the broker approved by the exchange; the broker remains responsible for their acts. Brokers must resolve complaints quickly, publish grievance details and participate in SCORES and the online dispute resolution mechanism for disputes not settled directly.

Common traps in the exam

  • A professional clearing member clears for others but does not trade itself.
  • Contract notes are due within 24 hours of the trade, not by T+1 evening.
  • The broker is liable for the acts of its authorised persons.
  • Running account settlement must happen monthly or quarterly as chosen by the client; it cannot be skipped indefinitely.

Quick recap

Brokers register with SEBI through the exchange and must hold client money and securities separately. Three clearing member types: self, trading-cum-clearing and professional. Onboarding needs KYC, UCC, rights and obligations and risk disclosure. Collect upfront margins, issue contract notes within 24 hours, settle running accounts monthly or quarterly, and answer for authorised persons' acts.
