SEBI (Stock Brokers) Regulations: NISM Series III-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SEBI (Stock Brokers) Regulations, from the NISM Series III-A (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Non-Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the regulatory framework for stock brokers and clearing members, including registration, eligibility, capital adequacy, the code of conduct, and the obligations of brokers towards clients. Key topics are client registration and KYC, risk disclosure, segregation and upstreaming of client funds and securities, running account settlement, margin collection and pledge, contract notes and statements, authorised persons, internal controls, grievance redressal and inspections. Focus on how client money and securities must be handled, documents given to clients, and conduct obligations, since these are frequent sources of violations and enforcement action.

SEBI (Stock Brokers) Regulations questions

Question 1

A broker holds client funds at the end of the trading day. Under current SEBI requirements, these client funds must be:

  1. Kept in the broker's proprietary account for safety
  2. Invested in fixed deposits in the broker's own name to earn interest for the firm
  3. Used to meet the broker's operating expenses
  4. Upstreamed to the clearing corporation, not retained with the broker or its bank
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Upstreamed to the clearing corporation, not retained with the broker or its bank

SEBI requires brokers to upstream clients' clear funds to clearing corporations at the end of each day, reducing the risk of misuse of client money.

Question 2

A client opts for quarterly running account settlement. This means the broker must:

  1. Settle the client's funds by returning unused credit balance as per the settlement schedule
  2. Keep the client's funds indefinitely without any settlement
  3. Transfer the balance to the broker's own account each quarter
  4. Settle the account only when the client closes it, regardless of any credit balance outstanding
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Settle the client's funds by returning unused credit balance as per the settlement schedule

Under running account settlement, brokers must periodically pay out clients' unutilised funds on the specified schedule, retaining only what is permitted.

Question 3

Under the margin pledge mechanism, when a client provides shares as margin, the shares:

  1. Are sold by the broker and the cash is kept as margin
  2. Remain in the client's demat account and are pledged in favour of the broker
  3. Are transferred to the broker's own demat account
  4. Are held in physical form by the broker
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Remain in the client's demat account and are pledged in favour of the broker

Since 2020, securities given as collateral must be pledged and re-pledged rather than transferred to the broker, keeping them in the client's account.

Question 4

An authorised person (AP) of a stock broker is:

  1. An agent appointed by the broker, with exchange approval, to deal with clients on its behalf
  2. An independent broker with direct exchange membership
  3. A SEBI official who audits stock brokers and their branch offices on behalf of the stock exchanges
  4. A client with power of attorney over other clients
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. An agent appointed by the broker, with exchange approval, to deal with clients on its behalf

APs act as agents of the broker and are approved by the stock exchange. The broker remains responsible for their acts.

Question 5

Which document must a broker give a client when the account is opened, explaining the risks of trading in the securities market?

  1. Risk Disclosure Document
  2. Scheme Information Document
  3. Offer Document
  4. Information Memorandum
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Risk Disclosure Document

The Risk Disclosure Document, along with the rights and obligations document, is provided at account opening to inform clients of trading risks.

Question 6

A broker executes a trade for a client without the client's instructions. This is:

  1. Acceptable during volatile market conditions
  2. Permitted if the trade makes a profit and the client is informed later
  3. Allowed if the client had a debit balance
  4. Unauthorised trading, a violation of the broker's obligations
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Unauthorised trading, a violation of the broker's obligations

Brokers must trade only on clients' instructions. Unauthorised trades breach the code of conduct and can lead to disciplinary action.

Question 7

Instead of a broad power of attorney, a client may now authorise a broker to transfer securities for settlement and pledging through a:

  1. Demat Debit and Pledge Instruction (DDPI)
  2. Letter of indemnity
  3. Delivery Instruction Slip signed in blank
  4. General power of attorney covering all assets
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Demat Debit and Pledge Instruction (DDPI)

SEBI introduced DDPI to limit the broker's authority to specific purposes such as settlement and margin pledge, replacing broad powers of attorney.

Question 8

A broker carries out proprietary trades and client trades. Under SEBI rules, the broker must:

  1. Combine proprietary and client trades in one account for efficiency
  2. Use clients' securities as margin for its own trades
  3. Keep proprietary and client accounts separate and disclose proprietary trading to clients
  4. Never trade on its own account under any circumstances
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Keep proprietary and client accounts separate and disclose proprietary trading to clients

Brokers must segregate proprietary and client accounts and inform clients whether they undertake proprietary trading, avoiding misuse of client assets.
