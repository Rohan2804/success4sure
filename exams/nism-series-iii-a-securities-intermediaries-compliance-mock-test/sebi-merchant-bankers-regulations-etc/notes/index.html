SEBI (Merchant Bankers) Regulations etc.: NISM Series III-A revision notes

This chapter groups the intermediaries who bring securities to market: merchant bankers who manage issues, underwriters who promise to buy unsubscribed portions, and bankers to an issue who collect application money. Questions test who can act as lead manager, merchant banker obligations under ICDR, the underwriter's commitment and restrictions on conflicts of interest.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Who is a merchant banker

A merchant banker manages issues of securities, acting as manager, consultant or adviser, including preparing offer documents, arranging underwriters and coordinating with other intermediaries. Only a SEBI-registered merchant banker may act as lead manager for public issues, rights issues above the specified size, buybacks and open offers.

2. Registration and capital

Merchant bankers must be body corporates (not NBFCs in the earlier sense), meet capital adequacy or net worth norms, employ experienced professionals and have necessary infrastructure. SEBI raised net worth requirements and introduced categories in 2024 amendments; check the current figures before quoting a number.

3. Due diligence role

The lead manager must exercise due diligence and certify that the offer document is fair and adequate to enable investors to make an informed decision. If disclosures later turn out to be false, the merchant banker can be held responsible. Exams stress that the due diligence certificate is filed with SEBI.

4. Restrictions and conflicts

A merchant banker cannot lead manage an issue of its associate, and must not deal in the securities of the issuer it is managing in a way that uses unpublished information. It cannot carry on business other than in the securities market, apart from permitted financial services. It must disclose any underwriting and stake details.

5. Underwriters

An underwriter agrees to subscribe to securities not taken up by the public, up to the amount underwritten. Example: if ₹100 crore is underwritten and the public subscribes ₹85 crore, the underwriter must take up ₹15 crore of securities. Underwriting obligations must be met within the time set in the agreement. Registered merchant bankers can act as underwriters.

6. Bankers to an issue

Bankers to an issue are scheduled banks registered with SEBI to accept application money, handle refunds and pay interest or dividends if engaged. Under ASBA, self-certified syndicate banks block funds in the applicant's account instead of collecting it. They must keep records and report collection details to the registrar and lead manager.

7. Code of conduct

Merchant bankers must act with integrity and fairness, avoid misleading statements about issues, keep client information confidential, not create a false market and not divulge confidential information about the issuer. Breaches can lead to suspension or cancellation under the Intermediaries Regulations.

8. Records and inspection

They must keep books of account, records and documents for the specified period, submit half-yearly results and reports on issues managed, and allow SEBI inspection. A compliance officer monitors adherence and reports to senior management and SEBI.

Common traps in the exam

  • A merchant banker cannot act as lead manager to an issue of its own associate.
  • Underwriters take up only the unsubscribed portion up to their commitment, not the whole issue.
  • Under ASBA the bank blocks money rather than debiting it, so investors earn interest until allotment.
  • Due diligence responsibility rests with the lead manager even when the issuer prepares the draft.

Quick recap

Merchant bankers manage issues, prepare offer documents and certify due diligence; only registered ones may lead public issues, buybacks and open offers, and never for associates. Underwriters take up the shortfall up to their commitment. Bankers to an issue collect or block application money and handle refunds. All follow a code of conduct, keep records and face Intermediaries Regulations enforcement.
