SEBI (Research Analysts) Regulations, 2014: NISM Series III-A revision notes

The Research Analysts Regulations, 2014 govern anyone who prepares research reports or makes buy, sell or hold recommendations on securities for consideration. They aim to reduce conflicts of interest and improve disclosure. Questions test who must register, qualifications, trading restrictions around reports, mandatory disclosures and the separation of research from other businesses.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Who is a research analyst

A research analyst prepares or publishes research reports, makes buy, sell or hold recommendations, gives price targets or offers opinions on public offers. Example: a firm sending paid stock tips with targets through a messaging channel must register as an RA. Journalists writing general market news, or advisers giving advice only to their own clients, may fall outside.

2. Registration

Individuals, partnership firms, LLPs and body corporates can register. An entity with RA activity must ensure employees involved in research hold required qualifications and certifications, primarily NISM Series XV. Individuals and entities must meet net worth or deposit conditions specified by SEBI.

3. Qualifications

Research analysts need a qualifying educational background as specified by SEBI (graduate-level qualifications, with SEBI having eased the discipline requirements in recent amendments) and the NISM Series XV certification, with periodic renewal through continuing education or re-examination.

4. Conflict management

An entity doing research alongside investment banking or broking must separate the research function by Chinese walls. Research analysts cannot report to the investment banking or sales team, and their pay cannot be tied to specific investment banking deals. Research must be independent of the business that benefits from it.

5. Trading restrictions

Research analysts and their associates must not trade in a security from 30 days before to 5 days after publishing a report on it, and must not trade contrary to their recommendation. Exceptions exist with compliance approval for genuine personal hardship. This prevents front running of their own reports.

6. Disclosures in reports

Reports must disclose the RA's financial interest or that of associates in the subject company, holdings of 1% or more, compensation received from the company in the past twelve months, whether the analyst has served as an officer, and any other material conflict. Recommendations must have a reasonable basis and be fair.

7. Fees and client dealings

RAs dealing with individual and HUF clients must follow SEBI's fee limits, client agreement and advance-fee rules introduced in 2024 amendments (as of 2026 — check the latest SEBI circular). They must not promise returns or assure profits, and must maintain records of research, recommendations and the rationale.

8. Proxy advisors and records

The regulations also contain a chapter for proxy advisers, which advise institutions on voting. All RAs must keep records for five years, have a compliance officer where applicable, and undergo annual compliance audits.

Common traps in the exam

  • The trading restriction is 30 days before and 5 days after the report, not the other way round.
  • Holding of 1% or more in the subject company must be disclosed in the report.
  • An RA cannot report to investment banking or sales staff, even if research is in the same group.
  • Assuring returns or profit in recommendations is prohibited for registered RAs as well.

Quick recap

Anyone publishing research or buy/sell/hold calls for payment needs RA registration with NISM XV certification. Research must be separated from investment banking and sales. No trading in the covered stock 30 days before to 5 days after a report or against the call. Disclose financial interests, 1% holdings and compensation from the company. Follow fee rules, keep records five years.
