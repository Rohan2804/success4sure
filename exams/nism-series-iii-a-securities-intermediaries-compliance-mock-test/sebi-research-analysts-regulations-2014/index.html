SEBI (Research Analysts) Regulations, 2014: NISM Series III-A practice questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the SEBI (Research Analysts) Regulations, 2014, which regulate persons who prepare and publish research reports or make buy, sell or hold recommendations on securities. It explains who needs registration and who is exempt, qualification and certification requirements, the management of conflicts of interest, disclosures required in research reports and public appearances, restrictions on personal trading, separation of research from investment banking and sales, record keeping, fee and client-facing obligations, and the role of the administration and supervisory body. Focus on disclosure requirements, independence from business pressures and limits on trading by analysts.

SEBI (Research Analysts) Regulations, 2014 questions

Question 1

A research entity also has an investment banking division. To protect the independence of research, it must:

  1. Maintain an effective separation (Chinese wall) between research and investment banking
  2. Publish research only on companies that are its banking clients
  3. Link each analyst's pay to the revenue earned from specific investment banking deals it covers
  4. Allow investment bankers to approve research reports before release
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Maintain an effective separation (Chinese wall) between research and investment banking

The regulations require separation of research from investment banking and sales, and bar analysts' compensation from depending on specific banking transactions.

Question 2

In a research report, an analyst must disclose whether the analyst or associates had actual or beneficial ownership of 1% or more of the subject company's securities. The purpose of this is to:

  1. Let readers judge possible conflicts of interest
  2. Advertise the analyst's personal portfolio
  3. Inform tax authorities of the analyst's income
  4. Help the company calculate its public shareholding
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Let readers judge possible conflicts of interest

Disclosure of financial interests and holdings in the subject company helps readers assess whether the recommendation may be biased. Material conflicts must also be disclosed.

Question 3

A research analyst is asked to take part in a roadshow to market an IPO for which his firm is the lead manager. Under the regulations he should:

  1. Participate and present a buy recommendation
  2. Participate only if paid separately by the issuer
  3. Participate after informing the stock exchange and the issuer
  4. Not participate in the marketing or roadshow of the issue
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Not participate in the marketing or roadshow of the issue

Analysts must not take part in business solicitation or marketing activities such as roadshows for issues handled by their firm, as this compromises independence.

Question 4

Which of the following persons generally needs registration as a research analyst?

  1. A person who publishes research reports with buy or sell recommendations for consideration
  2. A company publishing its own audited results
  3. A journalist writing a general market news report
  4. An investor discussing his own trades with family
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A person who publishes research reports with buy or sell recommendations for consideration

Registration is needed by persons preparing research reports or making recommendations on securities as a business. General news and personal discussions are outside the scope.

Question 5

A research analyst appears on a television channel and recommends a stock. He must:

  1. Guarantee a target price to viewers
  2. Ask viewers to trade only through his firm so that the trades can be tracked
  3. Disclose any financial interest or conflict of interest he has in that stock
  4. Avoid mentioning that he is registered with SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Disclose any financial interest or conflict of interest he has in that stock

Disclosure requirements apply to public appearances too, so viewers know of any holdings or conflicts that may affect the recommendation.

Question 6

A research analyst uses an AI tool to generate parts of research reports for clients. Under SEBI's framework, the analyst is:

  1. Prohibited from using any software in research
  2. Required to get SEBI's prior approval for every report that is prepared with such tools
  3. Fully responsible for the output and must disclose the use of such tools to clients
  4. Not responsible for errors made by the AI tool
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Fully responsible for the output and must disclose the use of such tools to clients

SEBI's amended framework makes analysts solely responsible for data security, confidentiality and accuracy of output generated using AI tools, with disclosure to clients.

Question 7

A research analyst's compensation should NOT be:

  1. Paid as a fixed salary
  2. Reviewed periodically by a committee
  3. Based on specific investment banking or brokerage transactions
  4. Linked to the quality and accuracy of research
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Based on specific investment banking or brokerage transactions

To maintain objectivity, analysts' pay cannot be tied to specific investment banking or brokerage transactions, though it can reflect research quality.

Question 8

Under the RA Regulations, a research analyst must maintain records of research reports and the basis of recommendations so that:

  1. The analyst can claim tax deductions
  2. The analyst can avoid giving disclosures in reports
  3. The rationale for each recommendation can be verified during inspection
  4. Clients can resell the reports to other investors for a fee at a later date
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The rationale for each recommendation can be verified during inspection

Analysts must keep records of research, the data used and the rationale, and make them available to SEBI or the supervisory body during inspections.
