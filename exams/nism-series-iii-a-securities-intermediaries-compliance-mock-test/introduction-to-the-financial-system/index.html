Introduction to the Financial System: NISM Series III-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Introduction to the Financial System, from the NISM Series III-A (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Non-Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces the building blocks of a financial system: financial institutions, markets, instruments and services, and how they move savings from households to businesses and government. Learners should be clear on the difference between primary and secondary markets, money and capital markets, direct and intermediated finance, and the roles played by banks, NBFCs, mutual funds, insurers and market intermediaries. When revising, focus on classifying instruments by maturity and market, understanding why intermediaries reduce transaction costs and information gaps, and how a well-functioning system supports price discovery, liquidity and economic growth.

Introduction to the Financial System questions

Question 1

A company raises fresh capital by issuing shares to investors for the first time. In which segment does this transaction take place?

  1. Primary market
  2. Money market
  3. Secondary market
  4. Over-the-counter derivatives market
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Primary market

Fresh issue of securities by an issuer, where money flows to the company, happens in the primary market. In the secondary market, existing securities change hands between investors and the issuer receives nothing.

Question 2

Which of the following is the best example of a capital market instrument?

  1. A ten-year corporate bond
  2. A three-month commercial paper
  3. An overnight call money loan
  4. A 91-day treasury bill
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A ten-year corporate bond

Capital market instruments have maturities above one year and fund long-term needs. Treasury bills, commercial paper and call money are short-term money market instruments.

Question 3

The main benefit of secondary market trading for an investor who bought shares in an IPO is that it:

  1. Allows the investor to return the shares to the issuer for a refund
  2. Guarantees that the shares will always trade above the issue price once they are listed
  3. Converts the equity shares into fixed-income instruments over time
  4. Provides liquidity, allowing the investor to exit by selling to another investor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Provides liquidity, allowing the investor to exit by selling to another investor

The secondary market gives investors an exit route and continuous price discovery. It does not guarantee returns, and shares are not normally returned to the issuer for a refund.

Question 4

When a household deposits money in a bank and the bank lends it to a business, the process is called:

  1. Securitisation
  2. Financial intermediation
  3. Disintermediation
  4. Dematerialisation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Financial intermediation

The bank stands between savers and borrowers, pooling deposits and lending them on, which is financial intermediation. Disintermediation is the opposite, where borrowers raise funds directly from investors.

Question 5

A large company stops borrowing from banks and instead raises funds by issuing commercial paper directly to investors. This trend is known as:

  1. Monetisation of debt
  2. Financial repression
  3. Credit rationing
  4. Disintermediation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Disintermediation

Disintermediation means bypassing intermediaries such as banks and tapping savers directly through market instruments like commercial paper or bonds. It reduces the role of banks as intermediaries.

Question 6

Which of the following is a function of a financial system rather than of a single intermediary?

  1. Issuing a contract note to one trading client
  2. Collecting the premium on one insurance policy
  3. Price discovery through the interaction of many buyers and sellers
  4. Maintaining a particular client's demat account
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Price discovery through the interaction of many buyers and sellers

Price discovery emerges from the collective interaction of market participants. The other options are specific services provided by individual intermediaries.

Question 7

Which pair correctly matches the market with the typical maturity of its instruments?

  1. Money market - more than five years; capital market - up to one year
  2. Money market - exactly one year; capital market - exactly ten years
  3. Money market - up to one year; capital market - more than one year
  4. Money market and capital market - no difference in maturity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Money market - up to one year; capital market - more than one year

The money market deals in short-term funds with maturity up to one year, while the capital market deals in long-term instruments such as equity and bonds.

Question 8

Why are market intermediaries such as brokers, depositories and registrars regulated more strictly than ordinary businesses?

  1. They are exempt from company law and need a separate legal framework
  2. They are not allowed to make profits from their services
  3. They are owned by the government and must therefore follow all public sector rules on pay and hiring
  4. They hold client money or securities and their failure can harm investors and market integrity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. They hold client money or securities and their failure can harm investors and market integrity

Intermediaries handle investor assets and information, so lapses can cause wide losses and erode trust. That is why registration, capital, conduct and compliance requirements apply to them.
