SEBI (Debenture Trustees) Regulations, 1993: NISM Series III-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SEBI (Debenture Trustees) Regulations, 1993, from the NISM Series III-A (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Non-Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the SEBI (Debenture Trustees) Regulations, 1993, which regulate entities that act as trustees for debenture holders. It covers eligibility and capital requirements, restrictions arising from conflicts of interest, the debenture trust deed, due diligence on security and assets before issue, monitoring of covenants and asset cover, calling meetings of debenture holders, actions on default including enforcement of security, the recovery expense fund, disclosures and reporting to exchanges, and the code of conduct. When revising, focus on the trustee's duties before, during and after the issue and the steps taken when an issuer defaults.

SEBI (Debenture Trustees) Regulations, 1993 questions

Question 1

Before a secured debt issue opens, the debenture trustee must:

  1. Underwrite the issue to ensure full subscription
  2. Fix the coupon rate of the debentures
  3. Allot the debentures to investors and dispatch the allotment advice to each successful applicant
  4. Carry out due diligence on the assets proposed to be charged and the adequacy of security
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Carry out due diligence on the assets proposed to be charged and the adequacy of security

Debenture trustees must exercise independent due diligence to confirm that assets are free and adequate to create the stated security before the issue.

Question 2

The document setting out the terms on which the debenture trustee holds security for the benefit of debenture holders is the:

  1. Underwriting agreement
  2. Debenture trust deed
  3. Memorandum of association
  4. Red herring prospectus
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Debenture trust deed

The debenture trust deed, executed between the issuer and the trustee, records the security, covenants, and rights and duties of the trustee.

Question 3

Issuers of listed debt securities must create a Recovery Expense Fund. This fund is used to:

  1. Pay the debenture trustee's annual fees
  2. Meet expenses incurred by the debenture trustee for enforcement and recovery on default
  3. Buy back the debentures at a premium from holders who want to exit well before maturity
  4. Pay interest to debenture holders every quarter
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Meet expenses incurred by the debenture trustee for enforcement and recovery on default

The Recovery Expense Fund, kept with the stock exchange, finances legal and enforcement action by the trustee if the issuer defaults.

Question 4

If an issuer defaults on interest payments, the debenture trustee should:

  1. Wait until maturity before taking any action
  2. Reduce the interest rate on behalf of holders
  3. Resign immediately and appoint a new trustee
  4. Inform debenture holders, seek their consent and take steps to enforce the security
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Inform debenture holders, seek their consent and take steps to enforce the security

On default, the trustee must notify holders, convene meetings, follow their decisions and take action, such as enforcing security or entering inter-creditor arrangements.

Question 5

Which of these would create a conflict of interest that bars an entity from acting as debenture trustee for an issue?

  1. Being a scheduled commercial bank
  2. Being an associate of the issuer company
  3. Having long experience in debenture trusteeship
  4. Having its registered office in Mumbai
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Being an associate of the issuer company

An entity cannot be a debenture trustee for an issuer it is associated with, since it must act independently for the debenture holders.

Question 6

A debenture trustee should periodically verify the issuer's asset cover. Asset cover refers to:

  1. The number of debenture holders in the issue
  2. The credit rating assigned to the issuer's parent or holding company by a CRA
  3. The value of secured assets in relation to the outstanding debentures
  4. The share price of the issuer company
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The value of secured assets in relation to the outstanding debentures

Asset cover shows whether the charged assets are adequate to cover the outstanding principal and interest, and trustees must monitor and report it.

Question 7

Debenture holders want the trustee to convene a meeting to discuss a breach of covenant. The trustee must call a meeting when requested by holders representing at least:

  1. Every single debenture holder
  2. One-tenth of the outstanding amount of debentures
  3. Half of the outstanding amount of debentures
  4. Three-fourths of the outstanding amount of debentures
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. One-tenth of the outstanding amount of debentures

The regulations require the trustee to call a meeting of debenture holders on a requisition by holders of at least one-tenth in value of the outstanding debentures.

Question 8

A debenture trustee must disclose information such as defaults and breach of covenants to:

  1. No one, as such information is confidential
  2. Only the Registrar of Companies
  3. Only the issuer's statutory auditor
  4. The stock exchanges and debenture holders, as required
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The stock exchanges and debenture holders, as required

Trustees must report defaults, breaches and actions taken to the stock exchanges and keep debenture holders informed, as part of their transparency obligations.
