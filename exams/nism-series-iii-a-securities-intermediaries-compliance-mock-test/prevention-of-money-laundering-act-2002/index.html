Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002: NISM Series III-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002, from the NISM Series III-A (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Non-Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002, the PML Rules and SEBI's AML/CFT guidelines for intermediaries. It explains what money laundering is, its stages of placement, layering and integration, and the obligations of reporting entities: client due diligence, identifying beneficial owners, risk-based categorisation, ongoing monitoring, record keeping and reporting to FIU-IND through cash transaction and suspicious transaction reports. It also covers the Principal Officer and Designated Director, the ban on tipping off and staff training. Focus on reporting timelines, beneficial ownership thresholds and red flags in client behaviour.

Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 questions

Question 1

Which stage of money laundering involves moving funds through many complex transactions to hide their origin?

  1. Structuring of tax returns
  2. Layering
  3. Placement
  4. Integration
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Layering

Placement introduces illicit cash into the system, layering disguises the trail through multiple transactions, and integration brings the funds back into the economy as apparently legitimate.

Question 2

A reporting entity files an STR on a client. A staff member then tells the client about the report. This is:

  1. Tipping off, which is prohibited
  2. Allowed if the client asks directly
  3. Good customer service
  4. Required by law within seven days
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Tipping off, which is prohibited

Disclosing to the client that an STR has been filed is prohibited, as it may help the client evade investigation.

Question 3

Under the PML Rules, besides the Principal Officer, a reporting entity must appoint a:

  1. Chief Investment Officer to approve all large transactions of high-value clients
  2. Statutory auditor to file STRs
  3. Designated Director to ensure overall compliance with PMLA obligations
  4. Public relations officer for FIU-IND
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Designated Director to ensure overall compliance with PMLA obligations

The Designated Director, typically the managing director or a whole-time director, is responsible for overall compliance with PMLA obligations. Both officers' details must be shared with FIU-IND.

Question 4

A client who is a politically exposed person (PEP) opens a trading account. Under SEBI's AML guidelines, the intermediary should:

  1. Refuse the account in all cases
  2. Open the account without any additional checks
  3. Apply simplified due diligence because PEPs are well known
  4. Apply enhanced due diligence and obtain senior management approval for the relationship
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Apply enhanced due diligence and obtain senior management approval for the relationship

PEPs are high-risk clients, so intermediaries must perform enhanced due diligence, verify source of funds and get senior management approval to establish or continue the relationship.

Question 5

Which of the following is a typical red flag of possible money laundering in a broking account?

  1. A client choosing to receive contract notes electronically
  2. Large funds deposited and withdrawn soon after with little trading activity
  3. A client updating his correspondence address after relocating to another city for work
  4. A salaried client investing a small amount every month
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Large funds deposited and withdrawn soon after with little trading activity

Funds routed through an account with little genuine trading suggest the account is being used to move money, which should be examined for an STR.

Question 6

FIU-IND, which receives reports from reporting entities, functions under:

  1. SEBI's Integrated Surveillance Department
  2. The Reserve Bank of India
  3. The Ministry of Home Affairs
  4. The Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance

Financial Intelligence Unit-India is the central agency for receiving, analysing and disseminating financial transaction information and functions under the Department of Revenue.

Question 7

For a client that is a company, the PML Rules identify a beneficial owner as a natural person who, alone or together, holds a controlling ownership interest of more than:

  1. 51% of shares, capital or profits
  2. 10% of shares, capital or profits
  3. 75% of shares, capital or profits
  4. 1% of shares, capital or profits
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 10% of shares, capital or profits

Following the 2023 amendment to the PML Rules, the threshold for controlling ownership interest in a company is more than 10% of shares, capital or profits.

Question 8

Under the PMLA, a reporting entity applies a risk-based approach. This means it should:

  1. Apply due diligence checks only to clients who invest more than ₹1 crore in a financial year
  2. Apply due diligence measures in proportion to the money laundering risk each client poses
  3. Treat all clients as high risk and reject most applications
  4. Leave risk assessment to FIU-IND
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Apply due diligence measures in proportion to the money laundering risk each client poses

Clients are categorised as low, medium or high risk, and the depth of due diligence and monitoring is matched to that risk.
