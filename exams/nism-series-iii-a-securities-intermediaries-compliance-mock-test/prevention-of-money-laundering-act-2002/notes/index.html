Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002: NISM Series III-A revision notes

Every SEBI intermediary is a reporting entity under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002. This chapter covers what money laundering is, the obligations of brokers, DPs and others to verify clients and report suspicious activity to FIU-IND, and the record keeping rules. Questions often test reporting deadlines, the principal officer's role, beneficial ownership and red-flag scenarios.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Stages of money laundering

Placement puts illegal cash into the financial system (for example, many small cash deposits). Layering hides the trail through a series of transactions, such as rapid buying and selling of illiquid shares across accounts. Integration brings the money back looking legitimate, for instance as sale proceeds of investments. Exam stems describe one stage and ask you to name it.

2. Offence and punishment

Section 3 makes it an offence to be involved in any process or activity connected with proceeds of crime, including concealment, possession, use or projecting it as untainted. Punishment under Section 4 is rigorous imprisonment of three to seven years and fine, extendable to ten years where the predicate offence is under the NDPS Act.

3. Reporting entities and FIU-IND

Banks, financial institutions and intermediaries such as stock brokers, DPs, mutual funds, portfolio managers and RTAs are reporting entities. They report to the Financial Intelligence Unit-India, the central agency that receives, analyses and disseminates information on suspicious financial transactions to enforcement agencies.

4. Principal officer and designated director

Each reporting entity appoints a principal officer, a senior person responsible for monitoring and reporting transactions to FIU-IND, and a designated director responsible for overall compliance with PMLA obligations. Their names and changes must be communicated to FIU-IND.

5. Client due diligence

Before opening an account, the intermediary identifies the client and verifies identity using officially valid documents, identifies the beneficial owner, understands the purpose of the relationship and does ongoing monitoring. Enhanced due diligence applies to high-risk clients such as politically exposed persons, non-face-to-face clients from high-risk jurisdictions and clients with unusual patterns.

6. Beneficial ownership

For a company, the beneficial owner is the natural person who ultimately owns or controls a specified percentage of shares, capital or profits, or exercises control by other means. The thresholds were tightened in 2023 (to 10% for companies, as of 2026 — check the PML Rules). If no natural person qualifies, the senior managing official is treated as the beneficial owner.

7. Reports and timelines

Cash Transaction Reports cover cash transactions above ₹10 lakh (or integrally connected series) and are filed monthly by the 15th of the following month. Suspicious Transaction Reports must be filed within 7 days of the principal officer concluding that a transaction is suspicious. Securities intermediaries rarely handle cash, so STRs are the main obligation.

8. Record keeping and tipping off

Records of transactions and of identity must be kept for at least five years after the transaction or after the business relationship ends, whichever is later. Staff must not tell the client that an STR has been or will be filed; tipping off defeats the investigation. Filing an STR does not require stopping the account unless directed.

Common traps in the exam

  • STR is due within 7 days of the conclusion of suspicion, not 7 days from the transaction date.
  • CTR is monthly, by the 15th of the next month; STR is event-based.
  • Records must be kept five years from the end of the relationship, not just five years from account opening.
  • Informing the client about an STR (tipping off) is prohibited even if the client asks why there is a delay.

Quick recap

Placement, layering, integration. PMLA Section 3 offence, Section 4 punishment of 3 to 7 years (10 for NDPS). Intermediaries are reporting entities reporting to FIU-IND through a principal officer, with a designated director accountable. Do CDD, identify the beneficial owner, apply EDD to high-risk clients. CTR monthly by the 15th; STR within 7 days of suspicion; keep records five years; never tip off.
