Depositories Act, 1996: NISM Series III-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Depositories Act, 1996, from the NISM Series III-A (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Non-Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

The Depositories Act, 1996 provides the legal basis for holding and transferring securities in electronic form. This chapter covers the definitions of depository, participant, issuer and beneficial owner, the investor's option to hold securities in physical or demat form, the role of the depository as registered owner for transfer purposes, fungibility, register of beneficial owners, pledge and hypothecation, the rights of beneficial owners, opting out of a depository, liability of depositories for losses, and penalties. When revising, focus on the distinction between registered owner and beneficial owner and the legal effect of dematerialisation.

Depositories Act, 1996 questions

Question 1

For securities held in a depository, the depository is deemed to be the registered owner. This status allows it to:

  1. Vote at company meetings on the shares on behalf of all its account holders
  2. Receive dividends on the shares for itself
  3. Effect transfer of ownership on behalf of the beneficial owner
  4. Sell the shares without the owner's instructions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Effect transfer of ownership on behalf of the beneficial owner

Under Section 10, the depository is the registered owner only to effect transfers. It has no voting or other rights; those belong to the beneficial owner.

Question 2

A beneficial owner wants to pledge his demat shares as security for a loan. Under the Depositories Act, he:

  1. Needs prior approval from SEBI for each pledge
  2. Cannot pledge demat shares at all
  3. Must first convert the shares into physical form
  4. Can create the pledge through the depository, following prescribed procedures
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Can create the pledge through the depository, following prescribed procedures

Section 12 allows beneficial owners to pledge or hypothecate securities held in a depository, with the pledge recorded by the depository.

Question 3

A beneficial owner wishes to opt out of the depository system for some securities. The process is called:

  1. Dematerialisation
  2. Consolidation
  3. Sub-division
  4. Rematerialisation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Rematerialisation

Under Section 14, an investor can opt out of a depository, and the issuer then issues physical certificates. This reverse process is rematerialisation.

Question 4

If a beneficial owner suffers a loss due to the negligence of a depository or its participant, under the Act:

  1. The depository must indemnify the beneficial owner
  2. The issuer company must make good the investor's loss
  3. The investor bears the loss entirely
  4. SEBI compensates the investor from its own funds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The depository must indemnify the beneficial owner

Section 16 makes the depository liable to indemnify beneficial owners for losses caused by its own or its participant's negligence.

Question 5

Under the Depositories Act, a depository must be:

  1. A department of the Reserve Bank of India that is constituted under the Banking Regulation Act, 1949
  2. A trust registered with the Charity Commissioner
  3. A partnership firm owned by brokers
  4. A company registered under the Companies Act and holding a SEBI certificate of registration
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A company registered under the Companies Act and holding a SEBI certificate of registration

A depository is defined as a company formed and registered under the Companies Act that has been granted a certificate of registration by SEBI.

Question 6

Which document does an issuer maintain for securities held in demat form, based on details provided by the depository?

  1. Register of charges
  2. Minutes book of board meetings
  3. Register of contracts with related parties
  4. Register and index of beneficial owners
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Register and index of beneficial owners

Section 11 requires the depository to maintain a register of beneficial owners, which is deemed to be the issuer's register of members for demat holdings.

Question 7

An investor holds physical shares and wants to hold them electronically. Under the Act, he must:

  1. Destroy the certificates and inform the company
  2. Send the certificates directly to SEBI
  3. Surrender the certificates to the issuer through a depository participant for dematerialisation
  4. Sell the shares and buy them again in demat form
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Surrender the certificates to the issuer through a depository participant for dematerialisation

The investor surrenders physical certificates via the DP, the issuer verifies and cancels them, and the depository credits the securities to the beneficial owner's account.

Question 8

Which right does a beneficial owner have for securities held through a depository?

  1. Only voting rights, while the dividends are paid to the depository participant
  2. All rights and benefits, such as voting and dividends, and all liabilities
  3. No rights until the shares are rematerialised
  4. Only the right to sell, with dividends going to the depository
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. All rights and benefits, such as voting and dividends, and all liabilities

Section 10(3) gives the beneficial owner all rights, benefits and liabilities attached to the securities held by a depository. The depository itself holds no such rights.
