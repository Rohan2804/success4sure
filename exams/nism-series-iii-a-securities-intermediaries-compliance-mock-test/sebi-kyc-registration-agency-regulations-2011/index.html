SEBI (KYC Registration Agency) Regulations, 2011: NISM Series III-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SEBI (KYC Registration Agency) Regulations, 2011, from the NISM Series III-A (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Non-Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the SEBI (KYC Registration Agency) Regulations, 2011, under which KRAs maintain centralised KYC records of investors in the securities market. It explains registration of KRAs, their functions and obligations, the duties of intermediaries in performing initial KYC, in-person verification and uploading records, and the validation of KYC records by KRAs against official databases. It also covers data security, confidentiality and the link with the Central KYC Records Registry. While revising, focus on the division of responsibility between intermediary and KRA, the 'one-time KYC' benefit for investors and the restrictions on how KRAs may use investor data.

SEBI (KYC Registration Agency) Regulations, 2011 questions

Question 1

An investor completes KYC with a stock broker and later opens an account with a mutual fund distributor. Because of the KRA system, the investor:

  1. Must visit SEBI's office to transfer the KYC
  2. Need not repeat the full KYC process, as the second intermediary can fetch the KYC record from the KRA
  3. Must complete full KYC again with every new intermediary
  4. Can invest only after the broker gives written consent
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Need not repeat the full KYC process, as the second intermediary can fetch the KYC record from the KRA

KRAs hold KYC records centrally so that an investor's KYC done once can be relied upon by other SEBI-registered intermediaries, avoiding duplication.

Question 2

Under the KRA Regulations, a KRA may use the KYC data it holds:

  1. To recommend suitable investment products to investors based on their income details
  2. To sell investor contact lists to marketing companies
  3. To publish investor details on its website
  4. Only for the purposes permitted under the regulations, keeping it confidential
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Only for the purposes permitted under the regulations, keeping it confidential

KRAs must maintain confidentiality and data security and may not use KYC information for any purpose other than what the regulations permit.

Question 3

Which entity is primarily responsible for carrying out the initial KYC of a client, including obtaining documents and in-person verification where needed?

  1. The client's bank, which has already verified the client while opening the savings account
  2. The KYC Registration Agency
  3. The SEBI-registered intermediary with whom the client first opens an account
  4. SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The SEBI-registered intermediary with whom the client first opens an account

The intermediary performs initial KYC and uploads it to the KRA. The KRA stores, validates and makes the record available to other intermediaries.

Question 4

A KRA validates a client's KYC record by checking it against official sources such as the Income Tax Department database. The purpose of this validation is to:

  1. Approve the client's trading limit
  2. Calculate the client's tax liability
  3. Determine the client's eligibility for IPO allotment
  4. Confirm key attributes like PAN, name and address so the record can be relied upon
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Confirm key attributes like PAN, name and address so the record can be relied upon

KRAs independently validate records against official databases so that intermediaries can rely on them, which also supports portability of KYC.

Question 5

To be registered as a KRA, an applicant must obtain a certificate of registration from:

  1. CERSAI
  2. SEBI
  3. The stock exchanges jointly
  4. The Reserve Bank of India
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. SEBI

KRAs are registered and regulated by SEBI under the KRA Regulations, 2011. CERSAI runs the Central KYC Records Registry, which is a different system.

Question 6

If an intermediary finds that a client's KYC details have changed, such as a new address, it should:

  1. Collect supporting documents and update the record in the KRA system
  2. Report the change only to the stock exchange
  3. Ignore the change until the annual review
  4. Close the existing account and open a fresh account with new KYC documents
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Collect supporting documents and update the record in the KRA system

Intermediaries must update changes in KYC details with the KRA so that the central record stays accurate for all intermediaries relying on it.

Question 7

A KRA must have adequate systems to ensure that KYC records are:

  1. Destroyed immediately after an account is opened
  2. Available freely to any member of the public
  3. Stored only in paper form in fireproof cabinets at the head office
  4. Protected against unauthorised access, alteration or loss
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Protected against unauthorised access, alteration or loss

KRA Regulations require robust systems for data security, integrity and confidentiality, along with disaster recovery arrangements. Paper-only storage would not meet these standards.

Question 8

Which of the following is the main benefit of KRAs to intermediaries?

  1. Reduced duplication of KYC work and access to a uniform, verified KYC record
  2. Freedom from having to know their clients at all
  3. Exemption from all anti-money laundering obligations
  4. A guarantee from SEBI against client fraud
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Reduced duplication of KYC work and access to a uniform, verified KYC record

KRAs save repeated KYC effort and provide uniform records, but intermediaries still remain responsible for due diligence and AML obligations.
