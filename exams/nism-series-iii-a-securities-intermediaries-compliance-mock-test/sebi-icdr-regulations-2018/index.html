SEBI (ICDR) Regulations, 2018: NISM Series III-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SEBI (ICDR) Regulations, 2018, from the NISM Series III-A (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Non-Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

The SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018 govern public issues, rights issues, preferential issues, qualified institutions placements, bonus issues and SME issues. The chapter covers eligibility for an IPO under Regulations 6(1) and 6(2), offer document filing, price discovery through book building, allocation to investor categories, anchor investors, minimum promoter contribution and lock-in, pricing of preferential allotments, and timelines for listing. When revising, focus on eligibility conditions, allocation percentages, lock-in rules and the differences among issue types, and keep track of the reduced listing timeline after issue closure.

SEBI (ICDR) Regulations, 2018 questions

Question 1

A company that does not meet the profitability track record under Regulation 6(1) can still make an IPO under Regulation 6(2) if:

  1. At least 75% of the net offer is allotted to qualified institutional buyers
  2. At least 75% of the net offer is allotted to retail investors
  3. The issue is priced below face value
  4. Promoters guarantee a minimum listing price to all applicants in the public issue
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. At least 75% of the net offer is allotted to qualified institutional buyers

Under Regulation 6(2), a company not meeting 6(1) criteria must allot at least 75% of the net offer to QIBs, with retail allocation capped at 10%.

Question 2

In a book-built IPO, anchor investors are:

  1. Retail investors applying for less than ₹2 lakh
  2. Qualified institutional buyers who are allocated shares a day before the issue opens
  3. Employees applying under the employee reservation portion at a discount to the issue price
  4. Promoters contributing to the minimum promoter contribution
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Qualified institutional buyers who are allocated shares a day before the issue opens

Anchor investors are QIBs who bid one working day before the issue opens, receive allocation from the QIB portion and are subject to lock-in.

Question 3

Under ICDR, equity shares held by persons other than promoters before an IPO are generally:

  1. Locked in for ten years
  2. Locked in for six months from the date of allotment in the IPO
  3. Compulsorily sold in the offer for sale
  4. Free from any lock-in
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Locked in for six months from the date of allotment in the IPO

Pre-issue shareholding of non-promoters is generally locked in for six months from IPO allotment, subject to specified exemptions. Promoter shareholding is subject to separate, longer lock-in rules.

Question 4

After a public issue closes, SEBI requires listing and commencement of trading within:

  1. Six months of issue closure
  2. Three working days of issue closure (T+3)
  3. Thirty days of issue closure
  4. Fifteen working days after the issue closure
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Three working days of issue closure (T+3)

SEBI reduced the listing timeline from T+6 to T+3 working days, made mandatory from December 2023, so investors get allotment and refunds faster.

Question 5

A company issues equity shares to a select group of investors on a private basis that is not a rights or public issue. Under ICDR this is a:

  1. Bonus issue
  2. Rights issue
  3. Offer for sale
  4. Preferential issue
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Preferential issue

A preferential issue is an issue to selected persons on a private placement basis, governed by Chapter V of ICDR including pricing and lock-in rules.

Question 6

In a bonus issue, shareholders receive additional shares:

  1. At a discount to market price, payable in cash within thirty days
  2. In exchange for surrendering their debentures
  3. Without paying any consideration, by capitalising reserves
  4. Only if they apply within the offer period
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Without paying any consideration, by capitalising reserves

Bonus shares are issued free of cost by capitalising free reserves or securities premium, in proportion to existing holdings. No money is raised from shareholders in a bonus issue.

Question 7

A listed company raises funds by issuing shares only to qualified institutional buyers through a placement document. This route is called:

  1. Employee stock option scheme
  2. Qualified Institutions Placement (QIP)
  3. Rights issue
  4. Initial Public Offer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Qualified Institutions Placement (QIP)

A QIP allows listed companies to raise capital quickly from QIBs under Chapter VI of ICDR without filing a draft offer document with SEBI for observations.

Question 8

In a rights issue, an existing shareholder who does not wish to subscribe can:

  1. Transfer the rights only to the promoter group at the issue price
  2. Renounce the rights entitlement in favour of another person
  3. Demand cash compensation from the company
  4. Convert the rights into bonus shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Renounce the rights entitlement in favour of another person

Rights entitlements are credited in demat form and can be renounced or traded, so a shareholder who does not subscribe can still realise value.
