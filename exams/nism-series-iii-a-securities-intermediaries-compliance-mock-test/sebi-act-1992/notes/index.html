SEBI Act, 1992: NISM Series III-A revision notes

The SEBI Act, 1992 is the parent law for most of NISM III-A. It creates SEBI, lists its duties and powers, requires intermediaries to register, sets out penalties and provides for appeals. Questions commonly ask section-wise powers, the composition of the Board, who can be penalised for what, and appeal time limits. Section numbers are tested, so revise the main ones.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Origin and objective

SEBI was set up in 1988 as a non-statutory body and became statutory in 1992 through the SEBI Act. Its preamble states three aims: protect the interests of investors in securities, promote the development of the securities market, and regulate it. Many exam options paraphrase these three objectives.

2. Composition of the Board

The Board has a Chairman, two members from Central Government ministries dealing with finance and company law, one member from RBI, and five other members of whom at least three are whole-time members. All are appointed by the Central Government. Know this nine-member structure.

3. Section 11: functions and powers

Section 11 gives SEBI its broad mandate: regulating stock exchanges and intermediaries, registering collective investment schemes and mutual funds, prohibiting fraudulent and unfair trade practices and insider trading, promoting investor education, and conducting inspections and inquiries. Section 11(4) allows interim measures such as impounding proceeds or restraining persons from the market.

4. Section 11B directions

Under Section 11B SEBI can issue directions in the interest of investors or orderly market development, including directions to disgorge wrongful gains. A typical order: a promoter is barred from the securities market for two years and told to disgorge ₹3 crore with interest.

5. Section 12: registration

No stock broker, sub-broker or other intermediary associated with the securities market may buy, sell or deal in securities except under a certificate of registration from SEBI. Depositories, DPs, custodians, credit rating agencies, venture capital funds and collective investment schemes are also covered. Operating without registration is a serious violation.

6. Penalties under Chapter VIA

Sections 15A to 15HB prescribe adjudication penalties: failure to furnish information or returns (15A), failure by intermediaries to enter agreements with clients (15B), failure to redress investor grievances (15C), insider trading (15G), fraudulent and unfair trade practices (15HA), and a residual penalty for any other violation (15HB). Amounts are revised from time to time, so learn the heads rather than figures.

7. Adjudication factors

When fixing a penalty the adjudicating officer considers the disproportionate gain or unfair advantage made, the loss caused to investors and whether the default is repetitive (Section 15J). This is why two similar violations can attract different penalties.

8. Settlement and recovery

Section 15JB permits settlement of proceedings on payment of a settlement amount, without admission or denial of guilt, under the Settlement Proceedings Regulations. Section 28A lets the recovery officer attach bank accounts and property when penalties or disgorgement are not paid.

9. Appeals

Appeals against SEBI or adjudicating officer orders lie to the Securities Appellate Tribunal within 45 days (Section 15T); SAT may condone delay for sufficient cause. Appeals from SAT go to the Supreme Court within 60 days (Section 15Z). Civil courts have no jurisdiction over matters SAT can decide (Section 15Y).

Common traps in the exam

  • Section 11 is about functions and powers; Section 12 is about registration. Options often swap these.
  • The SAT limit is 45 days; the Supreme Court limit after SAT is 60 days.
  • Settlement under 15JB does not require the noticee to admit guilt.
  • The RBI nominee is one member; two members come from the Central Government ministries.

Quick recap

SEBI Act 1992: protect investors, develop and regulate the market. Board of nine: Chairman, two Central Government, one RBI, five others with at least three whole-time. Section 11 powers, 11B directions and disgorgement, 12 registration, 15A to 15HB penalties, 15J penalty factors, 15JB settlement, 28A recovery, 15T appeal to SAT in 45 days, 15Z appeal to Supreme Court in 60 days.
