SEBI Act, 1992: NISM Series III-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SEBI Act, 1992, from the NISM Series III-A (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Non-Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the SEBI Act, 1992, which created SEBI as a statutory body to protect investors and to develop and regulate the securities market. Key areas are the composition of the SEBI Board, its functions and powers under Section 11, powers of investigation, inspection, search and seizure, interim orders, registration under Section 12, adjudication and monetary penalties, settlement of proceedings, and appeals to the Securities Appellate Tribunal and then the Supreme Court. Focus on which section gives which power, the difference between adjudication and directions, and the offences and penalties provided for violations.

SEBI Act, 1992 questions

Question 1

The preamble of the SEBI Act, 1992 states SEBI's objectives as:

  1. Collecting taxes on capital gains made in the securities market
  2. Fixing the prices at which shares are issued to the public
  3. Protecting investors' interests in securities and developing and regulating the securities market
  4. Regulating banks and controlling inflation in the economy
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Protecting investors' interests in securities and developing and regulating the securities market

SEBI was set up to protect investors, develop the securities market and regulate it. Monetary policy and banks are RBI's domain, and SEBI does not fix issue prices or collect taxes.

Question 2

Before the SEBI Act, 1992 was passed, SEBI existed as:

  1. A non-statutory body set up by a government resolution in 1988
  2. A wing of the Ministry of Corporate Affairs
  3. A self-regulatory organisation jointly owned by the major stock exchanges
  4. A department of the Reserve Bank of India
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A non-statutory body set up by a government resolution in 1988

SEBI was first set up in April 1988 through a resolution of the Government of India and was given statutory powers by the SEBI Act, 1992.

Question 3

A person aggrieved by an order of a SEBI adjudicating officer wants to challenge it. To which forum should he first appeal?

  1. National Company Law Tribunal
  2. Supreme Court of India
  3. Securities Appellate Tribunal
  4. Central Government
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Securities Appellate Tribunal

Orders of SEBI and its adjudicating officers are appealable to the Securities Appellate Tribunal. Only after a SAT order does an appeal lie to the Supreme Court.

Question 4

During an investigation, SEBI wants to stop a company from issuing further securities until the matter is decided. Which power is it using?

  1. Power to pass interim orders pending investigation
  2. Power to cancel the company's incorporation
  3. Power to levy a monetary penalty through adjudication
  4. Power to prosecute the company in a criminal court
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Power to pass interim orders pending investigation

SEBI can pass interim or ex-parte orders under Sections 11 and 11B, such as restraining an issue, to protect investors while an investigation is pending.

Question 5

A person facing SEBI proceedings applies to settle them by paying a settlement amount without admitting or denying guilt. This is possible under:

  1. The SCRR, 1957
  2. The Depositories Act, 1996
  3. The settlement mechanism under Section 15JB of the SEBI Act
  4. The compounding provisions of the Income-tax Act, 1961 for tax offences
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The settlement mechanism under Section 15JB of the SEBI Act

Section 15JB allows settlement of administrative and civil proceedings on payment of settlement amount and other terms, subject to SEBI's settlement regulations.

Question 6

Under the SEBI Act, which of these is an investigative power SEBI can use?

  1. Freezing every bank account in the country during an inquiry
  2. Calling for information and records from intermediaries and persons associated with the securities market
  3. Ordering the arrest of any market participant without a court warrant
  4. Changing the tax rate on securities transactions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Calling for information and records from intermediaries and persons associated with the securities market

SEBI can call for information, inspect books, summon persons and examine records. Search and seizure need approval of a designated court, and SEBI has no power to arrest.

Question 7

A person who knowingly fails to pay a penalty imposed by an adjudicating officer and does not comply with SEBI's directions may face:

  1. Recovery proceedings, including attachment of bank accounts and property, by a SEBI recovery officer
  2. Transfer of the matter to the Reserve Bank for recovery
  3. Only a warning letter, since penalties imposed by an adjudicating officer cannot be enforced against assets
  4. Automatic waiver of the penalty after one year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Recovery proceedings, including attachment of bank accounts and property, by a SEBI recovery officer

Section 28A empowers SEBI's recovery officers to recover unpaid penalties and amounts through attachment and sale of assets and bank accounts, and arrest in some cases.

Question 8

An appeal against a SEBI order must normally be filed with the Securities Appellate Tribunal within:

  1. Any time, as there is no time limit
  2. Seven days from the date the order is received
  3. 45 days from the date the order is received
  4. One year from the date of the order
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 45 days from the date the order is received

Section 15T allows 45 days for filing an appeal with SAT, which may admit a late appeal if there was sufficient cause for the delay.
