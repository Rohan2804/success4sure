SEBI (Foreign Portfolio Investors) Regulations, 2019: NISM Series III-A revision notes

The FPI Regulations, 2019 govern how foreign investors buy Indian securities through portfolio routes. They define who may register, how registration is granted through designated depository participants, what an FPI can invest in and the limits that separate portfolio investment from FDI. Questions test the two categories, the DDP's role, the below-10% limit and offshore derivative instruments.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Meaning of FPI

A foreign portfolio investor is a person registered under these regulations to invest in Indian securities. Portfolio investment means holdings below the FDI threshold, intended for financial return rather than management control. Residents cannot be FPIs, but NRIs, OCIs and resident Indians may participate in an FPI within limits set by SEBI.

2. Two categories

Category I includes government and government-related investors (central banks, sovereign wealth funds), pension funds, regulated entities like banks, insurers, asset managers and broker-dealers from FATF-member jurisdictions, and entities from India's IFSC. Category II includes everyone else eligible, such as appropriately regulated funds not in Category I, unregulated funds, corporates, family offices and individuals.

3. Registration through DDP

SEBI has authorised designated depository participants, typically custodian banks, to grant FPI registration on its behalf after due diligence. The DDP checks eligibility, KYC and beneficial ownership. Registration is permanent unless suspended, cancelled or surrendered, subject to payment of fees for the applicable block period.

4. Eligibility conditions

The applicant must not be resident in India, must be from a jurisdiction that is not identified by FATF as high risk, must be eligible to invest under its home laws, and must be fit and proper. Its beneficial owners must be identified under the PMLA framework.

5. Investment limits

Purchase of equity shares of a company by a single FPI or an investor group must be below 10% of the total paid-up equity capital on a fully diluted basis. If it crosses 10%, the FPI must divest the excess within five trading days or the holding is reclassified as FDI. Aggregate FPI holding is subject to the sectoral cap.

6. Investor group concept

FPIs with common ownership of more than 50% or common control are treated as one investor group for the 10% limit. This stops a single foreign investor from splitting money across several entities to cross the portfolio limit indirectly.

7. Permitted instruments

FPIs may invest in listed or to-be-listed equity, units of mutual funds, AIFs and REITs/InvITs, government and corporate debt (subject to RBI limits), derivatives traded on recognised exchanges, and security receipts, among others. Debt investments follow RBI's FPI debt framework and route-specific limits.

8. Offshore derivative instruments

ODIs such as participatory notes are issued abroad by Category I FPIs with Indian securities as underlying. They can be issued only to persons regulated in their home jurisdiction and fit and proper, not to unregulated entities, and must be reported to SEBI. ODI holdings are clubbed with the FPI holdings of the same investor group for limits.

Common traps in the exam

  • The DDP grants registration on behalf of SEBI; the applicant does not apply directly to SEBI.
  • The limit is 'below 10%'; crossing it requires divestment within five trading days or reclassification as FDI.
  • Only Category I FPIs can issue ODIs, and only to regulated subscribers.
  • Investor-group clubbing applies at more than 50% common ownership or common control.

Quick recap

FPIs are non-resident portfolio investors registered through DDPs on SEBI's behalf. Category I: governments, pension funds and regulated entities from FATF jurisdictions; Category II: the rest. Single FPI or investor group must hold below 10% of a company; excess divested in five trading days or treated as FDI. ODIs issued only by Category I FPIs to regulated persons, with reporting to SEBI.
