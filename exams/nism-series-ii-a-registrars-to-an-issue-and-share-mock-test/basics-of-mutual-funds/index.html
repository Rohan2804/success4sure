Basics of Mutual Funds: NISM Series II-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Basics of Mutual Funds, from the NISM Series II-A (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents – Corporate) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how mutual funds are structured and operated in India: the sponsor, trust and trustees, asset management company, custodian and registrar and transfer agent. Learners should understand open-ended and closed-ended schemes, NAV, exit load, systematic investment plans, folios, ELSS lock-in and the consolidated account statement. Practise working out redemption value after exit load. As many RTAs also service mutual funds, pay attention to the RTA's role in processing transactions and maintaining unit holder records under the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996.

Basics of Mutual Funds questions

Question 1

An investor redeems 500 units of a scheme when the NAV is ₹40. The exit load is 1%. What amount will he receive (ignoring tax)?

  1. ₹20,000
  2. ₹19,800
  3. ₹20,200
  4. ₹19,600
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹19,800

Gross value is 500 × ₹40 = ₹20,000. An exit load of 1% reduces the redemption price to ₹39.60, so the investor receives 500 × ₹39.60 = ₹19,800.

Question 2

Which entity is responsible for the safekeeping of securities purchased by a mutual fund scheme?

  1. The sponsor
  2. The registrar and transfer agent
  3. The custodian
  4. The distributor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The custodian

A SEBI-registered custodian holds the scheme's securities and handles their settlement and corporate actions. The RTA maintains unit holder records, the sponsor sets up the fund and distributors sell units.

Question 3

For a mutual fund, which activity is typically handled by its registrar and transfer agent?

  1. Selecting securities for the scheme's portfolio and deciding when to sell them
  2. Approving the scheme's investment objective
  3. Processing purchases and redemptions and maintaining unit holder records
  4. Holding the scheme's securities in safe custody
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Processing purchases and redemptions and maintaining unit holder records

The MF RTA processes transactions, updates folios, issues account statements and handles investor service requests. Portfolio decisions are made by the AMC, and securities are held by the custodian.

Question 4

Investments in an Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS) are subject to a lock-in period of:

  1. One year
  2. Five years
  3. Seven years
  4. Three years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Three years

ELSS units are locked in for three years from the date of allotment, the shortest lock-in among the traditional tax-saving options. Each SIP instalment has its own three-year lock-in.

Question 5

Closed-ended mutual fund schemes are required to list their units on a stock exchange mainly because:

  1. Listing allows the scheme to raise fresh money from the public on every trading day until maturity
  2. Exchange trading removes the need to calculate the NAV of the scheme
  3. Only listed schemes are allowed to invest in equity shares
  4. Investors cannot redeem units with the fund before maturity, so listing provides an exit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Investors cannot redeem units with the fund before maturity, so listing provides an exit

A closed-ended scheme has a fixed maturity and does not offer continuous redemption. Listing on an exchange gives investors liquidity before maturity. NAV must still be computed and disclosed.

Question 6

Investing a fixed amount in a scheme every month through an SIP helps the investor mainly by:

  1. Guaranteeing a minimum return on the amount invested each month
  2. Removing all market risk from equity investments
  3. Buying more units when NAV is low and fewer when it is high
  4. Avoiding the need to complete KYC requirements
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Buying more units when NAV is low and fewer when it is high

This is rupee cost averaging: a fixed amount buys more units at lower NAVs, which can lower the average cost over time. An SIP does not guarantee returns or remove market risk.

Question 7

In mutual fund records, a 'folio' is:

  1. An account number identifying an investor's holdings with a fund house
  2. The list of securities held by a mutual fund scheme on a given date
  3. The daily NAV report sent by the AMC to SEBI
  4. The certificate issued to a distributor by AMFI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. An account number identifying an investor's holdings with a fund house

A folio number identifies an investor's account with an AMC; several schemes can be held under one folio. The list of securities held is the scheme's portfolio.

Question 8

An investor holds some mutual fund units in a demat account and some in non-demat folios. Who sends him the single Consolidated Account Statement?

  1. Each AMC separately for its own schemes only, through its own RTA
  2. The stock exchange where he trades
  3. The depository with which his demat account is held
  4. SEBI through the SCORES portal
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The depository with which his demat account is held

Under SEBI norms, investors with a demat account receive a single CAS from the depository covering both demat holdings and MF folios. Investors without demat accounts receive CAS from the MF RTAs.
