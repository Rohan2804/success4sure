Characteristics of Debt Securities: NISM Series II-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Characteristics of Debt Securities, from the NISM Series II-A (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents – Corporate) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers bonds and debentures: coupon, face value, maturity, security, convertibility and redemption features. Learners should understand secured versus unsecured debentures, fixed and floating coupons, callable instruments, partly convertible debentures, the role of credit ratings and debenture trustees, and the basic link between bond prices and yields. Practise current yield calculations and remember how yield to maturity compares with the coupon when a bond trades at a premium or discount. RTAs handle interest payments and redemptions for debenture holders, so record-keeping aspects matter too.

Characteristics of Debt Securities questions

Question 1

A bond of face value ₹1,000 with a 9% annual coupon is trading at ₹1,125. What is its current yield?

  1. 9.00%
  2. 11.25%
  3. 8.00%
  4. 7.20%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 8.00%

Current yield is annual coupon divided by market price. The coupon is ₹90 (9% of ₹1,000), so ₹90 ÷ ₹1,125 = 8.00%. The coupon rate of 9% is based on face value, not price.

Question 2

A debenture backed by a charge on specific assets of the issuer is called a:

  1. Naked debenture
  2. Secured debenture
  3. Perpetual debenture
  4. Zero-coupon debenture
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Secured debenture

When a charge is created on the issuer's assets in favour of debenture holders, the debenture is secured. A naked debenture is unsecured, while perpetual and zero-coupon describe maturity and interest features.

Question 3

Which intermediary is appointed to protect the interests of holders of listed debentures and to monitor the security created for them?

  1. Registrar to the issue
  2. Credit rating agency
  3. Banker to the issue
  4. Debenture trustee
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Debenture trustee

A SEBI-registered debenture trustee acts for debenture holders, monitors asset cover and can enforce security on default. The registrar maintains records, the rating agency rates the issue and the banker handles funds.

Question 4

A credit rating assigned to a debenture issue primarily indicates:

  1. The relative likelihood of timely payment of interest and principal
  2. The expected price at which the debenture will list on the exchange
  3. A guarantee by the rating agency that investors will be repaid
  4. The outlook for the issuer's equity share price over the next year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The relative likelihood of timely payment of interest and principal

A rating is an opinion on credit risk, that is, the chance of timely servicing of the debt. It is not a guarantee of repayment and says nothing about listing price or equity returns.

Question 5

A bond whose coupon is reset every six months based on a benchmark rate plus a fixed spread is a:

  1. Floating rate bond
  2. Zero-coupon bond
  3. Deep discount bond
  4. Fixed rate bond
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Floating rate bond

In a floating rate bond, the coupon moves with a reference rate such as a T-bill yield, plus a spread. Zero-coupon and deep discount bonds pay no periodic interest, and fixed rate bonds keep the same coupon.

Question 6

A bond that gives the issuer the right to redeem it before maturity at a specified price is a:

  1. Puttable bond
  2. Callable bond
  3. Convertible bond
  4. Step-up bond
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Callable bond

A call option belongs to the issuer, who may redeem early, often when rates fall. A put option belongs to the investor. Convertible bonds convert into shares, and step-up bonds have rising coupons.

Question 7

In a partly convertible debenture (PCD), what happens at the time of conversion?

  1. The entire debenture converts into equity shares on the conversion date fixed at issue
  2. The holder receives only cash and no shares at all
  3. One part converts into equity shares and the remaining part is redeemed as debt
  4. The debenture is cancelled and replaced by preference shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. One part converts into equity shares and the remaining part is redeemed as debt

A PCD has a convertible portion that becomes equity and a non-convertible portion that continues as debt and is redeemed in cash. A fully convertible debenture converts entirely into equity.

Question 8

A bond is trading in the market below its face value. Its yield to maturity will be:

  1. Higher than its coupon rate
  2. Lower than its coupon rate
  3. Exactly equal to its coupon rate
  4. Zero until the bond matures
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Higher than its coupon rate

When a bond trades at a discount, the investor earns the coupon plus the gain from price rising to face value at maturity. So YTM exceeds the coupon. A bond at a premium has YTM below its coupon.
