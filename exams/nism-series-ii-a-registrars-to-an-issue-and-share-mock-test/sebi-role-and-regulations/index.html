SEBI – Role and Regulations: NISM Series II-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SEBI – Role and Regulations, from the NISM Series II-A (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents – Corporate) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the Securities and Exchange Board of India: its objectives under the SEBI Act, 1992, its powers of registration, inspection, investigation, direction and penalty, and the appeal route through the Securities Appellate Tribunal to the Supreme Court. It also covers how SEBI regulates RTAs as intermediaries, the role of the compliance officer, and related rules such as the prohibition of insider trading. Focus on what SEBI can do, under which provision, and what an RTA must do when inspected or when it holds unpublished price sensitive information.

SEBI – Role and Regulations questions

Question 1

Which of the following is NOT an objective stated in the preamble to the SEBI Act, 1992?

  1. Protecting the interests of investors in securities
  2. Promoting the development of the securities market
  3. Regulating the securities market
  4. Setting the policy repo rate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Setting the policy repo rate

The SEBI Act's preamble names investor protection, development and regulation of the securities market. The policy repo rate is set by the RBI's Monetary Policy Committee, not by SEBI.

Question 2

An entity that maintains the register of holders and processes transfers and transmissions for a company, but does not handle public issues, is acting as a:

  1. Registrar to an issue
  2. Merchant banker
  3. Depository participant
  4. Share transfer agent
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Share transfer agent

A registrar to an issue handles application and allotment work for issues, while a share transfer agent maintains records and processes post-issue requests. An RTA may be registered to do one or both.

Question 3

SEBI orders an inspection of an RTA's books and records. What is the RTA expected to do?

  1. Produce the books, records and documents sought and cooperate with the inspecting officer
  2. Decline the inspection until a court order is produced
  3. Produce only those records that its issuer clients have approved in writing for inspection
  4. Provide only the audited annual accounts and nothing else
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Produce the books, records and documents sought and cooperate with the inspecting officer

SEBI's regulations oblige intermediaries to give inspecting officers access to books, records and documents and to cooperate fully. A court order or client approval is not required.

Question 4

An appeal against an order of the Securities Appellate Tribunal lies with:

  1. The High Court of the state
  2. The SEBI board
  3. The Supreme Court of India
  4. The National Company Law Tribunal
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The Supreme Court of India

Under Section 15Z of the SEBI Act, a person aggrieved by a SAT decision may appeal to the Supreme Court on a question of law. SAT itself hears appeals against orders of SEBI.

Question 5

SEBI's power to issue directions to an intermediary in the interest of investors or the orderly development of the market comes mainly from:

  1. Section 12 of the SEBI Act
  2. Section 15Z of the SEBI Act
  3. Section 42 of the Companies Act
  4. Section 11B of the SEBI Act
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Section 11B of the SEBI Act

Section 11B empowers SEBI to issue directions to intermediaries and others to protect investors. Section 12 deals with registration of intermediaries, Section 15Z with appeals to the Supreme Court.

Question 6

Within an RTA, monitoring compliance with SEBI rules and the redressal of investor grievances is the responsibility of the:

  1. Statutory auditor appointed by the members
  2. Compliance officer
  3. Debenture trustee
  4. Lead manager
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Compliance officer

SEBI requires every RTA to appoint a compliance officer to monitor compliance with laws and regulations and to oversee investor grievance redressal. Auditors, trustees and lead managers have other roles.

Question 7

An RTA employee learns that a client company's board will approve a large bonus issue tomorrow. Before announcement, the employee buys that company's shares. This is likely to violate:

  1. SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996
  2. SEBI (Depositories and Participants) Regulations, 2018, as amended
  3. SEBI (Portfolio Managers) Regulations, 2020
  4. SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015

A bonus announcement is unpublished price sensitive information. Trading while in possession of UPSI breaches the PIT Regulations, which apply to connected persons such as RTA staff handling the company's work.

Question 8

SEBI may levy monetary penalties on an intermediary for failures such as not redressing investor grievances. These penalties are imposed by:

  1. An adjudicating officer appointed under the SEBI Act
  2. The stock exchange's disciplinary committee
  3. The Registrar of Companies
  4. The Securities Appellate Tribunal acting on its own motion
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. An adjudicating officer appointed under the SEBI Act

Chapter VIA of the SEBI Act provides for penalties imposed after an adjudication by an officer appointed by SEBI. SAT hears appeals against such orders rather than imposing penalties itself.
