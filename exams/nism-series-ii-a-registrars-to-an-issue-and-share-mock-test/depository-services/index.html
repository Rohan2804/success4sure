Depository Services: NISM Series II-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Depository Services, from the NISM Series II-A (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents – Corporate) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains the depository system in India: the two depositories, depository participants, beneficial owners, issuers and RTAs, and the services available to account holders. Learners should know what a Basic Services Demat Account is, how the Demat Debit and Pledge Instruction replaced broad powers of attorney, limits on joint holders, who can nominate, how freezing works, and why demat securities are fungible. Focus on the rights of the beneficial owner and how the depository's records drive dividends, voting and other corporate benefits.

Depository Services questions

Question 1

An investor who wants to hold shares in electronic form opens an account with a:

  1. Registrar to the issue
  2. Depository participant
  3. Credit rating agency
  4. Clearing corporation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Depository participant

Investors cannot open accounts directly with NSDL or CDSL; they open a demat account through a SEBI-registered depository participant such as a bank or broker that is an agent of the depository.

Question 2

A Basic Services Demat Account (BSDA) is meant for:

  1. Foreign portfolio investors who trade actively in equity derivatives
  2. Companies that hold treasury shares
  3. Brokers who hold clients' securities in a pool
  4. Individuals with a single demat account and small holdings, at reduced charges
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Individuals with a single demat account and small holdings, at reduced charges

The BSDA is designed for small individual investors who have only one demat account and holdings below a value set by SEBI. Annual maintenance charges are nil or low, depending on holding value.

Question 3

To let a stock broker debit shares from a client's demat account for sales and pledges, SEBI now requires the client to give a:

  1. Demat Debit and Pledge Instruction (DDPI)
  2. General power of attorney for all purposes
  3. Delivery instruction slip for every trade
  4. Letter of confirmation from the RTA
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Demat Debit and Pledge Instruction (DDPI)

The DDPI replaced the broad power of attorney and limits the broker's authority to specific purposes such as settlement of trades and margin pledges. It reduces the risk of misuse of client securities.

Question 4

What is the maximum number of holders allowed in a single demat account?

  1. Three
  2. Two
  3. Four, if all are resident individuals
  4. Five
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Three

A demat account may be held by one person singly or by up to three joint holders. The order of names in the account must match the order on securities for demat requests to be accepted.

Question 5

Which demat account holder cannot make a nomination for the account?

  1. A body corporate
  2. A sole individual holder
  3. Individual joint holders
  4. A resident individual holding shares singly
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A body corporate

Nomination is available to individuals holding singly or jointly. Non-individuals such as companies, trusts and HUFs cannot nominate, since succession rules on death do not apply to them.

Question 6

A demat account holder going abroad for a long period wants to prevent any debit from her account. She can ask her DP to:

  1. Close the account and reopen it later
  2. Rematerialise all her holdings
  3. Transfer holdings to her broker's pool account
  4. Freeze her account for debits
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Freeze her account for debits

Depositories let account holders freeze their accounts for debits, credits or both. Freezing debits protects holdings from unauthorised transfers while still allowing credits such as bonus shares.

Question 7

Shares held in demat form do not carry distinctive numbers because they are:

  1. Held only by the issuer company and not by individual investors
  2. Fungible, so any share of that ISIN is identical to any other
  3. Not entitled to corporate benefits
  4. Converted into debentures on credit to the account
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Fungible, so any share of that ISIN is identical to any other

Demat securities of the same ISIN are fungible; ownership is recorded as a quantity rather than as specific numbered certificates. This makes transfer and settlement fast and avoids certificate-level tracking.

Question 8

For dematerialised shares, who is entitled to receive dividends and to vote at general meetings?

  1. The depository, since it is the registered owner
  2. The beneficial owner as shown in the depository's records
  3. The depository participant that maintains the beneficial owner's account
  4. The RTA of the issuer company
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The beneficial owner as shown in the depository's records

Although the depository is the registered owner, the Depositories Act gives all rights and benefits, including dividend and voting, to the beneficial owner. The depository has no voting rights on such shares.
