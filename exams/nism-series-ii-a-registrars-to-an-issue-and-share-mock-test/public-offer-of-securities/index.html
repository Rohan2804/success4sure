Public Offer of Securities: NISM Series II-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Public Offer of Securities, from the NISM Series II-A (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents – Corporate) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the types of public issues and the ICDR framework for an IPO: fixed price and book building, price bands, allocation among QIBs, non-institutional and retail investors, anchor investors, offer documents, minimum subscription, promoter contribution and the bid period. Learners should be comfortable with the limits on the price band, how the bid period changes when the band is revised, and what happens when an issue is undersubscribed. Revise the purpose of the DRHP, RHP and prospectus, since the RTA's work follows from what these documents promise.

Public Offer of Securities questions

Question 1

In a book-built IPO, the floor price is ₹300. What is the highest cap price permitted under SEBI's ICDR rules?

  1. ₹330
  2. ₹315
  3. ₹390
  4. ₹360
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹360

The cap price cannot exceed 120% of the floor price, so the maximum is ₹300 × 1.2 = ₹360. ₹315 is the minimum cap, because the cap must be at least 105% of the floor.

Question 2

In a book-built issue by a company eligible under Regulation 6(1) of ICDR, the portion for retail individual investors must be at least:

  1. 35% of the net offer
  2. 10% of the net offer
  3. 15% of the net offer
  4. 50% of the net offer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 35% of the net offer

For companies meeting the profitability criteria, not less than 35% goes to retail individual investors, not less than 15% to non-institutional investors, and not more than 50% to QIBs.

Question 3

In a book-built IPO, shares allocated to anchor investors come out of the:

  1. QIB portion
  2. Retail individual investor portion
  3. Non-institutional investor portion
  4. Employee reservation portion
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. QIB portion

Anchor investors are qualified institutional buyers, and up to 60% of the QIB portion may be allocated to them on a discretionary basis one working day before the issue opens.

Question 4

A red herring prospectus differs from the final prospectus mainly because it does not contain:

  1. The objects of the issue and the planned deployment of funds
  2. The risk factors relating to the issuer
  3. Complete details of the final issue price and the number of shares offered
  4. The names of the lead managers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Complete details of the final issue price and the number of shares offered

The RHP is used during book building, before the price is discovered, so the final price and quantity are left out. Objects, risk factors and intermediaries are disclosed in the RHP.

Question 5

A fresh-issue IPO closes with valid bids for only 82% of the issue size. What follows?

  1. The issue fails for not meeting minimum subscription, and blocked amounts are released
  2. Shares are allotted to the 82% and the remaining shares lapse permanently
  3. The lead manager must extend the bid period until full 100% subscription is reached
  4. The issuer may proceed if the promoters bring in the shortfall later
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The issue fails for not meeting minimum subscription, and blocked amounts are released

ICDR requires minimum subscription of 90% of the fresh issue. If it is not received, no allotment is made and all application money must be unblocked or refunded within the prescribed time.

Question 6

During the bid period, an issuer revises the price band. What must happen to the bid period?

  1. It must close the same day and fresh bids must be invited
  2. It continues unchanged since only the price has moved
  3. It must be extended by at least three working days, subject to a total of ten
  4. It must be restarted from the first day, with a full fresh bid period for all categories
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. It must be extended by at least three working days, subject to a total of ten

ICDR requires the bid period to be extended by at least three additional working days after a price band revision, but the total bid period cannot exceed ten working days. The revision must also be publicised.

Question 7

In an IPO, the minimum promoters' contribution that must be locked in is generally:

  1. 5% of the pre-issue capital
  2. 51% of the post-issue capital
  3. 20% of the post-issue capital
  4. 10% of the issue size
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 20% of the post-issue capital

ICDR requires promoters to contribute and lock in at least 20% of the post-issue capital, which gives promoters a continuing stake. Holdings beyond this minimum have a shorter lock-in.

Question 8

In a fixed price issue, unlike a book-built issue, the investor:

  1. Bids within a band and the price is found later
  2. Knows the exact issue price before applying
  3. Can bid at the cut-off price only
  4. Pays only after the shares are listed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Knows the exact issue price before applying

In a fixed price issue the price is stated in the offer document, so investors apply at that price. In book building investors bid within a band and the final price is discovered from demand.
