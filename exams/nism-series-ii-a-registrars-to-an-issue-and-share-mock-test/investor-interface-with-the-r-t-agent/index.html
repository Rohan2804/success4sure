Investor Interface with the R&T Agent: NISM Series II-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Investor Interface with the R&T Agent, from the NISM Series II-A (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents – Corporate) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the service requests an RTA handles directly for investors, especially holders of physical securities: KYC updation, nomination, transmission, signature updation, duplicate securities and dividend payment. Learners should know the purpose of the common ISR and SH forms, how deletion of a deceased joint holder is treated, why demat holders must update details through their DP, and how unclaimed dividends and shares move to the Investor Education and Protection Fund. Focus on matching each request with the correct process and documents.

Investor Interface with the R&T Agent questions

Question 1

A holder of physical shares wants to register his PAN, mobile number and email address with the RTA. Which form should he submit?

  1. Form ISR-4
  2. Form SH-13
  3. Form ISR-1
  4. Form ISR-5
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Form ISR-1

SEBI's common forms use ISR-1 for registering or updating PAN, KYC and contact details. ISR-4 is for service requests like duplicate securities, SH-13 for nomination and ISR-5 for transmission.

Question 2

A holder of physical shares wishes to register a nominee for the first time. Which form is used?

  1. Form SH-14
  2. Form SH-13
  3. Form ISR-3
  4. Form ISR-2
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Form SH-13

SH-13 under the Companies Act rules is used to make a nomination. SH-14 is for cancelling or varying a nomination, ISR-3 for opting out of nomination and ISR-2 for banker's attestation of signature.

Question 3

Shares are held jointly by Suresh and Kavita. Suresh dies, and Kavita asks the RTA to delete his name. This request is processed as:

  1. Transmission in favour of the surviving holder
  2. Transposition of names
  3. Transfer by way of gift
  4. Rematerialisation of shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Transmission in favour of the surviving holder

On the death of a joint holder, the deceased's name is deleted by transmission to the surviving holder, on submission of the death certificate. Transposition only changes the order of living holders.

Question 4

An investor's signature on a request does not match the specimen in the RTA's records. The RTA should ask for:

  1. A fresh demat account opening form
  2. Form ISR-2 with the signature attested by the investor's banker
  3. A copy of the company's annual report
  4. A transmission request supported by a death certificate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Form ISR-2 with the signature attested by the investor's banker

SEBI norms allow the RTA to update a signature using Form ISR-2, in which the investor's banker attests the signature and confirms bank details. This prevents fraudulent requests.

Question 5

A holder of physical shares has not registered bank account details with the RTA. Under current SEBI norms, how will his dividend be paid?

  1. By a physical dividend warrant posted to his address
  2. In cash at the RTA's investor service centre
  3. By crediting it to his broker's client account
  4. Only electronically, after he furnishes the required KYC and bank details
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Only electronically, after he furnishes the required KYC and bank details

SEBI requires dividends and other payments to physical holders to be made only electronically once PAN, contact, bank details and specimen signature are registered. Payment is held until then.

Question 6

To issue duplicate securities in place of lost share certificates, an RTA typically requires:

  1. Only a phone call from the registered mobile number
  2. A no-objection letter from the stock exchange
  3. Form ISR-4 along with an affidavit and an indemnity from the holder
  4. A letter from any other shareholder of the company
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Form ISR-4 along with an affidavit and an indemnity from the holder

Because duplicates replace lost certificates, the holder must protect the company against misuse through an affidavit and indemnity, with other documents depending on value. The RTA then issues a letter of confirmation.

Question 7

A shareholder holding only demat shares writes to the company's RTA to change her address. What should the RTA advise?

  1. Submit Form ISR-1 to the RTA so that it can change the depository records
  2. Update the address through her DP, since the RTA uses depository records for demat holdings
  3. Convert the shares into physical form first
  4. Write to SEBI, which updates addresses for all investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Update the address through her DP, since the RTA uses depository records for demat holdings

For demat holdings, the RTA relies on beneficial owner details downloaded from the depositories. Address and bank changes must therefore be made through the DP, after which they flow to the company's records.

Question 8

Shares on which dividend has been unclaimed for seven consecutive years were transferred to the IEPF. How can the shareholder claim them back?

  1. By filing Form IEPF-5 online with the IEPF Authority
  2. By submitting Form ISR-1 to the company's RTA
  3. By asking the DP to reverse the transfer
  4. The shares cannot be claimed once transferred
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. By filing Form IEPF-5 online with the IEPF Authority

Shares and unclaimed amounts transferred to the IEPF can be claimed by filing Form IEPF-5, with documents verified by the company. Neither the RTA nor the DP can reverse the transfer directly.
