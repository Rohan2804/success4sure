Characteristics of Other Securities: NISM Series II-A practice questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter looks beyond plain equity and debt to preference shares, depository receipts, foreign currency convertible bonds, units of REITs and InvITs, and derivatives. Learners should know cumulative versus non-cumulative and participating preference shares, the Companies Act bar on irredeemable preference shares, the difference between ADRs, GDRs and IDRs, and what assets REITs and InvITs hold. Focus on which investor rights each instrument carries, because RTAs maintain registers and process distributions for many of these securities, not only for ordinary shares.

Characteristics of Other Securities questions

Question 1

A company has 8% cumulative preference shares. It paid no preference dividend for two years and now has profits to distribute. Which is correct?

  1. Arrears lapse and only the current year's preference dividend is payable
  2. Equity holders are paid first and preference holders receive the balance
  3. Preference holders must convert their shares into equity to claim arrears
  4. Arrears of preference dividend must be paid before any equity dividend
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Arrears of preference dividend must be paid before any equity dividend

With cumulative preference shares, unpaid dividends accumulate as arrears. Before any dividend is paid to equity holders, the arrears and the current preference dividend must be cleared. Arrears lapse only for non-cumulative shares.

Question 2

Under the Companies Act, 2013, a company is NOT permitted to issue:

  1. Cumulative preference shares
  2. Irredeemable preference shares
  3. Convertible preference shares
  4. Participating preference shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Irredeemable preference shares

Section 55 prohibits issuing irredeemable preference shares; all preference shares must be redeemable within the prescribed period. Cumulative, convertible and participating features are permitted.

Question 3

Depository receipts issued by an Indian company and listed on a stock exchange in the United States are called:

  1. Indian Depository Receipts
  2. Foreign Currency Convertible Bonds
  3. American Depository Receipts
  4. Masala bonds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. American Depository Receipts

ADRs represent underlying shares of a non-US company and trade in the US. IDRs are issued by foreign companies in India, FCCBs are convertible bonds, and masala bonds are rupee-denominated bonds issued abroad.

Question 4

Foreign Currency Convertible Bonds (FCCBs) issued by an Indian company are:

  1. Rupee bonds issued in India that can be converted into shares of a foreign parent company
  2. Equity shares listed only on overseas exchanges
  3. Bonds denominated in a foreign currency that can be converted into the company's equity shares
  4. Short-term loans from foreign banks repayable in rupees
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Bonds denominated in a foreign currency that can be converted into the company's equity shares

FCCBs are raised overseas in foreign currency, pay interest like bonds and give holders the option to convert into equity of the Indian issuer at a set price. They are not equity shares or bank loans.

Question 5

Units of a Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT) give investors exposure mainly to:

  1. Undeveloped agricultural land
  2. Residential housing loans
  3. Completed, income-generating commercial real estate
  4. Shares of listed real estate brokers and housing agents
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Completed, income-generating commercial real estate

SEBI's REIT rules require most assets to be in completed, rent-generating properties such as office parks, and a large share of distributable cash flow to be paid out to unit holders.

Question 6

An Infrastructure Investment Trust (InvIT) typically holds:

  1. Infrastructure assets such as toll roads and power transmission lines
  2. Listed equity shares of banks, NBFCs and insurance companies
  3. Gold and other precious metals
  4. Government treasury bills
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Infrastructure assets such as toll roads and power transmission lines

InvITs pool investor money to own revenue-generating infrastructure projects, such as roads, transmission lines and pipelines, and distribute cash flows to unit holders. They do not primarily hold shares, gold or T-bills.

Question 7

A financial contract whose value depends on an underlying asset such as a share, index or commodity is a:

  1. Debenture
  2. Derivative
  3. Depository receipt
  4. Treasury bill
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Derivative

Futures and options are derivatives because their value is derived from an underlying. Debentures and T-bills are direct debt claims, and a depository receipt represents ownership of underlying shares.

Question 8

Participating preference shares give the holder, in addition to the fixed dividend:

  1. A right to share in surplus profits and, if so provided, in surplus assets on winding up
  2. The right to convert into debentures at any time
  3. Voting rights equal to equity holders in all matters at all times, including routine resolutions
  4. An exemption from tax on the dividend received each year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A right to share in surplus profits and, if so provided, in surplus assets on winding up

Participating preference shares share in surplus profits after equity holders get a specified return, and may share surplus assets on winding up. Voting rights for preference holders arise only in limited cases.
