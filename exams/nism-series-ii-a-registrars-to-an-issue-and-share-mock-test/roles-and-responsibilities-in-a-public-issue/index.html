Roles and Responsibilities in a Public Issue: NISM Series II-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Roles and Responsibilities in a Public Issue, from the NISM Series II-A (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents – Corporate) syllabus.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

A public issue involves many intermediaries: the issuer, lead managers, registrar to the issue, bankers, sponsor bank, SCSBs, syndicate members, registered brokers, collecting depository participants, underwriters, monitoring agency and the stock exchanges. This chapter covers what each does and the requirements SEBI places on them. Learners should know who monitors the use of issue proceeds, where investors can submit ASBA forms, the account into which funds of successful bidders move, the role of the designated stock exchange and the special market-making requirement for SME issues.

Roles and Responsibilities in a Public Issue questions

Question 1

Monitoring the use of issue proceeds in a large IPO is done by a monitoring agency, which must be a:

  1. Statutory auditor of the issuer company
  2. Credit rating agency registered with SEBI
  3. Registrar to the issue for that IPO
  4. Scheduled commercial bank acting as banker to the issue
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Credit rating agency registered with SEBI

Since 2022, ICDR requires the monitoring agency for issues above the threshold to be a SEBI-registered credit rating agency. It reports on utilisation of proceeds against the stated objects.

Question 2

In a public issue, the intermediary that agrees to subscribe to any shares left unsubscribed by the public is the:

  1. Underwriter
  2. Registrar to the issue
  3. Sponsor bank
  4. Debenture trustee
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Underwriter

An underwriter commits to take up the shortfall in subscription, for a commission. The registrar processes applications, the sponsor bank handles UPI mandates and a debenture trustee serves debenture holders.

Question 3

Apart from the branches of SCSBs, an investor can submit a physical ASBA bid form at:

  1. Designated locations of syndicate members, registered brokers, RTAs and CDPs
  2. Any office of the Registrar of Companies in the issuer's state
  3. The issuer's registered office only
  4. Any branch of SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Designated locations of syndicate members, registered brokers, RTAs and CDPs

SEBI widened bid collection to syndicate members, registered stock brokers, registrars and collecting DPs at designated centres. They upload bids to the exchange platform and forward forms for blocking of funds.

Question 4

When an issue is to be listed on more than one exchange, the issuer chooses one of them, for allotment and related purposes, as the:

  1. Principal clearing exchange
  2. Designated stock exchange
  3. Lead stock exchange
  4. Primary listing authority
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Designated stock exchange

The issuer names a designated stock exchange in the offer document. The basis of allotment is finalised in consultation with it, and its representative is involved in allotment-related processes.

Question 5

Once allotment is finalised, the blocked amounts of successful ASBA bidders are transferred to the:

  1. Investor Education and Protection Fund
  2. Depository's settlement account
  3. Public issue account
  4. Lead manager's current account
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Public issue account

On instructions from the registrar, SCSBs and the sponsor bank debit the blocked funds for successful bids and transfer them to the public issue account opened by the issuer with the banker to the issue.

Question 6

Under ICDR, the issuer must appoint as Registrar to the issue an entity that:

  1. Is a subsidiary of the lead manager
  2. Holds at least 1% of the issuer's paid-up equity shares
  3. Is also the issuer's statutory auditor
  4. Has electronic connectivity with both depositories
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Has electronic connectivity with both depositories

Since allotment is made only in demat form, ICDR requires the issuer to appoint a registrar with connectivity to both NSDL and CDSL. Shareholding in the issuer or links with the auditor are not requirements.

Question 7

A successful IPO applicant finds that allotted shares have not been credited to his demat account. Whom should he contact first?

  1. The registrar to the issue
  2. The stock exchange's trading desk
  3. The Reserve Bank of India
  4. His mutual fund distributor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The registrar to the issue

The registrar handles post-issue matters such as allotment, demat credit and unblocking, and must redress such complaints. If not resolved, the investor can escalate through SEBI's grievance system.

Question 8

For IPOs on the SME platform, the lead manager must ensure compulsory market making for a minimum of:

  1. Six months from listing
  2. One year from listing
  3. Ten years from listing or until migration to the main board
  4. Three years from listing
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Three years from listing

ICDR requires market making for SME issues for at least three years from listing so that the shares have liquidity. The market maker gives two-way quotes for a minimum portion of the trading day.
