Processes related to Public Offering of Shares: NISM Series II-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Processes related to Public Offering of Shares, from the NISM Series II-A (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents – Corporate) syllabus.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter traces the issue process from bidding to listing: ASBA and UPI mandates, collection of bids, reconciliation of bid data with fund blocking, technical rejections, finalising the basis of allotment, unblocking funds and crediting shares. Learners should know who can bid at the cut-off price, how retail allotment works when an issue is oversubscribed, where the registrar gets investors' address and bank details, and when anchor investors bid. These are core RTA activities, so focus on the sequence of steps and the reasons applications are rejected.

Processes related to Public Offering of Shares questions

Question 1

A retail category in an IPO is heavily oversubscribed, so not every applicant can receive the minimum bid lot. How is allotment made?

  1. Every applicant gets a proportionate fraction of a lot rounded down
  2. The minimum bid lot is allotted to as many applicants as possible, chosen by draw of lots
  3. Applicants who bid the largest amounts are allotted shares first, in descending order of amount
  4. Applications received earliest in the bid period are preferred
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The minimum bid lot is allotted to as many applicants as possible, chosen by draw of lots

ICDR aims to give the minimum bid lot to the maximum number of retail individual investors. Where that is not possible for all, successful applicants are selected by lottery in consultation with the designated exchange.

Question 2

A retail investor places an IPO bid using UPI but does not approve the mandate request in her UPI app before the cut-off. What is the result?

  1. The broker pays the amount and recovers it after allotment
  2. The bid is considered and money is debited on allotment
  3. Funds are not blocked and the bid is not considered for allotment
  4. The registrar approves the mandate on her behalf after the issue closes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Funds are not blocked and the bid is not considered for allotment

Under the UPI mechanism, the investor must accept the mandate to block funds. Without approval, no funds are blocked and the bid is treated as invalid; nobody else can approve it for her.

Question 3

Before finalising the basis of allotment, the Registrar to the issue reconciles:

  1. The issuer's audited accounts with the lead manager's due diligence report
  2. Share prices on the grey market with the final issue price
  3. Bid details from the stock exchanges with fund-blocking data from SCSBs and the sponsor bank
  4. The list of promoters with the latest shareholding pattern
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Bid details from the stock exchanges with fund-blocking data from SCSBs and the sponsor bank

Only bids backed by successfully blocked funds are valid. The registrar matches electronic bid files from the exchanges with blocking confirmations from banks and rejects unmatched or deficient bids.

Question 4

Which of the following is a ground for technical rejection of an IPO application?

  1. Bidding at the cut-off price as a retail individual investor in the issue
  2. Bidding for the minimum lot size
  3. Applying through a registered broker instead of a bank
  4. Multiple bids made under the same PAN in the retail category
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Multiple bids made under the same PAN in the retail category

Multiple applications under one PAN in the same category are treated as multiple bids and liable to rejection. Bidding at cut-off, bidding for the minimum lot and using a broker are all permitted.

Question 5

For sending allotment communication to IPO applicants, the registrar takes their address and bank details from:

  1. Details handwritten on the application form
  2. The issuer's register of members
  3. Depository records linked to the DP ID and client ID in the bid
  4. The lead manager's marketing database of prospective investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Depository records linked to the DP ID and client ID in the bid

Demographic details for allotment and communication are fetched from the depositories using the DP ID, client ID and PAN given in the bid, which is why investors must keep demat records updated.

Question 6

Which category of bidder is allowed to bid at the 'cut-off' price in a book-built IPO?

  1. Qualified institutional buyers
  2. Retail individual investors
  3. Non-institutional investors bidding above ₹2 lakh
  4. Anchor investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Retail individual investors

Only retail individual investors (and eligible employees and shareholders in reserved portions within the limit) may bid at cut-off. QIBs and NIIs must bid at a specific price and cannot use the cut-off option.

Question 7

Anchor investors in a book-built IPO submit their bids:

  1. On the last day of the bid period
  2. One working day before the issue opens to the public
  3. On the day of listing
  4. After the basis of allotment is finalised by the registrar
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. One working day before the issue opens to the public

The anchor investor bid/issue period is one working day before the issue opening date. Allocation to anchors is disclosed before the public issue opens, which signals institutional interest.

Question 8

An ASBA bidder in an IPO is not allotted any shares. What happens to the application money?

  1. The blocked amount is unblocked in the bidder's bank account
  2. A refund cheque is posted to the bidder's address
  3. The amount is adjusted against the next IPO
  4. It is transferred to the issuer and refunded later by cheque
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The blocked amount is unblocked in the bidder's bank account

Under ASBA, money never leaves the bidder's account until allotment. For unsuccessful bids, the bank simply releases the block on instruction, so no refund cheque or transfer is needed.
