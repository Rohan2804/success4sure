Secondary Market Transactions: NISM Series II-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Secondary Market Transactions, from the NISM Series II-A (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents – Corporate) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how trades in listed securities are executed and settled: trading members, contract notes, the clearing corporation, pay-in and pay-out, auctions for short delivery, bulk deal disclosures and the link between ex-date and record date under T+1 settlement. Learners should understand how securities reach the buyer's demat account under current direct pay-out rules and what happens when a seller fails to deliver. Since RTAs depend on depository data as on the record date, knowing the trading and settlement cycle helps in getting corporate action entitlements right.

Secondary Market Transactions questions

Question 1

Who can place orders directly in a stock exchange's trading system?

  1. Any investor with a PAN
  2. The company's RTA
  3. Trading members registered with SEBI
  4. Depository participants that are banks
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Trading members registered with SEBI

Only trading members (stock brokers) registered with SEBI and admitted by the exchange can enter orders. Investors trade through these brokers, while RTAs and DPs have no trading access.

Question 2

A stock broker must issue a contract note to a client for trades executed on an exchange within:

  1. Seven days of the trade
  2. One month of the trade
  3. The next quarterly statement
  4. 24 hours of the trade
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 24 hours of the trade

SEBI requires brokers to issue contract notes, physically or electronically, within 24 hours of execution. The note records price, quantity, brokerage and charges, and is evidence of the trade.

Question 3

In the settlement of exchange trades, 'pay-in' refers to:

  1. The clearing corporation releasing funds and securities to members
  2. Investors paying dividends to the company
  3. The company paying listing fees to the exchange
  4. Clearing members delivering funds and securities to the clearing corporation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Clearing members delivering funds and securities to the clearing corporation

Pay-in is when members deliver their obligations of funds and securities to the clearing corporation. Pay-out is the reverse, when the clearing corporation releases funds and securities to receiving members.

Question 4

A selling broker fails to deliver shares on the settlement day. To meet the buyer's entitlement, the clearing corporation normally:

  1. Cancels the buyer's trade and refunds the brokerage only
  2. Conducts an auction to buy the shares, or closes out if the auction fails
  3. Asks the issuer to print new shares for the buyer
  4. Waits until the seller delivers, however long it takes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Conducts an auction to buy the shares, or closes out if the auction fails

Short deliveries are handled through an auction in which shares are bought for the buyer, with the cost recovered from the defaulting seller. If the auction fails, the trade is closed out at a prescribed price.

Question 5

Under current SEBI norms, securities bought by a client in the cash segment are paid out:

  1. First to the broker's pool account and later to the client
  2. To the RTA, which then credits the client
  3. To the depository's suspense account for seven days
  4. Directly to the client's demat account by the clearing corporation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Directly to the client's demat account by the clearing corporation

To protect client securities, SEBI mandated direct pay-out of securities by the clearing corporation to clients' demat accounts, instead of routing them through brokers' pool accounts.

Question 6

A deal in a single scrip in which the total quantity bought or sold by a client exceeds 0.5% of the company's listed equity shares is called a:

  1. Block deal
  2. Spot deal
  3. Bulk deal
  4. Odd-lot deal
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Bulk deal

Trades by a client in a day totalling more than 0.5% of listed equity shares are bulk deals and must be disclosed by the exchanges. Block deals are large trades executed in a separate window.

Question 7

Under the T+1 rolling settlement cycle, the ex-date for a corporate action such as a dividend is:

  1. One working day before the record date
  2. The same day as the record date
  3. Two working days after the record date
  4. The date of the board meeting
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The same day as the record date

Since a buyer on T receives shares on T+1, a buyer on the record date would not be on the record. So with T+1, the ex-date and record date fall on the same day.

Question 8

An investor buys shares on the exchange and wants to check that delivery has been completed. Which record should she verify?

  1. Her demat account statement or holding balance with the DP
  2. The company's register of members for physical holders
  3. The lead manager's allotment list
  4. The RTA's list of unclaimed dividends
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Her demat account statement or holding balance with the DP

Exchange-traded purchases are delivered by credit to the buyer's demat account, so the DP holding statement confirms receipt. The company's physical register and IPO allotment lists are not relevant here.
