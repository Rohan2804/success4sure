Client Servicing: NISM Series II-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Client Servicing, from the NISM Series II-A (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents – Corporate) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter deals with how RTAs serve investors and resolve grievances: the difference between queries and complaints, SEBI's SCORES platform and its timelines, online dispute resolution, unpaid dividend accounts, investor charters, data confidentiality and good service practices. Learners should know the time allowed to resolve a SCORES complaint, the next step if an investor is still dissatisfied, where unclaimed dividends go and how to handle incomplete requests efficiently. Focus on practical scenarios, because client servicing questions often test the right response rather than a definition.

Client Servicing questions

Question 1

Under SEBI's SCORES system, an entity receiving an investor complaint must resolve it and file an action taken report within:

  1. 7 calendar days of receipt
  2. 21 calendar days of receipt
  3. 45 calendar days of receipt
  4. 90 calendar days of receipt
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 21 calendar days of receipt

SEBI's revised SCORES framework requires the entity to redress the complaint and upload an action taken report within 21 calendar days. Delays are escalated and monitored by the designated body and SEBI.

Question 2

An investor remains dissatisfied after the RTA's response and the SCORES process. Which mechanism can he use next for conciliation or arbitration?

  1. The online dispute resolution portal (SMART ODR)
  2. The Registrar of Companies
  3. The consumer helpline of the RBI
  4. The company's AGM
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The online dispute resolution portal (SMART ODR)

SEBI's online dispute resolution framework lets investors start online conciliation and, if needed, arbitration against market participants, including RTAs, through the common ODR portal.

Question 3

A company's dividend warrant remains unpaid or unclaimed 30 days after declaration. The unpaid amount must be transferred to:

  1. The company's general reserve
  2. The Investor Education and Protection Fund immediately
  3. The RTA's own current account
  4. A special unpaid dividend account of the company with a scheduled bank
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A special unpaid dividend account of the company with a scheduled bank

Section 124 of the Companies Act requires unpaid dividends to be moved to an Unpaid Dividend Account within seven days after the 30-day period. Only amounts unclaimed for seven years go to the IEPF.

Question 4

SEBI requires RTAs to display an 'investor charter' on their websites. Its main purpose is to:

  1. Publish the RTA's annual profit and loss account
  2. List all shares held by the RTA's employees
  3. Advertise new IPOs managed by the RTA
  4. Tell investors about services, timelines, their rights and the grievance process
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Tell investors about services, timelines, their rights and the grievance process

The investor charter sets out services offered, expected timelines, investors' rights and duties, and how to escalate grievances. It aims to make service standards transparent, not to publish financials or adverts.

Question 5

An investor phones the RTA to ask about the record date for an upcoming dividend. How should this be treated?

  1. As a complaint, to be reported on SCORES
  2. As a transmission request needing documents
  3. As a query, to be answered promptly with accurate information
  4. As a request to be referred to SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. As a query, to be answered promptly with accurate information

A request for information with no allegation of deficiency is a query. Only grievances about service failure, such as non-receipt of dividend, are complaints recorded and redressed as such.

Question 6

A marketing firm asks an RTA for a list of shareholders' mobile numbers and email addresses for a promotion. The RTA should:

  1. Share it for a fee, since the data belongs to the RTA
  2. Share it if the firm promises to use it carefully
  3. Share only email addresses, since they are not sensitive
  4. Decline, since investor data must be kept confidential and used only for authorised purposes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Decline, since investor data must be kept confidential and used only for authorised purposes

RTAs hold investor data on behalf of issuers and must protect it. Sharing it with third parties for marketing would breach confidentiality obligations under SEBI norms and data protection law.

Question 7

An RTA finds three deficiencies in a transmission request. What is the best practice?

  1. Reject the request without giving any reasons
  2. Raise one deficiency at a time as each is fixed
  3. Inform the claimant of all the deficiencies together in a single communication
  4. Process it anyway and seek documents after transfer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Inform the claimant of all the deficiencies together in a single communication

Listing all deficiencies at once avoids repeated rounds and delays. Processing without the required documents could cause wrongful transmission, and rejection without reasons is poor service.

Question 8

Which of the following is an example of a complaint rather than a query?

  1. An investor asks for the RTA's office address
  2. An investor reports that a declared dividend was not credited to his bank account
  3. An investor asks when the AGM will be held
  4. An investor asks for a copy of the nomination form
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. An investor reports that a declared dividend was not credited to his bank account

A complaint alleges a service failure needing redressal, such as non-receipt of a dividend. Requests for addresses, dates or forms are queries and can be answered directly.
