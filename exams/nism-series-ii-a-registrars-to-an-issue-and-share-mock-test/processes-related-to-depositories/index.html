Processes related to Depositories: NISM Series II-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Processes related to Depositories, from the NISM Series II-A (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents – Corporate) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter describes how depository processes work end to end: dematerialisation and rematerialisation, pledge, off-market transfers, transmission on death and corporate actions. The RTA plays a key role in confirming demat and remat requests, cancelling certificates, updating the register of members and reconciling capital. Learners should know the sequence of steps and who initiates each request, common reasons for rejection of demat requests, and the quarterly reconciliation of share capital audit. Pay attention to what the RTA does with physical certificates and how records are kept consistent between depositories and the issuer.

Processes related to Depositories questions

Question 1

After an investor submits a demat request, the shares are credited to her demat account only when:

  1. The DP receives the original certificates by post from the investor
  2. The issuer's RTA confirms the request electronically to the depository
  3. The stock exchange approves the request
  4. The investor sells the shares in the market
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The issuer's RTA confirms the request electronically to the depository

The DP forwards the request electronically and sends certificates to the RTA. Only after the RTA verifies and confirms does the depository credit the beneficial owner's account.

Question 2

Which of these is a common reason for an RTA to reject a demat request?

  1. The investor's depository participant is a private sector bank
  2. The signature on the request does not match the specimen in the RTA's records
  3. The shares have a face value of ₹1 each
  4. The investor already holds other shares in demat
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The signature on the request does not match the specimen in the RTA's records

RTAs verify certificates, names and signatures against their records. A signature mismatch, a name mismatch or certificates not matching the folio are typical reasons for rejection.

Question 3

After confirming a demat request, what does the RTA do with the physical certificates?

  1. Returns them to the investor for safe keeping
  2. Sends them to the stock exchange for storage
  3. Keeps them active in case the investor later wants to sell in physical form
  4. Cancels and mutilates them and records the depository as the holder of those shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Cancels and mutilates them and records the depository as the holder of those shares

Once confirmed, the certificates are cancelled and mutilated so they cannot be reused, and the register of members is updated to show the depository as the registered owner.

Question 4

In rematerialisation of shares, the physical certificates are printed and dispatched to the investor by:

  1. The depository participant
  2. The issuer's RTA
  3. The depository
  4. The clearing corporation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The issuer's RTA

The investor submits a remat request through the DP. The depository blocks the holding and informs the RTA, which prints and dispatches certificates and confirms, after which the demat balance is debited.

Question 5

In a pledge of dematerialised shares, the pledge creation request is initiated by the:

  1. Pledgee, without any involvement or consent of the pledgor
  2. RTA of the issuer company
  3. Stock exchange
  4. Pledgor, through its depository participant
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Pledgor, through its depository participant

The pledgor (the borrower who owns the shares) initiates the pledge through its DP. The pledgee confirms the request through its own DP, after which the shares are marked as pledged.

Question 6

Anil wants to gift 50 shares from his demat account to his sister's demat account. He instructs his DP using:

  1. A demat request form
  2. A remat request form
  3. A transmission request form signed by both of them
  4. An off-market delivery instruction
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. An off-market delivery instruction

Gifts and other non-exchange transfers are made as off-market transfers using a delivery instruction slip or its online equivalent, giving the reason for transfer. Demat, remat and transmission forms have other uses.

Question 7

The reconciliation of share capital audit, which ties total issued capital to holdings in NSDL, CDSL and physical form, is carried out:

  1. Once in five years by the statutory auditor
  2. Every quarter by a practising chartered accountant or company secretary
  3. Only at the time of an IPO by the lead manager
  4. Every month by the stock exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Every quarter by a practising chartered accountant or company secretary

The Depositories and Participants Regulations require issuers to get this audit done every quarter by a qualified practising CA or CS and to submit the report to the stock exchanges.

Question 8

A sole demat account holder dies, leaving a registered nominee. How are the shares transmitted?

  1. The RTA issues physical certificates in the nominee's name
  2. The nominee must buy the shares on the exchange
  3. The shares are cancelled and the company pays their face value
  4. The DP transfers them to the nominee's demat account on receiving the required documents
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The DP transfers them to the nominee's demat account on receiving the required documents

The nominee submits a transmission request form and death certificate to the deceased's DP. After verification, the securities are transmitted to the nominee's demat account; the nominee needs a demat account.
