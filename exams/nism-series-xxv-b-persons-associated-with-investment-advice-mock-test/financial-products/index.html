Financial Products: NISM Series XXV-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Financial Products, from the NISM Series XXV-B (Persons Associated with Investment Advice (Sales and Other Non-Core Services)) syllabus.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers financial products that advisers commonly recommend: mutual funds (structure, categories, direct and regular plans, NAV, riskometer, SIPs), ETFs and index funds, PMS, AIFs and Specialised Investment Funds, small savings schemes, NPS, insurance (term, health, ULIPs, endowment) and bank deposits. Learners should know minimum investments, costs, liquidity, regulators and suitability of each product, and be able to compare them for different client situations.

Financial Products questions

Question 1

A client wants to invest ₹10,000 a month into an equity fund for 15 years. The most suitable mechanism is:

  1. A Systematic Withdrawal Plan from an existing debt scheme
  2. A Systematic Investment Plan
  3. A one-time lump sum in a fixed maturity plan
  4. A five-year bank recurring deposit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A Systematic Investment Plan

A SIP invests a fixed amount at regular intervals, building wealth gradually and averaging purchase costs over market cycles. An SWP does the opposite, withdrawing money.

Question 2

The direct plan of a mutual fund scheme has a lower expense ratio than the regular plan because:

  1. It invests in a completely different portfolio of securities
  2. It does not pay distributor commission
  3. It is managed by a less experienced fund manager
  4. SEBI subsidises its costs from public funds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. It does not pay distributor commission

Both plans share the same portfolio and manager. The regular plan's higher TER includes distribution commission, which the direct plan excludes.

Question 3

An investor puts ₹25,000 into a scheme at NAV ₹62.50 (no stamp duty or loads). The units allotted are:

  1. 390.6
  2. 400
  3. 2,500
  4. 40
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 400

Units = amount ÷ NAV = 25,000 ÷ 62.50 = 400. Units allotted depend on the applicable NAV, which is determined by the time of receipt of funds.

Question 4

The minimum investment in a Specialised Investment Fund (SIF) under SEBI's framework is:

  1. ₹500 through a monthly SIP
  2. ₹1 crore, the same as AIFs
  3. ₹10 lakh per investor across SIF strategies
  4. ₹50 lakh, the same as PMS
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹10 lakh per investor across SIF strategies

SEBI set a ₹10 lakh minimum per investor across all SIF strategies of an AMC (accredited investors excepted), positioning SIFs between mutual funds and PMS.

Question 5

A ULIP differs from a term insurance plan mainly because a ULIP:

  1. Offers only a pure death benefit with no maturity value
  2. Guarantees a fixed maturity amount in all cases
  3. Combines insurance with market-linked investment
  4. Is regulated by SEBI rather than IRDAI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Combines insurance with market-linked investment

ULIPs invest part of the premium in market-linked funds and provide some life cover. Term plans give pure protection. ULIPs are regulated by IRDAI.

Question 6

Which product would generally suit a retired client who needs regular income with low risk?

  1. Senior Citizens' Savings Scheme
  2. A small-cap equity fund with high volatility
  3. A Category III AIF using leverage
  4. An equity derivatives trading strategy
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Senior Citizens' Savings Scheme

SCSS is a government-backed scheme paying quarterly interest, suitable for retirees seeking safety and income. The other options carry high volatility.

Question 7

An ELSS mutual fund scheme is characterised by:

  1. A guaranteed return over five years
  2. A three-year lock-in with equity exposure
  3. Investment only in government bonds
  4. No lock-in and daily redemption
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A three-year lock-in with equity exposure

Equity Linked Savings Schemes invest mainly in equities and have a three-year lock-in. Tax deductions depend on the tax regime the investor chooses.

Question 8

The riskometer of a mutual fund scheme helps the investor to:

  1. Understand the scheme's risk level
  2. Know the exact return the scheme will deliver
  3. See the fund manager's personal investments
  4. Compare the scheme's fee with PMS fees
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Understand the scheme's risk level

The riskometer classifies a scheme's risk on a six-level scale from Low to Very High based on portfolio characteristics, and is reviewed monthly. It does not predict returns.
