Financial Asset Classes: NISM Series XXV-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Financial Asset Classes, from the NISM Series XXV-B (Persons Associated with Investment Advice (Sales and Other Non-Core Services)) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains the main financial asset classes: equity (shares and their rights), fixed income (government securities, corporate bonds, deposits, money-market instruments), gold and commodities, real estate, and derivatives. It covers each asset's sources of return, risks (market, interest rate, credit, liquidity, inflation), credit ratings, deposit insurance and basic derivative concepts. Focus on comparing asset classes and on explaining their risk-return features in plain language to clients.

Financial Asset Classes questions

Question 1

Which risk most directly affects the holder of a corporate bond rated BBB compared with a AAA bond of similar maturity?

  1. Higher currency risk since BBB bonds are always issued abroad
  2. Lower interest rate risk because of the lower rating
  3. Higher credit risk of default
  4. Zero liquidity risk because BBB bonds trade daily
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Higher credit risk of default

Lower-rated bonds have a higher chance of default, so they pay higher yields. Ratings do not determine currency or remove liquidity risk.

Question 2

Sovereign Gold Bonds offer investors:

  1. Physical delivery of gold bars at maturity with no interest
  2. Guaranteed return of capital regardless of gold prices
  3. Gold price exposure plus fixed interest
  4. Exposure to gold mining company shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Gold price exposure plus fixed interest

SGBs track the gold price and pay fixed annual interest on the issue value. Redemption is in cash at prevailing gold prices, so value can fall.

Question 3

A client compares a company's non-convertible debenture with its equity share. Which feature belongs to the debenture rather than the share?

  1. A contractual right to interest and repayment
  2. Voting rights at the company's general meetings on major decisions
  3. A claim on residual profits after all other obligations are met
  4. Unlimited upside if the company's profits grow strongly
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A contractual right to interest and repayment

Debenture holders are lenders with a contractual claim to interest and principal, ranking ahead of shareholders. Voting rights, residual profits and unlimited upside are features of equity ownership.

Question 4

A buyer of a put option on a stock benefits when:

  1. The stock price rises well above the strike price
  2. The stock price stays exactly at the purchase price
  3. The stock price falls below the strike price
  4. The company declares a large dividend in cash
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The stock price falls below the strike price

A put gives the right to sell at the strike price, so it gains value as the underlying price falls. The buyer's maximum loss is the premium paid.

Question 5

Treasury bills issued by the Government of India:

  1. Pay a fixed coupon every six months for ten years
  2. Are equity instruments with voting rights
  3. Are issued at a discount and redeemed at face value
  4. Carry credit risk similar to unrated corporate bonds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Are issued at a discount and redeemed at face value

T-bills are short-term discount instruments with maturities up to 364 days. The difference between issue price and face value is the investor's return.

Question 6

Liquidity risk is highest for which asset?

  1. A residential flat in a small town
  2. Units of a liquid mutual fund scheme
  3. Nifty 50 index ETF units
  4. A savings bank deposit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A residential flat in a small town

Property can take months to sell and may require a price discount. Liquid funds, index ETFs and savings deposits can be converted to cash quickly.

Question 7

Why do long-term government bonds fall more in price than short-term ones when interest rates rise?

  1. They have higher credit risk than short-term bonds
  2. They have higher duration
  3. They pay no coupon at all
  4. They are not traded on any market
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. They have higher duration

Duration measures price sensitivity to rate changes, and longer bonds have higher duration. Government bonds carry little credit risk, but they still carry interest rate risk.

Question 8

REITs are suitable for investors who want:

  1. Guaranteed rental income fixed for 20 years
  2. Ownership of a specific flat in their own name
  3. An instrument with no market price risk
  4. Real estate exposure with smaller tickets and listing
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Real estate exposure with smaller tickets and listing

REITs pool income-producing commercial properties and list on exchanges, offering smaller-ticket, more liquid access. Distributions vary and unit prices fluctuate.
