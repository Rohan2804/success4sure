Securities Markets & Basics of Investment: NISM Series XXV-B practice questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the basics of investing and how securities markets work. It includes the time value of money, simple and compound interest, real versus nominal returns, risk and return, asset allocation and rebalancing, diversification, investment horizon, and the market infrastructure of exchanges, depositories, clearing corporations, brokers and depository participants. Practise basic calculations such as compounding, real return and the Rule of 72, and be able to explain asset allocation simply to clients.

Securities Markets & Basics of Investment questions

Question 1

₹2 lakh invested at 8% compounded annually will grow in 3 years to approximately:

  1. ₹2.48 lakh
  2. ₹2.52 lakh
  3. ₹2.16 lakh
  4. ₹2.60 lakh
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹2.52 lakh

Future value = 2 × (1.08)³ = 2 × 1.2597 ≈ ₹2.52 lakh. Simple interest would give only ₹2.48 lakh, because compounding earns interest on interest.

Question 2

The present value of ₹1,10,000 receivable in one year, discounted at 10%, is:

  1. ₹99,000
  2. ₹1,21,000
  3. ₹1,00,000
  4. ₹1,10,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹1,00,000

Present value = 1,10,000 ÷ 1.10 = ₹1,00,000. Money received in the future is worth less today because it could be invested meanwhile.

Question 3

Asset allocation is considered the most important investment decision for most clients because it:

  1. Guarantees that the portfolio will never fall in value
  2. Removes the need to review the portfolio ever again
  3. Ensures that the client beats every benchmark each year
  4. Largely determines a portfolio's overall risk and return
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Largely determines a portfolio's overall risk and return

How money is split among equity, debt, gold and cash drives most of the variability in portfolio returns. It does not guarantee outcomes or remove the need for reviews.

Question 4

Under the Rule of 72, an investment growing at 12% a year will double in roughly:

  1. 12 years
  2. 8 years
  3. 6 years
  4. 4 years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 6 years

Years to double ≈ 72 ÷ 12 = 6 years. The rule is a quick approximation useful for explaining compounding to clients.

Question 5

An investor wants to hold shares in electronic form. The account needed with a depository participant is a:

  1. Savings bank account linked to a debit card
  2. Demat account
  3. Public Provident Fund account at a post office
  4. Trading account with a mutual fund distributor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Demat account

A demat account, opened with a DP of NSDL or CDSL, holds securities electronically. A trading account with a broker is used to place orders.

Question 6

A client's portfolio target is 50% equity and 50% debt. After a market fall, it is 40% equity and 60% debt. Rebalancing would mean:

  1. Selling more equity to cut losses and move entirely to debt
  2. Leaving the portfolio unchanged indefinitely
  3. Buying equity and selling debt to restore 50:50
  4. Moving the whole portfolio into gold
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Buying equity and selling debt to restore 50:50

Rebalancing restores the target by buying the underweight asset and selling the overweight one. It enforces discipline and keeps risk at the intended level.

Question 7

An investor earns 9% on a bond fund when inflation is 6%. The approximate real return is:

  1. 15%
  2. 3%
  3. 9%
  4. 1.5%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 3%

Real return ≈ nominal return − inflation = 9 − 6 = 3%. The exact figure (1.09 ÷ 1.06 − 1) is about 2.8%.

Question 8

Diversification across asset classes helps mainly because:

  1. It guarantees a positive return every single year
  2. It eliminates market risk across all assets
  3. Different assets often do not move together
  4. It ensures the highest possible returns
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Different assets often do not move together

Assets with low correlation can offset each other, smoothing overall returns. Diversification reduces but does not eliminate risk or guarantee returns.
