Free NISM Series XXV-B practice questions

Question 1 · Personal Financial Planning & Financial Markets

'Risk capacity' of a client refers to:

  1. The client's emotional comfort with volatility
  2. The number of risky products the client already holds
  3. The financial ability to bear losses, based on income, wealth, obligations and time horizon
  4. The maximum return the client expects
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The financial ability to bear losses, based on income, wealth, obligations and time horizon

Risk capacity is an objective financial measure; risk tolerance is the psychological willingness to take risk.

Question 2 · Securities Markets & Basics of Investment

₹1,00,000 invested at 10% per annum compounded annually will grow in 2 years to:

  1. ₹1,20,000
  2. ₹1,10,000
  3. ₹1,25,000
  4. ₹1,21,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹1,21,000

1,00,000 × 1.10² = ₹1,21,000; compounding earns interest on interest.

Question 3 · Financial Asset Classes

When market interest rates rise, the price of an existing fixed-coupon bond generally:

  1. Rises
  2. Remains unchanged
  3. Doubles
  4. Falls
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Falls

Bond prices and yields move inversely.

Question 4 · Financial Products

Under SEBI's scheme categorisation, a 'large cap' company for mutual funds is one ranked:

  1. 1st to 250th
  2. 101st to 250th
  3. 251st onwards
  4. 1st to 100th by full market capitalisation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 1st to 100th by full market capitalisation

Large caps are the top 100 companies, mid caps 101–250 and small caps from 251 onward.

Question 5 · Sales Skills

A sales staff member is asked by a 70-year-old retiree, who needs steady income, about a small-cap fund that did well last year. The appropriate response is to:

  1. Recommend investing all savings since last year's returns were high
  2. Promise that small caps will repeat last year's returns
  3. Explain that small-cap funds are highly volatile and may not suit their income needs and risk profile
  4. Refuse to discuss the client's needs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Explain that small-cap funds are highly volatile and may not suit their income needs and risk profile

Suitability requires matching recommendations to the client's needs, horizon and risk profile, not past returns.

Question 6 · Professionalism, Ethics & Compliance

Investment advisers in India are regulated under:

  1. SEBI (Research Analysts) Regulations, 2014
  2. SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013
  3. IRDAI (Insurance Brokers) Regulations
  4. SEBI (Portfolio Managers) Regulations, 2020
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013

Investment advisers are governed by the SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013, as amended.

Question 7 · Personal Financial Planning & Financial Markets

A 28-year-old with stable income and no dependants, but who panics at small losses, has:

  1. Low risk capacity and high risk tolerance
  2. High risk capacity but low risk tolerance
  3. High risk capacity and high risk tolerance
  4. Low risk capacity and low risk tolerance
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. High risk capacity but low risk tolerance

Age, income and few obligations give high capacity, while emotional discomfort with losses indicates low tolerance.

Question 8 · Securities Markets & Basics of Investment

Which statement best distinguishes investing from saving?

  1. Investing deploys money in assets with the aim of growth, accepting some risk; saving sets aside money, typically with low risk and high liquidity
  2. Saving always earns more than investing
  3. Investing involves no risk at all
  4. There is no difference between them
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Investing deploys money in assets with the aim of growth, accepting some risk; saving sets aside money, typically with low risk and high liquidity

Investing seeks returns above inflation by taking risk; saving prioritises safety and liquidity.

Question 9 · Financial Asset Classes

Which bond would be most sensitive to a change in interest rates, other things being equal?

  1. A bond maturing in one month
  2. A bond with a longer duration
  3. A treasury bill
  4. An overnight money market instrument
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A bond with a longer duration

Duration measures interest-rate sensitivity; longer duration means larger price change.

Question 10 · Financial Products

What is the main difference between the direct plan and the regular plan of a mutual fund scheme?

  1. The direct plan invests in different securities
  2. The regular plan has guaranteed returns
  3. The direct plan has no distributor commission, so its expense ratio is lower
  4. The direct plan cannot be redeemed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The direct plan has no distributor commission, so its expense ratio is lower

Both plans share the same portfolio; the regular plan's expense ratio includes distribution costs.

Question 11 · Sales Skills

Which statement reflects ethical negotiation and closing in advisory sales?

  1. Use fear of missing out to close quickly
  2. Close only when the client understands the advice and agrees voluntarily, without pressure
  3. Offer a cash rebate to close the deal
  4. Withhold fee details until after signing
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Close only when the client understands the advice and agrees voluntarily, without pressure

Ethical closing relies on informed, voluntary consent and full disclosure of fees.

Question 12 · Professionalism, Ethics & Compliance

An investment adviser acting in a fiduciary capacity must:

  1. Recommend products that maximise the adviser's income
  2. Guarantee returns to clients
  3. Follow the instructions of product manufacturers
  4. Act in the best interest of the client and disclose any conflicts of interest
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Act in the best interest of the client and disclose any conflicts of interest

The IA Regulations impose a fiduciary duty to act in the client's best interest.

Question 13 · Personal Financial Planning & Financial Markets

Which of the following best describes a SMART financial goal?

  1. Become rich someday
  2. Save more money
  3. Invest in good stocks
  4. Accumulate ₹15 lakh for a child's education in 10 years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Accumulate ₹15 lakh for a child's education in 10 years

SMART goals are specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound.

Question 14 · Securities Markets & Basics of Investment

Asset allocation refers to:

  1. Picking the single best-performing stock
  2. Investing all money in one mutual fund
  3. Dividing a portfolio among asset classes such as equity, debt and gold according to goals and risk profile
  4. Timing the market to buy at the bottom
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Dividing a portfolio among asset classes such as equity, debt and gold according to goals and risk profile

Asset allocation is the mix across asset classes and is a key determinant of portfolio risk and return.

Question 15 · Financial Asset Classes

Government securities issued by the Central Government are generally considered to have:

  1. High credit risk and no interest-rate risk
  2. Negligible credit (default) risk but exposure to interest-rate risk
  3. No risk of any kind
  4. Equity-like risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Negligible credit (default) risk but exposure to interest-rate risk

G-secs are sovereign-backed, so default risk is minimal, but their prices change with interest rates.
