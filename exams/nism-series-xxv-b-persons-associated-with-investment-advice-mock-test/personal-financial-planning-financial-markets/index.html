Personal Financial Planning & Financial Markets: NISM Series XXV-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Personal Financial Planning & Financial Markets, from the NISM Series XXV-B (Persons Associated with Investment Advice (Sales and Other Non-Core Services)) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces personal financial planning and the role of financial markets in meeting individual goals. It covers the planning process (gathering data, setting goals, analysing the current position, recommending, implementing and reviewing), life-cycle needs, budgeting and emergency funds, risk profiling (risk tolerance versus risk capacity), insurance and retirement needs, and the structure of financial markets and regulators (SEBI, RBI, IRDAI, PFRDA). For persons associated with investment advice in sales roles, the focus is understanding the process clearly enough to support advisers and explain it to clients.

Personal Financial Planning & Financial Markets questions

Question 1

A client earns ₹1.2 lakh a month and spends ₹80,000. A common guideline is to hold 6 months of expenses as an emergency fund. The target fund is:

  1. ₹7.2 lakh
  2. ₹2.4 lakh
  3. ₹6.0 lakh
  4. ₹4.8 lakh
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹4.8 lakh

Emergency funds are based on expenses, not income: 6 × ₹80,000 = ₹4.8 lakh. Using income would give ₹7.2 lakh, which overstates the need.

Question 2

Which regulator oversees insurance companies and insurance intermediaries in India?

  1. IRDAI
  2. The Securities and Exchange Board of India through its investment adviser department
  3. The Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority
  4. The Reserve Bank of India through its banking ombudsman
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. IRDAI

The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India regulates insurers and insurance intermediaries. SEBI regulates securities markets, PFRDA regulates pensions and RBI regulates banks.

Question 3

A client's net worth is calculated as:

  1. Total assets minus total liabilities
  2. Annual income minus annual taxes paid to the government
  3. Total assets plus the value of all outstanding loans
  4. Monthly savings multiplied by the number of working years left
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Total assets minus total liabilities

Net worth shows what the client would have if all assets were sold and all debts repaid. It is a key starting point for analysing the client's current financial position.

Question 4

A 30-year-old wants ₹50 lakh for his child's education in 15 years. In financial planning terms, this is best classified as a:

  1. Short-term goal that should be funded through a savings account
  2. Liquidity need that must be met within the next six months
  3. Long-term goal
  4. Risk tolerance measure based on psychology
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Long-term goal

Goals more than about seven to ten years away are long-term, which generally allows a greater share of growth assets. The horizon shapes the investment approach.

Question 5

Why is adequate term insurance usually addressed before investing in equity for a young earning parent?

  1. Term insurance gives higher returns than equity over all periods
  2. It protects dependants if the earner dies prematurely
  3. Equity investments are prohibited until insurance is purchased
  4. Term insurance returns all premiums with a large bonus
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. It protects dependants if the earner dies prematurely

Protecting dependants' income is a foundation of planning. Pure term cover provides a large sum assured at low cost; it is protection, not an investment.

Question 6

In the financial planning process, the step after implementing recommendations is:

  1. Monitoring and periodic review
  2. Collecting the client's KYC documents for the first time
  3. Setting the client's goals and objectives for the first time
  4. Closing the client relationship permanently
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Monitoring and periodic review

Plans must be reviewed as markets, laws and the client's life change. Data gathering and goal setting come at the start of the process.

Question 7

A client with high income, large savings and no debts, but who loses sleep over small market falls, has:

  1. High risk capacity but low risk tolerance
  2. Low risk capacity and high risk tolerance
  3. High risk tolerance and high risk capacity
  4. No need for any risk profiling
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. High risk capacity but low risk tolerance

Risk capacity is the financial ability to bear losses, while risk tolerance is the emotional willingness. Advice should respect the lower of the two.

Question 8

Which regulator administers the National Pension System?

  1. SEBI, through the stock exchanges and depositories
  2. PFRDA
  3. IRDAI, through the life insurance companies
  4. The Ministry of Corporate Affairs, through the RoC
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. PFRDA

The Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority regulates NPS and pension fund managers. Other regulators oversee securities, insurance and companies.
