Interest Rate Derivatives: NISM Series IV practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Interest Rate Derivatives, from the NISM Series IV (Interest Rate Derivatives) syllabus.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains the main OTC and exchange-traded interest rate derivatives: forward rate agreements, interest rate swaps (including overnight indexed swaps), and bond and T-bill futures. Focus on how each contract is settled, who gains when rates rise or fall, and how futures are priced using cost of carry. For bond futures, understand the deliverable basket, conversion factor, basis and cheapest-to-deliver logic. Know that swap notionals are not exchanged and that a fairly priced swap has zero value at inception.

Interest Rate Derivatives questions

Question 1

In an overnight indexed swap (OIS), the floating leg payment is usually calculated by:

  1. Using the 3-month term rate fixed once at the start of the reset period
  2. Averaging the 10-year G-sec yield across the period
  3. Compounding the daily overnight reference rate over the period
  4. Taking only the overnight rate on the payment date
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Compounding the daily overnight reference rate over the period

In an OIS the floating leg reflects the overnight rate compounded for every day of the reset period. In India the common benchmark is the overnight MIBOR, so the floating leg tracks actual daily funding costs.

Question 2

A company has issued fixed-rate debentures but now expects interest rates to fall. Through an interest rate swap, it can benefit from falling rates by:

  1. Receiving fixed and paying floating
  2. Paying fixed and receiving floating
  3. Paying fixed and receiving fixed
  4. Buying a forward rate agreement
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Receiving fixed and paying floating

Receiving fixed offsets its fixed coupon outflow, leaving it effectively paying floating. If rates fall, its floating payments drop. Paying fixed would lock in the high cost, which suits a borrower fearing rising rates.

Question 3

A '3×9 FRA' refers to an agreement covering:

  1. A 9-month period starting 3 months from today
  2. A 6-month period starting 3 months from today
  3. A 3-month period starting 9 months from today
  4. A 12-month period starting 3 months from today
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A 6-month period starting 3 months from today

FRA notation gives the start and end months from today. A 3×9 FRA starts in 3 months and ends in 9 months, so it fixes the rate for a 6-month period beginning three months ahead.

Question 4

A deliverable bond's dirty price is ₹100. The repo (financing) rate is 6% p.a. and the bond earns coupon income at 7% p.a. Ignoring compounding, what is the theoretical 3-month futures price?

  1. ₹100.25
  2. ₹101.50
  3. ₹98.25
  4. ₹99.75
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹99.75

Futures = spot + financing cost − coupon income = 100 + (100 × 6% × 0.25) − (100 × 7% × 0.25) = 100 + 1.50 − 1.75 = ₹99.75. Because carry is positive, futures trade below spot.

Question 5

In a plain vanilla rupee interest rate swap, what is exchanged between the parties at the start of the contract?

  1. Nothing; the notional amount is not exchanged
  2. The full notional amount, paid by each party to the other
  3. The first year's interest paid in advance
  4. A margin equal to 10% of the notional amount
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Nothing; the notional amount is not exchanged

The notional only serves to calculate interest payments. In a single-currency IRS it is never exchanged, and on each payment date only the net difference between fixed and floating interest changes hands.

Question 6

At inception, the value of a fairly priced interest rate swap to each counterparty is:

  1. Equal to the notional amount
  2. Zero
  3. Equal to one fixed-leg payment
  4. Positive for the fixed payer only
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Zero

The fixed rate in a fair swap is set so that the present value of the fixed leg equals the present value of the expected floating leg. Value arises later only as market rates move away from the contract rate.

Question 7

Which of the following is NOT an interest rate derivative?

  1. 10-year G-sec futures
  2. 91-day T-bill futures
  3. An overnight indexed swap
  4. USD-INR currency futures
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. USD-INR currency futures

Currency futures derive value from an exchange rate, not an interest rate. G-sec futures, T-bill futures and OIS all derive their value from interest rates or interest-bearing instruments.

Question 8

In bond futures, the 'gross basis' of a deliverable bond is defined as:

  1. Futures price minus the bond's coupon rate
  2. Conversion factor minus the bond's yield
  3. Cash price minus (futures price × conversion factor)
  4. Futures price multiplied by the accrued interest
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Cash price minus (futures price × conversion factor)

Gross basis compares the bond's cash price with its adjusted futures price (futures × conversion factor). It reflects carry and the delivery option; the bond with the lowest net basis is usually the cheapest to deliver.
