Accounting and Taxation: NISM Series IV practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Accounting and Taxation, from the NISM Series IV (Interest Rate Derivatives) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how interest rate derivative positions are recorded in books of accounts and how profits are taxed. Revise the treatment of initial margin and option premium, recognition of mark-to-market gains and losses, the difference between fair value hedges and cash flow hedges under Ind AS 109, and the principle-based hedge-effectiveness test. On taxation, focus on why eligible exchange-traded derivative trades are treated as non-speculative business income, the documentation required, and how losses can be carried forward. Avoid memorising section numbers, which changed with the new Income-tax Act.

Accounting and Taxation questions

Question 1

Initial margin paid to the broker for an IRF position is best recorded in the books as:

  1. An asset, being a deposit recoverable from the broker
  2. An expense charged to profit and loss immediately
  3. A liability payable to the clearing corporation
  4. Part of share capital of the business
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. An asset, being a deposit recoverable from the broker

Initial margin is money placed with the broker or clearing member as security and is refundable when positions close, so it is shown as a current asset, not as an expense.

Question 2

A bank hedges the change in fair value of its fixed-rate bond holdings by selling interest rate futures. Under Ind AS 109, this is a:

  1. Cash flow hedge
  2. Net investment hedge
  3. Fair value hedge
  4. Speculative position
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Fair value hedge

A fair value hedge protects against changes in the value of a recognised asset or liability. Hedging the price of fixed-rate bonds against rate moves is a classic fair value hedge.

Question 3

A company uses an IRS to fix the interest it will pay on a floating-rate loan. Under a cash flow hedge, the effective portion of the swap's fair value change is recognised in:

  1. Retained earnings directly
  2. Share premium account
  3. Revenue from operations
  4. Other comprehensive income
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Other comprehensive income

In a cash flow hedge, the effective part of the derivative's gain or loss is taken to OCI (cash flow hedge reserve) and later reclassified to profit or loss when the hedged interest affects earnings.

Question 4

A business loss from eligible exchange-traded interest rate derivative trading, treated as non-speculative, can be carried forward for up to:

  1. 4 assessment years
  2. 8 assessment years
  3. 2 assessment years
  4. Only the same year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 8 assessment years

Non-speculative business losses can be carried forward for up to eight assessment years and set off against business income. Speculative losses have a shorter carry-forward period.

Question 5

For a derivative trade on a recognised exchange to be treated as non-speculative for tax purposes, it must be supported by:

  1. An auditor's certificate issued for each trade
  2. A bank statement showing a debit of the full notional
  3. A time-stamped contract note with the client's unique code and PAN
  4. A demat statement showing delivery of the bond
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A time-stamped contract note with the client's unique code and PAN

Tax law treats eligible derivative transactions on recognised exchanges as non-speculative only when they are carried out through a registered broker and supported by a time-stamped contract note showing unique client identity and PAN.

Question 6

When a buyer pays premium for an interest rate option, the premium is initially recorded as:

  1. Income in the profit and loss account
  2. A reduction in the bank loan balance
  3. Part of the borrower's equity reserve
  4. An asset, until the option is exercised or expires
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. An asset, until the option is exercised or expires

The premium buys a right, so it is carried as an asset (measured at fair value). It is adjusted on exercise, sale or expiry, with any loss reflected in profit or loss.

Question 7

Under Ind AS 109, hedge effectiveness is assessed mainly by checking that:

  1. Hedge results stay within a fixed 80%–125% offset range at every single reporting date
  2. An economic relationship exists and the hedge ratio reflects actual risk management
  3. The hedging instrument is always traded on an exchange
  4. The hedge produces a profit in every reporting period
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. An economic relationship exists and the hedge ratio reflects actual risk management

Ind AS 109 dropped the rigid 80–125% test of the older standard. Instead it requires an economic relationship, credit risk not dominating value changes, and a hedge ratio consistent with actual risk management.

Question 8

A trader closes out an IRF position held for trading purposes. The cumulative gain on the position is recognised:

  1. In profit and loss
  2. Directly in share capital
  3. As a contingent liability
  4. Only when the trader files a tax return
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. In profit and loss

Futures held for trading are measured at fair value through profit or loss, so gains and losses flow into the statement of profit and loss, both daily through MTM and on final close-out.
