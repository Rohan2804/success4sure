Code of Conduct & Investor Protection: NISM Series IV practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Code of Conduct & Investor Protection, from the NISM Series IV (Interest Rate Derivatives) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter sets out the conduct expected from trading members who deal with clients in interest rate derivatives, and the protections available to investors. Revise KYC and risk disclosure duties, issue of contract notes, segregation and running-account settlement of client funds, prohibition on guaranteed returns and front-running, suitability for unsophisticated clients, and grievance and compensation routes such as SCORES, online dispute resolution and the exchange Investor Protection Fund. Most questions are scenario-based: identify the correct, ethical action for the broker.

Code of Conduct & Investor Protection questions

Question 1

Before a client starts trading in exchange-traded derivatives, the trading member must provide the client with:

  1. A guarantee that losses will be limited
  2. The Risk Disclosure Document explaining derivative risks
  3. A copy of the exchange's annual report
  4. The trading member's own income tax return
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The Risk Disclosure Document explaining derivative risks

Exchanges and SEBI require members to give clients a Risk Disclosure Document covering leverage, margin calls and possible losses, so the client understands derivative risks before trading.

Question 2

A trading member must issue a contract note for executed trades to the client within:

  1. 7 working days of the trade
  2. The end of the calendar month
  3. The expiry date of the contract
  4. 24 hours of the trade
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 24 hours of the trade

Contract notes, physical or electronic, must reach the client within 24 hours of execution. They record trade details and charges and serve as legal evidence of the transaction.

Question 3

Running-account settlement of an active client's unused funds by the broker must be done:

  1. Periodically, at least quarterly or monthly as the client opts
  2. Only when the client finally closes the trading account with the broker
  3. Once every two years after an audit
  4. Only if the client files a written complaint
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Periodically, at least quarterly or monthly as the client opts

SEBI requires brokers to settle unused client funds on a periodic basis according to the client's choice of monthly or quarterly settlement, so client money does not stay idle with the broker.

Question 4

A dealer tells a client, 'This IRF strategy cannot lose money; I guarantee 12% a year.' This is:

  1. Acceptable if the client is a bank
  2. Acceptable if given in writing
  3. A violation of the code of conduct
  4. Required to help the client decide
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A violation of the code of conduct

Brokers must not guarantee returns or make misleading statements about derivatives, which can always cause losses. Such assurances violate the SEBI code of conduct for stock brokers.

Question 5

A dealer learns of a large client order to sell bond futures and first sells for the firm's own account. This practice is called:

  1. Hedging
  2. Arbitrage
  3. Netting
  4. Front-running
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Front-running

Trading ahead of a known client order to profit from its price impact is front-running. It misuses confidential client information and is prohibited.

Question 6

The Investor Protection Fund (IPF) of a stock exchange is used mainly to:

  1. Pay eligible claims of investors against a defaulting trading member
  2. Make good the market losses suffered by investors on their open trading positions
  3. Fund the exchange's advertising campaigns
  4. Pay incentives to brokers with high volumes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Pay eligible claims of investors against a defaulting trading member

When a trading member is declared a defaulter, the IPF compensates eligible investor claims up to set limits. It does not cover losses from adverse market movements.

Question 7

A broker uses money lying in Client A's account to meet Client B's margin call. This is:

  1. Permitted if both clients use the same branch
  2. Permitted for up to three working days
  3. Not permitted, as each client's funds must be used only for that client
  4. Permitted when the broker is a clearing member
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Not permitted, as each client's funds must be used only for that client

Client funds are held for that client's own obligations. Using one client's funds for another, or for the broker's own purposes, breaches SEBI rules on segregation and monitoring of client funds.

Question 8

A retired client with no derivatives experience asks to take large leveraged IRF positions after hearing a tip. The broker's most appropriate step is to:

  1. Execute the order immediately, since the client has clearly asked for it in writing
  2. Explain the risks, assess suitability and record the advice given
  3. Refuse to open any account for the client
  4. Promise to reverse any losses within a week
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Explain the risks, assess suitability and record the advice given

Brokers should ensure the client understands leverage and margin calls, check whether the product fits the client's profile and keep records. Refusing outright or blindly executing are both poor practice.
