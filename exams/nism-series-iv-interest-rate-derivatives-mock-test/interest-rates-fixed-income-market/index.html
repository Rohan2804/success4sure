What this chapter covers

This chapter builds the base for every interest rate derivative: how bonds and money market instruments are priced, how yields and prices move in opposite directions, and how duration and convexity measure that sensitivity. Revise the main Indian instruments (G-secs, T-bills, CDs, CPs, TREPS and repo), the shape of the yield curve, clean versus dirty price, and the relationship between coupon, current yield and yield to maturity. Practise small calculations such as zero-coupon pricing and duration-based price change, because they reappear in the hedging chapters.

Interest Rates & Fixed Income Market questions

Question 1

A zero-coupon bond with face value ₹100 matures in exactly 2 years. If the annual yield is 8% (annual compounding), what is its price?

  1. ₹84.00
  2. ₹85.73
  3. ₹92.59
  4. ₹86.21
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹85.73

Price = 100 / (1.08)^2 = 100 / 1.1664 = ₹85.73. ₹84.00 wrongly deducts simple interest of 16%, while ₹92.59 discounts for only one year.

Question 2

An inverted yield curve means that:

  1. Long-term yields are higher than short-term yields
  2. Yields are the same for every maturity bucket
  3. Corporate bond yields are below G-sec yields
  4. Short-term yields are higher than long-term yields
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Short-term yields are higher than long-term yields

An inverted curve slopes downward: short-tenor yields exceed long-tenor yields. It often signals expectations of falling rates or slower growth. A normal curve slopes upward.

Question 3

Which money market instrument is issued by scheduled commercial banks to raise short-term funds?

  1. Certificate of Deposit
  2. Commercial Paper
  3. Treasury Bill
  4. Cash Management Bill
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Certificate of Deposit

Certificates of Deposit are negotiable money market instruments issued by banks (and select financial institutions). Commercial Paper is issued by companies, while T-bills and Cash Management Bills are issued for the Government of India.

Question 4

Which of these bonds will have the highest duration, assuming all yields are similar?

  1. A 10-year bond paying a 9% coupon
  2. A 5-year zero-coupon bond
  3. A 10-year zero-coupon bond
  4. A 10-year floating rate bond
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A 10-year zero-coupon bond

A zero-coupon bond's duration equals its maturity, so the 10-year zero has duration of about 10 years. A coupon bond of the same maturity has lower duration, and a floating rate bond resets close to market, so its duration is very short.

Question 5

The price a buyer actually pays for a bond bought between two coupon dates is the:

  1. Clean price, i.e. the quoted screen price without any adjustment
  2. Face value adjusted for the coupon rate
  3. Clean price minus the accrued interest
  4. Dirty price, i.e. clean price plus accrued interest
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Dirty price, i.e. clean price plus accrued interest

Bonds in India are quoted at clean price, but settlement happens at the dirty (full) price, which adds interest accrued since the last coupon date. The seller is thus paid for the interest earned while holding the bond.

Question 6

In which money market segment do participants borrow and lend against government securities through a tri-party arrangement, with CCIL as the central counterparty?

  1. TREPS
  2. Call money market
  3. Notice money market
  4. Commercial paper market
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. TREPS

TREPS (Tri-party Repo Dealing and Settlement) is a collateralised segment where CCIL acts as the tri-party agent and central counterparty. Call and notice money are uncollateralised interbank loans.

Question 7

An 8% coupon bond is trading at ₹104 (face value ₹100). Which relationship among its yields is correct?

  1. Coupon rate < current yield < YTM
  2. Current yield < YTM < coupon rate
  3. YTM < current yield < coupon rate
  4. YTM = current yield = coupon rate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. YTM < current yield < coupon rate

A bond priced at a premium has a current yield (8/104 = 7.69%) below its coupon, and its YTM is lower still because the ₹4 premium is lost by maturity. For discount bonds the order reverses.

Question 8

A bond has a modified duration of 7 and convexity of 60. If its yield falls by 100 basis points, its approximate percentage price change is:

  1. +6.70%
  2. +7.30%
  3. +7.00%
  4. +7.60%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. +7.30%

Change ≈ −MD × Δy + ½ × convexity × (Δy)² = −7 × (−0.01) + 0.5 × 60 × 0.0001 = 7% + 0.30% = +7.30%. Ignoring convexity gives 7.00%, which understates the gain.
