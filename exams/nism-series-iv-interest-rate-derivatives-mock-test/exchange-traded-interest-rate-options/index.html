Exchange Traded Interest Rate Options: NISM Series IV practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Exchange Traded Interest Rate Options, from the NISM Series IV (Interest Rate Derivatives) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers options on interest rates and on bond prices or bond futures, along with caps, floors, collars and swaptions. The key idea is direction: bond-price calls gain when rates fall, while bond-price puts and interest rate caps gain when rates rise. Revise intrinsic and time value, break-even for buyers, the payoff of a caplet, and the net cost of a collar. Remember that option buyers pay a premium and have limited loss, while writers face larger risk and therefore post margins.

Exchange Traded Interest Rate Options questions

Question 1

A trader holds a put option on bond futures. The option gains value when interest rates:

  1. Fall, because bond futures prices rise
  2. Stay unchanged until expiry
  3. Fall, because put premiums move with rates
  4. Rise, because bond futures prices fall
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Rise, because bond futures prices fall

Bond prices move opposite to yields. When rates rise, bond futures fall, so a put on bond futures moves into the money. A call on bond futures is the instrument that gains when rates fall.

Question 2

A buyer pays a premium of ₹1.20 for a call option on a bond with a strike price of ₹100 (per ₹100 face value). Ignoring costs, the break-even price at expiry is:

  1. ₹98.80
  2. ₹101.20
  3. ₹100.00
  4. ₹102.40
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹101.20

A call buyer breaks even when the underlying price equals strike plus premium: 100 + 1.20 = ₹101.20. Below that the option does not recover its cost. ₹98.80 is the break-even for a put buyer.

Question 3

A borrower's cap has a strike of 7% on a notional of ₹10 crore. For a quarterly period (0.25 year) the reference rate is set at 8%. The caplet payoff is:

  1. ₹10,00,000
  2. ₹25,00,000
  3. ₹2,50,000
  4. ₹25,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹2,50,000

Payoff = notional × (reference rate − cap rate) × period = ₹10 crore × 1% × 0.25 = ₹2,50,000. ₹10 lakh ignores the quarterly period fraction.

Question 4

A put option on bond futures has a strike of 99.00 while the futures price is 97.50. Its intrinsic value is:

  1. 1.50
  2. 0.00
  3. 97.50
  4. 3.00
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 1.50

Put intrinsic value = strike − underlying price, if positive: 99.00 − 97.50 = 1.50. Any premium above 1.50 is time value. A call with the same strike would have zero intrinsic value.

Question 5

Which of these options on bond futures has zero intrinsic value?

  1. A call whose strike is below the futures price
  2. A call whose strike is above the futures price
  3. A put whose strike is above the futures price
  4. A deep in-the-money put close to expiry
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A call whose strike is above the futures price

A call has intrinsic value only when the underlying price exceeds the strike. With the strike above the futures price the call is out of the money, so its whole premium is time value.

Question 6

A 'payer swaption' gives its holder the right, but not the obligation, to:

  1. Enter into a swap receiving the fixed rate
  2. Cancel an existing floating-rate loan
  3. Enter into a swap paying the fixed rate
  4. Buy a bond at a fixed future price
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Enter into a swap paying the fixed rate

A swaption is an option on an interest rate swap. A payer swaption lets the buyer pay fixed (and receive floating), which is valuable if rates rise. A receiver swaption gives the right to receive fixed.

Question 7

On exchange-traded interest rate options, margins are mainly collected from:

  1. Option writers, since buyers pay the full premium upfront
  2. Option buyers, since writers have already received the premium in cash
  3. Both sides equally, as a fixed share of notional
  4. Neither side, because options are fully pre-paid
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Option writers, since buyers pay the full premium upfront

A buyer's maximum loss is the premium already paid, so no further margin is needed. A writer's potential loss is large, so the clearing corporation collects margins from the seller to cover adverse moves.

Question 8

A borrower buys a cap at 7.5% for a premium of 0.40% and sells a floor at 5.5% for a premium of 0.30% on the same notional. The net upfront cost of this collar is:

  1. 0.70% of notional
  2. 0.40% of notional
  3. 0.30% of notional
  4. 0.10% of notional
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 0.10% of notional

Net cost = premium paid − premium received = 0.40% − 0.30% = 0.10%. The floor income lowers the cost of protection, in exchange for giving up gains if rates fall below 5.5%.
