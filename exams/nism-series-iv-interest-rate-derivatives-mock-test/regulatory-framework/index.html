Regulatory Framework: NISM Series IV practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Regulatory Framework, from the NISM Series IV (Interest Rate Derivatives) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter maps which authority regulates what in India's interest rate derivatives market. RBI frames the overall policy for rupee interest rate derivatives, including OTC products, user and market-maker categories and FPI limits, while SEBI regulates stock exchanges, clearing corporations and brokers on which exchange-traded IRDs are traded. Revise the key statutes (RBI Act, SCRA, SEBI Act), the suitability duties towards retail users, and SEBI regulations against fraudulent and unfair trade practices. Questions are usually conceptual rather than numeric.

Regulatory Framework questions

Question 1

Stock exchanges and clearing corporations on which interest rate futures are traded are regulated by:

  1. Reserve Bank of India
  2. Ministry of Corporate Affairs
  3. Clearing Corporation of India Ltd
  4. SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. SEBI

SEBI regulates market infrastructure institutions such as stock exchanges and clearing corporations, including their exchange-traded interest rate derivative segments, while RBI sets the broader policy for IRDs.

Question 2

RBI's power to regulate interest rate derivatives comes mainly from:

  1. The Companies Act, 2013
  2. Chapter III-D of the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934
  3. The Depositories Act, 1996
  4. The Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Chapter III-D of the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934

Chapter III-D of the RBI Act gives RBI powers over interest rate derivatives, money market instruments and related transactions. RBI's Rupee Interest Rate Derivatives Directions are issued under these powers.

Question 3

Under RBI's framework for OTC rupee IRDs, a corporate that enters into a swap with a bank purely to hedge its loan is classified as a:

  1. Market-maker
  2. Clearing member
  3. User
  4. Primary dealer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. User

RBI divides participants into market-makers (who quote prices, such as banks and primary dealers) and users (who use derivatives to hedge or manage risk). A corporate hedging its loan is a user.

Question 4

Before offering an OTC interest rate derivative to a retail user, a market-maker must:

  1. Carry out a suitability and appropriateness assessment
  2. Obtain prior written approval from SEBI for each individual transaction
  3. Guarantee the user a minimum return
  4. Ensure the user holds a demat account with CDSL
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Carry out a suitability and appropriateness assessment

RBI requires market-makers to assess whether the product suits the retail user's needs and understanding, and to disclose risks clearly. This protects less sophisticated users from unsuitable products.

Question 5

Spreading false information to move bond futures prices for personal gain would violate:

  1. SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations
  2. SEBI (Prohibition of Fraudulent and Unfair Trade Practices) Regulations
  3. The Foreign Exchange Management Act's current account rules
  4. The Banking Regulation Act's licensing provisions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. SEBI (Prohibition of Fraudulent and Unfair Trade Practices) Regulations

The PFUTP Regulations prohibit manipulative and deceptive practices in the securities market, including spreading misleading information to influence prices of exchange-traded derivatives.

Question 6

An entity wishing to deal for clients in exchange-traded interest rate futures must be:

  1. Licensed by RBI as a scheduled commercial bank with a treasury desk
  2. Registered with IRDAI as a corporate agent
  3. Registered with SEBI as a stock broker and be an exchange member
  4. Approved by AMFI as a distributor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Registered with SEBI as a stock broker and be an exchange member

Trading members in the exchange IRD segment are stock brokers registered with SEBI and admitted as members of the exchange. Bank status or AMFI registration is not what permits trading for clients.

Question 7

Investment limits for foreign portfolio investors in exchange-traded interest rate futures are prescribed by:

  1. RBI
  2. SEBI alone
  3. The stock exchange board
  4. The Ministry of Corporate Affairs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. RBI

Because IRF positions relate to the government debt market and capital flows, RBI sets the limits for FPI participation. SEBI and exchanges then implement monitoring of those limits.

Question 8

Under the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956, a contract for differences or an instrument deriving value from an underlying is treated as a:

  1. Negotiable instrument
  2. Deposit
  3. Commodity
  4. Security
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Security

The SCRA's definition of 'securities' includes derivatives. This is why exchange-traded derivatives, including interest rate futures, fall within SEBI's regulatory framework.
