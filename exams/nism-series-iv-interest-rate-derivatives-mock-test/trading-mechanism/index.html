Trading Mechanism: NISM Series IV practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Trading Mechanism, from the NISM Series IV (Interest Rate Derivatives) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how interest rate futures and options are traded on Indian exchanges: contract specifications, how prices are quoted, order types and their conditions, matching rules, client codes and the roles of trading and clearing members. Revise how T-bill futures are quoted, what happens to time priority when an order is modified, how open interest changes, and the difference between trading members and professional clearing members. Expect short scenario questions on order behaviour and open interest.

Trading Mechanism questions

Question 1

On NSE, 91-day T-bill futures are quoted as:

  1. The discount yield expressed directly in %
  2. 100 minus the annualised discount yield
  3. The face value of the T-bill in rupees
  4. The clean price of the T-bill per ₹1,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 100 minus the annualised discount yield

T-bill futures are quoted as 100 minus the futures discount yield, so the quote rises when yields fall. This keeps the usual futures behaviour of price moving opposite to rates.

Question 2

A sell stop-loss order on IRF with a trigger price of 98.50 becomes active when:

  1. The market price rises to 98.50 or higher
  2. The trading session ends at the closing bell
  3. Open interest crosses a preset limit
  4. The market price falls to 98.50 or lower
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The market price falls to 98.50 or lower

A sell stop-loss is used to cap losses on a long position. It stays inactive until the price drops to the trigger, at which point it is released as a limit or market order.

Question 3

An order placed without any price limit that executes at the best price available in the order book is a:

  1. Market order
  2. Limit order
  3. Stop-loss order
  4. Good-till-cancelled order
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Market order

A market order seeks immediate execution at the best available price. It offers certainty of execution but not of price. A limit order sets the worst acceptable price.

Question 4

A trader changes the price of a pending limit order in the order book. What happens to its time priority?

  1. It keeps its original time priority
  2. It gets priority over all orders at that price
  3. It loses its original time priority
  4. It is automatically converted to a market order
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. It loses its original time priority

Under price-time priority, a modification of price (or an increase in quantity) is treated as a fresh order and receives a new time stamp. This keeps the queue fair for orders already waiting.

Question 5

Every client trading in exchange-traded IRDs must be identified on the trading system through a:

  1. Demat account number of the trading member
  2. Bank account number of the clearing member
  3. Contract note number issued after trading
  4. Unique Client Code linked to the client's PAN
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Unique Client Code linked to the client's PAN

Exchanges require every order to carry a Unique Client Code mapped to the client's KYC details, including PAN. This enables surveillance, position monitoring and correct allocation of trades.

Question 6

A Professional Clearing Member (PCM) in the derivatives segment:

  1. Clears and settles trades of trading members but does not trade
  2. Trades on its own account and for clients but cannot clear any trades
  3. Only provides price quotes as a market maker
  4. Manages investor grievances for the exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Clears and settles trades of trading members but does not trade

A PCM is a clearing-only entity, typically a bank or custodian, that clears and settles trades executed by trading members tied up with it. It does not execute trades on the exchange.

Question 7

Open interest in a futures contract represents:

  1. The total number of contracts bought and sold during the whole trading day
  2. The number of clients holding a demat account
  3. The number of outstanding contracts not yet closed or settled
  4. The total margin deposited with the clearing corporation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The number of outstanding contracts not yet closed or settled

Open interest counts positions still open at the end of a period. Volume counts all trades in the period, including those that open and close positions on the same day.

Question 8

Trader X opens a new long position of 10 contracts. The seller, Y, is closing out an existing long position of 10 contracts. What happens to open interest?

  1. It rises by 10 contracts
  2. It remains unchanged
  3. It falls by 10 contracts
  4. It rises by 20 contracts
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. It remains unchanged

One side opens and the other closes, so the open position simply passes from Y to X. Open interest rises only when both sides open new positions and falls when both sides close.
