Clearing, Settlement & Risk Management: NISM Series IV practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Clearing, Settlement & Risk Management, from the NISM Series IV (Interest Rate Derivatives) syllabus.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how the clearing corporation guarantees exchange-traded interest rate derivative trades through novation, margins and default resources. Revise the types of margin (initial, extreme loss, calendar spread and mark-to-market), how daily MTM profit and loss is calculated, the role of clearing members and clearing banks, position limits, and the default waterfall including the Settlement Guarantee Fund. Understand cash settlement at expiry and practise simple MTM calculations using contract size and price quotes per ₹100.

Clearing, Settlement & Risk Management questions

Question 1

Initial margin on interest rate futures is generally computed using:

  1. A flat 50% of the contract's notional value
  2. The client's annual income declared in KYC
  3. A portfolio-based risk model such as SPAN
  4. The previous day's trading volume
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A portfolio-based risk model such as SPAN

Clearing corporations use portfolio-based models (SPAN-type methodology) that estimate the worst likely one-day loss across scenarios. The margin therefore reflects actual risk, with offsets for hedged positions.

Question 2

A trader buys 10 IRF contracts (face value ₹2 lakh, quoted per ₹100) at 99.00. The day's settlement price is 99.40. The mark-to-market for the day is:

  1. Gain of ₹8,000
  2. Gain of ₹800
  3. Loss of ₹8,000
  4. Gain of ₹80,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Gain of ₹8,000

MTM = 10 × 2,00,000 × (99.40 − 99.00) / 100 = ₹8,000. As a buyer, the trader gains when the settlement price rises. It is credited in daily settlement.

Question 3

A clearing member's main role in the derivatives segment is to:

  1. Design and list new interest rate futures contracts for the exchange
  2. Settle the obligations of trades executed by its trading members
  3. Set the policy repo rate for the market
  4. Issue government securities on behalf of RBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Settle the obligations of trades executed by its trading members

Clearing members meet funds settlement obligations with the clearing corporation for trades of their own and of the trading members whose trades they clear, and they collect margins from them.

Question 4

The Settlement Guarantee Fund (Core SGF) of a clearing corporation is used to:

  1. Pay brokerage to trading members
  2. Fund new product launches by the exchange
  3. Compensate investors for market losses
  4. Complete settlement when a member defaults
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Complete settlement when a member defaults

The Core SGF is a pool that ensures trades settle even if a clearing member fails to meet obligations. It is used after the defaulter's own margins and contributions in the default waterfall.

Question 5

Why is margin on a calendar spread position usually lower than on two outright positions?

  1. The long and short legs largely offset each other's risk
  2. Spread positions cannot make any loss
  3. Calendar spreads are not marked to market
  4. Clearing corporations ignore far-month contracts
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The long and short legs largely offset each other's risk

In a calendar spread the two legs move closely together, so the net risk is much smaller than that of two independent positions. Margin models recognise this with a lower spread charge.

Question 6

For a cash-settled IRF, the final settlement price at expiry is derived from:

  1. The last traded price of any equity index
  2. Prices or yields of the underlying security in the cash market
  3. The opening price of the contract on its first day
  4. A price chosen by the client before expiry
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Prices or yields of the underlying security in the cash market

Cash-settled contracts close by paying the difference between the trade price and a final settlement price, computed from prices or yields of the underlying G-sec or T-bill in the cash market.

Question 7

Position limits on interest rate futures for clients and members are mainly meant to:

  1. Guarantee a minimum daily profit to every participant in the contract
  2. Fix the maximum brokerage a member can charge
  3. Restrict trading to institutional investors only
  4. Prevent excessive concentration and market manipulation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Prevent excessive concentration and market manipulation

Position limits stop any single participant from building a dominant position that could distort prices or create settlement risk. They are a key part of the risk containment framework.

Question 8

A client fails to pay the mark-to-market loss on IRF positions. Who remains liable to the clearing corporation for the settlement obligation?

  1. The stock exchange on whose platform the trades were originally executed
  2. SEBI, as the market regulator
  3. The clearing member through whom the trades are cleared
  4. The issuer of the underlying government security
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The clearing member through whom the trades are cleared

The clearing corporation deals only with clearing members, who must meet obligations on time whether or not their clients pay. The member then recovers dues from the trading member or client.
