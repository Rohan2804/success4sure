Performance of Mutual Funds: NISM Series V-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Performance of Mutual Funds, from the NISM Series V-B (Mutual Fund Foundation) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains simple ways to judge how a mutual fund has performed. Revise how NAV changes and IDCW payouts make up total return, absolute versus annualised return, the role of benchmarks, comparing schemes only within the same category, and why expenses matter over long periods. Understand that past performance does not guarantee future results and that consistency over time is more meaningful than a single good year. Practise basic return calculations with whole numbers.

Performance of Mutual Funds questions

Question 1

A scheme's NAV rose from ₹40 to ₹46 in one year with no IDCW. What is the return?

  1. 6%
  2. 13%
  3. 46%
  4. 15%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 15%

Return = (46 − 40) / 40 × 100 = 15%. ₹6 is the gain in rupees, not the percentage, and 13% wrongly divides by the ending NAV.

Question 2

An investor bought a unit at ₹20, received an IDCW of ₹1, and the unit is now worth ₹21. What is the total return for the year?

  1. 5%
  2. 10%
  3. 15%
  4. 21%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 10%

Total return = (21 − 20 + 1) / 20 × 100 = 10%. Ignoring the IDCW would give only 5%, which understates what the investor earned.

Question 3

Which statement about past performance is correct?

  1. It guarantees at least the same return next year
  2. It is the only factor needed to choose a fund
  3. It does not guarantee future returns
  4. It is fixed by SEBI for every category
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. It does not guarantee future returns

Markets change and so do fund returns. Past performance is useful for judging consistency, but it is not a promise of future results, as every scheme document states.

Question 4

Two equity funds hold the same portfolio. Fund A has a TER of 0.5% and Fund B 1.5%. Over 20 years, Fund A will most likely:

  1. Deliver higher returns because of lower costs
  2. Deliver lower returns because cheap funds perform worse
  3. Deliver exactly the same returns as Fund B
  4. Be closed by SEBI for charging too little
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Deliver higher returns because of lower costs

With identical portfolios, the only difference is cost. A 1% lower expense each year compounds into a meaningful difference over 20 years.

Question 5

A scheme's benchmark is used to:

  1. Decide the investor's tax rate
  2. Judge if the manager added value
  3. Fix the exit load of the scheme
  4. Calculate the distributor's commission
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Judge if the manager added value

A benchmark represents the market the scheme invests in. Beating it after costs shows the manager added value; lagging it shows the opposite.

Question 6

Investment of ₹1,00,000 grew to ₹1,50,000 in 3 years. The absolute return is:

  1. 16.7%
  2. 14.5%
  3. 50%
  4. 150%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 50%

Absolute return = (1,50,000 − 1,00,000) / 1,00,000 = 50%. 16.7% is a simple yearly average, and 14.5% is the compounded annual rate, both different measures.

Question 7

Which comparison is appropriate when evaluating a large cap fund?

  1. Comparing it with large cap peers and index
  2. Comparing it with a liquid fund's return
  3. Comparing it with a bank savings rate only
  4. Comparing it with a gold ETF's return
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Comparing it with large cap peers and index

Fair comparison needs similar risk and objectives. A large cap fund should be compared with its category peers and a large cap benchmark.

Question 8

A fund that returned 40% last year but lost heavily in other years may be less attractive than one with steady returns because:

  1. High returns are not allowed under SEBI rules
  2. Steady funds are guaranteed by the AMC
  3. A 40% return is always taxed at a higher rate
  4. Consistency is a better guide than one year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Consistency is a better guide than one year

One outstanding year can be luck or due to a short-lived theme. Consistent performance across years and market conditions is a more reliable signal of quality.
