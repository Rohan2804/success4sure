Taxation: NISM Series V-B revision notes

Distributors are not tax advisers, but they must explain the basic tax treatment of the products they sell. V-B tests the equity-oriented definition, the holding period for long-term status, how IDCW is taxed and what STT is. Rates change with Budgets; rates below are as of 2026 and should be checked with the income tax department.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Equity-oriented fund

A scheme is equity-oriented for tax purposes if at least 65% of its assets are in equity shares of domestic listed companies. Most diversified equity funds, index funds on Indian indices, ELSS and arbitrage funds qualify, which gives them concessional capital gains treatment.

2. Holding period

Units of an equity-oriented fund become long-term capital assets when held for more than 12 months. Sell within 12 months and the gain is short-term. Holding periods are counted separately for each purchase, including every SIP instalment. Selling the oldest units first applies (first in, first out).

3. Capital gains rates

As of 2026, long-term gains on equity-oriented funds above ₹1.25 lakh in a financial year are taxed at 12.5%, while short-term gains are taxed at 20%. Debt-heavy funds bought after 1 April 2023 are taxed at the investor's slab rate. Check the current Finance Act before quoting rates.

4. Growth vs IDCW

Under the growth option, tax arises only when units are sold. IDCW payouts are added to the investor's income and taxed at slab rates in the year received, with TDS above a threshold. A high-bracket investor may pay more tax under IDCW.

5. Securities Transaction Tax

STT is a small tax charged on the sale (redemption) of equity-oriented fund units and on trades in equity ETFs on exchanges. It is collected automatically and is separate from capital gains tax. Debt fund redemptions do not attract STT.

6. ELSS tax benefit

ELSS investments qualify for a deduction within the overall limit for eligible investments, but only for taxpayers who choose the old tax regime. Under the new regime there is no deduction, though ELSS remains a regular equity fund with a three-year lock-in.

7. Switches are sales

Switching from one scheme to another, or from a regular plan to a direct plan of the same scheme, is treated as redeeming the old units and buying new ones. Capital gains tax and exit load can apply. Distributors should point this out before suggesting any switch.

8. Stamp duty

A stamp duty of 0.005% is charged on purchases of mutual fund units, including each SIP instalment. It is not an income tax, but it slightly reduces the number of units allotted. On a ₹10,000 SIP instalment it amounts to just 50 paise, and redemptions are not charged.

Common traps in the exam

  • Assuming equity fund units become long-term after 36 months - for equity-oriented funds the period is 12 months.
  • Treating a switch within the same fund house as tax-free; it is a redemption and fresh purchase.
  • Believing IDCW is tax-free in the investor's hands - it is taxed at slab rates.
  • Quoting today's tax rates as fixed; they change through Budgets.

Quick recap

Equity-oriented = at least 65% domestic equity; long-term after 12 months. LTCG above the annual exemption gets a concessional rate; STCG is higher. Debt funds bought after April 2023 are taxed at slab. IDCW is taxed at slab. STT applies on equity fund redemptions. ELSS deduction only in the old regime. Switches are taxable.
