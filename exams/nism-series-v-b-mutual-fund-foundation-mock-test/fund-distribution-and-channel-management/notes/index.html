Fund Distribution and Channel Management: NISM Series V-B revision notes

This chapter explains how a new cadre distributor starts and runs the business: certification, registration, how commission works, what must be disclosed and how to avoid mis-selling. Questions are practical - whom the cheque should be drawn to, where commission appears, or why a recommendation is unsuitable.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Getting started

To sell mutual funds and earn commission, an individual must pass the relevant NISM certification and register with AMFI to obtain an ARN. KYD (Know Your Distributor) verification is part of this. Employees of distribution firms who deal with investors need their own EUIN.

2. Renewal

The NISM certificate is valid for three years. Distributors must renew it through CPE or by passing the exam again, and keep the ARN valid; otherwise they cannot earn commission on new investments. A new cadre distributor can later upgrade by passing NISM V-A to sell the full range.

3. Trail commission

Distributors are paid trail commission, a small percentage of the investor's holding value, for as long as the money stays invested. Upfront commission out of scheme expenses is not allowed. This links the distributor's income to keeping clients invested and satisfied over years.

4. Commission disclosure

Investors can see the commission paid to their distributor on their holdings in the Consolidated Account Statement. Distributors must disclose commissions from the different schemes they recommend so the investor can judge any conflict of interest. This helps investors weigh the cost of advice and service.

5. Direct plan option

Every scheme has a direct plan with no distributor commission and therefore a lower expense ratio. Investors who prefer the distributor's guidance and service choose the regular plan, which includes the commission cost. Both plans hold exactly the same portfolio, so the direct plan's NAV gradually becomes higher than the regular plan's.

6. Suitability

Recommending a small cap fund to a risk-averse retiree because it gave high recent returns is mis-selling. A distributor must understand the client's age, income, goals and risk tolerance, and recommend only products that fit. Past returns should never be presented as a promise.

7. Payment rules

Investment cheques and online payments must be in favour of the scheme, never the distributor. Third-party payments are generally not accepted, with limited exceptions such as parents investing for a minor child. Cash investments are allowed only up to a small annual limit per investor.

8. Prohibited practices

Distributors must not rebate commission to attract clients, churn investors between schemes, give false information about schemes or forge signatures. AMCs can stop commission and AMFI can suspend or cancel the ARN for breaches. Such misconduct also damages trust in the industry as a whole.

Common traps in the exam

  • Thinking a cheque can be written to the distributor's firm for convenience - it must be in favour of the scheme.
  • Believing upfront commission from scheme expenses is still paid; trail commission is the norm.
  • Assuming the direct plan has a different portfolio from the regular plan; only the expense ratio differs.
  • Treating a new cadre ARN as permission to sell every type of scheme.

Quick recap

Pass NISM, get an ARN (employees need EUIN), renew every three years. Earn trail commission, visible to clients in the CAS. Direct plans carry no commission. Recommend only suitable products; never promise returns. Payments go to the scheme; no rebating or churning.
