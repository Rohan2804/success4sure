Investment Landscape: NISM Series V-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Investment Landscape, from the NISM Series V-B (Mutual Fund Foundation) syllabus.

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces basic investing ideas for first-time investors: why saving alone is not enough, how inflation reduces purchasing power, and the difference between saving and investing. It covers common asset classes (equity, debt, gold, real estate, cash), their typical risk and return, and core concepts such as liquidity, compounding, diversification and matching investments to goals and time horizons. Questions are simple and practical; practise compound interest and inflation calculations with small numbers.

Investment Landscape questions

Question 1

Money kept idle in a locker for 10 years while prices rise by about 6% a year will:

  1. Keep exactly the same purchasing power
  2. Lose purchasing power over time
  3. Grow in value at the rate of inflation
  4. Earn interest from the Reserve Bank
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Lose purchasing power over time

Cash earns nothing, so rising prices reduce what the same amount can buy. This is why savings need to be invested in assets that aim to beat inflation.

Question 2

Which asset class gives a part-owner's share in a company's profits and growth?

  1. Fixed deposits
  2. Government bonds
  3. Savings bank accounts
  4. Equity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Equity

Equity shares represent ownership, so shareholders share in profits and growth (and losses). Deposits and bonds are loans to the issuer that pay interest instead.

Question 3

If ₹50,000 is invested at 8% a year compounded annually, what is it worth after 2 years?

  1. ₹58,320
  2. ₹58,000
  3. ₹54,000
  4. ₹59,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹58,320

Value = 50,000 × 1.08 × 1.08 = ₹58,320. ₹58,000 is simple interest of ₹4,000 a year, which ignores interest earned on interest.

Question 4

Which of these generally offers the highest long-term return potential along with the highest short-term volatility?

  1. Bank fixed deposits of five years
  2. Treasury bills
  3. Equity shares
  4. Savings bank deposits
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Equity shares

Equity has historically offered the highest long-term growth but its prices swing sharply in the short run. Deposits and T-bills are more stable but offer lower returns.

Question 5

An investor needs money for a child's school fees in six months. Which feature matters most when choosing where to keep this money?

  1. Highest possible long-term growth
  2. Tax benefit under a long lock-in
  3. Exposure to small companies
  4. Safety and liquidity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Safety and liquidity

Short-term goals need money that will be available and not fall in value. Seeking high growth with volatile assets could leave a shortfall exactly when the fees are due.

Question 6

Gold is often added to a portfolio mainly because it:

  1. Tends to behave differently from equities and can hedge against uncertainty
  2. Pays a fixed rate of interest every year, like a government savings bond held to maturity
  3. Has no price risk at all
  4. Is fully guaranteed by the government
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Tends to behave differently from equities and can hedge against uncertainty

Gold often holds value during market stress or currency weakness, so it can diversify a portfolio. It pays no interest and its price can be volatile.

Question 7

'Do not put all your eggs in one basket' describes the principle of:

  1. Leverage
  2. Speculation
  3. Diversification
  4. Market timing
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Diversification

Diversification means spreading money across many investments so that a problem in one does not hurt the whole portfolio badly. It reduces risk specific to any single investment.

Question 8

The main difference between saving and investing is that investing:

  1. Keeps all the money only as cash at home or in a locker, so that it is always within reach
  2. Puts money into assets aiming for growth, accepting some risk
  3. Never involves any risk to capital
  4. Is meant only for very wealthy people
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Puts money into assets aiming for growth, accepting some risk

Saving sets aside money from income; investing deploys it into assets such as equity, debt or gold to earn returns, usually with some risk. Anyone can invest small amounts through SIPs.
