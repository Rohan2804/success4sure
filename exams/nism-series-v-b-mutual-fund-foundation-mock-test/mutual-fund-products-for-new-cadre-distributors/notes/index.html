Mutual Fund Products for New Cadre Distributors: NISM Series V-B revision notes

This chapter is unique to V-B. New cadre distributors may sell only a defined list of simple schemes, so the exam tests those products in detail: how index funds and ETFs work, why diversified equity is safer than sectoral funds, ELSS lock-in rules and basic debt options. Many questions describe an investor and ask which product suits them.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Who is a new cadre distributor

The simplified 'new cadre' category was created to widen distribution to smaller towns. Individuals such as retired government and bank officials, teachers and postal agents can qualify by passing NISM V-B and obtaining an ARN. They can distribute only the simple schemes on AMFI's eligible list - check AMFI for the current list.

2. Index funds

An index fund buys the stocks of an index, such as the Nifty 50, in the same proportions. It does not try to beat the index, only to track it. Costs are low because there is no stock research, and the risk of the fund manager picking wrong stocks is removed.

3. Exchange Traded Funds

ETFs also track an index but are listed on a stock exchange. Investors buy and sell them through a broker during market hours at market prices close to the indicative NAV, and need a demat and trading account. Market makers help keep the price close to NAV.

4. Tracking error

Passive funds do not match their index exactly because of expenses, cash held for redemptions and the time taken to rebalance. The variability of this gap is tracking error. Lower tracking error indicates better replication - an important comparison point between two index funds on the same index.

5. Diversified equity funds

Large cap, flexi cap and similar diversified funds spread money across many sectors. If banking shares fall, holdings in FMCG, IT or pharma cushion the impact. A sectoral fund that holds only banking shares would take the full hit, which is why diversified funds are considered less risky.

6. ELSS

Equity Linked Savings Schemes invest at least 80% in equity and carry a three-year lock-in. Under the old tax regime, investments qualify for a deduction within the overall limit for eligible savings. Each SIP instalment is locked in for three years from its own date: units bought in July 2024 become free in July 2027.

7. Gold ETFs and gold funds

A gold ETF invests in physical gold of standard purity and trades on the exchange, so investors avoid storage, purity worries and making charges on jewellery. A gold fund of funds invests in gold ETFs and lets investors use SIPs without a demat account.

8. Simple debt options

Overnight funds invest in one-day securities and liquid funds in instruments up to 91 days. They suit investors parking money for days or weeks, such as a business keeping cash for monthly salaries. Their returns track short-term interest rates and are not assured.

9. Matching product to investor

A young salaried saver wanting low-cost long-term equity exposure fits an index fund SIP; a taxpayer under the old regime wanting a deduction may consider ELSS; someone parking a bonus for two months suits a liquid fund. Sectoral or thematic funds are not suitable for first-time, risk-averse investors.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Tracking difference = Fund return - Index return (tracking error is the standard deviation of this difference over time)

Common traps in the exam

  • Thinking an index fund aims to beat the index; it only tries to match it at low cost.
  • Believing ETF units are bought from the AMC at end-of-day NAV like ordinary funds - retail investors trade them on the exchange during market hours.
  • Assuming an ELSS SIP's whole investment unlocks three years after the first instalment; each instalment has its own lock-in.
  • Calling a sectoral fund 'diversified' just because it holds 30 stocks - all are in one sector.

Quick recap

New cadre distributors sell only simple schemes on AMFI's list. Index funds and ETFs track an index at low cost; ETFs trade on exchanges and need demat. Tracking error measures how closely they follow. Diversified equity beats sectoral funds on risk. ELSS: 80% equity, three-year lock-in per instalment. Liquid and overnight funds park short-term cash.
