Mutual Fund Products for New Cadre Distributors: NISM Series V-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Mutual Fund Products for New Cadre Distributors, from the NISM Series V-B (Mutual Fund Foundation) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the simple mutual fund products that new cadre distributors mainly deal with: index funds and ETFs, diversified equity schemes, ELSS, and simple debt and hybrid options. Revise how each product works, its risk level, its suitability, and features such as ELSS lock-in, ETF trading on exchanges and passive tracking of an index. Understand concepts such as tracking error, market capitalisation categories and why diversified funds are less risky than sector funds. Questions often ask which product suits a described investor.

Mutual Fund Products for New Cadre Distributors questions

Question 1

An ETF's price on the stock exchange during the day is determined by:

  1. Buying and selling by investors on the exchange, close to its intraday value
  2. Only the NAV declared by the AMC at the end of the previous business day
  3. A fixed price set by SEBI for each year
  4. The AMC's decision each morning
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Buying and selling by investors on the exchange, close to its intraday value

ETF units trade on exchanges like shares, so their market price moves through the day with demand and supply. Market makers help keep it close to the indicative NAV.

Question 2

To buy units of an ETF on the exchange, an investor generally needs:

  1. Only a bank fixed deposit account
  2. A loan from the AMC
  3. A demat and trading account
  4. A Public Provident Fund account
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A demat and trading account

ETFs are listed securities, so buying them requires a trading account with a broker and a demat account to hold the units, just like shares.

Question 3

Which product would suit an investor who wants both a tax deduction under the old regime and long-term equity growth?

  1. An overnight fund
  2. A liquid fund
  3. A gold ETF
  4. An ELSS scheme
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. An ELSS scheme

ELSS is an equity scheme with a three-year lock-in that qualifies for a tax deduction under the old tax regime. Overnight, liquid and gold ETFs do not offer this deduction.

Question 4

An index fund's NAV moves slightly differently from its index mainly because of:

  1. The fund manager's stock-picking bets
  2. Expenses and cash holdings of the fund
  3. A guaranteed bonus paid by the AMC
  4. SEBI's daily adjustments to NAV
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Expenses and cash holdings of the fund

Index funds copy the index, but expenses, cash for redemptions and timing of trades cause small gaps known as tracking error. They do not involve active stock picking.

Question 5

A sectoral fund investing only in banking shares is riskier than a diversified equity fund because:

  1. It is not regulated by SEBI
  2. It cannot be redeemed for five years
  3. Its performance depends heavily on one sector
  4. It invests only in government bonds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Its performance depends heavily on one sector

Concentration in one sector means a downturn in that sector hits the whole portfolio. A diversified fund spreads investments across many sectors.

Question 6

Which of these is TRUE of a large cap fund?

  1. It invests only in companies ranked 251st and below by full market capitalisation
  2. It must hold at least 50% in bonds
  3. It guarantees returns higher than bank deposits
  4. It invests mainly in the 100 largest companies by market capitalisation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. It invests mainly in the 100 largest companies by market capitalisation

Large cap funds invest at least 80% in the top 100 companies by full market capitalisation, which are generally more established and less volatile than mid or small caps.

Question 7

An investor starts an ELSS SIP of ₹2,000 a month. When can the instalment invested in March 2026 be redeemed at the earliest?

  1. March 2027
  2. March 2029
  3. March 2031
  4. Any time after March 2026
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. March 2029

Each ELSS instalment has its own three-year lock-in from the date of investment. The March 2026 instalment can therefore be redeemed only from March 2029.

Question 8

A Gold ETF is suitable for an investor who wants:

  1. Exposure to gold prices without holding physical gold
  2. A fixed interest income credited to the bank account on the same date every month
  3. Ownership of gold mining company shares only
  4. A guaranteed return linked to the repo rate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Exposure to gold prices without holding physical gold

A Gold ETF holds physical gold of defined purity and tracks domestic gold prices. It avoids storage and purity worries but pays no interest.
