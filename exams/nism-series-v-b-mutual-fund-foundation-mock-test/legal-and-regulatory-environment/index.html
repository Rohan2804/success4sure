Legal and Regulatory Environment: NISM Series V-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Legal and Regulatory Environment, from the NISM Series V-B (Mutual Fund Foundation) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how mutual funds are regulated in India and how investors are protected. SEBI is the regulator under the SEBI Act, 1992 and the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996; AMFI sets industry codes of conduct for AMCs and distributors. Revise investor rights (information, timely payment, exit option on fundamental changes), the grievance route from AMC to SCORES to online dispute resolution, and what distributors must and must not do under the code of conduct.

Legal and Regulatory Environment questions

Question 1

If an AMC does not resolve an investor's complaint satisfactorily, the investor can escalate it online through:

  1. The RBI Integrated Ombudsman
  2. The Insurance Ombudsman
  3. SEBI's SCORES platform
  4. The local police station
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. SEBI's SCORES platform

SCORES is SEBI's online grievance system for complaints against listed companies and intermediaries, including mutual funds. Unresolved disputes can then go to online dispute resolution.

Question 2

Which of these is an investor's right in a mutual fund scheme?

  1. Timely payment of redemption proceeds
  2. Receiving a guaranteed minimum return
  3. Choosing the stocks the fund buys
  4. Getting back losses from the AMC
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Timely payment of redemption proceeds

Investors have rights to information, timely payment and fair treatment. Returns are not guaranteed and investment decisions remain with the AMC.

Question 3

AMFI's code of conduct for distributors requires them to:

  1. Guarantee returns on equity schemes
  2. Recommend suitable schemes and disclose key facts
  3. Promise to buy back units at a higher price
  4. Share the investor's details with other agents
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Recommend suitable schemes and disclose key facts

The code requires distributors to act in investors' interest, give suitable advice, provide scheme documents and keep information confidential.

Question 4

Which regulator's rules govern how mutual funds may advertise their schemes?

  1. AMFI alone, with no SEBI role
  2. RBI
  3. Ministry of Finance only
  4. SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. SEBI

SEBI's mutual fund regulations include an advertisement code that sets rules on disclosures, standard warnings and performance presentation. AMFI issues supporting industry guidelines.

Question 5

Mutual funds in India are mainly regulated under:

  1. SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996
  2. The Banking Regulation Act, 1949
  3. The Insurance Act, 1938
  4. The Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996

SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996 set rules for sponsors, trustees, AMCs, schemes, investments and disclosures. Banking and insurance laws apply to other sectors.

Question 6

A distributor collects cash of ₹1 lakh from a client and promises to deposit it into a scheme later. This practice is:

  1. Acceptable if the distributor gives a handwritten receipt
  2. Not appropriate; money must go directly to the scheme
  3. Encouraged to save the investor's time
  4. Mandatory for investors without bank accounts
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Not appropriate; money must go directly to the scheme

Investor money must go directly to the scheme through permitted payment modes, not through the distributor. Collecting money personally is risky and against the code of conduct.

Question 7

If an AMC proposes to merge a scheme with another scheme, changing its fundamental attributes, existing investors must be:

  1. Forced to remain invested in the merged scheme
  2. Charged a higher exit load to discourage exit
  3. Paid a guaranteed bonus by the AMC
  4. Given an option to exit without exit load
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Given an option to exit without exit load

A merger that changes fundamental attributes requires informing unitholders and offering them an exit at NAV without load during the specified period.

Question 8

A dispute with a mutual fund remains unresolved even after SCORES. The investor can next use:

  1. The Consumer Court only, with no other route
  2. A complaint to AMFI's distributor desk
  3. The securities market Online Dispute Resolution (ODR) platform
  4. A complaint to the stock exchange where units are not listed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The securities market Online Dispute Resolution (ODR) platform

SEBI's ODR mechanism allows investors to resolve disputes with market intermediaries, including mutual funds, through online conciliation and arbitration.
