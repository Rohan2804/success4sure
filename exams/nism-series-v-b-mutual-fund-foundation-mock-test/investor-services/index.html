Investor Services: NISM Series V-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Investor Services, from the NISM Series V-B (Mutual Fund Foundation) syllabus.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how investors open accounts and transact with mutual funds. Revise KYC requirements (PAN, address proof, KYC through a KRA), cut-off times and applicable NAV, systematic plans (SIP, STP, SWP), switches, redemptions and their timelines, account statements, nomination, and how to update details such as bank account or address. Know the purpose of each systematic plan and the basic steps when an investor dies or when details change.

Investor Services questions

Question 1

A KYC done through a KYC Registration Agency (KRA) allows an investor to:

  1. Skip PAN for all future investments
  2. Invest across intermediaries without repeating KYC
  3. Avoid paying any tax on mutual fund gains
  4. Invest only in the schemes of one AMC
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Invest across intermediaries without repeating KYC

KYC recorded with a KRA is shared across SEBI-registered intermediaries, so investors do not have to repeat the full process for each AMC or broker.

Question 2

An investor wants to invest ₹5,000 every month automatically into an equity scheme. The suitable facility is a:

  1. Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP)
  2. One-time lumpsum purchase
  3. Switch from another scheme
  4. Systematic Investment Plan (SIP)
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Systematic Investment Plan (SIP)

A SIP debits a fixed amount from the bank account at regular intervals and buys units, building discipline and averaging purchase cost over time.

Question 3

An investor with a lump sum in a liquid fund wants to move ₹10,000 every month into an equity fund. The suitable facility is a:

  1. Systematic Transfer Plan (STP)
  2. Systematic Investment Plan from the bank
  3. Systematic Withdrawal Plan to the bank
  4. Fresh NFO application each month
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Systematic Transfer Plan (STP)

An STP transfers a fixed amount periodically from one scheme to another of the same fund house, letting the lump sum enter equity gradually.

Question 4

Redemption proceeds of most equity schemes are normally paid to the investor within:

  1. 15 working days
  2. 30 days
  3. 3 working days
  4. 6 months
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 3 working days

SEBI requires most schemes to pay redemption proceeds within three working days of the request (with some exceptions for schemes investing overseas or in special situations).

Question 5

Why should an investor register a nominee in a mutual fund folio?

  1. To get a higher NAV on redemption
  2. To avoid paying exit load
  3. To allow the nominee to trade on the investor's behalf now
  4. To simplify transmission after the investor's death
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. To simplify transmission after the investor's death

A nominee can claim the units easily after the holder's death, avoiding lengthy legal processes. A nominee has no rights while the investor is alive.

Question 6

An investor wants to change the bank account registered in a folio. The request should be made:

  1. With a written request and proof of the new bank account
  2. By telling the distributor on a phone call only
  3. By writing a new bank number on a redemption form
  4. By sending an SMS to SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. With a written request and proof of the new bank account

Change of bank mandate requires a signed request with proof such as a cancelled cheque or bank statement. This protects investors from fraud.

Question 7

Under the growth option of a scheme, the investor's gains are:

  1. Paid out as cash every month
  2. Converted to fixed deposits
  3. Retained in the scheme and reflected in a rising NAV
  4. Distributed among other unitholders
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Retained in the scheme and reflected in a rising NAV

In the growth option, income and gains stay invested, so the NAV increases. Gains are realised only when units are redeemed.

Question 8

For an equity scheme, a purchase of ₹50,000 received by 3 pm on a business day, with funds credited before the cut-off, gets:

  1. The NAV of the previous day
  2. The NAV of the same day
  3. The NAV fixed at the NFO
  4. The NAV of the next month
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The NAV of the same day

For equity schemes, the applicable NAV depends on when funds are realised in the scheme's account. If the money is available before the 3 pm cut-off, the same day's NAV applies.
