Investor Services: NISM Series V-B revision notes

New cadre distributors spend most of their time helping investors transact, so this chapter is heavily tested. It covers KYC, folios, systematic plans, cut-off timings and statements. Questions are short and practical: what document is mandatory, which plan moves money between schemes, which NAV applies.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. KYC requirements

Before investing, a resident individual must complete KYC once with a KYC Registration Agency. PAN is mandatory for regular investments, along with proof of address and in-person or video verification. Aadhaar-based eKYC speeds this up. Once done, KYC is valid across all fund houses.

2. Folio number

A folio number is the investor's account number with a particular fund house. One folio can hold several schemes of that AMC. Quoting the existing folio on new applications keeps all investments together and avoids repeated paperwork. Investors can also consolidate folios.

3. SIP

A Systematic Investment Plan invests a fixed amount at regular intervals - usually monthly - through an auto-debit mandate. Because more units are bought when NAV is low and fewer when it is high, the average cost per unit tends to be lower than the average NAV over the period.

4. STP

A Systematic Transfer Plan moves a fixed amount at regular intervals from one scheme to another of the same fund house - commonly from a liquid fund to an equity fund. It suits someone with a lump sum who wants to enter equity gradually.

5. SWP

A Systematic Withdrawal Plan lets an investor withdraw a fixed amount regularly from a scheme, for example ₹20,000 a month from a hybrid fund for a retiree's expenses. Each withdrawal redeems units, so the balance falls if returns are lower than withdrawals.

6. Cut-off timings

For most schemes, redemption requests received before 3 pm get that day's NAV. For purchases, the NAV depends on when the money reaches the scheme's account. Liquid and overnight funds have a 1:30 pm cut-off for purchases. Liquid funds then apply the previous day's NAV.

7. Switches

A switch redeems units in one scheme and buys units in another of the same fund house in a single instruction. It is treated as a redemption followed by a purchase, so exit load and capital gains tax may apply.

8. Statements and timelines

Investors get a Consolidated Account Statement covering all their fund holdings by PAN. Redemption money is credited to the registered bank account within three working days for most schemes, and IDCW within seven working days of the record date. Delays attract interest payable by the AMC.

9. Keeping records updated

Investors should update mobile number, email, bank account and nomination. Nomination, or a formal opt-out, is required. Changes of bank account need supporting documents, a safeguard against fraud. Outdated contact details are a common reason for missed communication and unclaimed redemption or IDCW amounts, which then sit with the fund house until claimed.

Common traps in the exam

  • Mixing STP (moves money between two schemes) with SWP (pays money out to the investor).
  • Thinking a switch is not a sale for tax; it is a redemption plus a purchase.
  • Assuming the folio number is the same across all AMCs - each fund house issues its own.
  • Applying the 3 pm purchase cut-off to liquid funds; theirs is 1:30 pm.

Quick recap

KYC once via KRA, PAN mandatory. Folio = account with one AMC. SIP invests regularly, STP transfers between schemes, SWP withdraws. 3 pm cut-off (1:30 pm for liquid purchases). Switches are taxable redemptions. CAS consolidates holdings; redemptions paid within three working days.
