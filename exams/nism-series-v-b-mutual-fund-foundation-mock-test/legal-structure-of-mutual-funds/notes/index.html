Legal Structure of Mutual Funds: NISM Series V-B revision notes

New distributors must be able to explain who is behind a mutual fund and how investor money is protected. This chapter covers the trust structure and the job of each constituent. Questions typically describe a role - 'processes transactions', 'holds securities', 'oversees the AMC' - and ask which entity performs it.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. A fund is a trust

Every Indian mutual fund is set up as a trust. The sponsor creates it through a trust deed, and the assets of all schemes are held for the benefit of unit holders. This means the money belongs to investors, not to the company that runs the fund.

2. Sponsor

The sponsor is the promoter - often a bank, financial group or insurer. It sets up the trust, appoints the trustees, and forms the asset management company, contributing to its capital. SEBI checks the sponsor's track record and financial strength before granting registration.

3. Trustees

Trustees act as guardians of unit holders' interests. They approve new schemes, review the AMC's activities and compliance, and report to SEBI. At least two-thirds of trustees must be independent of the sponsor so that oversight is genuine. They can also remove the AMC if needed.

4. Asset Management Company

The AMC is the professional manager. Its fund managers decide what to buy and sell; its teams handle research, operations, compliance and sales. SEBI requires an AMC to maintain net worth of at least ₹50 crore. The AMC earns a management fee as part of the scheme's expenses.

5. Custodian

The custodian keeps the scheme's securities safe, settles purchases and sales, and collects dividends and interest. Separating custody from the AMC means the fund manager cannot simply take securities away; they sit with an independent, SEBI-registered entity. It also tracks bonus and rights issues.

6. Registrar and Transfer Agent

The RTA is the investor's service desk. It processes purchases, redemptions, switches, KYC updates and address or bank changes, and sends account statements. In India, large RTAs serve many fund houses, which helps the consolidated statement system. CAMS and KFintech are the major RTAs.

7. Supporting entities

Auditors check scheme accounts; fund accountants calculate NAV; bankers collect and pay money; distributors bring in investors. AMFI, the industry association, registers distributors and runs investor awareness, but it is not the regulator - SEBI is. The AMC remains responsible for outsourced work.

8. Why investors are protected

Because scheme assets are owned by the trust and held by a custodian, they are not available to creditors of the sponsor or AMC. If an AMC is in trouble, the trustees can transfer management to another AMC, and investors' units continue to represent the same assets.

Common traps in the exam

  • Saying the AMC owns the scheme's assets; the trust owns them for unit holders and the custodian holds them.
  • Assigning portfolio management to trustees - trustees oversee; the AMC's fund managers manage.
  • Mixing up the custodian (holds securities) with the RTA (keeps investor records and processes transactions).
  • Forgetting that the minimum AMC net worth is ₹50 crore under current SEBI rules.

Quick recap

Sponsor sets up the trust and AMC; trustees protect investors and supervise; AMC (₹50 crore net worth) manages money; custodian holds securities; RTA services investors. Assets belong to the trust, so investors are protected even if the AMC or sponsor faces trouble. SEBI regulates; AMFI is the industry body.
