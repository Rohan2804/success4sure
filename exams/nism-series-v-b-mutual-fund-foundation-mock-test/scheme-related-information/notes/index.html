Scheme Related Information: NISM Series V-B revision notes

Before recommending any scheme, a distributor must know where its facts are published. This short chapter covers the offer documents, the riskometer and the regular disclosures fund houses make. Exam questions describe a piece of information and ask where it appears, or ask about riskometer levels.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Scheme Information Document

The SID is the scheme's rulebook. It states the investment objective, asset allocation pattern, strategy, risk factors, benchmark, fund manager, loads, minimum amounts and available plans and options. A distributor checking whether a scheme can invest in overseas shares or lower-rated bonds would look here.

2. Statement of Additional Information

The SAI covers the whole mutual fund rather than one scheme: details of the sponsor, AMC, trustees, custodian and other service providers, general tax information and investor rights. It is common to all schemes of that fund house and is read along with the SID.

3. Key Information Memorandum

The KIM is a short version of the SID that must be attached to every application form. It shows the objective, asset allocation, risk level, expense ratio, loads, past performance and minimum investment, giving investors the essentials in a few pages.

4. Riskometer levels

SEBI's riskometer shows a scheme's risk on six levels: Low, Low to Moderate, Moderate, Moderately High, High and Very High. Most equity funds sit at Very High; overnight funds are usually Low. The rating is reviewed monthly based on the actual portfolio and can change.

5. Benchmark riskometer

Scheme documents and factsheets also show the riskometer of the scheme's benchmark. Seeing both lets an investor judge whether the fund is taking more or less risk than its benchmark index. A large cap fund rated Very High alongside a benchmark also rated Very High is behaving as expected for its category.

6. Portfolio disclosure

Funds publish their full portfolios at regular intervals on their own and AMFI's websites. This helps investors check that a scheme sticks to its stated style - for example, that a large cap fund is not quietly loading up on small caps.

7. Factsheets and annual reports

Monthly factsheets summarise NAV, AUM, expense ratio, top holdings, sector allocation and returns versus benchmark. Annual reports or abridged summaries give audited accounts. Distributors use factsheets for quick reviews with clients. Distributors should always use the latest factsheet, since holdings and expense ratios change from month to month.

8. Changes to a scheme

If an AMC wants to change a scheme's fundamental attributes - its type, objective or asset allocation - unit holders must be informed and given an exit option without exit load before the change takes effect. Merging two schemes is a common trigger for this.

Common traps in the exam

  • Looking for the AMC's board or sponsor details in the SID; they are in the SAI.
  • Assuming a scheme's riskometer is fixed for life; it is reviewed monthly.
  • Thinking the KIM is optional reading material; it must accompany every application form.
  • Forgetting that changes to fundamental attributes give investors a load-free exit window.

Quick recap

SID = one scheme's details; SAI = fund-house-level details; KIM = summary attached to forms. Riskometer has six levels from Low to Very High and is reviewed monthly; benchmarks show one too. Portfolios and factsheets are published regularly. Fundamental changes need notice and a load-free exit.
