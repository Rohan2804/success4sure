Free NCFM practice questions

Question 1 · Securities Market Basics

The Nifty 50 index is calculated using the:

  1. Price-weighted method
  2. Equal-weighted method
  3. Free-float market capitalisation weighted method
  4. Full market capitalisation including promoter shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Free-float market capitalisation weighted method

Nifty weights stocks by market value of shares freely available for trading, excluding promoter and locked-in holdings.

Question 2 · Market Participants

An investor opens a demat account through a:

  1. Depository Participant
  2. Registrar to an issue
  3. Credit rating agency
  4. Clearing corporation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Depository Participant

Depository Participants (banks, brokers etc.) act as agents of the depository and provide account services to investors.

Question 3 · Trading Membership

The purpose of the Unique Client Code (UCC) is to:

  1. Fix brokerage rates for clients
  2. Generate the client's income-tax return
  3. Identify each client uniquely so that orders and trades are tagged to the correct client
  4. Allocate IPO shares on priority
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Identify each client uniquely so that orders and trades are tagged to the correct client

Every order must carry the client's UCC (linked to PAN) for audit trail, surveillance and risk control.

Question 4 · Trading System & Orders

Among outstanding buy orders for a stock, the 'best' buy order is the one with the:

  1. Highest price
  2. Lowest price
  3. Largest quantity
  4. Most recent time stamp
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Highest price

Buyers willing to pay more get priority; for sell orders the lowest price is best.

Question 5 · Clearing & Settlement

Novation by a clearing corporation means it:

  1. Becomes the legal counterparty – buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer
  2. Rejects trades above a certain value
  3. Nets only trades of the same client
  4. Charges an extra fee for settlement
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Becomes the legal counterparty – buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer

Novation substitutes the clearing corporation as central counterparty, removing bilateral counterparty risk.

Question 6 · Risk Management & Margins

VaR margin in the cash market is designed to cover:

  1. All possible losses under any circumstances
  2. Only brokerage and taxes
  3. The largest loss expected on most days (a high, e.g. 99%, confidence level) over a one-day horizon
  4. Losses over a one-year period
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The largest loss expected on most days (a high, e.g. 99%, confidence level) over a one-day horizon

VaR margin covers potential one-day losses at a high confidence level; extreme losses beyond it are covered by ELM.

Question 7 · Legal Framework

Recognition of stock exchanges in India is governed by the:

  1. Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956
  2. Depositories Act, 1996
  3. Companies Act, 2013
  4. Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956

SCRA 1956 provides for recognition of stock exchanges and regulation of contracts in securities.

Question 8 · Fundamental Valuation Concepts

A company earns a net profit of ₹50 crore and has 10 crore shares. If the share price is ₹100, its P/E ratio is:

  1. 5
  2. 10
  3. 50
  4. 20
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 20

EPS = 50 ÷ 10 = ₹5; P/E = 100 ÷ 5 = 20.

Question 9 · Securities Market Basics

Dematerialisation refers to:

  1. Converting shares into bonds
  2. Cancelling shares bought back by a company
  3. Converting physical share certificates into electronic holdings in a depository account
  4. Converting shares into cash on sale
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Converting physical share certificates into electronic holdings in a depository account

Demat holdings are electronic book entries with a depository, eliminating risks of physical certificates.

Question 10 · Market Participants

Foreign portfolio investors typically use a custodian to:

  1. Hold their securities in safekeeping and handle settlement and corporate-action services
  2. Rate their investments
  3. Fix the issue price of IPOs
  4. Act as market makers on the exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Hold their securities in safekeeping and handle settlement and corporate-action services

Custodians provide safekeeping, settlement and related services for institutional clients.

Question 11 · Trading Membership

A trading member must issue a contract note to the client:

  1. Within 7 days of the trade
  2. Within 24 hours of execution of the trade
  3. Only at the end of the month
  4. Only when the client requests it
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Within 24 hours of execution of the trade

SEBI requires contract notes (physical or electronic) within 24 hours of the trade.

Question 12 · Trading System & Orders

The order book has buy orders at ₹201 (entered first), ₹202 (entered second) and ₹200 (entered third). A new sell limit order at ₹200 arrives. It will first match with:

  1. The buy order at ₹201, at ₹200
  2. The buy order at ₹200, at ₹200
  3. The buy order at ₹201, at ₹201
  4. The buy order at ₹202, at a trade price of ₹202
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The buy order at ₹202, at a trade price of ₹202

The best (highest) buy order is matched first, and the trade occurs at the price of the passive order already in the book, i.e., ₹202.

Question 13 · Clearing & Settlement

A member, on its proprietary account, buys 500 shares of X at ₹100 and sells 300 shares of X at ₹102 on the same day. After netting, it must:

  1. Deliver 200 shares and receive ₹19,400
  2. Receive 500 shares and pay ₹50,000
  3. Receive 200 shares and pay ₹20,000
  4. Receive 200 shares and pay ₹19,400
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Receive 200 shares and pay ₹19,400

Net shares = 500 − 300 = 200 to receive; funds = 50,000 − 30,600 = ₹19,400 payable.

Question 14 · Risk Management & Margins

A client bought 200 shares at ₹450. The day's closing price is ₹438. The mark-to-market loss is:

  1. ₹1,200
  2. ₹2,400
  3. ₹8,760
  4. ₹12
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹2,400

MTM loss = (450 − 438) × 200 = ₹2,400.

Question 15 · Legal Framework

SEBI was given statutory powers by the:

  1. Securities and Exchange Board of India Act, 1992
  2. Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934
  3. Banking Regulation Act, 1949
  4. Indian Contract Act, 1872
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Securities and Exchange Board of India Act, 1992

SEBI was set up in 1988 and became a statutory regulator under the SEBI Act, 1992.
