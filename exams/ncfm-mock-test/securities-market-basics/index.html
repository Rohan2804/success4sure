Securities Market Basics: NCFM practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Securities Market Basics, from the NCFM (Capital Market (Dealers) Module) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces the Indian securities market: the primary market where companies raise capital through IPOs, FPOs and rights issues, and the secondary market where existing securities trade on stock exchanges. It covers types of securities (equity, preference shares, debentures, ETFs), the role of indices such as Nifty 50, dematerialised holding, corporate actions like bonus, split and dividends, and basic market terminology. When revising, be clear on how each corporate action changes the number of shares and the price, how free-float capitalisation is computed, and how primary and secondary markets differ in purpose.

Securities Market Basics questions

Question 1

A company with 100 crore shares trading at ₹250 has promoters holding 60%. Assuming the entire non-promoter holding is free float, its free-float market capitalisation is:

  1. ₹25,000 crore
  2. ₹6,000 crore
  3. ₹15,000 crore
  4. ₹10,000 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹10,000 crore

Free-float shares = 40% of 100 crore = 40 crore. Multiplying by ₹250 gives ₹10,000 crore. ₹25,000 crore is full market capitalisation and ₹15,000 crore wrongly uses the promoter stake.

Question 2

An investor holds 200 shares of face value ₹10 bought at ₹900. The company splits each share into five shares of face value ₹2. Ignoring market movement, after the split the investor holds:

  1. 200 shares worth about ₹180 each
  2. 1,000 shares worth about ₹180 each
  3. 1,000 shares worth about ₹900 each
  4. 40 shares worth about ₹4,500 each
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 1,000 shares worth about ₹180 each

A 1:5 split multiplies the number of shares by five (200 × 5 = 1,000) and divides the price by five (₹900 ÷ 5 = ₹180). Total investment value remains unchanged at ₹1,80,000.

Question 3

Which of the following is a secondary market transaction?

  1. An investor buys shares of a listed bank from another investor through the exchange
  2. A company allots shares to applicants in its initial public offer
  3. A listed company places fresh shares with institutions through a qualified institutional placement
  4. A listed company issues additional shares to existing holders in a rights issue
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. An investor buys shares of a listed bank from another investor through the exchange

In the secondary market, already-issued securities change hands between investors, and the company receives no money. IPOs, rights issues and QIPs are all primary market issues that raise fresh capital for the company.

Question 4

A bonus issue in the ratio 1:1 is announced. Which statement correctly describes its effect?

  1. The company's total net worth doubles because new capital is raised
  2. The face value of each existing share is halved to create new shares
  3. The number of shares doubles, while reserves are capitalised and no cash is paid by shareholders
  4. Shareholders must pay the face value in cash for each additional share received under the bonus issue
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The number of shares doubles, while reserves are capitalised and no cash is paid by shareholders

A bonus issue converts free reserves into share capital. Shareholders get one free share for each held, so the share count doubles, but net worth is unchanged. Halving face value describes a split, not a bonus.

Question 5

Preference shareholders differ from equity shareholders mainly because preference shareholders:

  1. Receive dividend only after all equity shareholders have been paid
  2. Get priority over equity holders for dividend and capital repayment on winding up
  3. Always enjoy full voting rights on every resolution placed before the company's general meetings
  4. Have no claim at all on the company's assets on liquidation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Get priority over equity holders for dividend and capital repayment on winding up

Preference shares carry preferential rights to dividend and to repayment of capital ahead of equity shares. Their voting rights are generally limited to matters directly affecting them, unlike equity shareholders.

Question 6

An exchange traded fund (ETF) tracking the Nifty 50 is best described as a:

  1. Derivative contract that expires on the last Thursday of each month
  2. Debt instrument issued by the exchange to track index returns
  3. Fund whose units trade on the exchange throughout the day at market prices
  4. Fund whose units can be bought only directly from the AMC at the NAV declared at the end of the day
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Fund whose units trade on the exchange throughout the day at market prices

ETF units are listed and traded on stock exchanges like shares, at prices that change during the day. Buying only from the AMC at end-of-day NAV describes ordinary open-ended mutual fund schemes.

Question 7

Which of these investors is MOST exposed to the company's business risk?

  1. A secured debenture holder of the company
  2. A bank that has lent to the company against property
  3. A fixed deposit holder of the company
  4. An equity shareholder of the company
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. An equity shareholder of the company

Equity holders are residual claimants: they are paid only after lenders and creditors, so their returns depend directly on business performance. Secured lenders and debenture holders have contractual claims and security, which reduces their risk.

Question 8

A rights issue is offered at a price below the current market price. An existing shareholder who does not wish to subscribe can usually:

  1. Renounce the rights entitlement in favour of another person
  2. Demand that the company buy back the existing shares
  3. Ask the exchange to cancel the rights issue for all holders
  4. Convert the entitlement into a bonus share at no cost
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Renounce the rights entitlement in favour of another person

Rights entitlements are generally renounceable, so a shareholder can sell or transfer them to someone else. This lets the holder capture some value instead of simply letting the entitlement lapse.
