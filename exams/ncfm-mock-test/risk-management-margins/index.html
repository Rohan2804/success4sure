Risk Management & Margins: NCFM practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Risk Management & Margins, from the NCFM (Capital Market (Dealers) Module) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how exchanges and clearing corporations manage the risk of default. Key elements include capital adequacy of members, margins such as VaR margin, extreme loss margin and mark-to-market margin, upfront collection of margins from clients, exposure and position limits, price bands, market-wide circuit breakers, and settlement guarantee funds. Focus on what each margin protects against, how to calculate MTM losses and total margin on a trade, and how price bands and circuit breakers are applied. Numerical questions on margins and price ranges are common.

Risk Management & Margins questions

Question 1

A client short-sold 300 shares at ₹210 in the cash market during the day. The closing price is ₹216. The mark-to-market loss on this position is:

  1. ₹2,100
  2. ₹6,300
  3. ₹1,200
  4. ₹1,800
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹1,800

For a short (sell) position, a rise in price is a loss. MTM loss = 300 × (₹216 − ₹210) = ₹1,800. ₹6,300 wrongly multiplies 300 by 21 instead of the price difference.

Question 2

For a purchase worth ₹8,00,000, the VaR margin rate is 10% and the extreme loss margin rate is 3.5%. The total of these two margins is:

  1. ₹1,08,000
  2. ₹28,000
  3. ₹1,35,000
  4. ₹80,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹1,08,000

Total margin = ₹8,00,000 × (10% + 3.5%) = ₹8,00,000 × 13.5% = ₹1,08,000. ₹80,000 is only the VaR margin and ₹28,000 is only the extreme loss margin.

Question 3

A stock with previous close of ₹640 is in a 10% price band. Which of these sell orders will be rejected by the system?

  1. Sell at ₹700
  2. Sell at ₹712
  3. Sell at ₹580
  4. Sell at ₹640
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Sell at ₹712

A 10% band on ₹640 allows prices from ₹576 to ₹704. ₹712 is above the upper limit, so the order is rejected. ₹580, ₹640 and ₹700 all fall inside the range.

Question 4

Mark-to-market margin is collected to cover:

  1. The cost of opening a demat account
  2. Potential losses in a future market crash only
  3. The notional loss on open positions based on the day's closing price
  4. The brokerage payable by the client
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The notional loss on open positions based on the day's closing price

MTM margin covers the actual loss already incurred on open positions compared with the closing price. Future extreme moves are covered by VaR and extreme loss margins, not by MTM.

Question 5

A market-wide circuit breaker is triggered by:

  1. A member failing to pay margins
  2. A single stock hitting its price band
  3. The clearing corporation's settlement guarantee fund falling below its minimum required corpus
  4. A sharp movement in a broad market index such as Nifty 50 or Sensex
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A sharp movement in a broad market index such as Nifty 50 or Sensex

Market-wide circuit breakers apply to the whole equity market and are triggered by large moves in the main indices, halting trading for set periods. Individual stock price bands work separately.

Question 6

Why is the capital adequacy requirement imposed on trading members?

  1. To fix the maximum brokerage a member can charge
  2. To ensure that members have enough resources to absorb losses and meet obligations
  3. To pay dividends to the exchange's shareholders
  4. To decide the number of clients a member may register
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. To ensure that members have enough resources to absorb losses and meet obligations

Capital adequacy norms require members to keep base and additional capital with the clearing corporation, which acts as a cushion against losses and default. They are not related to brokerage limits or client numbers.

Question 7

A broker allows a client to trade without collecting the required upfront margin, planning to collect it the next day. Under current SEBI norms this is:

  1. Permitted if the client signs an undertaking
  2. Permitted, but only in the cash segment and only for delivery-based trades of retail clients
  3. Not permitted, and short collection of margin attracts penalty
  4. Permitted for clients with a good track record
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Not permitted, and short collection of margin attracts penalty

SEBI requires margins to be collected from clients upfront, and exchanges levy penalties on short or non-collection. Track record or undertakings do not exempt the broker from this requirement.

Question 8

Value at Risk (VaR) margin is computed using:

  1. Statistical estimates of the likely maximum loss over a short horizon at a high confidence level
  2. The face value of the share multiplied by a fixed 50%
  3. The dividend history of the company over five years
  4. The book value per share of the company
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Statistical estimates of the likely maximum loss over a short horizon at a high confidence level

VaR margin is based on the volatility of the security and estimates potential loss at a chosen confidence level (such as 99%) over a short period. Face value, book value and dividends are not inputs.
