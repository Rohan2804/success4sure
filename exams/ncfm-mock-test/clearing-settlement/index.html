Clearing & Settlement: NCFM practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Clearing & Settlement, from the NCFM (Capital Market (Dealers) Module) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers what happens after a trade: trade confirmation, determination of obligations by the clearing corporation, netting, pay-in and pay-out of funds and securities, and the settlement cycle (currently T+1 for the cash market, with an optional shorter cycle for select stocks). It also covers novation, settlement guarantee funds, handling of shortages through auction or close-out, and the role of clearing banks and depositories. When revising, practise computing net obligations from a list of trades and understand the sequence of activities and who is responsible for each.

Clearing & Settlement questions

Question 1

A client buys 600 shares at ₹80 and sells 600 shares of the same stock at ₹82 on the same day in the same account. At settlement:

  1. The client pays ₹1,200 as the trade is reversed
  2. No shares change hands and the client receives the profit of ₹1,200
  3. The client must deliver 600 shares to the clearing corporation and receive ₹49,200 in return
  4. The client receives 600 shares and pays ₹48,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. No shares change hands and the client receives the profit of ₹1,200

Buying and selling the same quantity on the same day nets to zero securities. Only the difference of 600 × (₹82 − ₹80) = ₹1,200 is settled, received by the client as profit.

Question 2

Netting of obligations by the clearing corporation means that:

  1. Trades are cancelled if the member cannot pay
  2. Profits of one member are used to pay losses of another member
  3. Each member's buys and sells in a security are offset to arrive at one net amount to pay or receive
  4. Every trade is settled separately and gross with each counterparty
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Each member's buys and sells in a security are offset to arrive at one net amount to pay or receive

Multilateral netting combines all trades of a member in a security into a single net position for funds and securities. This reduces settlement volumes, unlike gross trade-by-trade settlement.

Question 3

Which entity moves funds between clearing members' accounts and the clearing corporation's account during settlement?

  1. Clearing banks
  2. Registrars and transfer agents
  3. Merchant bankers
  4. Depository participants
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Clearing banks

Funds settlement takes place through designated clearing banks, where members maintain clearing accounts. Depositories and their participants handle securities, while RTAs and merchant bankers have no role in funds pay-in.

Question 4

Under a T+1 settlement cycle, if a trade is executed on a Thursday and Friday is a normal working day, the settlement takes place on:

  1. Tuesday
  2. Monday
  3. Thursday itself
  4. Friday
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Friday

T+1 means settlement occurs one working day after the trade date. A Thursday trade is therefore settled on Friday, assuming Friday is a settlement working day.

Question 5

'Pay-out' in the settlement process refers to:

  1. Members depositing funds and securities with the clearing corporation
  2. The clearing corporation releasing funds and securities to members entitled to receive them
  3. A client paying brokerage to the trading member
  4. The company paying dividends to shareholders
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The clearing corporation releasing funds and securities to members entitled to receive them

Pay-in is when members deliver funds and securities; pay-out is the reverse, when the clearing corporation releases them to receiving members, who then pass them on to clients.

Question 6

If a buyer does not receive shares because the selling member defaulted on delivery and the auction also fails, the position is usually:

  1. Cancelled with no compensation to the buyer
  2. Carried forward indefinitely to the next settlement until the shares become available in the market
  3. Settled by the depository from its own holdings
  4. Closed out at a price determined under the clearing corporation's rules
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Closed out at a price determined under the clearing corporation's rules

When an auction cannot obtain the shares, the shortage is closed out and the buyer is compensated in cash at a close-out price set under clearing corporation norms. The depository does not supply shares itself.

Question 7

Novation helps market participants mainly by:

  1. Fixing the price at which all trades settle
  2. Eliminating the need for margins
  3. Removing counterparty risk between individual trading members
  4. Allowing members to cancel their trades unilaterally even after the settlement is complete
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Removing counterparty risk between individual trading members

Through novation the clearing corporation becomes buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer, so members are not exposed to each other's default. Margins are still needed because the clearing corporation bears that risk.

Question 8

Member X, on behalf of clients, has bought 1,000 shares of a stock and sold 700 shares of the same stock in the same settlement (all trades for one client). X's net securities obligation is to:

  1. Receive 300 shares
  2. Receive 1,000 shares and deliver 700 shares separately
  3. Deliver 1,700 shares
  4. Deliver 300 shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Receive 300 shares

Net position = 1,000 bought − 700 sold = 300 shares to be received. Netting replaces separate gross movements of 1,000 and 700 shares with a single receipt.
