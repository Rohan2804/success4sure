Fundamental Valuation Concepts: NCFM practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Fundamental Valuation Concepts, from the NCFM (Capital Market (Dealers) Module) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces the basics of valuing shares: time value of money (present and future value, annuities), and ratios used to assess companies, such as EPS, P/E, price-to-book, dividend yield, dividend payout, earnings yield, ROE and debt-equity. It also covers the dividend discount model, including constant-growth valuation, and the idea of intrinsic value versus market price. When revising, practise each formula with numbers, be careful with units such as crore and per share, and know what each ratio indicates about valuation, profitability or leverage.

Fundamental Valuation Concepts questions

Question 1

A company's net profit is ₹84 crore and it has 12 crore shares. Its share trades at ₹105. What is the P/E ratio?

  1. 8.75
  2. 15
  3. 7
  4. 12
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 15

EPS = ₹84 crore ÷ 12 crore = ₹7. P/E = ₹105 ÷ ₹7 = 15. The option 7 is the EPS itself, and 8.75 wrongly divides price by the number of shares.

Question 2

A company has just paid a dividend of ₹4 per share. Dividends are expected to grow at 6% a year forever, and investors require a 12% return. Using the constant-growth model, the share's value is about:

  1. ₹35.33
  2. ₹33.33
  3. ₹66.67
  4. ₹70.67
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹70.67

Next year's dividend D1 = ₹4 × 1.06 = ₹4.24. Value = D1 ÷ (r − g) = ₹4.24 ÷ 0.06 = ₹70.67. ₹66.67 wrongly uses the current dividend instead of D1.

Question 3

₹50,000 is invested at 10% per annum compounded annually. Its value after 3 years is:

  1. ₹66,000
  2. ₹65,000
  3. ₹66,550
  4. ₹60,500
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹66,550

Future value = ₹50,000 × (1.10)³ = ₹50,000 × 1.331 = ₹66,550. ₹65,000 is the simple interest answer, which ignores interest on interest.

Question 4

A share has EPS of ₹12 and pays a dividend of ₹3 per share. Its dividend payout ratio is:

  1. 25%
  2. 30%
  3. 75%
  4. 4%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 25%

Payout ratio = DPS ÷ EPS = ₹3 ÷ ₹12 = 25%. The remaining 75% is the retention ratio, which is the tempting wrong option.

Question 5

A company's net worth is ₹450 crore and it has 15 crore shares outstanding. Its book value per share is:

  1. ₹30
  2. ₹15
  3. ₹45
  4. ₹67.50
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹30

Book value per share = net worth ÷ number of shares = ₹450 crore ÷ 15 crore = ₹30. The other options come from mixing up the two figures.

Question 6

What is the present value of ₹10,000 received at the end of each year for 3 years, if the discount rate is 10% per annum?

  1. About ₹27,273
  2. About ₹24,869
  3. ₹30,000
  4. About ₹22,539
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. About ₹24,869

PV = 10,000/1.1 + 10,000/1.1² + 10,000/1.1³ = 9,091 + 8,264 + 7,513 ≈ ₹24,869. ₹30,000 ignores discounting altogether.

Question 7

A share priced at ₹160 has EPS of ₹8. Its earnings yield is:

  1. 2%
  2. 20%
  3. 5%
  4. 8%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 5%

Earnings yield = EPS ÷ price = ₹8 ÷ ₹160 = 5%, the inverse of the P/E ratio of 20. The option 20% confuses P/E with earnings yield.

Question 8

Company A has a debt-equity ratio of 0.5 and Company B has 2.0, with similar businesses. Which statement is correct?

  1. Both have the same financial risk as the businesses are similar
  2. Company B has no debt at all
  3. Company A is more leveraged than Company B
  4. Company B is more leveraged and carries higher financial risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Company B is more leveraged and carries higher financial risk

Debt-equity measures borrowed funds relative to owners' funds. B has ₹2 of debt per ₹1 of equity, versus ₹0.50 for A, so B has higher leverage and financial risk.
