Trading System & Orders: NCFM practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Trading System & Orders, from the NCFM (Capital Market (Dealers) Module) syllabus.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains the screen-based, anonymous, order-driven trading system used by Indian exchanges. It covers the order book, order matching on price-time priority, market types (normal, odd lot, auction), session timings, the pre-open call auction, and order types and conditions: market, limit, stop-loss, IOC, day orders and disclosed quantity. It also explains price bands and circuit filters. When revising, practise reading an order book, working out which orders match and at what price, and understanding how each order condition behaves when it is not fully executed.

Trading System & Orders questions

Question 1

A trader places a limit buy order for 500 shares at ₹120. The best sell orders are 200 shares at ₹119 and 400 shares at ₹121. What happens to the order on entry?

  1. 500 shares are bought at an average price of ₹120
  2. 200 shares are bought at ₹119 and 300 shares remain pending at ₹120
  3. 200 shares are bought at ₹119 and 300 shares are bought at ₹121
  4. No shares are bought because the full quantity is not available at ₹120
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 200 shares are bought at ₹119 and 300 shares remain pending at ₹120

A limit buy at ₹120 can match only sell orders at ₹120 or below, so 200 shares trade at ₹119. The remaining 300 shares stay in the book at ₹120 because ₹121 is above the limit.

Question 2

In NSE's trading system, when a passive order already in the book is matched with an incoming active order, the trade takes place at the:

  1. Last traded price of the stock at the time of the previous trade
  2. Average of the two order prices
  3. Price of the incoming active order
  4. Price of the passive order already in the book
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Price of the passive order already in the book

Trades execute at the price of the order resting in the book (passive order). The incoming order may thus get a better price than its limit, which is why using the active order's price is wrong.

Question 3

A trader holding shares bought at ₹500 wants to limit losses if the price falls to ₹470. Which order suits this need?

  1. A stop-loss buy order with a trigger price of ₹530
  2. A limit buy order at ₹470
  3. A stop-loss sell order with trigger price ₹470
  4. A market buy order placed immediately
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A stop-loss sell order with trigger price ₹470

A stop-loss sell order becomes active only when the price falls to the trigger, protecting the holder against further loss. A buy order would add to the position instead of limiting the loss.

Question 4

An order with the 'Day' validity condition that is not executed by the close of trading will:

  1. Be cancelled automatically at the end of the day
  2. Be executed compulsorily at the official closing price of the day
  3. Be carried forward to the next trading day
  4. Remain pending until the trader cancels it
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Be cancelled automatically at the end of the day

A Day order is valid only for the trading day on which it is entered. Unexecuted portions are cancelled by the system at the end of the session, not carried forward or forced to execute.

Question 5

A seller enters 10,000 shares with a disclosed quantity of 1,000. What does the market see?

  1. The order is hidden until the full 10,000 shares can be matched
  2. All 10,000 shares are displayed, but only 1,000 can trade per day
  3. Only 1,000 shares are sold and the rest are cancelled
  4. Only 1,000 shares are displayed at a time and the next 1,000 show after each lot trades
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Only 1,000 shares are displayed at a time and the next 1,000 show after each lot trades

Disclosed quantity reveals only part of a large order to the market. Once the disclosed portion is executed, the next portion is shown, until the full order is filled or cancelled.

Question 6

Which of the following orders is placed to sell at the best available price immediately, without specifying a price?

  1. Market order
  2. Disclosed quantity order
  3. Stop-loss limit order
  4. Limit order
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Market order

A market order carries no price and executes against the best available counter orders. A limit order specifies the worst acceptable price, and a stop-loss order waits for a trigger.

Question 7

In the buy side of an order book there are bids at ₹305 (time 10:01), ₹305 (time 10:00) and ₹306 (time 10:02). An incoming market sell order for a small quantity matches first with the bid at:

  1. ₹305 entered at 10:01
  2. The earliest bid, regardless of price
  3. ₹306 entered at 10:02
  4. ₹305 entered at 10:00
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹306 entered at 10:02

Price priority comes first, so the highest bid of ₹306 gets matched before the ₹305 bids, even though it was entered later. Time priority applies only between orders at the same price.

Question 8

Why do exchanges apply price bands on individual stocks?

  1. To guarantee that every order gets executed within the day
  2. To curb excessive volatility by rejecting orders outside a permitted range
  3. To prevent investors from placing market orders
  4. To fix the price at which the stock must close every day
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. To curb excessive volatility by rejecting orders outside a permitted range

Price bands restrict orders to a range around a reference price so that sharp, possibly erroneous or manipulative moves are checked. They neither guarantee execution nor fix the closing price.
