Legal Framework: NCFM practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Legal Framework, from the NCFM (Capital Market (Dealers) Module) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the laws and regulations that govern the Indian securities market: the SEBI Act, 1992, Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956 and its rules, the Depositories Act, 1996, the Companies Act, 2013, the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002, and SEBI regulations on insider trading (PIT) and fraudulent and unfair trade practices (PFUTP). It also covers investor grievance redressal and arbitration. When revising, map each law to its subject matter, know what constitutes insider trading and market manipulation, and understand the anti-money-laundering obligations of intermediaries.

Legal Framework questions

Question 1

Two brokers repeatedly trade a thinly traded stock between themselves at rising prices to create a false appearance of activity. This practice is called:

  1. Arbitrage, a legitimate trading strategy
  2. Hedging, permitted for risk management
  3. Market making, encouraged by exchanges
  4. Circular trading, a manipulative practice under PFUTP Regulations
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Circular trading, a manipulative practice under PFUTP Regulations

Trading among a group to create artificial volumes and prices is circular trading, prohibited under SEBI's PFUTP Regulations. Arbitrage and hedging are legitimate strategies that do not create false market appearance.

Question 2

Under the SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 'unpublished price sensitive information' includes:

  1. The company's name and registered office address
  2. The closing price of the company's share on the exchange
  3. Information on a proposed merger not yet disclosed to the stock exchanges
  4. Quarterly results already filed with the exchanges
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Information on a proposed merger not yet disclosed to the stock exchanges

UPSI is information that is not generally available and is likely to materially affect the price. Undisclosed merger plans qualify; filed results and public data are generally available information.

Question 3

Under anti-money-laundering requirements, an intermediary must identify the 'beneficial owner' of a client account in order to:

  1. Know the natural person who ultimately owns or controls the client
  2. Calculate the brokerage payable on each trade
  3. Determine the tax rate applicable to the account
  4. Decide whether the client is eligible for allotment in the retail category of IPOs and FPOs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Know the natural person who ultimately owns or controls the client

PMLA rules require intermediaries to identify the natural persons who ultimately own or control a client, especially companies and trusts. This prevents misuse of layered entities to launder money.

Question 4

A dispute between a client and a trading member about a trade that is not resolved through the exchange's grievance mechanism can be referred to:

  1. The Insurance Ombudsman
  2. Arbitration under the exchange's bye-laws
  3. The Registrar of Companies
  4. The Banking Ombudsman of RBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Arbitration under the exchange's bye-laws

Exchange bye-laws provide arbitration for disputes between members and clients. Insurance and banking ombudsmen handle other sectors, and the Registrar of Companies deals with company law filings.

Question 5

Which law contains provisions on the listing of securities and the conditions under which contracts in securities are legal?

  1. Indian Contract Act, 1872 alone
  2. Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956
  3. Depositories Act, 1996
  4. Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 and its rules
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956

The SCRA governs recognised stock exchanges, contracts in securities and listing. The Depositories Act covers electronic holding, and PMLA deals with money laundering.

Question 6

SEBI's powers under the SEBI Act, 1992 include:

  1. Fixing the interest rate on bank deposits
  2. Collecting income tax on capital gains
  3. Issuing currency notes in India
  4. Registering and regulating intermediaries and imposing penalties for violations
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Registering and regulating intermediaries and imposing penalties for violations

SEBI registers and regulates market intermediaries, conducts inspections and investigations, and imposes penalties. Interest rates and currency are RBI matters, while tax collection rests with the Income Tax Department.

Question 7

An employee of a listed company buys shares of the company after hearing about an unannounced big order from a colleague. He argues he was not involved in the deal. Under insider trading rules he:

  1. Can be treated as an insider because he possessed unpublished price sensitive information
  2. Is safe because he heard it informally from a colleague and did not see any official company document
  3. Is safe as long as he holds the shares for over a year
  4. Is safe because only directors can be insiders
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Can be treated as an insider because he possessed unpublished price sensitive information

Anyone who has access to or possesses UPSI can be an insider, regardless of designation or how the information was received. Trading while in possession of UPSI is prohibited, and holding period is irrelevant.

Question 8

The Depositories Act, 1996 primarily enabled:

  1. Mandatory listing of all public companies
  2. The creation of clearing corporations
  3. Holding and transfer of securities in electronic book-entry form
  4. The setting up of SEBI as a statutory body
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Holding and transfer of securities in electronic book-entry form

The Depositories Act allowed securities to be held in dematerialised form and transferred by book entry through depositories. SEBI's statutory status came from the SEBI Act, 1992.
