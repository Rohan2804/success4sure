Trading Membership: NCFM practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Trading Membership, from the NCFM (Capital Market (Dealers) Module) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how one becomes a trading member of an exchange, eligibility norms such as net worth and qualified staff, membership categories, the role of authorised persons, and the obligations of a member towards clients. Key areas include KYC and client registration, the know-your-client documents, risk disclosure documents, rights and obligations, segregation of client funds and securities, unique client codes, contract notes and the code of conduct. When revising, focus on what a member must do before accepting a client, what is prohibited, and how client assets must be protected.

Trading Membership questions

Question 1

Before executing a client's first order, a trading member must ensure that:

  1. The client has deposited at least one year's brokerage in advance
  2. The client has completed a training course conducted by the exchange
  3. KYC is completed and the client registration documents are executed
  4. The client has opened a bank account with the same broker
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. KYC is completed and the client registration documents are executed

Client registration, including KYC and the rights and obligations documents, must be completed before trading. There is no requirement of advance brokerage, exchange training or a bank account with the broker.

Question 2

A trading member uses securities lying in a client's account to meet the margin obligations of another client. This is:

  1. Prohibited, because client securities must be used only for that client
  2. Allowed if the broker returns the securities within a week
  3. Allowed if both clients belong to the same family
  4. Allowed for retail clients but not for institutions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Prohibited, because client securities must be used only for that client

Client assets must be segregated and used only for the respective client's obligations. Using one client's securities for another's margin is misuse of client assets and is prohibited regardless of when they are returned.

Question 3

The Risk Disclosure Document given to a client during registration mainly explains:

  1. The tax rates applicable to the client's capital gains
  2. The risks involved in trading in securities and derivatives
  3. The list of shares recommended by the broker for purchase
  4. The broker's own financial statements for the last three years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The risks involved in trading in securities and derivatives

The Risk Disclosure Document informs the client of the risks of trading, such as price volatility and leverage. It is not a statement of the broker's finances, tax advice or a stock recommendation.

Question 4

Which statement about a client's power of attorney or demat debit authorisation given to a broker is correct?

  1. It allows the broker to transfer the client's shares for any purpose
  2. It permits the broker to pledge client shares with lenders to raise the broker's own working capital
  3. It is compulsory for every client before trading can start
  4. It is limited to purposes such as meeting the client's own settlement and margin obligations
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. It is limited to purposes such as meeting the client's own settlement and margin obligations

SEBI has restricted such authorisations to specific uses linked to the client's own obligations, and they cannot be made mandatory. Using them for the broker's own funding or any other purpose is prohibited.

Question 5

A trading member receives a complaint from a client about an unauthorised trade. The most appropriate first step is to:

  1. Ignore it unless the amount involved exceeds ₹1 lakh
  2. Ask the client to approach the stock exchange directly
  3. Record and investigate the complaint and resolve it within the prescribed timeline
  4. Close the client's account to avoid further disputes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Record and investigate the complaint and resolve it within the prescribed timeline

Members must maintain a grievance redressal mechanism, register complaints and resolve them promptly. A client may escalate to the exchange or SEBI's SCORES only if not satisfied, so referring him away first is incorrect.

Question 6

Which of the following is a typical eligibility requirement for an individual or corporate seeking trading membership?

  1. Being registered as a banking company with RBI
  2. Holding a minimum of 1% of the exchange's shares
  3. Having at least ten years of experience as a listed company director
  4. Meeting the exchange's net worth and deposit norms and being a fit and proper person
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Meeting the exchange's net worth and deposit norms and being a fit and proper person

Exchanges prescribe financial criteria such as net worth and deposits, along with fit-and-proper and qualification requirements. Holding exchange shares, banking registration or directorship experience are not general conditions for membership.

Question 7

Why must each order entered on the trading system carry the client's Unique Client Code?

  1. So that every trade can be traced to the actual client for surveillance and settlement
  2. So that the exchange can charge a separate listing fee per client
  3. So that the client's PAN is shown publicly on the order book
  4. So that orders from clients get priority over proprietary orders
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. So that every trade can be traced to the actual client for surveillance and settlement

The UCC links each order to a registered client, enabling audit trail, surveillance and correct allocation. Client identity is not displayed publicly, and UCC does not affect order priority or fees.

Question 8

A client wants to trade through a broker's authorised person. Who is responsible to the client for the authorised person's acts in relation to the business?

  1. Only the authorised person, in his personal capacity, and never the trading member
  2. The trading member who appointed the authorised person
  3. The depository where the client's shares are held
  4. The stock exchange that approved the appointment
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The trading member who appointed the authorised person

The trading member is responsible for all acts of its authorised persons in connection with the securities business. The exchange approves the appointment but does not take over the member's responsibility.
