Reasoning Ability: IBPS Clerk practice questions

15 free questions with answers and explanations on Reasoning Ability, from the IBPS Clerk (IBPS Clerk (Prelims)) syllabus.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this section covers

Reasoning for IBPS Clerk includes linear seating, box and day-based puzzles, syllogisms, inequalities, blood relations, direction sense, coding-decoding, ranking, alphabet tests, odd one out and series. Puzzles are usually simpler than in PO but still take time, so draw neat diagrams and fix definite positions first. For syllogisms learn the 'either–or' case and complementary pairs. In coding, look for patterns such as opposite letters, shifts and letter-position values. Regular timed practice builds the speed this section needs.

Reasoning Ability questions

Question 1

Five friends A, B, C, D and E sit in a row facing north. • B sits second from the left end. • Only one person sits between B and D. • E sits immediately to the right of D. • A does not sit at either end. Who sits at the extreme left end?

  1. A
  2. C
  3. E
  4. B
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. C

B is 2nd, so D is 4th (there is no position 0). E is immediately right of D, so 5th. A cannot be at an end, so A is 3rd and C takes the left end: C B A D E.

Question 2

Six boxes P, Q, R, S, T and U are placed one above another. • U is at the bottom. • Only two boxes are placed between U and Q. • Q is immediately above T. • P is immediately above Q. • R is placed somewhere above S. Which box is at the top?

  1. P
  2. S
  3. Q
  4. R
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. R

U is 1st from bottom, so Q is 4th and T 3rd. P is 5th. R and S fill positions 2 and 6, and R must be above S, so R is on top (6th) and S is 2nd.

Question 3

Five officers V, W, X, Y and Z visit a branch on different days from Monday to Friday. • X visits on Wednesday. • W visits on the day immediately after Y. • Z visits on a day before X. • V does not visit on Monday. Who visits on Monday?

  1. V
  2. Y
  3. W
  4. Z
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Z

Y–W must be Monday–Tuesday or Thursday–Friday. If Monday–Tuesday, Z has no day before Wednesday, so Y–W are Thursday–Friday. V cannot take Monday, so V is Tuesday and Z is Monday.

Question 4

Statements: All banks are buildings. No building is a tent. Conclusions: I. No bank is a tent. II. Some buildings are banks.

  1. Only conclusion I follows
  2. Only conclusion II follows
  3. Both I and II follow
  4. Neither I nor II follows
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Both I and II follow

Banks lie wholly inside buildings, and buildings share nothing with tents, so no bank can be a tent. Since all banks are buildings, some buildings are certainly banks. Both conclusions follow.

Question 5

Statements: Some cups are plates. Some plates are spoons. Conclusions: I. Some cups are spoons. II. No cup is a spoon.

  1. Only conclusion I follows
  2. Only conclusion II follows
  3. Either I or II follows
  4. Neither I nor II follows
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Either I or II follows

Neither conclusion is certain on its own. But I and II form a complementary pair about the same two terms: one of them must always be true. Hence either I or II follows.

Question 6

In the following question, the relationship between different elements is shown. Decide which conclusion(s) definitely hold true. Given: R > S ≥ T ≥ U Conclusions: I. R > U II. S > U

  1. Only conclusion I follows
  2. Only conclusion II follows
  3. Both I and II follow
  4. Neither I nor II follows
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Only conclusion I follows

R > S ≥ T ≥ U gives R > U because one strict sign is present, so I follows. Between S and U all signs are ≥, so S may equal U; II is not definite. Only I follows.

Question 7

A is the brother of B. B is the daughter of C. D is the husband of C. How is A related to D?

  1. Son
  2. Nephew
  3. Brother
  4. Grandson
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Son

B is C's daughter, and A is B's brother, so A is C's son. D is C's husband, so D is A's father. Hence A is D's son.

Question 8

Neha's mother is the sister of Ravi's father. How is Ravi related to Neha?

  1. Brother
  2. Cousin
  3. Nephew
  4. Uncle
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Cousin

Neha's mother and Ravi's father are siblings. Children of siblings are cousins, so Ravi is Neha's cousin. He cannot be her brother because they have different parents.

Question 9

Mohan walks 15 m towards north, turns right and walks 8 m, then turns right again and walks 9 m. How far is he from his starting point?

  1. 10 m
  2. 14 m
  3. 12 m
  4. 8 m
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 10 m

North 15 m, then right (east) 8 m, then right again (south) 9 m. He is 15 − 9 = 6 m north and 8 m east of the start. Distance = √(6² + 8²) = 10 m.

Question 10

In a certain code, BANK is written as YZMP. How is CASH written in that code?

  1. XZGS
  2. DBTI
  3. XAHS
  4. XZHS
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. XZHS

Each letter is replaced by its opposite letter in the alphabet (A↔Z, B↔Y, N↔M, K↔P). For CASH: C→X, A→Z, S→H, H→S, giving XZHS.

Question 11

If BAG is coded as 14 (the product of the positions of its letters in the alphabet), what is the code for DEAF?

  1. 16
  2. 96
  3. 120
  4. 150
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 120

B, A, G are 2, 1, 7 and 2 × 1 × 7 = 14. For DEAF, the positions are 4, 5, 1, 6, so the product is 4 × 5 × 1 × 6 = 120. The sum, 16, is a trap.

Question 12

In a class of 35 students, Arun ranks 8th from the top. What is his rank from the bottom?

  1. 27th
  2. 28th
  3. 29th
  4. 26th
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 28th

Rank from bottom = total − rank from top + 1 = 35 − 8 + 1 = 28th. Forgetting to add 1 gives 27th, a common slip.

Question 13

Which letter of the English alphabet is 5th to the left of the 16th letter from the left end?

  1. K
  2. U
  3. L
  4. J
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. K

The 16th letter from the left is P. Moving 5 places to its left gives the 11th letter, K. Moving to the right instead would give U.

Question 14

Find the odd one out: 121, 169, 225, 289, 323

  1. 225
  2. 169
  3. 289
  4. 323
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 323

121, 169, 225 and 289 are squares of 11, 13, 15 and 17. 323 is not a perfect square (it is 17 × 19), so it is the odd one out.

Question 15

Find the wrong number in the series: 2, 5, 11, 23, 48, 95

  1. 23
  2. 11
  3. 48
  4. 95
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 48

Each term is double the previous term plus 1: 2, 5, 11, 23, 47, 95. So 48 should be 47. Note that 95 = 47 × 2 + 1, which confirms 48 is the wrong term.
