Quantitative Aptitude: SSC CHSL practice questions

15 free questions with answers and explanations on Quantitative Aptitude, from the SSC CHSL (Combined Higher Secondary Level (Tier 1)) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this section covers

Quantitative Aptitude covers arithmetic and elementary mathematics up to Class 10 level: number system, HCF and LCM, percentages, ratio and proportion, mixtures, profit and loss, discount, simple and compound interest, time and work, pipes, speed and trains, algebraic identities, geometry, mensuration, trigonometry and basic data interpretation. Learn standard shortcuts such as successive-percentage formulas, alligation and remainder cycles. Memorise squares, cubes and common fractions to speed up calculation, and always check units such as m/s versus km/h before choosing an answer.

Quantitative Aptitude questions

Question 1

What is the highest common factor (HCF) of 84 and 126?

  1. 21
  2. 14
  3. 42
  4. 63
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 42

84 = 2² × 3 × 7 and 126 = 2 × 3² × 7. Taking the lowest power of each common prime gives 2 × 3 × 7 = 42. Both 21 and 14 are common factors, but not the highest.

Question 2

The price of sugar rises by 25%. By what percentage must a family reduce its consumption so that its expenditure on sugar remains the same?

  1. 25%
  2. 20%
  3. 15%
  4. 22.5%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 20%

Required reduction = rise ÷ (100 + rise) × 100 = 25 ÷ 125 × 100 = 20%. Reducing by 25% would actually lower the expenditure, because the base price is now higher.

Question 3

Two successive discounts of 20% and 10% are equivalent to a single discount of:

  1. 28%
  2. 30%
  3. 27%
  4. 32%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 28%

After a 20% discount, ₹100 becomes ₹80, and a further 10% off gives ₹72. The total discount is ₹28 on ₹100, that is 28%, not the simple sum of 30%.

Question 4

What is the compound interest on ₹10,000 at 10% per annum for 2 years, compounded annually?

  1. ₹2,000
  2. ₹2,200
  3. ₹2,010
  4. ₹2,100
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹2,100

Amount = 10,000 × (1.1)² = ₹12,100, so compound interest = ₹2,100. Simple interest would be ₹2,000; the extra ₹100 is interest earned on the first year's interest.

Question 5

If A : B = 2 : 3 and B : C = 4 : 5, what is A : B : C?

  1. 2 : 3 : 5
  2. 8 : 12 : 20
  3. 8 : 12 : 15
  4. 6 : 9 : 10
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 8 : 12 : 15

Make B common by taking the LCM of 3 and 4, which is 12. A : B = 8 : 12 and B : C = 12 : 15, so A : B : C = 8 : 12 : 15.

Question 6

In what ratio must rice costing ₹40 per kg be mixed with rice costing ₹55 per kg so that the mixture is worth ₹45 per kg?

  1. 1 : 2
  2. 3 : 2
  3. 3 : 1
  4. 2 : 1
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 2 : 1

By alligation, cheaper : dearer = (55 − 45) : (45 − 40) = 10 : 5 = 2 : 1. The mixture price is closer to ₹40, so more of the cheaper rice is needed.

Question 7

A train 150 m long crosses a pole in 10 seconds. What is its speed in km/h?

  1. 15 km/h
  2. 45 km/h
  3. 60 km/h
  4. 54 km/h
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 54 km/h

To cross a pole the train covers its own length, so speed = 150 ÷ 10 = 15 m/s. Converting, 15 × 18/5 = 54 km/h. The figure 15 is the speed in m/s, not km/h.

Question 8

Pipe A can fill a tank in 12 hours and pipe B can fill it in 18 hours. If both are opened together, how long will they take to fill the tank?

  1. 7 hours 12 minutes
  2. 7 hours 30 minutes
  3. 6 hours
  4. 7 hours 20 minutes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 7 hours 12 minutes

Together they fill 1/12 + 1/18 = 5/36 of the tank per hour, so the time is 36/5 = 7.2 hours. 0.2 hour is 12 minutes, giving 7 hours 12 minutes.

Question 9

If x + 1/x = 5, what is the value of x² + 1/x²?

  1. 23
  2. 25
  3. 27
  4. 21
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 23

Squaring both sides gives x² + 2 + 1/x² = 25, so x² + 1/x² = 25 − 2 = 23. Adding 2 instead of subtracting gives the tempting wrong answer 27.

Question 10

What is the area of a triangle whose sides are 13 cm, 14 cm and 15 cm?

  1. 84 cm²
  2. 91 cm²
  3. 78 cm²
  4. 105 cm²
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 84 cm²

Using Heron's formula, s = (13 + 14 + 15)/2 = 21. Area = √(21 × 8 × 7 × 6) = √7056 = 84 cm².

Question 11

What is the volume of a cylinder with radius 7 cm and height 10 cm? (Take π = 22/7)

  1. 440 cm³
  2. 1540 cm³
  3. 1440 cm³
  4. 3080 cm³
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 1540 cm³

Volume = πr²h = 22/7 × 7 × 7 × 10 = 1540 cm³. The value 440 cm³ is the curved surface area, which uses 2πrh instead.

Question 12

If tan θ = 3/4 and θ is an acute angle, what is the value of sin θ + cos θ?

  1. 7/4
  2. 7/5
  3. 5/7
  4. 1
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 7/5

With opposite side 3 and adjacent side 4, the hypotenuse is 5. So sin θ = 3/5 and cos θ = 4/5, and their sum is 7/5.

Question 13

A shopkeeper marks his goods 40% above the cost price and allows a discount of 15% on the marked price. What is his profit percentage?

  1. 19%
  2. 25%
  3. 21%
  4. 17%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 19%

Take CP = ₹100. Marked price = ₹140, and after a 15% discount, SP = 140 × 0.85 = ₹119. Profit = ₹19 on ₹100, that is 19%. Simply subtracting 15 from 40 wrongly gives 25%.

Question 14

What is the remainder when 2³¹ is divided by 7?

  1. 1
  2. 2
  3. 4
  4. 3
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 2

2³ = 8 leaves remainder 1 when divided by 7. Since 31 = 3 × 10 + 1, 2³¹ = (2³)¹⁰ × 2, which leaves remainder 1 × 2 = 2.

Question 15

A company's sales (in ₹ lakh) were: 2021 – 120, 2022 – 150, 2023 – 135, 2024 – 180. What was the percentage increase in sales from 2023 to 2024?

  1. 25%
  2. 45%
  3. 33⅓%
  4. 30%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 33⅓%

Increase = 180 − 135 = 45 lakh. Percentage increase = 45 ÷ 135 × 100 = 33⅓%. Dividing by the new value 180 instead of the old value gives 25%, which is wrong.
