Question 1 · Verbal Ability & Reading Comprehension

Read the passage and answer the question. Fisheries managers once assumed that if a fish stock collapsed, the fleet would simply move elsewhere and the fish would recover. The cod off Newfoundland disproved this. Two decades after fishing was halted, the population remained a fraction of its former size, partly because the ecosystem had reorganised: smaller species that cod once preyed upon had multiplied and now fed on young cod. A collapse, in other words, can be self-perpetuating, because it alters the very conditions that made the earlier abundance possible. The 'self-perpetuating' collapse described in the passage is best understood as:

  1. a shift to a new stable state that resists a return to the earlier condition
  2. a temporary dip followed by automatic recovery once fishing stops
  3. a decline caused solely by continued overfishing after the ban
  4. a regular boom-and-bust cycle that repeats every decade
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. a shift to a new stable state that resists a return to the earlier condition

Because the collapse altered the conditions (predators of young cod multiplied), the system stayed in its new state even without fishing.

Question 2 · Data Interpretation & Logical Reasoning

Six people A, B, C, D, E and F sit in seats numbered 1 to 6 placed clockwise around a circular table. • A sits in seat 1 and D sits directly opposite A. • B sits in the seat immediately clockwise of A. • E does not sit next to D. • C sits next to B. Who sits between D and E?

  1. C
  2. F
  3. B
  4. A
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. F

D is in seat 4 and B in seat 2; E cannot be in 3 or 5, so E is in 6; C next to B takes seat 3, leaving F in seat 5, between D (4) and E (6).

Question 3 · Quantitative Ability

Pipe A can fill a tank in 12 hours and pipe B in 15 hours, while pipe C can empty the full tank in 20 hours. If all three are opened together on an empty tank, how long will it take to fill?

  1. 10 hours
  2. 8 hours
  3. 12 hours
  4. 9 hours
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 10 hours

Net rate = 1/12 + 1/15 − 1/20 = (5 + 4 − 3)/60 = 1/10, so 10 hours.

Question 4 · Verbal Ability & Reading Comprehension

Read the passage and answer the question. A translator of poetry faces a choice that prose translators can often evade. Prose carries most of its meaning in its propositions, so a faithful paraphrase preserves most of what matters. In a poem, however, the sound, the line break and the ambiguity of a single word may be the meaning. To translate the propositions alone is to deliver a summary; to chase the music is to write a new poem. The best translators accept that loss is certain and decide, case by case, which loss the poem can best survive. The author would most likely agree that:

  1. prose translation is a purely mechanical task requiring no judgement
  2. translators should always give priority to sound over sense
  3. a faithful paraphrase is the ideal form of poetic translation
  4. no translation of a poem can preserve everything that the original achieves
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. no translation of a poem can preserve everything that the original achieves

The author says 'loss is certain' in translating poetry; the other options overstate or contradict the passage.

Question 5 · Data Interpretation & Logical Reasoning

Four teams P, Q, R and S play a round-robin tournament (each pair plays once). A win earns 3 points, a draw 1 point and a loss 0. Final points: P = 7, Q = 5, R = 2, S = 1. What was the result of the match between Q and S?

  1. S won
  2. The match was drawn
  3. Cannot be determined
  4. Q won
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Q won

P (7) = 2W 1D, Q (5) = 1W 2D, R (2) = 2D 1L, S (1) = 1D 2L; P and Q never lose, so P–Q is a draw, P beats R and S, R's other games are draws, and Q's win must be over S.

Question 6 · Quantitative Ability

A trader buys goods at a 20% discount on the list price and sells them at 10% above the list price. What is his profit percentage?

  1. 30%
  2. 37.5%
  3. 32.5%
  4. 40%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 37.5%

Cost = 0.8L, selling price = 1.1L, so profit = 0.3L/0.8L = 37.5%.

Question 7 · Verbal Ability & Reading Comprehension

Read the passage and answer the question. A translator of poetry faces a choice that prose translators can often evade. Prose carries most of its meaning in its propositions, so a faithful paraphrase preserves most of what matters. In a poem, however, the sound, the line break and the ambiguity of a single word may be the meaning. To translate the propositions alone is to deliver a summary; to chase the music is to write a new poem. The best translators accept that loss is certain and decide, case by case, which loss the poem can best survive. The phrase 'which loss the poem can best survive' implies that translators should:

  1. judge which elements are least essential to a particular poem's effect
  2. avoid translating poems whose meaning depends on sound
  3. translate only the literal propositions of every poem
  4. add new material to make up for every feature that is lost
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. judge which elements are least essential to a particular poem's effect

Choosing which loss a poem 'can best survive' means deciding, case by case, what the poem can most afford to give up.

Question 8 · Data Interpretation & Logical Reasoning

In a survey of 200 students: 120 study Maths, 90 Physics and 70 Chemistry; 50 study both Maths and Physics, 40 both Maths and Chemistry, 30 both Physics and Chemistry, and 20 study all three. How many students study exactly one of the three subjects?

  1. 80
  2. 110
  3. 120
  4. 100
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 100

Only Maths = 120 − 50 − 40 + 20 = 50, only Physics = 90 − 50 − 30 + 20 = 30, only Chemistry = 70 − 40 − 30 + 20 = 20; total = 100.

Question 9 · Quantitative Ability

Two trains start at the same time from stations X and Y towards each other. After they meet, the train from X takes 9 hours to reach Y and the train from Y takes 16 hours to reach X. What is the ratio of the speed of the train from X to that of the train from Y?

  1. 4 : 3
  2. 3 : 4
  3. 16 : 9
  4. 9 : 16
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 4 : 3

Speed ratio = √(time of other after meeting / own time after meeting) = √(16/9) = 4 : 3.

Question 10 · Verbal Ability & Reading Comprehension

Read the passage and answer the question. Cities have long planted trees for shade, but planners now value them for a subtler reason. A street tree cools its surroundings less by blocking sunlight than by transpiring water, which absorbs heat as it evaporates. A tree's cooling benefit therefore depends on its access to water: a drought-stressed tree closes its pores and cools far less, precisely during the hot, dry spells when cooling is most needed. Planting programmes that count trees but ignore soil volume and irrigation may therefore deliver less relief than their numbers suggest. Which of the following is best supported by the passage?

  1. Shade from trees plays no role in cooling cities
  2. Two cities with the same number of street trees may get very different cooling benefits from them
  3. Drought-stressed trees make streets hotter than streets with no trees at all
  4. Irrigating street trees costs more than planting new ones
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Two cities with the same number of street trees may get very different cooling benefits from them

Since cooling depends on water access, equal tree counts can yield unequal cooling; the other options are extreme or unsupported.

Question 11 · Data Interpretation & Logical Reasoning

Five tasks J, K, L, M and N are scheduled one per day from Monday to Friday. • K is on the day immediately after J. • L is on neither Monday nor Friday. • M is on Wednesday. • N is on an earlier day than J. Which task is scheduled on Tuesday?

  1. N
  2. L
  3. J
  4. K
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. L

With M on Wednesday, J–K must be Mon–Tue or Thu–Fri; N before J rules out Mon–Tue, so J = Thu, K = Fri; L cannot be Monday, so L = Tuesday and N = Monday.

Question 12 · Quantitative Ability

The roots of x² − 13x + k = 0 differ by 7. What is the value of k?

  1. 36
  2. 30
  3. 40
  4. 42
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 30

Roots r and r + 7 sum to 13, so r = 3 and the roots are 3 and 10; k = 3 × 10 = 30.

Question 13 · Verbal Ability & Reading Comprehension

Read the passage and answer the question. Cities have long planted trees for shade, but planners now value them for a subtler reason. A street tree cools its surroundings less by blocking sunlight than by transpiring water, which absorbs heat as it evaporates. A tree's cooling benefit therefore depends on its access to water: a drought-stressed tree closes its pores and cools far less, precisely during the hot, dry spells when cooling is most needed. Planting programmes that count trees but ignore soil volume and irrigation may therefore deliver less relief than their numbers suggest. The last sentence of the passage primarily serves to:

  1. introduce a new scientific mechanism of cooling
  2. reject the idea that trees cool cities at all
  3. draw a practical implication for how tree-planting programmes should be assessed
  4. summarise the history of urban tree planting
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. draw a practical implication for how tree-planting programmes should be assessed

It applies the preceding explanation to planting programmes, warning that tree counts alone overstate relief.

Question 14 · Data Interpretation & Logical Reasoning

A player starts with ₹100. In each round she bets exactly half of the money she currently holds; if she wins she gains the amount bet, and if she loses she loses it. After 4 rounds she has won exactly 2 and lost exactly 2 (in some order). How much money does she have?

  1. ₹100
  2. ₹75
  3. ₹56.25
  4. ₹62.50
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹56.25

A win multiplies her money by 1.5 and a loss by 0.5, so in any order she ends with 100 × 1.5² × 0.5² = ₹56.25.

Question 15 · Quantitative Ability

How many integers x satisfy |x − 3| + |x + 2| ≤ 11?

  1. 11
  2. 13
  3. 12
  4. 10
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 12

For −2 ≤ x ≤ 3 the sum is 5; for x > 3, 2x − 1 ≤ 11 gives x ≤ 6; for x < −2, 1 − 2x ≤ 11 gives x ≥ −5; integers −5 to 6 number 12.
