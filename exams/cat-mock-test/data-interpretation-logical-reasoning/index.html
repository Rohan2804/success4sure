Data Interpretation & Logical Reasoning: CAT practice questions

15 free questions with answers and explanations on Data Interpretation & Logical Reasoning, from the CAT (Common Admission Test) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this section covers

Data Interpretation and Logical Reasoning (DILR) in CAT is built around sets: a table, chart or puzzle followed by several questions. Typical sets include arrangements, scheduling, tournaments, selections with conditions, games, Venn-type counting and data tables needing percentage or ratio work. Accuracy matters more than attempting every set, so the key skill is choosing which sets to solve. When practising, draw clear grids, list every condition, test cases systematically, and keep quick mental tools for percentage change, averages and ratios ready.

Data Interpretation & Logical Reasoning questions

Question 1

Quarterly sales (units) of four products: Product | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 A | 240 | 300 | 330 B | 180 | 216 | 270 C | 300 | 270 | 324 D | 150 | 195 | 210 Which product recorded the highest percentage growth in sales from Q1 to Q3?

  1. C
  2. B
  3. D
  4. A
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. B

Growth Q1 to Q3: A 90/240 = 37.5%, B 90/180 = 50%, C 24/300 = 8%, D 60/150 = 40%. B leads even though A has the same absolute increase of 90 units.

Question 2

Quarterly sales (units) of four products: Product | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 A | 240 | 300 | 330 B | 180 | 216 | 270 C | 300 | 270 | 324 D | 150 | 195 | 210 By approximately what percentage did the combined sales of the four products rise from Q1 to Q2?

  1. 11.3%
  2. 12.8%
  3. 10.6%
  4. 14.2%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 12.8%

Total Q1 = 240+180+300+150 = 870; total Q2 = 300+216+270+195 = 981. Increase = 111/870 × 100 ≈ 12.8%.

Question 3

Quarterly sales (units) of four products: Product | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 A | 240 | 300 | 330 B | 180 | 216 | 270 C | 300 | 270 | 324 D | 150 | 195 | 210 For how many products did sales increase in BOTH transitions, Q1 to Q2 and Q2 to Q3?

  1. 1
  2. 2
  3. 4
  4. 3
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 3

A, B and D rise in both quarters. C falls from 300 to 270 in Q2 before recovering to 324, so it does not qualify. Answer: 3.

Question 4

Employees of a company by department: Department | Employees | Women (%) HR | 80 | 45 Sales | 250 | 32 Tech | 400 | 25 Finance | 120 | 40 Ops | 150 | 20 How many women work in the company in total?

  1. 294
  2. 286
  3. 302
  4. 312
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 294

Women: HR 36, Sales 80, Tech 100, Finance 48, Ops 30. Total = 294, which is 29.4% of the 1,000 employees.

Question 5

Employees of a company by department: Department | Employees | Women (%) HR | 80 | 45 Sales | 250 | 32 Tech | 400 | 25 Finance | 120 | 40 Ops | 150 | 20 What is the ratio of men in Sales to men in Finance?

  1. 25 : 12
  2. 17 : 6
  3. 85 : 36
  4. 8 : 3
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 85 : 36

Men in Sales = 250 − 80 = 170; men in Finance = 120 − 48 = 72. Ratio 170 : 72 = 85 : 36. 25 : 12 wrongly compares total employees.

Question 6

Six friends G, H, I, J, K and L sit in a row facing north in seats 1 to 6 (seat 1 is at the extreme left). • J and H sit at the two ends. • H sits immediately to the right of K. • Exactly two people sit between I and J. • L is not adjacent to I. • G sits somewhere to the left of K. Who sits in seat 3?

  1. K
  2. L
  3. I
  4. G
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. G

J and H take the ends and K is immediately left of H, so H is at 6, K at 5, J at 1. I is three seats from J, at 4. L cannot be next to I, so L is at 2 and G at 3.

Question 7

Six friends G, H, I, J, K and L sit in a row facing north in seats 1 to 6 (seat 1 is at the extreme left). • J and H sit at the two ends. • H sits immediately to the right of K. • Exactly two people sit between I and J. • L is not adjacent to I. • G sits somewhere to the left of K. How many people sit between L and K?

  1. 1
  2. 2
  3. 0
  4. 3
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 2

The only valid arrangement from left is J, L, G, I, K, H. L is in seat 2 and K in seat 5, so G and I sit between them: two people.

Question 8

A committee of exactly three is to be chosen from P, Q, R, S, T and U, subject to: • P and Q cannot both be chosen. • If R is chosen, S must be chosen. • T can be chosen only if U is chosen. How many different committees are possible?

  1. 12
  2. 7
  3. 8
  4. 10
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 8

Listing valid sets: PRS, PSU, PTU, QRS, QSU, QTU, RSU, STU. Any set with R but not S, T but not U, or both P and Q is excluded, leaving 8.

Question 9

One of four suspects A, B, C and D broke a window. Exactly one of the following statements is true. A: "B did it." B: "D did it." C: "I did not do it." D: "B is lying." Who broke the window?

  1. C
  2. B
  3. D
  4. A
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. C

B's and D's statements contradict, so exactly one of them is true. Hence A and C are both false. C's statement being false means C did it; then B is false and D is true, as required.

Question 10

On a grid of streets, Rina walks from corner (0, 0) to corner (4, 3), moving only one block east or one block north at a time. The corner (2, 1) is closed. How many different shortest routes can she take?

  1. 18
  2. 17
  3. 20
  4. 35
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 17

All shortest routes: C(7,3) = 35. Routes through (2,1): C(3,1) × C(4,2) = 3 × 6 = 18. Routes avoiding it = 35 − 18 = 17.

Question 11

If 1 January 2025 was a Wednesday, what day of the week was 1 January 2029?

  1. Monday
  2. Tuesday
  3. Saturday
  4. Sunday
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Monday

From 2025 to 2028 there are 3 ordinary years (1 odd day each) and the leap year 2028 (2 odd days): 5 odd days. Wednesday + 5 = Monday.

Question 12

Five students scored whole-number marks (out of 100) in a test. The average is 72, the median is 75, and 80 is the only mode. What is the highest possible value of the lowest score?

  1. 60
  2. 55
  3. 62
  4. 65
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 62

Since 80 must appear at least twice and exceed the median, the top two scores are 80 and 80. The lowest two sum to 360 − 75 − 160 = 125 and must differ, so the best split is 62 and 63.

Question 13

There are 10 identical-looking coins, one of which is slightly heavier. Using only a two-pan balance, what is the minimum number of weighings that guarantees finding the heavier coin?

  1. 4
  2. 5
  3. 2
  4. 3
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 3

Each weighing has three outcomes, so k weighings separate at most 3^k coins. 3² = 9 < 10 ≤ 27 = 3³, so three weighings are needed and suffice.

Question 14

A shop's monthly sales (₹ lakh) for January to May were 48, 55, 42, 60 and 51. The average for January to June was ₹52 lakh. In how many of the six months were sales above the six-month average?

  1. 1
  2. 3
  3. 2
  4. 4
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 3

June = 6 × 52 − (48+55+42+60+51) = 312 − 256 = 56. Months above 52: February (55), April (60) and June (56), i.e. three months.

Question 15

Five boxes V, W, X, Y and Z are stacked one above another (position 1 is the bottom). • X is somewhere above W. • Exactly one box lies between Y and X. • Z is directly above V. • W is not at the bottom. • Y is not at the top. • V is not at the bottom. Which box is at the top?

  1. W
  2. X
  3. Y
  4. Z
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Z

Testing placements, the only arrangement meeting all conditions from bottom to top is Y, W, X, V, Z. Z sits directly on V at the top, X is above W, and exactly one box (W) lies between Y and X.
