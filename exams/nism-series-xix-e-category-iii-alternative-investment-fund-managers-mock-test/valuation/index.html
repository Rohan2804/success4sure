Valuation: NISM Series XIX-E practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Valuation, from the NISM Series XIX-E (Category III Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how a Category III fund values its holdings to compute NAV. It covers the principles of fair value, mark-to-market for liquid listed securities and exchange-traded derivatives, valuation of thinly traded or suspended securities, unlisted and OTC instruments, the role of independent valuers and valuation committees, and SEBI's expectations on valuation policy and independence. Focus on why consistent, independent valuation matters for fair treatment of incoming and outgoing investors and for performance-fee calculations.

Valuation questions

Question 1

If a Category III fund overstates its NAV, which group is harmed directly?

  1. Only the fund's custodian
  2. Nobody, because NAV errors cancel out
  3. Remaining and incoming investors, while redeeming investors are overpaid
  4. Only the stock exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Remaining and incoming investors, while redeeming investors are overpaid

An inflated NAV pays redeeming investors more than their fair share and charges new investors too much, leaving the fund poorer. It can also lead to excess performance fees.

Question 2

A listed stock held by the fund is suspended from trading for two months. The most appropriate valuation approach is to:

  1. Keep using the last traded price indefinitely without any review until trading in the stock resumes
  2. Value it at zero immediately in every case
  3. Value it at the fund's purchase price
  4. Fair value it using a documented method, such as recent fundamentals or comparables
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Fair value it using a documented method, such as recent fundamentals or comparables

A suspended stock's last price may be stale. The valuation policy should require fair valuation using reasonable inputs, with documentation and oversight, rather than mechanical rules.

Question 3

A fund holds an OTC interest rate swap with a bank. To value it independently, the administrator would most likely use:

  1. The swap's original notional amount
  2. The bank's own quote without any check
  3. The fund manager's target value
  4. A model based on current market yield curves
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A model based on current market yield curves

OTC swaps have no exchange price, so they are valued by discounting expected cash flows using current market curves. Relying only on the counterparty's or manager's numbers creates conflicts.

Question 4

The 'fair value hierarchy' gives greatest reliability to valuations based on:

  1. Management's internal estimates with no observable inputs
  2. Prices of different assets in inactive markets
  3. Quoted prices in active markets for identical assets
  4. The original cost of the investment
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Quoted prices in active markets for identical assets

Level 1 inputs — quoted prices for identical assets in active markets — are the most reliable. Level 3 inputs rely on unobservable assumptions and need greater scrutiny.

Question 5

Why does a fund's valuation committee often include members outside the investment team?

  1. To reduce the conflict arising because fees depend on NAV
  2. To make NAV calculation faster each day
  3. To avoid sharing valuations with auditors
  4. Because portfolio managers are not allowed to see prices
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. To reduce the conflict arising because fees depend on NAV

Managers earn management and performance fees based on NAV, so they have an incentive to value high. Independent members help ensure valuations are objective and defensible.

Question 6

A fund holds a large block of a small-cap stock equal to ten days' average trading volume. A conservative valuation practice might be to:

  1. Value it at a premium to the market price because the fund's stake in the company is large
  2. Exclude it from NAV until it is sold
  3. Use the highest price of the past year
  4. Consider a liquidity adjustment if the block cannot be sold at the quoted price
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Consider a liquidity adjustment if the block cannot be sold at the quoted price

The quoted price may not be achievable for a large position. Valuation policies may permit a liquidity discount, applied consistently and disclosed, to reflect realistic exit value.

Question 7

SEBI expects an AIF's valuation of investments to be carried out by:

  1. Any investor who holds the most units
  2. The distributor who sold the fund
  3. The fund's tax consultant only
  4. An independent valuer as per the valuation policy in the fund documents
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. An independent valuer as per the valuation policy in the fund documents

SEBI requires valuations by independent valuers following the methodology disclosed in the placement memorandum. Distributors and investors have conflicts and lack the mandate.

Question 8

A fund changes its valuation method for unlisted bonds in mid-year, which raises NAV by 3%. Investors should expect:

  1. No disclosure at all, since a fund's valuation methods are confidential business information
  2. Disclosure of the change, its reasons and its impact on NAV
  3. An automatic waiver of performance fees for ten years
  4. Immediate de-registration of the fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Disclosure of the change, its reasons and its impact on NAV

Valuation methods should be applied consistently, and any change must be justified and disclosed to investors. Unexplained changes that lift NAV may hide performance problems.
