Taxation: NISM Series XIX-E practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Taxation, from the NISM Series XIX-E (Category III Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how Category III AIFs and their investors are taxed in India. Unlike Category I and II, Category III AIFs do not enjoy statutory pass-through, so income is generally taxed at the fund level — for trusts, often through the representative assessee rules — and the characterisation of income (capital gains versus business income) significantly affects the rate. Learners should also understand GST on management fees, securities transaction tax, the treatment of derivative income, and tax issues for non-resident investors. Tax rates change with budgets, so focus on principles and check current rates.

Taxation questions

Question 1

A key tax difference between Category III AIFs and Category I/II AIFs is that:

  1. Category III funds are fully exempt from income tax
  2. Category III income is always taxed only in investors' hands
  3. Category III funds do not have statutory pass-through status
  4. Category I and II funds pay tax at the maximum marginal rate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Category III funds do not have statutory pass-through status

The Income-tax Act grants pass-through to Category I and II AIFs for non-business income. Category III funds lack this, so tax is generally paid at fund level, often through trustee-level taxation.

Question 2

Income from frequent trading in futures and options by a Category III fund is most likely to be treated as:

  1. Long-term capital gains
  2. Business income
  3. Income from house property
  4. Exempt agricultural income
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Business income

Derivative trading carried out as an activity is generally treated as non-speculative business income. That characterisation affects the applicable rate and how losses are set off.

Question 3

GST is generally levied on:

  1. Management and performance fees charged by the AIF manager
  2. The investor's capital contribution to the fund
  3. The sale proceeds of listed shares
  4. Dividends received by the fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Management and performance fees charged by the AIF manager

Fund management is a service, so GST applies to fees charged by the manager. Capital contributions, share sale proceeds and dividends are not taxable supplies for GST.

Question 4

Securities Transaction Tax (STT) is relevant to a Category III fund because it is:

  1. A tax on the fund manager's personal salary
  2. Levied on purchases and sales of listed securities and derivatives on exchanges
  3. Payable only on unlisted debentures
  4. A tax collected on investors' redemptions from bank fixed deposits and savings accounts
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Levied on purchases and sales of listed securities and derivatives on exchanges

STT is charged on exchange-traded transactions in equity shares, equity-oriented units and derivatives. It adds to trading costs, which matters for high-turnover strategies.

Question 5

For a Category III AIF set up as a determinate trust, where shares of beneficiaries are known, the tax is generally:

  1. Waived entirely because trusts are charitable
  2. Paid by the fund's custodian from its own funds, since the custodian holds the trust's assets
  3. Assessed on the trustee as representative assessee in respect of each beneficiary's share
  4. Postponed until the fund is wound up
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Assessed on the trustee as representative assessee in respect of each beneficiary's share

For determinate trusts, the trustee is taxed as representative assessee in the same way and to the same extent as the beneficiaries would be. Indeterminate trusts can face tax at the maximum marginal rate.

Question 6

Why does the characterisation of a Category III fund's equity gains as capital gains or business income matter?

  1. Business income is completely exempt from tax in India
  2. Capital gains are taxed only if the fund is listed abroad, while business income is never taxed in India
  3. Capital gains on listed equity can attract concessional rates, while business income is taxed at normal rates
  4. There is no difference since both are taxed identically
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Capital gains on listed equity can attract concessional rates, while business income is taxed at normal rates

Capital gains on listed equity enjoy specific rates, often lower than the slab or maximum rates on business income. Funds therefore document their intention and holding pattern carefully.

Question 7

A non-resident investor in a Category III AIF should check a tax treaty mainly because it may:

  1. Allow the investor to skip KYC requirements
  2. Affect how the investor's income from India is taxed or credited at home
  3. Guarantee that the fund will never make a loss on the investor's capital in any year
  4. Let the investor avoid SEBI's minimum investment
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Affect how the investor's income from India is taxed or credited at home

Double taxation avoidance agreements allocate taxing rights and provide relief for tax paid in India. They do not affect KYC, minimum investment or investment risk.

Question 8

Because Category III tax rules and rates change with Union Budgets, the most reliable practice for an investor is to:

  1. Assume last year's rates will apply forever
  2. Check the current law and the fund's tax disclosures before investing
  3. Rely on informal opinions shared on social media
  4. Ignore taxes completely, since they never make any real difference to net returns over time
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Check the current law and the fund's tax disclosures before investing

Tax can materially change post-tax returns. Investors should refer to current provisions and the PPM's tax section, and take professional advice where needed.
