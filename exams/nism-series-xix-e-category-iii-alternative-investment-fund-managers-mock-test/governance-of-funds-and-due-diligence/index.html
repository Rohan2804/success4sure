Governance of Funds and Due Diligence: NISM Series XIX-E practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Governance of Funds and Due Diligence, from the NISM Series XIX-E (Category III Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how Category III funds are governed and how investors assess them. Governance topics include the roles of the trustee, investment committee, compliance officer, conflicts of interest policies, trade allocation, personal trading rules and code of conduct. Due diligence includes investment due diligence (strategy, edge, risk management, track record) and operational due diligence (service providers, valuation, cash controls, IT and cyber security, compliance). Focus on red flags and on the practical questions an investor should ask.

Governance of Funds and Due Diligence questions

Question 1

Operational due diligence on a Category III fund focuses mainly on:

  1. Controls, service providers, valuation and cash-movement processes
  2. The fund's expected return for the next year
  3. The manager's views on the economy
  4. Which stocks the manager plans to buy next
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Controls, service providers, valuation and cash-movement processes

Operational due diligence checks whether the fund's infrastructure can protect assets and produce reliable numbers. Many hedge fund failures stem from operational weaknesses rather than poor investment ideas.

Question 2

Which of the following is a red flag in due diligence of a Category III fund?

  1. The fund uses an established custodian and independent administrator
  2. The fund has a written personal trading policy for staff
  3. The manager uses a small, unknown auditor and refuses to share audited accounts
  4. The fund's risk management team reports to someone other than the portfolio manager who takes positions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The manager uses a small, unknown auditor and refuses to share audited accounts

An obscure auditor and refusal to share audited statements prevent verification of assets and returns. The other choices are signs of good governance and independence.

Question 3

A fund manager's employees trade personally in stocks the fund also trades. The best governance control is:

  1. Allowing staff to trade freely if they disclose annually
  2. Banning only the fund manager from personal trading
  3. Requiring staff to trade only after the fund's NAV rises
  4. Pre-clearance of personal trades and restricted lists
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Pre-clearance of personal trades and restricted lists

Pre-clearance and restricted lists prevent front-running and misuse of fund information. Annual disclosure alone comes too late to stop conflicts arising.

Question 4

A manager runs two funds with different fee rates and allocates the best IPO allotments to the higher-fee fund. This is a breach of:

  1. The custodian's safekeeping duties
  2. Fair trade allocation among clients
  3. The auditor's independence requirement
  4. The fund's leverage limit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Fair trade allocation among clients

Managers must allocate trades fairly according to a pre-defined policy. Favouring the fund that pays more fees harms the other fund's investors and is a conflict of interest.

Question 5

Why do investors prefer a fund's risk management function to be independent of portfolio managers?

  1. So that portfolio managers can override the risk limits whenever markets become volatile
  2. So that the fund can avoid regulatory reporting
  3. So that risk limits can be enforced without pressure from those taking the risk
  4. So that the risk team can pick the stocks
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. So that risk limits can be enforced without pressure from those taking the risk

An independent risk function can challenge positions and escalate breaches. If it reports to the portfolio manager, limits may be ignored when they are most needed.

Question 6

During investment due diligence, an investor finds that the fund's track record comes from a previous employer. The investor should check:

  1. Only that the returns were above the Nifty 50
  2. Whether the manager has a large social media following and is often quoted in the financial press
  3. That the PPM contains the word 'guaranteed'
  4. Whether the record is attributable to this manager and verified independently
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Whether the record is attributable to this manager and verified independently

Portable track records may include work by other team members or different conditions. Investors should verify attribution and accuracy rather than accept headline numbers.

Question 7

A Category III fund's compliance officer is expected to:

  1. Decide the fund's asset allocation
  2. Approve the custodian's own audit
  3. Market the fund to new investors
  4. Monitor adherence to SEBI regulations and report non-compliance
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Monitor adherence to SEBI regulations and report non-compliance

The compliance officer ensures that the fund follows regulations and its own documents, and reports breaches. Investment decisions and marketing are outside this role.

Question 8

Why is cyber-security and data protection now part of operational due diligence?

  1. Because SEBI requires all AIFs to trade only online
  2. Breaches can lead to theft of assets, data or trading strategies
  3. Because cyber-security improves investment returns directly
  4. Because it replaces the need for a custodian
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Breaches can lead to theft of assets, data or trading strategies

Funds hold sensitive investor data and proprietary models, and payment fraud is a real risk. Weak cyber controls can cause losses that have nothing to do with market risk.
