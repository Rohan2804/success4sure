AIF Ecosystem: NISM Series XIX-E practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on AIF Ecosystem, from the NISM Series XIX-E (Category III Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter describes the participants around a Category III AIF: sponsor, investment manager, trustee or designated partners, custodian, prime broker, fund administrator and registrar, auditor, valuer, legal advisors, distributors and investors. Learners should know who does what — for example, who holds assets, who computes NAV, who provides financing and stock borrowing, and who safeguards investors' interests. Understand the conflicts and counterparty risks that arise among these parties and why independent service providers improve governance.

AIF Ecosystem questions

Question 1

In a Category III AIF set up as a trust, the sponsor is best described as the entity that:

  1. Holds the fund's securities in safe custody on behalf of investors
  2. Provides audit opinions on the fund's annual financial statements
  3. Sets up the AIF and contributes the required continuing interest
  4. Acts as the exchange where the fund's units are traded
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Sets up the AIF and contributes the required continuing interest

The sponsor establishes the fund and must keep a continuing interest, aligning it with investors. Custody, audit and trading venues are provided by separate parties.

Question 2

A prime broker typically provides a Category III fund with:

  1. Statutory audit and tax filing for the fund's investors
  2. Registration of the fund with SEBI and filing of all regulatory reports on the manager's behalf
  3. Financing, securities lending and consolidated clearing services
  4. Independent valuation of unlisted securities for NAV purposes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Financing, securities lending and consolidated clearing services

Prime brokers bundle services such as margin financing, stock borrowing for short sales, clearing and reporting. Audit, registration and independent valuation are performed by other parties.

Question 3

Why does SEBI require a custodian to be appointed for AIFs?

  1. To decide which stocks the fund should buy or sell
  2. To keep the fund's securities separate from the manager for investor protection
  3. To guarantee investors against any fall in NAV
  4. To market the fund's units to new investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. To keep the fund's securities separate from the manager for investor protection

Independent custody reduces the risk that assets are misused, as the manager cannot directly control safekeeping. The custodian does not make investment decisions or guarantee returns.

Question 4

A distributor of a Category III AIF learns that the fund pays higher trail commission than another suitable fund. The distributor should:

  1. Recommend the higher-paying fund without telling the client
  2. Rebate part of the commission secretly to win the client
  3. Recommend based on client suitability and disclose the commission received
  4. Avoid discussing fees altogether because AIF investors are sophisticated and can find out for themselves
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Recommend based on client suitability and disclose the commission received

Distributors must put suitability first and disclose the commission they earn so the investor can judge any conflict. Hidden commissions or secret rebates breach conduct norms.

Question 5

The role of the fund administrator in an open-ended Category III fund includes:

  1. Selecting the fund's long and short positions every day
  2. Providing leverage to the fund through margin loans
  3. Approving the fund's registration as an AIF
  4. Maintaining fund accounts and processing subscriptions and redemptions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Maintaining fund accounts and processing subscriptions and redemptions

Administrators handle accounting, investor records, capital activity and NAV-related work. Portfolio decisions belong to the manager, financing comes from the prime broker and registration is granted by SEBI.

Question 6

Concentrating all prime brokerage with a single provider exposes a fund to:

  1. Market risk from a sudden fall in the Nifty index
  2. Counterparty risk if that provider faces financial distress
  3. Inflation risk on the fund's cash holdings
  4. Currency risk on a purely rupee portfolio
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Counterparty risk if that provider faces financial distress

If the sole prime broker fails, the fund's collateral and positions may be frozen. Many funds use multiple prime brokers to diversify this counterparty exposure.

Question 7

The investment manager of an AIF is primarily responsible for:

  1. Making investment decisions in line with the fund documents and regulations
  2. Safekeeping of all securities held by the fund in its demat account and settling every trade
  3. Certifying the fund's accounts as the statutory auditor
  4. Resolving investor complaints as the regulator
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Making investment decisions in line with the fund documents and regulations

The manager runs the portfolio and must follow the private placement memorandum and SEBI rules. Custody, statutory audit and regulation are separate roles.

Question 8

A Category III fund appoints an auditor who is a close relative of the fund manager. The main concern is that:

  1. The fund's returns will automatically fall
  2. The auditor's independence may be compromised
  3. SEBI will cancel the fund's registration immediately
  4. The custodian will be unable to hold the fund's securities
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The auditor's independence may be compromised

Auditors must be independent so they can challenge the manager's accounts and valuations. A close relationship creates a conflict that weakens the assurance investors receive.
