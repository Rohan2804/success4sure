Modern Portfolio Theory and Capital Market Theory: NISM Series XIX-E practice questions

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers Markowitz's mean-variance framework and the Capital Asset Pricing Model. Key ideas include diversification and correlation, the efficient frontier, the minimum variance portfolio, the capital allocation line, the Capital Market Line, the Security Market Line, beta and systematic versus unsystematic risk. It also introduces criticisms and extensions such as multi-factor models and why hedge-fund returns often do not fit normal-distribution assumptions. Practise CAPM and portfolio beta calculations, and understand the difference between total risk (standard deviation) and market risk (beta).

Modern Portfolio Theory and Capital Market Theory questions

Question 1

The risk-free rate is 6%, the expected market return is 13% and a stock's beta is 0.8. Under CAPM, its required return is:

  1. 10.4%
  2. 13.0%
  3. 11.6%
  4. 16.4%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 11.6%

CAPM: required return = Rf + beta × (Rm − Rf) = 6% + 0.8 × 7% = 6% + 5.6% = 11.6%. A beta below 1 implies a return below the market's 13%.

Question 2

Adding a new asset to a portfolio will reduce portfolio risk the most when its correlation with the existing portfolio is:

  1. Exactly +1
  2. Slightly positive
  3. Strongly negative
  4. Equal to the asset's beta
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Strongly negative

The lower the correlation, the greater the diversification benefit. Perfect positive correlation gives no risk reduction, while negative correlation lets losses in one holding be offset by gains in the other.

Question 3

Portfolios lying below the efficient frontier are:

  1. Unattainable, because no combination of the available assets can ever produce them
  2. Sub-optimal, because another portfolio offers higher return for the same risk
  3. Optimal for investors who are highly risk-averse
  4. Always made up only of the risk-free asset
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Sub-optimal, because another portfolio offers higher return for the same risk

The efficient frontier shows the best return for each level of risk. Anything below it is dominated. Portfolios above the frontier are the ones that cannot be achieved.

Question 4

Under CAPM, which risk is rewarded with a higher expected return?

  1. Company-specific risk that can be diversified away
  2. Total risk measured by standard deviation alone
  3. Systematic risk measured by beta
  4. Liquidity risk of the investor's own bank account
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Systematic risk measured by beta

CAPM assumes investors hold diversified portfolios, so only non-diversifiable market risk earns a premium. Unsystematic risk can be eliminated by diversification and so earns no extra return.

Question 5

The Security Market Line shows a stock's required return of 12%, but analysts expect 15%. According to CAPM, the stock is:

  1. Overvalued, with a negative alpha of 3%
  2. Correctly priced because it lies on the CML
  3. Risk-free because its return exceeds the requirement
  4. Undervalued, with a positive alpha of 3%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Undervalued, with a positive alpha of 3%

When expected return exceeds the CAPM-required return, the stock plots above the SML and appears underpriced. The 3% excess is its alpha. That does not make the stock risk-free.

Question 6

Many hedge-fund return series show negative skewness and fat tails. The main implication for risk measurement is that:

  1. Standard deviation alone may understate the chance of large losses
  2. The Sharpe ratio becomes a perfect and complete measure of the fund's risk-adjusted return
  3. Beta to the equity market will always be exactly zero
  4. Returns can be assumed to follow a normal distribution
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Standard deviation alone may understate the chance of large losses

Fat tails mean extreme outcomes happen more often than a normal distribution predicts, and negative skew means big losses are more likely than big gains. Measures relying on standard deviation can therefore mislead.

Question 7

A fund has ₹100 crore, with 70% in a portfolio of beta 1.2 and the rest in cash (beta 0). It then sells index futures worth ₹40 crore. The fund's approximate beta becomes:

  1. 0.84
  2. 1.20
  3. 0.40
  4. 0.44
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 0.44

Equity beta exposure = 0.7 × 1.2 = 0.84. Short futures of ₹40 crore on ₹100 crore reduce beta by 0.40. Net beta = 0.84 − 0.40 = 0.44.

Question 8

Arbitrage Pricing Theory and multi-factor models extend CAPM mainly by:

  1. Allowing several systematic risk factors to explain expected returns
  2. Assuming that the market portfolio is the one and only source of priced risk for all assets
  3. Removing the need for any risk-free rate in pricing
  4. Proving that diversification increases unsystematic risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Allowing several systematic risk factors to explain expected returns

Multi-factor models use factors such as size, value, momentum or macro variables, each with its own sensitivity. CAPM uses only one factor, the market portfolio.
