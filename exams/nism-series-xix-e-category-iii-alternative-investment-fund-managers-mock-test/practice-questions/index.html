Free NISM Series XIX-E practice questions

Question 1 · Investments Landscape

An investor's 'risk capacity' differs from 'risk tolerance' in that capacity refers to:

  1. The investor's emotional comfort with volatility
  2. The financial ability to bear losses given income, wealth and obligations
  3. The fund's leverage limit
  4. The volatility of the benchmark
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The financial ability to bear losses given income, wealth and obligations

Capacity is objective (finances); tolerance is psychological willingness.

Question 2 · Types of Investments

A fund buys a put option with strike ₹1,000 for a premium of ₹30. At expiry the underlying is at ₹940. The net payoff per unit is:

  1. ₹60 profit
  2. ₹30 profit
  3. ₹30 loss
  4. ₹90 profit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹30 profit

Intrinsic value = 1,000 − 940 = ₹60; net = 60 − 30 premium = ₹30 profit.

Question 3 · Modern Portfolio Theory and Capital Market Theory

The Capital Market Line (CML) plots:

  1. Expected return against beta for individual securities
  2. Bond yields against maturity
  3. NAV against time
  4. Expected return against total risk for efficient portfolios combining the risk-free asset and the market portfolio
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Expected return against total risk for efficient portfolios combining the risk-free asset and the market portfolio

The CML uses standard deviation and applies to efficient portfolios; the SML uses beta for any security.

Question 4 · AIFs in India and Its Suitability

Long-only listed-equity Category III AIFs compete with PMS. A key structural difference is that in a Category III AIF:

  1. Investors hold each stock directly in their demat account
  2. Investors hold units of a pooled fund rather than securities in their own names
  3. There is no fund manager
  4. There is no minimum investment
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Investors hold units of a pooled fund rather than securities in their own names

AIFs pool money and issue units; PMS holds securities in each client's own account.

Question 5 · AIF Ecosystem

Which service provider computes and validates the NAV of an open-ended Category III fund?

  1. Placement agent
  2. Fund accountant / fund administrator
  3. Investee company
  4. Stock exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Fund accountant / fund administrator

Fund administration includes independent NAV computation and investor record-keeping.

Question 6 · AIF Structuring

In an open-ended Category III fund, 'equalisation' or series accounting is used to:

  1. Equalise returns across all investors regardless of entry
  2. Ensure each investor bears performance fees only on gains they actually earned
  3. Equalise management fees with performance fees
  4. Convert the fund to close-ended
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Ensure each investor bears performance fees only on gains they actually earned

Investors enter at different NAVs, so equalisation or series of units allocates performance fees fairly.

Question 7 · Fee Structure and Fund Performance

A Category III fund charges 2% management fee and 20% performance fee over a 10% hurdle (no catch-up). The gross return before fees is 25% on ₹100 NAV. Ignoring the management fee's effect on the hurdle, the performance fee per unit is:

  1. ₹5
  2. ₹3
  3. ₹2
  4. ₹4.6
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹3

Excess over hurdle = 25 − 10 = ₹15; 20% × 15 = ₹3 per unit.

Question 8 · Indices and Benchmarking

A total return index (TRI) differs from a price return index because it:

  1. Excludes all dividends
  2. Is equally weighted
  3. Assumes dividends are reinvested
  4. Contains only bonds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Assumes dividends are reinvested

TRI captures both price change and reinvested dividends, making it the fair benchmark for funds that receive dividends.

Question 9 · Legal Documentation and Negotiations

The PPM of a Category III fund should clearly disclose leverage because:

  1. Leverage is not regulated
  2. It is required only for Category I
  3. It reduces investor risk
  4. Leverage magnifies both gains and losses and is central to the fund's risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Leverage magnifies both gains and losses and is central to the fund's risk

Investors must understand how much leverage is used and its impact on risk.

Question 10 · Investment Strategies

A pair trade long Bank A and short Bank B is designed primarily to profit from:

  1. A rise in the overall market
  2. A fall in interest rates only
  3. Relative outperformance of Bank A over Bank B
  4. Dividends from both banks
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Relative outperformance of Bank A over Bank B

Market and sector exposure is hedged, so the fund gains if A beats B regardless of direction.

Question 11 · Governance of Funds and Due Diligence

Which control best mitigates 'fat-finger' and unauthorised trading risk in a Category III fund?

  1. Pre-trade limits in the order system with independent risk monitoring
  2. Allowing the PM to settle trades personally
  3. Reviewing trades once a year
  4. Relying solely on the broker
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Pre-trade limits in the order system with independent risk monitoring

Automated limits and independent risk oversight prevent errors and rogue trading.

Question 12 · Valuation

For an exchange-traded derivative held by a Category III fund, the appropriate valuation is:

  1. The premium originally paid
  2. The notional value
  3. The manager's forecast price
  4. The exchange settlement or closing price on the valuation date
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The exchange settlement or closing price on the valuation date

Liquid derivatives are marked to market using exchange prices.

Question 13 · Fund Monitoring, Reporting and Exit

Value at Risk (VaR) of ₹2 crore at 95% confidence over one day means:

  1. There is a 5% probability that the one-day loss will exceed ₹2 crore under normal conditions
  2. The fund can never lose more than ₹2 crore
  3. The fund will lose ₹2 crore on 95% of days
  4. The maximum gain is ₹2 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. There is a 5% probability that the one-day loss will exceed ₹2 crore under normal conditions

VaR estimates a threshold loss at a given confidence; it does not cap the worst case.

Question 14 · Taxation

A key reason Category III AIFs have different tax outcomes from Category I and II AIFs is that:

  1. Category III cannot invest in Indian securities
  2. Category III income is always exempt
  3. Category III does not enjoy the statutory pass-through available to Category I and II
  4. Category III is taxed under GST
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Category III does not enjoy the statutory pass-through available to Category I and II

Pass-through is statutorily restricted to Category I and II AIFs.

Question 15 · Regulatory Framework

SEBI permits Category III AIFs to use leverage, but subject to:

  1. No limits at all
  2. A cap of 10 times NAV
  3. A cap on gross exposure of 2 times NAV and disclosure to investors
  4. Prior RBI approval for every trade
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A cap on gross exposure of 2 times NAV and disclosure to investors

Category III leverage is capped at 2x NAV, with leverage disclosed to investors and SEBI.
