Legal Documentation and Negotiations: NISM Series XIX-E practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Legal Documentation and Negotiations, from the NISM Series XIX-E (Category III Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the documents that govern a Category III AIF and the terms investors negotiate. Key documents include the private placement memorandum (PPM), the indenture of trust or LLP agreement, the contribution or subscription agreement, investment management agreement and side letters. Terms to understand include lock-ins, gates, side pockets, key-person clauses, most-favoured-nation clauses, fee terms, removal of the manager and reporting rights. Focus on what each clause protects and the conflicts it may create between investors.

Legal Documentation and Negotiations questions

Question 1

A 'key person' clause in an AIF's documents typically gives investors the right to:

  1. Suspend new investments or exit if named key managers leave the fund
  2. Appoint their own fund manager every year
  3. Receive a fixed dividend every quarter
  4. Avoid paying any management fee in a year in which the fund's NAV declines
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Suspend new investments or exit if named key managers leave the fund

Investors often back a fund because of specific individuals. A key person clause protects them by triggering suspension or other remedies if those people depart or stop devoting enough time.

Question 2

A large investor negotiates a lower management fee through a side letter. A most-favoured-nation (MFN) clause would allow other investors to:

  1. Cancel their commitments without notice
  2. Take over management of the fund
  3. Receive the large investor's share of profits
  4. Elect to receive similar terms granted to comparable investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Elect to receive similar terms granted to comparable investors

An MFN clause lets eligible investors see and opt into better terms given to others of similar size or type. It addresses fairness concerns created by side letters.

Question 3

The private placement memorandum of a Category III fund is primarily meant to:

  1. Guarantee that the fund will meet its target return
  2. Disclose the strategy, risks, fees, conflicts and terms to prospective investors
  3. Replace the need for any trust deed, contribution agreement or investment management agreement
  4. Serve as an advertisement to the general public
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Disclose the strategy, risks, fees, conflicts and terms to prospective investors

The PPM is the main disclosure document for an AIF. It must be balanced and comprehensive, cannot promise returns, and AIFs cannot be marketed to the general public.

Question 4

A fund's documents permit the manager to 'gate' redemptions at 15% of NAV per quarter. During a crisis, investors ask for 40% of NAV. The likely outcome is:

  1. All redemptions are paid in full immediately
  2. The fund is wound up automatically
  3. Redemptions are scaled back proportionately, with the rest carried forward
  4. Only the earliest requests received are paid in full and all the later requests are cancelled
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Redemptions are scaled back proportionately, with the rest carried forward

A gate limits total redemptions in a period to protect remaining investors from fire sales. Requests above the gate are usually met pro rata and carried forward to later periods.

Question 5

Investors in a Category III fund worry that the manager may materially change the fund's strategy after they invest. SEBI's framework addresses this by requiring that:

  1. The manager may change the strategy freely as long as returns stay positive
  2. Only the custodian needs to be told about any change in investment strategy
  3. Strategy changes are banned for the full tenure, even with unanimous approval
  4. Material changes need investor consent, and dissenters get an exit option
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Material changes need investor consent, and dissenters get an exit option

Changes to material terms of the placement memorandum require approval from a specified majority of investors, and those who disagree must be offered an exit. This protects investors who chose the fund for its stated strategy.

Question 6

Investor rights to remove the manager 'without cause' usually require:

  1. A decision by the fund's custodian alone
  2. Approval by a specified super-majority of investors
  3. Consent of the stock exchange
  4. No approval, since any single investor can do it
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Approval by a specified super-majority of investors

Removal without cause is a serious step, so documents typically require a high percentage of investor commitments to agree. This balances investor protection with stability of management.

Question 7

Before signing a contribution agreement, an investor should carefully check the 'indemnification' clause because it determines:

  1. The fund's annual target return
  2. The list of stocks the fund will buy
  3. When the fund bears costs of claims against the manager
  4. The custodian's fee structure
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. When the fund bears costs of claims against the manager

Indemnification can require the fund, and therefore investors, to pay legal costs and losses arising from claims against the manager. Investors negotiate carve-outs for fraud, gross negligence or wilful misconduct.

Question 8

Side letters with preferential terms for some investors raise the concern that:

  1. Other investors may be disadvantaged without knowing the terms
  2. The fund's NAV cannot be computed
  3. The PPM becomes legally void
  4. The fund can no longer use leverage
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Other investors may be disadvantaged without knowing the terms

Preferential liquidity or information rights may harm other investors, for example by letting some exit earlier. SEBI expects disclosure of such arrangements and fair treatment of all investors.
