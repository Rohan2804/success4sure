Regulatory Framework: NISM Series XIX-E practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Regulatory Framework, from the NISM Series XIX-E (Category III Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers SEBI (Alternative Investment Funds) Regulations, 2012 and related circulars as they apply to Category III funds. Topics include registration, eligibility of sponsor and manager, minimum corpus and investment, maximum number of investors, continuing interest, investment conditions and concentration limits, leverage limits, valuation and NAV disclosure, the compliance and certification requirements for key investment team members, investor charter and grievance redressal, and SEBI's powers to inspect and penalise. Thresholds are revised from time to time, so revise the current circulars carefully.

Regulatory Framework questions

Question 1

An AIF may raise money only through:

  1. Public issue of units through a prospectus
  2. Private placement to eligible investors
  3. Daily retail SIPs through bank branches
  4. Unsolicited cold-calls to the general public
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Private placement to eligible investors

AIFs are privately pooled vehicles and must raise money through private placement using a placement memorandum. Public advertising and retail offerings are not permitted.

Question 2

Under the AIF Regulations, the minimum investment for an investor (other than accredited investors and certain employees/directors) is:

  1. ₹10 lakh
  2. ₹50 lakh
  3. ₹25 crore
  4. ₹1 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹1 crore

The general minimum is ₹1 crore per investor, with a lower ₹25 lakh limit for employees or directors of the AIF or its manager. Accredited investors can be exempted. ₹50 lakh is the PMS minimum.

Question 3

An AIF scheme (other than an angel fund) cannot have more than how many investors?

  1. 1,000
  2. 200
  3. 50
  4. 10,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 1,000

SEBI caps each AIF scheme at 1,000 investors, keeping AIFs as private pools. Angel funds have a lower limit of 200 investors.

Question 4

SEBI requires certain key investment team members of an AIF manager to obtain which certification?

  1. The relevant NISM certification for AIF managers
  2. A certificate from the Bar Council of India
  3. An insurance agent licence from IRDAI
  4. A chartered accountancy membership only
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The relevant NISM certification for AIF managers

SEBI has made an NISM certification on AIF management mandatory for key investment team members of AIF managers, so they understand the regulations, strategies and risks. Other professional licences do not satisfy this requirement.

Question 5

Leverage employed by a Category III AIF is subject to:

  1. No limit at all since investors are sophisticated
  2. Limits set by SEBI and disclosure to investors
  3. Approval by the stock exchange for each trade
  4. A ban, since Category III cannot borrow or use derivatives
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Limits set by SEBI and disclosure to investors

Category III AIFs may use leverage, but SEBI caps gross exposure relative to NAV and requires disclosures of leverage to investors and the regulator.

Question 6

Investors in an AIF who have a grievance not resolved by the manager may approach:

  1. Only the civil court located in the city where the fund's trust is registered
  2. SEBI's SCORES platform and, thereafter, online dispute resolution
  3. The Reserve Bank of India's ombudsman
  4. The fund's distributor as final authority
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. SEBI's SCORES platform and, thereafter, online dispute resolution

SEBI-registered AIFs are covered by SCORES, and investors can escalate further through the online dispute resolution mechanism. RBI's ombudsman covers banking services, not AIFs.

Question 7

A Category III fund wants to invest a large share of its investable funds in a single listed company. It should first check:

  1. Whether the investee company has more than 1,000 employees and a long operating history
  2. Whether the company pays dividends every month
  3. The custodian's personal opinion on the stock
  4. SEBI's concentration limit on investment in one investee company
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. SEBI's concentration limit on investment in one investee company

SEBI imposes a concentration cap per investee company for Category III funds, expressed relative to NAV or investable funds. The fund must comply regardless of the manager's conviction.

Question 8

Which body grants registration to an AIF in India?

  1. Association of Mutual Funds in India (AMFI)
  2. Ministry of Corporate Affairs
  3. Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)
  4. Reserve Bank of India
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)

AIFs are registered and regulated by SEBI under the AIF Regulations, 2012. AMFI is the mutual fund industry body, and RBI regulates banks and NBFCs.
