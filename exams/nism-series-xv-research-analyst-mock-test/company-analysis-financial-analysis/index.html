What this chapter covers

This chapter covers financial statement analysis: reading the income statement, balance sheet and cash flow statement, and computing ratios for profitability, efficiency, liquidity, leverage and growth. It also covers DuPont analysis, quality of earnings, working capital cycle and common accounting red flags. When revising, practise ratio calculations from raw data, interpret trends rather than single numbers, and connect ratios back to the company's business model and competitive position.

Company Analysis – Financial Analysis questions

Question 1

A company has revenue of ₹500 crore and cost of goods sold of ₹300 crore. Its gross margin is:

  1. 60%
  2. 66.7%
  3. 30%
  4. 40%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 40%

Gross margin = (revenue − COGS) ÷ revenue = 200 ÷ 500 = 40%. 60% is COGS as a share of revenue.

Question 2

A company has trade receivables of ₹60 crore and annual sales of ₹730 crore. Using 365 days, its receivable days are:

  1. 12 days
  2. 60 days
  3. 30 days
  4. 45 days
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 30 days

Receivable days = receivables ÷ sales × 365 = 60 ÷ 730 × 365 = 30 days. It shows how long customers take to pay on average.

Question 3

Cash flow from operations is ₹150 crore and capital expenditure is ₹60 crore. Free cash flow (simple measure) is:

  1. ₹90 crore
  2. ₹210 crore
  3. ₹60 crore
  4. ₹150 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹90 crore

Free cash flow = operating cash flow − capex = 150 − 60 = ₹90 crore. This is cash available for debt repayment, dividends or growth.

Question 4

A company's sales are ₹900 crore and total assets ₹600 crore. Its asset turnover ratio is:

  1. 0.67 times
  2. 1.5 times
  3. 1.0 times
  4. 3.0 times
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 1.5 times

Asset turnover = sales ÷ total assets = 900 ÷ 600 = 1.5. It measures how efficiently assets generate revenue; 0.67 inverts the ratio.

Question 5

A rapidly growing company's receivables are rising much faster than its sales. This may indicate:

  1. Improving cash collection from customers across all regions
  2. Lower working capital needs
  3. Aggressive revenue recognition or poor collection
  4. Higher quality of earnings
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Aggressive revenue recognition or poor collection

Receivables outpacing sales can mean sales booked before cash is likely, loose credit terms or collection problems. It weakens earnings quality.

Question 6

Which ratio measures a company's ability to meet short-term obligations without relying on inventory?

  1. Quick ratio
  2. Inventory turnover
  3. Debt-to-equity ratio
  4. Return on equity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Quick ratio

The quick ratio divides current assets less inventory by current liabilities. It is stricter than the current ratio as inventory may be hard to liquidate.

Question 7

A firm reports high profits but negative operating cash flows every year. An analyst should:

  1. Ignore cash flows as unimportant
  2. Investigate working capital and accounting quality
  3. Raise the target price immediately on the strength of profits
  4. Assume profits will always turn to cash
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Investigate working capital and accounting quality

Persistent gaps between profit and cash flow can signal aggressive accounting or unsustainable working capital build-up. Analysts must dig deeper.

Question 8

Return on capital employed (ROCE) is useful because it:

  1. Ignores the debt used by the firm
  2. Shows only the dividend yield
  3. Measures stock price changes
  4. Measures returns on equity plus debt
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Measures returns on equity plus debt

ROCE = EBIT ÷ capital employed (equity + debt), showing returns on all capital regardless of financing. ROE looks only at shareholders' equity.
