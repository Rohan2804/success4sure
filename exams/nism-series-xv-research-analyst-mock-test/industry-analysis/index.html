Industry Analysis: NISM Series XV practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Industry Analysis, from the NISM Series XV (Research Analyst) syllabus.

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how analysts study industries: industry structure and concentration, Porter's five forces, industry life cycle, competitive dynamics, regulation, demand drivers, cost structure and key industry metrics. It also covers SWOT and PESTLE frameworks. When revising, apply frameworks to real industries, interpret concentration measures, identify the stage of the life cycle from data, and understand which factors give an industry pricing power and sustainable profitability.

Industry Analysis questions

Question 1

An industry has four firms with market shares of 40%, 30%, 20% and 10%. Its Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) is:

  1. 100
  2. 3,000
  3. 2,500
  4. 1,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 3,000

HHI = 40² + 30² + 20² + 10² = 1,600 + 900 + 400 + 100 = 3,000. A high HHI indicates a concentrated industry.

Question 2

In Porter's framework, which factor increases the bargaining power of buyers?

  1. Many small, scattered buyers
  2. Highly differentiated products
  3. Few buyers purchasing large volumes
  4. High switching costs for buyers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Few buyers purchasing large volumes

Concentrated buyers can negotiate lower prices. Many small buyers, differentiation and high switching costs reduce buyer power.

Question 3

An industry with rapid sales growth, new entrants and rising investment is likely in which stage?

  1. Growth stage
  2. Decline stage
  3. Maturity stage
  4. Shake-out after decline
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Growth stage

The growth stage features fast demand growth, attracting entrants and capacity additions. Maturity brings slower growth and consolidation.

Question 4

Which PESTLE factor covers a new law on data privacy affecting tech firms?

  1. Economic
  2. Social
  3. Environmental
  4. Legal
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Legal

PESTLE includes Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal and Environmental factors. A new data protection law is a legal factor.

Question 5

High fixed costs in an industry mean that:

  1. Profits swing sharply with volumes
  2. Profits are unaffected by volumes
  3. Prices never change
  4. Companies have no debt
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Profits swing sharply with volumes

With high fixed costs, operating leverage is high: small changes in volume cause large changes in profits, making earnings volatile.

Question 6

Which is a barrier to entry for new firms in an industry?

  1. Low capital requirements and no licensing at all
  2. Easy access to distribution
  3. Large capital needs and strict licensing
  4. Undifferentiated products
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Large capital needs and strict licensing

Barriers such as heavy investment, licences, patents and strong brands protect incumbents. Low capital needs and easy access invite entrants.

Question 7

The threat of substitutes is high when:

  1. No alternatives exist
  2. Customers can switch to alternatives easily
  3. Switching costs for customers are very high
  4. Products are patented
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Customers can switch to alternatives easily

When customers can easily switch to other products serving the same need, pricing power falls. High switching costs and patents reduce this threat.

Question 8

Why does regulation matter in industry analysis?

  1. It only affects tax filing deadlines
  2. It has no effect on companies
  3. It decides daily share prices
  4. It shapes pricing, entry and profits
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. It shapes pricing, entry and profits

Regulation can cap prices, restrict entry and set standards, directly affecting industry profitability. Telecom, power and pharma are clear examples.
