Question 1 · Introduction to Research Analyst Profession

Which activity is the core function of a research analyst?

  1. Executing client orders on the stock exchange
  2. Holding client securities in safe custody
  3. Analysing securities and issuing research reports with recommendations or opinions
  4. Underwriting public issues
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Analysing securities and issuing research reports with recommendations or opinions

Research analysts analyse issuers and securities and publish reports, recommendations or price targets; execution, custody and underwriting are other intermediaries' roles.

Question 2 · Introduction to Securities Market

What is the main function of a depository in the Indian securities market?

  1. Fixing the market price of shares
  2. Holding securities in electronic (dematerialised) form and recording their transfer
  3. Lending money to investors for margin trading
  4. Approving corporate actions of listed companies
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Holding securities in electronic (dematerialised) form and recording their transfer

Depositories such as NSDL and CDSL maintain electronic holdings and effect transfers through depository participants.

Question 3 · Terminology in Equity and Debt Markets

A company has equity share capital of ₹50 crore and reserves of ₹250 crore, with 10 crore shares outstanding. What is its book value per share?

  1. ₹30
  2. ₹25
  3. ₹5
  4. ₹35
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹30

Book value per share = (50 + 250) ÷ 10 = ₹30.

Question 4 · Fundamentals of Research

In the top-down approach to equity research, the analyst begins with:

  1. Selection of individual companies, then the industry
  2. Technical charts of the chosen stock
  3. The company's dividend history only
  4. Analysis of the economy, then the industry, and finally the company
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Analysis of the economy, then the industry, and finally the company

Top-down research moves from macro conditions to attractive sectors and then to companies within them; bottom-up starts with the company.

Question 5 · Economic Analysis

If the RBI raises the policy repo rate, which effect is most likely in the short run?

  1. Bank lending rates fall, boosting loan demand
  2. Bond prices rise sharply across maturities
  3. Borrowing costs rise, which tends to weigh on rate-sensitive sectors such as real estate and autos
  4. Inflation immediately rises due to cheaper credit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Borrowing costs rise, which tends to weigh on rate-sensitive sectors such as real estate and autos

A repo hike tightens liquidity and raises borrowing costs, hurting interest-sensitive demand and typically lowering bond prices.

Question 6 · Industry Analysis

Which of the following is NOT one of Porter's five competitive forces?

  1. The central bank's monetary policy stance
  2. Threat of new entrants
  3. Bargaining power of buyers
  4. Threat of substitute products
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The central bank's monetary policy stance

Porter's five forces are rivalry, threat of entrants, threat of substitutes and the bargaining power of buyers and suppliers; monetary policy is a macro factor.

Question 7 · Company Analysis – Business & Governance

Which is the best example of an economic moat?

  1. A one-time gain from sale of land
  2. A payments platform that becomes more valuable to each user as more users join
  3. A temporary spike in commodity prices
  4. A large cash balance raised through a recent IPO
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A payments platform that becomes more valuable to each user as more users join

Network effects create a durable competitive advantage that rivals find hard to replicate; one-offs are not moats.

Question 8 · Company Analysis – Financial Analysis

A company has current assets of ₹300 crore and current liabilities of ₹200 crore. What is its current ratio?

  1. 0.67
  2. 1.0
  3. 2.5
  4. 1.5
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 1.5

Current ratio = current assets ÷ current liabilities = 300 ÷ 200 = 1.5.

Question 9 · Corporate Valuation

A company has just paid a dividend of ₹10 per share. Dividends are expected to grow at 6% a year forever and the required return is 12%. What is the share's intrinsic value under the Gordon growth model?

  1. ₹166.67
  2. ₹83.33
  3. ₹176.67
  4. ₹88.33
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹176.67

D1 = 10 × 1.06 = 10.6; value = 10.6 ÷ (0.12 − 0.06) = ₹176.67. Using D0 instead of D1 would wrongly give ₹166.67.

Question 10 · Fundamentals of Risk and Return

The risk-free rate is 7%, a stock's beta is 1.3 and the expected market return is 13%. What is the stock's required return under CAPM?

  1. 16.9%
  2. 13.0%
  3. 7.8%
  4. 14.8%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 14.8%

Required return = 7 + 1.3 × (13 − 7) = 7 + 7.8 = 14.8%.

Question 11 · Qualities of a Good Research Report

Which element is essential in a good equity research report?

  1. Guaranteed returns over the target horizon
  2. Key risks that could invalidate the investment thesis
  3. The analyst's personal portfolio returns
  4. Only positive information about the company
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Key risks that could invalidate the investment thesis

A balanced report states its assumptions and the risks that could make the recommendation wrong, so readers can judge it.

Question 12 · Legal and Regulatory Environment

Under SEBI's Research Analyst Regulations, a research analyst and its associates are prohibited from trading in a security they recommend during which period around the publication of a research report?

  1. 5 days before and 30 days after publication
  2. 7 days before and 7 days after publication
  3. 30 days before and 5 days after publication
  4. Only on the day of publication
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 30 days before and 5 days after publication

The regulations bar dealing in the recommended security or its derivatives from 30 days before to 5 days after the report is published.

Question 13 · Introduction to Research Analyst Profession

An analyst at an integrated firm is asked by the investment banking team to upgrade a stock because the company is a prospective banking client. What should the analyst do?

  1. Upgrade the stock to support the banking relationship
  2. Maintain an independent view and not let the banking team influence the rating
  3. Ask the company's management to choose the rating
  4. Delay the report until the deal closes and then upgrade
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Maintain an independent view and not let the banking team influence the rating

Research must be independent and objective; firms are required to keep research insulated from investment banking pressure.

Question 14 · Introduction to Securities Market

Indices such as the Nifty 50 are weighted by free-float market capitalisation. Free float generally excludes:

  1. Shares held by retail investors
  2. Shares held by foreign portfolio investors
  3. Shares held by promoters and promoter groups
  4. Shares held by mutual funds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Shares held by promoters and promoter groups

Free float counts only shares readily available for trading, so promoter and similar locked-in holdings are excluded.

Question 15 · Terminology in Equity and Debt Markets

Yield to maturity (YTM) of a bond represents:

  1. The return earned if the bond is held to maturity and all coupons are reinvested at the same yield
  2. The coupon divided by face value
  3. The annual coupon divided by current price only
  4. The return earned if the bond is sold the next day
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The return earned if the bond is held to maturity and all coupons are reinvested at the same yield

YTM is the discount rate that equates the bond's price to its future cash flows, implicitly assuming holding to maturity and reinvestment at the YTM.
