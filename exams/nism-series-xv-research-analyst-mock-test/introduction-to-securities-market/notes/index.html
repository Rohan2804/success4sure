Introduction to Securities Market: NISM Series XV revision notes

Analysts interpret prices and volumes, so they must understand how the market itself works. This chapter covers primary and secondary markets, market infrastructure, how indices are built, settlement, liquidity and the efficient market hypothesis. Questions are mostly conceptual, with some on index construction and forms of market efficiency.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Primary vs secondary market

The primary market issues new securities through IPOs, FPOs, rights issues, QIPs and private placements, bringing money to issuers. The secondary market provides liquidity by allowing investors to trade existing securities on exchanges, which in turn makes investors willing to buy in the primary market.

2. Market participants

Participants include issuers, retail and institutional investors, FPIs, brokers, depository participants, custodians, merchant bankers, credit rating agencies, research analysts and investment advisers. Each is registered with and regulated by SEBI or other regulators.

3. Market infrastructure

Stock exchanges (NSE, BSE) provide trading; clearing corporations guarantee and settle trades through novation; depositories (NSDL, CDSL) hold securities electronically. Equity trades settle on T+1. Analysts should understand these mechanics when interpreting delivery volumes and corporate action dates.

4. How indices are built

Major Indian indices such as Nifty 50 and Sensex use free-float market capitalisation weighting: each stock's weight is based on the market value of shares available for trading, excluding promoter and locked-in holdings. A company with large promoter holding gets less weight than its full market cap suggests.

5. Uses of indices

Indices measure market performance, serve as benchmarks for funds and portfolio managers, and underlie index funds, ETFs and derivatives. Sectoral and thematic indices help analysts compare sector performance. Index rebalancing can cause large flows into or out of stocks.

6. Liquidity and impact cost

Liquidity means the ability to trade quickly without moving prices. Impact cost measures the cost of executing a trade of a given size relative to the ideal price, and is used in index eligibility. Low liquidity widens bid-ask spreads and makes reported prices less reliable.

7. Efficient market hypothesis

In the weak form, prices reflect all past price information, so technical analysis should not earn consistent excess returns. In the semi-strong form, prices reflect all public information, so fundamental analysis of public data should not either. In the strong form, prices reflect all information, including private.

8. Market efficiency in practice

Real markets are not perfectly efficient: anomalies, behavioural biases and information gaps create opportunities for skilled analysis. However, competition among analysts tends to reduce mispricing in heavily researched large caps, while small caps may be less efficiently priced.

9. Regulatory structure

SEBI regulates securities markets under the SEBI Act, SCRA and Depositories Act. RBI regulates money and government securities markets. Listed companies follow SEBI's LODR regulations on disclosures, which are a primary source of information for analysts.

Common traps in the exam

  • Using total market cap for Nifty weights. Indices use free-float market cap.
  • Mixing up weak and semi-strong forms. Weak covers past prices; semi-strong covers all public information.
  • Assuming an efficient market means prices never change. Prices change quickly as new information arrives.
  • Thinking the issuer benefits from secondary market trades. Only primary issues raise money for the issuer.

Quick recap

Primary market raises capital; secondary market provides liquidity. Exchanges trade, clearing corporations settle on T+1, depositories hold. Indices use free-float market cap weighting. Impact cost measures liquidity. EMH: weak (past prices), semi-strong (public info), strong (all info). LODR disclosures feed research.
