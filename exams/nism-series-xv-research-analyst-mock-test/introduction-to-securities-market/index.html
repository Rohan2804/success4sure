What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the securities market from an analyst's perspective: primary and secondary markets, market participants, stock exchanges, clearing corporations, depositories, indices and the regulatory structure. It also covers market efficiency and the types of securities traded. When revising, understand how trading and settlement work, how indices are built, and how market structure affects liquidity and price discovery, since these influence how analysts interpret prices and volumes.

Introduction to Securities Market questions

Question 1

A market where prices quickly reflect all publicly available information is called:

  1. Weak form efficient only, not semi-strong
  2. Strong form efficient
  3. Semi-strong form efficient
  4. Inefficient
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Semi-strong form efficient

Semi-strong efficiency means public information is rapidly priced in, so fundamental analysis of public data alone should not give consistent excess returns. Strong form also includes private information.

Question 2

Which participant provides liquidity by quoting both buy and sell prices?

  1. A market maker
  2. A depository
  3. A registrar
  4. A credit rating agency
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A market maker

Market makers stand ready to buy and sell, narrowing spreads and improving liquidity. Depositories hold securities, registrars keep records and rating agencies assess credit.

Question 3

Why do analysts watch trading volumes along with prices?

  1. Volume decides the company's dividend
  2. Volume fixes the face value of shares
  3. Volume is the same as market cap
  4. Volume shows the strength of a move
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Volume shows the strength of a move

A price move on high volume suggests broad participation and conviction; low-volume moves may be less reliable.

Question 4

Market capitalisation of a company equals:

  1. Face value × shares outstanding
  2. Share price × shares outstanding
  3. Total assets − total liabilities
  4. Net profit × P/E of the index
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Share price × shares outstanding

Market cap is the market value of all outstanding shares. Face value times shares is paid-up capital, and assets minus liabilities is book value.

Question 5

A sector index differs from a broad market index because it:

  1. Includes every listed company
  2. Is not based on prices
  3. Is computed only once a year
  4. Tracks stocks of one industry
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Tracks stocks of one industry

Sector indices such as banking or IT indices track a single industry, helping analysts compare sector performance with the broader market.

Question 6

Which is a primary market activity?

  1. A company issuing new shares in an IPO
  2. Two investors trading shares on NSE
  3. A fund selling shares on BSE
  4. A broker squaring off a client's trade
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A company issuing new shares in an IPO

Primary market activity involves new securities issued by companies. Trading existing shares among investors is secondary market activity.

Question 7

Who regulates the securities market in India?

  1. RBI
  2. SEBI
  3. IRDAI
  4. CCI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. SEBI

SEBI regulates exchanges, intermediaries, issuers' disclosures and research analysts. RBI handles banks and money markets, IRDAI insurance and CCI competition.

Question 8

Why do analysts compare a stock's return with a benchmark index?

  1. To set the stock's dividend
  2. To decide the company's tax
  3. To judge relative performance
  4. To fix the index's base value
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. To judge relative performance

Comparing returns with a benchmark shows whether a stock outperformed or underperformed the market or sector, which helps judge recommendations.
