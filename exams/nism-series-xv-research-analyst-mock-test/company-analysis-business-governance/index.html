What this chapter covers

This chapter covers qualitative analysis of a company: its business model, revenue drivers, competitive advantages or moats, management quality, strategy, capital allocation and corporate governance. It also covers promoter behaviour, board independence, related-party transactions, pledging of shares and ESG considerations. When revising, learn to identify durable advantages, assess management track record and incentives, and recognise governance red flags that should make an analyst cautious regardless of attractive financials.

Company Analysis – Business & Governance questions

Question 1

A large part of a promoter's shareholding is pledged with lenders. Why is this a concern for analysts?

  1. Pledged shares always pay higher dividends
  2. Pledging increases the company's revenue
  3. Pledged shares cannot be traded by anyone
  4. A price fall may trigger forced selling
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A price fall may trigger forced selling

If prices fall, lenders may sell pledged shares to recover dues, pushing prices lower and possibly changing control. High pledging also signals promoter financial stress.

Question 2

A company frequently changes its statutory auditors and its CFO. This is best seen as:

  1. A sign of strong growth
  2. A governance red flag
  3. A normal cost-cutting measure
  4. Proof of a strong economic moat
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A governance red flag

Frequent exits of auditors and finance heads can indicate disagreements over accounting or controls. Analysts should investigate before relying on reported numbers.

Question 3

Which is an example of a network-effect moat?

  1. A firm with the cheapest factory labour
  2. A company with a new logo
  3. A payments app more useful as users grow
  4. A business with high debt
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A payments app more useful as users grow

Network effects make a product more valuable as more people use it, creating barriers to competitors. Low labour costs can be replicated and logos are not moats.

Question 4

Capital allocation by management refers to:

  1. How profits are reinvested or returned
  2. How the share price is fixed
  3. How SEBI allocates IPO shares among investor categories
  4. How auditors are paid
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. How profits are reinvested or returned

Capital allocation covers decisions on reinvestment, acquisitions, debt reduction, dividends and buybacks. Good allocation creates value for shareholders over time.

Question 5

Which board feature supports good corporate governance?

  1. All directors from the promoter family
  2. No audit committee
  3. A CEO who also audits the accounts
  4. Truly independent directors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Truly independent directors

Independent directors provide objective oversight and protect minority shareholders. Family-dominated boards and missing audit committees weaken governance.

Question 6

In ESG analysis, a cement company's carbon emissions fall under:

  1. Social factors
  2. Governance factors
  3. Environmental factors
  4. Liquidity factors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Environmental factors

ESG covers Environmental, Social and Governance issues. Emissions and resource use are environmental factors, which can affect costs and regulation.

Question 7

A company's revenue depends heavily on a single customer. This creates:

  1. Strong pricing power
  2. Customer concentration risk
  3. A diversified revenue base
  4. Lower business risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Customer concentration risk

Dependence on one customer means losing or renegotiating that contract could hit revenue sharply. Analysts look for diversified customer bases.

Question 8

Which management behaviour builds analyst confidence?

  1. Consistently meeting stated guidance
  2. Changing strategy every quarter
  3. Avoiding analyst questions
  4. Selling large personal shareholdings shortly before results
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Consistently meeting stated guidance

Delivering on guidance shows credibility and realistic planning. Frequent strategy shifts, evasiveness and pre-result selling undermine trust.
