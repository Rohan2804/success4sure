Fundamentals of Research: NISM Series XV practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Fundamentals of Research, from the NISM Series XV (Research Analyst) syllabus.

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how research is done: fundamental, technical and quantitative approaches, top-down and bottom-up methods, sources of primary and secondary data, building financial models and forecasts, and testing assumptions. It also covers behavioural pitfalls in research. When revising, compare research approaches, know when to use each data source, and understand how to build a logical research thesis supported by evidence and sensitivity analysis.

Fundamentals of Research questions

Question 1

An analyst starts by picking strong companies regardless of the economic outlook. This is a:

  1. Bottom-up approach
  2. Top-down approach
  3. Technical approach
  4. Index approach
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Bottom-up approach

A bottom-up approach focuses on company fundamentals first. Top-down starts with the economy, then industries, then companies.

Question 2

Which is an example of secondary data?

  1. Interviews with a company's distributors
  2. A survey the analyst runs among consumers
  3. An industry report from a trade body
  4. A factory visit by the analyst
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. An industry report from a trade body

Secondary data is collected by others, such as published reports and statistics. Interviews, surveys and site visits generate primary data.

Question 3

Sensitivity analysis in a financial model shows:

  1. The company's past share prices
  2. How outputs change with key assumptions
  3. The analyst's personal portfolio holdings over the years
  4. The exchange's trading hours
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. How outputs change with key assumptions

Sensitivity analysis varies assumptions such as growth or margins to see their impact on earnings or valuation, highlighting key risks to the thesis.

Question 4

A quantitative analyst builds a model that ranks stocks on value and momentum factors. This approach relies mainly on:

  1. Management interviews only
  2. Chart patterns drawn by hand
  3. Rumours on social media
  4. Statistical data and rules
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Statistical data and rules

Quantitative research uses data and systematic rules to identify opportunities, reducing subjective judgement. It differs from discretionary fundamental or chart-based methods.

Question 5

An analyst's earnings forecast for a cement firm should be most closely linked to:

  1. The CEO's favourite colour
  2. Last week's share price movement and trading volume only
  3. The number of shareholders
  4. Volume, price and cost assumptions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Volume, price and cost assumptions

Forecasts should be built from operating drivers such as capacity utilisation, realisations and input costs, which determine revenue and margins.

Question 6

Channel checks by an analyst involve:

  1. Talking to dealers and suppliers
  2. Checking television channels
  3. Reading only the annual report
  4. Watching share price charts
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Talking to dealers and suppliers

Channel checks gather primary information from distributors, dealers and suppliers on demand and pricing trends, helping verify company claims.

Question 7

In technical analysis, a 'support' level is:

  1. A price where selling tends to cap rises
  2. The company's book value per share
  3. A price where buying tends to halt falls
  4. The 52-week high price
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A price where buying tends to halt falls

Support is a level where demand has historically stopped declines. Resistance is where selling has historically capped rises.

Question 8

An analyst updates a model only with data that supports his buy call. This is an example of:

  1. Sensitivity analysis
  2. Confirmation bias
  3. Peer comparison
  4. Scenario planning
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Confirmation bias

Confirmation bias is favouring information that supports existing views. Good research seeks disconfirming evidence and tests alternative scenarios.
