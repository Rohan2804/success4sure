Introduction to Research Analyst Profession: NISM Series XV practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Introduction to Research Analyst Profession, from the NISM Series XV (Research Analyst) syllabus.

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces the research analyst's role in capital markets: types of analysts (sell-side, buy-side, independent), the research process, the skills required, and how research supports investors, fund managers and companies. It also covers the ethical foundations of the profession: independence, objectivity, managing conflicts and protecting confidential information. When revising, understand who uses research, how analysts add value, and why independence from investment banking and trading interests is so important.

Introduction to Research Analyst Profession questions

Question 1

An independent research firm sells reports to institutional investors and has no broking or banking business. Its main advantage is:

  1. Guaranteed accuracy of forecasts
  2. Access to insider information
  3. Fewer conflicts of interest
  4. Exemption from SEBI regulation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Fewer conflicts of interest

Without broking or investment banking revenue, independent research faces fewer pressures to be positive on clients' stocks. It is still regulated and cannot use inside information.

Question 2

Which skill is most essential for an equity research analyst?

  1. Designing company logos
  2. Operating a trading terminal for clients
  3. Selling insurance policies
  4. Analysing financial statements
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Analysing financial statements

Analysts must interpret financial statements, model earnings and value businesses. Design, dealing and insurance sales are different professions.

Question 3

A buy-side analyst at a mutual fund mainly produces research for:

  1. Retail clients of a broker
  2. The fund's own portfolio managers
  3. The company being analysed
  4. The general public through newspapers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The fund's own portfolio managers

Buy-side research supports internal investment decisions of the fund. Sell-side research is distributed to clients of brokers and banks.

Question 4

Why do analysts follow a structured research process?

  1. To make conclusions consistent and well supported
  2. To ensure every report ends with a buy rating on the stock
  3. To avoid reading company disclosures
  4. To meet a quota of reports each day
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. To make conclusions consistent and well supported

A structured process of data gathering, analysis, valuation and review helps produce objective, defensible recommendations. It does not dictate ratings.

Question 5

A company management offers an analyst a free foreign holiday ahead of a results briefing. The analyst should:

  1. Decline it to protect independence
  2. Accept it and publish a positive report
  3. Accept it if the report is delayed
  4. Accept it and share it with colleagues
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Decline it to protect independence

Accepting valuable gifts from covered companies compromises objectivity. Analysts should decline such benefits or follow strict firm policies on modest hospitality.

Question 6

Which is a key way research analysts add value to markets?

  1. Fixing share prices for exchanges
  2. Guaranteeing returns to investors
  3. Improving price discovery
  4. Approving IPO documents for SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Improving price discovery

By analysing information and publishing views, analysts help prices reflect fundamentals. They do not set prices, guarantee returns or approve offer documents.

Question 7

A sell-side analyst's report is most likely to be distributed to:

  1. Only the analyst's family members
  2. Only the board of directors of the company being covered
  3. Only SEBI officials
  4. The broker's institutional and retail clients
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The broker's institutional and retail clients

Sell-side research is produced by brokers and banks and distributed to their clients to support trading and investment decisions.

Question 8

Professional scepticism in research means the analyst should:

  1. Accept management guidance without any independent checks
  2. Question management claims and verify them
  3. Rely only on market rumours
  4. Copy other analysts' estimates
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Question management claims and verify them

Analysts should test management's statements against data, industry evidence and independent sources rather than accept them at face value.
