Corporate Valuation: NISM Series XV practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Corporate Valuation, from the NISM Series XV (Research Analyst) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers methods of valuing companies: discounted cash flow (FCFF and FCFE), dividend discount models, relative valuation using P/E, P/B, EV/EBITDA and PEG, and sum-of-the-parts. It also covers cost of capital, terminal value and sensitivity of value to assumptions. When revising, practise each method numerically, know which multiples suit which industries, and understand how growth, risk and return on capital drive valuation multiples.

Corporate Valuation questions

Question 1

A stock's P/E is 30 and its expected EPS growth is 20% a year. Its PEG ratio is:

  1. 1.5
  2. 0.67
  3. 600
  4. 10
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 1.5

PEG = P/E ÷ growth (in %) = 30 ÷ 20 = 1.5. The PEG adjusts P/E for growth; lower values suggest cheaper growth.

Question 2

A company earns ROE of 18% and retains 60% of earnings. Its sustainable growth rate is:

  1. 7.2%
  2. 10.8%
  3. 18%
  4. 30%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 10.8%

Sustainable growth = ROE × retention ratio = 18% × 0.6 = 10.8%. 7.2% wrongly uses the payout ratio of 40%.

Question 3

A firm's payout ratio is 40%, cost of equity 12% and growth 8%. Its justified forward P/E using the dividend discount model is:

  1. 5
  2. 3.3
  3. 10
  4. 20
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 10

Justified forward P/E = payout ÷ (r − g) = 0.40 ÷ (0.12 − 0.08) = 10. Higher growth or lower risk would raise the justified multiple.

Question 4

A company has market cap ₹800 crore, debt ₹200 crore and cash ₹50 crore. Its enterprise value is:

  1. ₹1,050 crore
  2. ₹800 crore
  3. ₹550 crore
  4. ₹950 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹950 crore

EV = market cap + debt − cash = 800 + 200 − 50 = ₹950 crore. Adding cash instead of subtracting it gives ₹1,050 crore.

Question 5

Which multiple is most commonly used to value banks?

  1. EV/EBITDA
  2. EV/Sales
  3. Price-to-capacity
  4. Price-to-book value
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Price-to-book value

Banks' assets and liabilities are largely financial and marked close to value, so P/B (often linked to ROE) is the standard multiple. EV/EBITDA does not suit lenders.

Question 6

In a DCF valuation, free cash flow to the firm should be discounted at:

  1. The weighted average cost of capital
  2. The cost of equity only
  3. The risk-free rate
  4. The dividend yield
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The weighted average cost of capital

FCFF belongs to all capital providers, so it is discounted at WACC. FCFE is discounted at the cost of equity.

Question 7

A sum-of-the-parts valuation is most useful for:

  1. A single-product start-up with one plant and one market
  2. A bank with one lending business
  3. A conglomerate with distinct businesses
  4. A newly listed shell company
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A conglomerate with distinct businesses

SOTP values each business separately using suitable methods and adds them, which suits diversified groups whose segments have different growth and risk.

Question 8

If an analyst raises the discount rate in a DCF model, the estimated value will:

  1. Rise
  2. Fall
  3. Remain unchanged
  4. Become negative always
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Fall

A higher discount rate reduces the present value of future cash flows and terminal value, lowering the estimated intrinsic value.
