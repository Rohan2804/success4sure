Terminology in Equity and Debt Markets: NISM Series XV practice questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers key terms analysts use in equity and debt markets: face value, book value, market price, EPS, dividend, payout ratio, P/E, earnings yield, market capitalisation, corporate actions, coupon, yield to maturity, current yield, duration, credit ratings and spreads. When revising, make sure you can compute and interpret each measure, understand how corporate actions affect per-share data, and know the relationship between bond prices, coupons and yields.

Terminology in Equity and Debt Markets questions

Question 1

A stock trades at a P/E of 25. What is its earnings yield?

  1. 4%
  2. 25%
  3. 2.5%
  4. 40%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 4%

Earnings yield = 1 ÷ P/E = 1 ÷ 25 = 4%. It expresses earnings as a percentage of price and is often compared with bond yields.

Question 2

A company earns EPS of ₹20 and pays a dividend of ₹6 per share. What is its dividend payout ratio?

  1. 70%
  2. 3.3%
  3. 6%
  4. 30%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 30%

Payout ratio = DPS ÷ EPS = 6 ÷ 20 = 30%. The retention ratio is 70%, the share of earnings kept for reinvestment.

Question 3

When a bond trades at a premium to face value, its YTM is:

  1. Above its coupon rate
  2. Equal to its coupon rate
  3. Below its coupon rate
  4. Always zero
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Below its coupon rate

Paying above face value lowers the effective return, so YTM is below the coupon. Discount bonds have YTMs above their coupons.

Question 4

A rights issue at a price below market typically leads to:

  1. An automatic rise in EPS for all existing holders
  2. A fall in the share price ex-rights
  3. An increase in face value
  4. No change in share count
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A fall in the share price ex-rights

After the record date, the share price adjusts down because new shares are issued at a discount, diluting value per share. The share count rises.

Question 5

Credit spread on a corporate bond is:

  1. Its yield minus a comparable G-sec yield
  2. Its coupon minus its face value
  3. The difference between bid and ask prices
  4. The rating agency's fee
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Its yield minus a comparable G-sec yield

The credit spread measures extra yield for credit and liquidity risk over a risk-free government security of similar maturity.

Question 6

Book value per share is computed as:

  1. Market price ÷ EPS
  2. Net profit ÷ total assets
  3. Shareholders' equity ÷ shares outstanding
  4. Total debt ÷ total shareholders' equity on the balance sheet
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Shareholders' equity ÷ shares outstanding

Book value per share = net worth ÷ number of shares. Price ÷ EPS is the P/E, profit ÷ assets is ROA and debt ÷ equity is leverage.

Question 7

Modified duration of a bond indicates:

  1. The time until the next coupon
  2. Price change for a 1% change in yield
  3. The bond's credit rating
  4. The number of coupons the bond pays in each year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Price change for a 1% change in yield

Modified duration approximates the percentage price change for a one percentage point change in yield. Longer duration means greater interest rate sensitivity.

Question 8

A company's free float refers to:

  1. Shares held by promoters only
  2. Shares bought back by the company
  3. Shares pledged with banks
  4. Shares available for public trading
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Shares available for public trading

Free float is the portion of shares held by the public and available to trade, excluding promoter and other locked-in holdings. Indices often weight by free float.
