Economic Analysis: NISM Series XV revision notes

Macroeconomic conditions set the backdrop for all sectors and companies. This chapter covers GDP, inflation, interest rates, fiscal policy, external balances, currency movements and business cycles, and how analysts translate these into sector views. Expect questions on indicator interpretation and on how policy changes transmit to markets.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. GDP and its components

GDP measures the value of final goods and services produced in a period. By expenditure, GDP = private consumption + investment + government spending + net exports. In India, private consumption is the largest component, so rural and urban demand trends matter greatly for consumer sectors.

2. Inflation measures

CPI measures retail price changes and is RBI's target indicator; WPI tracks wholesale prices. RBI's inflation target is 4% with a tolerance band of 2% to 6% (as of 2026). High inflation squeezes margins and real incomes; falling inflation usually supports lower rates and higher valuations.

3. Monetary policy

RBI's Monetary Policy Committee sets the repo rate. Rate cuts lower borrowing costs, support demand and benefit rate-sensitive sectors such as banks, autos and real estate. Rate hikes do the opposite. Liquidity tools such as CRR changes and open market operations also affect market rates.

4. Fiscal policy

Government spending, taxation and borrowing make up fiscal policy. The fiscal deficit is total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings. Higher deficits increase government borrowing, which can push up bond yields and crowd out private investment, while capital spending on infrastructure supports cement, steel and capital goods.

5. Balance of payments

The current account records trade in goods and services, remittances and income; the capital account records investment and loans. India usually runs a current account deficit, financed by capital inflows such as FDI and FPI. A widening deficit can pressure the rupee.

6. Exchange rates

A weaker rupee helps exporters such as IT services and pharmaceuticals, and hurts importers such as oil marketing companies and firms with foreign-currency debt. Analysts assess each company's net currency exposure rather than assuming uniform effects.

7. High-frequency indicators

The Purchasing Managers' Index above 50 signals expansion and below 50 contraction. The Index of Industrial Production tracks factory output. GST collections, auto sales, power demand, credit growth and core sector data help analysts spot turning points before GDP data is released.

8. Business cycle and sectors

In expansion, cyclicals such as autos, metals, capital goods and banks tend to do well. In slowdowns, defensives such as FMCG, pharma and utilities hold up better. Analysts map their view of the cycle to sector allocation, adjusting for valuations already in prices.

9. Interest rates and valuations

Lower interest rates reduce the discount rate used in valuations, increasing the present value of future earnings, especially for long-duration growth stocks. Rising rates compress valuation multiples. This is why equity markets often react sharply to monetary policy surprises.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • GDP (expenditure) = C + I + G + (X − M).
  • Fiscal deficit = total expenditure − (revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts).

Common traps in the exam

  • Treating WPI as RBI's target. RBI targets CPI inflation.
  • Reading PMI of 49 as growth. Below 50 signals contraction.
  • Assuming a weaker rupee helps every company. Importers and firms with foreign debt lose.
  • Including borrowings in receipts when computing fiscal deficit. Borrowings are excluded.

Quick recap

GDP = C + I + G + (X − M). RBI targets CPI at 4% (2–6% band). Rate cuts help rate-sensitives; hikes compress valuations. Fiscal deficit excludes borrowings. Current account deficit pressures the rupee; a weak rupee helps exporters. PMI above 50 = expansion. Cyclicals lead in expansion, defensives in slowdowns.
