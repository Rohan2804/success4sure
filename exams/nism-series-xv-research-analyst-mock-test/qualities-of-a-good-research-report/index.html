Qualities of a Good Research Report: NISM Series XV practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Qualities of a Good Research Report, from the NISM Series XV (Research Analyst) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers what makes a research report useful and compliant: clear investment thesis, accurate data, sound valuation, explicit rating and target price with time frame, key risks, disclosures of conflicts and holdings, and clear, plain language. It also covers presentation, consistency and timeliness. When revising, learn the essential sections of a report, what disclosures are mandatory, and how to distinguish fact from opinion so readers can trust and act on the research.

Qualities of a Good Research Report questions

Question 1

Which section of a research report summarises the recommendation and key reasons at the start?

  1. The appendix
  2. The disclaimer
  3. The executive summary
  4. The glossary
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The executive summary

The executive summary gives readers the rating, target price and core thesis upfront. Appendices and disclaimers provide supporting detail and legal notes.

Question 2

Why should a research report clearly separate facts from the analyst's opinions?

  1. So readers can judge the reasoning
  2. To make the report longer
  3. To avoid giving any recommendation
  4. Because opinions are banned in reports
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. So readers can judge the reasoning

Distinguishing verified facts from estimates and opinions lets readers evaluate the strength of the argument and the assumptions involved.

Question 3

A report gives a 'buy' rating but no target price, time frame or valuation basis. Its main weakness is:

  1. It has too many disclosures
  2. The call cannot be evaluated
  3. It is too short to read
  4. It mentions risks
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The call cannot be evaluated

Without a target, horizon and valuation method, readers cannot judge or later verify the recommendation. Good reports state all three.

Question 4

Which is the best practice when presenting key risks to a recommendation?

  1. Mentioning that markets can go up or down
  2. Leaving out risks entirely to keep the overall tone of the report positive
  3. Mentioning risks only for sell ratings
  4. Listing specific risks that could invalidate the thesis
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Listing specific risks that could invalidate the thesis

Useful risk sections name specific factors, such as raw material prices or regulatory changes, that could undermine the call, for all ratings.

Question 5

A good research report should use data that is:

  1. Copied without checking
  2. Accurate, sourced and up to date
  3. Older than three years only
  4. From unnamed social media posts
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Accurate, sourced and up to date

Reliable reports use verified, current data and cite sources so readers can check them. Unverified or stale data undermines credibility.

Question 6

When an analyst changes a rating, the new report should:

  1. Hide the previous rating
  2. Repeat the old thesis unchanged
  3. Avoid giving any reasons
  4. Explain what changed and why
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Explain what changed and why

Transparent reports explain changes in assumptions, events or valuation behind a revised rating, helping readers follow the analyst's thinking.

Question 7

Plain language in a research report helps because:

  1. Readers can understand and act on it
  2. Jargon is banned by SEBI
  3. It shortens the analyst's working hours
  4. It removes the need for valuation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Readers can understand and act on it

Clear writing makes the thesis accessible to a wider audience. Technical terms may be used but should be explained where needed.

Question 8

Which disclosure helps readers judge an analyst's objectivity?

  1. The analyst's home address
  2. The analyst's school and college examination marks
  3. Any financial interest in the subject company
  4. The analyst's favourite sport
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Any financial interest in the subject company

Disclosing holdings, compensation links and business relationships with the subject company reveals potential conflicts that may bias the research.
