Fundamentals of Risk and Return: NISM Series XV practice questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the measurement of risk and return: holding period and annualised returns, expected return using probabilities, variance and standard deviation, covariance, correlation, beta, systematic and unsystematic risk, diversification, CAPM and risk-adjusted performance measures. When revising, practise expected return and beta calculations, understand why only systematic risk is priced in CAPM, and know how correlation drives the benefits of diversification.

Fundamentals of Risk and Return questions

Question 1

A stock may return 20%, 10% or −5% with probabilities of 0.3, 0.5 and 0.2. Its expected return is:

  1. 8.3%
  2. 12.5%
  3. 25%
  4. 10%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 10%

Expected return = 0.3 × 20 + 0.5 × 10 + 0.2 × (−5) = 6 + 5 − 1 = 10%. 8.3% is the simple average, which ignores probabilities.

Question 2

A stock's covariance with the market is 0.024 and the market's variance is 0.020. Its beta is:

  1. 0.83
  2. 0.048
  3. 1.2
  4. 0.44
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 1.2

Beta = covariance ÷ market variance = 0.024 ÷ 0.020 = 1.2. Inverting the ratio gives 0.83.

Question 3

A portfolio holds 50% each in assets expected to return 14% and 8%. Its expected return is:

  1. 22%
  2. 11%
  3. 6%
  4. 10%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 11%

Portfolio expected return is the weighted average: 0.5 × 14 + 0.5 × 8 = 11%. Unlike risk, expected return does not depend on correlation.

Question 4

An asset has an expected return of 12% and a standard deviation of 18%. Its coefficient of variation is:

  1. 1.5
  2. 0.67
  3. 6
  4. 30
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 1.5

Coefficient of variation = standard deviation ÷ mean = 18 ÷ 12 = 1.5, showing risk per unit of return. A lower value is better.

Question 5

Two stocks have a correlation of −1. Combining them in the right proportions can:

  1. Eliminate portfolio risk
  2. Double portfolio risk
  3. Leave risk unchanged
  4. Guarantee a 100% return
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Eliminate portfolio risk

With perfect negative correlation, gains on one exactly offset losses on the other, so a suitable mix can reduce portfolio standard deviation to zero.

Question 6

Under CAPM, investors are compensated only for:

  1. Total risk measured by standard deviation
  2. Systematic risk measured by beta
  3. Company-specific risk
  4. Liquidity of the stock
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Systematic risk measured by beta

CAPM assumes unsystematic risk can be diversified away, so only market-related risk, measured by beta, earns a risk premium.

Question 7

Which event represents systematic risk?

  1. A strike at one company's plant
  2. A product recall by one firm
  3. A CEO's sudden resignation
  4. An unexpected RBI rate hike
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. An unexpected RBI rate hike

Policy rate changes affect almost all companies and cannot be diversified away. Plant strikes, recalls and CEO exits are company-specific.

Question 8

A stock earned 12% when CAPM predicted 10%. Its Jensen's alpha is:

  1. −2%
  2. 22%
  3. +2%
  4. 1.2%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. +2%

Alpha = actual return − CAPM required return = 12% − 10% = +2%, indicating outperformance after adjusting for systematic risk.
