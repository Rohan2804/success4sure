Accounting and Taxation: NISM Series XIII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Accounting and Taxation, from the NISM Series XIII (Common Derivatives) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the accounting and tax treatment of derivatives across asset classes. It includes accounting for margins, mark-to-market, premiums and open positions under accounting standards, the treatment of hedge accounting, the classification of derivatives income for tax, Securities Transaction Tax on equity derivatives and Commodity Transaction Tax on commodity derivatives, and stamp duty. Rates change through Finance Acts, so focus on what each tax applies to, who pays it and how income is classified.

Accounting and Taxation questions

Question 1

Commodity Transaction Tax (CTT) is levied on:

  1. Purchase of physical agricultural goods
  2. Currency futures trades
  3. Sale of non-agricultural commodity derivatives
  4. Equity cash market trades
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Sale of non-agricultural commodity derivatives

CTT applies to taxable commodity derivatives, mainly non-agricultural contracts, on the seller. Currency derivatives do not attract STT or CTT.

Question 2

Under Ind AS, hedge accounting allows a company to:

  1. Match hedge and hedged-item results
  2. Avoid recording derivatives in books
  3. Show derivatives only at cost
  4. Postpone all losses indefinitely
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Match hedge and hedged-item results

Hedge accounting aligns recognition of gains and losses on the hedging instrument with those on the hedged item, reducing artificial volatility in profit.

Question 3

For an individual, income from trading currency derivatives on a recognised exchange is generally treated as:

  1. Speculative income
  2. Exempt income
  3. Long-term capital gain
  4. Non-speculative business income
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Non-speculative business income

Eligible derivatives traded on recognised exchanges, including currency derivatives, are excluded from speculative transactions and treated as non-speculative business income.

Question 4

Do exchange-traded currency futures attract Securities Transaction Tax?

  1. Yes, on the buyer
  2. No
  3. Yes, on both parties
  4. Only on expiry day
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. No

STT applies to securities transactions such as equity and equity derivatives. Currency derivatives are not covered by STT.

Question 5

At year-end, an open futures position with an unrealised loss is accounted for by:

  1. Ignoring it until the position is closed
  2. Adding it to share capital
  3. Recognising the loss in the accounts
  4. Treating it as a gain
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Recognising the loss in the accounts

Accounting standards require derivatives to be measured at fair value, so MTM losses on open positions are recognised. Prudence does not allow ignoring them.

Question 6

Premium received by an option writer on an open position is initially shown as:

  1. Income immediately
  2. A liability until expiry or exercise
  3. Share capital
  4. A fixed asset
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A liability until expiry or exercise

The premium received is held as a liability because the writer still has an obligation. It is recognised as income when the option expires or is closed.

Question 7

Losses from F&O trading treated as non-speculative business can be carried forward for up to:

  1. Two years
  2. Four years
  3. Indefinitely
  4. Eight years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Eight years

Non-speculative business losses can be carried forward for eight assessment years to be set off against business income, provided the return is filed on time.

Question 8

Stamp duty on derivatives trades in India is collected:

  1. Through exchanges, from the buyer
  2. By SEBI from the seller
  3. By the RBI from banks
  4. By the depository from issuers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Through exchanges, from the buyer

Stamp duty on securities transactions, including derivatives, is collected centrally by exchanges or clearing corporations from the buyer and passed to states.
