Accounting and Taxation: NISM Series XIII revision notes

This short chapter covers how derivative transactions are recorded in books and taxed in India. Questions test where margin and MTM entries go, how option premium is treated, why F&O income is non-speculative business income, and how turnover is computed for tax audit. Tax rates change, so remember the principle and check the current rate from the income tax department.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Accounting for futures margin

Initial margin paid to the broker or clearing member is recorded as a current asset (deposit), not an expense. When margin is in the form of bank guarantee or securities, it is disclosed rather than booked as cash. The margin returns when positions are closed.

2. Accounting for daily MTM

MTM received or paid is credited or debited to a separate MTM account, a current asset or liability. On final settlement or squaring off, the net balance is transferred to profit and loss. Unrealised losses on open positions at the balance sheet date are provided for under prudent accounting.

3. Accounting for options

Premium paid by the buyer is shown as a current asset until expiry or exercise; premium received by the seller is a current liability. On expiry or exercise, the premium is transferred to profit and loss. Companies following Ind AS treat derivatives as financial instruments measured at fair value.

4. Tax nature of F&O income

Under Section 43(5) of the Income-tax Act, derivative transactions on a recognised exchange are excluded from 'speculative transactions'. So income or loss from F&O trading is taxed as non-speculative business income. Losses can be set off against other business income and carried forward for eight years.

5. Intraday equity is different

Intraday equity cash trades (no delivery) are speculative business, while F&O is non-speculative. Confusing the two is a common error. Investors using F&O for hedging also report results as business income.

6. Securities transaction tax

STT is charged on the sale of futures (on traded value) and on the sale of options (on premium) as well as on exercised options (on settlement value). Rates were increased in 2024; as of 2026 check the current rates on the income tax department website. STT paid on business transactions is allowed as a deduction from business income.

7. Turnover for tax audit

For F&O, turnover is the aggregate of absolute profits and losses on each trade (positive and negative differences), plus premium received on sale of options where included in profit computation, as per ICAI guidance. Tax audit applicability depends on this turnover and on profit levels under Section 44AB.

8. Currency and interest rate derivatives

Exchange-traded currency and interest rate derivatives are also excluded from speculative transactions and taxed as business income. STT does not apply to them, but stamp duty and exchange charges do.

Common traps in the exam

  • F&O income is non-speculative business income, not capital gains and not speculative income.
  • Initial margin is a deposit (asset), not an expense.
  • STT on options is charged on premium for sale, but on settlement value when exercised.
  • F&O turnover for tax audit is the sum of absolute profits and losses, not total contract value traded.

Quick recap

Margins are assets; MTM goes to a separate account and to P&L on settlement; option premium is a current asset for buyers and a liability for sellers until expiry. F&O on recognised exchanges is non-speculative business income under Section 43(5); losses carry forward eight years. STT applies to equity derivatives, not currency or IRF. Turnover equals absolute profits plus losses.
