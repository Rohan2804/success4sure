Free NISM Series XIII practice questions

Question 1 · Basics of Derivatives

A key risk that both parties to an OTC forward contract face is:

  1. Default by the other party before settlement
  2. Daily margin calls from a clearing corporation
  3. Securities Transaction Tax
  4. Delisting of the contract by the exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Default by the other party before settlement

There is no clearing corporation guarantee in a forward, so each side bears counterparty risk.

Question 2 · Introduction to the Underlying Markets

In the currency pair GBP/INR, the quote (price) currency is:

  1. GBP
  2. USD
  3. EUR
  4. INR
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. INR

The second currency in a pair is the quote currency, showing how many rupees one pound costs.

Question 3 · Introduction to Forwards and Futures

A stock trades at ₹800 and the interest rate is 9% p.a. (simple), with no dividends. The fair value of a 4-month future is:

  1. ₹872
  2. ₹806
  3. ₹800
  4. ₹824
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹824

Fair value = 800 × (1 + 0.09 × 4/12) = 800 × 1.03 = ₹824.

Question 4 · Strategies Using Futures

To reduce a portfolio's beta from 1.2 to zero using index futures, the investor should:

  1. Buy index futures equal to the portfolio value
  2. Sell stock futures of unrelated companies
  3. Sell index futures equal to the beta-adjusted portfolio value
  4. Buy call options on the index
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Sell index futures equal to the beta-adjusted portfolio value

Selling futures worth beta × portfolio value offsets the portfolio's market exposure.

Question 5 · Introduction to Options

A trader buys a call (lot 250 shares) with strike ₹1,000 at a premium of ₹40. At expiry the stock settles at ₹1,070. His net profit is:

  1. ₹7,500
  2. ₹17,500
  3. ₹10,000
  4. ₹27,500
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹7,500

Payoff = (1,070 − 1,000) − 40 = ₹30 per share; × 250 = ₹7,500.

Question 6 · Option Trading Strategies

Selling a higher-strike put and buying a lower-strike put on the same underlying and expiry creates a:

  1. Bear put spread
  2. Bull put spread
  3. Long straddle
  4. Protective put
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Bull put spread

This credit spread profits if the underlying stays above the higher strike, with limited risk.

Question 7 · Trading, Clearing, Settlement & Risk Management

The process by which the clearing corporation interposes itself between buyer and seller of every trade is called:

  1. Netting
  2. Novation
  3. Assignment
  4. Rollover
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Novation

Novation replaces the original contract with two contracts, each against the clearing corporation.

Question 8 · Legal and Regulatory Environment

The statutory definition of 'derivative' under which exchange-traded equity derivatives are regulated in India is contained in the:

  1. Indian Contract Act, 1872
  2. Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881
  3. Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956
  4. Sale of Goods Act, 1930
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956

The SCRA defines derivatives as securities, bringing them under exchange regulation.

Question 9 · Accounting and Taxation

For an individual, gains from trading equity F&O on a recognised exchange are generally treated as:

  1. Speculative business income
  2. Long-term capital gains
  3. Non-speculative business income
  4. Exempt agricultural income
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Non-speculative business income

Eligible derivative transactions on recognised exchanges are excluded from the definition of speculative transactions.

Question 10 · Sales Practices, Code of Conduct & Investor Protection

Recommending a complex, leveraged options strategy to a retiree whose objective is capital safety is an example of:

  1. Good risk management
  2. Portfolio diversification
  3. Unsuitable advice (mis-selling)
  4. Mandatory regulatory disclosure
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Unsuitable advice (mis-selling)

Products must match the client's objectives and risk tolerance; leveraged strategies clash with capital safety.

Question 11 · Basics of Derivatives

Exchange-traded equity derivatives in India began with the launch of index futures in:

  1. 1995
  2. 2000
  3. 2005
  4. 2008
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 2000

Index futures were introduced in June 2000, followed by index options, stock options and stock futures.

Question 12 · Introduction to the Underlying Markets

USD/INR spot is 84.00 and the 3-month forward premium is 45 paise. The 3-month outright forward rate is:

  1. 83.55
  2. 84.045
  3. 84.45
  4. 88.50
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 84.45

Outright forward = spot + premium = 84.00 + 0.45 = 84.45.

Question 13 · Introduction to Forwards and Futures

Backwardation refers to a situation where:

  1. The futures price is above the spot price
  2. Futures and spot prices are always equal
  3. Open interest is zero
  4. The futures price is below the spot price
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The futures price is below the spot price

Backwardation is the reverse of contango and can occur with high dividends or convenience yield.

Question 14 · Strategies Using Futures

If stock futures trade well above their cost-of-carry fair value, an arbitrageur would:

  1. Buy the stock in the cash market and sell the futures
  2. Sell the stock and buy the futures
  3. Buy both stock and futures
  4. Sell both stock and futures
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Buy the stock in the cash market and sell the futures

This cash-and-carry trade locks in the excess of the futures price over fair value.

Question 15 · Introduction to Options

The holder of a put option has the:

  1. Obligation to buy the underlying at the strike price
  2. Right to buy the underlying at the strike price
  3. Obligation to sell the underlying at the market price
  4. Right, but not the obligation, to sell the underlying at the strike price
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Right, but not the obligation, to sell the underlying at the strike price

A put conveys the right to sell; only the writer carries an obligation.
