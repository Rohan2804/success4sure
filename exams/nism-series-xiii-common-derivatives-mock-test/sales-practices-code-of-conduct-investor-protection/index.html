Sales Practices, Code of Conduct & Investor Protection: NISM Series XIII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Sales Practices, Code of Conduct & Investor Protection, from the NISM Series XIII (Common Derivatives) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers ethical selling and investor protection for derivatives across asset classes: suitability and risk disclosure, KYC, code of conduct for brokers and their representatives, handling of client funds and securities, avoiding mis-selling and unauthorised trades, and grievance redressal through exchanges, SCORES and online dispute resolution. When revising, focus on practical scenarios: what a broker should say or do, what is prohibited, and how a client can seek redress.

Sales Practices, Code of Conduct & Investor Protection questions

Question 1

A broker's employee advises a first-time client to put all his savings into commodity futures because 'prices only go up'. This is:

  1. Mis-selling and a code breach
  2. Good advice for a new investor
  3. Permitted if the client agrees
  4. Required to increase market liquidity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Mis-selling and a code breach

Recommending highly leveraged products without suitability assessment and with false claims is mis-selling and breaches the code of conduct.

Question 2

A client notices trades in his account that he did not authorise. His first step should be to:

  1. Ignore it if the trades made profit
  2. Wait for the year-end statement
  3. Complain to the broker at once
  4. Close his bank account
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Complain to the broker at once

Clients should raise unauthorised trades immediately with the broker and escalate to the exchange or SCORES if unresolved. Delay can weaken the claim.

Question 3

Brokers must give clients a risk disclosure document mainly to:

  1. Advertise new products
  2. Collect personal data for marketing
  3. Guarantee returns
  4. Explain the risks of trading
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Explain the risks of trading

The risk disclosure document informs clients of leverage, potential losses and other risks before trading, enabling informed decisions.

Question 4

Why should a broker not accept cash from clients for margins?

  1. Cash is never accepted anywhere in India
  2. Rules require traceable banking channels
  3. Cash payments lower the margin needed
  4. Cash is safer than bank transfers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Rules require traceable banking channels

SEBI norms require client funds to be received through banking channels from the client's own account for traceability and to prevent misuse.

Question 5

A broker shares a client's trading details with another client without consent. This breaches:

  1. Confidentiality obligations
  2. Position limits
  3. Price band rules
  4. Margin rules
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Confidentiality obligations

Brokers must keep client information confidential except where law or consent permits disclosure. Sharing with other clients is a breach.

Question 6

Which platform offers online conciliation and arbitration for disputes between investors and market intermediaries?

  1. The Insurance Ombudsman
  2. The Consumer Affairs helpline
  3. SEBI's ODR portal
  4. The Income Tax portal
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. SEBI's ODR portal

SEBI's Online Dispute Resolution platform handles conciliation and arbitration of securities market disputes, including derivatives, after initial complaint routes.

Question 7

Before trading derivatives, a client must be assessed for:

  1. His preferred news channel
  2. Suitability and financial capacity
  3. His favourite sports team
  4. His social media followers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Suitability and financial capacity

Brokers must assess whether the client has adequate financial capacity and understanding, often through income proof, before enabling derivatives.

Question 8

A broker uses one client's credit balance to meet another client's margin shortfall. This is:

  1. Allowed if both are related
  2. A standard netting practice
  3. Required by exchange rules
  4. Prohibited misuse of client funds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Prohibited misuse of client funds

Client funds must be used only for that client's obligations. Using them for another client is misuse and attracts strict action.
