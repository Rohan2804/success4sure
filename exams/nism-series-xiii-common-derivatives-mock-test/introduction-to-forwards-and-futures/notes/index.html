Introduction to Forwards and Futures: NISM Series XIII revision notes

This chapter explains how forwards and futures work and how they are priced across equity, currency and interest rate markets. It is one of the heaviest chapters in NISM XIII, with calculation questions on fair value, basis, mark-to-market and currency forward premium. Practise the cost of carry and interest rate parity formulas until they are automatic.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Futures versus forwards

Futures are standardised, exchange-traded and centrally cleared; forwards are bilateral and customised. Futures settle gains and losses daily through mark to market, while forwards settle once at maturity. Futures can be squared off before expiry; forwards normally need the counterparty's agreement to cancel.

2. Cost of carry model

The fair futures price equals spot plus the cost of carrying the asset till expiry, minus income received. For Nifty at 24,000, interest of 7% a year and 30 days to expiry, fair value is about 24,000 x (1 + 0.07 x 30/365) = 24,138, less the value of dividends expected in the period.

3. Basis and convergence

Basis is spot minus futures (some texts use futures minus spot; read each question carefully). As expiry approaches, the futures price converges to the spot price and basis narrows to zero. On the expiry day, index futures settle at the closing index value.

4. Contango and backwardation

When futures trade above spot, the market is in contango, the usual state when carry costs exceed income. When futures trade below spot, it is backwardation, which can happen with large expected dividends, high demand for the underlying, or strong bearish sentiment.

5. Currency futures pricing

Interest rate parity links forward and spot rates: the currency with the higher interest rate trades at a forward discount. With USD/INR spot at 84.00, Indian rate 7% and US rate 5%, the one-year forward is about 84 x 1.07/1.05 = 85.60. So USD trades at a forward premium against INR.

6. Interest rate futures

Indian IRFs are mainly on 10-year and other G-secs (cash settled based on a weighted average price) and on T-bills. If interest rates rise, bond prices fall, so a long IRF position loses. An investor expecting rates to rise would sell IRF to hedge a bond portfolio.

7. Mark to market

Every day, open positions are revalued at the daily settlement price and the difference is paid or received. If you bought one Nifty futures lot of 75 units at 24,000 and it settles at 23,900, you pay 100 x 75 = ₹7,500 that day. Lot sizes are revised by exchanges, so always use the lot given in the question.

8. Contract specifications

Every futures contract specifies underlying, lot size, tick size, expiry day, settlement method and price bands. Equity index futures are cash settled; stock futures are physically settled at expiry. USD/INR futures have a lot size of USD 1,000, are quoted in rupees per dollar to four decimals and settle in cash.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Fair futures price F = S x (1 + r x t) - dividends (or S x e^(rt) with continuous compounding).
  • Currency forward F = S x (1 + r_domestic x t) / (1 + r_foreign x t).
  • Annualised forward premium % = (F - S)/S x 12/months x 100.
  • Daily MTM = (today's settlement price - previous settlement or trade price) x lot size x number of lots.

Common traps in the exam

  • Contango means futures above spot; backwardation means futures below spot. Students often mix the terms.
  • In currency futures, the higher-interest currency trades at a forward discount, not a premium.
  • Stock futures in India are physically settled at expiry; index futures are cash settled.
  • Basis can be defined either way; check whether the question uses spot minus futures or futures minus spot.

Quick recap

Futures are standardised, cleared, marked to market forwards. Fair value = spot plus carry minus income. Futures converge to spot at expiry. Contango: futures above spot; backwardation: below. Interest rate parity prices currency forwards; higher-rate currency is at a forward discount. Long IRF loses when rates rise. Index futures cash settle; stock futures settle physically; USD/INR lot is USD 1,000.
