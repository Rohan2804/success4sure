What this chapter covers

This chapter covers options on equity, currency, commodity and interest rate underlyings: calls and puts, buyers and sellers, strike, premium, expiry, moneyness, intrinsic and time value, exercise styles and settlement. It introduces option pricing factors and the Greeks: delta, gamma, theta, vega and rho. When revising, practise payoff calculations for each position, understand how volatility and time affect premiums, and know how options on different underlyings are settled in India.

Introduction to Options questions

Question 1

A USD/INR call option has a strike of 84.00 and the spot is 84.75. Its intrinsic value is:

  1. ₹0.75
  2. Zero
  3. ₹84.75
  4. ₹1.50
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹0.75

Call intrinsic value = spot − strike = 84.75 − 84.00 = ₹0.75 per dollar. It is positive because the call is in the money.

Question 2

Theta of a long option position measures:

  1. Change in value as volatility rises
  2. Change in value as interest rates rise
  3. Loss in value as time passes
  4. Change in delta as price moves
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Loss in value as time passes

Theta is time decay, the fall in an option's value as expiry approaches, other factors constant. Vega relates to volatility and rho to interest rates.

Question 3

A put option with strike ₹1,000 and premium ₹30 is bought. At expiry the stock is at ₹1,050. The buyer's net result per share is:

  1. Gain of ₹50
  2. Loss of ₹30
  3. Loss of ₹80
  4. Gain of ₹20
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Loss of ₹30

The put expires worthless because the stock is above the strike, so the buyer loses only the premium of ₹30.

Question 4

A call option's delta of 0.5 means that for a ₹1 rise in the underlying, the option price rises by about:

  1. ₹1.00
  2. ₹5.00
  3. ₹0.05
  4. ₹0.50
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹0.50

Delta measures the change in option price for a one-unit change in the underlying. A delta of 0.5 implies about ₹0.50 change, typical for at-the-money calls.

Question 5

Which option is out of the money?

  1. A call with strike above spot
  2. A call with strike below spot
  3. A put with strike above spot
  4. A put with strike equal to spot
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A call with strike above spot

A call is OTM when strike exceeds spot, as exercise would mean buying above market. A put with strike above spot is in the money.

Question 6

An option that can be exercised only on the expiry date is called:

  1. American style
  2. Bermudan style
  3. Asian style
  4. European style
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. European style

European options can be exercised only at expiry, which is the style used for exchange-traded options in India. American options can be exercised any time before expiry.

Question 7

Which factor increases the premium of both calls and puts?

  1. Lower volatility of the underlying
  2. Higher volatility of the underlying
  3. Shorter time to expiry
  4. A drop in market liquidity only
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Higher volatility of the underlying

Higher volatility raises the chance of large moves, benefiting option holders, so both call and put premiums rise. Shorter time to expiry reduces premiums.

Question 8

The seller of a put option receives the premium and is obliged to:

  1. Sell the underlying at the strike if exercised
  2. Pay the premium back at expiry
  3. Buy the underlying at the strike if exercised
  4. Deliver a call option to the buyer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Buy the underlying at the strike if exercised

A put gives its buyer the right to sell; the put writer must buy at the strike if exercised. Call writers have the obligation to sell.
