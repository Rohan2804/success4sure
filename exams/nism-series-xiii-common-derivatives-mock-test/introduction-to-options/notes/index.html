Introduction to Options: NISM Series XIII revision notes

Options make up a large share of NISM XIII questions. This chapter covers terminology, payoffs, moneyness, factors that affect premium, option Greeks, put-call parity and contract specifications in equity and currency options. Calculation questions on intrinsic value, breakeven and payoff appear regularly, as do conceptual questions on which way premium moves when a factor changes.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Calls and puts

A call gives the buyer the right to buy at the strike; a put gives the right to sell. The buyer pays a premium upfront; the seller receives it and must perform if exercised. A long call profits when prices rise above strike plus premium; a long put profits when prices fall below strike minus premium.

2. Moneyness

A call is in the money when spot is above strike, at the money when equal, and out of the money when below. For puts it is the reverse. With Nifty at 24,000, a 23,800 call is ITM by 200 points, while a 23,800 put is OTM.

3. Intrinsic and time value

Premium = intrinsic value + time value. If a 23,800 call trades at 350 with Nifty at 24,000, intrinsic value is 200 and time value 150. OTM options have only time value. Time value decays to zero at expiry, and the decay speeds up in the final weeks.

4. Premium drivers

Call premium rises with higher spot, lower strike, longer time, higher volatility and higher interest rates; it falls with expected dividends. Put premium rises with lower spot, higher strike, longer time and higher volatility; it falls with higher interest rates and rises with dividends. Volatility increases both calls and puts.

5. Option pricing models

The Black-Scholes model prices European options using spot, strike, time, volatility and the risk-free rate. The binomial model breaks time into steps and also handles American options. Implied volatility is the volatility that makes the model price equal the market premium; India VIX is built from Nifty option implied volatility.

6. Greeks

Delta measures change in premium for a one-unit change in the underlying (call 0 to 1, put -1 to 0; ATM about 0.5). Gamma measures change in delta. Theta is time decay, usually negative for buyers. Vega measures sensitivity to volatility. Rho measures sensitivity to interest rates. Option buyers are long gamma and vega, short theta.

7. Put-call parity

For European options on the same underlying, strike and expiry: call premium + present value of strike = put premium + spot. If it does not hold, arbitrage is possible. Example: with S = K = 100 and PV(K) = 98, C minus P should be 2; a call at 6 and put at 4 satisfies parity, while a call at 8 would not.

8. Indian contract rules

Index and stock options on NSE and BSE are European style, exercisable only at expiry. Stock options in-the-money at expiry are physically settled; index options are cash settled. Currency options on USD/INR are European and cash settled in rupees. Exchanges set strikes, lot sizes and expiry days, which SEBI has revised in recent years.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Call intrinsic value = max(0, S - K); put intrinsic value = max(0, K - S).
  • Breakeven: long call = K + premium; long put = K - premium.
  • Put-call parity: C + K/(1+r)^t = P + S.

Common traps in the exam

  • Higher interest rates raise call premiums and lower put premiums.
  • Higher volatility increases both call and put premiums.
  • Indian stock options are European, not American, since 2011.
  • Time value is highest for at-the-money options, not deep ITM ones.
  • Delta of a put is negative; a delta of 0.4 for a put is impossible.

Quick recap

Calls = right to buy, puts = right to sell; buyers pay premium, sellers take obligation. Premium = intrinsic + time value; ATM has most time value. Volatility raises all premiums; rates raise calls and lower puts. Greeks: delta, gamma, theta, vega, rho. Put-call parity: C + PV(K) = P + S. Indian equity and currency options are European; stock options settle physically.
