Legal and Regulatory Environment: NISM Series XIII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Legal and Regulatory Environment, from the NISM Series XIII (Common Derivatives) syllabus.

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the legal and regulatory framework for equity, currency, interest rate and commodity derivatives in India: the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, the SEBI Act, FEMA and RBI directions for currency and interest rate derivatives, and the integration of commodity market regulation into SEBI. It also covers rules on participants, position limits and market abuse. When revising, know which regulator governs which aspect, why currency derivatives involve both SEBI and RBI, and how regulators curb manipulation.

Legal and Regulatory Environment questions

Question 1

Exchange-traded currency derivatives in India are jointly governed by:

  1. SEBI and the RBI
  2. IRDAI and PFRDA
  3. Only the Ministry of Commerce
  4. Only the stock exchanges
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. SEBI and the RBI

Currency derivatives fall under FEMA, administered by the RBI, while exchanges and brokers are regulated by SEBI. Hence both regulators issue directions.

Question 2

Which law governs foreign exchange transactions, including the use of currency derivatives by residents?

  1. Companies Act, 2013
  2. Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999
  3. Insurance Laws (Amendment) Act, 2015 and the Insurance Act
  4. Indian Partnership Act, 1932
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999

FEMA regulates foreign exchange dealings; RBI frames rules under it for currency derivatives. The other laws concern companies, insurance and partnerships.

Question 3

A commodity broker must register with:

  1. FMC
  2. RBI
  3. SEBI
  4. NABARD
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. SEBI

After the FMC merged with SEBI in 2015, commodity derivatives brokers are registered and regulated by SEBI like other stock brokers.

Question 4

Spreading false rumours to move the price of a futures contract for personal gain is:

  1. A permitted trading strategy
  2. Allowed if done on social media
  3. Only an ethical concern with no penalty
  4. A fraudulent practice punishable by SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A fraudulent practice punishable by SEBI

Spreading false information to influence prices is prohibited under SEBI's rules against fraudulent and unfair trade practices and can attract penalties.

Question 5

SEBI imposes position limits on derivatives mainly to:

  1. Prevent concentration and manipulation
  2. Guarantee profits to small traders
  3. Increase brokerage income
  4. Fix the price of derivatives
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Prevent concentration and manipulation

Position limits stop any single participant from dominating a contract, reducing manipulation and default risk.

Question 6

Who frames the regulations on margins, position limits and contract design for exchange-traded derivatives?

  1. Each client individually
  2. The Ministry of Corporate Affairs
  3. Credit rating agencies
  4. SEBI, with exchanges implementing them
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. SEBI, with exchanges implementing them

SEBI sets the framework, and exchanges and clearing corporations implement contract specifications, margins and limits within it.

Question 7

An entity wants to act as a clearing member in the derivatives segment. It must:

  1. Only open a bank account
  2. Get approval from the RBI alone
  3. Meet net worth norms and be registered
  4. Only be a listed company
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Meet net worth norms and be registered

Clearing members must meet SEBI and clearing corporation criteria, including net worth and deposits, and be registered. Listing or a bank account alone is insufficient.

Question 8

Insider trading rules apply to derivatives because:

  1. Derivatives are not regulated by SEBI
  2. Their prices depend on the underlying
  3. Only cash market trades can be manipulated
  4. Insiders are barred only from mutual funds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Their prices depend on the underlying

Trading derivatives on unpublished price-sensitive information gives the same unfair advantage as trading shares, so insider trading regulations cover them.
