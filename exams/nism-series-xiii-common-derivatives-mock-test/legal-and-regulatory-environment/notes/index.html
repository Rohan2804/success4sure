Legal and Regulatory Environment: NISM Series XIII revision notes

Exchange-traded derivatives in India sit under several laws and regulators. Equity derivatives are governed by SEBI under SCRA and the SEBI Act, while currency and interest rate derivatives involve RBI because they touch FEMA and monetary policy. Questions test which regulator decides what, the committees that shaped the market and the eligibility criteria for derivatives on stocks.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Legal basis

SCRA's definition of securities was amended in 1999 to include derivatives, allowing exchange trading. Section 18A says derivative contracts are legal and valid if traded on a recognised stock exchange and settled through its clearing house. The SEBI Act gives SEBI power to regulate these exchanges and intermediaries.

2. Committees

The L.C. Gupta Committee (1998) recommended the regulatory framework for equity derivatives and a phased introduction starting with index futures. The J.R. Varma Committee recommended risk containment measures, including margining based on value at risk. Later committees on currency and interest rate futures were set up jointly by RBI and SEBI.

3. Joint regulation of currency and IRF

Currency and interest rate derivatives on exchanges are permitted by RBI under FEMA and RBI Act provisions, while SEBI regulates exchange operations, membership and surveillance. Changes such as permitted currency pairs, participant eligibility and exposure requirements are announced through joint or coordinated circulars.

4. Exchange requirements

A derivatives exchange or segment needs separate approval, a separate clearing corporation or clearing segment, adequate net worth for members, online surveillance and risk management systems, and investor protection measures. Members of the derivatives segment need specific registration.

5. Eligibility of stocks for F&O

SEBI sets criteria for including stocks in the derivatives segment based on market capitalisation, average daily traded value, and median quarter-sigma order size and market-wide position limits. Stocks that no longer meet criteria are phased out. These criteria are revised periodically, so learn the factors rather than exact numbers.

6. Position limits for participants

Position limits are prescribed for clients, trading members, FPIs and mutual funds at index and stock level. For currency derivatives, participants who take positions must have an underlying exposure as per RBI's rules. Hedging positions by institutions may receive higher limits.

7. Recent investor protection measures

SEBI introduced measures in 2024 to curb speculative excess in index derivatives: higher minimum contract size, upfront collection of option premium, intraday monitoring of position limits, removal of calendar spread benefit on expiry day and fewer weekly expiries. Check the latest circulars for exact values.

8. Surveillance and enforcement

Exchanges run real-time surveillance for manipulation, spoofing, synchronised trades and position limit breaches. Violations attract penalties under exchange bye-laws and action by SEBI under the SEBI Act and PFUTP Regulations.

Common traps in the exam

  • Section 18A of SCRA validates exchange-traded derivatives; OTC equity derivatives are not covered by it.
  • L.C. Gupta was about the framework; J.R. Varma was about risk containment and margins.
  • RBI and SEBI jointly govern exchange-traded currency and interest rate derivatives; SEBI alone does not decide currency pairs.
  • Stock eligibility for F&O is reviewed periodically; a stock can be removed from the segment.

Quick recap

Derivatives became securities under SCRA in 1999; Section 18A validates exchange-traded contracts. L.C. Gupta Committee designed the framework, J.R. Varma Committee the risk management. SEBI regulates equity derivatives; RBI and SEBI jointly regulate currency and interest rate derivatives. Stocks must meet SEBI eligibility criteria. Position limits apply to clients, members, FPIs and MFs.
