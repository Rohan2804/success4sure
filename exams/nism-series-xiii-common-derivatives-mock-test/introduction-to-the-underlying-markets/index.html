Introduction to the Underlying Markets: NISM Series XIII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Introduction to the Underlying Markets, from the NISM Series XIII (Common Derivatives) syllabus.

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the cash markets that underlie the derivatives in this exam: equity markets and indices, the foreign exchange market and currency quotes, the government securities and money markets, and physical commodity markets. Learn how prices are quoted in each, what drives them, how indices are built, how forex quotes and cross rates work, and how bond prices relate to yields. When revising, practise cross-rate and yield calculations and understand the main participants and regulators of each market.

Introduction to the Underlying Markets questions

Question 1

EUR/USD is 1.08 and USD/INR is 84.00. What is the implied EUR/INR rate?

  1. 90.72
  2. 77.78
  3. 85.08
  4. 83.00
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 90.72

EUR/INR = EUR/USD × USD/INR = 1.08 × 84.00 = 90.72. Dividing instead gives 77.78, which is the wrong direction for this cross.

Question 2

In the quote USD/INR = 84.00, the base currency is:

  1. INR
  2. USD
  3. Both equally
  4. Neither, it is a cross rate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. USD

In a pair quoted as A/B, A is the base currency and the price shows how many units of B buy one unit of A. Here one USD costs 84 rupees.

Question 3

If the rupee depreciates against the dollar, the USD/INR rate will:

  1. Fall
  2. Remain unchanged
  3. Become negative
  4. Rise
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Rise

Depreciation means more rupees are needed to buy one dollar, so USD/INR increases, for example from 84 to 85.

Question 4

Spot gold is ₹72,000 per 10 g and the near-month futures price is ₹72,900. The basis (spot minus futures) is:

  1. +₹900
  2. ₹0
  3. −₹900
  4. −₹72,900
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. −₹900

Basis = spot − futures = 72,000 − 72,900 = −₹900. A negative basis indicates futures trading above spot, as in a contango market.

Question 5

Who conducts auctions of government dated securities and treasury bills on behalf of the Government of India?

  1. SEBI
  2. Reserve Bank of India
  3. NSE
  4. Clearing Corporation of India
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Reserve Bank of India

The RBI manages public debt and conducts primary auctions of G-secs and T-bills. CCIL clears and settles trades in these securities.

Question 6

Which factor is most likely to strengthen the rupee against the dollar?

  1. A sharp rise in India's oil import bill
  2. Large outflows by foreign portfolio investors
  3. Large foreign capital inflows into India
  4. Higher inflation in India than in the US
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Large foreign capital inflows into India

Capital inflows increase demand for rupees, supporting its value. Higher imports, outflows and higher relative inflation tend to weaken the rupee.

Question 7

When market interest rates rise, the prices of existing government bonds generally:

  1. Fall
  2. Rise
  3. Stay the same
  4. Double
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Fall

Bond prices move inversely to yields. Existing bonds with lower coupons become less attractive when new bonds offer higher rates, so their prices fall.

Question 8

A physical commodity market differs from the equity market because commodity prices are strongly affected by:

  1. Company quarterly earnings
  2. Dividend announcements
  3. Share buyback decisions
  4. Weather, harvests and global supply
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Weather, harvests and global supply

Agricultural and other commodity prices depend on supply factors like weather, crop size, inventories and global demand. Earnings and dividends drive equity prices.
