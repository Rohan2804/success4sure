Trading, Clearing, Settlement & Risk Management: NISM Series XIII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Trading, Clearing, Settlement & Risk Management, from the NISM Series XIII (Common Derivatives) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the operational framework for exchange-traded derivatives: trading systems, order types, membership categories, position limits, clearing and settlement through clearing corporations, mark-to-market and final settlement, margining and risk management including the settlement guarantee fund. It also covers settlement of currency, interest rate and commodity contracts. When revising, understand who settles with whom, how margins work, and why clearing corporations are central to managing default risk.

Trading, Clearing, Settlement & Risk Management questions

Question 1

A futures position worth ₹12 lakh requires an initial margin of 10%. How much initial margin is needed?

  1. ₹1,20,000
  2. ₹12,000
  3. ₹10,00,000
  4. ₹1,00,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹1,20,000

Initial margin = 10% × ₹12,00,000 = ₹1,20,000. ₹12,000 is 1% of the value, which misplaces the decimal.

Question 2

In the derivatives segment, a self-clearing member clears and settles:

  1. Trades of all other members
  2. Only trades in the cash segment
  3. Trades of foreign exchanges
  4. Only its own and its clients' trades
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Only its own and its clients' trades

A self-clearing member clears its own proprietary and client trades. A professional or general clearing member can clear trades of other trading members.

Question 3

Exchange-traded currency futures in India are settled:

  1. By physical delivery of US dollars
  2. In cash in Indian rupees
  3. In gold at the exchange vault
  4. Through the RBI's forex reserves
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. In cash in Indian rupees

Currency futures on Indian exchanges are cash settled in rupees; no foreign currency is delivered. Settlement is based on the FBIL reference rate at expiry.

Question 4

A client's open position exceeds the client-level position limit. What is the likely consequence?

  1. The exchange increases the limit for him
  2. His broker's licence is automatically cancelled
  3. Penalties and required reduction
  4. Nothing, limits are only advisory
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Penalties and required reduction

Exchanges levy penalties and require positions above limits to be reduced. Limits are binding and monitored to prevent concentration.

Question 5

Why do clearing corporations conduct daily mark-to-market settlement of futures?

  1. To generate extra revenue for members
  2. To set the next day's opening price
  3. To decide which stocks enter the index
  4. To stop losses piling up
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. To stop losses piling up

Settling gains and losses daily keeps exposures small and lowers default risk. MTM is not a revenue tool or a price-setting mechanism.

Question 6

If a client fails to pay a margin call, the broker may:

  1. Ask the exchange to waive the margin
  2. Close out the client's position
  3. Transfer the loss to another client
  4. Extend the deadline indefinitely
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Close out the client's position

Brokers can square off positions of clients who fail to meet margin calls, to limit losses. Using other clients' funds is prohibited.

Question 7

Novation in exchange-traded derivatives means:

  1. The exchange cancels all trades at day end
  2. Brokers trade only with their own clients
  3. The clearing corporation is counterparty to all
  4. SEBI approves each trade individually
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The clearing corporation is counterparty to all

Through novation, the clearing corporation interposes itself as buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer, guaranteeing settlement.

Question 8

Extreme loss margin is charged in addition to initial margin to cover:

  1. Losses beyond normal risk estimates
  2. Brokerage and stamp duty
  3. Losses only on expiry day
  4. Taxes payable on profits
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Losses beyond normal risk estimates

Extreme loss margin covers losses in situations beyond those captured by the initial margin model, adding a further buffer for the clearing corporation.
