Trading, Clearing, Settlement & Risk Management: NISM Series XIII revision notes

This chapter explains how derivatives trades move from order entry to final settlement and how exchanges and clearing corporations control risk. Margin types, daily settlement, final settlement and position limits are favourite topics. Expect questions on who pays which margin, how option premiums and exercise are settled, and the purpose of the core settlement guarantee fund.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Trading system

Derivatives trade on screen-based, order-driven systems matched on price-time priority. Orders can be limit, market, stop-loss or immediate-or-cancel, and can be day orders. Each trade carries the client's UCC. Price bands and dynamic circuit filters prevent erroneous orders from executing at absurd prices.

2. Clearing and novation

After a trade, the clearing corporation becomes the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer through novation. This removes counterparty risk between trading members. Clearing members are responsible to the clearing corporation for their own trades and those of the trading members they clear for.

3. Initial margin

Initial margin is collected upfront to cover the potential one-day loss on a portfolio. Indian clearing corporations historically used SPAN, which simulates price and volatility scenarios to find the worst likely loss for the whole portfolio. Hedged positions get margin benefit because losses on one leg are offset by gains on the other.

4. Exposure and other margins

Exposure margin or extreme loss margin is charged on top of initial margin to cover moves beyond the SPAN scenarios. Premium margin is collected from option buyers until the premium is paid; assignment margin on exercised positions; delivery margin for physically settled stock derivatives near expiry. Clients must pay margins upfront.

5. Daily settlement

Futures are marked to market daily at the settlement price, with profits paid and losses collected in cash the next day. Option premium is paid by buyers and received by sellers on T+1. Options are not marked to market for the buyer, but sellers' margins are revalued daily.

6. Final settlement

Index futures and options settle in cash at the final settlement price (the closing value of the index on expiry). ITM stock futures and options positions at expiry are physically settled through delivery of shares. Currency derivatives settle in cash in rupees at the reference rate.

7. Position limits

Exchanges set market-wide, trading member and client-level position limits to prevent concentration. When open interest in a stock crosses 95% of the market-wide position limit, the stock enters a ban period: no fresh positions can be taken, only reduction of existing positions, until it falls below 80%.

8. Settlement guarantee fund and defaults

Each clearing corporation keeps a core settlement guarantee fund contributed by itself, exchanges and clearing members to complete settlement if a member defaults. The default waterfall uses the defaulter's margins first, then its SGF contribution, then the clearing corporation's resources and others' contributions in a defined order.

Common traps in the exam

  • Option buyers pay premium, not SPAN margin; sellers pay margin.
  • The F&O ban period starts above 95% of market-wide position limit and ends when it falls below 80%.
  • Novation makes the clearing corporation the counterparty; exchanges do not guarantee trades directly.
  • Stock derivatives are physically settled at expiry; index derivatives are cash settled.

Quick recap

Order-driven screen trading with UCC and price bands. Clearing corporation becomes central counterparty through novation. Margins: SPAN-type initial margin, exposure or ELM, premium, assignment and delivery margins, all upfront. Futures MTM daily; option premium T+1. Index and currency derivatives cash settle; stock derivatives physically settle. Ban at 95% of MWPL, exit below 80%. Core SGF backs defaults.
