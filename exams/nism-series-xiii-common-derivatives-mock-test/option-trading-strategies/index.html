Option Trading Strategies: NISM Series XIII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Option Trading Strategies, from the NISM Series XIII (Common Derivatives) syllabus.

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers option strategies for different market views: buying and writing calls and puts, covered calls, protective puts, bull and bear spreads, straddles, strangles, butterflies, collars and ratio spreads, along with their use in currency and commodity markets. When revising, compute maximum profit, maximum loss and break-even points, match strategies to views on direction and volatility, and understand the risk of strategies with short options.

Option Trading Strategies questions

Question 1

A trader sells a ₹500 call at ₹14 and buys a ₹520 call at ₹5, same expiry. What is his maximum profit per share?

  1. ₹20
  2. ₹11
  3. ₹19
  4. ₹9
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹9

This bear call spread earns a net credit of 14 − 5 = ₹9, the maximum profit if the stock ends below ₹500. Maximum loss is 20 − 9 = ₹11.

Question 2

A trader expects a big move in a stock after results but is unsure of direction. Which strategy fits?

  1. Short straddle
  2. Covered call
  3. Long straddle
  4. Bull call spread
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Long straddle

A long straddle (buy call and put at the same strike) profits from a large move either way. A short straddle profits only if the price stays still.

Question 3

A long strangle differs from a long straddle because the strangle:

  1. Uses different out-of-the-money strikes
  2. Uses the same strike for call and put
  3. Involves selling both options
  4. Has unlimited risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Uses different out-of-the-money strikes

A long strangle buys an OTM call and OTM put, making it cheaper than a straddle but needing a bigger move to profit. Both have limited risk.

Question 4

An exporter expecting USD inflows buys a USD/INR put option. This protects him against:

  1. A rise in USD/INR
  2. A fall in USD/INR
  3. Rising interest rates
  4. Higher brokerage
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A fall in USD/INR

A put gains if USD/INR falls, offsetting lower rupee value of his dollar receipts, while letting him benefit if the rupee weakens.

Question 5

A trader buys one ₹100 call, sells two ₹110 calls and buys one ₹120 call. His maximum profit occurs if the stock ends at:

  1. ₹100
  2. ₹110
  3. ₹120
  4. ₹130
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹110

This is a long call butterfly, which peaks at the middle strike of ₹110 and has limited loss on either side.

Question 6

Which strategy is suitable for an investor who is mildly bullish and wants to reduce the cost of buying a call?

  1. Long put
  2. Bear put spread
  3. Short call alone
  4. Bull call spread
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Bull call spread

A bull call spread buys a lower-strike call and sells a higher-strike call, reducing cost while capping upside. Puts and bear spreads are bearish.

Question 7

A short straddle carries which risk?

  1. Loss limited to the premium paid
  2. Loss only if the price stays still
  3. Large losses on big moves either way
  4. No risk, as premiums are received
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Large losses on big moves either way

Selling a call and a put at the same strike earns premium but loses heavily if the price moves sharply in either direction.

Question 8

An investor holds a stock and sells an OTM call on it to earn income. If the stock rises sharply, she will:

  1. Miss gains above the strike
  2. Lose more than the stock's rise
  3. Receive unlimited profits
  4. Lose the premium received
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Miss gains above the strike

A covered call caps upside at the strike, as she must deliver the stock if assigned. She keeps the premium but gives up further gains.
