Language Comprehension: MAT practice questions

15 free questions with answers and explanations on Language Comprehension, from the MAT (Management Aptitude Test) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this section covers

Language Comprehension in MAT tests everyday command of English. Expect synonyms and antonyms, idioms, one-word substitution, fill-in-the-blank with vocabulary and prepositions, error spotting based on subject-verb agreement and tense, sentence rearrangement, direct and indirect speech, active and passive voice, spellings, and short reading passages with main-idea, detail, vocabulary-in-context and inference questions. Revise core grammar rules, build vocabulary through regular reading, and for passages answer only from what the text actually says rather than from outside knowledge.

Language Comprehension questions

Question 1

A meticulous editor checks every comma twice. Which word is a synonym of 'meticulous' here?

  1. Painstaking
  2. Careless
  3. Indifferent
  4. Hasty
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Painstaking

Meticulous means showing great attention to detail, which is what painstaking means. Careless and hasty are nearly opposite, and indifferent means lacking interest.

Question 2

Ravi is frugal: he cooks at home and saves most of his salary. Pick the antonym of 'frugal'.

  1. Economical
  2. Extravagant
  3. Prudent
  4. Thrifty
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Extravagant

Frugal means careful and sparing with money. Extravagant, meaning spending lavishly, is its opposite. Thrifty and economical are synonyms, and prudent is close in sense.

Question 3

Choose the correct meaning of the idiom 'a red herring'.

  1. An embarrassing mistake made in public
  2. A rare and valuable opportunity that should be seized
  3. A warning sign of danger ahead
  4. Something that distracts attention from the real issue
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Something that distracts attention from the real issue

A red herring is a misleading clue or irrelevant point that draws attention away from the main question, common in debates and detective stories. It has nothing to do with danger signs or opportunities.

Question 4

Choose the one word for: 'a person who can speak several languages'.

  1. Polymath
  2. Ventriloquist
  3. Polyglot
  4. Bibliophile
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Polyglot

A polyglot knows or speaks many languages. A polymath has wide learning in many subjects, a bibliophile loves books, and a ventriloquist throws his voice.

Question 5

Fill in the blank with the most appropriate word. The committee's report was so ______ that even experts struggled to follow its argument.

  1. concise
  2. lucid
  3. convoluted
  4. candid
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. convoluted

'Even experts struggled to follow' signals a report that was complicated and hard to follow, which is convoluted. Lucid and concise would make it easy to follow; candid concerns honesty, not clarity.

Question 6

Fill in the blank with the correct preposition. She has been working in this company ______ 2019.

  1. for
  2. from
  3. by
  4. since
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. since

With the present perfect continuous, 'since' is used before a point in time (2019) and 'for' before a period (five years). 'From' needs an end point such as 'to 2023'.

Question 7

Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error. Neither the manager nor his assistants / was present / at the meeting / held yesterday.

  1. held yesterday
  2. was present
  3. at the meeting
  4. Neither the manager nor his assistants
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. was present

With 'neither…nor', the verb agrees with the nearer subject. 'Assistants' is plural, so the verb should be 'were present', not 'was present'.

Question 8

Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error. He is one of those leaders / who has always / put the country / before himself.

  1. who has always
  2. He is one of those leaders
  3. before himself
  4. put the country
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. who has always

In 'one of those leaders who…', the relative pronoun 'who' refers to the plural 'leaders', so the verb must be plural: 'who have always put the country before themselves'.

Question 9

The first sentence is given. Arrange P, Q, R and S to form a coherent paragraph. 1. The monsoon is vital to Indian agriculture. P. As a result, a delayed or weak monsoon can hurt farm output and rural incomes. Q. A large part of the country's farmland depends on rainfall rather than irrigation. R. This, in turn, can push up food prices in towns and cities. S. Policymakers therefore track the monsoon's progress closely.

  1. PQRS
  2. QPRS
  3. QPSR
  4. QRPS
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. QPRS

Q explains why the monsoon is vital; P gives the result ('As a result'); R extends the effect to cities ('in turn'); S concludes ('therefore'). Hence QPRS.

Question 10

Choose the correct indirect speech of: He said, "I will finish the report tomorrow."

  1. He told that he would finish the report the next day.
  2. He said that I would finish the report the next day.
  3. He said that he would finish the report the next day.
  4. He said that he will finish the report tomorrow.
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. He said that he would finish the report the next day.

In reported speech after a past reporting verb, 'will' becomes 'would', 'I' becomes 'he' and 'tomorrow' becomes 'the next day'. 'Told' needs an object, so 'He told that' is incorrect.

Question 11

Which of the following words is spelt correctly?

  1. Millenium
  2. Occurence
  3. Embarassment
  4. Accommodate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Accommodate

Accommodate has double 'c' and double 'm'. The correct spellings of the others are occurrence (double r), embarrassment (double r and double s) and millennium (double l and double n).

Question 12

Read the passage and answer the question. Stepwells, found mainly in Gujarat and Rajasthan, were built to reach groundwater that sank far below the surface in the dry months. Flights of steps led down several storeys to the water, so that people could draw it whatever its level. Because the lower levels stayed cooler than the scorching ground above, stepwells also served as places of rest and social gathering, and many were richly carved. Under colonial rule, piped water and hand-pumps were promoted as more hygienic, and many stepwells fell into neglect, some becoming dumps for rubbish. Today a number are being restored, not only as heritage sites but also as a way of recharging groundwater in regions where it is being rapidly depleted. According to the passage, stepwells had long flights of steps mainly:

  1. to give access to water whose level changed through the year
  2. to display the wealth of their builders
  3. to keep animals away from the water
  4. to provide seating for large public ceremonies held at the well
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. to give access to water whose level changed through the year

The passage says steps led down several storeys 'so that people could draw it whatever its level', because groundwater sank in the dry months. The other purposes are not stated.

Question 13

Read the passage and answer the question. Stepwells, found mainly in Gujarat and Rajasthan, were built to reach groundwater that sank far below the surface in the dry months. Flights of steps led down several storeys to the water, so that people could draw it whatever its level. Because the lower levels stayed cooler than the scorching ground above, stepwells also served as places of rest and social gathering, and many were richly carved. Under colonial rule, piped water and hand-pumps were promoted as more hygienic, and many stepwells fell into neglect, some becoming dumps for rubbish. Today a number are being restored, not only as heritage sites but also as a way of recharging groundwater in regions where it is being rapidly depleted. Which of the following is NOT mentioned in the passage as a role of stepwells?

  1. Providing access to groundwater
  2. Serving as a place for social gathering
  3. Offering a cool place to rest
  4. Storing grain during famines
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Storing grain during famines

The passage mentions access to groundwater, cool lower levels for rest, and social gathering. Storage of grain is never mentioned, so it is the answer.

Question 14

Read the passage and answer the question. Stepwells, found mainly in Gujarat and Rajasthan, were built to reach groundwater that sank far below the surface in the dry months. Flights of steps led down several storeys to the water, so that people could draw it whatever its level. Because the lower levels stayed cooler than the scorching ground above, stepwells also served as places of rest and social gathering, and many were richly carved. Under colonial rule, piped water and hand-pumps were promoted as more hygienic, and many stepwells fell into neglect, some becoming dumps for rubbish. Today a number are being restored, not only as heritage sites but also as a way of recharging groundwater in regions where it is being rapidly depleted. As used in the passage, the word 'depleted' most nearly means:

  1. used up
  2. measured
  3. polluted
  4. replenished
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. used up

Depleted means reduced or exhausted. Restoring stepwells to recharge groundwater makes sense only if groundwater is being used up. 'Replenished' is the opposite, and 'polluted' changes quality, not quantity.

Question 15

Read the passage and answer the question. Stepwells, found mainly in Gujarat and Rajasthan, were built to reach groundwater that sank far below the surface in the dry months. Flights of steps led down several storeys to the water, so that people could draw it whatever its level. Because the lower levels stayed cooler than the scorching ground above, stepwells also served as places of rest and social gathering, and many were richly carved. Under colonial rule, piped water and hand-pumps were promoted as more hygienic, and many stepwells fell into neglect, some becoming dumps for rubbish. Today a number are being restored, not only as heritage sites but also as a way of recharging groundwater in regions where it is being rapidly depleted. Which of the following can be inferred from the passage?

  1. Piped water reached the stepwell regions only after independence.
  2. Stepwells may have practical value beyond their historical importance.
  3. All the stepwells in Rajasthan have now been fully restored.
  4. Stepwells were built mainly to store rainwater for temples.
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Stepwells may have practical value beyond their historical importance.

Restoration is valued 'not only as heritage sites but also' for recharging groundwater, which shows a practical value. The passage says only 'a number' are being restored and links piped water to colonial rule.
