Valuation: NISM Series XIX-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Valuation, from the NISM Series XIX-D (Category I and II Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers fair valuation of Category I and II AIF portfolios: approaches based on market multiples, comparable transactions, discounted cash flows, net assets and the price of recent investment; adjustments for preferences, control and marketability; valuation of debt and distressed holdings; and SEBI's requirements on independent valuers, frequency, and use of IPEV or industry-endorsed guidelines. Practise EV-to-equity bridges and round-based valuations, and understand when values must be recalibrated.

Valuation questions

Question 1

A company has revenue of ₹60 crore, peers trade at 4x EV/revenue, and the company holds net cash of ₹20 crore. Its equity value is:

  1. ₹260 crore
  2. ₹240 crore
  3. ₹220 crore
  4. ₹280 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹260 crore

EV = 60 × 4 = ₹240 crore. Net cash adds to equity: 240 + 20 = ₹260 crore.

Question 2

Revenue multiples are most often used to value:

  1. Mature utilities with steady earnings
  2. Government bonds
  3. Early-stage companies with little or no profit
  4. Cash balances
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Early-stage companies with little or no profit

When earnings are negative or volatile, revenue multiples provide a usable reference.

Question 3

A loan held by a private credit fund that is performing normally is typically valued with reference to:

  1. The borrower's share price only
  2. Discounted cash flows at a market-based yield
  3. Its original amount regardless of credit change
  4. The fund's carry
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Discounted cash flows at a market-based yield

Loans are valued by discounting expected cash flows at yields reflecting current credit risk.

Question 4

A down round at a much lower price than the fund's last mark signals that the fund should:

  1. Reassess and likely lower the carrying value
  2. Keep the earlier value unchanged until the company is finally sold or listed
  3. Raise the value
  4. Stop valuing the investment
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Reassess and likely lower the carrying value

New arm's-length pricing is strong evidence of fair value and usually requires a markdown.

Question 5

Net asset value approach is most suitable for:

  1. Asset-holding companies such as real estate holdcos
  2. High-growth software start-ups
  3. Companies with no assets
  4. Listed blue chips
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Asset-holding companies such as real estate holdcos

Where value lies mainly in underlying assets, summing their fair values less liabilities is appropriate.

Question 6

Why does SEBI require AIF valuations to follow a disclosed methodology?

  1. To guarantee high returns
  2. To ensure consistency and allow investors to understand values
  3. To eliminate all professional judgement from the valuation of unlisted holdings
  4. To reduce taxes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. To ensure consistency and allow investors to understand values

Disclosure and consistency help investors assess NAV reliability and compare over time.

Question 7

A stressed company's equity is valued close to zero while its secured loans retain value because:

  1. Secured lenders rank ahead in recovery
  2. Equity always has higher seniority
  3. Loans are guaranteed by SEBI
  4. Equity holders get paid first
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Secured lenders rank ahead in recovery

In distress, recoveries flow first to secured creditors according to priority, while equity absorbs losses and may be worth little.

Question 8

A valuer adjusts a minority stake's value for lack of marketability because:

  1. The stake cannot be readily sold
  2. It is listed and liquid
  3. Minority stakes are always worth more
  4. SEBI fixes the discount
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The stake cannot be readily sold

Illiquid holdings typically warrant a discount reflecting difficulty of sale; the amount is judgement-based.
