Investments Landscape: NISM Series XIX-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Investments Landscape, from the NISM Series XIX-D (Category I and II Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter places Category I and II AIFs within the wider investment landscape. It covers asset classes, the difference between public and private markets, the investor universe (HNIs, family offices, pension funds, insurers, endowments, development finance institutions and foreign LPs), and how investors' horizons and liabilities shape their appetite for illiquid assets. It also explains the illiquidity premium and the role of private capital in financing start-ups, growth companies and infrastructure in India. Focus on matching investor types to private-market commitments and on why private markets can offer return premiums.

Investments Landscape questions

Question 1

Which type of investor commonly acts as an anchor in Indian infrastructure and SME funds, often with development objectives?

  1. Retail investors through monthly SIPs in liquid funds
  2. Corporate treasuries parking surplus cash for a week
  3. Day traders seeking intraday gains
  4. Development finance institutions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Development finance institutions

Development finance institutions and government-backed funds frequently anchor funds that support infrastructure and SMEs. Short-horizon investors cannot commit to illiquid funds.

Question 2

An insurer considering a private equity allocation must mainly reconcile it with:

  1. The daily NAV movements of the liquid mutual fund units it holds for claims
  2. Its brand advertising budget for the year
  3. Its liability profile and regulatory investment limits
  4. The number of agents it employs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Its liability profile and regulatory investment limits

Insurers invest against long-term liabilities and are subject to IRDAI investment norms, which determine how much illiquid exposure they can take.

Question 3

Private capital matters for the Indian economy mainly because it:

  1. Replaces the need for any bank lending to small and mid-sized businesses in India
  2. Guarantees jobs in every company it backs
  3. Is available only to government-owned companies
  4. Funds companies that cannot easily raise bank loans or list
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Funds companies that cannot easily raise bank loans or list

Start-ups and growth firms often lack collateral or scale for bank loans or IPOs; private equity and venture capital fill that gap.

Question 4

Compared with a listed equity fund, a Category II PE fund offers investors:

  1. Daily liquidity and lower fees than an index fund
  2. Potential illiquidity premium with long lock-in
  3. Guaranteed returns because holdings are unlisted
  4. Freedom to redeem at NAV whenever needed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Potential illiquidity premium with long lock-in

PE funds may earn a premium for illiquidity and active ownership, but capital is locked up for years and fees are higher.

Question 5

A family office with ₹500 crore and no near-term spending needs wants to add private equity. A reasonable first step is to:

  1. Set a target allocation and commit across vintages
  2. Invest the full amount in one fund in one year
  3. Choose the single fund with the highest past IRR and commit the whole amount to it
  4. Wait until markets are at an all-time high
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Set a target allocation and commit across vintages

A planned target allocation, built over several vintages and managers, manages timing and manager risk. Concentrating in one fund or chasing past IRR is risky.

Question 6

The 'denominator effect' faced by institutional investors refers to:

  1. A fall in the value of private assets caused by higher management fees in a weak market
  2. The rise in the number of funds in the market
  3. Private assets exceeding target weight after public markets fall
  4. The reduction in management fees over time
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Private assets exceeding target weight after public markets fall

When listed assets fall sharply, the slower-marked private allocation becomes a larger share of the portfolio, possibly breaching targets and limiting new commitments.

Question 7

Why are pension funds generally considered natural long-term investors in private markets?

  1. They need daily liquidity for all benefits
  2. They are barred by their regulators from holding any listed equities at all
  3. They pay no attention to risk
  4. Their liabilities stretch decades into the future
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Their liabilities stretch decades into the future

Pension liabilities are long-dated and predictable, allowing part of the portfolio to be locked up for higher returns.

Question 8

Which statement about private markets versus public markets is most accurate?

  1. Private markets publish transaction prices continuously, like stock exchanges
  2. Public markets have no regulation
  3. Private markets are free of all risk
  4. Private markets have less disclosure and price discovery
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Private markets have less disclosure and price discovery

Private markets rely on negotiated deals with limited disclosure and periodic valuations, unlike continuously priced, highly regulated public markets.
