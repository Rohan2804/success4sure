Types of Investments: NISM Series XIX-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Types of Investments, from the NISM Series XIX-D (Category I and II Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the instruments and strategies used by Category I and II AIFs: common and preference equity, compulsorily convertible instruments, optionally convertible debentures, mezzanine and structured debt, venture debt, private credit, real estate and infrastructure investments, and special situation assets. It explains seniority in the capital structure, liquidation preferences, conversion terms, warrants and covenants, and how FEMA influences instrument choice for foreign capital. Focus on the risk–return trade-offs of each instrument and on which instruments suit early-stage, growth, buyout and credit strategies.

Types of Investments questions

Question 1

A start-up issues CCPS to a fund at ₹100 per share with a 1x non-participating liquidation preference. On a sale at a low price, the fund will receive:

  1. The higher of its preference amount or its as-converted share
  2. Only its as-converted share of sale proceeds, even if this is below its investment
  3. Twice its investment in all circumstances
  4. Nothing until all equity holders are paid
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The higher of its preference amount or its as-converted share

A non-participating preference gives the holder either its preference amount or what it would get on conversion, whichever is higher.

Question 2

An optionally convertible debenture (OCD) differs from a CCPS mainly because the OCD:

  1. May be repaid as debt if not converted
  2. Must always convert into equity on a fixed date
  3. Ranks below equity in a liquidation
  4. Cannot carry any coupon
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. May be repaid as debt if not converted

OCDs give the holder a choice to convert or be repaid, so they behave like debt unless converted. CCPS must convert into equity.

Question 3

A mezzanine lender often receives an 'equity kicker'. This is:

  1. A higher seniority than senior secured debt
  2. A guarantee from the government
  3. Warrants or conversion rights adding upside
  4. An exemption from interest payments
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Warrants or conversion rights adding upside

Equity kickers such as warrants compensate mezzanine lenders for subordination with a share in equity upside.

Question 4

For a stressed company, a special situation fund may acquire its loans from banks mainly in order to:

  1. Earn the original coupon on the loans without taking any credit or recovery risk
  2. Drive a restructuring or resolution and gain from recovery
  3. Avoid all legal processes
  4. List the loans on a stock exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Drive a restructuring or resolution and gain from recovery

Buying stressed loans at a discount gives the fund influence over restructuring or insolvency resolution and upside from recovery.

Question 5

Which covenant protects a private credit lender by restricting a borrower's further borrowing?

  1. A tag-along right that lets the lender sell its shares alongside the promoter
  2. A dividend reinvestment plan
  3. A negative covenant limiting additional debt
  4. A right of first offer on shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A negative covenant limiting additional debt

Negative covenants restrict actions such as taking more debt or selling assets, protecting the lender's position.

Question 6

Real estate equity funds generally earn returns from:

  1. Fixed coupons guaranteed by the developers whatever the sales of the project
  2. Interest on government securities
  3. Development profits, rental income and value appreciation
  4. Daily trading of property shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Development profits, rental income and value appreciation

Real estate equity returns come from project margins, rents and capital gains, with risks of delay and market downturns.

Question 7

Why do foreign investors in Indian start-ups often prefer CCPS over OCDs?

  1. OCDs cannot be issued by Indian companies
  2. CCPS carry a dividend guaranteed by the company irrespective of its profits
  3. CCPS can be redeemed at any time
  4. FEMA treats CCPS as equity, while OCDs are treated as debt
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. FEMA treats CCPS as equity, while OCDs are treated as debt

Under FEMA, instruments that compulsorily convert are equity; optionally convertible instruments are debt, subject to ECB rules.

Question 8

Venture debt carries more risk than a typical corporate loan mainly because borrowers:

  1. Have long records of stable profits and plenty of fixed assets to pledge
  2. Are government-owned entities
  3. Have no equity investors
  4. Are often loss-making with limited collateral
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Are often loss-making with limited collateral

Venture borrowers depend on future equity funding and usually lack cash flows and hard assets, so lenders rely on investor backing and warrants.
