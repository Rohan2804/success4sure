AIFs in India and Its Suitability: NISM Series XIX-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on AIFs in India and Its Suitability, from the NISM Series XIX-D (Category I and II Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains Category I and II AIFs under SEBI's regulations — venture capital and angel funds, SME funds, social impact funds, infrastructure funds, special situation funds, private equity, private debt and funds of funds — and their role in India's capital formation. It also discusses suitability: which investors can bear illiquidity, long horizons, capital calls and uncertain outcomes, and how a manager should assess investor sophistication. Focus on classifying funds into the right category and sub-category, and on spotting situations where an AIF is unsuitable.

AIFs in India and Its Suitability questions

Question 1

A fund will invest in road and renewable energy projects and seeks to register under a category viewed as economically desirable. It should register as:

  1. Category III long-short fund
  2. Category II fund of funds
  3. Category I infrastructure fund
  4. A real estate investment trust
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Category I infrastructure fund

Infrastructure funds are a Category I sub-category. REITs and InvITs are listed trusts under separate SEBI regulations.

Question 2

A fund lending to mid-market companies through structured debt, without leverage, would usually register as:

  1. Category I SME fund
  2. Category III AIF
  3. A non-banking finance company
  4. Category II AIF
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Category II AIF

Private debt funds without leverage or special incentives fall under Category II.

Question 3

Which investor is the best fit for a Category I venture capital fund?

  1. A retiree who needs stable monthly income from a modest savings corpus
  2. A saver who wants capital protection
  3. A trader seeking returns within a month
  4. One who can tolerate high failure rates and a long lock-in
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. One who can tolerate high failure rates and a long lock-in

VC investing involves many failures and a few big winners over long periods, suiting investors with high risk tolerance and patience.

Question 4

Why is a capital-call structure important to explain to a new AIF investor?

  1. She can skip drawdowns without any consequence
  2. The fund takes all money upfront and returns it monthly
  3. Capital calls are optional for all investors
  4. She must keep cash ready to meet drawdowns when called
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. She must keep cash ready to meet drawdowns when called

Investors commit an amount drawn over time; failing to meet calls can trigger default penalties, so liquidity planning matters.

Question 5

A Category II AIF plans to invest mainly in units of other AIFs. It is a:

  1. Special situation fund
  2. Social impact fund
  3. Fund of funds
  4. Angel fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Fund of funds

A fund that invests in other AIFs is a fund of funds, typically registered under Category II.

Question 6

A client with ₹1.5 crore of total savings wants to put ₹1 crore into a PE fund. A manager should be concerned mainly about:

  1. Her preferred sector for investment and whether the fund matches that sector
  2. Whether she lives in a metro city
  3. The colour of the fund's brochure
  4. Concentration and illiquidity relative to her wealth
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Concentration and illiquidity relative to her wealth

Committing two-thirds of total savings to an illiquid fund is risky and likely unsuitable, regardless of the investor's interest.

Question 7

Special situation funds are permitted to acquire stressed loans, subject to conditions, under:

  1. The mutual fund regulations applicable to credit risk and debt schemes
  2. The insurance regulations
  3. SEBI's framework for special situation funds
  4. The PFRDA regulations
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. SEBI's framework for special situation funds

SEBI's special situation fund framework under Category I allows acquisition of stressed loans in line with RBI's directions.

Question 8

Category I and II AIFs must be close-ended mainly because:

  1. SEBI wants them to be listed on stock exchanges and traded daily like shares
  2. They invest only in government bonds
  3. Investors demand daily NAV
  4. Their unlisted holdings cannot be sold quickly to meet redemptions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Their unlisted holdings cannot be sold quickly to meet redemptions

Illiquid unlisted investments make open-ended redemptions impractical and unfair to remaining investors.
