Free NISM Series XIX-D practice questions

Question 1 · Investments Landscape

The 'illiquidity premium' in private markets refers to:

  1. Extra expected return investors demand for locking up capital
  2. A fee paid to exit early
  3. The discount on listed shares at IPO
  4. A regulatory charge on AIFs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Extra expected return investors demand for locking up capital

Investors require compensation for being unable to sell freely, raising expected returns on illiquid assets.

Question 2 · Types of Investments

A private credit fund lending to mid-sized companies earns returns mainly from:

  1. Dividend income from listed stocks
  2. Daily trading of index futures
  3. Rental income from offices
  4. Interest income, fees and repayment of principal
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Interest income, fees and repayment of principal

Private credit returns are contractual cash flows from loans rather than equity appreciation.

Question 3 · AIFs in India and Its Suitability

Why can Category I and II AIFs not be open-ended?

  1. SEBI prohibits all open-ended funds
  2. Open-ended funds cannot hold equity
  3. They invest in illiquid unlisted assets, so the regulations require them to be close-ended
  4. Investors in them are always foreign
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. They invest in illiquid unlisted assets, so the regulations require them to be close-ended

Illiquid portfolios cannot meet ongoing redemptions, so a close-ended structure with fixed tenure is mandated.

Question 4 · AIF Ecosystem

Which participant provides independent assurance on the AIF's financial statements?

  1. Investment manager
  2. Placement agent
  3. Investee company
  4. Statutory auditor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Statutory auditor

The auditor examines and reports on the accounts independently.

Question 5 · AIF Structuring

Compared with a trust, an LLP structure for an AIF:

  1. Is mandatory for Category II AIFs
  2. Offers limited liability to partners but is less commonly used for Indian AIFs
  3. Cannot be registered with SEBI
  4. Has no legal personality
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Offers limited liability to partners but is less commonly used for Indian AIFs

LLPs are permitted for AIFs but trusts dominate due to flexibility and established tax treatment.

Question 6 · Fee Structure of AIFs

Management fees during a fund's investment period are usually charged on:

  1. Only realised gains
  2. Committed capital
  3. The investee companies' revenues
  4. Distributions made
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Committed capital

Fees on commitments fund the manager while capital is being deployed; post-investment period fees often step down to invested capital.

Question 7 · Fund Performance and Benchmarking

A fund invested ₹20 crore in a company and realised ₹50 crore on exit. The multiple on invested capital (MOIC) is:

  1. 1.5x
  2. 2.5x
  3. 3.0x
  4. 0.4x
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 2.5x

MOIC = 50 / 20 = 2.5x.

Question 8 · Legal Documentation and Negotiations

The Limited Partner Advisory Committee (or investor advisory committee) of a fund typically:

  1. Reviews conflicts of interest and certain matters requiring investor consent
  2. Makes all investment decisions
  3. Acts as the fund's custodian
  4. Markets the fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Reviews conflicts of interest and certain matters requiring investor consent

LPACs provide an investor check on conflicts, valuations and waivers without running the portfolio.

Question 9 · Investment Strategies

In a PE deal, an 'anti-dilution' right protects the investor if:

  1. The company later issues shares at a lower price than the investor paid
  2. The company's revenue grows faster than expected
  3. The investor wants to sell to a third party
  4. The fund's management fee changes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The company later issues shares at a lower price than the investor paid

Anti-dilution adjusts the investor's conversion price or shareholding in a down round.

Question 10 · Investment Process and Governance of Funds

Which sequence best reflects the PE investment process?

  1. Exit → closing → sourcing → due diligence
  2. Closing → due diligence → sourcing → exit
  3. Sourcing → screening → due diligence → investment committee approval → closing → monitoring → exit
  4. Monitoring → sourcing → exit → screening
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Sourcing → screening → due diligence → investment committee approval → closing → monitoring → exit

Deals are found and screened, then investigated, approved, closed, monitored and finally exited.

Question 11 · Valuation

Under SEBI's valuation framework, Category I and II AIFs value investments using:

  1. IPEV guidelines or other valuation guidelines endorsed by an AIF industry association
  2. Only historical cost
  3. Any method chosen by the investee
  4. Face value
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. IPEV guidelines or other valuation guidelines endorsed by an AIF industry association

SEBI specifies IPEV or industry-endorsed guidelines to standardise valuation.

Question 12 · Fund Monitoring, Reporting and Exit

Which exit route usually gives a PE fund the most control over timing and price?

  1. Waiting for the investee to declare dividends
  2. Strategic sale negotiated with a buyer
  3. Open-market sale of unlisted shares
  4. Liquidation of the company
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Strategic sale negotiated with a buyer

Negotiated trade sales let the fund control process and price, unlike IPO windows that depend on markets.

Question 13 · Taxation

For tax purposes, an AIF that is a Category I or II trust must provide each investor with:

  1. Only a statement of total commitment
  2. A statement of income allocated to them, broken down by nature
  3. A GST invoice for returns
  4. Nothing, as investors calculate their own income
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A statement of income allocated to them, broken down by nature

Pass-through requires the fund to report the character and amount of income allocated to each investor.

Question 14 · Regulatory Framework

Category I and II AIFs may borrow only for temporary funding needs. Which of the following is a condition SEBI attaches to such borrowing?

  1. It can be used to double the fund's investments
  2. It is for meeting temporary shortfalls and is limited in amount, duration and frequency
  3. It needs no disclosure to investors
  4. It may be secured on investors' personal assets
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. It is for meeting temporary shortfalls and is limited in amount, duration and frequency

SEBI permits only short-term operational borrowing with caps on size, tenure and frequency.

Question 15 · Types of Investments

Which instrument gives a PE investor downside protection through priority in liquidation while retaining upside?

  1. Plain equity shares
  2. Convertible preference shares with a liquidation preference
  3. Treasury bills
  4. Unsecured trade payables
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Convertible preference shares with a liquidation preference

Liquidation preference returns capital first, while conversion allows participation in upside.
