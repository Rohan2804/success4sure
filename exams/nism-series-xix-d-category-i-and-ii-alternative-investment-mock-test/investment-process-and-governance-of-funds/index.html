Investment Process and Governance of Funds: NISM Series XIX-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Investment Process and Governance of Funds, from the NISM Series XIX-D (Category I and II Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the investment process of Category I and II AIFs — sourcing, screening, preliminary evaluation, term sheet, due diligence (commercial, financial, legal, tax, ESG), IC approval, documentation, closing, monitoring, value creation and exit — and fund governance: the investment committee, conflict-of-interest policy, related-party approvals, allocation policy, compliance with PPM and regulations, stewardship responsibilities, and roles of trustee and compliance officer. Focus on sequencing and on governance actions that protect investors.

Investment Process and Governance of Funds questions

Question 1

Commercial due diligence on a target company mainly assesses:

  1. Only the company's tax filings
  2. Market size, competition and the business model
  3. The fund's own fee structure and how it compares with peer funds
  4. Investors' KYC documents
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Market size, competition and the business model

Commercial diligence evaluates the market, competitive position and sustainability of the business plan.

Question 2

A term sheet is usually signed:

  1. After the fund exits the investment
  2. Before detailed due diligence and final documents
  3. After closing and funding
  4. Only when the company lists
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Before detailed due diligence and final documents

The term sheet records key commercial terms and often grants exclusivity before detailed diligence.

Question 3

The 100-day plan after a buyout refers to:

  1. Priority actions for value creation after acquisition
  2. The period before SEBI registration
  3. The time allowed to investors to redeem units after the fund's first close
  4. The fund's marketing campaign
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Priority actions for value creation after acquisition

Buyout funds agree early priorities with management, such as operational fixes and governance changes.

Question 4

Under SEBI's stewardship code, an AIF should:

  1. Avoid any engagement with investee management
  2. Vote only as the promoter wishes
  3. Keep its voting policy secret
  4. Monitor and engage with investee companies and disclose voting policy
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Monitor and engage with investee companies and disclose voting policy

The stewardship code requires institutional investors, including AIFs, to have policies on monitoring, engagement and voting.

Question 5

A manager discovers that an investee's CFO is the manager's partner's relative. The manager should:

  1. Disclose the relationship and follow the conflict policy
  2. Ignore it, since a family relationship has no bearing on the fund's interests
  3. Hide it from the investment committee
  4. Immediately sell the stake at any price
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Disclose the relationship and follow the conflict policy

Potential conflicts involving relatives of the manager's team must be disclosed and handled under the fund's conflict-of-interest policy.

Question 6

Legal due diligence on a target typically reviews:

  1. Market growth forecasts only
  2. Fund investors' tax status
  3. Title, licences, contracts and litigation
  4. The fund's carry calculation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Title, licences, contracts and litigation

Legal due diligence checks ownership of shares and assets, licences and approvals, material contracts and pending litigation of the target.

Question 7

Which governance practice reduces the risk of biased investment decisions?

  1. Decisions taken by one senior partner alone, without minutes or written records
  2. Recusal of conflicted members and recorded IC minutes
  3. Allowing founders to vote in the IC
  4. Skipping IC review for small deals
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Recusal of conflicted members and recorded IC minutes

Recusal of conflicted members and recorded minutes make investment committee decisions objective, traceable and accountable to investors.

Question 8

After investment, value creation in a PE portfolio company often includes:

  1. Strengthening management, governance and operations
  2. Daily trading of its shares
  3. Withdrawing all board involvement
  4. Reducing reporting to investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Strengthening management, governance and operations

Active ownership adds value by strengthening management, systems, strategy and governance of portfolio companies, not by trading their shares.
