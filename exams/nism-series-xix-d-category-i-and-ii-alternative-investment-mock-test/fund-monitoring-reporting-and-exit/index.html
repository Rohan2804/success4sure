Fund Monitoring, Reporting and Exit: NISM Series XIX-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Fund Monitoring, Reporting and Exit, from the NISM Series XIX-D (Category I and II Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers monitoring and reporting during a Category I or II AIF's life and the exits that end it. Topics include portfolio monitoring through board seats and KPIs, investor reporting (capital account statements, quarterly reports, valuation and ESG reports), reporting to SEBI, the annual PPM audit, exit routes (IPO, strategic sale, secondary sale to financial investors, buyback, recapitalisation), GP-led continuation funds, tenure extension, dissolution and in-specie distribution. Focus on exit selection and the protections around end-of-life decisions.

Fund Monitoring, Reporting and Exit questions

Question 1

In a GP-led continuation fund transaction, a key safeguard for existing investors is:

  1. Mandatory rollover of all investors
  2. No disclosure of the transfer price to the existing investors of the fund
  3. Manager setting the price alone
  4. An independent price-discovery process such as a fairness opinion
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. An independent price-discovery process such as a fairness opinion

Because the manager is on both sides, independent pricing and informed investor choice are essential.

Question 2

Lock-in rules after an IPO affect a PE fund's exit mainly by:

  1. Allowing immediate full sale on listing day
  2. Cancelling the shares
  3. Increasing carry automatically
  4. Delaying the sale of pre-IPO shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Delaying the sale of pre-IPO shares

Pre-IPO shareholders may face lock-ins, so funds often exit gradually after listing.

Question 3

A quarterly investor report of a PE fund typically includes:

  1. Daily stock tips
  2. Other investors' personal details
  3. Portfolio updates, valuations and cash-flow summaries
  4. The manager's personal tax returns
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Portfolio updates, valuations and cash-flow summaries

Quarterly reports cover portfolio performance, valuations, capital calls and distributions, and key developments in investee companies.

Question 4

A strategic buyer may pay more than a financial buyer because it:

  1. Is required by SEBI to pay a premium
  2. Avoids due diligence
  3. Can realise synergies with its existing business
  4. Does not need to finance the deal
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Can realise synergies with its existing business

Synergies in costs or revenues allow strategic acquirers to justify higher prices.

Question 5

When a fund reaches the end of its extended tenure with unsold assets, investors may approve:

  1. Conversion to an open-ended mutual fund
  2. A dissolution period or in-specie distribution as per SEBI's framework
  3. Indefinite extension of the fund's life by the manager without investor approval
  4. Forced sale to the sponsor at cost
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A dissolution period or in-specie distribution as per SEBI's framework

SEBI provides options such as a dissolution period, liquidation scheme or in-specie distribution, with investor approval.

Question 6

ESG reporting to investors helps them:

  1. Compute the fund's carry
  2. Avoid tax on distributions
  3. Redeem units early
  4. Track how portfolio companies manage ESG risks
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Track how portfolio companies manage ESG risks

ESG reports show progress on environmental, social and governance issues in the portfolio.

Question 7

A secondary sale of a portfolio stake to another PE fund is common when:

  1. The company is not ready for IPO but the fund must exit
  2. The company has just listed
  3. The fund is still in the first year of its investment period
  4. SEBI bans IPOs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The company is not ready for IPO but the fund must exit

Sales to other financial investors provide an exit where IPO or strategic sale is unavailable.

Question 8

The annual PPM compliance audit for an AIF checks:

  1. Only the investees' accounts
  2. Adherence to the terms disclosed in the PPM
  3. Investors' income tax
  4. The stock exchange's operations
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Adherence to the terms disclosed in the PPM

It verifies that the scheme has followed its PPM, with findings reported to the trustee, manager and SEBI as required.
