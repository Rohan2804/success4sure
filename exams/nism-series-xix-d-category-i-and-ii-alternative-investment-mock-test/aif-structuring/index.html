AIF Structuring: NISM Series XIX-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on AIF Structuring, from the NISM Series XIX-D (Category I and II Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers structuring Category I and II AIFs: the choice of trust, LLP or company; the trust deed, contribution agreement, investment management agreement and PPM; unit classes and differential rights; onshore and offshore pooling; feeder funds and parallel funds; co-investment by investors; accredited-investor-only and large value fund schemes; and FEMA treatment of downstream investment. Focus on why a structure is chosen and how SEBI's rules on investor rights, co-investment and disclosure constrain structuring choices.

AIF Structuring questions

Question 1

A parallel fund structure is used when:

  1. One fund invests all its money into another fund managed by the same manager
  2. Separate vehicles invest side by side for different investor groups
  3. Two managers compete for the same deals
  4. A fund invests in listed shares only
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Separate vehicles invest side by side for different investor groups

Parallel funds, often onshore and offshore, invest alongside each other in agreed proportions for investors with different needs.

Question 2

Under SEBI's rules, co-investment by a Category I or II AIF's investors in an investee company must be:

  1. Always at a lower price than the AIF paid, to reward the co-investors
  2. Kept undisclosed to other investors
  3. On terms not more favourable than the AIF's
  4. Made only by the manager's relatives
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. On terms not more favourable than the AIF's

SEBI requires co-investment terms to be no more favourable than those of the AIF for the same investee, among other conditions.

Question 3

A manager structures a scheme for accredited investors only. A key benefit is:

  1. Exemption from SEBI registration and from filing a placement memorandum
  2. Certain regulatory relaxations available to such schemes
  3. Guaranteed tax exemption
  4. Permission to accept retail investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Certain regulatory relaxations available to such schemes

Accredited-investor-only schemes and large value funds receive some relaxations, as such investors are considered better able to assess risk.

Question 4

A trust deed for an AIF mainly sets out:

  1. The detailed terms of each portfolio company investment the fund will make
  2. The salary of the manager's employees
  3. The trust's objectives and powers of the trustee
  4. Daily NAV of the fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The trust's objectives and powers of the trustee

The trust deed creates the trust and defines its objects, the trustee's powers and the beneficiaries.

Question 5

Why might a manager offer a separate unit class to an anchor investor with a fee discount?

  1. To hide fees from other investors
  2. To allow the anchor investor to take over management of the fund's portfolio
  3. To reward a large early commitment, as disclosed
  4. To avoid KYC of the anchor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. To reward a large early commitment, as disclosed

Fee discounts for anchors are common if disclosed and consistent with SEBI's framework on differential rights.

Question 6

An offshore feeder investing into an Indian AIF would typically be subject to:

  1. No regulation at all
  2. Rules of both its home jurisdiction and Indian law
  3. Only Indian mutual fund rules
  4. Only US securities law
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Rules of both its home jurisdiction and Indian law

Feeders must follow their own jurisdiction's rules and Indian regulations such as FEMA and SEBI norms.

Question 7

A Category II AIF set up as an LLP gives its partners:

  1. Limited liability
  2. Unlimited personal liability
  3. Exemption from SEBI rules
  4. Daily redemption rights
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Limited liability

LLPs offer limited liability to partners, though they are less common for Indian AIFs than trusts.

Question 8

Investors of a Category I or II AIF generally have rights that are:

  1. Pro-rata to their commitments, subject to permitted exceptions
  2. Decided by the manager case by case for each investor, without any disclosure
  3. Equal irrespective of commitment
  4. Determined by the investee
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Pro-rata to their commitments, subject to permitted exceptions

SEBI requires pro-rata rights in line with commitments, with limited permitted differential rights.
