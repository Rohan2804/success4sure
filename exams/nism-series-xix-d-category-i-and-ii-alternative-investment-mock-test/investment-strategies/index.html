Investment Strategies: NISM Series XIX-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Investment Strategies, from the NISM Series XIX-D (Category I and II Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers strategies used by Category I and II AIFs: venture capital (seed to late stage), growth equity, buyouts, PIPEs, private credit and venture debt, real estate, infrastructure, special situations and distressed investing, and funds of funds and secondaries. It also covers deal terms that support each strategy, such as anti-dilution, tag-along, drag-along and liquidation preference, and how valuation and exits differ across strategies. Focus on how each strategy creates value and its characteristic risks.

Investment Strategies questions

Question 1

A secondaries fund in private equity primarily:

  1. Invests only in seed-stage start-ups through convertible notes and SAFEs
  2. Lends to real estate developers
  3. Buys existing fund interests or portfolios from other investors
  4. Trades listed shares daily
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Buys existing fund interests or portfolios from other investors

Secondaries funds buy LP interests or portfolios, often at a discount, providing liquidity to sellers and shorter J-curves.

Question 2

A start-up raises ₹50 crore from a fund for a 20% post-money stake. The pre-money valuation is:

  1. ₹250 crore
  2. ₹50 crore
  3. ₹300 crore
  4. ₹200 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹200 crore

Post-money = 50 ÷ 20% = ₹250 crore; pre-money = 250 − 50 = ₹200 crore.

Question 3

A founder owns 60% of a company. A new round issues shares equal to 25% of the post-money company. The founder's stake becomes:

  1. 35%
  2. 48%
  3. 60%
  4. 45%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 45%

The new round dilutes all existing holders by 25%, so the founder holds 60% × 0.75 = 45%. 35% wrongly subtracts 25 points.

Question 4

A PIPE investment by a Category II AIF means investing in:

  1. A listed company through a private placement
  2. An unlisted start-up's seed round
  3. Government bonds
  4. Real estate projects
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A listed company through a private placement

PIPE is a private investment in public equity — a negotiated placement in a listed company.

Question 5

The main risk in an early-stage VC strategy is:

  1. Interest-rate changes on bank deposits
  2. A high failure rate among portfolio companies
  3. Low volatility of start-up valuations
  4. Guaranteed dilution protection
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A high failure rate among portfolio companies

Most early-stage companies fail or return little; returns depend on a few big winners.

Question 6

A growth equity fund typically takes:

  1. Minority stakes in profitable or scaling companies
  2. Majority control in every deal, using heavy acquisition debt
  3. Only debt instruments
  4. Stakes only in listed blue chips
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Minority stakes in profitable or scaling companies

Growth equity provides expansion capital through minority stakes with lower leverage than buyouts.

Question 7

An infrastructure fund's long-term returns depend heavily on:

  1. Daily stock market movements
  2. Regulatory and concession terms of the projects
  3. The fund's office location
  4. Retail investor sentiment
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Regulatory and concession terms of the projects

Infrastructure cash flows depend on tariffs, concessions and regulation, making these key risks.

Question 8

A fund of funds investing in Category II AIFs offers access to diverse managers but:

  1. Guarantees top-quartile returns
  2. Adds a second layer of fees
  3. Removes all illiquidity
  4. Allows daily redemptions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Adds a second layer of fees

FoFs charge their own fees on top of underlying fund fees, reducing net returns.
