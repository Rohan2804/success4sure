Fund Performance and Benchmarking: NISM Series XIX-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Fund Performance and Benchmarking, from the NISM Series XIX-D (Category I and II Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers performance measurement for Category I and II AIFs: IRR (gross and net), multiples such as MOIC, TVPI, DPI and RVPI, the J-curve, the effect of subscription lines, vintage-year and strategy peer benchmarks, quartile rankings, public market equivalent methods, and SEBI's benchmarking framework through benchmarking agencies. Practise calculating each multiple and interpreting differences between IRR and multiples, and understand why comparisons should use the same vintage and strategy.

Fund Performance and Benchmarking questions

Question 1

A fund invested ₹25 crore in a company and realised ₹90 crore on exit. The MOIC is:

  1. 3.6x
  2. 2.6x
  3. 3.0x
  4. 0.28x
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 3.6x

MOIC = 90 ÷ 25 = 3.6x. 2.6x would be the net gain multiple (65 ÷ 25).

Question 2

A fund has paid-in capital of ₹120 crore, distributions of ₹108 crore and NAV of ₹60 crore. Its DPI is:

  1. 1.4x
  2. 0.5x
  3. 0.9x
  4. 1.8x
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 0.9x

DPI = 108 ÷ 120 = 0.9x. TVPI is 1.4x and RVPI is 0.5x.

Question 3

An investor puts in ₹100 crore and receives ₹133.1 crore exactly three years later. The IRR is:

  1. 11%
  2. 33.1%
  3. 9%
  4. 10%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 10%

100 × (1 + r)³ = 133.1 gives r = 10%. 11% is the simple average of 33.1%.

Question 4

Why should a 2018-vintage fund be compared with 2018-vintage peers?

  1. All vintages always deliver the same returns
  2. SEBI requires every manager to raise exactly one fund in each calendar year
  3. Fees differ only by vintage
  4. Entry valuations and market conditions were similar
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Entry valuations and market conditions were similar

Funds of the same vintage faced comparable entry valuations and market conditions, which makes peer comparisons fairer.

Question 5

A fund's DPI is low but TVPI is high in year eight. This means:

  1. Most of the value is still unrealised
  2. Most value has already been returned
  3. The fund has negative returns
  4. Fees have been waived
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Most of the value is still unrealised

A high TVPI with low DPI shows value sits in NAV rather than cash returned, so realisation risk remains.

Question 6

Net IRR is usually lower than gross IRR because net IRR:

  1. Includes only unrealised investments
  2. Ignores cash flow timing
  3. Deducts fees, expenses and carry
  4. Adds back management fees
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Deducts fees, expenses and carry

Net IRR measures investors' actual returns after management fees, fund expenses and carried interest, so it is below gross IRR.

Question 7

A public market equivalent analysis shows a fund under-performed the Nifty on a cash-flow-matched basis. This means:

  1. The fund outperformed the index after adjusting for the timing of cash flows
  2. The fund's IRR was negative
  3. The index cannot be compared
  4. Investing the same flows in the index would have earned more
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Investing the same flows in the index would have earned more

PME compares the fund with a hypothetical index investment using the same cash flows; underperformance means the index route would have done better.

Question 8

SEBI's performance benchmarking framework for AIFs aims mainly to:

  1. Fix minimum returns for each fund
  2. Give investors industry-wide data for comparing funds
  3. Replace valuation by independent valuers with an index-based valuation
  4. Set management fees
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Give investors industry-wide data for comparing funds

Benchmarking agencies publish industry benchmarks based on AIF data, aiding comparison and transparency.
