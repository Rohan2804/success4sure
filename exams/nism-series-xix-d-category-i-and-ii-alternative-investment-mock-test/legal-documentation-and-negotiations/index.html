Legal Documentation and Negotiations: NISM Series XIX-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Legal Documentation and Negotiations, from the NISM Series XIX-D (Category I and II Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers documents and negotiated terms at two levels: between the fund and its investors (PPM, trust deed, contribution agreement, investment management agreement, side letters, key-person, removal, default, transfer, MFN and advisory committee clauses) and between the fund and investee companies (term sheet, share subscription agreement, shareholders' agreement, conditions precedent, representations and warranties, indemnities, board rights, veto rights, anti-dilution, tag-along, drag-along, ROFR and exit rights). Focus on the purpose of each clause and how it allocates risk.

Legal Documentation and Negotiations questions

Question 1

In a share subscription agreement, 'representations and warranties' given by the company and promoters mainly:

  1. Confirm facts on which the investor relies, backed by indemnity
  2. Guarantee a minimum return on the investment for the life of the fund
  3. Fix the exit date of the fund
  4. Set the fund's management fee
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Confirm facts on which the investor relies, backed by indemnity

Reps and warranties state facts about the business; if untrue, the investor can claim under indemnity provisions.

Question 2

'Conditions precedent' in a deal document are:

  1. Rights that arise only after the fund has exited the investee company
  2. Clauses about the fund's tax status
  3. Investors' redemption rights
  4. Requirements to be met before the investment is completed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Requirements to be met before the investment is completed

CPs, such as approvals and diligence closures, must be satisfied before the investor funds the deal.

Question 3

Affirmative or veto rights in a shareholders' agreement allow a minority PE investor to:

  1. Run day-to-day operations of the company
  2. Receive a guaranteed dividend
  3. Block key decisions without its consent
  4. Force the company to list within a year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Block key decisions without its consent

Veto rights over matters such as new issuances, budgets and related-party deals protect minority investors.

Question 4

A 'no-fault' removal clause in fund documents lets investors:

  1. Remove the manager only after a court has found it guilty of misconduct
  2. Redeem units at NAV
  3. Remove the manager by supermajority without proving cause
  4. Cancel past investments
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Remove the manager by supermajority without proving cause

No-fault removal gives investors a route to replace the manager by a high vote threshold even without misconduct.

Question 5

A drag-along right is typically subject to conditions such as:

  1. Unlimited use at any price by any shareholder
  2. Approval by SEBI for every sale
  3. A ban on strategic buyers
  4. Minimum price or timing thresholds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Minimum price or timing thresholds

Founders usually negotiate safeguards like minimum valuations or time windows before a drag can be exercised.

Question 6

Why do investors negotiate a cap on the fund's organisational expenses in the LPA or contribution agreement?

  1. To increase the manager's carry
  2. To limit set-up costs charged to the fund
  3. To allow early redemption
  4. To avoid KYC
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. To limit set-up costs charged to the fund

Caps on organisational expenses prevent excessive legal and set-up costs from being charged to the fund and reducing investors' returns.

Question 7

A default by an investor on a capital call may lead to:

  1. The fund being dissolved automatically and its assets distributed to the others
  2. SEBI paying the amount
  3. Penalties such as interest, suspension of rights or forced sale
  4. Higher returns for the defaulter
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Penalties such as interest, suspension of rights or forced sale

Default clauses protect the fund and other investors through penalties and possible forfeiture.

Question 8

Information covenants in a PE deal require the company to:

  1. List within two years
  2. Provide regular financial and operational reports
  3. Pay a fixed dividend
  4. Give the fund majority control
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Provide regular financial and operational reports

Information covenants give the investor periodic accounts, MIS and notices of material events for monitoring.
