Fee Structure of AIFs: NISM Series XIX-D practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Fee Structure of AIFs, from the NISM Series XIX-D (Category I and II Alternative Investment Fund Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how Category I and II AIF managers are compensated: management fees on commitments or invested capital, step-downs after the investment period, fund expenses and caps, carried interest, hurdle or preferred return, GP catch-up, whole-of-fund and deal-by-deal waterfalls, clawback and escrow, fee offsets from transaction and monitoring fees, and SEBI's disclosure requirements. Practise waterfall calculations and fee computations, and understand how terms shift value between investors and the manager.

Fee Structure of AIFs questions

Question 1

A fund with commitments of ₹600 crore charges 2% a year on commitments during a five-year investment period. Total management fees over that period are:

  1. ₹12 crore
  2. ₹60 crore
  3. ₹30 crore
  4. ₹120 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹60 crore

Annual fee = 2% × 600 = ₹12 crore. Over five years: 12 × 5 = ₹60 crore.

Question 2

Fund capital is ₹150 crore, total proceeds ₹240 crore and the preferred return is ₹12 crore. With 20% carry and no catch-up, the carry is:

  1. ₹18.0 crore
  2. ₹15.6 crore
  3. ₹2.4 crore
  4. ₹48.0 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹15.6 crore

Profit = ₹90 crore; above hurdle = 90 − 12 = ₹78 crore; carry = 20% × 78 = ₹15.6 crore.

Question 3

For the same fund (capital ₹150 crore, proceeds ₹240 crore, preferred return ₹12 crore, 20% carry) with a full catch-up, carry is:

  1. ₹15.6 crore
  2. ₹12 crore
  3. ₹18 crore
  4. ₹20 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹18 crore

With full catch-up and sufficient profits, the manager receives 20% of total profit: 20% × 90 = ₹18 crore.

Question 4

An 8% hurdle compounded annually on ₹50 crore of contributed capital for two years requires investors to receive first:

  1. ₹58.00 crore
  2. ₹54.00 crore
  3. ₹66.64 crore
  4. ₹58.32 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹58.32 crore

With annual compounding, 50 × 1.08² = ₹58.32 crore must go to investors first. ₹58 crore wrongly applies simple interest.

Question 5

A 'management fee offset' means that:

  1. Investors pay double management fees whenever the fund makes a new investment
  2. The manager pays investors a fee
  3. Carry is reduced to zero
  4. Transaction or monitoring fees reduce the management fee
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Transaction or monitoring fees reduce the management fee

Fees the manager earns from portfolio companies are partly or fully credited against management fees, so investors are not charged twice.

Question 6

A fund's management fee steps down after the investment period mainly because:

  1. The manager's workload shifts from investing to managing exits
  2. SEBI prohibits fees after five years
  3. Carry is paid instead of fees
  4. Investors stop paying any expenses
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The manager's workload shifts from investing to managing exits

Fees often fall or move to invested capital after the investment period, reflecting reduced deployment work.

Question 7

Why do investors prefer a whole-of-fund waterfall to a deal-by-deal waterfall?

  1. Carry is paid only after all contributed capital is returned
  2. The manager receives carry earlier, which improves alignment with investors
  3. It removes hurdle rates
  4. It increases management fees
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Carry is paid only after all contributed capital is returned

Whole-of-fund waterfalls defer carry until investors recover capital and the hurdle, reducing clawback risk.

Question 8

A clawback obliges the manager to:

  1. Return excess carry received, after final accounting
  2. Return management fees each year
  3. Pay investors a fixed return
  4. Buy all unsold assets of the fund at book value at the end of its life
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Return excess carry received, after final accounting

If carry received exceeds the manager's entitlement on final reckoning, the clawback requires repayment.
