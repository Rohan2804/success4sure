Taxation of Retirement Benefits: NISM Series XVII revision notes

Retirees receive several types of payments, each taxed differently. This chapter covers gratuity, leave encashment, pension (commuted and uncommuted), provident fund, NPS and annuity income, and benefits for senior citizens. Questions test which amounts are exempt and what limits apply. Tax limits change through Finance Acts, so treat every figure here as as of 2026 and verify with the income tax department.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Gratuity

For employees covered by the Payment of Gratuity Act, gratuity = 15/26 x last drawn salary (basic plus DA) x completed years of service. The exemption is the least of the formula amount, the statutory ceiling (₹20 lakh) and the actual gratuity. Government employees' gratuity is fully exempt.

2. Leave encashment

Leave encashment on retirement is fully exempt for government employees. For non-government employees it is exempt up to the least of specified limits, with the overall cap raised to ₹25 lakh from April 2023. Encashment during service is fully taxable.

3. Uncommuted and commuted pension

Uncommuted (monthly) pension is taxed as salary, and the standard deduction applies. Commuted pension (lump sum) is fully exempt for government employees. For others, one-third of the full commuted value is exempt if gratuity is also received, and one-half if not.

4. Family pension

Family pension received by heirs is taxed under 'income from other sources' with a deduction of one-third or a fixed amount, whichever is lower (the fixed amount is higher in the new regime).

5. Provident fund

EPF withdrawal after five years of continuous service is exempt. Withdrawal before five years is taxable unless due to specified reasons. Interest on employee contributions above ₹2.5 lakh a year is taxable. PPF is fully exempt.

6. NPS and annuity

At exit, up to 60% of the NPS corpus withdrawn as lump sum is exempt, and partial withdrawals within rules are exempt. Annuity income is taxable in the year received. Employer contributions under 80CCD(2) are deductible, with an overall cap on tax-free employer retirement contributions.

7. Senior citizen benefits

Seniors (60+) enjoy a higher basic exemption in the old regime, Section 80TTB deduction on interest up to ₹50,000, higher 80D limits for health insurance, and higher TDS thresholds on interest. Those aged 75+ with only pension and interest from the same bank can be exempt from filing returns under Section 194P conditions.

8. Old versus new regime

Many deductions (80C, 80CCD(1B), 80TTB, 80D) are available only in the old regime, while the new regime offers lower slab rates and a higher standard deduction. Retirees should compare both each year; the better choice depends on deductions and income mix.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Gratuity (covered employees) = 15/26 x last drawn monthly salary x completed years of service.
  • Exempt commuted pension (non-government) = 1/3 of full value if gratuity received, else 1/2.

Common traps in the exam

  • Monthly pension is taxed as salary, not as income from other sources; family pension is other sources.
  • Commuted pension exemption for non-government employees depends on whether gratuity is received.
  • EPF interest is taxable on employee contributions above ₹2.5 lakh a year even before withdrawal.
  • 80TTB applies only to senior citizens and only in the old regime.

Quick recap

Gratuity: 15/26 x salary x years, exempt up to ₹20 lakh; government fully exempt. Leave encashment cap ₹25 lakh for non-government. Monthly pension = salary income; commuted pension exempt fully (government) or 1/3 or 1/2 (others). Family pension: other sources with deduction. EPF exempt after five years. NPS 60% lump sum exempt; annuity taxable. Compare old and new regimes yearly.
