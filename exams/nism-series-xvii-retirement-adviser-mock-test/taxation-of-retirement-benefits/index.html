Taxation of Retirement Benefits: NISM Series XVII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Taxation of Retirement Benefits, from the NISM Series XVII (Retirement Adviser) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the tax treatment of retirement savings and benefits. It explains deductions for contributions to NPS and other schemes (including the employer's NPS contribution), the treatment of EPF and PPF, the taxability of pension, commuted pension, gratuity, leave encashment, annuity income and NPS withdrawals, and the choice between the old and new tax regimes. Since tax law and section numbers have changed with the new Income-tax Act, focus on concepts — which benefits are deductible, exempt or taxable, and under what conditions — rather than memorising rates and limits that may change.

Taxation of Retirement Benefits questions

Question 1

Which NPS-related deduction remains available to a salaried employee even under the new (default) tax regime?

  1. Deduction for the employee's own additional NPS contribution of up to ₹50,000
  2. Deduction for contributions to NPS Tier II
  3. Deduction for contributions to Atal Pension Yojana
  4. Deduction for the employer's contribution to the employee's NPS account
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Deduction for the employer's contribution to the employee's NPS account

Under the new regime, most deductions are unavailable, but the employer's contribution to NPS remains deductible within the prescribed percentage of salary. The additional ₹50,000 deduction for own contribution is an old-regime benefit.

Question 2

Gratuity under the Payment of Gratuity Act is computed as 15/26 × last drawn salary × completed years of service. For a last drawn salary of ₹52,000 and 20 years of service, gratuity is:

  1. ₹6,00,000
  2. ₹5,20,000
  3. ₹10,40,000
  4. ₹3,00,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹6,00,000

Gratuity = 15 ÷ 26 × 52,000 × 20 = ₹6,00,000. ₹10,40,000 would ignore the 15/26 factor.

Question 3

For a non-government employee who also receives gratuity, how much of the commuted pension is exempt from tax?

  1. One-third of the full value of the pension commuted
  2. One-half of the commuted amount
  3. The entire commuted amount
  4. None of it
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. One-third of the full value of the pension commuted

For non-government employees receiving gratuity, one-third of the commuted value of full pension is exempt; without gratuity, one-half is exempt. Government employees get full exemption.

Question 4

A private-sector employee withdraws his EPF balance after three years of continuous service to start a business. The withdrawal is generally:

  1. Fully exempt in all cases
  2. Taxable, because continuous service is less than five years
  3. Exempt only if he invests in NPS
  4. Taxable only on the employer's contribution and never on interest
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Taxable, because continuous service is less than five years

EPF withdrawals before five years of continuous service are generally taxable unless termination is due to reasons beyond the employee's control. After five years, withdrawals are exempt.

Question 5

Uncommuted (monthly) pension received by a retired employee from a former employer is taxed as:

  1. Income from other sources
  2. Capital gains
  3. Salary income
  4. Exempt income
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Salary income

Pension received from a former employer is treated as salary, so the standard deduction applies. Pension received by a family member after the employee's death is treated differently.

Question 6

Leave encashment received by a private-sector employee at retirement is:

  1. Exempt up to a statutory ceiling, with the excess taxable
  2. Fully taxable as salary without any exemption, whatever the employee's length of service
  3. Fully exempt regardless of the amount
  4. Taxed as long-term capital gains
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Exempt up to a statutory ceiling, with the excess taxable

Leave encashment at retirement for non-government employees is exempt up to the statutory limit and computation rules; amounts above that are taxable as salary. Government employees enjoy full exemption.

Question 7

Employer contributions to an employee's EPF, NPS and superannuation fund together exceed the aggregate limit specified in tax law. The excess is:

  1. Taxed as a perquisite in the employee's hands
  2. Exempt because retirement savings are never taxed
  3. Taxed in the employer's hands
  4. Refunded to the employer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Taxed as a perquisite in the employee's hands

Tax law caps the aggregate employer contribution to these funds; the excess, along with related accretions, is taxable as a perquisite to the employee.

Question 8

Interest earned on a Senior Citizens' Savings Scheme account is:

  1. Fully exempt like PPF interest
  2. Taxable only on maturity
  3. Taxable as income, though senior citizens may claim specified deductions
  4. Taxed at a flat 1%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Taxable as income, though senior citizens may claim specified deductions

SCSS interest is taxable at slab rates and attracts TDS above thresholds. Under the old regime, senior citizens may claim a deduction for interest income within limits, but it is not exempt like PPF.
