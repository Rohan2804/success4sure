Investment & Asset Allocation for Retirement: NISM Series XVII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Investment & Asset Allocation for Retirement, from the NISM Series XVII (Retirement Adviser) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how to build and manage portfolios for retirement. It covers the risk and return characteristics of equity, debt, gold and real estate, strategic and tactical asset allocation, age-based and glide-path approaches, rebalancing, diversification, the bucket strategy, and the need to beat inflation over a long retirement. It also discusses real returns, the effect of costs and taxes, and the trade-off between growth and stability. When revising, focus on matching allocation to the client's horizon, risk capacity and income needs, and on why some equity is still needed after retirement.

Investment & Asset Allocation for Retirement questions

Question 1

A retiree earns 8% on his investments while inflation is 6%. His real rate of return (exact) is:

  1. 1.89%
  2. 2.00%
  3. 14.48%
  4. 1.33%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 1.89%

Real return = (1.08 ÷ 1.06) − 1 ≈ 1.89%. Simple subtraction gives 2%, which is only an approximation.

Question 2

A 'bucket strategy' for retirement income typically involves:

  1. Investing the entire corpus in a single balanced fund and withdrawing a fixed sum every month
  2. Holding only gold and real estate after retirement
  3. Spending the equity portion first and keeping cash for later years
  4. Keeping near-term needs in safe assets and longer-term money in growth assets
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Keeping near-term needs in safe assets and longer-term money in growth assets

The bucket approach holds a few years of expenses in liquid, safe assets and invests money for later years in growth assets, reducing the need to sell equities in a downturn.

Question 3

A client's target allocation is 40% equity and 60% debt. After a strong year, equity has risen to 52% of the portfolio. Rebalancing means:

  1. Selling some equity and buying debt to restore 40:60
  2. Buying more equity to ride the momentum
  3. Switching entirely to debt to lock in gains
  4. Doing nothing until equity falls back
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Selling some equity and buying debt to restore 40:60

Rebalancing returns the portfolio to its target risk level by selling the asset that has grown above its weight and buying the one below. It is a discipline, not a market call.

Question 4

Why do most planners keep some equity exposure even for retirees in their sixties?

  1. Equity returns are guaranteed over a five-year period
  2. Retirement may last decades, and equity helps the corpus keep pace with inflation
  3. Retirees are exempt from tax on equity gains once they cross the age of sixty
  4. Debt instruments are not available to senior citizens
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Retirement may last decades, and equity helps the corpus keep pace with inflation

A retirement of 25 to 30 years needs growth to offset inflation. Equity provides that potential but is not guaranteed, and there is no special tax exemption for retirees on equity gains.

Question 5

Which client is likely to have the highest capacity to take equity risk in retirement savings?

  1. A 63-year-old retiree with no pension and modest savings
  2. A 35-year-old with stable income and 25 years to retirement
  3. A 58-year-old planning to retire in two years with large EMIs
  4. A 70-year-old who depends fully on his corpus for expenses
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A 35-year-old with stable income and 25 years to retirement

Risk capacity depends on time horizon, income stability and dependence on the portfolio. A young earner with a long horizon can absorb volatility far better than retirees who depend on the corpus.

Question 6

Strategic asset allocation differs from tactical asset allocation in that strategic allocation:

  1. Sets long-term target weights based on goals and risk profile
  2. Shifts weights frequently to exploit short-term market views
  3. Is applied only to equity mutual funds
  4. Ignores the client's time horizon
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Sets long-term target weights based on goals and risk profile

Strategic allocation is the long-term policy mix tied to goals and risk tolerance. Tactical allocation involves short-term deviations from it to exploit market opportunities.

Question 7

A retiree's portfolio includes 70% in a single private-sector company's shares, where he worked for 30 years. The main risk the adviser should highlight is:

  1. Inflation risk on fixed deposits
  2. Concentration risk
  3. Currency risk
  4. Reinvestment risk on annuities
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Concentration risk

Holding most wealth in one stock exposes the client to company-specific losses. Diversification across issuers and asset classes reduces this risk.

Question 8

A retiree compares two debt funds with similar risk. Fund A has an expense ratio of 0.3% and Fund B 1.2%. Over 20 years, the higher cost will most likely:

  1. Have no effect because both funds hold similar bonds
  2. Increase returns, because higher fees usually mean better and more active management
  3. Matter only in the first year
  4. Reduce the final corpus noticeably due to compounding of the cost difference
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Reduce the final corpus noticeably due to compounding of the cost difference

Costs compound over time, so an extra 0.9% a year significantly lowers final value. Higher fees do not guarantee better performance in similar-risk debt funds.
