Investment & Asset Allocation for Retirement: NISM Series XVII revision notes

This chapter turns the retirement number into an investment plan. It covers asset allocation across life stages, glide paths that reduce risk with age, the bucket approach for retirees, rebalancing, and the role of SIPs and SWPs. Questions are conceptual and scenario-based: pick the best allocation for a client or explain why a strategy reduces a specific risk.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Asset allocation drives outcomes

The split between equity, debt, gold and cash explains most of the variation in a portfolio's long-term return and risk. For long accumulation periods, a meaningful equity share is needed to beat inflation. For a 30-year-old, 70% to 80% equity may be reasonable; for someone at 58, much less.

2. Glide path

A glide path is a pre-set schedule for reducing equity and increasing debt as retirement approaches. NPS lifecycle funds and target-date funds follow one automatically. It protects the corpus from a large equity fall just before retirement, when there is little time to recover.

3. Rules of thumb

The '100 minus age' rule suggests equity percentage; at 40, about 60% equity. It is a starting point, not a prescription, and should be adjusted for risk tolerance, other income such as pensions, and health. Some advisers use 110 or 120 minus age given longer lives.

4. Sequence-of-returns risk

Two retirees with the same average return can end with very different outcomes if one suffers losses early in retirement while withdrawing money. Selling units in a falling market locks in losses. Keeping two to three years of expenses in safe assets reduces forced selling.

5. Bucket strategy

Divide the retirement corpus into buckets: short-term (one to three years of expenses in liquid or short-term debt funds and deposits), medium-term (hybrid or debt funds for years four to ten), and long-term (equity for growth beyond ten years). Refill the short bucket from the others periodically.

6. Rebalancing

Market moves change the allocation over time. If equity rises from 50% to 62%, sell some equity and buy debt to return to target. Rebalancing annually or at set bands enforces 'buy low, sell high' discipline. Tax and exit loads should be considered.

7. Products by phase

Accumulation: equity mutual funds via SIPs, NPS with higher equity, EPF and PPF as debt anchors. Transition: shift gradually to hybrid and debt funds, lock in gains. Distribution: SCSS, annuities, debt funds with SWPs, and a smaller equity portion to fight inflation.

8. Behavioural pitfalls

Panic selling in crashes, chasing recent winners, holding too much cash, and keeping all money in fixed deposits out of fear are common mistakes. An adviser's job includes keeping clients disciplined during volatility.

Common traps in the exam

  • A glide path reduces equity as retirement nears; it does not eliminate equity entirely at retirement.
  • Sequence risk depends on the order of returns, not the average return.
  • In a bucket strategy the short-term bucket holds safe, liquid assets, not equities.
  • '100 minus age' is a rough guide, not a regulatory rule.

Quick recap

Asset allocation drives retirement outcomes. Glide paths reduce equity with age; NPS lifecycle funds do this automatically. '100 minus age' is a starting rule of thumb. Sequence-of-returns risk is highest around retirement; keep two to three years of expenses safe. Bucket strategy: short (safe), medium (hybrid/debt), long (equity). Rebalance periodically and manage behaviour.
