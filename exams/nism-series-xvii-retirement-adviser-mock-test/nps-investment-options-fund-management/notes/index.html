NPS – Investment Options & Fund Management: NISM Series XVII revision notes

This chapter covers how NPS money is invested: the asset classes, the active and auto choice options, lifecycle funds, pension fund managers and rules for switching. Questions test equity limits by age, what each asset class holds, who decides allocation under each choice, and what happens for government employees. Treat specific percentage caps as as of 2026.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Asset classes

E is equity (mainly large-cap index stocks), C is corporate debt, G is government securities, and A is alternative assets such as REITs, InvITs and AIFs, with a small cap on allocation. Each pension fund manages separate schemes for each asset class.

2. Active choice

Under active choice the subscriber decides the allocation across E, C, G and A. Equity is capped at 75% up to a certain age, after which the cap tapers each year, and asset class A has a low maximum. A 35-year-old could choose 75% E, 15% C and 10% G.

3. Auto choice: lifecycle funds

Under auto choice the allocation is set by age through lifecycle funds. LC75 (aggressive), LC50 (moderate) and LC25 (conservative) start with maximum equity of 75%, 50% and 25% respectively and reduce equity as the subscriber ages, shifting to G. A balanced lifecycle option was added later. LC50 is the default if no choice is made.

4. Why taper equity

Equity has higher expected returns but higher volatility. As retirement approaches, a sharp fall could permanently hurt the corpus because there is little time to recover. Lifecycle funds reduce this sequence risk automatically, without the subscriber having to rebalance.

5. Pension fund managers

Subscribers choose one pension fund manager from those registered with PFRDA, such as funds sponsored by SBI, LIC, UTI, HDFC, ICICI Prudential, Kotak, Aditya Birla, Axis, Tata and others. The PFM invests within PFRDA's investment guidelines. Fees are capped and returns are published regularly.

6. Switching

Subscribers can change their pension fund manager and their scheme preference or allocation a limited number of times a year, as permitted by PFRDA (for example, PFM change once a year and allocation changes a few times a year). Switching does not attract tax.

7. Government subscribers

Government employees were traditionally invested in a default scheme split among government-sponsored pension funds with a conservative mix. They now have the option to choose active or auto choice and private PFMs, subject to conditions.

8. Returns and risk

NPS returns are market linked and not guaranteed. Equity schemes have delivered higher long-term returns than G and C schemes but with greater volatility. Advisers should match choice to risk profile and time horizon rather than chasing last year's best fund.

Common traps in the exam

  • Under active choice the subscriber decides allocation; under auto choice the lifecycle fund decides by age.
  • LC75 is aggressive, LC25 conservative; LC50 is the default auto choice.
  • Asset class A has a very low cap and is not a large equity alternative.
  • NPS returns are not guaranteed, even though costs are low.

Quick recap

NPS invests in E (equity), C (corporate debt), G (government securities) and A (alternatives, small cap). Active choice: subscriber decides, equity capped at 75% with age-based taper. Auto choice: lifecycle funds LC75, LC50 (default), LC25 and a balanced option reduce equity with age. Choose one PFM; limited annual switches allowed without tax. Returns are market linked.
