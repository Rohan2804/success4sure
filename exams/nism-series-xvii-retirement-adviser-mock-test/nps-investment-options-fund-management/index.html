NPS – Investment Options & Fund Management: NISM Series XVII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on NPS – Investment Options & Fund Management, from the NISM Series XVII (Retirement Adviser) syllabus.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how NPS money is invested. It explains the asset classes (equity, corporate debt and government securities, with limited alternative assets), Active Choice and Auto Choice with life-cycle funds, default schemes for government subscribers, the selection and change of pension fund managers, and investment guidelines prescribed by PFRDA. Learners should understand how life-cycle funds reduce equity with age, how active choice allocations are capped, the trade-off between return and volatility across asset classes, and how often a subscriber can revise choices. Use concepts rather than memorising limits that PFRDA revises from time to time.

NPS – Investment Options & Fund Management questions

Question 1

If a subscriber in the all-citizen model does not indicate any investment choice, his contributions are invested under:

  1. Active Choice with 100% government securities
  2. Auto Choice using the aggressive life-cycle fund
  3. Active Choice with an equal split across all asset classes
  4. Auto Choice using the moderate life-cycle fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Auto Choice using the moderate life-cycle fund

Where no choice is made, NPS defaults to Auto Choice with the moderate life-cycle fund (LC50). Aggressive or conservative options must be selected actively by the subscriber.

Question 2

Which type of securities forms the main holding of the NPS 'C' asset class?

  1. Corporate bonds and other fixed-income instruments of companies
  2. Commodities such as gold and silver
  3. Central government securities only, including treasury bills and state development loans
  4. Cash held with the trustee bank
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Corporate bonds and other fixed-income instruments of companies

Asset class C is the corporate debt class, covering bonds and debt instruments of companies and similar entities. Government securities form asset class G.

Question 3

Ankit, 50, chooses Active Choice and wants to allocate the maximum permitted to equity. Which statement is correct?

  1. There is no upper limit on equity allocation under Active Choice for any subscriber
  2. PFRDA caps equity under Active Choice and tapers the cap as the subscriber ages
  3. Active Choice permits equity only for subscribers under 35
  4. Equity is compulsory at 75% under Active Choice for everyone
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. PFRDA caps equity under Active Choice and tapers the cap as the subscriber ages

Active Choice lets subscribers decide allocation within limits; PFRDA sets a ceiling on equity which reduces with age in later years. There is no compulsory equity allocation.

Question 4

A subscriber is dissatisfied with her pension fund's performance. What can she do?

  1. Withdraw her entire Tier I corpus immediately without restriction
  2. Ask the CRA to manage the money directly
  3. Transfer the corpus to a mutual fund of her choice
  4. Switch to another pension fund manager as permitted by PFRDA
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Switch to another pension fund manager as permitted by PFRDA

NPS allows subscribers to change their pension fund manager periodically within PFRDA rules. Tier I money cannot be freely withdrawn or moved into mutual funds, and the CRA does not manage investments.

Question 5

Compared with the aggressive life-cycle fund, the conservative life-cycle fund under Auto Choice:

  1. Keeps equity constant throughout the subscriber's life
  2. Invests only in corporate bonds
  3. Is available only to Government sector subscribers
  4. Starts with a lower maximum equity allocation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Starts with a lower maximum equity allocation

The conservative life-cycle fund starts with a lower equity cap than the moderate and aggressive funds, and all reduce equity with age. It is available to non-government subscribers choosing Auto Choice.

Question 6

Why does a life-cycle fund reduce equity exposure as the subscriber approaches retirement?

  1. Because equities always underperform debt over long periods
  2. Because PFRDA does not permit equity after the age of 45
  3. To lower the risk of a large loss just before the corpus is needed
  4. To maximise returns in the final years of saving when the corpus is at its largest
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. To lower the risk of a large loss just before the corpus is needed

As retirement nears, there is less time to recover from a market fall, so de-risking protects the corpus. Equities usually outperform over long periods, which is why they dominate early on.

Question 7

Government sector NPS subscribers who do not exercise a choice have their contributions invested in:

  1. Only the equity scheme of a single pension fund
  2. The Tier II account by default
  3. Atal Pension Yojana
  4. A default scheme split across pension funds in proportions set by the government and PFRDA
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A default scheme split across pension funds in proportions set by the government and PFRDA

For government employees, default contributions go to a default scheme managed by specified pension funds in prescribed proportions. Many government subscribers now also have the option to choose.

Question 8

Which factor is LEAST relevant when a 28-year-old chooses between Auto and Active Choice?

  1. The name of the CRA that issued her PRAN
  2. Her risk appetite and investment knowledge
  3. Her willingness to review allocations regularly
  4. Her time horizon to retirement
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The name of the CRA that issued her PRAN

Choice of investment option should depend on risk appetite, time horizon and ability to manage allocations. The CRA only maintains records and has no bearing on investment outcomes.
