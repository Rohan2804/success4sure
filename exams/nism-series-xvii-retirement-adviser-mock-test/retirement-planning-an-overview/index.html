Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter sets out why retirement planning matters and the risks a retiree faces: longevity risk, inflation risk, health-cost risk, sequence-of-returns risk and the decline of traditional family support. It contrasts defined-benefit and defined-contribution arrangements, explains the shift in India from government pensions to contributory schemes, and introduces the life-cycle of accumulation, transition and distribution. When revising, focus on identifying each retirement risk from a scenario, understanding why starting early matters, and how inflation erodes the purchasing power of a fixed income over a long retirement.

Retirement Planning: An Overview questions

Question 1

A retiree's biggest worry is that she may live to 95 and exhaust her savings. This is known as:

  1. Liquidity risk
  2. Reinvestment risk
  3. Longevity risk
  4. Credit risk of the issuer of her bonds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Longevity risk

Longevity risk is the risk of outliving one's savings because life turns out longer than planned. Liquidity, reinvestment and credit risks are different investment risks.

Question 2

Inflation averages 8% a year. Using the Rule of 72, roughly how long will it take for the purchasing power of a fixed pension to halve?

  1. About 12 years
  2. About 6 years
  3. About 9 years
  4. About 18 years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. About 9 years

Under the Rule of 72, time to halve purchasing power ≈ 72 ÷ inflation rate = 72 ÷ 8 = 9 years. Twelve years would apply at 6% inflation.

Question 3

In a defined-benefit pension plan, who mainly bears the investment risk?

  1. The individual member
  2. The employer or plan sponsor
  3. The pension fund's custodian
  4. The member's nominee
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The employer or plan sponsor

In a defined-benefit plan, the pension is fixed by formula, so the sponsor must make good any shortfall in fund returns. In a defined-contribution plan the member bears this risk.

Question 4

A retiree suffers heavy equity losses in the first two years of retirement while also withdrawing money. Even if markets recover later, his corpus may not. This illustrates:

  1. Interest-rate risk on deposits
  2. Currency risk
  3. Sequence-of-returns risk
  4. Concentration risk in a single stock
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Sequence-of-returns risk

Sequence-of-returns risk arises because withdrawals during early losses lock in those losses, leaving a smaller base to benefit from later recovery. The order of returns matters during withdrawal.

Question 5

Which of the following is the best description of the 'accumulation phase' in retirement planning?

  1. The years after retirement when the corpus is drawn down
  2. The working years in which savings are built up for retirement
  3. The period in which an annuity is paying out to the retiree
  4. The time taken to settle the retiree's estate after death
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The working years in which savings are built up for retirement

The accumulation phase covers working years when contributions are made and invested. The distribution or decumulation phase comes after retirement, when savings are drawn down.

Question 6

Why do many advisers recommend that retirement planning start in a person's twenties?

  1. Tax laws allow retirement savings only for people below 30
  2. Longer compounding periods sharply reduce the monthly saving needed
  3. Pension products are not available to investors above 40
  4. Employers stop contributing to provident funds after 35
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Longer compounding periods sharply reduce the monthly saving needed

The longer money compounds, the smaller the regular contribution needed to reach a target. There is no tax or product rule restricting retirement saving to young people.

Question 7

A self-employed doctor has no employer pension. Compared with a government employee covered by an old defined-benefit pension, her main additional need is to:

  1. Build her own retirement corpus through voluntary saving
  2. Avoid all equity investments to preserve capital
  3. Rely on gratuity under the Payment of Gratuity Act
  4. Depend on the Employees' Pension Scheme
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Build her own retirement corpus through voluntary saving

Self-employed people have no employer pension, gratuity or EPS, so they must create their own corpus through products like NPS, PPF and mutual funds. Avoiding equity entirely would raise inflation risk.

Question 8

Which statement about the impact of medical inflation on retirement planning is most accurate?

  1. Medical costs fall after retirement because people work less
  2. Government schemes fully cover all private hospital expenses of retirees
  3. Healthcare costs often rise faster than general inflation, so they need a separate provision
  4. Health costs can be ignored if the retiree has a large equity portfolio
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Healthcare costs often rise faster than general inflation, so they need a separate provision

Medical inflation in India typically exceeds headline inflation, and health needs rise with age. Planners therefore provide for health insurance and a separate medical reserve rather than assuming general inflation.
