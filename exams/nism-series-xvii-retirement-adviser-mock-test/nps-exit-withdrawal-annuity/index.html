NPS – Exit, Withdrawal & Annuity: NISM Series XVII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on NPS – Exit, Withdrawal & Annuity, from the NISM Series XVII (Retirement Adviser) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how money leaves NPS: normal exit at superannuation or the prescribed age, premature exit before that age, exit on death, partial withdrawals from Tier I for specified purposes, deferment of lump sum and annuity, systematic lump-sum withdrawal options and the requirement to use part of the corpus to buy an annuity. It also explains the choice of annuity service provider and annuity type. Since PFRDA has revised exit limits over time, focus on the structure — the annuity requirement, the small-corpus exception, the stricter treatment of premature exit and the documents and process for claims.

NPS – Exit, Withdrawal & Annuity questions

Question 1

Compared with normal exit at the retirement age, premature exit from NPS before that age generally requires:

  1. No annuity purchase at all
  2. The full corpus to be paid as lump sum
  3. A penalty equal to all employer contributions made since the account was opened
  4. A larger share of the corpus to be used for buying an annuity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A larger share of the corpus to be used for buying an annuity

NPS discourages early exit by requiring a larger proportion to be annuitised on premature exit than on normal exit, except where the corpus is below the small-corpus threshold.

Question 2

An NPS subscriber at exit has a very small accumulated corpus, below the threshold specified by PFRDA. Typically, she can:

  1. Only buy an annuity with the entire corpus
  2. Not exit until the corpus crosses the threshold
  3. Withdraw the entire corpus as a lump sum without buying an annuity
  4. Transfer the corpus to her EPF account
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Withdraw the entire corpus as a lump sum without buying an annuity

PFRDA allows full lump-sum withdrawal where the corpus is below a prescribed small-amount threshold, because an annuity on a tiny amount would be uneconomic.

Question 3

Partial withdrawal from NPS Tier I is allowed for specified purposes. Which is a permitted purpose?

  1. Treatment of a specified critical illness of the subscriber or family
  2. Repayment of an outstanding credit card bill or personal loan of the subscriber
  3. Purchase of shares in an IPO
  4. Foreign holiday travel
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Treatment of a specified critical illness of the subscriber or family

Partial withdrawals are allowed only for listed purposes such as children's education or marriage, house purchase or construction and treatment of specified illnesses. Consumption or investment needs do not qualify.

Question 4

Partial withdrawals from NPS Tier I are calculated with reference to:

  1. The subscriber's own contributions, not the employer's
  2. The entire corpus including employer contributions and returns
  3. Only the returns earned in the last financial year
  4. The annuity value expected at retirement
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The subscriber's own contributions, not the employer's

Partial withdrawal limits are a percentage of the subscriber's own contributions (excluding employer contributions), subject to minimum holding period and number of withdrawals.

Question 5

A subscriber at 60 wants to receive the lump-sum portion in instalments rather than at once. NPS permits this through:

  1. Conversion of Tier I into a fixed deposit with the trustee bank
  2. Transferring the lump sum to Tier II only
  3. Systematic withdrawal of the lump sum over a chosen period
  4. A loan against the NPS corpus
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Systematic withdrawal of the lump sum over a chosen period

NPS offers systematic lump-sum withdrawal, letting the subscriber draw the lump-sum portion periodically while the balance stays invested. NPS does not provide loans against the corpus.

Question 6

A subscriber wants the highest possible annuity income now but also wants his wife to continue receiving pension after his death. Which option balances these needs?

  1. Joint-life annuity without return of purchase price
  2. Single-life annuity with return of purchase price
  3. Single-life annuity without return of purchase price
  4. Annuity certain for five years only
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Joint-life annuity without return of purchase price

A joint-life annuity continues to the spouse; choosing it without return of purchase price keeps the payout higher than a return-of-purchase-price variant. A single-life annuity stops at his death.

Question 7

Which document is essential for the nominee to claim NPS benefits after the subscriber's death?

  1. The subscriber's latest income-tax return only
  2. The nominee's EPF passbook
  3. A no-objection certificate from the subscriber's employer in all cases
  4. The subscriber's death certificate along with the claim form and KYC
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The subscriber's death certificate along with the claim form and KYC

Death claims require a death certificate, the withdrawal form and the nominee's KYC and bank details. Income-tax returns and EPF passbooks are not required.

Question 8

A subscriber may defer buying the annuity at exit. The main advantage of deferment is that:

  1. The corpus becomes exempt from all market risk during the whole period of deferment
  2. The corpus remains invested and the annuity can be bought later, possibly at better rates
  3. It allows him to withdraw 100% in cash later
  4. Annuity rates are guaranteed to rise each year of deferment
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The corpus remains invested and the annuity can be bought later, possibly at better rates

Deferring the annuity keeps money invested and lets the subscriber choose a later time, perhaps when he needs income or rates are better. The corpus still carries market risk and rates are not guaranteed.
