Distribution Phase & Annuities: NISM Series XVII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Distribution Phase & Annuities, from the NISM Series XVII (Retirement Adviser) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter deals with converting a corpus into retirement income. It covers annuity types (immediate and deferred, life annuity, with or without return of purchase price, joint-life, increasing and guaranteed-period annuities), annuity pricing and the factors that affect annuity rates, withdrawal strategies such as fixed-percentage rules and systematic withdrawals, annuity laddering, and managing longevity and sequence risk while drawing down. Revise how to choose between annuity options for a given client situation and how to compute simple payouts and sustainable withdrawal amounts.

Distribution Phase & Annuities questions

Question 1

A retiree buys an annuity with ₹60 lakh at a rate of 6.8% a year payable monthly. The approximate monthly annuity before tax is:

  1. ₹40,800
  2. ₹34,000
  3. ₹28,333
  4. ₹3,40,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹34,000

Annual annuity = 60 lakh × 6.8% = ₹4,08,000. Monthly ≈ 4,08,000 ÷ 12 = ₹34,000. ₹40,800 confuses the annual figure's decimal place.

Question 2

A retiree has a corpus of ₹1.2 crore and withdraws ₹4.8 lakh in the first year. His initial withdrawal rate is:

  1. 2.5%
  2. 4%
  3. 5%
  4. 4.8%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 4%

Withdrawal rate = 4.8 lakh ÷ 120 lakh = 4%. 4.8% would treat ₹4.8 lakh as a percentage directly.

Question 3

Which factor generally leads to a HIGHER annuity payout for the same purchase price?

  1. Choosing the return-of-purchase-price option
  2. Adding a spouse under a joint-life option
  3. Choosing an annuity that increases every year
  4. Buying the annuity at an older age
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Buying the annuity at an older age

Older buyers have a shorter expected payout period, so insurers offer a higher rate. Return of purchase price, joint-life and increasing features all reduce the starting payout.

Question 4

Annuity laddering means:

  1. Buying an annuity whose payout increases each year
  2. Investing the monthly annuity income in equity funds to build a second corpus
  3. Buying several annuities at different times instead of one at retirement
  4. Switching annuity providers every year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Buying several annuities at different times instead of one at retirement

Laddering spreads annuity purchases over time, reducing the risk of locking the whole corpus at low rates and allowing higher rates at older ages.

Question 5

A key disadvantage of a life annuity without return of purchase price is that:

  1. Its payout is lower than all other annuity options
  2. It stops paying after a fixed term even if the annuitant is alive
  3. Its payouts change with stock market levels
  4. Nothing is left for heirs if the annuitant dies early
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Nothing is left for heirs if the annuitant dies early

This option gives the highest payout, but on death the purchase price is not returned, so heirs receive nothing. Payments continue for life and do not depend on markets.

Question 6

A retiree wants protection against inflation in her annuity income. Which annuity feature addresses this?

  1. A life annuity with return of purchase price
  2. An increasing annuity whose payout rises at a fixed rate each year
  3. An annuity certain for ten years
  4. A joint-life annuity with 50% to the spouse
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. An increasing annuity whose payout rises at a fixed rate each year

An increasing annuity raises payments periodically, offsetting part of inflation. The other options address legacy, guaranteed term or spouse protection, not inflation.

Question 7

Why might a retiree combine an annuity with a mutual fund SWP rather than putting the entire corpus into an annuity?

  1. Because annuities cannot be bought for more than ₹10 lakh
  2. Because SWPs guarantee a higher income than annuities for the rest of the retiree's life
  3. To get guaranteed lifelong income while keeping some growth and liquidity
  4. To avoid paying any tax on retirement income
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. To get guaranteed lifelong income while keeping some growth and liquidity

An annuity covers essential expenses for life, while an SWP portion offers growth potential and access to capital. Neither eliminates tax, and SWP income is not guaranteed.

Question 8

A deferred annuity is most suitable for a client who:

  1. Needs income starting next month
  2. Wants to withdraw the entire amount in cash at any time
  3. Is still working and wants pension income to start some years later
  4. Has no need for any regular income
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Is still working and wants pension income to start some years later

A deferred annuity accumulates during a deferment period and starts payouts later, suiting people who are still working. Immediate annuities begin almost at once.
