Distribution Phase & Annuities: NISM Series XVII revision notes

The distribution phase is about turning a corpus into reliable income for life. This chapter covers annuities in detail, systematic withdrawal plans from mutual funds, reverse mortgage and sustainable withdrawal rates. Questions include simple annuity income calculations, matching annuity types to needs, and comparing annuities with SWPs for flexibility, tax and longevity protection.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. What an annuity does

An annuity converts a lump sum into regular income, usually for life, from a life insurer. It transfers longevity risk to the insurer. ₹50 lakh at an annuity rate of 7% gives ₹3.5 lakh a year, or about ₹29,167 a month before tax.

2. Immediate and deferred

An immediate annuity starts paying soon after purchase, typically at retirement. A deferred annuity accumulates for some years before payments begin. NPS annuities on exit are immediate annuities purchased from empanelled insurers.

3. Annuity options

Life annuity (highest income, stops on death), life annuity with return of purchase price (lower income, corpus returned to nominee), joint life last survivor (continues to spouse), increasing annuity (rises by a fixed rate each year), and annuity certain for a fixed period then life. More features mean lower income.

4. Choosing an option

A married client wanting to protect a spouse should consider joint life last survivor. A client wanting to leave money to children may choose return of purchase price. A client with no dependants and high longevity concern may prefer plain life annuity for maximum income.

5. Limitations of annuities

Annuity rates are fixed at purchase and do not rise with inflation unless an increasing option is chosen. The corpus is usually irrevocable. Annuity income is fully taxable as income. Many advisers suggest annuitising only part of the corpus.

6. Systematic withdrawal plans

An SWP redeems a fixed amount from a mutual fund at regular intervals. Only the gains portion of each redemption is taxed, which can make post-tax income higher than an annuity. The corpus stays invested and accessible, but there is no guarantee and it can run out if returns are poor.

7. Withdrawal rates

A sustainable withdrawal rate is the percentage of the initial corpus that can be withdrawn each year, adjusted for inflation, without exhausting it. The US '4% rule' is often cited; with higher Indian inflation, advisers often use more conservative rates or flexible withdrawals.

8. Reverse mortgage

Senior citizens owning a house can borrow against it from banks and receive periodic payments while continuing to live there. The loan is repaid from sale of the house after death or by heirs. Payments received under an approved reverse mortgage are not taxable as income.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Annual annuity income = purchase price x annuity rate; monthly = annual / 12.
  • Withdrawal rate = first-year withdrawal / initial corpus.

Common traps in the exam

  • Return of purchase price gives lower income than plain life annuity because the capital is returned.
  • Annuity income is taxable; only the gains portion of an SWP redemption is taxable.
  • Annuities transfer longevity risk; SWPs do not.
  • Reverse mortgage payments are loan receipts, not taxable income.

Quick recap

Annuities turn a lump sum into lifelong income from insurers: ₹50 lakh at 7% gives about ₹29,167 a month. Options: life, return of purchase price, joint life last survivor, increasing. More features mean less income. Annuity income is fully taxable; SWPs are taxed only on gains but carry market and longevity risk. Reverse mortgage unlocks home value tax-free.
