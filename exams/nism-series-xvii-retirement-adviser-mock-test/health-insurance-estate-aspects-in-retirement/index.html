Health, Insurance & Estate Aspects in Retirement: NISM Series XVII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Health, Insurance & Estate Aspects in Retirement, from the NISM Series XVII (Retirement Adviser) syllabus.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers non-investment risks in retirement: health costs, insurance needs and estate planning. Topics include individual and senior-citizen health policies, top-up and super top-up covers, waiting periods for pre-existing diseases, government health schemes, the declining need for life cover after retirement, long-term care, reverse mortgage, nominations versus wills, succession laws, probate and trusts. When revising, focus on matching health cover to the retiree's situation, understanding how nominees and legal heirs differ, and the steps a retiree should take to ensure a smooth transfer of assets.

Health, Insurance & Estate Aspects in Retirement questions

Question 1

A retiree has a base health policy of ₹5 lakh. A super top-up policy will:

  1. Replace the base policy entirely
  2. Pay a fixed daily cash amount regardless of bills
  3. Pay hospital bills above a deductible once the aggregate claims in a year exceed it
  4. Cover only outpatient consultations
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Pay hospital bills above a deductible once the aggregate claims in a year exceed it

A super top-up pays when total claims in a policy year cross the deductible, making it a cost-effective way to raise cover above the base policy. Hospital cash plans pay fixed daily amounts.

Question 2

Under a will, a person's mutual fund units are left to his daughter, but his son is the registered nominee. Generally, the nominee:

  1. Becomes the absolute owner, overriding the will
  2. Cannot receive the units until the will is challenged
  3. Receives the units from the fund but holds them for the legal heirs as per the will
  4. Must sell the units and pay the proceeds to the government
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Receives the units from the fund but holds them for the legal heirs as per the will

In most financial assets, the nominee is a custodian who receives the asset for the benefit of the legal heirs or beneficiaries under the will. Nomination does not override succession.

Question 3

Why does a retiree's need for life insurance usually decline after retirement?

  1. Dependants are fewer and liabilities such as home loans are usually paid off
  2. Life insurance is not available after 60
  3. Pension income is fully exempt from tax
  4. Health insurance replaces life insurance automatically once a person turns sixty
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Dependants are fewer and liabilities such as home loans are usually paid off

Life insurance replaces income for dependants. In retirement, children are typically independent and loans repaid, reducing the need. Some cover may still be useful for a spouse or estate purposes.

Question 4

A reverse mortgage is most suitable for which retiree?

  1. A tenant looking for a cheaper rental
  2. A homeowner with low income who wishes to stay in the house
  3. A retiree wanting to buy a second home
  4. A young professional saving for a down payment
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A homeowner with low income who wishes to stay in the house

A reverse mortgage lets senior homeowners convert home equity into income while continuing to live in the house. It needs a self-owned residential property.

Question 5

Which step most reduces disputes over a retiree's estate among his children?

  1. Holding all assets in cash at home
  2. Avoiding nominations to prevent confusion
  3. Making a clear, properly witnessed will and keeping it accessible
  4. Transferring everything to one child informally and asking him to share it fairly
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Making a clear, properly witnessed will and keeping it accessible

A clear and valid will, executed with witnesses and known to the executor, minimises disputes. Informal transfers or missing nominations tend to create conflict and delays.

Question 6

A retiree buying a new health policy at 62 should pay special attention to:

  1. The insurer's share price
  2. Whether premiums are deductible under the new tax regime
  3. Waiting periods for pre-existing diseases and co-payment clauses
  4. The policy's maturity benefit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Waiting periods for pre-existing diseases and co-payment clauses

Senior citizen policies often have waiting periods, co-payment and sub-limits that affect claims. Health policies do not pay maturity benefits, and premium deduction is an old-regime benefit.

Question 7

A retired couple wants to ensure that if one becomes mentally incapacitated, the other can manage bank accounts and investments. A useful legal tool is:

  1. A durable power of attorney
  2. A gift deed
  3. A nomination form
  4. A rent agreement
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A durable power of attorney

A power of attorney authorises another person to act on one's behalf. A nomination works only after death, while a gift deed transfers ownership outright.

Question 8

Why might a retiree with a disabled child consider setting up a private trust?

  1. To avoid all income tax permanently on the family's assets and on the child's income
  2. Because wills are not valid for parents of disabled children
  3. To manage assets for the child's lifetime through a trustee after the parents' death
  4. To transfer assets without any documentation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. To manage assets for the child's lifetime through a trustee after the parents' death

A trust lets a trustee manage and use assets for the child's benefit over the long term. It does not remove all taxes, and wills remain valid; a trust simply provides ongoing management.
