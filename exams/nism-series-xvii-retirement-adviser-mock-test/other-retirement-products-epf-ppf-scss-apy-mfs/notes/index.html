Other Retirement Products (EPF, PPF, SCSS, APY, MFs): NISM Series XVII revision notes

Besides NPS, Indian retirement planning uses employer schemes, small savings schemes, social security schemes and mutual funds. This chapter compares EPF and EPS, PPF, the Senior Citizens' Savings Scheme, Atal Pension Yojana and retirement-oriented mutual funds. Questions test contribution splits, lock-ins, eligibility ages and tax status. Rates on small savings change quarterly.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. EPF and EPS

Under the EPF scheme, the employee contributes 12% of basic pay plus DA. Of the employer's 12%, 8.33% (on wages up to the ceiling of ₹15,000 a month, so up to ₹1,250) goes to the Employees' Pension Scheme and the rest to EPF. EPFO declares the EPF interest rate each year.

2. EPS pension

EPS is a defined benefit pension: monthly pension = pensionable salary x pensionable service / 70, payable from 58 with at least 10 years of service. Example: pensionable salary ₹15,000 and 30 years' service gives ₹15,000 x 30 / 70 = about ₹6,429 a month.

3. EPF withdrawals and tax

EPF can be withdrawn on retirement or after two months of unemployment, with partial withdrawals for housing, illness and education. Withdrawal is tax free after five years of continuous service. Interest on employee contributions above ₹2.5 lakh a year is taxable.

4. PPF

PPF has a 15-year lock-in, extendable in blocks of five years, a minimum deposit of ₹500 and maximum of ₹1.5 lakh a year. It is EEE (exempt contribution under 80C, exempt interest, exempt maturity). Loans are allowed from the third to sixth year and partial withdrawals from the seventh year.

5. SCSS

The Senior Citizens' Savings Scheme is open to those aged 60 and above (with relaxations for certain retirees), with a five-year tenure extendable by three years and a maximum investment of ₹30 lakh. Interest is paid quarterly and is taxable; the deposit qualifies for 80C. Rate is set quarterly by government.

6. Atal Pension Yojana

APY is a government-backed pension for unorganised-sector workers aged 18 to 40, giving a guaranteed monthly pension of ₹1,000 to ₹5,000 from 60, depending on contribution and joining age. On death, the spouse gets the same pension and the nominee gets the corpus. Income taxpayers are not eligible to join since October 2022.

7. Mutual funds for retirement

Retirement solution-oriented funds have a five-year lock-in or until retirement, whichever is earlier. Diversified equity funds suit long accumulation through SIPs; hybrid and debt funds suit the transition and distribution phases. SWPs give regular income with tax only on the gains portion.

8. Comparing products

EPF, PPF and SCSS offer government-backed safety but limited flexibility; NPS and mutual funds offer market-linked growth with low cost; APY gives a guaranteed small pension. A combination usually works better than any single product.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • EPS monthly pension = pensionable salary x pensionable service / 70.
  • Employer EPS share = 8.33% x min(wage, ₹15,000).

Common traps in the exam

  • Of the employer's 12%, only 8.33% (capped at ₹1,250 a month) goes to EPS; the rest goes to EPF.
  • SCSS interest is taxable even though the deposit qualifies for 80C.
  • APY entry age is 18 to 40, not up to 60; the pension starts at 60.
  • PPF partial withdrawal starts in the seventh year; loans are in years three to six.

Quick recap

EPF: 12% plus 12%, with 8.33% of employer share (up to ₹1,250) to EPS; EPS pension = salary x service / 70. PPF: 15 years, ₹1.5 lakh cap, EEE. SCSS: 60+, ₹30 lakh max, five years, quarterly taxable interest. APY: join 18 to 40, guaranteed ₹1,000 to ₹5,000 from 60, spouse continues. Mutual funds: SIPs to accumulate, SWPs to draw.
