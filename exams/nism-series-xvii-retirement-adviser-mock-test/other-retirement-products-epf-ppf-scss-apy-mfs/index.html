Other Retirement Products (EPF, PPF, SCSS, APY, MFs): NISM Series XVII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Other Retirement Products (EPF, PPF, SCSS, APY, MFs), from the NISM Series XVII (Retirement Adviser) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers retirement products other than NPS: Employees' Provident Fund and Employees' Pension Scheme, Voluntary Provident Fund, Public Provident Fund, Senior Citizens' Savings Scheme, Atal Pension Yojana, post office schemes, and mutual fund options such as retirement funds, balanced advantage funds and systematic withdrawal plans. Learners should know each product's eligibility, tenure, liquidity, risk and tax treatment, and match products to client needs. Interest rates on small savings are revised quarterly, so focus on features and suitability rather than current rates.

Other Retirement Products (EPF, PPF, SCSS, APY, MFs) questions

Question 1

The monthly pension under the Employees' Pension Scheme is calculated as pensionable salary × pensionable service ÷ 70. For pensionable salary of ₹15,000 and 35 years of service, the pension is:

  1. ₹5,250
  2. ₹15,000
  3. ₹10,500
  4. ₹7,500
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹7,500

Pension = 15,000 × 35 ÷ 70 = ₹7,500 per month. ₹5,250 would result from dividing by 100 instead of 70.

Question 2

Which of the following can a PPF subscriber do after the account completes its initial tenure?

  1. Convert it into an NPS Tier I account
  2. Transfer it to the employer for EPF credit
  3. Withdraw only the interest and leave the principal locked forever
  4. Extend it in blocks, with or without further contributions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Extend it in blocks, with or without further contributions

On maturity, a PPF account can be extended in blocks with or without fresh contributions, or closed. It cannot be converted to NPS or EPF.

Question 3

Who among the following is eligible to open a Senior Citizens' Savings Scheme account before 60, subject to conditions?

  1. Any salaried employee aged 45 or above
  2. Any NRI aged 55 or above
  3. A minor whose parent is a senior citizen
  4. A person who retired under a voluntary retirement scheme and meets the age and timing conditions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A person who retired under a voluntary retirement scheme and meets the age and timing conditions

SCSS is primarily for resident individuals aged 60 or more, but certain retirees, such as those on VRS or superannuation within the specified age band, may join earlier if they invest within the prescribed time of receiving benefits. NRIs cannot open SCSS.

Question 4

Interest on a Senior Citizens' Savings Scheme account is paid:

  1. Only at maturity
  2. Monthly
  3. Half-yearly in arrears after the third year
  4. Quarterly
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Quarterly

SCSS pays interest every quarter, making it suitable for regular income. It is not cumulative and does not pay monthly.

Question 5

Which person is NOT eligible to join Atal Pension Yojana under the current rules?

  1. A 25-year-old domestic worker with a savings bank account
  2. An income-tax payer aged 30
  3. A 35-year-old farm labourer
  4. A 19-year-old street vendor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. An income-tax payer aged 30

Since October 2022, income-tax payers are not eligible to join APY. Other citizens within the entry age band can join through a bank or post office savings account.

Question 6

A retiree invests in a mutual fund and sets up a systematic withdrawal plan. Compared with a bank fixed deposit giving monthly interest, the SWP:

  1. Guarantees a fixed return like a deposit
  2. Is fully exempt from tax in all cases, irrespective of holding period or the fund type
  3. Requires a lock-in of five years
  4. Can be more tax-efficient but its value fluctuates with the market
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Can be more tax-efficient but its value fluctuates with the market

SWP withdrawals are partly capital redemption, so only gains are taxed, making them potentially tax-efficient. However, the underlying units carry market risk, unlike a fixed deposit.

Question 7

Why might a retiree prefer SCSS to an ordinary bank fixed deposit for part of his corpus?

  1. It has no upper limit on investment
  2. It allows withdrawals at any time without any deduction or penalty on the interest
  3. It is government-backed and usually offers a higher rate than regular deposits
  4. Its interest is exempt from income tax
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. It is government-backed and usually offers a higher rate than regular deposits

SCSS carries sovereign backing and its rate is typically higher than standard bank deposit rates. It has an investment ceiling, premature closure attracts a deduction, and interest is taxable.

Question 8

An employee voluntarily contributes more than the mandatory 12% to EPF. This additional contribution is called:

  1. Employees' Deposit Linked Insurance
  2. Employer's matching contribution
  3. Pension Fund Tier II
  4. Voluntary Provident Fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Voluntary Provident Fund

Contributions above the mandatory rate go to the Voluntary Provident Fund, which earns the EPF rate. EDLI is a life insurance scheme, and the employer is not required to match VPF.
