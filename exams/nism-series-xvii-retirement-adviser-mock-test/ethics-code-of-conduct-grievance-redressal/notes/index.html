Ethics, Code of Conduct & Grievance Redressal: NISM Series XVII revision notes

Retirement advice affects a client's income for decades, so NISM XVII ends with ethics and grievance handling. The chapter covers the conduct expected of PFRDA-registered retirement advisers, suitability and disclosure duties, conflicts of interest, and the grievance redressal system for NPS subscribers. Questions are mostly scenarios: identify the ethical action or the right grievance route.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Registration and certification

Retirement advisers are registered with PFRDA under its Retirement Adviser Regulations and must pass NISM Series XVII. They must renew certification as required and display registration details in communications with clients.

2. Fiduciary approach

An adviser must place the client's interest first. Recommending a product because it pays a higher commission, when a cheaper suitable option exists, breaches this duty. Advice should be based on the client's goals, risk profile, tax position and existing assets.

3. Know your client and suitability

Collect full information on income, expenses, liabilities, dependants, health and risk tolerance. A 58-year-old with no other pension who wants to move 90% of the corpus into small-cap funds should be counselled on risk; suitability documentation protects both sides.

4. Disclosure

Advisers must disclose fees, commissions, conflicts of interest and the limits of their services in writing. They must explain product features, risks, charges, lock-ins and exit rules clearly, especially NPS annuity requirements and withdrawal restrictions.

5. Prohibited conduct

Advisers must not promise assured returns, misrepresent product features, pressure clients, handle client money in cash beyond permitted modes, or share client data without consent. Advertising must be fair and not misleading.

6. NPS grievance channels

NPS subscribers can lodge grievances through the CRA's Central Grievance Management System (CGMS) online, by phone or through the POP. The CRA routes complaints to the relevant entity: POP, pension fund, nodal office or annuity service provider.

7. Escalation

If the grievance is not resolved within the prescribed time or the subscriber is unhappy, it can be escalated to the NPS Trust and then to the ombudsman appointed under PFRDA's grievance redressal regulations. Annuity complaints against insurers may also go to the insurance ombudsman.

8. Record keeping

Advisers should keep records of client information, risk profiles, advice and the reasons for it, disclosures and communications. Good records help resolve disputes and demonstrate compliance during PFRDA inspections.

Common traps in the exam

  • NPS grievances are lodged through the CRA's CGMS, not directly with PFRDA as a first step.
  • A client's insistence does not remove the adviser's duty to warn about unsuitable choices.
  • Promising a fixed return on NPS is prohibited because returns are market linked.
  • Commission and fee disclosure must be in writing, before the advice is acted on.

Quick recap

Retirement advisers register with PFRDA and hold NISM XVII. Put the client first, know the client, give suitable advice, disclose fees and conflicts in writing, never promise assured returns or mislead. NPS grievances: CGMS via CRA (or POP), then NPS Trust, then ombudsman. Keep records of advice and rationale for inspections and disputes.
