Ethics, Code of Conduct & Grievance Redressal: NISM Series XVII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Ethics, Code of Conduct & Grievance Redressal, from the NISM Series XVII (Retirement Adviser) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the conduct expected of retirement advisers and NPS intermediaries. Topics include fiduciary duty, suitability and know-your-client, disclosure of conflicts and remuneration, confidentiality, avoiding mis-selling and misleading claims, record keeping, and the code of conduct under PFRDA's regulations. It also covers grievance redressal for NPS subscribers — the Central Grievance Management System, escalation to the NPS Trust and PFRDA, and the ombudsman mechanism. Revise how to handle ethical dilemmas in scenario questions and the order in which a subscriber can escalate a complaint.

Ethics, Code of Conduct & Grievance Redressal questions

Question 1

An adviser recommends a product mainly because it pays her a higher commission, though a cheaper suitable product exists. This violates the principle of:

  1. Acting in the client's best interest
  2. Maintaining confidentiality
  3. Record keeping
  4. Continuing professional education
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Acting in the client's best interest

Advisers must put the client's interest first. Choosing a product for higher commission when a more suitable option exists breaches this duty, even if the product is not wholly unsuitable.

Question 2

An NPS subscriber's grievance has not been resolved satisfactorily by the intermediary within the specified time. A further escalation option available under PFRDA's framework is:

  1. Filing a complaint with the EPFO
  2. Approaching the NPS Trust and then the ombudsman appointed under PFRDA regulations
  3. Approaching the Insurance Ombudsman for all NPS matters, including those involving POPs
  4. Escalating to the SEBI SCORES portal
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Approaching the NPS Trust and then the ombudsman appointed under PFRDA regulations

NPS grievances are lodged through CGMS; unresolved complaints can be escalated to the NPS Trust and then to the ombudsman under PFRDA regulations. EPFO and SEBI do not handle NPS grievances.

Question 3

Before recommending an asset allocation, the adviser must first:

  1. Understand the client's goals, finances and risk profile
  2. Select the products with the best past returns
  3. Ask the client to sign a waiver of liability
  4. Fix the client's retirement age at 60 regardless of preference
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Understand the client's goals, finances and risk profile

Suitability requires understanding the client's needs, circumstances and risk tolerance before recommending. Past returns alone are not a basis for advice.

Question 4

An adviser tells a 59-year-old client that an equity fund 'will surely give 15% every year'. This is:

  1. Acceptable since equity has historically delivered high returns
  2. Allowed if the client is a senior citizen
  3. A misleading assurance of returns that must be avoided
  4. Permitted if mentioned only verbally
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A misleading assurance of returns that must be avoided

Market-linked returns cannot be guaranteed. Promising assured returns is misleading and breaches the code of conduct regardless of the mode of communication.

Question 5

Why must a retirement adviser keep records of advice given and the client's risk profile?

  1. To share with other clients as case studies
  2. To demonstrate that recommendations were suitable if questioned later
  3. Because records allow the adviser to justify charging higher fees to long-standing clients
  4. Records are optional if the client trusts the adviser
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. To demonstrate that recommendations were suitable if questioned later

Records provide evidence of the basis for advice and are required by regulation. They must be kept confidential and are not optional.

Question 6

A client asks the adviser to backdate a document to qualify for a tax benefit. The adviser should:

  1. Agree if the client is a long-standing customer
  2. Agree if the amount involved is small
  3. Forward the request to the CRA for approval
  4. Refuse, as falsifying documents is unethical and illegal
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Refuse, as falsifying documents is unethical and illegal

Falsifying records is fraudulent and breaches the code of conduct and law. No relationship or amount justifies it.

Question 7

An adviser receives a referral fee from an annuity provider whenever clients buy its annuities. She should:

  1. Keep it confidential to avoid confusing clients
  2. Disclose the arrangement to clients before they decide
  3. Recommend only that provider's annuities to maximise income
  4. Refund the fee to the provider
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Disclose the arrangement to clients before they decide

Material conflicts of interest, including referral fees, must be disclosed so clients can judge the advice. Disclosure does not remove the duty to recommend what is suitable.

Question 8

Which online system lets an NPS subscriber lodge and track grievances?

  1. SEBI SCORES
  2. IRDAI Bima Bharosa
  3. The Central Grievance Management System of the CRAs
  4. RBI Integrated Ombudsman portal for complaints against banks acting as POPs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The Central Grievance Management System of the CRAs

NPS grievances are registered and tracked through CGMS hosted by the CRAs. SCORES, Bima Bharosa and RBI's portal cover securities, insurance and banking complaints respectively.
