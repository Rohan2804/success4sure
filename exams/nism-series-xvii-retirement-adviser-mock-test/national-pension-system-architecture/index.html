National Pension System – Architecture: NISM Series XVII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on National Pension System – Architecture, from the NISM Series XVII (Retirement Adviser) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how NPS is organised for different groups of subscribers: the Government sector model for Central and State Government employees, the corporate model, the all-citizen model and NPS Vatsalya for minors. It covers Tier I and Tier II accounts, the PRAN, KYC and onboarding through POPs and eNPS, contribution rules, portability across jobs and locations, and the flow of contributions through the trustee bank to pension funds. Focus on who can join under which model, how Tier I and Tier II differ in withdrawal and tax treatment, and the role of each intermediary in the contribution cycle.

National Pension System – Architecture questions

Question 1

A subscriber changes jobs from a private company to a different city. What happens to his NPS account?

  1. He must close the account and open a new one with a fresh PRAN
  2. His corpus is transferred compulsorily to EPF
  3. The account is frozen until he reaches 60
  4. The same PRAN continues; only the employer or POP mapping changes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The same PRAN continues; only the employer or POP mapping changes

NPS is portable: the PRAN stays the same across jobs and locations, and only the associated employer or POP changes. No closure or transfer to EPF is required.

Question 2

Which statement about the NPS Tier II account is correct?

  1. It is a voluntary account with flexible withdrawals and no lock-in
  2. It has the same withdrawal restrictions as Tier I
  3. It can be opened without holding a Tier I account
  4. Its corpus must be used to buy an annuity at 60
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. It is a voluntary account with flexible withdrawals and no lock-in

Tier II is a voluntary savings facility with free withdrawals. It requires an active Tier I account and has no annuity requirement. Tier I is the restricted pension account.

Question 3

Rahul, a salaried employee, wants to open NPS online without visiting any branch. Which channel can he use?

  1. Only a post office savings bank counter
  2. eNPS, using Aadhaar or PAN-based online KYC
  3. Only through his employer's HR department
  4. A registered mutual fund distributor's offline form
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. eNPS, using Aadhaar or PAN-based online KYC

eNPS allows individuals to open and fund NPS accounts online using Aadhaar or PAN-based verification. Post offices and employers are other channels but not the only ones.

Question 4

NPS Vatsalya accounts are opened for:

  1. Senior citizens above 70 who cannot join regular NPS
  2. Minors, with contributions made by a parent or guardian
  3. Non-resident Indians only
  4. Government employees covered by the Unified Pension Scheme
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Minors, with contributions made by a parent or guardian

NPS Vatsalya allows parents or guardians to save for a minor's pension. It is operated by the guardian until the child reaches majority.

Question 5

Under the corporate model of NPS, which of the following is true?

  1. Employer contributions can be made in addition to the employee's own contributions
  2. Only the employer can contribute; employees cannot add anything
  3. The employer chooses the pension fund and employees can never change it
  4. Employees lose their PRAN on leaving the company
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Employer contributions can be made in addition to the employee's own contributions

In the corporate model, both the employer and the employee may contribute. Subscribers retain their PRAN when they leave, and choices of fund and allocation can be offered to employees.

Question 6

Under the all-citizen model, an NPS subscriber's contribution passes through which sequence?

  1. Pension fund → POP → trustee bank
  2. Custodian → CRA → POP
  3. POP → trustee bank → pension fund
  4. Annuity service provider → trustee bank → POP
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. POP → trustee bank → pension fund

The subscriber contributes through a POP, which remits money to the trustee bank; after reconciliation with the CRA's records, funds go to the chosen pension fund for investment.

Question 7

Which of the following is a function of the Central Recordkeeping Agency for an NPS subscriber?

  1. Selecting stocks for the subscriber's equity scheme
  2. Issuing the PRAN and maintaining records of contributions and units
  3. Paying the monthly annuity after exit
  4. Holding the securities bought by pension funds and settling their trades with exchanges
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Issuing the PRAN and maintaining records of contributions and units

The CRA issues PRANs, maintains records of contributions, switches and units, and provides statements. Stock selection is done by pension funds, annuities by ASPs and custody by the custodian.

Question 8

Which individual is NOT eligible to open an NPS account under the all-citizen model?

  1. A citizen aged 75 seeking to open a new account
  2. A citizen aged 19 with a savings bank account
  3. An NRI who is a citizen of India, subject to applicable rules
  4. A self-employed citizen aged 45
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A citizen aged 75 seeking to open a new account

The all-citizen model is open to Indian citizens within the prescribed entry age band, which is far below 75. NRIs who are citizens may join subject to FEMA and NPS rules.
