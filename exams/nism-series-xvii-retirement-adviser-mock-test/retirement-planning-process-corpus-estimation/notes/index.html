Retirement Planning Process & Corpus Estimation: NISM Series XVII revision notes

This is the most calculation-heavy chapter of NISM XVII and carries many marks. It walks through the planning process and the maths of estimating the corpus needed at retirement, then working out how much more must be saved. Practise inflating expenses, computing the real rate, valuing a growing annuity and finding the shortfall until each step takes under two minutes.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. The planning process

Gather data (age, income, expenses, assets, liabilities, goals, health), set the retirement age and life expectancy, estimate expenses at retirement, compute the corpus required, value existing savings at retirement, find the shortfall, design a savings and investment plan, implement it and review at least yearly.

2. Expenses at retirement

Start with today's annual expenses that will continue in retirement, adjust for items that will fall or rise, and inflate to the retirement year. ₹6 lakh a year today at 6% inflation becomes about ₹6 lakh x (1.06)^15 = ₹14.4 lakh in 15 years.

3. Real rate of return

During retirement the corpus earns a return while withdrawals grow with inflation. The inflation-adjusted (real) rate is (1 + return)/(1 + inflation) - 1, not simply return minus inflation. With 8% return and 6% inflation, real rate = 1.08/1.06 - 1 = 1.89%.

4. Corpus as growing annuity

The corpus needed is the present value, at retirement, of withdrawals that grow with inflation for the retirement period, discounted at the real rate. For first-year need of ₹8 lakh, 20 years, withdrawals at the start of each year, at a real rate of 1.89%, the corpus is about ₹8 lakh x 16.84 = ₹1.35 crore.

5. Value of existing savings

Project current retirement investments to the retirement date at their expected return. ₹20 lakh growing at 9% for 20 years becomes 20 x (1.09)^20 = about ₹1.12 crore. Include EPF, PPF, NPS and dedicated mutual funds; exclude assets earmarked for other goals.

6. Shortfall and savings required

Shortfall = corpus required - projected value of existing savings. If ₹2.5 crore is needed and existing savings will grow to ₹1.12 crore, the gap is ₹1.38 crore. At 9% for 20 years, the annual saving needed is 1.38 crore / 51.16 = about ₹2.7 lakh a year, or a monthly SIP computed similarly.

7. Assumptions drive results

Small changes in inflation, return or life expectancy change the corpus a lot. Use conservative returns, realistic inflation (including higher medical inflation) and a long life expectancy. Show clients a range of outcomes rather than one precise number.

8. Review and adjust

Plans must be reviewed annually and after life events such as job change, inheritance or illness. Step-up SIPs that increase contributions with salary can close the gap faster than a flat SIP.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Expense at retirement = current expense x (1 + i)^n.
  • Real rate = (1 + r)/(1 + i) - 1.
  • Corpus (withdrawals at start of year) = E1 x [1 - (1 + real)^-N] / real x (1 + real).
  • FV of existing savings = PV x (1 + r)^n; annual saving = shortfall x r / [(1 + r)^n - 1].

Common traps in the exam

  • Real rate is (1+r)/(1+i) - 1, not r - i; the difference matters in exam options.
  • Withdrawals at the beginning of the year need an annuity-due factor, which gives a larger corpus.
  • Use the expense at retirement, not today's expense, as the first withdrawal.
  • Existing savings must be grown to the retirement date before subtracting from the corpus.

Quick recap

Process: gather data, estimate expenses at retirement, compute corpus, project existing savings, find shortfall, plan savings, review. Inflate expenses with (1+i)^n. Real rate = (1+r)/(1+i) - 1. Corpus = PV of inflation-growing withdrawals at the real rate. Shortfall = corpus minus future value of existing savings; divide by the FV annuity factor to get yearly saving.
