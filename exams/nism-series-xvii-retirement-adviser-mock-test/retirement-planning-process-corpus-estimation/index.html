Retirement Planning Process & Corpus Estimation: NISM Series XVII practice questions

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the step-by-step retirement planning process and the calculations behind it. Steps include gathering client data, estimating current and future expenses, projecting them with inflation to the retirement date, choosing assumptions for return, inflation and life expectancy, computing the corpus needed using present-value-of-annuity methods, valuing existing assets, identifying the shortfall and determining the saving required. Revise time-value-of-money calculations — future value, present value, annuities and growing annuities — and the effect of real rates of return. Always state assumptions and review the plan periodically.

Retirement Planning Process & Corpus Estimation questions

Question 1

A client's current monthly household expenses are ₹50,000. If inflation is 7% a year, what will these expenses be in 18 years (approximately)?

  1. ₹1.13 lakh
  2. ₹1.28 lakh
  3. ₹2.10 lakh
  4. ₹1.69 lakh
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹1.69 lakh

Future expense = 50,000 × 1.07^18 ≈ 50,000 × 3.38 ≈ ₹1.69 lakh a month. ₹1.13 lakh would result from simple rather than compound inflation.

Question 2

A client wants ₹1 crore in 20 years and expects 10% a year compounded monthly. How much must he invest at the end of each month (approximately)?

  1. ₹41,667
  2. ₹13,169
  3. ₹9,650
  4. ₹16,700
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹13,169

Using the future value of an annuity formula with r = 10%/12 and n = 240, the monthly SIP ≈ ₹13,169. ₹41,667 simply divides ₹1 crore by 240, ignoring returns.

Question 3

A client has ₹25 lakh in retirement savings that will grow at 7% a year for 10 years until he retires. What will this amount to at retirement (approximately)?

  1. ₹42.5 lakh
  2. ₹35.0 lakh
  3. ₹49.2 lakh
  4. ₹57.6 lakh
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹49.2 lakh

FV = 25 × 1.07^10 ≈ 25 × 1.967 ≈ ₹49.2 lakh. ₹42.5 lakh is simple interest (25 + 25 × 7% × 10).

Question 4

A retiree needs ₹6 lakh a year in today's terms for 25 years, withdrawn at the start of each year. If the real rate of return (after inflation) is 1.5%, the corpus needed is about:

  1. ₹1.50 crore
  2. ₹96 lakh
  3. ₹1.26 crore
  4. ₹1.08 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹1.26 crore

PV of an annuity due of ₹6 lakh for 25 years at 1.5% ≈ ₹126.2 lakh, i.e. about ₹1.26 crore. ₹1.50 crore ignores returns entirely (6 × 25).

Question 5

A client needs ₹2 crore in 15 years. What amount must be saved at the end of each year if investments earn 10% a year (approximately)?

  1. ₹13.33 lakh
  2. ₹4.80 lakh
  3. ₹8.10 lakh
  4. ₹6.29 lakh
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹6.29 lakh

Annual saving = 2 crore × 0.10 ÷ (1.10^15 − 1) ≈ ₹6.29 lakh. ₹13.33 lakh divides the target by 15 years and ignores compounding.

Question 6

Why is the real rate of return used when computing the corpus for an inflation-indexed retirement income?

  1. It allows payments in today's terms to be discounted while allowing for rising expenses
  2. It always gives a higher corpus than nominal calculations do
  3. It removes the need to estimate life expectancy, since real returns adjust for longevity
  4. Because inflation does not affect expenses after retirement
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. It allows payments in today's terms to be discounted while allowing for rising expenses

Using the real rate converts a growing stream into constant real payments, simplifying the calculation of a corpus that must fund inflation-linked withdrawals. Life expectancy is still needed.

Question 7

A plan was prepared five years ago assuming 6% inflation. Actual inflation has averaged 8%. The adviser should:

  1. Keep the plan unchanged because it was prepared by a professional
  2. Review the assumptions and revise the corpus and saving targets
  3. Reduce the client's equity allocation to zero
  4. Advise the client to stop contributing until inflation falls
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Review the assumptions and revise the corpus and saving targets

Retirement plans need periodic review. Higher actual inflation increases future expenses, so assumptions and saving targets should be revised. Abandoning equity or stopping savings would worsen the gap.

Question 8

Which assumption, if overestimated, will most likely cause a retirement corpus to fall short?

  1. The expected rate of return on investments
  2. The client's life expectancy
  3. The rate of inflation
  4. The amount of expenses in retirement
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The expected rate of return on investments

An overly optimistic return assumption means too little is saved. Overestimating life expectancy, inflation or expenses would instead make the plan more conservative.
