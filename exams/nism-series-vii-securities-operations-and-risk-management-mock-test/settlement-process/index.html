Settlement Process: NISM Series VII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Settlement Process, from the NISM Series VII (Securities Operations and Risk Management) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers settlement of trades under the T+1 cycle (and the optional T+0 framework): pay-in and pay-out timelines, direct pay-out to clients, handling of short deliveries through auction and close-out, bad deliveries, settlement holidays, and how corporate actions affect open positions. Practise working out settlement dates around weekends and holidays, and understand who bears the cost of a failed delivery.

Settlement Process questions

Question 1

A trade is executed on Wednesday. Thursday is a settlement holiday. Under T+1, settlement happens on:

  1. Thursday
  2. Wednesday
  3. Friday
  4. Monday
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Friday

T+1 counts working days for settlement. With Thursday a settlement holiday, the next working day, Friday, is the settlement day.

Question 2

If a security cannot be bought in the auction after a short delivery, the trade is:

  1. Closed out at a prescribed price
  2. Cancelled with no payment to either party
  3. Kept open indefinitely
  4. Settled by SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Closed out at a prescribed price

When an auction fails, the buyer is compensated through close-out at a price set by the clearing corporation's rules, usually higher than the trade price.

Question 3

The cost of an auction resulting from a seller's short delivery is borne by:

  1. The buyer
  2. The defaulting seller
  3. The exchange
  4. SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The defaulting seller

The seller who failed to deliver bears the auction price difference and penalties, discouraging short sales without delivery in the cash segment.

Question 4

Under the optional T+0 settlement cycle, trades are settled:

  1. One day later
  2. Two days later
  3. At month end
  4. On the same day
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. On the same day

SEBI's optional T+0 (beta) framework lets a limited set of stocks settle on the trade day itself, alongside the regular T+1 cycle.

Question 5

A client sells shares on Monday under T+1. The broker transfers the sale proceeds to the client:

  1. After pay-out on Tuesday
  2. Before the sale on Monday
  3. Next month
  4. Only on request after a year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. After pay-out on Tuesday

Funds pay-out happens on settlement day (Tuesday); the broker then credits the client, subject to SEBI's timelines for releasing client funds.

Question 6

Funds pay-in for a clearing member must be made:

  1. Any time within a week
  2. By the settlement-day pay-in time
  3. Only after pay-out
  4. On the trade date before market opens
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. By the settlement-day pay-in time

Clearing members must ensure funds are available in their clearing bank account by the pay-in deadline on settlement day so pay-out can proceed.

Question 7

When a company declares a stock split, open stock futures positions are:

  1. Closed out by the exchange at the last traded price
  2. Left unchanged
  3. Converted into options
  4. Adjusted in lot size and price
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Adjusted in lot size and price

Exchanges adjust derivative contracts for corporate actions like splits and bonuses, changing lot size and price so the position's value is preserved.

Question 8

Which statement about settlement of trades in the cash segment is correct?

  1. Each trade settles separately on a gross basis
  2. Settlement depends on client consent
  3. Obligations are settled on a net basis
  4. Settlement is optional
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Obligations are settled on a net basis

Clearing corporations net each member's buys and sells across trades in a settlement, and only net obligations are settled.
