Other Services Provided by Brokers: NISM Series VII revision notes

Brokers today offer much more than order execution. This chapter covers margin trading, securities lending and borrowing, portfolio management, mutual fund distribution through exchange platforms, research, internet and algorithmic trading, and depository services. For each, know what permission or registration is needed and the main safeguards for clients.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Margin trading facility

Under MTF, a broker funds part of the purchase value of eligible shares, and the client pays the rest as margin. For example, for a ₹1 lakh purchase with a 25% margin, the client pays ₹25,000 and the broker funds ₹75,000, charging interest. The purchased shares are pledged to the broker as collateral.

2. MTF eligibility and safeguards

Only brokers meeting SEBI's net worth and other requirements can offer MTF, and only on eligible liquid securities. Funding must come from the broker's own funds or permitted borrowings, never from other clients' money. Clients must sign a separate MTF agreement and receive daily margin reports.

3. Risk of MTF

Leverage magnifies gains and losses. If the stock price falls and margin drops below the requirement, the client gets a margin call; if not met, the broker can sell the shares. Interest costs also reduce returns, especially for longer holding periods.

4. Securities lending and borrowing

SLB lets investors lend shares they hold to borrowers through the clearing corporation for a fee, for periods up to 12 months. Borrowers use SLB to cover short sales or meet delivery obligations. The clearing corporation guarantees that lent securities come back, reducing counterparty risk for lenders.

5. Portfolio management

Brokers can offer PMS only after registering separately with SEBI as portfolio managers. The minimum investment per client is ₹50 lakh (as of 2026). PMS can be discretionary, non-discretionary or advisory, and the client's securities are held in a separate demat account.

6. Mutual fund distribution

Brokers can distribute mutual funds through exchange platforms such as NSE's MF platform and BSE StAR MF after obtaining an ARN through AMFI and the required NISM certification. Units are held in the client's demat account or in statement form.

7. Research services

Brokers that publish research reports must register as research analysts or have research services handled by registered persons. Research must be independent of investment banking and trading interests, with disclosures of conflicts.

8. Internet and algorithmic trading

Internet trading requires exchange approval, secure systems, two-factor authentication and clear risk disclosures. Algorithmic trading needs exchange approval of algorithms, risk checks such as order limits and price bands, and audit trails. SEBI has extended a framework for retail algo trading via APIs with controls on brokers.

9. Depository services

Many brokers are also DPs, offering demat accounts to their clients. The two roles remain distinct, with separate agreements and charges. Investors can choose a DP different from their broker if they prefer.

Common traps in the exam

  • Funding MTF positions from client money. MTF must be funded only from the broker's own funds or permitted borrowings.
  • Assuming a broker can offer PMS without separate registration. PMS requires SEBI registration as a portfolio manager.
  • Treating SLB as an unregulated private loan. It runs through the clearing corporation, which guarantees that lent securities come back.
  • Thinking MTF reduces risk. Leverage magnifies both gains and losses.

Quick recap

MTF: broker funds part of purchase of eligible stocks, client pays margin, shares pledged, own funds only. SLB through clearing corporation for up to 12 months. PMS needs separate registration, minimum ₹50 lakh. MF distribution needs ARN. Research needs RA compliance. Algo and internet trading need exchange approval and risk controls.
