Introduction to Securities Broking Operations: NISM Series VII revision notes

This chapter looks inside a broking firm and follows a trade from client onboarding to settlement. It is practical and heavily tested: order types, what a contract note must contain and when it is issued, how client funds and securities must be handled, and the purpose of the unique client code. Think like an operations executive when answering.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Front, middle and back office

The front office deals with clients and places orders through dealers or online platforms. The middle office handles risk management, margin monitoring and surveillance. The back office handles confirmation, contract notes, billing, settlement of funds and securities, accounting and regulatory reporting. Questions often ask which office performs a given task.

2. Client onboarding

Onboarding includes KYC (PAN, identity, address, in-person or video verification), the account opening form, rights and obligations document, risk disclosure document, guidance note, tariff sheet and policies and procedures. Clients choose segments; derivatives activation needs proof of financial capacity. KYC is uploaded to a KRA.

3. Unique client code

Every order must carry the client's unique client code linked to PAN, so the exchange knows exactly who traded. UCC details must be uploaded to the exchange before the client trades. This prevents brokers from shifting trades between clients and supports surveillance.

4. Order types

A market order executes at the best available price. A limit order executes only at the specified price or better. A stop-loss order becomes active when a trigger price is hit. Day orders expire at the end of the day; IOC (immediate or cancel) orders execute immediately to the extent possible and cancel the rest.

5. Trade life cycle

The life cycle runs: order placement, risk checks, order matching, trade confirmation, contract note issue, clearing (computing obligations), pay-in of funds and securities, pay-out and finally reconciliation and statements. Under T+1, pay-in and pay-out happen on the next trading day.

6. Contract notes

A contract note is the legal record of the trade, issued within 24 hours of execution, physically or electronically with a digital signature. It contains order and trade numbers, time, quantity, price, brokerage, taxes such as STT and GST, and the client's UCC. Brokerage must be shown separately and within permitted limits.

7. Client funds handling

Client money must be kept in client bank accounts, separate from the broker's own funds. Client funds are upstreamed to the clearing corporation at the end of each day, so they are not exposed to broker misuse. Unused funds must be settled back to clients at the chosen monthly or quarterly running account settlement.

8. Client securities handling

Client securities must remain in the client's own demat account. Sales use the block mechanism; purchases are paid out directly. Margins in securities are taken through margin pledge, not transfer. Brokers must not use one client's securities for another client's obligations.

9. Statements and reconciliation

Brokers send clients periodic statements of funds and securities, and must reconcile client balances with bank and demat records daily. Exchanges publish client balances so clients can verify their holdings with exchanges directly.

Common traps in the exam

  • Confusing stop-loss orders with limit orders. A stop-loss activates only when the trigger price is reached.
  • Thinking contract notes can be issued within a week. They must be issued within 24 hours of trade execution.
  • Assuming brokers can use one client's credit balance to fund another client's purchase. Client funds are segregated per client.
  • Allocating middle office tasks to the back office. Risk management and margin monitoring sit in the middle office.

Quick recap

Front office takes orders, middle office manages risk, back office settles and reports. Every order carries a PAN-linked UCC. Market, limit, stop-loss, day and IOC orders behave differently. Contract notes within 24 hours, with brokerage and taxes shown. Client funds upstreamed daily and settled monthly or quarterly; client securities stay in client demat accounts.
