Introduction to Securities Broking Operations: NISM Series VII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Introduction to Securities Broking Operations, from the NISM Series VII (Securities Operations and Risk Management) syllabus.

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how a broking firm operates: front, middle and back office functions; client onboarding and KYC; order types and order management; the trade life cycle from order to settlement; contract notes; and client funds handling. Revise order types (market, limit, stop-loss, IOC, day orders), what a contract note contains and when it must be issued, client-level segregation, and the role of unique client codes. Questions are practical and scenario-based.

Introduction to Securities Broking Operations questions

Question 1

A client wants an order that stays active only until the end of the trading day if not executed. This is a:

  1. Immediate or cancel order
  2. Good till cancelled order
  3. Market order
  4. Day order
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Day order

A day order is valid only for the trading day; unexecuted quantity is cancelled at the close. An IOC must execute immediately or be cancelled.

Question 2

A contract note issued by a broker must contain:

  1. The client's net worth
  2. Trade details and charges
  3. The client's tax computation
  4. The broker's balance sheet
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Trade details and charges

Contract notes show trade time, price, quantity, brokerage, taxes and other levies. They are legal records of the trade and must be issued within 24 hours.

Question 3

A client places a market order to buy 100 shares. The order will:

  1. Execute only at the previous close
  2. Wait until a limit price is reached
  3. Execute at the best available price
  4. Be cancelled if not executed in 10 minutes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Execute at the best available price

A market order executes immediately against the best available sell orders, giving certainty of execution but not of price.

Question 4

Which unit in a broking firm usually handles settlement of funds and securities with the clearing corporation?

  1. Back office
  2. Front office
  3. Research desk
  4. Marketing team
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Back office

The back office handles post-trade work: settlement, reconciliation, client ledgers and statutory reporting. The front office takes orders.

Question 5

Every order placed on the exchange must be tagged with the client's:

  1. Bank branch code
  2. Unique Client Code
  3. ISIN
  4. Contract note number
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Unique Client Code

The UCC links each order to a registered client with KYC details, enabling exchange surveillance and client-level monitoring.

Question 6

A client wants to buy only if the price falls to ₹420 or below. The client should place a:

  1. Market buy order
  2. Stop-loss sell order at ₹420
  3. Limit buy order at ₹420
  4. IOC sell order
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Limit buy order at ₹420

A limit buy order executes only at the specified price or lower. A market order would buy at the current price, above ₹420.

Question 7

Funds received from clients must be:

  1. Kept in client bank accounts, segregated
  2. Used for the broker's proprietary trades
  3. Deposited in the broker's own savings account
  4. Lent to other clients
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Kept in client bank accounts, segregated

Client funds must be kept in designated client bank accounts and used only for that client's obligations, under SEBI's segregation rules.

Question 8

Before executing trades for a new client, a broker must ensure:

  1. The client has traded elsewhere before
  2. The client has a minimum income of ₹10 lakh
  3. The client has bought an IPO
  4. KYC and client registration are complete
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. KYC and client registration are complete

KYC, PAN verification and client registration documentation are prerequisites for trading. No minimum income or prior trading history is required.
