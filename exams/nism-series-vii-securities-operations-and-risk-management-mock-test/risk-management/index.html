Risk Management: NISM Series VII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Risk Management, from the NISM Series VII (Securities Operations and Risk Management) syllabus.

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how exchanges, clearing corporations and brokers control risk. Revise the margin system in the cash segment (VaR margin, extreme loss margin, mark-to-market), upfront and peak margin collection, penalties for short collection, position and concentration limits, the Core Settlement Guarantee Fund, and broker-level risk controls such as square-off policies. Practise MTM and margin calculations and understand what each type of margin protects against.

Risk Management questions

Question 1

A client sells 200 shares at ₹750. The day's closing price is ₹765. The mark-to-market loss is:

  1. ₹3,000
  2. ₹1,500
  3. ₹15,000
  4. No loss, since the sale is complete
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹3,000

The short position loses when price rises: 200 × (765 − 750) = ₹3,000. MTM is computed against the closing price until settlement.

Question 2

Peak margin requirements mean that brokers must ensure:

  1. Margins are checked only at end of day
  2. Clients pay margin after settlement
  3. Margins cover intraday peak positions
  4. No margin applies to intraday trades
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Margins cover intraday peak positions

Clearing corporations take multiple intraday snapshots and check collected margin against the peak requirement, so clients cannot take positions without adequate margin.

Question 3

Upfront margin in the cash segment must be collected from clients:

  1. Within seven days of the trade
  2. Only on loss-making trades
  3. At the end of the month
  4. Before trading
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Before trading

SEBI requires brokers to collect VaR and ELM margins upfront from clients before trades are executed, reducing default risk.

Question 4

Concentration limits imposed by a broker on a single client mainly reduce:

  1. Brokerage revenue earned from the client
  2. Exposure to one client or stock
  3. Number of trades
  4. Exchange fees
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Exposure to one client or stock

Concentration limits prevent a large part of the broker's risk being tied to one client or one illiquid stock, which could cause big losses on default.

Question 5

A broker collects less margin than required for a client's position. The consequence is that:

  1. The trade is automatically cancelled
  2. The client gets a refund
  3. A penalty for short collection is levied
  4. SEBI pays the shortfall
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A penalty for short collection is levied

Clearing corporations levy penalties on short collection of margins, which brokers cannot pass on in a manner violating norms; the aim is to enforce upfront margin discipline.

Question 6

VaR margin is typically computed to cover potential losses on a position over:

  1. The entire remaining life of the company
  2. Ten years of history
  3. Only the next minute
  4. A short period at a high confidence level
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A short period at a high confidence level

VaR estimates the maximum likely loss over a short horizon (usually a day or two) at a high confidence level such as 99%.

Question 7

A client's ledger shows an unpaid debit and the stock is falling. A broker's risk policy would typically allow it to:

  1. Ignore it till the client pays
  2. Square off positions after notice
  3. Use another client's securities
  4. Charge interest only
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Square off positions after notice

Brokers can liquidate positions as per their disclosed risk management policy to recover dues and limit further losses.

Question 8

The Core SGF of a clearing corporation is used:

  1. To meet settlement when a member defaults
  2. To pay dividends to investors of listed companies
  3. To fund IPOs
  4. To pay exchange salaries
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. To meet settlement when a member defaults

The Core Settlement Guarantee Fund guarantees completion of settlement if a clearing member defaults, after using the defaulter's own resources.
