Risk Management: NISM Series VII revision notes

Risk management is one of the most important and calculation-heavy chapters in this exam. You must know the types of margins collected in the cash segment and what each protects against, the rules on upfront collection, penalties for short collection, position limits and the settlement guarantee fund. Practise MTM and margin calculations until they are routine.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Why margins exist

Between trade and settlement, prices can move and a party may default. The clearing corporation guarantees settlement through novation, so it must collect margins that cover likely losses. Margins are collected from clearing members, who collect from trading members and clients.

2. VaR margin

Value at Risk margin covers the potential one-day loss on a position at a high confidence level, typically 99%. It is computed from the volatility of the security. Securities are grouped by liquidity and trading frequency, and less liquid securities attract higher VaR rates.

3. Extreme loss margin

ELM covers losses beyond what VaR captures, for example a sudden market crash. It is charged in addition to VaR as a percentage of the gross position value, with a minimum prescribed by SEBI for equity in the cash segment (around 3.5% as of 2026; check exchange circulars).

4. Mark-to-market margin

MTM is the notional loss on open positions, computed by comparing the trade price with the closing price. If a client bought 1,000 shares at ₹500 and the closing price is ₹480, the MTM loss is ₹20,000, which must be paid. MTM gains are not paid out in the cash segment before settlement.

5. Upfront and peak margins

Brokers must collect VaR and ELM margins from clients upfront, before trades are executed. The clearing corporation checks margin compliance at several random intraday snapshots, so peak positions during the day are fully margined. Intraday leverage beyond collected margin is not allowed.

6. Penalty for short collection

If a broker collects less margin from a client than required, the clearing corporation levies a penalty on the broker, graded by the shortfall amount and repetition. Brokers cannot pass this penalty to clients except where the client is at fault under rules. This creates a strong incentive for upfront collection.

7. Position and concentration limits

Exchanges set limits on positions a client or broker can hold in particular securities, especially in derivatives, and monitor concentration to prevent any one entity from dominating a security. Market-wide position limits trigger a ban period in stock derivatives when exceeded.

8. Core Settlement Guarantee Fund

Each clearing corporation maintains a Core SGF to meet settlement obligations if members default. It is funded by contributions from the clearing corporation, the stock exchange and clearing members, sized by stress tests covering the default of the largest members. The default waterfall uses the defaulter's margins first, then the SGF and other resources.

9. Broker-level controls

Brokers set their own client limits, monitor real-time exposure, use pre-trade risk checks and have square-off policies for positions where margins fall short. Policies on when and how positions will be squared off must be disclosed to clients in advance.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • MTM loss = (trade price − closing price) × quantity for a long position; for a short position, (closing price − trade price) × quantity.
  • Total initial margin (cash segment) = (VaR % + ELM %) × position value; e.g. (12% + 3.5%) × ₹2,00,000 = ₹31,000.

Common traps in the exam

  • Thinking MTM gains are paid to the client before settlement in the cash segment. Only losses are collected.
  • Confusing VaR with ELM. VaR covers normal one-day risk; ELM covers extreme moves beyond VaR.
  • Assuming margins can be collected after the trade. VaR and ELM must be collected upfront.
  • Believing the Core SGF is used first in a default. The defaulter's own margins and collateral are used first.

Quick recap

Margins protect the clearing corporation between trade and settlement. VaR covers one-day risk at 99%; ELM covers extreme moves; MTM collects notional losses. Collect upfront; peak positions are checked through intraday snapshots. Short collection draws penalties. Core SGF backs settlement after defaulter's margins.
