Market Participants in the Securities Market: NISM Series VII revision notes

This chapter introduces everyone who takes part in the securities market and how each is regulated. Expect questions that describe a role and ask who performs it, or that test how investors are categorised in public issues. A clear grasp of who does what makes later chapters on broking, clearing and settlement much easier.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Investors

Investors include retail individuals, high-net-worth individuals, NRIs, HUFs, corporates, trusts, mutual funds, insurers, pension funds, banks and foreign portfolio investors. In public issues, retail individual investors bid up to ₹2 lakh, non-institutional investors bid above ₹2 lakh, and qualified institutional buyers include mutual funds, FPIs, banks, insurers and similar institutions.

2. Issuers

Issuers are entities that raise money by issuing securities: companies, governments, public sector units, banks, mutual funds (through units) and infrastructure and real estate trusts. Listed issuers must follow SEBI's listing regulations on disclosure and governance.

3. Stock brokers

Brokers are members of stock exchanges registered with SEBI who execute trades for clients and themselves. They may be trading members only, or trading-cum-clearing members, or professional clearing members who only clear. Brokers must keep client funds and securities segregated.

4. Authorised persons

An authorised person is appointed by a broker, with exchange approval, to provide access to trading for clients. The AP acts as the broker's agent; the client relationship and contract notes remain with the broker. The older sub-broker category has been merged into the AP framework.

5. Depository participants and custodians

DPs open and maintain demat accounts on behalf of depositories. Custodians hold securities and settle trades for institutional clients such as FPIs and mutual funds, handle corporate actions and maintain records. Many large banks act as both DPs and custodians.

6. Clearing members and clearing banks

Clearing members settle trades with the clearing corporation for themselves and for trading members who appoint them. Clearing banks act as the funds settlement channel, debiting and crediting clearing members' accounts on pay-in and pay-out.

7. Merchant bankers and RTAs

Merchant bankers manage public issues, perform due diligence and advise on takeovers. RTAs maintain shareholder records, process allotments, handle corporate actions and investor service requests for issuers. Both need SEBI registration.

8. Credit rating agencies and others

Credit rating agencies assess the credit risk of debt instruments and issuers. Other SEBI-registered participants include investment advisers, research analysts, portfolio managers, mutual funds, alternative investment funds, debenture trustees and bankers to an issue. Each has its own regulations and code of conduct.

9. Market infrastructure institutions

Stock exchanges, clearing corporations and depositories are MIIs. Their governing boards must include public interest directors, and they must maintain net worth and settlement guarantee funds. Brokers and DPs are members or participants of MIIs, which supervise them on SEBI's behalf.

Common traps in the exam

  • Confusing authorised persons with brokers. An AP is the broker's agent; the client remains the broker's client.
  • Putting HNIs among QIBs. Individuals bidding above ₹2 lakh are non-institutional investors, not QIBs.
  • Assuming custodians only hold securities. They also settle trades and process corporate actions for institutional clients.
  • Mixing up a professional clearing member with a trading member. A PCM clears and settles but does not trade on the exchange.

Quick recap

Investors: retail (bids up to ₹2 lakh), NIIs (above ₹2 lakh) and QIBs (institutions). Brokers trade; APs act as brokers' agents; clearing members settle; clearing banks move funds. DPs and custodians hold securities; RTAs keep issuer records; merchant bankers manage issues. MIIs (exchanges, clearing corporations, depositories) supervise members.
