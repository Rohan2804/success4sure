Clearing Process: NISM Series VII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Clearing Process, from the NISM Series VII (Securities Operations and Risk Management) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains clearing: determining who owes what after trading. Revise novation by the clearing corporation, multilateral netting, the roles of clearing members, custodians and clearing banks, confirmation of institutional trades, the interface with depositories, and the settlement calendar. Understand the difference between clearing (computing obligations) and settlement (actual exchange of funds and securities), and practise simple netting examples.

Clearing Process questions

Question 1

A broker's clients buy 1,000 shares and sell 700 shares of the same stock in the same settlement. The broker's net securities obligation is to:

  1. Deliver 300 shares
  2. Receive 300 shares
  3. Receive 1,700 shares
  4. Deliver 1,000 shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Receive 300 shares

Netting offsets buys and sells: 1,000 bought − 700 sold = net receipt of 300 shares. Netting reduces the volume of securities and funds that must move.

Question 2

Novation in clearing means that:

  1. Trades are cancelled if prices move by more than the circuit limit
  2. Brokers settle directly with each other
  3. SEBI guarantees each trade
  4. The clearing corporation becomes counterparty to both sides
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The clearing corporation becomes counterparty to both sides

With novation, the clearing corporation interposes itself between buyer and seller, so each party's counterparty risk is on the clearing corporation.

Question 3

Institutional trades executed by a broker for a custodian's client become final only after:

  1. The custodian confirms them
  2. The issuer approves them
  3. SEBI approves them
  4. The client pays tax
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The custodian confirms them

Trades with a custodial participant code must be confirmed by the custodian; on confirmation, settlement responsibility moves to the custodian's clearing member.

Question 4

The difference between clearing and settlement is that clearing:

  1. Moves securities; settlement computes obligations
  2. Happens after settlement
  3. Determines obligations; settlement discharges them
  4. Is done by depositories only
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Determines obligations; settlement discharges them

Clearing works out each member's net funds and securities obligations. Settlement is the actual pay-in and pay-out of those funds and securities.

Question 5

Clearing members' fund obligations are settled through:

  1. Cash deposits at the exchange
  2. Cheques to SEBI
  3. The RTA's account
  4. Designated clearing banks
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Designated clearing banks

Each clearing member maintains a settlement account with a clearing bank, which is debited or credited as instructed by the clearing corporation.

Question 6

A trader buys and sells the same quantity of a stock on the same day in the cash segment. Under netting, the delivery obligation is:

  1. Nil, only price difference settled
  2. Full delivery of both legs
  3. Delivery of the bought shares
  4. Settled next month
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Nil, only price difference settled

Intraday buy and sell of equal quantity net to zero shares; only the money difference (profit or loss) is settled.

Question 7

Which entity provides clearing corporations with the details of trades executed?

  1. The depository
  2. The RTA
  3. The stock exchange
  4. The clearing bank
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The stock exchange

Exchanges pass trade data to the clearing corporation, which computes obligations and coordinates with depositories and clearing banks for settlement.

Question 8

Why does netting reduce settlement risk?

  1. It eliminates price risk for both buyers and sellers
  2. Fewer funds and securities need to move
  3. It guarantees profits
  4. It removes the need for margins
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Fewer funds and securities need to move

By offsetting buys and sells, netting lowers the gross amount to be settled, reducing liquidity needs and the impact of any single failure.
