Clearing Process: NISM Series VII revision notes

Clearing determines exactly what each party must deliver and receive after a day's trading. This chapter covers novation, multilateral netting, the roles of clearing members, custodians and clearing banks, and the depository interface. Make sure you can distinguish clearing from settlement and work through a simple netting example.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Clearing vs settlement

Clearing is the process of identifying and computing obligations: who owes how many securities and how much money. Settlement is the actual transfer of securities and funds to discharge those obligations. In exam terms: clearing calculates, settlement completes.

2. Novation

The clearing corporation steps in as the counterparty to every trade: it becomes buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer. Each member therefore faces only the clearing corporation, which guarantees settlement even if the original counterparty defaults. Novation is the foundation of counterparty risk management.

3. Multilateral netting

Instead of settling each trade separately, the clearing corporation nets all buys and sells of a member in each security across counterparties. If a member bought 1,000 shares and sold 700 shares of the same company in a settlement, it receives a net 300 shares. Funds are also netted, reducing liquidity needs.

4. Netting at client level

Netting for delivery is done at the client level, not across clients. If client A sells 100 shares and client B buys 100 shares of the same company through the same broker, both obligations remain; the broker cannot simply net them off and keep A's shares for B.

5. Clearing members

Trading members who clear their own trades are trading-cum-clearing members; those who only clear for others are professional clearing members. Clearing members maintain margin deposits and must meet net worth requirements. They receive obligation files from the clearing corporation after trading.

6. Custodial trades

Institutional investors trade through brokers but settle through custodians. The custodian must confirm the trade within a deadline on trade day; once confirmed, the custodian takes on the settlement obligation. If the custodian rejects it, the obligation falls back on the broker.

7. Clearing banks

Clearing members maintain clearing accounts with designated clearing banks. On pay-in, the clearing corporation debits these accounts; on pay-out, it credits them. Clearing banks therefore act as the funds settlement backbone of the clearing system.

8. Depository interface

For securities, the clearing corporation sends instructions to the depositories to debit sellers' accounts (blocked shares) and credit buyers' accounts at pay-out. The interface is electronic and automated, which allows T+1 settlement.

9. Settlement calendar

Exchanges publish a settlement calendar showing trade dates, pay-in and pay-out dates for each settlement. Settlement holidays, which can differ from trading holidays, shift these dates. Operations teams plan funding and deliveries around this calendar.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Net obligation = total bought − total sold, per security per client; positive means receive, negative means deliver.

Common traps in the exam

  • Using clearing and settlement as synonyms. Clearing computes obligations; settlement discharges them.
  • Assuming netting happens across different clients of the same broker. Delivery obligations are netted only within each client's account.
  • Thinking novation removes all risk. It shifts counterparty risk to the clearing corporation, which manages it through margins and the SGF.
  • Forgetting that unconfirmed custodial trades become the broker's obligation.

Quick recap

Clearing calculates obligations; settlement completes them. Novation makes the clearing corporation the counterparty to all trades. Multilateral netting reduces obligations to net positions per security, within each client. Custodians confirm institutional trades; clearing banks move funds; depositories move securities. Follow the exchange settlement calendar.
