Introduction to the Securities Market: NISM Series VII revision notes

The Securities Operations and Risk Management exam begins with a broad map of the market that the rest of the syllabus builds on. Questions here are conceptual: what the market does, how the primary and secondary segments differ, what kinds of issues companies make, which products trade where and which regulator oversees each part.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Functions of the market

The securities market moves savings to productive uses (capital formation), gives investors liquidity because they can sell holdings, discovers prices through continuous trading, and spreads risk across many investors. It also improves corporate governance by forcing listed companies to disclose information regularly.

2. Primary market issues

Companies raise fresh capital through an IPO (first public issue), FPO (further public issue by a listed company), rights issue (offered to existing shareholders in proportion to holdings), private placement to a small group, preferential allotment, and qualified institutions placement (QIP) to QIBs only. An offer for sale by existing shareholders does not bring money to the company.

3. Secondary market

Listed securities trade on recognised stock exchanges such as NSE and BSE through an anonymous order-matching system. Trades are guaranteed by clearing corporations. Over-the-counter trades, such as many bond deals, are reported to exchange platforms for transparency and settled through clearing corporations.

4. Market segments

Equity cash segment trades shares and ETFs; the equity derivatives segment trades futures and options on indices and stocks; the debt segment trades corporate bonds and G-secs; currency and interest rate derivatives trade in separate segments. Commodity derivatives also trade on exchanges under SEBI regulation.

5. Money market vs capital market

The money market deals in short-term instruments of up to one year, such as treasury bills, commercial paper, certificates of deposit, call money and repo. The capital market deals in long-term instruments such as equity and bonds. RBI regulates most money market instruments, while SEBI regulates capital market securities.

6. Regulators

SEBI regulates the securities market and intermediaries; RBI regulates banks, money and government securities markets and foreign exchange; IRDAI regulates insurance; PFRDA regulates pensions; IFSCA regulates financial services in IFSCs such as GIFT City; MCA administers company law. The Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act and Depositories Act are key laws besides the SEBI Act.

7. Government securities vs corporate securities

Government securities are issued by RBI on behalf of the central and state governments, carry negligible credit risk and are traded mostly by institutions on the NDS-OM platform. Corporate bonds carry credit risk reflected in their ratings and spreads over G-secs. Both carry interest rate risk.

8. Products in the market

Common products include equity shares, preference shares, debentures, bonds, mutual fund units, ETFs, REIT and InvIT units, warrants, sovereign gold bonds, and derivatives. Each has different risk, return and liquidity features, and operations teams must handle their settlement and corporate actions differently.

9. Role of market infrastructure

Stock exchanges, clearing corporations and depositories form the backbone. Exchanges provide trading platforms and surveillance, clearing corporations provide settlement guarantee and risk management, and depositories hold securities electronically. Their failure would affect the whole market, so SEBI regulates them as systemically important institutions.

Common traps in the exam

  • Treating an offer for sale as raising capital for the company. OFS proceeds go to the selling shareholders.
  • Confusing QIP with private placement to anyone. A QIP is only to qualified institutional buyers.
  • Assuming SEBI regulates T-bills and call money. These are money market instruments under RBI.
  • Thinking a rights issue is open to the public. It is offered to existing shareholders, who may renounce their entitlement.

Quick recap

The market raises capital, provides liquidity and discovers prices. Primary issues: IPO, FPO, rights, private placement, preferential, QIP; OFS money goes to sellers. Secondary trading on exchanges is guaranteed by clearing corporations. SEBI regulates securities, RBI money markets and G-secs, IFSCA GIFT City. Money market is up to one year.
