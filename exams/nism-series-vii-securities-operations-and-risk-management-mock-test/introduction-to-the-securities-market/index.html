What this chapter covers

This chapter gives an overview of India's securities market: primary and secondary segments, equity, debt, derivatives and money markets, the main products traded, and the regulators involved (SEBI, RBI, IFSCA and others). Revise the functions of the market (capital formation, liquidity, price discovery), types of issues (IPO, FPO, rights, private placement, QIP), and how government securities and money market instruments differ from corporate securities. Questions are mostly conceptual.

Introduction to the Securities Market questions

Question 1

A listed company raises fresh capital by allotting shares only to qualified institutional buyers. This is a:

  1. Rights issue to all existing shareholders
  2. Qualified Institutions Placement
  3. Bonus issue
  4. Initial public offer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Qualified Institutions Placement

A QIP lets listed companies raise money quickly from QIBs without a full public issue. A rights issue is offered to existing shareholders, and a bonus issue raises no money.

Question 2

Which of these is a money market instrument?

  1. Equity share
  2. Ten-year corporate debenture
  3. Convertible preference share
  4. Commercial paper
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Commercial paper

Commercial paper is an unsecured short-term instrument (up to one year) issued by companies. Shares and long-term debentures are capital market instruments.

Question 3

A company already listed on the exchange makes a further public offer of new shares. This is called an:

  1. FPO
  2. IPO
  3. OFS by promoters
  4. ESOP
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. FPO

A follow-on public offer is a fresh issue by an already listed company. An IPO is the first public offer, and an offer for sale involves existing shareholders selling, not new shares.

Question 4

Price discovery in the secondary market means that:

  1. SEBI fixes share prices daily
  2. Issuers set prices each morning
  3. Prices emerge from buy and sell orders
  4. Prices remain unchanged till results
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Prices emerge from buy and sell orders

In the secondary market, interaction of many buyers and sellers determines the market price, reflecting available information.

Question 5

Which regulator oversees banks' participation in the government securities and money markets?

  1. SEBI
  2. IRDAI
  3. PFRDA
  4. RBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. RBI

RBI regulates the government securities, money and forex markets and the banks that participate in them, while SEBI regulates stock exchanges and securities intermediaries.

Question 6

An offer of new shares by a company to its existing shareholders in proportion to their holdings is a:

  1. Rights issue
  2. Private placement
  3. Preferential allotment to promoters
  4. Buyback offer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Rights issue

Rights issues let existing shareholders subscribe in proportion to holdings, often at a discount; they can renounce rights entitlements if they do not wish to subscribe.

Question 7

Which of these trades on the derivatives segment of a stock exchange?

  1. Treasury bills
  2. Bank fixed deposits
  3. Index futures
  4. Commercial paper
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Index futures

Index futures and options are exchange-traded derivatives. T-bills and CPs are money market instruments, and FDs are bank deposits.

Question 8

Interest rates in the overnight call money market are most directly influenced by:

  1. Daily movement of the Nifty and Sensex indices
  2. RBI's liquidity operations and policy rate
  3. Company dividend announcements
  4. Gold prices
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. RBI's liquidity operations and policy rate

Call money rates move within RBI's policy corridor and respond to RBI's liquidity operations, which add or absorb banking system funds.
