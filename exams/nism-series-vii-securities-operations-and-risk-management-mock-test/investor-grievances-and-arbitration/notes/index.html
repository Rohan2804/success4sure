Investor Grievances and Arbitration: NISM Series VII revision notes

Investors need a clear path to resolve disputes with brokers, listed companies and other intermediaries. This chapter explains the escalation ladder, SEBI's SCORES platform, the online dispute resolution mechanism and the Investor Protection Fund. Questions are often scenarios: a client has a complaint — where should they go first, and what happens next?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. First stop: the intermediary

Investors should first complain to the broker or intermediary. Every broker must have a grievance redressal mechanism, a designated email ID for complaints and an investor charter. Many complaints, such as delays in fund payout or contract note issues, are resolved at this stage.

2. Exchange grievance cell

If the broker does not resolve the complaint, the investor can approach the stock exchange's investor service cell. The exchange takes up the matter with the broker and can direct corrective action. Exchanges have committees that also review claims involving default by members.

3. SCORES

SEBI's SCORES platform allows online complaints against listed companies and intermediaries. The entity must resolve the complaint within 21 calendar days, and a first-level review by the designated body follows if the investor is unhappy. Complaints must be filed within a prescribed period, generally three years from the cause of action.

4. Online dispute resolution

Disputes that are not resolved can be taken to the Smart ODR portal. The process begins with online conciliation by an independent conciliator. If conciliation fails within its time limit, the dispute moves to online arbitration, where an arbitrator issues an award. Both parties can participate remotely.

5. Arbitration award and appeal

The arbitral award is binding, subject to the limited challenge available under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act. Fees for ODR are prescribed and may vary by claim size. Investors should keep contract notes, statements and communication records to support their claim.

6. Investor Protection Fund

Each exchange maintains an IPF to compensate investors whose claims are admitted after a broker is declared a defaulter. Compensation is subject to a per-investor ceiling set by the exchange. The IPF is not used to compensate trading losses due to market movements.

7. What is not a valid complaint

Losses due to market price movements, dissatisfaction with investment performance, or complaints about investment advice from unregistered tipsters are not grounds for compensation against a broker. Investors who traded through unregistered entities may not get protection.

8. Complaints against listed companies

Complaints about non-receipt of dividends, transfers or corporate benefits go to the company or RTA first, then to SCORES. Exchanges can also take up complaints against listed companies and may act against non-compliant companies.

9. Investor education

Exchanges and depositories use part of their investor protection funds for investor awareness programmes. Investors are encouraged to verify broker registration, check alerts and statements, and avoid sharing passwords or giving blank authorisations.

Common traps in the exam

  • Going straight to arbitration. The ODR process begins with conciliation; arbitration follows only if conciliation fails.
  • Expecting the IPF to cover market losses. It covers admitted claims against defaulting brokers, within limits.
  • Assuming SCORES can be used before approaching the intermediary. The investor should first complain to the intermediary.
  • Thinking trades through unregistered tipsters are protected. Dealings with unregistered entities are generally outside the protection framework.

Quick recap

Complain first to the broker, then the exchange, then SCORES (21-day resolution). Unresolved disputes go to Smart ODR: conciliation, then arbitration. IPF compensates admitted claims after broker default, subject to a ceiling, never market losses. Keep records. Avoid unregistered entities.
