Investor Grievances and Arbitration: NISM Series VII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Investor Grievances and Arbitration, from the NISM Series VII (Securities Operations and Risk Management) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how investors can resolve complaints against brokers, listed companies and other intermediaries. Revise the escalation path (intermediary, exchange, SCORES), timelines for resolution, the Online Dispute Resolution mechanism with conciliation and arbitration, the Investor Protection Fund and its use when a broker defaults, and what is not a valid ground for complaint. Questions are mostly conceptual and scenario-based.

Investor Grievances and Arbitration questions

Question 1

Under SEBI's ODR mechanism, a dispute is first attempted to be resolved through:

  1. A civil court hearing
  2. Direct arbitration only
  3. A complaint to RBI
  4. Online conciliation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Online conciliation

The ODR process starts with online conciliation. If that fails, the matter can proceed to online arbitration through empanelled ODR institutions.

Question 2

The Investor Protection Fund of a stock exchange pays claims when:

  1. Share prices fall
  2. A broker is declared a defaulter
  3. A company skips a dividend
  4. An IPO is undersubscribed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A broker is declared a defaulter

IPF compensates eligible investor claims against defaulting brokers up to specified limits; it does not cover investment losses due to market movement.

Question 3

Four clients write to their broker. Whose grievance is a genuine service deficiency by the broker?

  1. Loss on a stock bought on own decision
  2. The market fell after purchase
  3. Non-receipt of funds after a sale
  4. A company cut its dividend
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Non-receipt of funds after a sale

Failure to pay out sale proceeds is a service deficiency. Market losses on one's own decisions are not grounds for complaint.

Question 4

An investor wants to complain online against a listed company and track the complaint. The platform is:

  1. SCORES
  2. UPI
  3. e-voting
  4. MF Utility
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. SCORES

SCORES is SEBI's online grievance redress system for listed companies and intermediaries, with tracking of status and escalation.

Question 5

Arbitration awards under the exchange or ODR mechanism are:

  1. Merely advisory and can be ignored by the broker
  2. Binding, subject to legal challenge
  3. Valid only if SEBI signs
  4. Applicable only to brokers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Binding, subject to legal challenge

Arbitral awards are binding on parties, subject to challenge under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act. They provide a faster route than civil courts.

Question 6

A client finds unauthorised trades in her account. Her first step should be to:

  1. Ignore it if the amount involved is small
  2. Sell all holdings
  3. Raise it with the broker in writing
  4. Close her bank account
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Raise it with the broker in writing

Investors should promptly report unauthorised trades to the broker in writing; if not resolved, escalate to the exchange or SCORES and then ODR.

Question 7

Exchanges maintain a mechanism to resolve complaints between investors and listed companies or brokers mainly because:

  1. They supervise their members and listed companies
  2. They guarantee investment returns
  3. They are owned by investors
  4. They set income tax rates
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. They supervise their members and listed companies

Exchanges have regulatory responsibilities over members and listed companies, so they handle investor complaints and can take action against them.

Question 8

Which of these is NOT covered by the Investor Protection Fund?

  1. Securities due from a defaulting broker
  2. Funds due from a defaulting broker
  3. Eligible claims after default
  4. Losses from speculative trades
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Losses from speculative trades

IPF covers eligible claims for funds and securities due from a defaulting broker. It does not compensate losses on trades made by the investor.
