Role of Portfolio Managers: NISM Series XXI-A revision notes

This chapter defines who a portfolio manager is under the SEBI (Portfolio Managers) Regulations, 2020, the types of services offered and the duties owed to clients. Expect questions on who takes decisions in each service type, the minimum investment, the principal officer's role, the distributor's role and which clients PMS suits.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Definition

A portfolio manager is a body corporate that, under a contract with a client, advises, directs or undertakes the management or administration of the client's portfolio of securities or funds. Only SEBI-registered entities can offer portfolio management services.

2. Discretionary PMS

In discretionary PMS, the portfolio manager decides what to buy and sell, and when, within the agreed investment approach, without seeking the client's approval for each transaction. Most PMS assets in India are discretionary. The client relies heavily on the manager's skill and integrity.

3. Non-discretionary PMS

In non-discretionary PMS, the portfolio manager recommends transactions but executes them only after the client approves. The client keeps control over decisions, while the manager handles execution, reporting and administration.

4. Advisory PMS

In advisory PMS, the portfolio manager only gives advice; the client decides and executes trades through their own arrangements. The manager is responsible for the quality of advice but not for execution.

5. Minimum investment

The minimum investment per client is ₹50 lakh (as of 2026), which can be brought in as cash, securities or a combination. Securities brought in are valued at market price on the date of transfer. Large value accredited investors may agree to different terms.

6. Fiduciary duty

A portfolio manager acts in a fiduciary capacity, putting the client's interests first. It must manage the portfolio in line with the agreement and disclosure document, avoid conflicts or disclose them, ensure best execution and keep client assets segregated from its own.

7. Principal officer

The principal officer is responsible for decisions on managing client portfolios and for the overall activities of the portfolio manager. SEBI prescribes qualifications, experience and NISM certification for the principal officer and for employees involved in decision-making.

8. Role of the distributor

Distributors introduce clients to portfolio managers, explain products, help with documentation and service clients over time. They must be registered with APMI (Association of Portfolio Managers in India) and hold NISM Series XXI-A certification. They cannot manage portfolios or promise returns.

9. Suitable clients and uses

PMS suits high-net-worth investors with long horizons who want direct ownership, customisation and a focused strategy. It can be used for concentrated equity portfolios, debt or multi-asset strategies, or for specific needs like tax-efficient transition of existing holdings.

Common traps in the exam

  • Assuming the client approves each trade in discretionary PMS. The manager decides within the agreed approach.
  • Treating advisory PMS as including execution. The client executes trades in advisory PMS.
  • Thinking the minimum can only be met with cash. Securities can be contributed at market value.
  • Believing a distributor can make investment decisions. Distributors only introduce and service clients.

Quick recap

Portfolio manager: SEBI-registered body corporate managing client portfolios under contract. Discretionary: manager decides. Non-discretionary: client approves. Advisory: advice only. Minimum ₹50 lakh in cash or securities. Fiduciary duty and segregation of assets. Principal officer leads decisions. Distributors: APMI-registered, NISM XXI-A certified, no decisions.
