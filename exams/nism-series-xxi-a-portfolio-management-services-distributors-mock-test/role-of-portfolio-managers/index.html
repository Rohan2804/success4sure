Role of Portfolio Managers: NISM Series XXI-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Role of Portfolio Managers, from the NISM Series XXI-A (Portfolio Management Services (PMS) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains who a portfolio manager is under SEBI (Portfolio Managers) Regulations, 2020, and the types of services offered: discretionary, non-discretionary and advisory. It covers the portfolio manager's duties as a fiduciary, the minimum investment per client, the client profile suited to PMS, the role of the principal officer and the distributor, and how PMS can be used for specific needs like concentrated equity, debt or multi-asset strategies. Know clearly who takes decisions in each service type and what the client should expect.

Role of Portfolio Managers questions

Question 1

Which of the following can offer portfolio management services to clients in India?

  1. Any stock broker, without needing a separate registration, if clients agree
  2. A chartered accountant who manages money for friends and relatives for a fee
  3. A bank branch manager acting in a personal capacity outside the bank
  4. An entity registered with SEBI as a portfolio manager
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. An entity registered with SEBI as a portfolio manager

Managing client portfolios for consideration requires SEBI registration under the Portfolio Managers Regulations, 2020. A broker, accountant or banker cannot offer PMS without obtaining that registration.

Question 2

Under SEBI rules, the minimum amount a client (other than an accredited investor) must invest in PMS is:

  1. ₹50 lakh, in funds or securities
  2. ₹25 lakh, in cash only
  3. ₹1 crore, in cash only
  4. ₹10 lakh, in funds or securities
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹50 lakh, in funds or securities

The PMS minimum is ₹50 lakh, which may be brought in as funds, securities or a combination, valued as specified. Accredited investors may be exempted under the framework.

Question 3

Who is responsible for the overall activities of a portfolio manager's business and is required to meet qualification and experience norms?

  1. The custodian
  2. The distributor
  3. The client's chartered accountant
  4. The principal officer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The principal officer

The principal officer is designated to be responsible for the PMS's decisions and functioning and must meet SEBI's qualification, experience and certification requirements.

Question 4

A client wants a portfolio manager to manage her money but insists on approving every trade. Which service fits?

  1. Discretionary portfolio management
  2. Investment in a Category III AIF
  3. Non-discretionary portfolio management
  4. A passive index fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Non-discretionary portfolio management

Non-discretionary PMS keeps the client in control of decisions while the manager researches, recommends and executes. Discretionary PMS gives decision-making power to the manager.

Question 5

As a fiduciary, a portfolio manager must:

  1. Maximise its own fee income above all
  2. Guarantee a minimum return every year
  3. Place the client's interests above its own
  4. Invest only in its group companies
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Place the client's interests above its own

Fiduciary duty means acting in good faith and in the client's best interest, avoiding or managing conflicts. It does not include guaranteeing returns.

Question 6

A PMS distributor's main role is to:

  1. Explain the PMS offering to suitable clients and facilitate onboarding
  2. Take discretionary investment decisions for clients
  3. Hold client securities in custody
  4. Audit the portfolio manager's accounts
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Explain the PMS offering to suitable clients and facilitate onboarding

Distributors introduce clients to PMS, explain features, risks and costs, and help with paperwork. Investment decisions, custody and audit are handled by others.

Question 7

Which client is generally most suited to a concentrated equity PMS strategy?

  1. A high-net-worth investor with a long horizon and high risk capacity
  2. A retiree who depends fully on the portfolio for regular monthly household expenses
  3. A young investor with ₹5 lakh savings
  4. A trust that needs capital safety within one year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A high-net-worth investor with a long horizon and high risk capacity

Concentrated equity portfolios can be volatile. They suit wealthy, long-horizon investors who can absorb drawdowns, not those needing safety, income or small-ticket investing.

Question 8

In an advisory PMS arrangement, the execution of trades is done by:

  1. The portfolio manager without client consent
  2. The stock exchange on the manager's instructions
  3. The custodian on its own judgement
  4. The client, based on advice received
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The client, based on advice received

Advisory services provide recommendations only. The client decides and executes, and the portfolio manager has no control over the account.
