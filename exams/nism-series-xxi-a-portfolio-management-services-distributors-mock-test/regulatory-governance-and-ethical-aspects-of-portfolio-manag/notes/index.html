Regulatory, Governance and Ethical Aspects of Portfolio Managers: NISM Series XXI-A revision notes

This chapter covers the SEBI (Portfolio Managers) Regulations, 2020 and the conduct expected of portfolio managers and their distributors. Expect scenario questions on what a distributor should or should not do, along with questions on registration, net worth, investment restrictions, conflicts of interest and grievance redress. Ethical selling is the theme throughout.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Registration and net worth

A portfolio manager must be a body corporate registered with SEBI, with net worth of at least ₹5 crore (as of 2026), adequate infrastructure and qualified key personnel. Registration is permanent subject to compliance and fees, and SEBI can suspend or cancel it for violations.

2. Key personnel

The principal officer must meet prescribed qualification and experience criteria and hold NISM certification for portfolio managers. A separate compliance officer monitors compliance with laws and handles regulatory reporting. Employees involved in decision-making also need certification.

3. Code of conduct

Portfolio managers must act with integrity, fairness and in the clients' best interest, maintain high standards of service, avoid conflicts or disclose them, protect client confidentiality, keep client assets separate and never make misleading statements about performance.

4. Investment restrictions

Client funds can be invested in unlisted securities only within prescribed limits (25% of AUM, with flexibility for certain accredited investor arrangements). Investments in securities of associates and related parties are capped, with an overall limit of 30% of client AUM. Leverage of client portfolios is not permitted.

5. Conflict of interest

Managers must disclose conflicts, such as investments in group companies or commissions from intermediaries. They cannot favour one client over another in allocation of trades, and cannot use client portfolios to support group company securities.

6. Distributor obligations

Distributors must be registered with APMI and certified. They must explain the strategy, risks, fees and lock-ins honestly, disclose all commissions received, assess whether PMS is appropriate for the client and never assure returns. They must not handle client cash or securities.

7. Prevention of mis-selling

Mis-selling includes recommending PMS to a client who cannot bear its risks, making false performance claims, hiding fees, encouraging churning between products for commissions and misrepresenting tax benefits. Trail-only commissions and direct plans were introduced to reduce such incentives.

8. Grievance redress

Clients should first approach the portfolio manager, which must resolve complaints within prescribed timelines. If unresolved, they can complain on SCORES and then use SEBI's online dispute resolution mechanism with conciliation and arbitration.

9. Penalties

Violations can lead to warnings, monetary penalties, suspension or cancellation of registration, and prosecution under the SEBI Act. Distributors who violate norms can lose their APMI registration and face action.

Common traps in the exam

  • Allowing a distributor to collect client cheques in their own name. Distributors must not handle client funds.
  • Assuming PMS can invest freely in group companies. Associate investments are capped.
  • Thinking it is acceptable to quote expected returns. Assurance of returns is prohibited.
  • Skipping the portfolio manager and going straight to SCORES. Complaints start with the entity.

Quick recap

Portfolio managers: body corporate, SEBI registration, net worth ₹5 crore (as of 2026), qualified principal officer and compliance officer. Unlisted and associate investments capped; no leverage. Code of conduct: integrity, client first, disclose conflicts. Distributors: APMI-registered, disclose commissions, assess appropriateness, no assured returns, no handling client money. Grievances: entity, SCORES, ODR.
