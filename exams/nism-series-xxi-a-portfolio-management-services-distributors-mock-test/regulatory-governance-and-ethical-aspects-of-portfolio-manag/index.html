Regulatory, Governance and Ethical Aspects of Portfolio Managers: NISM Series XXI-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Regulatory, Governance and Ethical Aspects of Portfolio Managers, from the NISM Series XXI-A (Portfolio Management Services (PMS) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers SEBI (Portfolio Managers) Regulations, 2020 and related circulars: registration and net worth requirements, principal officer and compliance officer, code of conduct, conflict of interest, restrictions on investment in associates and unlisted securities, prevention of mis-selling, distributor obligations, grievance redressal through SCORES and online dispute resolution, and penalties. It also covers ethical selling: suitability, disclosure of commissions, no assurance of returns and fair treatment. Expect scenario questions on what a distributor should or should not do.

Regulatory, Governance and Ethical Aspects of Portfolio Managers questions

Question 1

A PMS distributor promises a client that the strategy 'cannot fall more than 5%'. This is:

  1. Acceptable if the past three years support it
  2. Misleading, since no market-linked strategy can assure limits on losses
  3. Required disclosure under SEBI rules
  4. Permitted, as long as the client is a high-net-worth individual who understands markets
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Misleading, since no market-linked strategy can assure limits on losses

Assuring returns or limits on losses misrepresents risk. Distributors must give balanced information and not promise outcomes that depend on markets.

Question 2

A PMS distributor receives commission from the portfolio manager. SEBI expects this commission to be:

  1. Kept confidential from the client
  2. Disclosed to the client and paid on a trail basis
  3. Paid fully upfront at account opening
  4. Deducted directly from the client's capital without disclosure
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Disclosed to the client and paid on a trail basis

Distributors must disclose all commissions to clients, and SEBI requires trail-based payment, discouraging churning and mis-selling driven by upfront payouts.

Question 3

An investor's complaint against a portfolio manager remains unresolved. The investor can next approach:

  1. SEBI's SCORES platform, and then online dispute resolution if needed
  2. The RBI Banking Ombudsman
  3. The local police station only
  4. The stock exchange's listing department
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. SEBI's SCORES platform, and then online dispute resolution if needed

SEBI-registered intermediaries are covered by SCORES, and further escalation is possible through the online dispute resolution (ODR) mechanism. The banking ombudsman covers banks.

Question 4

A distributor recommends a high-risk small-cap PMS strategy to a 72-year-old client needing steady income, because it pays higher commission. This is primarily:

  1. Mis-selling that breaches the suitability obligation
  2. Acceptable, because the client has already read and signed the PMS agreement
  3. Good practice since small caps have high returns
  4. A tax planning strategy
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Mis-selling that breaches the suitability obligation

Recommendations must match the client's profile. Pushing an unsuitable product for higher commission is mis-selling, regardless of the client's signature.

Question 5

Which certification must individuals distributing PMS products generally hold?

  1. NISM Series XXI-A certification
  2. NISM Series V-A only
  3. An IRDAI insurance agent licence
  4. A CA qualification
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. NISM Series XXI-A certification

SEBI requires PMS distributors and their employees engaged in selling PMS to hold NISM Series XXI-A (Portfolio Management Services Distributors) certification.

Question 6

A portfolio manager invests a client's funds in securities of its own associate companies. SEBI requires:

  1. No limits at all, as long as the manager genuinely believes in the associate company's stock
  2. A ban on any investment in listed companies
  3. Only the distributor's approval
  4. Compliance with specified limits and prior client consent
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Compliance with specified limits and prior client consent

Investing in associates creates a conflict of interest. SEBI caps such exposure and requires client consent and disclosure to protect clients.

Question 7

Apart from the principal officer, SEBI requires a portfolio manager to appoint which official to monitor adherence to regulations and report non-compliance?

  1. An independent statutory auditor nominated by the distributor
  2. A client representative elected by all PMS investors
  3. A depository participant appointed for each client account
  4. A compliance officer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A compliance officer

Portfolio managers must appoint a compliance officer, separate from the principal officer, who monitors compliance with the Act, regulations and circulars and reports any non-compliance to SEBI.

Question 8

A distributor shares a client's portfolio statement with another prospect to show 'how well clients are doing'. This breaches:

  1. Exit load rules
  2. Minimum investment rules
  3. Client confidentiality
  4. Net worth requirements
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Client confidentiality

Client information must be kept confidential and used only for the client's service. Showing it to others without consent breaches the code of conduct and data protection norms.
