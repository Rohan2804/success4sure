Operational Aspects of Portfolio Managers: NISM Series XXI-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Operational Aspects of Portfolio Managers, from the NISM Series XXI-A (Portfolio Management Services (PMS) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the operational framework for PMS: the disclosure document, client agreement, KYC and account opening, separate demat and bank accounts, custody, fees and charges (management fee, performance fee with high-water mark, operating expenses, exit load), periodic reporting, audits, direct onboarding without distributors, and closure of accounts. These rules exist to protect clients and ensure transparency, so focus on what must be disclosed and when, and how fees are calculated.

Operational Aspects of Portfolio Managers questions

Question 1

Before signing a PMS agreement, the client must receive the portfolio manager's:

  1. Annual tax return
  2. Scheme information document
  3. Red herring prospectus
  4. Disclosure document
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Disclosure document

The disclosure document describes the manager, services, fees, risks, performance and conflicts. It must be provided to the client before the agreement is executed.

Question 2

Under SEBI norms, a portfolio manager charges a performance fee on a high-water mark basis. This means the fee is charged:

  1. On every positive return in a quarter
  2. On the original investment amount every year, irrespective of the portfolio's returns
  3. Only when the client redeems fully
  4. Only on gains above the highest value the portfolio has previously reached
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Only on gains above the highest value the portfolio has previously reached

The high-water mark ensures clients do not pay performance fees for recovering past losses. Fees apply only to new gains above the previous peak value.

Question 3

A client exits a PMS within the first year. Exit load, if charged, is capped by SEBI at:

  1. 1% of the amount redeemed
  2. 5% of the amount redeemed
  3. 10% of the amount redeemed
  4. 3% of the amount redeemed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 3% of the amount redeemed

SEBI caps PMS exit load at 3% in the first year, 2% in the second, 1% in the third, and nil thereafter. This prevents excessive penalties on exit.

Question 4

Which of the following charges is NOT permitted to be collected from PMS clients?

  1. Management fees
  2. Performance fees
  3. Upfront fees
  4. Exit load within limits
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Upfront fees

SEBI prohibits portfolio managers from charging upfront fees directly or indirectly. Management fees, performance fees and capped exit loads are permitted with disclosure.

Question 5

A client's PMS portfolio started the year at ₹60 lakh and ended at ₹69 lakh (no flows). With a 2% management fee on average assets of ₹64.5 lakh and no performance fee, the management fee is:

  1. ₹1.20 lakh
  2. ₹1.29 lakh
  3. ₹1.38 lakh
  4. ₹0.18 lakh
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹1.29 lakh

Management fee = 2% × ₹64.5 lakh = ₹1.29 lakh. Using opening or closing values would give a different number, so the agreement must specify the fee base.

Question 6

PMS clients can come on board directly with a portfolio manager. The benefit of direct onboarding is that:

  1. The client is exempt from the minimum investment
  2. No distribution commission is charged to the client
  3. The client is guaranteed better returns
  4. The client need not sign an agreement
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. No distribution commission is charged to the client

SEBI requires portfolio managers to offer direct onboarding without distributor involvement, so clients avoid distribution costs. All other requirements still apply.

Question 7

Securities of each discretionary PMS client must be held:

  1. In the portfolio manager's own proprietary demat account, along with its own investments
  2. In a single pooled account shared by all clients
  3. In a separate demat account in the client's name, with a custodian
  4. In physical form with the distributor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. In a separate demat account in the client's name, with a custodian

Each client's securities are kept separately in the client's name and held through a custodian. This protects clients if the manager faces problems.

Question 8

The portfolio manager must send each client periodic reports. These reports typically show:

  1. Only the manager's marketing brochures
  2. Personal details of other clients
  3. Holdings, transactions, fees charged and portfolio performance
  4. Only the stock market's index levels
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Holdings, transactions, fees charged and portfolio performance

Periodic reports provide transparency on what the client owns, trades done, expenses and fees debited, and returns. Other clients' data must never be shared.
