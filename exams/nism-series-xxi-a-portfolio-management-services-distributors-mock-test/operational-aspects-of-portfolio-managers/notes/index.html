Operational Aspects of Portfolio Managers: NISM Series XXI-A revision notes

Operational rules make PMS transparent and protect clients. This chapter covers the disclosure document, client agreement, KYC and account set-up, custody, fees and charges, reporting, audit, direct onboarding and account closure. Fee calculations, especially performance fees with a high-water mark, are frequently tested, so practise with numbers.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Disclosure document

The disclosure document describes the portfolio manager, its investment approaches, risks, fees, performance and conflicts of interest. It is filed with SEBI, certified by an independent chartered accountant and given to the prospective client before signing the agreement. It must be updated when material changes occur.

2. Client agreement

The agreement sets out the service type, investment approach, fees, rights and obligations, reporting and termination terms. It must follow SEBI's minimum contents, and any additional clauses cannot dilute client protections. Clients sign it after reviewing the disclosure document.

3. Accounts and custody

Each client's funds and securities are held in a separate bank account and demat account in the client's name, or a pooled bank account with clear segregation under specified conditions. A custodian holds the securities and settles trades. Client assets cannot be mixed with the portfolio manager's own.

4. Fees and expenses

Fees can include a fixed management fee, a performance-based fee, or a hybrid. Operating expenses other than brokerage are capped as a percentage of average daily AUM. All fees and expenses are charged as per the agreement and shown in client reports, with an illustration of fee calculation in the disclosure document.

5. High-water mark

Performance fees are charged only on gains above the highest net asset value on which a fee was earlier charged. If a portfolio rises from ₹50 lakh to ₹60 lakh (fee charged), falls to ₹55 lakh and then rises to ₹65 lakh, the next performance fee applies only to the ₹5 lakh above ₹60 lakh.

6. Exit load

Portfolio managers may charge an exit load if the client exits early: up to 3% in the first year, 2% in the second year and 1% in the third year, with no exit load after three years (as of 2026; check SEBI circulars). Partial withdrawals are permitted subject to the minimum investment rules.

7. Direct onboarding

Portfolio managers must offer a direct option, where clients join without a distributor and pay no distribution commission. Distributors are paid only through trail commissions, not upfront fees, which reduces incentives for churning.

8. Reporting and audit

Clients receive periodic reports, at least quarterly, covering holdings, transactions, fees, performance and benchmark comparison. Client accounts are audited annually by an independent chartered accountant, and the portfolio manager reports to SEBI regularly.

9. Account closure

On termination, the portfolio manager settles the account, returning funds and transferring securities to the client's own demat account after deducting due fees and charges. Securities can be transferred in kind, avoiding forced sales and immediate tax on gains.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Performance fee base = closing value − max(high-water mark, hurdle-adjusted value), only if positive.

Common traps in the exam

  • Charging performance fees on every gain. Fees apply only above the high-water mark.
  • Assuming the disclosure document is given after signing. It is given before the agreement.
  • Thinking distributors can take upfront commissions. Only trail commissions are allowed.
  • Believing exit loads can apply indefinitely. They are allowed only within the first three years, on a declining scale.

Quick recap

Disclosure document before agreement, filed with SEBI and certified by a CA. Separate demat and bank accounts in client's name; custodian holds securities. Fees: fixed, performance or hybrid; performance fee only above the high-water mark; operating expenses capped. Exit load at most 3/2/1% in years 1–3. Direct option available; trail-only commissions. At least quarterly reporting.
