Investments: NISM Series XXI-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Investments, from the NISM Series XXI-A (Portfolio Management Services (PMS) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This opening chapter explains what investing means, how it differs from saving and speculation, and the broad asset classes available to Indian investors: equity, debt, real estate, gold, cash and alternatives. It covers the risk–return trade-off, the time value of money, compounding, inflation and real returns, and the idea of matching investments to goals. For a PMS distributor, the key takeaway is that every product recommendation must start from the client's objectives, horizon and risk profile. Practise simple compounding and return calculations and be clear on the features of each asset class.

Investments questions

Question 1

₹5 lakh invested at 9% per annum compounded annually will grow in 3 years to approximately:

  1. ₹6.48 lakh
  2. ₹6.35 lakh
  3. ₹6.62 lakh
  4. ₹5.45 lakh
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹6.48 lakh

Future value = 5 × (1.09)³ = 5 × 1.295 = ₹6.48 lakh. Simple interest would give only ₹6.35 lakh, because compounding earns interest on interest each year.

Question 2

Which statement about the risk–return trade-off is generally correct?

  1. Higher-risk assets always deliver higher returns every year
  2. Low-risk assets typically offer the highest long-term returns
  3. Assets with higher expected return usually carry higher risk
  4. Risk and return have no relationship in financial markets
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Assets with higher expected return usually carry higher risk

Investors demand compensation for bearing uncertainty, so riskier assets are priced to offer higher expected returns. Expected does not mean guaranteed: risky assets can underperform for long periods.

Question 3

A client keeps ₹40 lakh in a savings account earning 3% while inflation is 6%. The main risk the client faces is:

  1. Default risk of the bank in the short term
  2. Erosion of purchasing power over time
  3. High volatility in the account balance every day
  4. Reinvestment risk from receiving coupons on bonds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Erosion of purchasing power over time

With returns below inflation, the real value of savings falls each year. Bank deposits have low volatility, and deposit insurance limits default risk for smaller balances, but inflation erodes wealth.

Question 4

Gold is often included in Indian portfolios mainly because it:

  1. Pays a regular dividend like equity shares do
  2. Has historically provided diversification and a hedge in times of stress
  3. Is guaranteed by the government to rise in price every year, making it risk-free
  4. Has no price volatility at all
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Has historically provided diversification and a hedge in times of stress

Gold tends to have low or negative correlation with equities during crises and is seen as a store of value. It pays no income and its price can be volatile.

Question 5

Using the Rule of 72, money growing at 8% per year doubles in about:

  1. 9 years
  2. 8 years
  3. 12 years
  4. 6 years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 9 years

Rule of 72: years to double ≈ 72 ÷ rate = 72 ÷ 8 = 9 years. It is a quick approximation that works well for rates between about 6% and 12%.

Question 6

A 'speculator' differs from an 'investor' mainly because the speculator:

  1. Holds assets only for the long-term income they generate and their underlying fundamentals
  2. Avoids any form of market risk by holding cash
  3. Seeks large short-term gains from price movements, accepting high risk
  4. Buys only government securities to maturity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Seeks large short-term gains from price movements, accepting high risk

Speculation is short-term and based on price movement with high risk. Investing focuses on fundamentals and long-term value creation, often with income.

Question 7

An investor's 'time horizon' is important in asset allocation because:

  1. Short horizons always favour small-cap equity funds
  2. Longer horizons can absorb short-term volatility in growth assets
  3. Time horizon alone decides the tax rate applicable on every investment the client makes
  4. Horizon does not affect the choice of assets
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Longer horizons can absorb short-term volatility in growth assets

With more time, temporary falls in equity have a chance to recover, so long-horizon investors can hold more growth assets. Short horizons call for stable, liquid assets.

Question 8

Real estate as an investment is characterised by:

  1. Daily liquidity at a transparent exchange price
  2. Very small ticket sizes suitable for all investors
  3. No transaction costs on purchase or sale
  4. Low liquidity and large ticket sizes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Low liquidity and large ticket sizes

Physical property is lumpy, slow to sell and involves stamp duty and other costs. REITs offer a more liquid, smaller-ticket route to the same asset class.
