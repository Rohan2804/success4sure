Investments: NISM Series XXI-A revision notes

The PMS Distributors exam opens with the basics of investing that every client conversation depends on. This chapter covers how investing differs from saving and speculation, the main asset classes, the risk-return trade-off, time value of money, compounding and real returns. The key message for a distributor: recommendations start from the client's goals, horizon and risk profile.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Saving, investing and speculation

Saving is setting aside income, often in safe and liquid forms such as bank deposits. Investing is committing money to assets expected to grow or produce income over time, accepting some risk. Speculation is taking high risks for short-term gains, often based on price movements rather than fundamentals.

2. Asset classes

Equity offers ownership and long-term growth with high volatility. Debt offers regular income with lower volatility but interest rate and credit risk. Real estate offers rental income and appreciation but is illiquid and lumpy. Gold is a hedge against crises and currency weakness. Cash offers liquidity and safety but low returns. Alternatives include private equity, hedge strategies and structured products.

3. Risk-return trade-off

Higher expected returns come with higher risk. Over long periods Indian equities have outperformed fixed deposits, but with sharp falls along the way. A client who cannot tolerate a 30% temporary loss should not hold a portfolio that is 100% small-cap equity, whatever its past return.

4. Time value of money

A rupee today is worth more than a rupee tomorrow because it can be invested. Future value = present value × (1 + r)^n. ₹10 lakh invested at 10% a year grows to about ₹25.9 lakh in 10 years. Present value works in reverse, discounting future amounts.

5. Power of compounding

Compounding means earning returns on past returns. The rule of 72 gives the approximate doubling time: at 12% a year, money doubles in about 6 years; at 8%, about 9 years. Longer holding periods magnify the effect, which is why early and patient investing matters.

6. Inflation and real returns

Inflation reduces purchasing power. A fixed deposit earning 7% when inflation is 6% gives a real return of only about 1%, and less after tax. Investors seeking to grow wealth need assets whose returns beat inflation after tax over their horizon.

7. Liquidity and horizon

Liquidity is how quickly an asset can be converted to cash without loss. Matching liquidity to needs is essential: money needed within a year should not be in volatile or locked assets. PMS portfolios suit investors with long horizons and surplus funds, since they hold equity-heavy, concentrated portfolios.

8. Goal-based investing

Investments should be linked to specific goals with amounts and dates: retirement, children's education, a home. Each goal's horizon and importance determine the right asset mix. A distributor who starts with a product rather than the goal risks mis-selling.

9. Risk profiling

Risk profile combines risk capacity (financial ability to bear losses, based on income, wealth, dependants and horizon) and risk tolerance (psychological willingness). A wealthy client with low tolerance should still not be pushed into aggressive strategies.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Future value = PV × (1 + r)^n; rule of 72: years to double ≈ 72 ÷ annual return %.

Common traps in the exam

  • Treating speculation as investing. Speculation chases short-term price moves with high risk.
  • Comparing nominal returns without inflation and tax. Real post-tax returns matter.
  • Using the rule of 72 incorrectly: divide 72 by the rate to get years, not the reverse.
  • Equating high wealth with high risk tolerance. Capacity and tolerance must both be assessed.

Quick recap

Saving preserves, investing grows with risk, speculation gambles on short-term moves. Know each asset class's risk, return and liquidity. Higher return needs higher risk. FV = PV × (1 + r)^n; rule of 72 for doubling. Real return ≈ nominal minus inflation. Start every recommendation from goals, horizon and risk profile.
