Free NISM Series XXI-A practice questions

Question 1 · Investments

₹10 lakh grows to ₹14.64 lakh in 4 years. The compound annual growth rate is approximately:

  1. 10%
  2. 11.6%
  3. 14.6%
  4. 9%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 10%

(14.64 / 10)^(1/4) − 1 = 1.1 − 1 = 10%, since 1.1^4 = 1.4641.

Question 2 · Introduction to Securities Markets

Which entity holds investors' securities in electronic form in India?

  1. Stock exchanges
  2. Depositories such as NSDL and CDSL
  3. SEBI
  4. Registrar of Companies
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Depositories such as NSDL and CDSL

Depositories maintain demat holdings through depository participants.

Question 3 · Investing in Stocks

A company with consistently high return on equity and reinvesting most profits at high rates is typical of a:

  1. Deep-value stock
  2. Growth stock
  3. Distressed stock
  4. Fixed income security
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Growth stock

Growth companies compound earnings by reinvesting at attractive returns.

Question 4 · Investing in Fixed Income Securities

A bond with face value ₹1,000 pays an 8% annual coupon and trades at ₹800. Its current yield is:

  1. 8%
  2. 6.4%
  3. 12.5%
  4. 10%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 10%

Current yield = annual coupon / price = 80 / 800 = 10%.

Question 5 · Derivatives

A call option with strike ₹500 is bought for ₹20. At expiry the stock is at ₹540. The net profit per share is:

  1. ₹40
  2. ₹20
  3. ₹60
  4. ₹520
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹20

Payoff = 540 − 500 = ₹40; minus premium ₹20 = ₹20 profit.

Question 6 · Collective Investment Vehicles

A key difference between PMS and a mutual fund is that in PMS:

  1. Investors buy units of a pooled fund
  2. The minimum investment is ₹500
  3. Securities are held in the client's own demat account, allowing a customised portfolio
  4. There is no fund manager
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Securities are held in the client's own demat account, allowing a customised portfolio

PMS portfolios are individual accounts, unlike pooled mutual fund units.

Question 7 · Role of Portfolio Managers

In a discretionary PMS, the portfolio manager:

  1. Must seek client approval for every trade
  2. Decides investments on the client's behalf within the agreed mandate
  3. Only gives advice without executing
  4. Guarantees a fixed return
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Decides investments on the client's behalf within the agreed mandate

Discretionary mandates give the manager authority to invest without trade-by-trade approval.

Question 8 · Operational Aspects of Portfolio Managers

Before entering into an agreement with a client, a portfolio manager must provide the client with:

  1. The scheme information document of a mutual fund
  2. A guaranteed-return certificate
  3. The PPM of an AIF
  4. The disclosure document
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The disclosure document

The disclosure document gives details of the manager, services, fees, risks and performance.

Question 9 · Portfolio Management Process

The first step of the portfolio management process is:

  1. Selecting stocks
  2. Understanding the client's objectives and constraints and preparing an investment policy statement
  3. Measuring performance
  4. Rebalancing
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Understanding the client's objectives and constraints and preparing an investment policy statement

Planning — defining return goals, risk tolerance and constraints — comes before execution.

Question 10 · Performance Measurement and Evaluation of Portfolio Managers

SEBI requires portfolio managers to report performance using:

  1. Time-weighted rate of return (TWRR)
  2. Simple average of monthly returns
  3. Money-weighted return chosen by each manager
  4. Dividend yield
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Time-weighted rate of return (TWRR)

TWRR removes the effect of client cash flows, allowing comparison across managers.

Question 11 · Taxation

In PMS, capital gains on the sale of securities are taxed in the hands of:

  1. The portfolio manager
  2. The custodian
  3. The distributor
  4. The client
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The client

Securities are owned by the client, so each transaction's tax falls on the client.

Question 12 · Regulatory, Governance and Ethical Aspects of Portfolio Managers

A distributor of PMS products is required to be registered with:

  1. The Association of Portfolio Managers in India (APMI)
  2. RBI
  3. IRDAI
  4. The stock exchange as a sub-broker
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The Association of Portfolio Managers in India (APMI)

SEBI requires PMS distributors to register with APMI and be appropriately certified.

Question 13 · Investments

Which asset class is most suitable for preserving liquidity for an emergency fund?

  1. Small-cap equities
  2. Liquid or overnight debt instruments
  3. Unlisted private equity
  4. Real estate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Liquid or overnight debt instruments

Emergency money needs stable value and quick access, which short-term debt provides.

Question 14 · Introduction to Securities Markets

When a company issues new shares to the public through an IPO, the transaction takes place in the:

  1. Primary market
  2. Secondary market
  3. Money market
  4. Derivatives market
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Primary market

Fresh issuance from the issuer to investors is a primary market activity.

Question 15 · Investing in Stocks

Which analysis studies price and volume charts to identify trading patterns?

  1. Fundamental analysis
  2. Credit analysis
  3. Technical analysis
  4. Actuarial analysis
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Technical analysis

Technical analysis infers future price moves from historical market data.
