Performance Measurement and Evaluation of Portfolio Managers: NISM Series XXI-A revision notes

Clients compare portfolio managers mainly on reported performance, so the way returns are calculated matters. This chapter covers holding period and annualised returns, time-weighted versus money-weighted returns, risk measures, risk-adjusted ratios and SEBI's standardised benchmarking framework for PMS. Calculations are common, and TWRR is a favourite topic.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Holding period and annualised returns

Holding period return measures total return over a period. To compare periods of different length, annualise: a 50% gain over three years is (1.5)^(1/3) − 1 ≈ 14.5% a year. Returns for periods under one year should not be annualised in client reports.

2. Time-weighted return

TWRR breaks the period into sub-periods between cash flows, computes each sub-period return and links them geometrically. It removes the effect of the timing and size of client deposits and withdrawals, so it reflects only the manager's decisions. SEBI mandates TWRR for PMS performance reporting.

3. TWRR example

If a portfolio returns 20% in the first half-year, then the client adds money, and the portfolio returns −10% in the second half, TWRR = 1.20 × 0.90 − 1 = 8% for the year, regardless of how much money was added mid-year.

4. Money-weighted return

MWRR is the internal rate of return of all cash flows, reflecting the investor's actual experience including timing. If the client added a large amount just before the −10% half, the MWRR would be lower than 8%. MWRR is useful for clients but unfair for judging managers who do not control cash flows.

5. Risk measures

Standard deviation measures total volatility. Beta measures sensitivity to the market. Tracking error is the standard deviation of the difference between portfolio and benchmark returns, showing how far the manager deviates from the benchmark.

6. Sharpe and Treynor ratios

Sharpe = (portfolio return − risk-free rate) ÷ standard deviation. Treynor = (portfolio return − risk-free rate) ÷ beta. A PMS with 18% return, 7% risk-free rate and 22% standard deviation has a Sharpe ratio of 0.5. Concentrated PMS portfolios are better judged by Sharpe because they carry significant unsystematic risk.

7. Jensen's alpha and information ratio

Jensen's alpha = actual return − [Rf + β(Rm − Rf)], showing return beyond what market risk explains. Information ratio = (portfolio return − benchmark return) ÷ tracking error, showing how consistently the manager beats the benchmark per unit of active risk.

8. Benchmarking framework

SEBI requires portfolio managers to report performance of each investment approach against a common benchmark for its strategy category (equity, debt, hybrid, multi-asset), chosen from benchmarks notified by APMI. Performance data is published on APMI's platform, allowing like-for-like comparison.

9. Interpreting performance

Past performance does not indicate future results. Distributors should present returns net of fees and expenses, over consistent periods, alongside the benchmark and risk measures, and avoid selective time periods that flatter performance.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • TWRR = (1 + r1) × (1 + r2) × … × (1 + rn) − 1.
  • Information ratio = (Rp − Rb) ÷ tracking error; Sharpe = (Rp − Rf) ÷ σp.

Common traps in the exam

  • Using MWRR to judge a manager. TWRR removes client cash flow effects and is mandated for PMS.
  • Annualising returns for periods under a year in client reports.
  • Comparing a PMS against an unrelated benchmark. Use the prescribed strategy benchmark.
  • Quoting gross returns. Present returns net of fees and expenses.

Quick recap

Annualise with (1 + HPR)^(1/n) − 1. TWRR links sub-period returns and removes client cash flow effects; SEBI mandates it. MWRR is the IRR of the investor's flows. Sharpe uses SD; Treynor uses beta; alpha is excess over CAPM; information ratio is active return ÷ tracking error. Compare with APMI-notified strategy benchmarks, net of fees.
