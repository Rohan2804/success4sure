Performance Measurement and Evaluation of Portfolio Managers: NISM Series XXI-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Performance Measurement and Evaluation of Portfolio Managers, from the NISM Series XXI-A (Portfolio Management Services (PMS) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how PMS returns are calculated and compared. It covers holding period and annualised returns, time-weighted rate of return (TWRR) as mandated by SEBI, money-weighted returns, risk measures (standard deviation, beta, tracking error), risk-adjusted ratios (Sharpe, Treynor, Jensen's alpha, information ratio), and SEBI's standardised benchmarking framework for PMS strategies. Practise calculations and understand why TWRR removes the effect of client cash flows.

Performance Measurement and Evaluation of Portfolio Managers questions

Question 1

SEBI requires portfolio managers to report performance using TWRR mainly because it:

  1. Always shows higher returns than other methods, which makes the manager look better
  2. Removes the effect of client deposits and withdrawals on returns
  3. Includes the client's tax payments in returns
  4. Is easier to compute without any data
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Removes the effect of client deposits and withdrawals on returns

TWRR links returns of sub-periods between cash flows, so the manager is judged on investment decisions, not on when clients added or withdrew money.

Question 2

A portfolio returned +20% in year 1 and −10% in year 2. Its cumulative return over two years is:

  1. 10%
  2. 5%
  3. 8%
  4. 30%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 8%

Cumulative return = 1.20 × 0.90 − 1 = 1.08 − 1 = 8%. Simply adding gives 10%, which overstates the result because losses apply to a larger base.

Question 3

A portfolio earned 15% with beta 0.9. The risk-free rate is 6% and the market returned 14%. Jensen's alpha is:

  1. 1.8%
  2. 1.0%
  3. 9.0%
  4. 0.8%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 1.8%

CAPM expected = 6 + 0.9 × (14 − 6) = 6 + 7.2 = 13.2%. Alpha = 15 − 13.2 = 1.8%. A positive alpha indicates return above what the portfolio's beta would justify.

Question 4

Under SEBI's PMS benchmarking framework, the benchmark for each strategy is chosen from:

  1. Any index the manager prefers, selected after the year ends based on relative performance
  2. The best-performing mutual fund of the year
  3. A set of benchmarks prescribed for the strategy's broad investment approach
  4. The repo rate set by the RBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A set of benchmarks prescribed for the strategy's broad investment approach

SEBI requires managers to choose a Tier-1 benchmark linked to the strategy's investment approach (equity, debt, hybrid or multi-asset) so clients can compare strategies consistently.

Question 5

Two PMS strategies earned 18%. Strategy P had standard deviation 15% and Q had 20%; the risk-free rate is 6%. Which is better on the Sharpe ratio?

  1. Q, with Sharpe 0.90 against 0.80
  2. Both are equal at 1.20
  3. P, with Sharpe 0.80 against 0.60
  4. Q, because higher risk deserves preference
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. P, with Sharpe 0.80 against 0.60

Sharpe P = (18 − 6) / 15 = 0.80; Sharpe Q = (18 − 6) / 20 = 0.60. With equal returns, the strategy with lower volatility delivers more return per unit of total risk.

Question 6

Tracking error of a portfolio measures:

  1. The number of trading errors made by the manager
  2. The variability of its returns relative to its benchmark
  3. The difference between gross and net returns
  4. The total return over one year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The variability of its returns relative to its benchmark

Tracking error is the standard deviation of the difference between portfolio and benchmark returns. Higher tracking error means the portfolio deviates more from its benchmark.

Question 7

Which measure is most appropriate when a PMS portfolio is only a small part of a client's well-diversified wealth?

  1. Sharpe ratio
  2. Maximum drawdown alone
  3. Treynor ratio
  4. Dividend yield
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Treynor ratio

If the client is already diversified, only the portfolio's systematic risk matters, measured by beta. Treynor divides excess return by beta, making it suitable here.

Question 8

A distributor shows a client only the best-performing PMS account of a manager. This is misleading because:

  1. Performance should reflect the strategy's overall results, not selected accounts
  2. Best performance by one account is always repeated by all other accounts in the future
  3. Individual accounts cannot be measured
  4. SEBI forbids any mention of returns
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Performance should reflect the strategy's overall results, not selected accounts

Cherry-picking the best account hides the real experience of most clients of that strategy. SEBI requires strategy-level performance reporting so that prospective clients see representative, comparable results.
