Collective Investment Vehicles: NISM Series XXI-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Collective Investment Vehicles, from the NISM Series XXI-A (Portfolio Management Services (PMS) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter compares the main pooled and managed investment products in India: mutual funds (open and close-ended, active and passive, ETFs), Alternative Investment Funds (Categories I, II and III), Portfolio Management Services, REITs and InvITs, and the newer Specialised Investment Funds. Learners should know the structure, regulator, minimum investment, liquidity, transparency, costs and tax treatment of each, and how to position PMS against these alternatives for clients of different sizes and needs.

Collective Investment Vehicles questions

Question 1

A key structural feature of a mutual fund that is NOT present in a PMS account is that:

  1. Investors' holdings are fully customised to each investor
  2. Investors own units of a pooled scheme rather than securities directly
  3. Securities are held in each investor's own demat account
  4. Each investor receives a separate, individually managed portfolio from the fund manager
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Investors own units of a pooled scheme rather than securities directly

In a mutual fund, money is pooled and investors hold units. In PMS, securities are held in each client's own name and the portfolio can be tailored.

Question 2

REITs in India primarily invest in:

  1. Unlisted start-up equity
  2. Short-term government securities
  3. Agricultural commodity futures
  4. Income-generating commercial real estate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Income-generating commercial real estate

Real Estate Investment Trusts own rent-yielding properties such as offices and malls, and distribute most of their income to unitholders. InvITs do the same for infrastructure assets.

Question 3

Exchange-traded funds differ from regular open-ended index funds mainly because ETFs:

  1. Are always actively managed by star fund managers
  2. Trade on stock exchanges at market prices during the day
  3. Can only be bought from the AMC at end-of-day NAV
  4. Are not regulated by SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Trade on stock exchanges at market prices during the day

ETF units are listed and trade throughout the day like shares, at prices close to their indicative NAV. Index funds are bought and redeemed at end-of-day NAV through the AMC.

Question 4

A client with ₹30 lakh wants professionally managed, diversified equity exposure. Which product is suitable given minimum investment rules?

  1. A discretionary PMS account
  2. A Category III AIF
  3. An equity mutual fund scheme
  4. A Category II private equity AIF
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. An equity mutual fund scheme

PMS requires a minimum of ₹50 lakh and AIFs generally ₹1 crore, so ₹30 lakh is too little for them. Equity mutual funds suit this client with low minimums and daily liquidity.

Question 5

Which is a typical advantage of PMS over mutual funds for a high-net-worth client?

  1. Lower minimum investment and lower costs than mutual funds in all cases
  2. Portfolio customisation and direct ownership of securities
  3. Guaranteed outperformance of mutual funds
  4. Exemption from capital gains tax
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Portfolio customisation and direct ownership of securities

PMS offers tailoring, concentrated portfolios and transparency at the security level. It often costs more, needs a large minimum and carries no guarantee of better returns.

Question 6

Specialised Investment Funds (SIFs) introduced by SEBI are designed to:

  1. Replace all PMS services from 2026
  2. Fill the gap between mutual funds and PMS, with a higher minimum ticket
  3. Offer guaranteed returns to retail investors
  4. Invest only in unlisted real estate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Fill the gap between mutual funds and PMS, with a higher minimum ticket

SIFs are offered by eligible mutual funds under a separate brand, allow more flexible strategies than regular schemes, and have a higher minimum investment than mutual funds.

Question 7

InvITs pay unitholders mainly from:

  1. Profits from trading equity shares
  2. Premiums collected on insurance policies
  3. Interest on credit card loans
  4. Cash flows from infrastructure assets such as roads or transmission lines
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Cash flows from infrastructure assets such as roads or transmission lines

Infrastructure Investment Trusts own operating infrastructure and distribute much of the cash it generates, such as toll or transmission charges, to unitholders.

Question 8

Which collective vehicle typically offers the highest transparency of daily portfolio value to retail investors?

  1. A close-ended Category II AIF
  2. A private real estate fund
  3. An open-ended mutual fund with daily NAV
  4. An unlisted venture capital fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. An open-ended mutual fund with daily NAV

Open-ended mutual funds publish NAV every business day. Close-ended AIFs and private funds value holdings less frequently and report periodically to investors.
