Collective Investment Vehicles: NISM Series XXI-A revision notes

Clients considering PMS will compare it with other managed products, so distributors must know the alternatives well. This chapter compares mutual funds, ETFs, alternative investment funds, PMS, REITs, InvITs and the newer Specialised Investment Funds on structure, regulator, minimum investment, liquidity, transparency, costs and taxation. Expect comparison and positioning questions.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Mutual funds

Mutual funds pool money from many investors into a trust managed by an AMC, regulated by SEBI. Open-ended schemes allow daily purchase and redemption at NAV; close-ended schemes have fixed maturities and list on exchanges. Minimum investment can be as low as ₹100 to ₹500, with high transparency and capped expense ratios.

2. ETFs and index funds

ETFs track an index and trade on exchanges throughout the day; index funds track an index but are bought and redeemed at NAV. Both are passive, low-cost options. They suit clients who want market returns without manager selection risk.

3. Alternative investment funds

AIFs are privately pooled vehicles with a minimum investment of ₹1 crore per investor (lower for employees and certain cases). Category I invests in start-ups, SMEs and infrastructure; Category II includes private equity and debt funds without leverage; Category III uses complex strategies, including leverage and derivatives, such as hedge funds.

4. PMS

PMS offers a separately managed portfolio in the client's own name with a minimum investment of ₹50 lakh (as of 2026). It provides customisation and direct ownership of securities, but higher concentration, higher minimums and fee structures that can include performance fees.

5. Specialised Investment Funds

SIFs, introduced by SEBI in 2025, are offered by eligible mutual fund houses with a minimum investment of ₹10 lakh per investor. They allow strategies such as long-short equity with limited derivative exposure, sitting between mutual funds and PMS or AIFs in risk and flexibility.

6. REITs and InvITs

Real Estate Investment Trusts own income-producing commercial property; Infrastructure Investment Trusts own infrastructure assets like roads and power lines. Both distribute most of their cash flows to unitholders and trade on exchanges. They give access to real assets with smaller amounts than direct ownership.

7. Transparency and liquidity

Mutual funds disclose portfolios monthly and allow daily liquidity in open-ended schemes. PMS clients see their own holdings directly and can exit, though exit loads may apply in early years. AIFs have lock-ins and limited liquidity. REITs and InvITs are liquid only to the extent of exchange volumes.

8. Taxation differences

Mutual funds are pass-through vehicles: investors pay tax only on redemption or dividends. In PMS, each transaction in the portfolio is taxed in the client's hands. Category I and II AIFs have pass-through status for most income; Category III is generally taxed at the fund level. These differences affect post-tax returns.

9. Positioning PMS

PMS suits clients with large investible surplus, long horizons, tolerance for concentration and volatility, and a preference for customised portfolios or direct ownership. For smaller investors or those wanting high liquidity and low cost, mutual funds are usually more suitable.

Common traps in the exam

  • Assuming PMS minimum is the same as AIF. PMS is ₹50 lakh; AIF is ₹1 crore (as of 2026).
  • Treating mutual funds and PMS as taxed alike. MF gains are taxed on redemption; PMS trades are taxed each time in the client's hands.
  • Thinking Category III AIFs are low risk. They can use leverage and complex strategies.
  • Positioning PMS for every wealthy client. Suitability depends on horizon, risk appetite and needs.

Quick recap

Mutual funds: low minimum, daily liquidity, high transparency, pass-through tax. ETFs and index funds are passive. AIFs: ₹1 crore minimum, three categories. PMS: ₹50 lakh, own demat account, customised, trade-by-trade taxation. SIFs: ₹10 lakh, between MFs and PMS. REITs and InvITs give exchange-traded real-asset exposure.
