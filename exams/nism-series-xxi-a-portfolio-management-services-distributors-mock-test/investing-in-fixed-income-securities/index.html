Investing in Fixed Income Securities: NISM Series XXI-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Investing in Fixed Income Securities, from the NISM Series XXI-A (Portfolio Management Services (PMS) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains bonds and other debt instruments: face value, coupon, maturity, yield to maturity, current yield, price–yield relationship, duration and convexity, credit ratings and spreads, and the main types of issuers in India (Government securities, state development loans, PSU and corporate bonds, commercial paper, certificates of deposit). Risks covered include interest rate risk, credit risk, reinvestment risk and liquidity risk. Practise simple yield and duration calculations and be able to explain to clients why bond prices fall when rates rise.

Investing in Fixed Income Securities questions

Question 1

A bond's price falls when market yields rise because:

  1. The issuer stops paying coupons when rates rise
  2. Its fixed coupons become less attractive than new bonds paying higher rates
  3. The bond's face value is reduced by the regulator
  4. Coupon payments become taxable at a higher rate whenever market interest rates rise
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Its fixed coupons become less attractive than new bonds paying higher rates

With yields higher, investors will buy an existing bond only at a lower price so that its yield matches the market. The issuer's obligations remain unchanged.

Question 2

Which bond carries the highest reinvestment risk, other things being equal?

  1. A zero-coupon bond held to maturity
  2. A high-coupon bond paying interest frequently
  3. A treasury bill held to maturity
  4. A low-coupon bond with annual payment
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A high-coupon bond paying interest frequently

Reinvestment risk arises when coupons must be reinvested at possibly lower rates. Bonds paying large, frequent coupons are most exposed; zero-coupon bonds have none if held to maturity.

Question 3

A 1-year bond with face value ₹1,000 and an annual coupon of 7% trades at ₹990. Its yield to maturity is closest to:

  1. 8.08%
  2. 7.00%
  3. 7.07%
  4. 6.00%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 8.08%

At maturity the investor receives ₹1,070 for ₹990 paid. YTM = 1,070 ÷ 990 − 1 = 8.08%. Current yield would be 70 ÷ 990 = 7.07%, which ignores the price gain.

Question 4

A credit rating downgrade of a corporate bond from AA to A will most likely cause:

  1. Its coupon to rise automatically
  2. Its price to rise because risk is higher
  3. Its price to fall as the credit spread widens
  4. No change because ratings do not matter
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Its price to fall as the credit spread widens

A downgrade signals higher default risk, so investors demand a higher yield. With a fixed coupon, the bond's price must fall to deliver that higher yield.

Question 5

Commercial paper in India is best described as:

  1. Short-term unsecured promissory notes issued by companies
  2. Long-term secured bonds issued by the Government of India through RBI auctions
  3. Certificates issued by banks against deposits
  4. Shares issued to employees at a discount
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Short-term unsecured promissory notes issued by companies

CP is a money-market instrument issued at a discount by highly rated corporates for short-term funding. Banks issue certificates of deposit, and the government issues T-bills and G-secs.

Question 6

A bond fund has a modified duration of 3. If yields fall by 1%, the fund's value will change by approximately:

  1. +3%
  2. −3%
  3. +1%
  4. +0.33%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. +3%

Price change ≈ −modified duration × yield change = −3 × (−1%) = +3%. When yields fall, prices rise in proportion to duration.

Question 7

Government securities are considered free of credit risk but still carry:

  1. Interest rate risk
  2. Default risk
  3. Equity market risk
  4. Currency risk on rupee bonds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Interest rate risk

The Government of India is not expected to default on rupee debt, but G-sec prices change with interest rates, and long-dated G-secs can be quite volatile.

Question 8

For two bonds with the same coupon and yield, the one with the longer maturity will have:

  1. Lower duration and less price sensitivity
  2. Exactly the same price sensitivity
  3. No sensitivity to interest rates
  4. Higher duration and greater price sensitivity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Higher duration and greater price sensitivity

Longer maturity means cash flows are received further in the future, raising duration. That makes the bond's price more sensitive to changes in yield.
