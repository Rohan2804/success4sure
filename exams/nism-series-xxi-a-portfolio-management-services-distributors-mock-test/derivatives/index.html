Derivatives: NISM Series XXI-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Derivatives, from the NISM Series XXI-A (Portfolio Management Services (PMS) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces derivatives traded in India — forwards, futures, options and swaps — and their use by portfolio managers for hedging and portfolio rebalancing. Topics include underlying assets, lot size, margins and mark-to-market, cost of carry pricing, option terminology (call, put, strike, premium, intrinsic and time value, in/at/out of the money), basic option strategies and the risks of leverage. PMS can use derivatives only within regulatory limits, so focus on hedging uses and on explaining risks clearly to clients.

Derivatives questions

Question 1

A put option with strike ₹600 when the stock trades at ₹640 is:

  1. Out of the money
  2. In the money
  3. At the money
  4. Deep in the money
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Out of the money

A put is in the money when the market price is below the strike. Here the price is above the strike, so exercising would not be profitable and the put is out of the money.

Question 2

A futures position is marked to market daily, which means:

  1. Gains and losses are settled each day through the margin account
  2. The position can be closed only at expiry
  3. The contract price stays fixed until expiry and there are no interim cash flows at all
  4. Only the buyer pays margins, not the seller
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Gains and losses are settled each day through the margin account

Daily mark-to-market credits gains and debits losses to both parties' accounts, reducing default risk. Positions can be closed any time before expiry.

Question 3

A trader buys a call with strike ₹1,200 for ₹35. At expiry the stock is at ₹1,220. The net result per share is:

  1. Profit of ₹20
  2. Loss of ₹15
  3. Loss of ₹35
  4. Profit of ₹55
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Loss of ₹15

Intrinsic value at expiry = 1,220 − 1,200 = ₹20. Net = 20 − 35 = −₹15. The option is in the money but not by enough to recover the premium.

Question 4

A PMS client holds a diversified equity portfolio worth ₹1 crore with beta 1.0. To temporarily hedge against a market fall, the manager could:

  1. Buy index futures worth ₹1 crore
  2. Buy call options on the index
  3. Sell index futures with notional value close to ₹1 crore
  4. Sell the client's bank fixed deposits
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Sell index futures with notional value close to ₹1 crore

Short index futures gain when the market falls, offsetting losses on the portfolio. Buying futures or calls increases market exposure rather than hedging it.

Question 5

Spot price is ₹2,000, the annual cost of carry is 9% and the contract expires in 2 months. The theoretical futures price is approximately:

  1. ₹2,180
  2. ₹2,015
  3. ₹1,970
  4. ₹2,030
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹2,030

Futures ≈ spot × (1 + r × t) = 2,000 × (1 + 0.09 × 2/12) = 2,000 × 1.015 = ₹2,030. Futures usually trade above spot by the cost of carry.

Question 6

Compared with an option buyer, an option seller (writer):

  1. Pays the premium and has limited loss
  2. Has no obligation if the buyer exercises
  3. Receives the premium but faces potentially large losses
  4. Faces no margin requirements
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Receives the premium but faces potentially large losses

The writer collects the premium upfront and must perform if the buyer exercises. Losses can be large, so exchanges require writers to post margin.

Question 7

The main difference between a forward and a futures contract is that futures are:

  1. Customised and traded privately between two parties
  2. Free of any margin requirement
  3. Settled only by physical delivery of the underlying asset in all cases at expiry
  4. Standardised and traded on an exchange with a clearing guarantee
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Standardised and traded on an exchange with a clearing guarantee

Futures have standard lot sizes and expiry dates and are cleared through a clearing corporation. Forwards are bilateral, customised and carry counterparty risk.

Question 8

Which statement about using derivatives in a PMS portfolio is most accurate?

  1. They can be used to take unlimited leveraged bets on client money if the client agrees
  2. They may be used within SEBI limits, for example for hedging and rebalancing
  3. They are completely prohibited for all portfolio managers
  4. They remove all risk from the portfolio
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. They may be used within SEBI limits, for example for hedging and rebalancing

SEBI permits portfolio managers to use derivatives subject to limits and the client agreement, but not to leverage client portfolios. Hedging reduces, but never eliminates, risk.
