What this chapter covers

This chapter covers equity as an asset class: rights of shareholders, sources of equity returns (dividends and capital gains), and approaches to stock analysis. It explains fundamental analysis using economy, industry and company factors, valuation ratios (P/E, P/B, EV/EBITDA, dividend yield), financial statement metrics (ROE, debt-equity, margins), technical analysis basics, and investment styles such as growth, value, large-cap and small-cap. Practise ratio calculations and learn which ratio is most useful for which type of company.

Investing in Stocks questions

Question 1

A company has net profit of ₹60 crore and shareholders' equity of ₹400 crore. Its return on equity is:

  1. 6.7%
  2. 12%
  3. 15%
  4. 24%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 15%

ROE = net profit ÷ shareholders' equity = 60 ÷ 400 = 15%. It shows how much profit the company generates for each rupee of shareholders' capital.

Question 2

Price-to-book (P/B) ratio is generally most useful for valuing:

  1. Banks and financial companies with large balance sheets
  2. Early-stage software start-ups with few assets
  3. Companies with negative book value
  4. Firms whose value lies mainly in brands
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Banks and financial companies with large balance sheets

For banks, assets and liabilities are mostly financial and marked close to market value, so book value is meaningful. Asset-light or brand-driven firms are poorly captured by book value.

Question 3

An investor who buys stocks trading below their intrinsic value based on low P/E and P/B ratios follows which style?

  1. Momentum investing
  2. Growth investing
  3. Value investing
  4. Index investing
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Value investing

Value investors look for stocks priced cheaply relative to fundamentals, expecting the market to correct. Growth investors pay up for high expected earnings growth.

Question 4

A company has total debt of ₹300 crore and equity of ₹200 crore. Its debt-equity ratio is:

  1. 1.5
  2. 0.67
  3. 2.5
  4. 0.6
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 1.5

Debt-equity = debt ÷ equity = 300 ÷ 200 = 1.5. A higher ratio indicates greater financial leverage and more fixed obligations, which raises risk in a downturn.

Question 5

Equity shareholders have a claim on a company's assets on liquidation that is:

  1. Ahead of all bank lenders
  2. Equal to that of secured debenture holders
  3. Guaranteed at the face value of their shares, ahead of unsecured creditors and suppliers
  4. Residual, after all creditors and preference shareholders are paid
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Residual, after all creditors and preference shareholders are paid

Equity holders are owners and rank last on liquidation. They benefit from unlimited upside but bear the first loss when the company fails.

Question 6

Which economic factor is most likely to hurt the share prices of rate-sensitive sectors like real estate and automobiles?

  1. A fall in crude oil prices
  2. Strong growth in rural incomes
  3. A cut in the repo rate
  4. A sharp rise in interest rates
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A sharp rise in interest rates

Higher rates raise borrowing costs for buyers of homes and vehicles, reducing demand and profits. Rate cuts and rising incomes usually help these sectors.

Question 7

A stock trades at ₹900 with EPS of ₹45; its peer group trades at a P/E of 25. On P/E alone, the stock looks:

  1. Expensive, at a P/E of 30
  2. Cheaper than peers, at a P/E of 20
  3. Fairly valued, at a P/E of 25
  4. Cheaper, at a P/E of 15
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Cheaper than peers, at a P/E of 20

P/E = 900 ÷ 45 = 20, below the peer P/E of 25. A lower P/E may indicate undervaluation, but it can also reflect weaker growth or higher risk, so further analysis is needed.

Question 8

A bonus issue of 1:1 by a company generally results in:

  1. An immediate doubling of the investor's wealth
  2. A cash payment to every shareholder equal to the face value of the shares already held
  3. Reduction in the number of shares held
  4. Double the number of shares, with the share price adjusting roughly by half
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Double the number of shares, with the share price adjusting roughly by half

A bonus issue capitalises reserves into new shares; it does not add value. The price adjusts so the total value of holdings stays roughly the same.
