Taxation: NISM Series XXI-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Taxation, from the NISM Series XXI-A (Portfolio Management Services (PMS) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains the tax treatment of investments held through PMS. Since securities are held in the client's name, each transaction is taxed in the client's hands: short-term and long-term capital gains on listed equity, debt instruments and mutual fund units, dividend income, interest income, STT, and the treatment of PMS fees. It also covers set-off and carry-forward of losses and tax issues for non-resident clients. Tax rates change with Budgets, so learn the principles and confirm current rates before advising.

Taxation questions

Question 1

In PMS, capital gains from each sale in a client's portfolio are taxed:

  1. At the portfolio manager's level as a business
  2. Only when the client closes the account
  3. In the client's own hands, transaction by transaction
  4. At a flat rate on the total portfolio value
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. In the client's own hands, transaction by transaction

The client owns the securities directly, so each sale creates a gain or loss for the client in the year of sale. This differs from a mutual fund, where tax arises on redemption of units.

Question 2

Listed equity shares held for more than 12 months and sold by a PMS client generate:

  1. Short-term capital gains
  2. Business income in all cases
  3. Income from other sources
  4. Long-term capital gains
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Long-term capital gains

For listed equity shares, the holding period threshold for long-term status is 12 months. Gains on shares held for 12 months or less are short-term.

Question 3

A PMS client books a short-term capital loss on listed shares. Under tax rules, this loss can be set off against:

  1. Salary income only
  2. Short-term or long-term capital gains
  3. Rental income only
  4. Only long-term gains in the same year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Short-term or long-term capital gains

Short-term capital losses can be set off against both short- and long-term capital gains, and unused losses may be carried forward for up to eight years if the return is filed on time.

Question 4

A PMS client books a long-term capital loss on sale of listed shares. Under tax rules, this loss can be set off against:

  1. Short-term capital gains as well as salary income in the same year
  2. Only long-term capital gains
  3. Any income, including business and rental income, in the same year
  4. Only short-term capital gains arising from debt mutual funds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Only long-term capital gains

Long-term capital losses can be set off only against long-term capital gains, and unused losses can be carried forward for up to eight years. Short-term losses, by contrast, can be set off against both types of gains.

Question 5

Frequent churning in a PMS portfolio matters for taxes mainly because it:

  1. Reduces the client's tax rate to zero
  2. Creates more short-term gains, which may be taxed at higher rates
  3. Converts all gains into exempt income
  4. Has no tax impact for PMS clients
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Creates more short-term gains, which may be taxed at higher rates

High turnover realises gains sooner and often as short-term gains, which are taxed at higher rates than long-term equity gains. Post-tax returns can fall sharply.

Question 6

A non-resident Indian investing through PMS should note that:

  1. NRIs are fully exempt from Indian capital gains tax on listed shares held through PMS
  2. NRIs cannot invest in Indian PMS at all
  3. Gains are taxed only in the PMS manager's hands
  4. Tax may be deducted at source on capital gains, subject to treaty relief
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Tax may be deducted at source on capital gains, subject to treaty relief

For NRIs, tax is generally withheld at source on capital gains. They may claim relief under applicable tax treaties by providing the required documents.

Question 7

Securities Transaction Tax (STT) in a PMS account is:

  1. A tax on the PMS management fee
  2. Paid on exchange transactions in equity and equity derivatives
  3. A refundable deposit collected by SEBI
  4. Charged only on government bonds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Paid on exchange transactions in equity and equity derivatives

STT is collected by the exchange on specified transactions such as sale and purchase of listed equity and derivatives. It forms part of transaction costs.

Question 8

Why should a PMS distributor avoid giving precise tax rate assurances to clients?

  1. Tax rates and rules change with Budgets and individual situations differ
  2. Because PMS clients never pay tax
  3. Because SEBI fixes tax rates every month
  4. Because taxes do not affect the net returns that PMS clients finally receive
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Tax rates and rules change with Budgets and individual situations differ

Tax laws are amended frequently, and the outcome depends on the client's residency, income and holding periods. Clients should verify current rules with a tax professional.
