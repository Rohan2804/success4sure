Taxation: NISM Series XXI-A revision notes

Because PMS securities are held in the client's own name, every transaction has tax consequences for the client, unlike in a mutual fund. This chapter covers how capital gains, dividends and interest are taxed, the role of STT, the treatment of PMS fees, set-off of losses and issues for non-resident clients. Rates change with Budgets, so learn the principles.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Taxed in the client's hands

Each purchase and sale in a PMS account is the client's own transaction. Gains and losses are computed per security using purchase cost and holding period, and reported in the client's return. High portfolio turnover can therefore create frequent short-term gains and a higher tax bill.

2. Listed equity gains

For listed shares held over 12 months, long-term gains are taxed at 12.5% above an annual exemption of ₹1.25 lakh; short-term gains are taxed at 20% (as of 2026, with STT paid; check current rates). Gains on mutual fund units held in PMS follow the rules for that type of fund.

3. Debt instruments

Interest on bonds is taxed at slab rates. Gains on listed bonds held over 12 months are long-term at 12.5% without indexation, while unlisted bonds and specified debt funds are taxed at slab rates regardless of holding period. Debt PMS strategies should be evaluated on post-tax yield.

4. Dividends

Dividends received in the PMS account are taxable in the client's hands at slab rates, with TDS deducted by the company above the threshold. For high-income clients, high dividend yield strategies may be less tax-efficient than growth strategies.

5. STT and transaction costs

Securities transaction tax is charged on equity trades on exchanges. STT is not deductible when computing capital gains, but brokerage and other transfer costs are generally included in cost or deducted from sale value.

6. Treatment of PMS fees

Whether management and performance fees can be deducted against capital gains has been debated, with tax authorities and tribunals taking different views. Clients should consult their tax adviser. Distributors must not give tax advice beyond general principles.

7. Set-off and carry forward

Short-term capital losses can be set off against both short and long-term gains; long-term losses only against long-term gains. Unabsorbed capital losses can be carried forward for eight years if the return is filed on time. PMS statements help clients track realised gains and losses.

8. Business income risk

Very frequent trading may lead tax authorities to treat gains as business income rather than capital gains. CBDT guidance allows taxpayers to choose capital gains treatment for listed shares held over 12 months, applied consistently.

9. Non-resident clients

NRIs can invest through PMS via the portfolio investment route, with tax deducted at source on capital gains and dividends at applicable rates. Double taxation avoidance agreements may reduce tax, subject to documentation such as a tax residency certificate.

Common traps in the exam

  • Assuming PMS is taxed like a mutual fund. Every trade is taxed in the client's hands.
  • Deducting STT from capital gains. STT is not deductible.
  • Setting off long-term losses against short-term gains. Long-term losses offset only long-term gains.
  • Promising tax benefits on PMS fees. Treatment is debated; refer clients to tax advisers.

Quick recap

PMS gains and income are taxed trade by trade in the client's hands. Listed equity: STCG 20%, LTCG 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh (as of 2026). Debt interest at slab rates. Dividends at slab rates with TDS. STT not deductible. Loss set-off rules apply with eight-year carry forward. Fee deductibility is debated. NRIs face TDS and DTAA rules.
