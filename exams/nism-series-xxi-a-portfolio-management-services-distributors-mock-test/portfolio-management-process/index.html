Portfolio Management Process: NISM Series XXI-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Portfolio Management Process, from the NISM Series XXI-A (Portfolio Management Services (PMS) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the step-by-step process of managing a client's portfolio: understanding the client (objectives, risk profile, constraints), drafting an investment policy, strategic and tactical asset allocation, security selection, execution, monitoring and rebalancing, and reviewing the plan as circumstances change. Constraints include liquidity, horizon, taxes, legal and regulatory factors and unique preferences. Questions often present a client scenario, so practise identifying which step or constraint is relevant.

Portfolio Management Process questions

Question 1

A client tells the portfolio manager she does not want to invest in tobacco or alcohol companies. In an investment policy, this is a:

  1. Liquidity constraint
  2. Time horizon constraint
  3. Unique circumstance or preference constraint
  4. Tax constraint
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Unique circumstance or preference constraint

Ethical or personal restrictions are recorded as unique preferences. Liquidity, horizon and tax constraints relate to cash needs, time and tax status.

Question 2

Tactical asset allocation differs from strategic allocation because it:

  1. Sets the long-term policy mix of assets based on the client's goals and constraints
  2. Never changes the asset weights once set
  3. Is decided by the stock exchange
  4. Makes short-term deviations to exploit perceived market opportunities
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Makes short-term deviations to exploit perceived market opportunities

Strategic allocation is the long-term baseline. Tactical allocation tilts away from it temporarily to take advantage of market views, within agreed ranges.

Question 3

A client's target is 70% equity and 30% debt. After a fall in equity, the mix is 60/40. Rebalancing would involve:

  1. Selling more equity to reduce risk further
  2. Holding the mix at 60/40 permanently
  3. Selling some debt and buying equity
  4. Moving everything into cash
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Selling some debt and buying equity

Rebalancing restores the target by selling the overweight asset (debt) and buying the underweight one (equity). It enforces a disciplined 'buy low, sell high' approach.

Question 4

Which is the best measure of a client's 'risk capacity'?

  1. How excited she gets about stock tips
  2. Her financial ability to absorb losses without affecting key goals
  3. Her preference for well-known brands
  4. The number of trading apps on her phone
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Her financial ability to absorb losses without affecting key goals

Risk capacity is objective: income, wealth, liabilities and horizon. Risk tolerance is the psychological willingness to take risk. Suitability needs both.

Question 5

Monitoring a PMS portfolio is necessary because:

  1. Markets, client circumstances and goals change over time
  2. The initial allocation is guaranteed to remain optimal for the client's whole life
  3. SEBI forbids any change after account opening
  4. Monitoring replaces the need for an investment policy
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Markets, client circumstances and goals change over time

Investment management is a continuous loop. Changes in markets or the client's life — such as retirement or a new liability — may require revisiting the allocation.

Question 6

A client aged 58 plans to retire in 2 years and will need regular income. The most appropriate change to his growth-oriented portfolio is to:

  1. Gradually increase the allocation to stable income-generating assets
  2. Shift entirely into small-cap stocks
  3. Borrow to increase equity exposure
  4. Ignore the coming retirement, since equity markets always rise over the long run
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Gradually increase the allocation to stable income-generating assets

Approaching retirement shortens the horizon and raises income needs. A gradual shift toward income assets reduces sequence-of-returns risk while keeping some growth.

Question 7

Which step usually comes first in the portfolio management process?

  1. Selecting individual stocks
  2. Measuring performance against benchmark
  3. Understanding the client's objectives and constraints
  4. Executing trades on the exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Understanding the client's objectives and constraints

All later steps depend on knowing what the client wants and what limits apply. Security selection and execution follow the investment policy, and evaluation comes last.

Question 8

An investor's liquidity constraint is best illustrated by:

  1. A wish to beat the Nifty 50 every year
  2. A preference for growth stocks over value stocks
  3. Interest in global markets
  4. A need to withdraw ₹15 lakh in 6 months for a home down-payment
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A need to withdraw ₹15 lakh in 6 months for a home down-payment

Liquidity constraints arise from known or likely cash needs. The portion needed soon must be kept in liquid, low-volatility assets.
