Introduction to Securities Markets: NISM Series XXI-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Introduction to Securities Markets, from the NISM Series XXI-A (Portfolio Management Services (PMS) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter describes the structure of Indian securities markets: primary and secondary markets, equity and debt segments, stock exchanges, clearing corporations, depositories and depository participants, brokers, custodians and the role of SEBI as regulator. It explains how trades are executed and settled, the T+1 settlement cycle, dematerialisation and the investor protection mechanisms available. For PMS distributors, understanding market infrastructure helps explain to clients how their securities are held and how trades flow from order to settlement.

Introduction to Securities Markets questions

Question 1

Which institution guarantees settlement of trades executed on a stock exchange in India?

  1. The stock broker of the buyer
  2. The depository participant of the seller
  3. The company whose shares are traded
  4. The clearing corporation acting as central counterparty
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The clearing corporation acting as central counterparty

The clearing corporation becomes the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer (novation), guaranteeing settlement. Brokers and DPs are intermediaries but do not guarantee trades.

Question 2

An investor who wants to hold shares in electronic form must open a demat account with a:

  1. Registrar of companies
  2. Stock exchange directly
  3. Credit rating agency
  4. Depository participant
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Depository participant

Depository participants such as banks and brokers act as agents of NSDL or CDSL and open demat accounts for investors. Exchanges and rating agencies do not hold investor securities.

Question 3

A company sells existing promoter shares to the public in an IPO through an offer for sale. The proceeds go to:

  1. The company, to fund new projects
  2. SEBI, as a listing fee
  3. The selling promoters, not the company
  4. The stock exchange, as transaction charges
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The selling promoters, not the company

In an offer for sale, existing shareholders sell their shares, so they receive the money. Only a fresh issue brings new capital into the company.

Question 4

The main purpose of SEBI's investor protection fund maintained by exchanges is to:

  1. Guarantee investors against any market losses suffered on shares bought through brokers
  2. Pay dividends when companies make losses
  3. Fund new IPOs of small companies
  4. Compensate investors for claims arising from defaulting brokers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Compensate investors for claims arising from defaulting brokers

Investor protection funds compensate eligible investors when a member broker defaults, within set limits. They do not cover losses from price falls.

Question 5

In the debt market, treasury bills are issued by the:

  1. Reserve Bank of India on behalf of the Government of India
  2. State Bank of India for its own funding
  3. SEBI for market stability
  4. Stock exchanges for liquidity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Reserve Bank of India on behalf of the Government of India

T-bills are short-term Central Government securities auctioned by the RBI as the government's debt manager. They are issued at a discount and redeemed at face value.

Question 6

A 'limit order' to buy a share at ₹250 will be executed:

  1. At whatever price the market is at
  2. Only at ₹250 or a lower price
  3. Only at a price above ₹250
  4. Only at the day's closing price
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Only at ₹250 or a lower price

A limit buy order sets the maximum price the buyer will pay. A market order executes at the best available price, which may be higher.

Question 7

Which market segment helps companies raise fresh capital directly from investors?

  1. Primary market
  2. Secondary market
  3. Derivatives market
  4. Currency market
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Primary market

In the primary market securities are issued for the first time and money flows to the issuer. The secondary market provides liquidity for existing securities among investors.

Question 8

Under T+1 settlement, a share sold on Monday (a trading day, with Tuesday also a trading day) will be settled on:

  1. Tuesday
  2. Monday itself
  3. Wednesday
  4. Friday
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Tuesday

T+1 means settlement one trading day after the trade date. A Monday trade therefore settles on Tuesday, assuming it is a working day.
