Introduction to Securities Markets: NISM Series XXI-A revision notes

PMS clients often ask how their securities are held and how trades are executed. This chapter explains the structure of Indian securities markets, the intermediaries involved, how trades flow from order to settlement, and the investor protection mechanisms. A distributor who can explain this clearly builds trust and avoids misunderstandings about custody.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Primary and secondary markets

Primary market issues bring fresh capital to companies through IPOs, FPOs, rights issues and private placements. Secondary markets, mainly NSE and BSE, let investors trade existing securities. PMS portfolios mostly operate in the secondary market but may also apply in IPOs.

2. Equity and debt segments

The equity segment trades shares, ETFs and REIT and InvIT units. The debt segment trades government securities and corporate bonds, though much bond trading is over the counter and reported to exchanges. Derivatives segments trade futures and options.

3. Market infrastructure

Stock exchanges match orders; clearing corporations become counterparty to every trade through novation and guarantee settlement; depositories (NSDL and CDSL) hold securities electronically in demat accounts maintained through DPs. Together they reduce counterparty and custody risk.

4. Brokers and custodians

Brokers execute trades on exchanges for clients, including portfolio managers. Custodians safeguard securities, settle trades and handle corporate actions for institutional clients. A portfolio manager must appoint a custodian, which keeps client securities separate from the manager's own assets.

5. How a PMS trade flows

The portfolio manager places an order through a broker; the exchange matches it; the clearing corporation computes obligations; the custodian settles on the client's behalf; securities are credited to the client's demat account. Contract notes and statements record each step.

6. T+1 settlement

Indian equity trades settle on the next trading day after the trade. Faster settlement reduces risk and frees capital quickly. An optional same-day settlement exists for a limited set of stocks.

7. Dematerialisation

Securities are held in electronic form in demat accounts, eliminating risks of forgery, loss and bad delivery. For PMS clients, holdings sit in a demat account in the client's own name, which they can verify through depository statements.

8. SEBI's role

SEBI regulates exchanges, intermediaries, listed companies and portfolio managers to protect investors and develop the market. It frames rules on disclosure, conduct, settlement, margins and grievance redress.

9. Investor protection

Protections include segregation of client assets, clearing corporation guarantees, investor protection funds at exchanges, the SCORES complaint system, online dispute resolution, and regular statements from depositories that let investors verify holdings independently.

Common traps in the exam

  • Thinking PMS securities are held in the portfolio manager's name. They are held in the client's own demat account.
  • Confusing custodian and broker. Brokers execute; custodians hold and settle.
  • Assuming clearing corporations only record trades. They guarantee settlement through novation.
  • Using T+2 as the current cycle. Indian equities settle on T+1.

Quick recap

Exchanges match, clearing corporations guarantee and settle, depositories hold in demat form. Brokers execute; custodians safeguard and settle for portfolio managers. PMS holdings stay in the client's own demat account. Settlement is T+1. SEBI regulates, and investors are protected through segregation, guarantees, IPFs, SCORES and ODR.
