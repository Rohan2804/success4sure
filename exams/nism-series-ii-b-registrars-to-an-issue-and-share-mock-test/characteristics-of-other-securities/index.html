Characteristics of Other Securities: NISM Series II-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Characteristics of Other Securities, from the NISM Series II-B (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents – Mutual Fund) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

Apart from plain equity and debt, the securities market offers several other instruments. This chapter covers preference shares and their types, warrants, depository receipts, hybrid securities, units of REITs and InvITs, and derivatives such as futures and options. Learners should understand who has a right and who has an obligation in each contract, how preference shares rank against equity, and how instruments like warrants and convertibles may lead to new equity. Focus on comparing features and on applying them to short scenarios, for example identifying whether an option buyer gains when prices rise or fall.

Characteristics of Other Securities questions

Question 1

Preference shareholders have priority over equity shareholders in:

  1. Voting on all company resolutions
  2. Payment of dividend and repayment of capital
  3. Receiving bonus shares and rights issues
  4. Appointing the company's directors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Payment of dividend and repayment of capital

Preference shares get preference over equity in receiving dividends, usually at a fixed rate, and in repayment of capital on winding up. They normally do not carry voting rights on all matters, so equity holders control the company.

Question 2

A company could not pay the dividend on its cumulative preference shares for two years. What happens to this unpaid dividend?

  1. It lapses and is not payable in later years
  2. It is converted into additional equity shares
  3. It is paid to the shareholders out of the Investor Education and Protection Fund
  4. It accumulates and must be paid before any equity dividend
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. It accumulates and must be paid before any equity dividend

For cumulative preference shares, unpaid dividends accumulate as arrears. The company must clear these arrears before it can declare any dividend to equity shareholders. For non-cumulative preference shares, a missed dividend lapses.

Question 3

A warrant issued by a company gives the holder:

  1. An obligation to sell shares to the company at maturity
  2. A fixed interest income for the life of the warrant
  3. The right to buy the company's shares at a set price in future
  4. Ownership of shares from the date of issue itself
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The right to buy the company's shares at a set price in future

A warrant entitles the holder to subscribe to the issuer's shares at a predetermined price within a specified period. The holder is not obliged to exercise it and does not own the shares until exercise. Warrants carry no interest.

Question 4

An investor buys a call option on a stock. Which statement correctly describes her position?

  1. She has the right, but not the obligation, to buy the stock
  2. She is obliged to buy the stock on the expiry date
  3. She has the right to sell the stock to the option writer at the strike price
  4. She receives the premium from the option seller
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. She has the right, but not the obligation, to buy the stock

A call option buyer pays a premium for the right, but not the obligation, to buy the underlying at the strike price. The seller receives the premium and has the obligation. The right to sell belongs to a put option buyer.

Question 5

In a futures contract on a stock, which of the following is true?

  1. Only the buyer has an obligation, the seller has a right
  2. The buyer pays a non-refundable premium to the seller
  3. Neither party has any obligation until the contract expires
  4. Both buyer and seller are obliged to complete the contract
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Both buyer and seller are obliged to complete the contract

A futures contract is a binding agreement in which both parties are obliged to buy or sell the underlying at the agreed price on expiry. There is no premium as in options; instead both parties deposit margins with the exchange.

Question 6

An Indian company issues shares abroad in the form of instruments listed on a US stock exchange. These instruments are called:

  1. American Depository Receipts
  2. Indian Depository Receipts
  3. Foreign Currency Convertible Bonds
  4. Global Treasury Receipts
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. American Depository Receipts

Depository receipts represent shares of a company held with a custodian and are listed abroad. When listed in the US they are called American Depository Receipts. Indian Depository Receipts are issued in India by foreign companies.

Question 7

A Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT) in India mainly invests in:

  1. Housing loans given to individual home buyers
  2. Shares of listed real estate developers only
  3. Completed, income-generating real estate such as office parks
  4. Undeveloped agricultural land, to be sold later when land prices have risen
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Completed, income-generating real estate such as office parks

REITs pool money from investors to own completed, rent-yielding properties, mainly commercial office space, and distribute most of their income. They are regulated by SEBI. They do not lend to home buyers or mainly hold developer shares.

Question 8

An investor buys a put option on an index with a strike price of 22,000. In which situation will the option be profitable on expiry, ignoring the premium?

  1. If the index closes above 22,000
  2. If the index closes below 22,000
  3. If the index closes exactly at 22,000
  4. Whatever the level at which the index closes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. If the index closes below 22,000

A put option gives the right to sell at the strike price. It has value on expiry only if the index is below the strike, because the holder can sell at 22,000 something worth less. Above the strike, the put expires worthless.
