Introduction to Securities: NISM Series II-B practice questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces securities and the structure of the Indian securities market. It covers the difference between the primary market, where issuers raise fresh capital, and the secondary market, where existing securities change hands. Learners should know the main intermediaries such as stock exchanges, clearing corporations, depositories and depository participants, the difference between money market and capital market instruments, and how government securities are issued. Revise the settlement process, the role of dematerialisation and how each intermediary protects investors, since RTA work depends on understanding these basic market linkages.

Introduction to Securities questions

Question 1

A company sells new shares to the public for the first time through an IPO. In which market does this transaction take place?

  1. Secondary market
  2. Money market
  3. Over-the-counter debt market
  4. Primary market
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Primary market

An IPO is a fresh issue of securities by the issuer to investors, so money flows to the company. That is a primary market transaction. Later trading of these shares between investors happens in the secondary market.

Question 2

A company wants to raise funds for ten years to build a new plant. Which instrument suits this need?

  1. A 91-day treasury bill
  2. A 10-year corporate bond
  3. A 3-month commercial paper
  4. A 6-month certificate of deposit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A 10-year corporate bond

Long-term needs are met through capital market instruments, which usually have maturities above one year. A 10-year bond qualifies, while treasury bills, commercial paper and certificates of deposit are short-term money market instruments maturing within a year.

Question 3

Shares held in dematerialised form by investors in India are held in electronic accounts with:

  1. NSDL or CDSL, through a depository participant
  2. The stock exchange where the shares are listed
  3. The registrar of companies of the issuer's state
  4. The company's own bankers as safe custody
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. NSDL or CDSL, through a depository participant

India has two depositories, NSDL and CDSL, which hold securities in electronic form. Investors access them through depository participants such as banks and brokers. Stock exchanges provide trading platforms but do not hold investors' securities.

Question 4

An investor opens a demat account through her bank to hold shares electronically. In this arrangement, the bank acts as a:

  1. Clearing member
  2. Registrar to an issue
  3. Depository participant
  4. Custodian of the depository
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Depository participant

A depository participant is the agent of a depository through which investors open and operate demat accounts. Many banks and brokers are registered DPs. A clearing member settles trades on an exchange, which is a different role.

Question 5

What is the main role of a clearing corporation in the stock market?

  1. Listing new companies after scrutiny of their offer documents
  2. Acting as counterparty to trades and guaranteeing settlement
  3. Holding shareholders' securities in electronic form
  4. Setting the price band of each listed share
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Acting as counterparty to trades and guaranteeing settlement

A clearing corporation clears and settles trades, often becoming the counterparty to both buyer and seller through novation. This guarantees settlement and removes counterparty risk. Holding securities electronically is the depository's job.

Question 6

What is the standard rolling settlement cycle for trades in the equity cash segment in India?

  1. T+2
  2. T+3
  3. T+1
  4. T+5
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. T+1

India moved fully to a T+1 rolling settlement cycle for the equity cash segment in January 2023, so trades settle one working day after the trade date. T+2 was the earlier cycle and is now outdated.

Question 7

Commercial paper is best described as:

  1. A long-term secured bond issued by the central government for infrastructure
  2. A deposit receipt issued by banks against fixed deposits
  3. A share certificate issued by a company in physical form
  4. An unsecured short-term promissory note issued by companies
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. An unsecured short-term promissory note issued by companies

Commercial paper is an unsecured money market instrument issued by highly rated companies to meet short-term funding needs, usually at a discount. Certificates of deposit are issued by banks, and government bonds are long-term instruments.

Question 8

Dated government securities of the Government of India are issued in the primary market through auctions conducted by:

  1. The Reserve Bank of India
  2. SEBI
  3. The National Stock Exchange
  4. The Ministry of Corporate Affairs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The Reserve Bank of India

The RBI manages the public debt of the Government of India and conducts auctions for dated securities and treasury bills. SEBI regulates the securities market but does not issue or auction government securities.
