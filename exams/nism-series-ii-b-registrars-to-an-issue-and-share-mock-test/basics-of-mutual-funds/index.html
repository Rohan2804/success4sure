Basics of Mutual Funds: NISM Series II-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Basics of Mutual Funds, from the NISM Series II-B (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents – Mutual Fund) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces the mutual fund as a pooled investment vehicle. Learners should understand how investors' money is pooled and managed by professionals, how net asset value is computed, and how NAV changes with market prices, expenses and income distributions. Revise the difference between open-ended, close-ended and interval schemes, direct and regular plans, and the main benefits and limitations of mutual funds such as diversification, liquidity, transparency, costs and absence of guaranteed returns. Practise NAV and simple return calculations, and be clear about why an IDCW payout reduces the NAV.

Basics of Mutual Funds questions

Question 1

On a given day a debt fund reports net assets of ₹245 crore, calculated after deducting ₹5 crore of payables from its ₹250 crore holdings. If 10 crore units are in issue, the NAV works out to:

  1. ₹25.00
  2. ₹24.50
  3. ₹25.50
  4. ₹24.00
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹24.50

NAV per unit equals net assets divided by units outstanding. Net assets are ₹250 crore minus ₹5 crore, which is ₹245 crore, and ₹245 crore ÷ 10 crore units gives ₹24.50. Ignoring or adding the liabilities gives the wrong values.

Question 2

An investor bought units at an NAV of ₹10 and the NAV is now ₹12.50. Ignoring loads and IDCW, what is her absolute return?

  1. 2.5%
  2. 20%
  3. 125%
  4. 25%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 25%

Absolute return is (current NAV minus purchase NAV) divided by purchase NAV. Here (12.50 − 10) ÷ 10 = 0.25, or 25%. The 20% figure wrongly divides the gain by the current NAV instead of the purchase NAV.

Question 3

In an open-ended scheme, investors can:

  1. Buy units only during the new fund offer
  2. Redeem units only on the scheme's maturity date
  3. Buy and redeem units from the fund on an ongoing basis
  4. Trade units only with other investors on stock exchanges during market hours
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Buy and redeem units from the fund on an ongoing basis

Open-ended schemes issue and redeem units on a continuous basis at NAV-based prices, so there is no fixed maturity. Close-ended schemes accept money only during the NFO and are listed for liquidity until maturity.

Question 4

An interval scheme is one that:

  1. Is open for purchase and redemption only during specified intervals
  2. Pays IDCW at fixed intervals of one month
  3. Invests only in securities with fixed maturity intervals
  4. Switches automatically between equity and debt
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Is open for purchase and redemption only during specified intervals

An interval scheme combines features of open and close-ended schemes. It is open for purchases and redemptions only during specified transaction periods and is otherwise closed. It must also be listed on an exchange.

Question 5

A scheme's NAV is ₹20 just before it pays an IDCW of ₹1 per unit. Assuming no other change, the NAV immediately after the payout will be about:

  1. ₹19
  2. ₹20
  3. ₹21
  4. ₹18
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹19

An IDCW is paid out of the scheme's assets, so the NAV falls by the amount distributed. ₹20 minus ₹1 leaves about ₹19. The investor's total wealth is unchanged before tax, as the payout simply moves value from the NAV to her bank account.

Question 6

Two plans of the same scheme hold the same portfolio. Why does the direct plan usually have a higher NAV than the regular plan over time?

  1. It invests in higher-return securities that the regular plan is not permitted to hold
  2. It has a lower expense ratio as no distributor commission is paid
  3. It is exempt from tax on capital gains and dividends
  4. It is managed by a more experienced fund manager
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. It has a lower expense ratio as no distributor commission is paid

Direct and regular plans share one portfolio and one fund manager. The direct plan carries no distribution commission, so its expense ratio is lower and less is deducted from its NAV, which therefore grows faster over time.

Question 7

Which of the following is a limitation of investing through mutual funds?

  1. Investors cannot redeem units from open-ended schemes
  2. Investors cannot see the scheme's portfolio holdings
  3. Investors cannot choose the individual securities held in the portfolio
  4. Investors cannot invest small amounts through SIPs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Investors cannot choose the individual securities held in the portfolio

In a mutual fund, the fund manager decides the portfolio within the scheme's objective, so investors cannot customise holdings. Open-ended schemes are liquid, portfolios are disclosed regularly, and SIPs allow small investments.

Question 8

The total expense ratio (TER) of a scheme refers to:

  1. The exit load as a percentage of redemption value
  2. The stamp duty as a percentage of purchase amount
  3. The fund manager's salary expressed as a share of the AMC's annual profit after tax
  4. Annual scheme expenses as a percentage of its average net assets
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Annual scheme expenses as a percentage of its average net assets

TER expresses the scheme's recurring expenses, such as management fees and distribution costs, as an annual percentage of average net assets. It is accrued daily in the NAV. Exit load and stamp duty are transaction charges, not part of TER.
