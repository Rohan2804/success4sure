SEBI – Role and Regulations: NISM Series II-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SEBI – Role and Regulations, from the NISM Series II-B (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents – Mutual Fund) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter deals with the Securities and Exchange Board of India, the regulator of the securities market including mutual funds and RTAs. Learners should know how SEBI was set up, its objectives of protecting investors, developing the market and regulating intermediaries, and the powers it has to register, inspect, investigate and penalise. Revise the appellate route through the Securities Appellate Tribunal, the investor grievance mechanisms such as SCORES and the online dispute resolution platform, and the role of industry bodies like AMFI, which are not regulators. Questions often ask which body handles which function.

SEBI – Role and Regulations questions

Question 1

SEBI was first set up in 1988 and was given statutory powers through:

  1. The Companies Act, 1956
  2. The SEBI Act, 1992
  3. The Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956
  4. The Depositories Act, 1996
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The SEBI Act, 1992

SEBI started as a non-statutory body in 1988 and became a statutory regulator when the SEBI Act was passed in 1992. The Act sets out its powers and functions. The other Acts deal with companies, contracts and depositories.

Question 2

The preamble of the SEBI Act states SEBI's purpose as:

  1. Fixing interest rates and controlling money supply in the economy
  2. Collecting taxes on capital gains from securities transactions
  3. Protecting investors and developing and regulating the securities market
  4. Guaranteeing returns to investors in listed securities
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Protecting investors and developing and regulating the securities market

SEBI's three core objectives are to protect the interests of investors in securities, to promote the development of the securities market and to regulate it. Monetary policy is the RBI's job, and taxation belongs to the income tax department.

Question 3

A mutual fund distributor is aggrieved by a penalty order passed by SEBI. To which body can the distributor appeal?

  1. Securities Appellate Tribunal
  2. National Company Law Tribunal
  3. AMFI's disciplinary committee
  4. The Reserve Bank of India
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Securities Appellate Tribunal

Appeals against orders of SEBI lie with the Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT). NCLT deals with company law matters, AMFI is an industry association and the RBI is the banking regulator; none of them hears appeals against SEBI.

Question 4

A party wants to challenge a decision of the Securities Appellate Tribunal. The next appeal lies with:

  1. SEBI's whole-time members
  2. The Ministry of Finance
  3. The relevant stock exchange
  4. The Supreme Court of India
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The Supreme Court of India

Under the SEBI Act, an appeal against an order of the Securities Appellate Tribunal lies with the Supreme Court. SEBI itself cannot review SAT orders, and neither the Ministry of Finance nor stock exchanges have appellate powers here.

Question 5

Which of the following best describes the Association of Mutual Funds in India (AMFI)?

  1. The statutory regulator of mutual funds under the SEBI Act
  2. An industry body of SEBI-registered AMCs that promotes ethical practices
  3. A government department under the Finance Ministry that approves new mutual fund schemes
  4. A depository that holds mutual fund units in electronic form
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. An industry body of SEBI-registered AMCs that promotes ethical practices

AMFI is an association of SEBI-registered asset management companies. It promotes professional and ethical standards, registers distributors through ARN and runs investor awareness programmes. SEBI, not AMFI, is the regulator that approves and regulates schemes.

Question 6

An investor's complaint against an RTA was closed on SCORES, but she is still not satisfied. Under SEBI's framework, she can next:

  1. File a case directly with the Securities Appellate Tribunal
  2. Ask the stock exchange to cancel the RTA's registration
  3. Complain to the RBI banking ombudsman
  4. Take the dispute to the online dispute resolution platform
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Take the dispute to the online dispute resolution platform

SEBI's framework lets investors who are not satisfied with the resolution through the intermediary or SCORES take the dispute to the online dispute resolution (ODR) platform, which uses conciliation and online arbitration. SAT hears appeals against SEBI orders, not such disputes.

Question 7

Which of these is a power available to SEBI under the SEBI Act?

  1. Granting banking licences to new private banks
  2. Fixing the face value of shares of every company
  3. Conducting inspections and investigations of intermediaries
  4. Deciding the income tax rate on dividends
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Conducting inspections and investigations of intermediaries

SEBI can call for information, inspect books, investigate intermediaries such as RTAs and AMCs, and impose penalties. Banking licences are issued by the RBI, face value is decided by companies within the law, and tax rates are set by Parliament.

Question 8

SEBI regulations aimed at preventing persons from dealing in securities on the basis of unpublished price-sensitive information relate to:

  1. Insider trading
  2. Takeovers
  3. Delisting
  4. Buyback of securities
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Insider trading

The SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations prohibit trading while in possession of unpublished price-sensitive information. Takeover, delisting and buyback regulations deal with acquisitions, removing shares from exchanges and companies purchasing their own shares.
