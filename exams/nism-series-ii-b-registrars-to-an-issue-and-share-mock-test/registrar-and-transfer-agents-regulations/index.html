Registrar and Transfer Agents Regulations: NISM Series II-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Registrar and Transfer Agents Regulations, from the NISM Series II-B (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents – Mutual Fund) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the regulatory framework for RTAs. Registrars to an issue and share transfer agents must be registered with SEBI and follow the regulations that govern them along with the common provisions for intermediaries. Learners should understand the categories of registration, eligibility and fit-and-proper criteria, the code of conduct, the duties to maintain records and redress grievances, the role of the compliance officer, and the consequences of default such as suspension or cancellation. Also revise obligations under anti-money laundering law and the enhanced requirements placed on large, systemically important RTAs. Concepts matter more than numerical limits here.

Registrar and Transfer Agents Regulations questions

Question 1

Under the RTA regulations, a Category II registrant may act as:

  1. Both a registrar to an issue and a share transfer agent, for listed and unlisted companies
  2. A registrar only for government securities
  3. A share transfer agent only for its group companies
  4. Either a registrar to an issue or a share transfer agent, but not both
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Either a registrar to an issue or a share transfer agent, but not both

Category I permits an entity to act as both registrar to an issue and share transfer agent. Category II permits it to act in only one of the two capacities. There is no category limited to government securities or group companies.

Question 2

Before an entity can act as a registrar or share transfer agent for securities, it must:

  1. Obtain a licence from the RBI
  2. Become a member of a stock exchange
  3. Obtain a certificate of registration from SEBI
  4. Register as a distributor with AMFI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Obtain a certificate of registration from SEBI

Acting as a registrar to an issue or share transfer agent requires a certificate of registration from SEBI under the relevant regulations. RBI licences, exchange membership and AMFI registration apply to other activities.

Question 3

Common requirements for registration and the 'fit and proper person' criteria applicable to RTAs are laid down in:

  1. SEBI (Intermediaries) Regulations, 2008
  2. SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996
  3. SEBI (Depositories and Participants) Regulations, 2018
  4. Indian Trusts Act, 1882
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. SEBI (Intermediaries) Regulations, 2008

The SEBI (Intermediaries) Regulations, 2008 contain common provisions for all intermediaries, including RTAs, such as the fit and proper criteria and the procedure for action in case of default. The other laws govern mutual funds, depositories and trusts.

Question 4

An RTA appoints a compliance officer. This officer is mainly responsible for:

  1. Managing the investment portfolio of the mutual fund schemes serviced by the RTA
  2. Monitoring compliance with SEBI rules and redressal of investor grievances
  3. Approving the RTA's annual budget and salaries
  4. Marketing the RTA's services to new AMC clients
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Monitoring compliance with SEBI rules and redressal of investor grievances

The compliance officer monitors the RTA's compliance with the SEBI Act, regulations and circulars, reports any non-compliance and oversees the redressal of investor grievances. Portfolio management belongs to the AMC, and budgets and marketing are business functions.

Question 5

As an intermediary, an RTA has obligations under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act. Which of these is one of them?

  1. Fixing the minimum investment amount for schemes
  2. Reporting suspicious transactions to FIU-IND
  3. Approving the KYC of foreign portfolio investors for SEBI
  4. Collecting income tax on capital gains from investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Reporting suspicious transactions to FIU-IND

Under the PMLA and SEBI's AML guidelines, intermediaries including RTAs must monitor transactions, maintain records and report suspicious transactions to the Financial Intelligence Unit-India. They do not set scheme minimums or collect capital gains tax.

Question 6

An RTA repeatedly fails to resolve investor complaints and ignores SEBI's directions. What action can SEBI take under the regulations?

  1. Transfer the RTA's shares to AMFI
  2. Order the AMCs to merge with the RTA
  3. Only issue an advisory without any penalty
  4. Suspend or cancel the RTA's registration after due process
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Suspend or cancel the RTA's registration after due process

SEBI can take action against an RTA that violates regulations, including monetary penalties, suspension or cancellation of its certificate of registration, after following the procedure in the Intermediaries Regulations. It cannot transfer ownership to AMFI.

Question 7

Which of the following is expected of an RTA under its code of conduct?

  1. Exercising due diligence and not misrepresenting facts to investors or clients
  2. Giving preference to large investors when processing requests
  3. Recommending schemes of AMCs that pay the RTA higher fees
  4. Sharing investor data with group companies so that they can cross-sell other products
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Exercising due diligence and not misrepresenting facts to investors or clients

The code of conduct requires an RTA to act with integrity, due diligence and fairness, treat all investors equally and keep information confidential. Favouring large investors, product pushing or sharing data for marketing would breach the code.

Question 8

SEBI has designated certain RTAs as Qualified RTAs (QRTAs). Such RTAs are identified on the basis of:

  1. Being owned by a public sector bank
  2. Being listed on a stock exchange for three years
  3. The large number of folios they service, making them systemically important
  4. Having only one AMC as a client
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The large number of folios they service, making them systemically important

QRTAs are RTAs that service a very large number of folios and are therefore systemically important. SEBI places enhanced obligations on them, covering governance, risk management, systems audit and business continuity. Ownership or listing is not the criterion.
