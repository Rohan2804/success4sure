Financial Transactions: NISM Series II-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Financial Transactions, from the NISM Series II-B (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents – Mutual Fund) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the financial transactions an RTA processes, such as purchases, redemptions, switches, systematic plans and IDCW payouts or reinvestment. Learners should be able to compute units allotted, redemption amounts after exit load, and units allotted on IDCW reinvestment. Revise the first-in-first-out principle for loads and holding periods, the treatment of switches, payment timelines and the interest payable for delays, and SIP modifications such as pausing or top-ups. Questions are often numerical or scenario-based, so practise working through each step and check how loads and timelines affect the final amount.

Financial Transactions questions

Question 1

An investor redeems 300 units at an NAV of ₹45. A 1% exit load applies. What amount will she receive, ignoring taxes?

  1. ₹13,365
  2. ₹13,500
  3. ₹13,455
  4. ₹13,635
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹13,365

Redemption value is 300 × ₹45 = ₹13,500. A 1% exit load is ₹135, so the investor receives ₹13,365. Adding the load instead of deducting it, or ignoring it altogether, gives the incorrect values.

Question 2

An investor wants to redeem ₹10,000 from a scheme whose NAV is ₹25, with no exit load. How many units will be redeemed?

  1. 250
  2. 400
  3. 2,500
  4. 4,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 400

For an amount-based redemption, units redeemed equal the amount divided by NAV. ₹10,000 ÷ ₹25 = 400 units. The RTA reduces the folio balance by 400 units and pays ₹10,000 to the registered bank account.

Question 3

An investor holds 1,000 units under the IDCW reinvestment option. The scheme declares ₹2 per unit and the ex-IDCW NAV is ₹20. Ignoring tax deduction, how many additional units will be allotted?

  1. 200
  2. 50
  3. 2,000
  4. 100
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 100

IDCW payable is 1,000 × ₹2 = ₹2,000. Under the reinvestment option this is used to buy units at the ex-IDCW NAV of ₹20, giving ₹2,000 ÷ ₹20 = 100 units. In practice, TDS may reduce the amount reinvested.

Question 4

An investor bought units in a scheme on several dates and now redeems part of her holding. For computing exit load, units are generally treated as redeemed:

  1. On a last-in-first-out basis
  2. In the order chosen by the distributor
  3. On a first-in-first-out basis
  4. At the average purchase date of all units
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. On a first-in-first-out basis

Mutual funds apply the first-in-first-out (FIFO) method, so the oldest units are redeemed first. Exit load applies only to those redeemed units that fall within the load period. The same principle is used for capital gains holding periods.

Question 5

An investor switches units from an equity scheme into a debt scheme of the same AMC within the exit load period of the equity scheme. Which statement is correct?

  1. Exit load of the equity scheme may apply on the switch-out
  2. No load applies because the AMC is the same
  3. Load is charged on the switch-in to the debt scheme instead of on the switch-out
  4. The switch is not allowed during the load period
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Exit load of the equity scheme may apply on the switch-out

A switch is a redemption from one scheme and a purchase in another. So the exit load of the source scheme can apply to units switched out within its load period, unless the scheme documents waive it. There is no entry load on the switch-in.

Question 6

If an AMC fails to transfer redemption proceeds within the time allowed by SEBI, it must pay the investor interest at the rate of:

  1. 6% per annum for the delay
  2. 15% per annum for the delay
  3. The bank's savings account rate
  4. No interest, only an apology letter
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 15% per annum for the delay

SEBI regulations require the AMC to pay interest at 15% per annum for the period of delay beyond the stipulated time for transfer of redemption or repurchase proceeds. This interest is borne by the AMC, not the scheme.

Question 7

An investor facing a temporary cash crunch wants to skip a few SIP instalments without cancelling the SIP. Which facility can she use, where the AMC offers it?

  1. Systematic transfer plan
  2. Switch to IDCW option
  3. SIP pause
  4. Change of nominee
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. SIP pause

Many AMCs offer an SIP pause facility, allowing investors to suspend instalments for a specified number of months, after which the SIP resumes automatically. STP and IDCW options serve different purposes, and nomination has no effect on SIP debits.

Question 8

An investor sets up an SIP with a 'top-up' feature. This means:

  1. Additional units are allotted free when NAV falls
  2. The SIP continues even after the bank mandate expires
  3. Units are bought only when the market is rising
  4. The SIP amount increases periodically by a specified amount or percentage
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The SIP amount increases periodically by a specified amount or percentage

An SIP top-up, also called step-up, increases the instalment amount at chosen intervals, usually yearly, by a fixed amount or percentage. It helps investments keep pace with rising income. It does not give free units or depend on market direction.
