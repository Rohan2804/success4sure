Operational Concepts of Mutual Funds: NISM Series II-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Operational Concepts of Mutual Funds, from the NISM Series II-B (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents – Mutual Fund) syllabus.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the operational rules that decide how transactions are priced and processed. Learners should know the cut-off timings for different scheme types, how the applicable NAV is fixed for purchases and redemptions, and the special rules for liquid and overnight funds. Revise loads, including the abolition of entry load and the crediting of exit load to the scheme, stamp duty on purchases, the computation of units allotted, NFO pricing and the daily accrual of expenses in the NAV. Practise unit and amount calculations with stamp duty and exit load, as these are common numerical questions.

Operational Concepts of Mutual Funds questions

Question 1

For a purchase in an equity scheme, where the funds are available for use before the cut-off, what is the cut-off time to get the same day's NAV?

  1. 3:00 p.m.
  2. 1:30 p.m.
  3. 12:00 noon
  4. 5:00 p.m.
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 3:00 p.m.

For equity and most debt schemes other than liquid and overnight funds, the cut-off is 3:00 p.m. If the application is received and funds are realised before then, the same day's NAV applies. The 1:30 p.m. cut-off applies to liquid and overnight funds.

Question 2

An investor submits a redemption request for an equity scheme at 2:30 p.m. on a business day. Which NAV applies?

  1. The NAV of the previous business day
  2. The NAV of the next business day
  3. The NAV of the same business day
  4. The average NAV of the week
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The NAV of the same business day

For redemptions in equity schemes, requests received before the 3:00 p.m. cut-off get the same day's closing NAV. Unlike purchases, redemption does not depend on realisation of funds, so time of receipt alone decides the NAV.

Question 3

An investor's SIP of ₹20,000 goes into a scheme with an NAV of ₹40. After stamp duty of 0.005%, how many units are allotted?

  1. 500.000
  2. 499.975
  3. 499.950
  4. 499.995
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 499.975

Stamp duty is 0.005% of ₹20,000, which is ₹1, so ₹19,999 is invested. Dividing by the NAV of ₹40 gives 499.975 units. Ignoring stamp duty would wrongly give 500 units.

Question 4

The exit load collected by a scheme when investors redeem units is:

  1. Kept by the AMC as its income
  2. Paid to the distributor as commission
  3. Transferred to SEBI's investor protection fund
  4. Credited back to the scheme, net of GST
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Credited back to the scheme, net of GST

SEBI requires exit load, net of GST, to be credited to the scheme rather than kept by the AMC. This benefits the remaining investors who bear the cost of redemptions. Distributor commission comes from scheme expenses, not exit load.

Question 5

Which statement about entry load in Indian mutual funds is correct?

  1. Entry load of up to 2.25% is allowed on equity schemes
  2. SEBI has abolished entry load on all schemes
  3. Entry load is charged only on regular plans
  4. Entry load applies only to SIP instalments
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. SEBI has abolished entry load on all schemes

SEBI abolished entry loads in 2009, so no load can be charged at the time of purchase. Distributors are paid commission by the AMC out of scheme expenses in regular plans, and investors in direct plans pay no commission.

Question 6

A purchase request for a liquid fund, with funds credited in time, is received at 2:00 p.m. on a Wednesday that is a business day. Which NAV applies?

  1. Wednesday's NAV
  2. Thursday's NAV
  3. Tuesday's NAV
  4. Friday's NAV
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Wednesday's NAV

For liquid funds, applications received after the 1:30 p.m. cut-off get the closing NAV of the day immediately preceding the next business day. As Thursday is the next business day, the NAV of Wednesday, the preceding day, applies.

Question 7

How are the recurring expenses of a scheme reflected for investors?

  1. They are billed to investors separately every quarter
  2. They are deducted only from redemption proceeds
  3. They are charged once a year on the anniversary of investment
  4. They are accrued daily and deducted while computing the NAV
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. They are accrued daily and deducted while computing the NAV

Recurring expenses within the TER limit are charged to the scheme and accrued every day in the NAV. Investors therefore do not receive separate bills; the published NAV is already net of expenses. Exit load, if any, is deducted on redemption.

Question 8

During a new fund offer, units of an open-ended scheme are usually allotted at:

  1. The NAV of an existing scheme of the same AMC
  2. A price decided by bidding among investors
  3. The face value of the units, commonly ₹10
  4. A premium of 10% to the face value
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The face value of the units, commonly ₹10

In an NFO, units are generally offered at face value, typically ₹10, because the scheme has no portfolio yet. After the scheme reopens for continuous transactions, purchases and redemptions take place at the prevailing NAV.
