Investors in Mutual Funds: NISM Series II-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Investors in Mutual Funds, from the NISM Series II-B (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents – Mutual Fund) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the types of investors who can invest in mutual funds and the documents and rules that apply to each. Learners should know the eligibility of resident individuals, minors through guardians, HUFs, non-individuals such as companies, trusts and societies, and NRIs and other foreign investors, with the difference between repatriable and non-repatriable investment. Revise modes of holding, KYC through KYC registration agencies, PAN-exempt KYC for small investments, and FATCA/CRS self-certification. Focus on the documents an RTA must check before accepting an application and on what changes when a minor or NRI's status changes.

Investors in Mutual Funds questions

Question 1

A Hindu Undivided Family wishes to invest in a mutual fund. The investment is made:

  1. In the name of each coparcener separately
  2. Only through a registered trust of the family
  3. In the name of the eldest son as guardian
  4. In the name of the HUF, through its Karta
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. In the name of the HUF, through its Karta

An HUF can invest in mutual funds, with the Karta signing and operating the investment on its behalf. KYC is done for the HUF and the Karta. There is no need to form a trust or invest separately for each coparcener.

Question 2

Who can act as guardian for a minor's mutual fund folio?

  1. Any adult relative nominated by the minor
  2. A natural guardian (parent) or a court-appointed legal guardian
  3. The distributor who sources the investment
  4. Any person who funds the investment
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A natural guardian (parent) or a court-appointed legal guardian

SEBI's norms allow either a natural guardian, that is, a parent, or a court-appointed legal guardian to operate a minor's folio. Proof of the relationship or the court order must be provided. Relatives or distributors cannot act as guardian merely by funding the investment.

Question 3

An NRI invests in a mutual fund using money from her NRO account. How is this investment treated?

  1. On a fully repatriable basis
  2. As a foreign portfolio investment
  3. On a non-repatriable basis
  4. As a deposit under FEMA
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. On a non-repatriable basis

Money from an NRO account represents income earned in India, so investments from it are generally non-repatriable, with limited repatriation allowed under RBI rules. Investments from NRE or FCNR accounts are repatriable.

Question 4

A folio is held jointly by two investors with the mode of holding 'joint'. What is required to redeem units?

  1. Signatures of both holders
  2. Signature of the first holder only
  3. Signature of either holder
  4. Signature of the nominee
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Signatures of both holders

In 'joint' mode, all holders must sign for transactions such as redemption. In 'anyone or survivor' mode, any one holder can transact. Nominees have no rights while the holders are alive.

Question 5

A private limited company wants to invest its surplus funds in a liquid fund. Which document is typically required along with KYC?

  1. A no-objection certificate from SEBI
  2. An approval letter from the company's bankers
  3. A board resolution authorising the investment and signatories
  4. A certificate from the registrar of companies for each investment
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A board resolution authorising the investment and signatories

Non-individual investors such as companies must submit a board resolution authorising the investment and naming authorised signatories, along with entity KYC. No SEBI, bank or ROC approval is needed for each investment.

Question 6

An investor completed KYC through one AMC. When she invests with a different AMC, what happens?

  1. Her KYC record with the KRA can be used, so full KYC need not be repeated
  2. She must complete fresh in-person verification with the new AMC before her first investment
  3. She must obtain a KYC transfer letter from SEBI
  4. She can invest only in the same category of schemes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Her KYC record with the KRA can be used, so full KYC need not be repeated

KYC details are uploaded to a KYC registration agency, and other SEBI-registered intermediaries can access and rely on that record. This avoids repeating KYC. Her KYC should be in a validated status for smooth processing.

Question 7

FATCA and CRS self-certification is collected from mutual fund investors mainly to:

  1. Verify their income for suitability of schemes
  2. Check whether they hold a demat account
  3. Calculate stamp duty on their purchases
  4. Identify their tax residency for reporting accounts of foreign tax residents
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Identify their tax residency for reporting accounts of foreign tax residents

Under FATCA and the Common Reporting Standard, financial institutions in India must identify accounts of foreign tax residents and report them to tax authorities. Investors therefore declare their country of tax residence, citizenship and related details.

Question 8

Small investors who do not have a PAN, such as for micro SIPs within a specified annual limit, can invest after completing:

  1. Exemption granted by SEBI on a case-by-case basis
  2. PAN-exempt KYC, with an alternate identity document
  3. A declaration from the distributor about their income
  4. No KYC at all, as the amounts are small
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. PAN-exempt KYC, with an alternate identity document

Investments within the specified limit, such as micro SIPs, are exempt from the PAN requirement but not from KYC. Investors complete PAN-exempt KYC using another officially valid identity document, and receive a reference number.
