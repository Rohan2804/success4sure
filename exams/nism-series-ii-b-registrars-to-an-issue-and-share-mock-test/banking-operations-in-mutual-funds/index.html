Banking Operations in Mutual Funds: NISM Series II-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Banking Operations in Mutual Funds, from the NISM Series II-B (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents – Mutual Fund) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the banking side of mutual fund transactions. Learners should understand bank mandates and why redemption and IDCW proceeds are paid only to registered bank accounts, registration of multiple bank accounts, bank account validation, and electronic payment modes such as NEFT, RTGS, IMPS, UPI and NACH. Revise the rules on third-party payments and their permitted exceptions, SEBI's ban on pooling of investor funds by intermediaries, and how SIP mandates are registered and cancelled. Focus on the RTA's controls against fraud and the actions to be taken when payments fail.

Banking Operations in Mutual Funds questions

Question 1

Redemption proceeds of a mutual fund folio are paid to:

  1. Any bank account mentioned in the redemption form
  2. The distributor's account for onward transfer
  3. The bank account registered in the folio
  4. The account of the nominee by default
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The bank account registered in the folio

To prevent fraud, redemption and IDCW proceeds are paid only to a bank account registered in the folio. A new account must first be registered through a proper change of bank mandate process. Distributors and nominees cannot receive proceeds.

Question 2

An individual investor may register more than one bank account in a folio. Up to how many accounts are allowed?

  1. 5
  2. 2
  3. 3
  4. 10
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 5

Individuals and HUFs can register up to five bank accounts in a folio, while non-individual investors can register up to ten. One account is marked as default for payouts, and the others can be used for payments or chosen for redemption.

Question 3

Which of these is a permitted exception to the ban on third-party payments for mutual fund purchases?

  1. A close friend paying for an investor's lump sum purchase through his own cheque
  2. An employer paying for an employee's SIP through payroll deductions
  3. A distributor paying on behalf of a client
  4. A neighbour paying through an online transfer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. An employer paying for an employee's SIP through payroll deductions

Third-party payments are generally not accepted. Exceptions include payment by an employer for employees through payroll deduction, by a parent or grandparent for a minor within a limit, and by a custodian for an FPI, with required declarations.

Question 4

Before registering a new bank account, many RTAs credit a small amount to the account to check the name and account number. This is known as:

  1. Cheque truncation
  2. Reverse mortgage
  3. Standing instruction
  4. Penny drop validation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Penny drop validation

In penny drop validation, a small amount such as ₹1 is credited to the account, and the bank's response confirms that the account is active and gives the holder's name. This is matched with the investor's name before registration.

Question 5

Under SEBI's rules for mutual fund transactions, investment platforms and distributors may NOT:

  1. Help investors place mutual fund orders through stock exchange platforms like BSE StAR MF
  2. Collect transaction orders from investors electronically
  3. Earn commission on investments in regular plans
  4. Pool investors' money in their own accounts before paying the AMC
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Pool investors' money in their own accounts before paying the AMC

SEBI has prohibited pooling of investor funds by intermediaries in mutual fund transactions. Money must flow directly from the investor's bank account to the mutual fund's account, and redemptions directly back, reducing the risk of misuse.

Question 6

An investor wants SIP instalments to be debited automatically from her bank account each month. What should be registered?

  1. A demand draft for each instalment
  2. A NACH mandate with her bank
  3. A standing order with the RTA's bank
  4. A power of attorney in favour of the AMC
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A NACH mandate with her bank

A NACH mandate authorises the mutual fund to debit the investor's bank account for SIP instalments up to the approved amount. It is registered with the investor's bank. Demand drafts or powers of attorney are not needed for SIPs.

Question 7

Large redemption payouts to investors are usually credited directly through:

  1. Cash payments at the RTA's service centre
  2. Demand drafts sent by ordinary post
  3. Electronic modes such as NEFT, RTGS or IMPS
  4. Postal money orders to the registered address
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Electronic modes such as NEFT, RTGS or IMPS

Mutual funds credit redemption and IDCW proceeds electronically to the registered bank account through NEFT, RTGS or IMPS, which is faster and safer. Cheques or drafts are used only where electronic credit is not possible, and cash is never paid.

Question 8

An investor wants to stop her SIP permanently and also cancel the auto-debit. What should she do?

  1. Submit an SIP cancellation request and cancel the NACH mandate
  2. Keep the account balance low so that debits fail
  3. Redeem all units, as this cancels the SIP automatically
  4. Write to SEBI to stop the debits
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Submit an SIP cancellation request and cancel the NACH mandate

SIPs should be cancelled through a formal request to the AMC or RTA, along with cancellation of the mandate. Letting debits fail is not correct practice, as banks may charge penalties. Redeeming units does not necessarily stop future SIP instalments.
