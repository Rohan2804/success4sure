About the NISM Series II-B exam

NISM Series II-B focuses on RTAs that service mutual funds — investor KYC, purchase and redemption processing, NAV application, SIP/STP/SWP handling, folio maintenance, statements and non-financial service requests.

Who should take it: Operations staff at RTAs and AMCs who process mutual fund transactions and investor service requests.

Free NISM Series II-B sample questions

Sample 1 · Introduction to Securities

Which of the following is a money market instrument?

  1. Treasury bill
  2. Equity share
  3. Ten-year government bond
  4. Convertible debenture
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Treasury bill

T-bills are short-term government securities with maturities under one year, which makes them money market instruments.

Sample 2 · Characteristics of Equity Shares

Which of the following is a right typically enjoyed by equity shareholders?

  1. Repayment of capital on a fixed maturity date
  2. Priority over creditors on liquidation
  3. A fixed rate of dividend every year
  4. Voting on resolutions at general meetings
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Voting on resolutions at general meetings

Equity shareholders own the company and vote; dividends are not fixed and they rank last on liquidation.

Sample 3 · Characteristics of Debt Securities

Credit risk in a debt security refers to the risk that:

  1. Inflation may erode real returns
  2. The security may not be easy to sell
  3. The issuer may fail to pay interest or principal on time
  4. Interest rates in the market may rise
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The issuer may fail to pay interest or principal on time

Credit (default) risk is about the issuer's ability to meet payment obligations.

Sample 4 · Characteristics of Other Securities

A security with features of both debt and equity, such as a convertible debenture, is described as a:

  1. Pure equity instrument
  2. Derivative
  3. Money market instrument
  4. Hybrid instrument
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Hybrid instrument

Convertibles start as debt and may convert into equity, so they combine features of both.

Sample 5 · SEBI – Role and Regulations

Mutual funds in India are regulated by SEBI under the:

  1. Insurance Act, 1938
  2. SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996
  3. Banking Regulation Act, 1949
  4. SEBI (Portfolio Managers) Regulations, 2020
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996

SEBI regulates mutual funds through the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996.
