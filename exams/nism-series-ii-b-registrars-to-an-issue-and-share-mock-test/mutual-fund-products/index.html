Mutual Fund Products: NISM Series II-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Mutual Fund Products, from the NISM Series II-B (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents – Mutual Fund) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter describes the categories of mutual fund schemes defined by SEBI's scheme categorisation framework. Learners should know the main equity categories such as large cap, mid cap, small cap, multi cap, flexi cap, focused and ELSS, the debt categories defined by maturity or credit quality such as overnight, liquid and gilt funds, and hybrid, solution-oriented and other schemes such as index funds, ETFs and funds of funds. Focus on the defining feature of each category, minimum allocations, lock-ins and the definition of large, mid and small cap companies. Application questions usually ask which category fits an investor's need.

Mutual Fund Products questions

Question 1

According to SEBI's definition, large cap companies are:

  1. The 1st to 50th companies by full market capitalisation
  2. Companies with a market cap above ₹10,000 crore
  3. The 1st to 250th companies by full market capitalisation
  4. The 1st to 100th companies by full market capitalisation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The 1st to 100th companies by full market capitalisation

SEBI defines large cap companies as the top 100 companies by full market capitalisation, mid caps as the 101st to 250th, and small caps as the 251st onwards. AMFI publishes the list periodically so that schemes follow a uniform definition.

Question 2

A multi cap fund must invest a minimum of 25% of its total assets in each of:

  1. Large cap, mid cap and small cap stocks
  2. Equity, debt and gold
  3. Indian equity, foreign equity and debt
  4. Large cap and mid cap stocks only
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Large cap, mid cap and small cap stocks

SEBI's rules require multi cap funds to invest at least 75% in equity, with at least 25% each in large, mid and small cap stocks. This differs from flexi cap funds, which can allocate freely across market capitalisations.

Question 3

A liquid fund can invest in debt and money market securities with a maturity of up to:

  1. 1 day
  2. 1 year
  3. 91 days
  4. 3 years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 91 days

Liquid funds invest in debt and money market instruments maturing within 91 days. Overnight funds are limited to one-day maturity, and ultra short or low duration funds hold longer maturities. This short maturity keeps interest rate risk low.

Question 4

An investor wants to benefit from price differences between the cash and futures markets with low risk. Which category suits this strategy?

  1. Small cap fund
  2. Arbitrage fund
  3. Credit risk fund
  4. Sectoral fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Arbitrage fund

Arbitrage funds buy a stock in the cash market and sell its futures at a higher price, earning the spread with limited market risk. Small cap, credit risk and sectoral funds take higher equity or credit risk and do not follow this strategy.

Question 5

A focused fund is an equity scheme that invests in:

  1. Only one sector of the economy
  2. Only stocks outside India
  3. Stocks that pay high dividends
  4. A maximum of 30 stocks
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A maximum of 30 stocks

SEBI defines a focused fund as one that holds a concentrated portfolio of at most 30 stocks. A single-sector scheme is a sectoral fund, overseas exposure belongs to international funds, and high-dividend stocks define dividend yield funds.

Question 6

A gilt fund must invest at least 80% of its total assets in:

  1. AAA-rated corporate bonds
  2. Government securities across maturities
  3. Bank certificates of deposit
  4. Treasury bills only
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Government securities across maturities

Gilt funds invest a minimum of 80% in government securities across maturities. They have negligible credit risk but can face significant interest rate risk. Corporate bonds and CDs belong to other debt categories.

Question 7

A parent invests in a children's fund, which is a solution-oriented scheme. What lock-in applies to such investments?

  1. 3 years from the date of each investment
  2. Till the child attains majority, with no other minimum lock-in period applying
  3. 5 years or till the child attains majority, whichever is earlier
  4. No lock-in, as it is an open-ended scheme
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 5 years or till the child attains majority, whichever is earlier

SEBI requires children's funds to have a lock-in of at least five years or until the child attains majority, whichever is earlier. Retirement funds have a similar rule linked to retirement age. A three-year lock-in applies to ELSS.

Question 8

A scheme that invests mainly in units of other mutual fund schemes is called a:

  1. Fund of funds
  2. Balanced advantage fund
  3. Exchange traded fund
  4. Multi asset allocation fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Fund of funds

A fund of funds invests in the units of other mutual fund schemes, domestic or overseas, rather than directly in securities. Investors bear expenses of both layers. ETFs and balanced advantage or multi asset funds invest directly in securities.
