Non-Financial Transactions: NISM Series II-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Non-Financial Transactions, from the NISM Series II-B (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents – Mutual Fund) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

Non-financial transactions change folio details without moving money, and they are a large part of an RTA's workload. This chapter covers nomination, transmission, changes in name, address, contact details and bank mandate, minor to major conversion, consolidation of folios, and marking of lien or pledge on units. Learners should know the documents and checks required for each request, including how RTAs guard against fraud in high-risk changes. Revise the rules on who can nominate, how transmission works with and without a nominee or surviving joint holder, and the role of KYC in updating investor details.

Non-Financial Transactions questions

Question 1

Which of the following investors cannot make a nomination in a mutual fund folio?

  1. A resident individual holding singly
  2. A joint holder in an individual folio
  3. A private limited company
  4. A parent investing in their own name
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A private limited company

Nomination is available only to individuals holding units on their own behalf, singly or jointly. Non-individuals such as companies, trusts, societies and the Karta of an HUF cannot nominate, as the entity's holding passes as per its own legal arrangements.

Question 2

A folio is held jointly by two persons. One of them dies. The units will normally be:

  1. Transferred to the nominee of the deceased
  2. Transmitted to the surviving joint holder
  3. Redeemed and paid to the legal heirs
  4. Frozen until a court order is received
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Transmitted to the surviving joint holder

In a joint folio, on the death of one holder, the units go to the surviving holder or holders, who submit the death certificate and updated KYC. Nominees come into the picture only when all holders have died.

Question 3

A sole unitholder has died without registering a nominee. Who can claim the units?

  1. The distributor who sold the scheme
  2. The AMC, which redeems and keeps the money
  3. Anyone who holds the original account statement and the deceased's PAN card copy
  4. The legal heirs, after submitting the required succession documents
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The legal heirs, after submitting the required succession documents

Without a nominee or joint holder, units are transmitted to the legal heirs. The RTA asks for documents such as the death certificate, proof of legal heirship and indemnity, or a succession certificate or probate where the value is high.

Question 4

An investor takes a loan against her mutual fund units. What does the RTA record in her folio?

  1. A lien or pledge in favour of the lender
  2. A transfer of the units to the lender's folio
  3. A redemption of the units in favour of the lender
  4. A change of the investor's name to the lender's name
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A lien or pledge in favour of the lender

When units are pledged, the RTA marks a lien in favour of the lender. The investor remains the owner, but cannot redeem or switch the pledged units until the lender releases the lien. If she defaults, the lender may invoke the pledge.

Question 5

An investor has three different folios with the same AMC with identical holders and details. She wants a single folio. This request is called:

  1. Transmission of folio units
  2. Consolidation of folios
  3. Switch of units
  4. Change of status
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Consolidation of folios

Consolidation merges multiple folios with the same holders in the same order and the same mode of holding into one folio, making records easier to manage. Transmission relates to death of a holder, and switches move money between schemes.

Question 6

A woman investor changes her surname after marriage and wants it updated in her folio. What is normally required?

  1. A request with proof of name change, such as a marriage certificate, and updated KYC
  2. Only a phone call to the RTA's call centre
  3. A new PAN card in the name of her spouse
  4. Redemption of units and fresh purchase in the new name
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A request with proof of name change, such as a marriage certificate, and updated KYC

Change of name requires a written request with supporting proof such as a marriage certificate or gazette notification, and the KYC record must also be updated. A phone call is not enough, and there is no need to redeem and reinvest.

Question 7

Why do RTAs verify a new email address or mobile number before updating it in a folio?

  1. Because SEBI charges a fee for each update
  2. Because distributors earn an extra commission whenever an investor's contact details change
  3. Because transaction alerts go there, and fraudulent changes could hide misuse
  4. Because the NAV depends on the investor's location
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Because transaction alerts go there, and fraudulent changes could hide misuse

Contact details receive OTPs, alerts and statements. If a fraudster changes them, the real investor may not learn of unauthorised transactions. Validation ensures the details belong to the investor. Fees, commissions and NAV are unrelated.

Question 8

After the death of a sole unitholder, the AMC transmits the units to the registered nominee. What is the effect for the AMC?

  1. It must hold the units until all legal heirs give consent
  2. It must pay the units' value to the legal heirs as well
  3. It must redeem the units and deposit the money in court until the heirs settle their claims
  4. It is discharged of its liability for those units towards the legal heirs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. It is discharged of its liability for those units towards the legal heirs

Transmission to a valid registered nominee gives the AMC a valid discharge. Any claim that the legal heirs may have is a matter between them and the nominee under succession law, and does not require the AMC to pay twice or hold the units.
