Basics of Registrars and Transfer Agents: NISM Series II-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Basics of Registrars and Transfer Agents, from the NISM Series II-B (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents – Mutual Fund) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains who registrars and transfer agents are and what they do for mutual funds. An RTA is appointed by the AMC to handle investor-facing operations: processing transactions, maintaining the unitholder register, computing distributor commissions, reconciling collections, sending statements and handling service requests. Learners should understand the folio structure, the meaning of an official point of acceptance and time-stamping, and the fact that the AMC remains responsible to investors for outsourced work. Know the large RTAs in the Indian mutual fund industry and shared platforms such as MF Central, and focus on how RTA functions link to investor service.

Basics of Registrars and Transfer Agents questions

Question 1

MF Central, a common platform where investors can carry out service requests across mutual fund houses, has been developed jointly by:

  1. SEBI and the Reserve Bank of India
  2. NSDL and CDSL
  3. AMFI and the stock exchanges
  4. The two large mutual fund RTAs, CAMS and KFintech
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The two large mutual fund RTAs, CAMS and KFintech

MF Central was developed by the RTAs CAMS and KFin Technologies, under SEBI's direction, as a single platform for investors to view holdings and submit financial and non-financial transactions across AMCs they service.

Question 2

An AMC outsources its investor servicing to an RTA. If an investor faces deficient service, who remains ultimately responsible to the investor?

  1. The AMC
  2. The RTA alone
  3. The investor's distributor
  4. AMFI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The AMC

An AMC can outsource operations to an RTA, but it cannot outsource its responsibility. The AMC remains accountable to investors for the RTA's work and must monitor service standards. The RTA is answerable to the AMC under their agreement.

Question 3

Applications received at an official point of acceptance are time-stamped mainly to:

  1. Calculate the trail commission payable to the distributor each month
  2. Determine the applicable NAV for the transaction
  3. Fix the date for the next IDCW payout
  4. Decide the investor's risk profile
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Determine the applicable NAV for the transaction

Time-stamping records the exact date and time an application is received. Since the applicable NAV depends on cut-off times, this record decides which day's NAV applies. It also provides an audit trail that protects investors from backdating.

Question 4

Which of these tasks is typically carried out by a mutual fund RTA on behalf of the AMC?

  1. Selecting securities for the scheme's portfolio
  2. Approving the scheme information document
  3. Calculating commission payable to distributors
  4. Fixing the scheme's benchmark index
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Calculating commission payable to distributors

RTAs compute the upfront and trail commission due to distributors based on transaction and holding data, which the AMC then pays. Portfolio selection is done by the fund manager, and approval of scheme documents involves the AMC, trustees and SEBI.

Question 5

A scheme declares an IDCW with a fixed record date. Which RTA function decides who is entitled to it?

  1. Valuing the scheme's securities at market prices
  2. Placing orders with brokers for the scheme
  3. Preparing the scheme's annual financial statements
  4. Maintaining the register of unitholders as on the record date
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Maintaining the register of unitholders as on the record date

IDCW is paid to unitholders whose names appear in the register on the record date. The RTA maintains this register, so it identifies eligible investors and their holdings. Valuation, trading and annual accounts are AMC or fund accounting functions.

Question 6

An investor already has a folio with an AMC and wants to invest in another scheme of the same AMC. Which statement is correct?

  1. A new folio must be opened with fresh KYC
  2. The new investment can be made under the same folio
  3. The investor must close the existing folio first
  4. Folios are linked to schemes, so one per scheme is required
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The new investment can be made under the same folio

A folio is an investor account with an AMC and can hold units of several schemes of that AMC. Investing under the existing folio keeps holdings consolidated and avoids repeating documentation, though a new folio is also allowed if the investor wishes.

Question 7

Which RTA activity matches money received in the scheme's collection bank account with the transactions submitted by investors?

  1. Portfolio rebalancing
  2. NAV computation
  3. Funds reconciliation
  4. Risk-o-meter updation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Funds reconciliation

Funds reconciliation compares credits in the collection account with application data so that units are allotted only against realised money. Mismatches are investigated. Rebalancing, NAV computation and risk labelling are not reconciliation activities.

Question 8

After an RTA processes a purchase, the investor receives an SMS and email with details of units allotted. This communication is sent:

  1. By the RTA on behalf of the AMC
  2. By SEBI after approving the allotment
  3. By the stock exchange where units trade
  4. By the investor's bank after the debit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. By the RTA on behalf of the AMC

RTAs send transaction confirmations and account statements to investors on behalf of the AMC, using the contact details in the folio. SEBI does not approve individual allotments, and the bank only confirms the debit of money.
