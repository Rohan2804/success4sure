Characteristics of Equity Shares: NISM Series II-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Characteristics of Equity Shares, from the NISM Series II-B (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents – Mutual Fund) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains what an equity share represents and the rights and risks that come with it. Focus on ownership, limited liability, voting, residual claim on profits and assets, and the absence of any guaranteed return. Revise corporate actions that change a shareholder's holding, such as bonus issues, rights issues, stock splits and buybacks, and be able to work out their effect on the number of shares and face value. Basic valuation ratios such as earnings per share and price-to-earnings are also useful. These concepts matter because RTAs process such corporate actions for shareholders.

Characteristics of Equity Shares questions

Question 1

A shareholder holds 100 shares of a company. The company announces a 1:1 bonus issue. After the bonus, the shareholder will hold:

  1. 150 shares, after paying the face value
  2. 200 shares, without paying any money
  3. 200 shares, after paying the market price
  4. 100 shares with double the face value
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 200 shares, without paying any money

In a 1:1 bonus, one free share is given for every share held, so 100 shares become 200. Bonus shares are issued by capitalising reserves, so the shareholder pays nothing. Total value of the holding does not change on its own.

Question 2

A company splits its shares of face value ₹10 into shares of face value ₹2. An investor holding 100 shares before the split will hold:

  1. 500 shares of ₹2 each
  2. 100 shares of ₹2 each
  3. 20 shares of ₹10 each
  4. 200 shares of ₹5 each
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 500 shares of ₹2 each

A stock split divides each share into smaller units. Splitting ₹10 into ₹2 gives five shares for each share held, so 100 shares become 500 shares of ₹2 each. The total face value of the holding stays at ₹1,000.

Question 3

In a rights issue, new shares are offered:

  1. Free of cost to all existing shareholders
  2. Only to qualified institutional investors at a discount to the market price
  3. To employees as part of their compensation
  4. To existing shareholders in proportion to their holdings
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. To existing shareholders in proportion to their holdings

A rights issue gives existing shareholders the right to buy additional shares, usually at a price below market, in proportion to what they already hold. Shares given free are bonus shares, and shares for employees come under ESOP schemes.

Question 4

If a company is wound up, equity shareholders are paid:

  1. Before secured lenders, since equity shareholders are the owners of the company
  2. Along with debenture holders, in equal priority
  3. Only after creditors and preference shareholders are paid
  4. Before preference shareholders but after banks
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Only after creditors and preference shareholders are paid

Equity shareholders have a residual claim. On liquidation, secured and unsecured creditors are paid first, then preference shareholders, and only what remains goes to equity shareholders. This is why equity carries higher risk than debt.

Question 5

What does 'limited liability' mean for an equity shareholder of a company limited by shares?

  1. Dividend is limited to a fixed rate each year
  2. Voting rights are limited to special resolutions
  3. The shares can be sold only to limited buyers
  4. Loss is limited to the amount invested or unpaid on the shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Loss is limited to the amount invested or unpaid on the shares

Limited liability means a shareholder cannot be asked to pay the company's debts beyond the amount unpaid on their shares. Once shares are fully paid, the maximum loss is the investment itself. It has nothing to do with dividend rate or voting.

Question 6

A share trades at ₹150 and the company's earnings per share is ₹5. What is its price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio?

  1. 7.5
  2. 3.3
  3. 30
  4. 750
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 30

The P/E ratio is market price divided by earnings per share. Here ₹150 ÷ ₹5 = 30, meaning investors pay ₹30 for every rupee of annual earnings. Multiplying the two, or dividing the other way round, gives the wrong figures.

Question 7

When a company buys back its own shares from shareholders, the usual effect is that:

  1. The number of shares outstanding is reduced
  2. The face value of each share is increased
  3. New shares are issued to existing shareholders
  4. The company's debt is converted into equity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The number of shares outstanding is reduced

In a buyback, the company uses its funds to purchase its own shares, which are then extinguished. This reduces the number of shares outstanding and can raise earnings per share. It does not change face value or issue new shares.

Question 8

Which statement about returns on equity shares is correct?

  1. Dividends are fixed at the time of issue and paid every year
  2. Returns come from dividends and price changes, neither of which is guaranteed
  3. Capital appreciation is assured if the shares are held for at least five continuous years
  4. Returns are protected by the depository against market losses
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Returns come from dividends and price changes, neither of which is guaranteed

Equity returns consist of dividends, which are paid at the board's discretion, and capital gains or losses from price movements. Neither is assured, which makes equity a risk investment. Depositories hold shares but do not protect their value.
