Characteristics of Debt Securities: NISM Series II-B practice questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers debt securities such as bonds, debentures and money market instruments. Key ideas are face value, coupon, maturity, yield and the inverse relationship between interest rates and bond prices. Learners should understand the main risks in debt, including interest rate risk, credit or default risk, reinvestment risk and liquidity risk, and how credit ratings signal safety. Revise special features such as zero coupon bonds, secured and unsecured debentures, and call and put options. Practise simple yield calculations, since current yield and price sensitivity are common application questions.

Characteristics of Debt Securities questions

Question 1

If market interest rates rise, what generally happens to the prices of existing fixed-coupon bonds?

  1. They rise
  2. They remain unchanged until maturity
  3. They fall
  4. They rise only for long-term bonds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. They fall

Bond prices move inversely to interest rates. When rates rise, new bonds offer higher coupons, so older bonds with lower coupons become less attractive and their prices fall until their yield matches the market.

Question 2

A zero coupon bond is one that:

  1. Is issued at a discount and pays face value at maturity
  2. Pays interest monthly at a floating rate
  3. Carries no credit risk at all, as such bonds are issued only by the government
  4. Is repaid in equal instalments over its life
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Is issued at a discount and pays face value at maturity

A zero coupon bond pays no periodic interest. It is issued at a discount to its face value and the investor earns the difference when it is redeemed at face value on maturity. Its issuer can be any borrower, so credit risk may exist.

Question 3

A bond with a face value of ₹100 carries an 8% annual coupon and is currently trading at ₹80. What is its current yield?

  1. 8%
  2. 10%
  3. 6.4%
  4. 12.5%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 10%

Current yield is annual coupon divided by market price. The coupon is ₹8 (8% of ₹100), and ₹8 ÷ ₹80 = 10%. The coupon rate of 8% is based on face value, not on the price the investor actually pays.

Question 4

A secured debenture differs from an unsecured debenture because a secured debenture:

  1. Is guaranteed by SEBI against default
  2. Always carries a higher coupon rate
  3. Can be converted into equity shares
  4. Is backed by a charge on specified assets of the issuer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Is backed by a charge on specified assets of the issuer

A secured debenture has a charge created on the issuer's assets, which holders can enforce if the issuer defaults. SEBI does not guarantee any security. Convertibility is a separate feature, and secured debt usually pays a lower coupon.

Question 5

Among the following long-term credit ratings, which one indicates the highest degree of safety for timely servicing of debt?

  1. AA+
  2. A
  3. AAA
  4. BBB-
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. AAA

Rating agencies use AAA for the highest safety and lowest credit risk. AA and A indicate high and adequate safety respectively, while BBB- is the lowest investment-grade rating. Ratings below BBB- are speculative grade.

Question 6

Two bonds have the same coupon and credit rating, but one matures in 2 years and the other in 15 years. If interest rates change, which statement is correct?

  1. The 2-year bond's price will change more
  2. Both prices will change by the same amount
  3. Neither price will change as the coupons are fixed
  4. The 15-year bond's price will change more
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The 15-year bond's price will change more

Longer maturity bonds have higher duration, so their prices are more sensitive to interest rate changes. The 15-year bond will therefore show a larger price movement than the 2-year bond for the same change in rates.

Question 7

A bond with a call option allows:

  1. The issuer to redeem the bond before its maturity date
  2. The investor to sell the bond back to the issuer early
  3. The investor to convert the bond into equity shares
  4. The issuer to stop paying interest for one year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The issuer to redeem the bond before its maturity date

A call option gives the issuer the right to repay the bond early, usually when interest rates fall. The investor's right to demand early repayment is a put option. Conversion into shares is a feature of convertible bonds.

Question 8

An investor receives half-yearly coupons from a bond but interest rates in the market have fallen, so the coupons can only be invested at lower rates. This is an example of:

  1. Credit risk
  2. Reinvestment risk
  3. Liquidity risk
  4. Market liquidity risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Reinvestment risk

Reinvestment risk is the risk that interim cash flows such as coupons must be reinvested at lower rates than expected. It is highest when rates fall. Credit risk relates to default by the issuer, and liquidity risk to difficulty in selling.
