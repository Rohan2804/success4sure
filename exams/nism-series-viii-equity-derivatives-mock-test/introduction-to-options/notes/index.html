Introduction to Options: NISM Series VIII revision notes

Options carry the most weight in Series VIII. You must handle payoffs for all four positions, compute intrinsic and time value, identify moneyness, apply put-call parity and know what each Greek measures. Expect several calculation questions; work through each with a small table of prices to avoid sign errors.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Rights and obligations

A call gives the buyer the right to buy the underlying at the strike price; a put gives the right to sell. The buyer pays a premium and has no further obligation. The seller (writer) receives the premium and must perform if the buyer exercises, so the seller pays margin.

2. Payoff of a long call

Buying a ₹500 call for ₹20: at expiry with the stock at ₹540, profit is 540 - 500 - 20 = ₹20 per share. Break-even is strike plus premium, ₹520. Below ₹500 the option expires worthless and the loss is limited to the ₹20 premium.

3. Payoff of a long put

Buying a ₹500 put for ₹15 gains when the price falls. At ₹460, profit is 500 - 460 - 15 = ₹25. Break-even is strike minus premium, ₹485. Maximum profit is strike minus premium if the price falls to zero; maximum loss is the premium.

4. Writers' risk

A call writer's loss is theoretically unlimited because prices can rise without limit. A put writer's maximum loss is strike minus premium. Writers' maximum gain is the premium received, which is why selling options without hedges is risky despite frequent small wins.

5. Moneyness

A call is in-the-money when spot is above strike, at-the-money when they are equal and out-of-the-money when spot is below strike. For puts, the reverse: a ₹500 put is in-the-money when the stock is at ₹480. Only in-the-money options have intrinsic value.

6. Intrinsic and time value

Premium = intrinsic value + time value. With spot ₹540, a ₹500 call priced at ₹55 has intrinsic value ₹40 and time value ₹15. Time value decays as expiry nears and is zero at expiry. Out-of-the-money options consist entirely of time value.

7. Option pricing inputs

The Black-Scholes model uses spot price, strike price, time to expiry, volatility and the risk-free rate (plus dividends). Higher volatility raises both call and put premiums. A higher spot raises call premiums and lowers put premiums; a higher strike does the opposite.

8. Put-call parity

For European options on the same underlying, strike and expiry: call + present value of strike = put + spot. If the call is ₹30, put ₹20, spot ₹500 and PV of strike ₹490, parity holds because 30 + 490 = 20 + 500. A violation creates an arbitrage opportunity.

9. The Greeks

Delta measures premium change for a ₹1 change in the underlying (calls 0 to 1, puts 0 to -1). Gamma measures the change in delta. Theta measures time decay, usually negative for buyers. Vega measures sensitivity to volatility. Rho measures sensitivity to interest rates.

10. Exercise styles in India

Index and stock options on Indian exchanges are European style and can be exercised only on expiry. In-the-money stock options are physically settled, while index options are cash settled. Exchanges automatically exercise in-the-money options at expiry. Stock options moved from American to European style in 2011, so early exercise is no longer possible for any equity option.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Call payoff at expiry = max(0, S - K) - premium; break-even = K + premium
  • Put payoff at expiry = max(0, K - S) - premium; break-even = K - premium
  • Premium = Intrinsic value + Time value
  • Put-call parity: C + K/(1+r)^t = P + S

Common traps in the exam

  • Calling a call with spot below strike 'in-the-money' - for calls, in-the-money means spot above strike; puts are the reverse.
  • Adding premium to strike for a put's break-even; for puts, subtract the premium.
  • Assuming time value can be negative or remains at expiry - it is zero at expiry.
  • Thinking option buyers must deposit margin like sellers; buyers pay only the premium.

Quick recap

Calls: right to buy; puts: right to sell; buyers risk only the premium, writers carry obligations. Call break-even = strike + premium; put = strike - premium. Premium = intrinsic + time value. Volatility raises both premiums. Put-call parity: C + PV(K) = P + S. Know delta, gamma, theta, vega, rho. Indian options are European.
