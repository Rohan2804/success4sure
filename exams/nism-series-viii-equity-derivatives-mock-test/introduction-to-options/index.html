What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces call and put options, option terminology (strike, premium, expiry, moneyness), payoffs for buyers and sellers, intrinsic and time value, put-call parity, and option pricing models such as Black-Scholes and binomial. It also covers the option Greeks: delta, gamma, theta, vega and rho. When revising, practise payoff and break-even calculations, understand how volatility and time affect premiums, and know how Greeks are used to manage option positions.

Introduction to Options questions

Question 1

A stock trades at ₹1,240 and a call option with a strike of ₹1,200 is priced at ₹62. What is the time value of the option?

  1. ₹40
  2. ₹62
  3. ₹22
  4. ₹102
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹22

Intrinsic value of the call = 1,240 − 1,200 = ₹40. Time value = premium − intrinsic value = 62 − 40 = ₹22. ₹40 is the intrinsic value, not the time value.

Question 2

A trader holds long call options covering 2,000 shares, each with a delta of 0.6. To make the position delta neutral, he should:

  1. Sell 1,200 shares of the underlying
  2. Buy 1,200 shares of the underlying
  3. Sell 2,000 shares of the underlying
  4. Buy 800 shares of the underlying
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Sell 1,200 shares of the underlying

Position delta = 2,000 × 0.6 = +1,200 share-equivalents. To neutralise it, he must sell 1,200 shares. Buying would increase the delta, and selling 2,000 shares would over-hedge.

Question 3

A put option with a strike of ₹500 is said to be out of the money when the underlying stock price is:

  1. ₹470
  2. ₹500
  3. ₹450
  4. ₹530
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹530

A put is out of the money when the spot price is above the strike, since exercising would mean selling below market. At ₹470 or ₹450 the put is in the money, and at ₹500 it is at the money.

Question 4

Which option position has a gain potential limited to the premium received but a potentially large loss?

  1. Long put
  2. Short put
  3. Long call
  4. Long straddle
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Short put

A put writer earns at most the premium, while losses grow as the stock falls, up to the strike minus premium. Long option positions risk only the premium paid and have large profit potential.

Question 5

Just before an important company announcement, implied volatility on its options rises sharply. Other things equal, this will:

  1. Increase the premiums of both calls and puts
  2. Increase call premiums but reduce put premiums
  3. Reduce the premiums of both calls and puts
  4. Leave premiums unchanged because the stock price has not moved
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Increase the premiums of both calls and puts

Higher volatility raises the probability of large moves in either direction, increasing the value of both calls and puts. This sensitivity to volatility is measured by vega, which is positive for long calls and long puts.

Question 6

Gamma of an option is usually highest when the option is:

  1. Deep out of the money with long time to expiry
  2. Deep in the money with long time to expiry
  3. At the money and close to expiry
  4. Far out of the money and close to expiry
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. At the money and close to expiry

Gamma measures how fast delta changes. Near-the-money options close to expiry have deltas that swing sharply between 0 and 1 for small price moves, so their gamma peaks. Deep ITM or OTM options have stable deltas.

Question 7

A call buyer pays a premium of ₹45 for a strike of ₹1,500. Ignoring costs, at what expiry price does he break even?

  1. ₹1,455
  2. ₹1,500
  3. ₹1,590
  4. ₹1,545
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹1,545

For a long call, break-even = strike + premium = 1,500 + 45 = ₹1,545. Above this level he makes a net profit. ₹1,455 would be the break-even for a long put with the same strike and premium.

Question 8

In the Black-Scholes model, which input is NOT directly observable in the market and must be estimated?

  1. Current price of the underlying
  2. Volatility of the underlying's returns
  3. Strike price of the option
  4. Time remaining to expiry
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Volatility of the underlying's returns

Spot price, strike and time to expiry are known. Volatility must be estimated, either historically or by backing it out of market prices as implied volatility, which is why it is the key uncertain input.
