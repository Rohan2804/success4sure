What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how equity derivatives are traded on Indian exchanges: the electronic order-matching system, types of orders, contract specifications, lot sizes, expiry rules, price bands, position limits, the market-wide position limit and ban period, and the roles of trading members, clearing members and clients. Revise order types and conditions, how open interest changes with trades, the meaning of the F&O ban period, and the eligibility criteria for stocks in the derivatives segment.

Trading Mechanism questions

Question 1

A trader places an order that must be executed immediately in full or partially, with any unexecuted part cancelled at once. This is an:

  1. Good Till Cancelled order
  2. Stop-loss order
  3. Immediate or Cancel (IOC) order
  4. Day order
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Immediate or Cancel (IOC) order

An IOC order executes whatever quantity is available immediately and cancels the rest. A day order stays in the book till the end of the day, and a stop-loss order activates only when a trigger price is reached.

Question 2

During the ban period for a stock in the F&O segment, a trader with an existing long futures position can:

  1. Freely increase the position
  2. Only reduce the position, not increase it
  3. Neither reduce nor increase the position
  4. Convert it into an options position at no cost
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Only reduce the position, not increase it

When a stock's open interest crosses the market-wide position limit, it enters the ban period: fresh positions that increase exposure are not allowed, but existing positions can be reduced or closed. Breaching this attracts penalties.

Question 3

Trades in the equity derivatives segment of NSE are matched on the basis of:

  1. Size of order only
  2. Alphabetical order of member codes
  3. Random selection by the system
  4. Price-time priority
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Price-time priority

The order-matching system gives priority first to the best price and, at the same price, to the order entered earlier. This transparent rule applies to all members and ensures fair execution.

Question 4

Two traders both close out existing positions against each other: one sells to close a long, the other buys to close a short. Open interest will:

  1. Decrease
  2. Increase
  3. Remain unchanged
  4. Double
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Decrease

Open interest counts outstanding contracts. When both sides are closing existing positions, one contract is extinguished, so open interest falls. If both were opening new positions, it would rise.

Question 5

Which entity places orders on the exchange trading system on behalf of clients?

  1. A trading member
  2. The clearing corporation
  3. The depository participant
  4. The registrar and share transfer agent
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A trading member

Trading members (brokers) are authorised to enter orders on the exchange. The clearing corporation clears and settles trades, depositories hold securities, and RTAs handle issuer registers.

Question 6

A trader places a sell order with a trigger price of ₹490 and a limit price of ₹488 on a stock futures contract trading at ₹500. This order will:

  1. Be executed immediately at ₹500
  2. Be executed only if the futures price first rises back up to ₹490
  3. Activate at ₹490, then sell at ₹488 or better
  4. Sell at any price once the market opens
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Activate at ₹490, then sell at ₹488 or better

A stop-loss sell order lies dormant until the trigger is hit. Once the price falls to ₹490, it becomes a limit order to sell at ₹488 or higher, protecting the trader from further losses.

Question 7

Why are stocks admitted to the derivatives segment required to meet criteria on liquidity, market cap and trading volume?

  1. To guarantee that those stocks will give positive returns
  2. To limit derivatives trading to government-owned companies
  3. To ensure that each stock pays regular dividends
  4. To ensure the underlying is liquid and hard to manipulate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. To ensure the underlying is liquid and hard to manipulate

Eligibility norms ensure the underlying cash market is deep and widely traded, making price manipulation harder and arbitrage and hedging feasible. They say nothing about future returns or dividends.

Question 8

A client wants to trade equity derivatives through a broker. Which of the following is mandatory before trading?

  1. Depositing the full contract value of every trade with the exchange
  2. Completing KYC and signing the required risk disclosure documents
  3. Obtaining a separate registration from SEBI as a derivatives trader
  4. Holding at least ₹10 lakh of shares in a demat account
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Completing KYC and signing the required risk disclosure documents

Clients must complete KYC and receive risk disclosure documents and rights and obligations before trading derivatives. They pay margins, not full contract value, and need no separate SEBI registration.
