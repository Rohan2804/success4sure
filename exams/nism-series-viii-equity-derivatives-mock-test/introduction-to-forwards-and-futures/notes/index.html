Introduction to Forwards and Futures: NISM Series VIII revision notes

This is a high-weight chapter with frequent numerical questions. It covers the difference between forwards and futures, contract terminology, how futures are priced using cost of carry, the basis, and the linear payoff of long and short futures positions. Practise fair value and MTM calculations until they are automatic.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Forward contracts

A forward is a private agreement to buy or sell an asset at a fixed price on a future date. Terms are customised, there is no daily settlement and each party bears the other's credit risk. Forwards are illiquid because exiting requires the counterparty's consent.

2. Futures contracts

Futures are standardised forwards traded on exchanges: lot size, expiry and settlement are fixed. A clearing corporation becomes counterparty to both sides, and positions are marked to market daily. Traders can exit any time by taking an opposite position. Daily MTM limits the build-up of losses.

3. Key terminology

Spot price is the current cash market price; futures price is the agreed price; expiry is the last trading day; contract size is lot size times price; basis is spot minus futures. Index and stock futures in India commonly have near, next and far monthly contracts.

4. Cost of carry pricing

Fair futures price = spot x (1 + r x t) minus the value of dividends expected before expiry, or with continuous compounding F = S x e^(rt). With Nifty at 24,000, a 7% annual rate and one month left, fair value is about 24,000 x (1 + 0.07/12) = 24,140.

5. Contango and backwardation

When futures trade above spot, the market is in contango, the normal situation for financial assets with positive carry. When futures trade below spot - often due to large expected dividends or strong bearish sentiment - the market is in backwardation. Basis converges to zero at expiry.

6. Long futures payoff

A buyer of futures gains one rupee for each rupee rise in the underlying and loses one rupee for each rupee fall. Buying a stock future at ₹500 with a lot of 1,000 shares and selling at ₹520 earns ₹20,000, before costs. The payoff is linear and theoretically unlimited both ways.

7. Short futures payoff

A seller profits when prices fall. Selling at ₹500 and buying back at ₹470 gains ₹30 per share. Losses are theoretically unlimited if prices rise sharply, which is why margins are collected from both buyers and sellers of futures. Short sellers face margin calls on rallies.

8. Settlement styles

Index futures in India are cash settled on expiry using the closing index value. Stock futures are physically settled: positions open at expiry result in actual delivery of shares, a rule SEBI introduced in phases from 2018. Traders not wanting delivery must close positions before expiry.

9. Expiry and contract changes

SEBI reviews lot sizes, weekly expiries and expiry days periodically; since 2025 each exchange has one fixed expiry day for its equity derivatives. Learn the concepts and check the current contract specifications on NSE and BSE websites, as of 2026.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Fair futures price F = S x (1 + r x t) - PV of dividends
  • Continuous compounding: F = S x e^(r x t)
  • Basis = Spot price - Futures price
  • Futures P&L = (Exit price - Entry price) x Lot size for a long position

Common traps in the exam

  • Defining basis as futures minus spot - the workbook defines basis as spot minus futures, so it is usually negative in contango.
  • Forgetting to subtract expected dividends when pricing stock or index futures.
  • Assuming stock futures are cash settled; they are physically settled in India.
  • Thinking only the futures seller pays margin - both sides do, because both can lose.

Quick recap

Forwards are custom OTC deals with counterparty risk; futures are standardised, cleared and marked to market. Fair value = spot plus carry minus dividends. Contango: futures above spot; backwardation: below. Basis converges to zero at expiry. Payoffs are linear. Index futures cash settled, stock futures physically settled.
