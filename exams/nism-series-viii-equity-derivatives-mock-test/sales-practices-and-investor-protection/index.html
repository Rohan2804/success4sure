Sales Practices and Investor Protection: NISM Series VIII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Sales Practices and Investor Protection, from the NISM Series VIII (Equity Derivatives) syllabus.

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers responsible selling and investor protection in equity derivatives: assessing client suitability, risk disclosure, the code of conduct for brokers and their representatives, KYC, avoiding mis-selling, handling complaints, and the investor grievance redressal system including SCORES and online dispute resolution. It also covers investor protection funds and investor education. Revise what information must be given to clients, how to match products to client profiles and where an investor can escalate a grievance.

Sales Practices and Investor Protection questions

Question 1

A broker's representative tells a new client that a particular options strategy is 'risk-free and sure to earn 5% a month'. This is:

  1. Acceptable if the client is experienced
  2. Permitted when the strategy has worked in the past
  3. A misleading statement that violates the code of conduct
  4. Allowed if it is said orally and not in writing
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A misleading statement that violates the code of conduct

Guaranteeing returns or describing leveraged options as risk-free misrepresents risk and breaches the broker code of conduct. No derivatives strategy can promise fixed monthly returns.

Question 2

Before allowing a client to trade in derivatives, a broker should verify the client's:

  1. Financial standing and risk-bearing ability
  2. Political affiliation and religious views
  3. Choice of mobile phone operator and internet provider
  4. Preference between NSE and BSE indices
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Financial standing and risk-bearing ability

Brokers must gather information on income, net worth and experience to judge whether derivatives are appropriate. Unrelated personal details have no bearing on suitability.

Question 3

An investor's complaint against a broker is not resolved satisfactorily. A next step available to him is to:

  1. File the complaint with the Insurance Ombudsman
  2. Approach the company whose shares he traded
  3. Ask the Reserve Bank of India to reverse the disputed trade
  4. Escalate on SCORES and use online dispute resolution
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Escalate on SCORES and use online dispute resolution

Securities market complaints can be lodged on SCORES and pursued through conciliation and arbitration on the ODR portal. Insurance ombudsman, the issuer company and RBI are not the right forums.

Question 4

Why must brokers send contract notes to clients after derivatives trades?

  1. To advertise new products to the client
  2. To give a legal record of executed trades and charges
  3. To request additional brokerage from the client
  4. To inform the client of the next day's market direction
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. To give a legal record of executed trades and charges

A contract note is the legal confirmation of executed trades, showing price, quantity, time and charges, and helps clients verify transactions and detect unauthorised trades.

Question 5

A client asks the broker's dealer to trade on his behalf without specific instructions each time. The dealer should:

  1. Trade only with valid written authorisation
  2. Trade freely, because the client trusts him
  3. Use the client's account to trade for other clients too
  4. Refuse to send any trade confirmation to the client
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Trade only with valid written authorisation

Discretionary trading without proper written authorisation is unauthorised trading. Even with authorisation, every trade must be reported to the client and limited to that client's account.

Question 6

Which practice best demonstrates fair treatment of a retail derivatives client?

  1. Highlighting only past winning trades of other clients
  2. Encouraging the client to borrow money to increase position size
  3. Avoiding discussion of losses so as not to discourage him
  4. Explaining margin calls and that losses can exceed capital
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Explaining margin calls and that losses can exceed capital

Fair dealing requires balanced disclosure of risks, including margin calls and losses beyond the initial deposit. Cherry-picked successes and encouraging borrowing for speculation are poor sales practices.

Question 7

A stock broker learns that a client's position will receive a large order from the broker's institutional client and trades ahead of it for its own account. This is called:

  1. Hedging
  2. Front running
  3. Arbitrage
  4. Rebalancing
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Front running

Trading ahead of a known pending client order to benefit from the expected price impact is front running, a prohibited practice. Hedging and arbitrage are legitimate strategies.

Question 8

SEBI's investor charter for stock brokers primarily aims to:

  1. Fix the brokerage that every broker must charge
  2. Guarantee profits on all trades placed through a broker
  3. Inform investors of their rights and redressal options
  4. List the stocks that investors are forbidden to buy
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Inform investors of their rights and redressal options

The investor charter sets out the vision, services, rights, responsibilities and grievance redressal steps for investors dealing with brokers. It does not fix brokerage or guarantee returns.
