Understanding Index: NISM Series VIII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Understanding Index, from the NISM Series VIII (Equity Derivatives) syllabus.

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains what a stock market index is, how it is constructed and maintained, and why it is used as an underlying for derivatives. Key topics are weighting methods (price-weighted, market-cap and free-float weighted), base value and base period, index maintenance for corporate actions, impact cost and the attributes of a good index. Revise how free-float market cap is computed, why index divisors change, and the uses of indices such as benchmarking, index funds, ETFs and index derivatives.

Understanding Index questions

Question 1

A company has total market capitalisation of ₹40,000 crore and its promoters hold 60% of the shares. If all other shares are free-float, what is its free-float market capitalisation?

  1. ₹24,000 crore
  2. ₹16,000 crore
  3. ₹40,000 crore
  4. ₹10,000 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹16,000 crore

Free float excludes promoter holding, so the free-float portion is 40% of shares. Free-float market cap = 40% × ₹40,000 crore = ₹16,000 crore. ₹24,000 crore is the promoter portion, not the free float.

Question 2

When a constituent stock of a market-cap weighted index is replaced by another stock, the index provider adjusts the divisor so that:

  1. The index value is reset to its original base value
  2. The new stock gets the same weight as the stock it replaced
  3. The index value rises by the new stock's market capitalisation
  4. The index value does not change merely because of the replacement
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The index value does not change merely because of the replacement

Divisor adjustments keep the index continuous so that only market price movements, not changes in composition or corporate actions, change the index level. Weights still depend on each stock's own free-float market cap.

Question 3

In a price-weighted index, which stock has the greatest influence on index movements?

  1. The stock with the highest share price
  2. The stock with the largest market capitalisation
  3. The stock with the highest trading volume
  4. The stock with the most free-float shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The stock with the highest share price

In a price-weighted index, each stock's weight depends on its price, so high-priced shares move the index most regardless of company size. Market-cap based indices instead give larger companies more weight.

Question 4

Which corporate action normally requires an index divisor adjustment in a free-float market-cap index?

  1. A normal daily rise in the price of a constituent
  2. A change in the company's statutory auditor
  3. A rights issue by a constituent company
  4. A constituent's announcement of quarterly results
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A rights issue by a constituent company

A rights issue changes the share count and hence market cap without a pure price move, so the divisor is adjusted to keep the index continuous. Daily price changes are what the index is meant to capture.

Question 5

An index fund manager's portfolio returned 11.4% while the benchmark index returned 12.0%. The 0.6% difference is best described as:

  1. Alpha generated by superior stock selection
  2. Tracking difference due to costs
  3. Impact cost of the index constituents
  4. Basis between the index and its futures
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Tracking difference due to costs

An index fund aims to match its benchmark; the gap between fund and index return is tracking difference, caused by expenses, cash holdings and replication lags. It is negative here, so it is not alpha.

Question 6

Lower impact cost for the stocks in an index is desirable for derivatives trading mainly because it:

  1. Guarantees that the index will rise over time
  2. Reduces the number of stocks needed in the index
  3. Makes index arbitrage and hedging cheaper to execute
  4. Removes the need for margins on index futures
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Makes index arbitrage and hedging cheaper to execute

Low impact cost means large trades in the constituents can be executed close to quoted prices, so arbitrageurs and hedgers can replicate the index cheaply. This keeps futures prices close to fair value.

Question 7

An index had a base value of 1,000 and its current free-float market cap is 25 times the base-period free-float market cap. Ignoring divisor adjustments, the index value is:

  1. 25,000
  2. 2,500
  3. 26,000
  4. 24,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 25,000

Index value = base value × (current market cap ÷ base market cap) = 1,000 × 25 = 25,000. The ratio of market caps is applied multiplicatively to the base value, not added to it.

Question 8

Which of these is NOT a common use of a stock market index?

  1. Benchmarking the performance of fund managers
  2. Serving as the underlying for index futures and options
  3. Creating index funds and exchange-traded funds
  4. Fixing the issue price of a company's IPO
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Fixing the issue price of a company's IPO

Indices are used to benchmark portfolios, as underlyings for derivatives and as the basis for passive products. IPO prices are discovered through book building or fixed by the issuer with the lead manager, not by an index.
