Question 1 · Basics of Derivatives

Compared with OTC derivatives, exchange-traded derivatives substantially reduce:

  1. Market price risk
  2. The need for any margin
  3. Taxes payable on profits
  4. Counterparty credit risk, because a clearing corporation guarantees settlement
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Counterparty credit risk, because a clearing corporation guarantees settlement

The clearing corporation acts as central counterparty with margins and MTM, so participants do not depend on each other.

Question 2 · Understanding Index

A free-float index had a base value of 1,000 when the free-float market cap was ₹2,00,000 crore. If free-float market cap is now ₹2,50,000 crore (no corporate actions), the index value is:

  1. 1,250
  2. 1,025
  3. 800
  4. 2,500
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 1,250

Index = Base value × Current mcap ÷ Base mcap = 1,000 × 2,50,000 ÷ 2,00,000 = 1,250.

Question 3 · Introduction to Forwards and Futures

A stock is at ₹1,500, the financing cost for 2 months is 6% p.a. (simple), and a dividend of ₹10 will be paid before expiry. The fair value of the 2-month future is:

  1. ₹1,515
  2. ₹1,505
  3. ₹1,490
  4. ₹1,525
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹1,505

Carry = 1,500 × 6% × 2/12 = 15; fair value = 1,500 + 15 − 10 = 1,505.

Question 4 · Introduction to Options

A trader buys a put (lot 1,000 shares) with strike ₹500 at a premium of ₹12. At expiry the stock settles at ₹470. His net profit is:

  1. ₹30,000
  2. ₹18,000
  3. ₹12,000
  4. ₹42,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹18,000

Payoff = (500 − 470) − 12 = ₹18 per share; × 1,000 = ₹18,000.

Question 5 · Strategies using Equity Futures and Options

An investor owns a stock and buys a put option on it to limit downside. This is a:

  1. Covered call
  2. Short strangle
  3. Calendar spread
  4. Protective put
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Protective put

The put sets a floor price on the holding while keeping upside potential, like insurance.

Question 6 · Trading Mechanism

When open interest in a stock's derivatives crosses 95% of the market-wide position limit, the stock enters a ban period during which:

  1. All trading in the stock's cash market stops
  2. Only trades that reduce existing positions are allowed
  3. Only new positions can be created
  4. Options on the stock are cancelled
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Only trades that reduce existing positions are allowed

The ban prevents further build-up of positions until open interest falls back below the threshold.

Question 7 · Clearing and Settlement System

Index options on Indian exchanges, when exercised at expiry, are settled by:

  1. Delivery of all index constituent shares
  2. Delivery of index futures
  3. Delivery of an index fund unit
  4. Cash payment of the difference between settlement price and strike
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Cash payment of the difference between settlement price and strike

An index cannot be delivered, so index options are cash settled.

Question 8 · Legal and Regulatory Environment

Equity derivatives became legally tradable on Indian exchanges after the SCRA, 1956 was amended to include derivatives within the definition of:

  1. Banking companies
  2. Securities
  3. Commodities
  4. Deposits
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Securities

The 1999 amendment brought derivatives under 'securities', allowing exchange trading under SEBI oversight.

Question 9 · Accounting and Taxation

Securities Transaction Tax on the sale of an equity option that is squared off (not exercised) is computed on:

  1. The option premium
  2. The strike price × lot size
  3. The intrinsic value
  4. The notional value of the underlying
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The option premium

For options sold before expiry, STT is charged on the premium; on exercise it is charged on the settlement (intrinsic) value.

Question 10 · Sales Practices and Investor Protection

Before recommending a derivatives product to a retail client, a distributor should primarily assess the client's:

  1. Social media following
  2. Financial situation, risk appetite and understanding of the product
  3. Preference for a particular brand
  4. Family members' trading history
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Financial situation, risk appetite and understanding of the product

Suitability requires matching the product's risk to the client's profile and knowledge.

Question 11 · Basics of Derivatives

A participant who simultaneously buys in one market and sells in another to profit from a price mismatch is a/an:

  1. Hedger
  2. Speculator
  3. Margin trader
  4. Arbitrageur
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Arbitrageur

Arbitrageurs exploit price discrepancies with little or no net market risk, helping align prices.

Question 12 · Understanding Index

Impact cost of a stock measures:

  1. The brokerage charged by the broker
  2. The tax payable on a trade
  3. The change in the index when the stock is added
  4. The extra cost of executing a trade of a given size relative to the ideal (mid) price, reflecting liquidity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The extra cost of executing a trade of a given size relative to the ideal (mid) price, reflecting liquidity

Lower impact cost means higher liquidity, which is a key criterion for index inclusion.

Question 13 · Introduction to Forwards and Futures

A market in which futures prices are higher than the spot price is said to be in:

  1. Contango
  2. Backwardation
  3. Convergence
  4. Inversion
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Contango

Contango is the normal cost-of-carry situation where futures trade at a premium to spot.

Question 14 · Introduction to Options

The maximum potential loss for the writer of a naked (uncovered) call option is:

  1. The premium received
  2. The strike price
  3. Zero
  4. Unlimited
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Unlimited

If the underlying rises without limit, the call writer's obligation grows without limit.

Question 15 · Strategies using Equity Futures and Options

A trader buys a 24,000 index put at 180 and sells a 23,800 put at 100, same expiry. Maximum profit in index points is:

  1. 200
  2. 120
  3. 80
  4. 280
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 120

Net debit = 80; max profit = strike difference − net debit = 200 − 80 = 120 points.
