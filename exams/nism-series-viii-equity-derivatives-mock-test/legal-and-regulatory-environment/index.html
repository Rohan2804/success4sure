Legal and Regulatory Environment: NISM Series VIII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Legal and Regulatory Environment, from the NISM Series VIII (Equity Derivatives) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the legal framework for equity derivatives in India: the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956, the SEBI Act, 1992, the Depositories Act, SEBI regulations for stock brokers and the role of SEBI and exchanges. It also covers the evolution of derivatives regulation, position limits, measures to curb excessive speculation and rules against market abuse. Focus on which law defines derivatives, which body approves contracts and what position limits and surveillance measures aim to achieve.

Legal and Regulatory Environment questions

Question 1

Which law gives SEBI the power to regulate the business in stock exchanges and protect investors' interests?

  1. The Banking Regulation Act, 1949
  2. The Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999
  3. The Indian Contract Act, 1872
  4. The Securities and Exchange Board of India Act, 1992
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The Securities and Exchange Board of India Act, 1992

The SEBI Act, 1992 established SEBI and gives it powers to regulate securities markets and protect investors. The Banking Regulation Act concerns banks, FEMA concerns foreign exchange, and the Contract Act covers general contracts.

Question 2

A broker repeatedly places buy and sell orders between related accounts to create a false appearance of trading activity in a stock option. This is prohibited under:

  1. SEBI's regulations governing the operations of mutual funds
  2. SEBI's PFUTP regulations on fraudulent trade practices
  3. The Companies Act provisions on share buyback
  4. The RBI's guidelines on call money market
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. SEBI's PFUTP regulations on fraudulent trade practices

Creating artificial volume through circular or synthetic trades is a manipulative practice under SEBI (PFUTP) Regulations. It misleads other participants and attracts penalties and debarment.

Question 3

Which body grants approval for a stock exchange to start trading in a new type of derivatives contract?

  1. The Ministry of Corporate Affairs
  2. The Reserve Bank of India
  3. SEBI
  4. The Institute of Chartered Accountants of India
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. SEBI

Stock exchanges need SEBI's approval to introduce derivative products. MCA administers company law, RBI governs money and forex markets, and ICAI regulates accountants.

Question 4

Position limits at the client level in equity derivatives aim mainly to:

  1. Stop any one client from building a price-distorting position
  2. Ensure that every client makes a profit
  3. Fix a minimum number of contracts a client must trade
  4. Restrict derivatives trading to large institutional investors only
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Stop any one client from building a price-distorting position

Client-level limits cap concentration, reducing the risk of manipulation and of large defaults. They do not guarantee profits, set minimum trading or bar retail clients.

Question 5

Under the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956, derivatives traded on recognised stock exchanges are:

  1. Included within the definition of 'securities'
  2. Excluded from the definition of 'securities'
  3. Treated as bank deposits
  4. Classified as insurance contracts
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Included within the definition of 'securities'

The SCRA was amended to include derivatives in the definition of securities, making exchange trading in derivatives legal and bringing them under SEBI's regulatory oversight.

Question 6

A stock broker in the derivatives segment must maintain client funds:

  1. In its own proprietary account to earn interest
  2. In any bank account it chooses, as long as the client is informed later
  3. With the clearing corporation only on expiry days
  4. Separately, using them only for that client's obligations
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Separately, using them only for that client's obligations

SEBI requires segregation of client funds from the broker's own money; using one client's money for another client or for the broker's own trading is prohibited.

Question 7

Which measure is designed by SEBI and exchanges to monitor and restrain excessive speculative positions in derivatives?

  1. A fixed guaranteed return on every derivatives contract
  2. Market-wide position limits and surveillance alerts
  3. A ban on all retail clients from trading options
  4. Compulsory physical delivery of index futures
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Market-wide position limits and surveillance alerts

Market-wide position limits, surveillance mechanisms and related measures monitor build-up of positions. They do not guarantee returns, ban retail clients outright, or require physical delivery of index contracts.

Question 8

Disputes between a client and a trading member relating to derivatives trades can be resolved through:

  1. The Reserve Bank's Integrated Ombudsman Scheme for banks
  2. The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority
  3. The exchange's grievance cell and SEBI's ODR platform
  4. The company whose shares are the underlying
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The exchange's grievance cell and SEBI's ODR platform

Client complaints against brokers are handled through the exchange's grievance system, SCORES and the Online Dispute Resolution (ODR) platform with conciliation and arbitration. The banking ombudsman and IRDAI do not cover these disputes.
