Legal and Regulatory Environment: NISM Series VIII revision notes

This chapter covers the laws and committees behind India's equity derivatives market and SEBI's regulatory role. It is a short chapter in the exam but easy marks if you remember which law covers what and which committee recommended what.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956

The SCRA governs stock exchanges and contracts in securities. Its definition of 'securities' was amended in 1999 to include derivatives, making them legal to trade on recognised exchanges. Derivatives must be traded on a recognised exchange and settled through its clearing corporation.

2. SEBI Act, 1992

The SEBI Act gives SEBI its mandate to protect investors, develop the securities market and regulate it. SEBI registers intermediaries such as brokers and clearing members, frames regulations, conducts inspections and can impose penalties or bar entities from the market.

3. SC(R) Rules, 1957

The Securities Contracts (Regulation) Rules lay down requirements such as listing conditions and the contents of contracts. Together with SEBI regulations and exchange bye-laws, they form the legal framework within which derivatives segments operate. Exchange bye-laws add operational detail such as trading hours, member conduct and arbitration of disputes between members and clients.

4. L.C. Gupta Committee

SEBI's L.C. Gupta Committee (report in 1998) recommended introducing exchange-traded derivatives, starting with index futures, and suggested that derivatives segments be separate from cash segments with their own governing councils and clearing arrangements. It also recommended that the derivatives exchange or segment have a separate clearing corporation or house, and that members meet higher capital requirements than cash-market brokers.

5. J.R. Varma Committee

The J.R. Varma Committee framed the risk containment measures for derivatives: margins based on value at risk, mark-to-market settlement, position limits and the methodology for setting margins. These ideas still underpin today's risk management. It recommended a 99% value-at-risk approach for initial margins and a liquid net worth requirement for clearing members, ensuring members could absorb losses without delaying settlement.

6. Exchange and member requirements

Exchanges need SEBI approval to start derivatives segments. Trading and clearing members must meet net worth and other eligibility conditions, have certified staff (NISM Series VIII for associated persons) and follow exchange rules on client dealing. Members must also maintain records, submit periodic reports to the exchange and undergo regular inspections by the exchange and SEBI.

7. Self-regulation by exchanges

Exchanges act as first-level regulators: they admit members, monitor trading through surveillance systems, impose penalties for violations and can suspend members. SEBI oversees the exchanges and clearing corporations themselves, while market infrastructure institutions must meet SEBI's governance and ownership norms.

8. Recent investor protection measures

Responding to studies showing large losses by individual traders, SEBI has tightened rules on index derivatives - larger contract sizes, fewer weekly expiries, upfront collection of option premium and intraday monitoring of position limits. Details change; check SEBI circulars as of 2026.

Common traps in the exam

  • Attributing risk containment measures to the L.C. Gupta Committee - those came from the J.R. Varma Committee.
  • Thinking derivatives were legal before the 1999 SCRA amendment; that amendment brought them within 'securities'.
  • Assuming RBI regulates equity derivatives; SEBI does.
  • Forgetting that associated persons dealing in equity derivatives need NISM Series VIII certification.

Quick recap

SCRA 1956, amended in 1999, made derivatives 'securities'. SEBI Act 1992 empowers SEBI. L.C. Gupta recommended starting derivatives with index futures; J.R. Varma designed margins and risk controls. Members need net worth and certified staff. SEBI keeps tightening F&O rules to protect individual traders.
