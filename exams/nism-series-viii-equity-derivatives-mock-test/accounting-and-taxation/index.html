Accounting and Taxation: NISM Series VIII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Accounting and Taxation, from the NISM Series VIII (Equity Derivatives) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how equity derivatives positions are accounted for and taxed in India. It includes accounting for initial margin, mark-to-market settlements, option premiums and open positions at year-end, and the tax treatment of F&O income as non-speculative business income, Securities Transaction Tax (STT), stamp duty and GST on charges. Rates change from time to time, so focus on concepts: what STT is levied on, who pays it, and how F&O profits and losses are classified and set off.

Accounting and Taxation questions

Question 1

Mark-to-market losses paid daily on an open futures position are accounted for by the client as:

  1. A debit to a mark-to-market account
  2. An increase in the value of shares held
  3. A loan given to the clearing corporation
  4. Revenue from operations
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A debit to a mark-to-market account

Daily MTM payments represent realised or notional changes in the value of the futures position and are recorded in an MTM account, which is closed to profit and loss on squaring off or at year-end per accounting standards.

Question 2

For a resident individual, a loss from trading equity futures on a recognised exchange can generally be set off against:

  1. Only salary income of the same year
  2. Long-term capital gains of the next decade without limit
  3. Agricultural income only
  4. Other income except salary, in the same year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Other income except salary, in the same year

Exchange-traded F&O is treated as non-speculative business. Its loss can be set off against other heads except salary in the same year and carried forward against business income for up to eight years.

Question 3

Securities Transaction Tax on an equity futures trade is levied on:

  1. The buyer, on the margin amount paid
  2. The seller, on the traded value of the futures contract
  3. Both parties, on the brokerage paid
  4. The clearing corporation, on open interest
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The seller, on the traded value of the futures contract

STT on futures applies to the sell side on the traded value. It is not computed on margin or brokerage. The exact rate is notified in the Finance Act and has been revised from time to time.

Question 4

An option buyer pays a premium of ₹20,000 and the option expires worthless. In the books, this premium is:

  1. Carried forward as an asset indefinitely
  2. Refunded by the clearing corporation
  3. Written off as a loss in the profit and loss account
  4. Added to the cost of shares in the portfolio
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Written off as a loss in the profit and loss account

Option premium paid is shown as an asset while the position is open. If the option lapses unexercised, the premium has no remaining value and is charged to profit and loss as a loss.

Question 5

Why is F&O trading on a recognised stock exchange treated differently from speculative business for tax purposes?

  1. Because derivatives never involve any delivery of shares
  2. Because SEBI collects income tax on F&O trades on behalf of the government
  3. Because the law expressly excludes them from speculative transactions
  4. Because all F&O profits are fully exempt from tax
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Because the law expressly excludes them from speculative transactions

Income-tax law expressly carves out eligible derivatives transactions on recognised exchanges from the speculative category, so they are non-speculative business. Profits are taxable, not exempt.

Question 6

A trader who trades F&O as a business must maintain books and may require a tax audit when:

  1. Statutory turnover or profit thresholds are met
  2. He trades even a single option contract
  3. His broker is located outside his city
  4. He has made a profit in every month of the year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Statutory turnover or profit thresholds are met

Tax audit applicability depends on statutory turnover and profit thresholds; F&O turnover is computed in a specific way. One contract alone does not trigger an audit, and location of the broker is irrelevant.

Question 7

Stamp duty on equity derivatives trades in India is:

  1. Paid by the seller to the stock exchange as a membership fee
  2. Collected centrally through exchanges and paid by the buyer
  3. Collected by SEBI as a regulatory fee
  4. Not applicable to derivatives transactions at all
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Collected centrally through exchanges and paid by the buyer

Under the amended Indian Stamp Act, stamp duty on securities transactions, including derivatives, is collected by exchanges or clearing corporations on behalf of states and is payable by the buyer.

Question 8

Which accounting treatment applies to the initial margin deposited by a client with the broker?

  1. It is booked as an expense in the year it is paid
  2. It is shown as income of the client
  3. It is treated as part of share capital
  4. It is shown as a current asset, being recoverable
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. It is shown as a current asset, being recoverable

Initial margin is a deposit, not a cost, so it remains a current asset until returned. Only actual MTM losses, premiums lost and charges are expenses.
