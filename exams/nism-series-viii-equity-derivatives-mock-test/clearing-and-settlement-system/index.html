Clearing and Settlement System: NISM Series VIII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Clearing and Settlement System, from the NISM Series VIII (Equity Derivatives) syllabus.

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how equity derivatives trades are cleared and settled: the role of the clearing corporation and clearing members, novation, determination of obligations, mark-to-market and final settlement, exercise and assignment of options, and the risk management system. Revise SPAN-style initial margin, exposure margin, premium margin, assignment margin, the settlement guarantee fund, client margin collection and how positions are closed out. Expect questions on who pays which margin and how settlement differs for futures, options, index and stock contracts.

Clearing and Settlement System questions

Question 1

A trader is short a stock call option and it is exercised at expiry. Which process determines that he must fulfil the obligation?

  1. Novation by the trading member
  2. Exercise by the option seller
  3. Assignment by the clearing corporation
  4. Netting by the depository
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Assignment by the clearing corporation

When long option holders exercise, the clearing corporation randomly assigns the obligation to short positions. Exercise is the buyer's right; assignment is how sellers are chosen to meet it.

Question 2

An option buyer pays the premium on the trade. When is the premium normally settled under current Indian practice?

  1. Upfront, at the time of the trade
  2. Only at expiry, if the option is exercised
  3. Daily, as mark-to-market on the option's value
  4. It is never paid if the option expires worthless
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Upfront, at the time of the trade

Option buyers must pay the premium upfront; brokers collect it before or at the time of the trade. Options are not marked to market daily like futures; the buyer's maximum loss is this premium.

Question 3

Initial margin in the equity derivatives segment is designed to cover:

  1. The full contract value of the position, payable on the trade date
  2. Potential one-day losses on a position under adverse price and volatility scenarios
  3. The brokerage and taxes payable on the trade
  4. Losses arising only on the expiry day
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Potential one-day losses on a position under adverse price and volatility scenarios

Initial margin is set using a risk-based model that estimates the largest likely one-day loss under various scenarios. It is a fraction of contract value, sized to protect the clearing corporation.

Question 4

If a clearing member fails to meet its settlement obligation, the first resource the clearing corporation uses is typically:

  1. The Core Settlement Guarantee Fund
  2. Contributions from non-defaulting clients
  3. A loan from the Reserve Bank of India
  4. The defaulting member's own margins and collateral
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The defaulting member's own margins and collateral

In the default waterfall, the defaulter's margins and deposits are used first. Only if these are insufficient does the clearing corporation draw on the settlement guarantee fund and other resources.

Question 5

A professional clearing member (PCM) in the derivatives segment:

  1. Trades only on its own account and clears its own trades
  2. Acts as a depository that holds all derivatives positions of clients
  3. Clears trades of trading members but does not trade itself
  4. Is a type of client that uses multiple brokers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Clears trades of trading members but does not trade itself

A PCM is a clearing-only entity, often a bank or custodian, that clears and settles trades executed by trading members or custodial participants. It does not have trading rights of its own.

Question 6

Final settlement of stock futures that remain open at expiry in India is done by:

  1. Cash settlement at the closing price of the index
  2. Physical delivery of the underlying shares
  3. Rolling over automatically to the next month
  4. Cancellation without any settlement
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Physical delivery of the underlying shares

Stock futures and stock options open at expiry are physically settled: the long receives shares and pays the settlement value. Index derivatives are cash settled because the index cannot be delivered.

Question 7

Why do brokers collect margins from clients upfront rather than after the trade?

  1. Exchange rules require it and penalise short collection
  2. Clients can only pay margins after their trades make a profit
  3. Margins are a fee paid to the broker for executing trades
  4. Upfront margins are collected only from institutional clients
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Exchange rules require it and penalise short collection

SEBI and exchange norms require brokers to collect initial margins from clients before trading, and short or non-collection attracts penalties on the member. Margins are collateral, not fees.

Question 8

A trader's short option position faces a sharp rise in volatility. Which margin is most likely to increase?

  1. The premium paid on his long option positions
  2. The settlement guarantee fund contribution of the exchange
  3. The stamp duty on the contract
  4. Initial margin on the short option
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Initial margin on the short option

Risk-based initial margins on short options rise when volatility increases, since potential losses widen. Premiums on longs are already paid, and stamp duty does not depend on volatility.
