Clearing and Settlement System: NISM Series VIII revision notes

Clearing and settlement ensures that every derivatives trade is honoured. This chapter covers the clearing corporation's role, margin types, daily mark-to-market, final settlement of futures and options, and default protections. Expect calculation questions on MTM plus conceptual questions on novation and margins.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Clearing corporations

NSE Clearing Ltd and Indian Clearing Corporation Ltd (for BSE) clear equity derivatives. Through novation, the clearing corporation becomes the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer, so participants never face each other's default risk. This guarantee is what makes exchange-traded contracts safer than OTC ones.

2. Initial margin and SPAN

Initial margin covers the potential one-day loss of a portfolio under adverse scenarios. It is computed with SPAN (Standard Portfolio Analysis of Risk), which evaluates the portfolio across price and volatility scenarios. Margins are collected upfront before positions are taken.

3. Exposure margin

Exposure (extreme loss) margin is collected in addition to initial margin to cover risks beyond those captured by SPAN. Index derivatives generally have lower exposure margin than stock derivatives because indices are less volatile. It is usually a fixed percentage of the notional contract value, added on top of SPAN.

4. Mark-to-market settlement

Futures positions are revalued daily at the settlement price, and profits or losses are settled in cash the next day. A trader long 1 lot of 1,000 shares bought at ₹500, with a settlement price of ₹508, receives ₹8,000 MTM profit; his new cost becomes ₹508.

5. Final settlement

On expiry, index futures and options are cash settled at the final settlement price (the closing value of the underlying). Stock futures and in-the-money stock options are physically settled through delivery of shares, so margins rise as expiry approaches for delivery obligations.

6. Premium settlement

Options premium is paid by the buyer and received by the seller on a T+1 basis. Option buyers pay only premium; sellers pay initial and exposure margins because they carry open-ended risk. Writers' premium receipts can count towards margins only as exchange rules permit, and buyers' premiums must be paid in full before trading.

7. Settlement Guarantee Fund

Each clearing corporation maintains a Core Settlement Guarantee Fund to complete settlement if a member defaults. Contributions come from the clearing corporation, the exchange and clearing members, as per SEBI norms. Its size is reviewed regularly through stress tests that assume the default of the largest members under extreme but plausible market conditions.

8. Risk controls

Clearing corporations monitor positions in real time, apply position limits, and can square off positions of members whose margins are insufficient. Brokers must collect margins from clients upfront; shortfalls attract penalties. Client funds and collateral must be segregated from the broker's own.

9. Spread margin benefit

A calendar spread - long near month, short far month of the same underlying - carries much lower risk than two outright positions because the legs offset. Clearing corporations therefore charge a reduced margin on recognised spreads. The benefit is withdrawn as the near leg approaches expiry.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Daily MTM = (Today's settlement price - Previous settlement price or trade price) x Quantity

Common traps in the exam

  • Thinking clearing members bear each other's default risk - novation makes the clearing corporation the counterparty.
  • Assuming option buyers pay SPAN margin; they pay premium, while sellers pay margin.
  • Believing MTM is settled only at expiry; it is settled daily.
  • Confusing the Core SGF (protects settlement against member default) with the Investor Protection Fund (compensates investors).

Quick recap

Novation makes the clearing corporation counterparty to all. Initial margin via SPAN plus exposure margin, collected upfront. Futures MTM settled daily in cash. Index derivatives cash settled; stock derivatives physically settled. Buyers pay premium, sellers pay margin. Core SGF backs settlement. Spreads get margin relief.
