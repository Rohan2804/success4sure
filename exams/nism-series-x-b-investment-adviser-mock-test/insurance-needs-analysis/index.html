Insurance Needs Analysis: NISM Series X-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Insurance Needs Analysis, from the NISM Series X-B (Investment Adviser (Level 2)) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter teaches how to estimate how much life, health and other insurance a client needs. It covers the human life value approach, the need-based (expense replacement) approach, and how to account for liabilities, goals, existing cover and assets. It also covers assessing health cover adequacy, disability needs and reviewing cover over the life cycle. When revising, practise needs calculations, understand when HLV and needs-based figures differ, and know the typical gaps in Indian households' insurance.

Insurance Needs Analysis questions

Question 1

A family's monthly expenses are ₹40,000 today. At 6% inflation, what will the same expenses cost per month in 20 years?

  1. About ₹1,28,285
  2. About ₹88,000
  3. About ₹2,00,000
  4. About ₹64,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. About ₹1,28,285

Future expense = 40,000 × 1.06^20 ≈ ₹1,28,285. ₹88,000 assumes simple growth of 6% a year. Inflation compounds, which is why needs analysis must project costs.

Question 2

In a needs-based analysis, which item reduces the additional life cover required?

  1. Outstanding home loan
  2. Existing liquid investments and cover
  3. Children's future education costs
  4. Family's future living expenses
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Existing liquid investments and cover

Required cover = liabilities + future needs − existing assets and insurance. Loans, education costs and expenses add to the need; existing resources reduce it.

Question 3

Why does a young earner's insurance need usually fall as he approaches retirement?

  1. Insurance premiums become tax-free after 50
  2. Liabilities always increase with age
  3. Life insurance cannot be bought after 50
  4. Fewer years of income remain to protect
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Fewer years of income remain to protect

Insurance replaces future income; as remaining working years shrink and assets grow, the gap insurance must fill gets smaller. Liabilities usually fall with age.

Question 4

A homemaker has no income. Why may she still need life insurance?

  1. Insurers do not offer cover to homemakers
  2. Her family would gain financially from her death
  3. Her services would cost money to replace
  4. Life cover is mandatory for all adults
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Her services would cost money to replace

Childcare, home management and other services have economic value that the family would need to pay for. Cover can be sized on this replacement cost.

Question 5

A client has ₹20 lakh of group health cover from his employer. What is the main risk of relying on it alone?

  1. Cover may end when he leaves the job
  2. Group cover is never valid in hospitals
  3. Group cover always excludes his spouse
  4. Group cover can only be used outside India
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Cover may end when he leaves the job

Employer cover usually lapses on leaving the job or retiring, when buying fresh cover may be harder or costlier. A personal policy provides continuity.

Question 6

Disability income insurance is meant to:

  1. Pay for vehicle repairs after an accident
  2. Repay home loans on job loss
  3. Replace income lost due to disability
  4. Fund children's education on death
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Replace income lost due to disability

Disability income cover pays a portion of income if illness or injury prevents the insured from working. It addresses a risk often more likely than early death.

Question 7

Which approach to life insurance needs looks at the economic value of a person's future earnings?

  1. Need-based approach
  2. Rule of 72
  3. Expense ratio method
  4. Human life value approach
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Human life value approach

HLV estimates the present value of future income the person would contribute to the family. The need-based approach instead sums specific future needs and liabilities.

Question 8

Which is the most appropriate first step when reviewing an existing client's life cover?

  1. Surrender all existing policies immediately
  2. Compare current cover with updated needs
  3. Buy an additional endowment plan
  4. Ignore cover once a policy is bought
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Compare current cover with updated needs

A review recalculates needs considering changes in income, family, loans and assets, then compares them with existing cover to identify gaps. Surrendering policies hastily can be costly.
