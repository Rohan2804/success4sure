Insurance Needs Analysis: NISM Series X-B revision notes

An adviser must estimate how much insurance a client really needs, not sell the largest or smallest policy. This chapter covers the human life value and needs-based approaches, how liabilities, goals, existing cover and assets change the answer, and how to judge health and disability needs. Calculations dominate, so practise them step by step.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Human life value

HLV estimates the economic value of a person to their family as the present value of future income they would contribute, net of their own consumption and taxes, until retirement. It answers: how much money would replace the earner's contribution if they died today?

2. HLV worked example

A 35-year-old earns ₹15 lakh a year after tax and spends ₹4 lakh on himself, so ₹11 lakh supports the family. With 25 working years and a discount rate equal to income growth (a real rate of zero for simplicity), HLV ≈ ₹11 lakh × 25 = ₹2.75 crore. A positive real discount rate would reduce this figure.

3. Needs-based approach

The needs or expense-replacement method adds up what the family will need: present value of household expenses until dependants become independent, outstanding loans, future goals such as children's education and marriage, and an emergency fund. Existing liquid assets and existing cover are then subtracted.

4. Needs-based example

Family expenses need a corpus of ₹1.2 crore, home loan outstanding ₹40 lakh, children's education goal ₹30 lakh in present value. Total ₹1.9 crore. Less investments ₹25 lakh and existing cover ₹50 lakh. Additional cover needed = ₹1.15 crore.

5. When HLV and needs differ

HLV focuses on the earner's income; needs analysis focuses on the family's requirements. A high earner with modest family needs may have a high HLV but low need. A single-income family with large loans may need more than HLV suggests. Advisers often use both and justify the final figure.

6. Reviewing over the life cycle

Insurance needs usually peak when dependants are young and loans are high, and fall as assets build up and children become independent. Reviews are needed after marriage, childbirth, a home loan, a job change or a big salary jump.

7. Health cover adequacy

Health cover should reflect city of residence, hospital preference, family size, ages and medical history. Rising medical inflation means cover that seemed adequate five years ago may be insufficient now. A base policy combined with a super top-up is a cost-effective way to raise cover.

8. Disability needs

Long-term disability can be financially worse than death, because the earner's expenses continue and medical costs rise. Personal accident and disability income covers fill this gap. Group covers from employers are useful but end when the job ends.

9. Typical gaps in Indian households

Common gaps include reliance on employer group cover alone, investment-oriented policies with tiny sum assured, inadequate cover for the main earner, no disability cover and no separate cover for homemakers whose services have economic value.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • HLV = PV of (annual income − personal consumption − taxes) over remaining working years at a real discount rate.
  • Additional life cover = (PV of expenses + liabilities + goals + emergency fund) − (liquid assets + existing cover).

Common traps in the exam

  • Using gross income for HLV. Use income net of personal consumption and taxes.
  • Forgetting to subtract existing cover and liquid assets in needs analysis.
  • Assuming insurance needs rise forever. They usually peak in mid-life and fall as assets grow and dependants become independent.
  • Relying only on employer group health cover. It lapses on job change or retirement.

Quick recap

HLV = present value of the earner's future contribution to the family. Needs-based = PV of expenses + loans + goals + emergency fund − assets − existing cover. Use both and review at life events. Health cover must track medical inflation; add disability cover. Avoid relying on group covers alone.
