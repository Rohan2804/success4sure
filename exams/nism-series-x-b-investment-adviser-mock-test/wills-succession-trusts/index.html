Wills, Succession & Trusts: NISM Series X-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Wills, Succession & Trusts, from the NISM Series X-B (Investment Adviser (Level 2)) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the law and practice of wills, intestate succession and trusts in India. It includes the requirements of a valid will, codicils, revocation, probate and letters of administration, succession under the Hindu Succession Act, the Indian Succession Act and Muslim personal law, and the creation and types of trusts under the Indian Trusts Act, 1882. When revising, practise distributing an estate under intestacy rules and know the roles of settlor, trustee and beneficiary.

Wills, Succession & Trusts questions

Question 1

A testator wants to make a small change to his existing will without rewriting it. He can do so through a:

  1. Probate
  2. Codicil
  3. Succession certificate
  4. Trust deed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Codicil

A codicil is a document that alters, explains or adds to a will and must be executed like a will. Probate and succession certificates are court documents.

Question 2

Is registration of a will compulsory in India?

  1. Yes, an unregistered will is void
  2. Only for wills of senior citizens
  3. Only if the estate exceeds ₹1 crore
  4. No, it is optional
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. No, it is optional

Registration of a will is optional; an unregistered will is valid if properly executed and attested. Registration can, however, help prove authenticity.

Question 3

A Hindu male dies intestate leaving his widow, two sons and his mother. Under the Hindu Succession Act, his self-acquired property is shared:

  1. Only between the two sons
  2. Half to the widow and half to the sons
  3. Equally among all four
  4. Entirely to the mother
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Equally among all four

Widow, sons and mother are all Class I heirs and take equal shares, so each gets one-fourth. Daughters, if any, would also share equally.

Question 4

A trust in which the trustee has discretion over how much each beneficiary receives is a:

  1. Discretionary trust
  2. Specific trust
  3. Public charitable trust
  4. Revocable will
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Discretionary trust

In a discretionary trust, beneficiaries' shares are not fixed and the trustee decides distributions. In a specific (determinate) trust, shares are fixed in the deed.

Question 5

Under the Indian Trusts Act, the person who holds and manages trust property for others is the:

  1. Settlor
  2. Beneficiary
  3. Trustee
  4. Executor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Trustee

The trustee holds legal title and manages property for beneficiaries as per the trust deed. The settlor creates the trust and the beneficiary enjoys the benefit.

Question 6

A will is automatically revoked when:

  1. The executor dies before the testator
  2. The testator makes a later will revoking it
  3. A witness to the will changes address
  4. The testator's property rises in value
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The testator makes a later will revoking it

A will can be revoked by a later will or codicil, or by destroying it with intent to revoke. The executor's death or a witness moving does not revoke it.

Question 7

Which court document is generally needed by heirs to collect debts and securities of a deceased person who left no will?

  1. Probate
  2. Codicil
  3. Power of attorney
  4. Succession certificate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Succession certificate

A succession certificate authorises heirs to collect debts and securities of an intestate deceased. Probate applies to a will, and a power of attorney ends at death.

Question 8

A 'living trust' created by a client during his lifetime can help by:

  1. Avoiding probate and managing assets
  2. Increasing his income tax rate
  3. Cancelling all his nominations
  4. Making his assets public records
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Avoiding probate and managing assets

A trust holds assets during life and after death per the deed, so the assets pass without probate and can be managed continuously. It also offers privacy.
