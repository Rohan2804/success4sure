Wills, Succession & Trusts: NISM Series X-B revision notes

This chapter covers the legal tools of estate planning in India. You need to know what makes a will valid, when probate is needed, how property is distributed when someone dies without a will under different personal laws, and how trusts are created. Expect distribution scenarios: a person dies intestate leaving certain relatives — who gets what?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Valid will

A will must be made by a person of sound mind who is not a minor, voluntarily and without coercion. Under the Indian Succession Act, it must be signed by the testator and attested by at least two witnesses who see the testator sign. Registration is optional, but it adds evidential strength. Witnesses should not be beneficiaries.

2. Codicil and revocation

A codicil is a document that adds to, changes or explains a will and must be executed with the same formalities. A will can be revoked by a later will, by a written declaration of revocation, or by destroying it with the intention to revoke. Under the Indian Succession Act, marriage revokes a will for some communities but not for Hindus.

3. Probate and letters of administration

Probate is a court's certification of a will's validity, granted to the executor. It is mandatory for wills of Hindus, Buddhists, Sikhs and Jains made in, or covering immovable property in, the former presidency towns of Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai. Letters of administration are granted when there is no executor or no will.

4. Hindu Succession Act basics

When a Hindu male dies intestate, his property goes first to Class I heirs, such as sons, daughters, widow and mother, who share equally. If there are none, it passes to Class II heirs, then agnates and cognates. Daughters have been coparceners in ancestral property, equal to sons, since the 2005 amendment.

5. Distribution example

A Hindu man dies intestate leaving his widow, mother, one son and one daughter. All four are Class I heirs and each gets one-fourth of his separate property. Children of a predeceased son or daughter take their parent's share together.

6. Hindu female's property

Property of a Hindu woman dying intestate goes first to her sons, daughters and husband; property inherited from her parents goes back to her father's heirs if she has no children; property inherited from her husband or father-in-law goes to the husband's heirs.

7. Muslim personal law

A Muslim can bequeath by will only up to one-third of the estate to non-heirs without the consent of the heirs; the rest passes under Islamic law with fixed shares. A will in favour of an heir needs the other heirs' consent. Christians and Parsis are governed by the Indian Succession Act for intestacy.

8. Trusts under the Indian Trusts Act

A private trust is created when the author (settlor) transfers property to a trustee for beneficiaries with a clear intention, purpose, beneficiary and property. Trusts of immovable property need a registered instrument. Public charitable trusts are governed by state laws and tax rules.

9. Types of trusts

A revocable trust can be cancelled by the settlor; an irrevocable trust cannot. In a specific trust, beneficiaries' shares are fixed; in a discretionary trust, trustees decide distributions. Trusts are used for minors, special-needs dependants, family businesses and to avoid fragmentation of assets.

Common traps in the exam

  • Thinking a will must be registered to be valid. Registration is optional; attestation by two witnesses is required.
  • Assuming the widow gets a larger share than children under Class I. Class I heirs share equally.
  • Believing a Muslim can freely will all property. Bequests are limited to one-third without heirs' consent.
  • Confusing probate and letters of administration. Probate validates a will with an executor; letters of administration apply when there is no executor or no will.

Quick recap

Valid will: adult of sound mind, signed, attested by two witnesses; registration optional. Codicil amends. Probate mandatory in specified cases for wills in Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai. Hindu Class I heirs share equally; daughters are coparceners. Muslims can will only one-third to non-heirs. Trusts need settlor, trustee, beneficiary and property.
