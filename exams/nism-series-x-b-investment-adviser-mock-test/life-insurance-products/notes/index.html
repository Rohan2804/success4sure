Life Insurance Products: NISM Series X-B revision notes

Advisers must evaluate life insurance products on cost, cover and suitability. This chapter covers the main product types, policy features such as surrender and paid-up values, nomination and assignment, and protection under the Married Women's Property Act. Questions often compare two products or ask what happens when a policyholder stops paying premiums.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Term insurance

Term plans pay the sum assured only if death occurs within the term, with no maturity benefit. They provide the highest cover per rupee of premium. A 30-year-old non-smoker can typically get ₹1 crore cover for a modest annual premium. Return-of-premium versions cost much more.

2. Endowment and money-back

Endowment plans pay the sum assured plus bonuses on death or maturity. Money-back plans pay part of the sum assured at intervals during the term. Both combine insurance with low-return savings, so cover per rupee is much lower than term insurance.

3. Whole life

Whole life policies cover the insured for life (often up to age 99 or 100), with premiums paid for a limited term or throughout. They suit estate planning and legacy needs but are expensive compared with term plans.

4. Participating vs non-participating

Participating policies share in the insurer's surplus through bonuses (reversionary and terminal), which are not guaranteed in advance. Non-participating policies have fixed, guaranteed benefits declared at inception and no bonus.

5. ULIPs

Unit linked insurance plans invest premiums in market-linked funds after deducting charges such as premium allocation, policy administration, mortality and fund management charges. They have a five-year lock-in. Investment risk is borne by the policyholder, and switching between funds is allowed.

6. Annuities and pension plans

Immediate annuities convert a lump sum into a regular income starting at once; deferred annuities accumulate first and pay later. Options include life annuity, annuity with return of purchase price, joint life and increasing annuity. Annuity income is taxable as income.

7. Riders

Riders add cover for a small extra premium: accidental death benefit, critical illness, waiver of premium on disability and term riders. IRDAI caps rider premiums relative to the base policy, so riders cannot dominate the contract.

8. Surrender and paid-up value

If premiums stop after the policy has acquired value, the policyholder may surrender for cash or keep the policy as paid-up with a reduced sum assured. Paid-up sum assured = sum assured × premiums paid ÷ premiums payable. Surrender early in a policy often means significant loss.

9. Nomination and assignment

Nomination names who receives the claim; it does not transfer ownership. Assignment transfers rights in the policy, absolutely (for example to a buyer) or conditionally (for a loan). On assignment, an existing nomination is generally cancelled except in certain cases.

10. MWP Act protection

A policy taken by a married man under section 6 of the Married Women's Property Act, 1874 for his wife and/or children creates a trust; the proceeds cannot be claimed by his creditors. This is useful for business owners with personal guarantees.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Paid-up sum assured = sum assured × (number of premiums paid ÷ total premiums payable); e.g. ₹10 lakh × 6/20 = ₹3 lakh.

Common traps in the exam

  • Assuming bonuses are guaranteed in participating plans. Reversionary and terminal bonuses depend on the insurer's surplus.
  • Thinking ULIP investment risk is borne by the insurer. The policyholder bears it.
  • Treating nomination as transferring ownership. Assignment transfers ownership; nomination only names a recipient.
  • Recommending endowment plans for high cover needs. Term plans give far more cover per rupee.

Quick recap

Term plans give the most cover per rupee; endowment, money-back and whole life mix savings with low cover. Par policies earn non-guaranteed bonuses; non-par are fixed. ULIPs carry market risk and a five-year lock-in. Paid-up value = SA × paid ÷ payable. Assignment transfers rights; MWP Act protects proceeds from creditors.
