Life Insurance Products: NISM Series X-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Life Insurance Products, from the NISM Series X-B (Investment Adviser (Level 2)) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the life insurance products an adviser must evaluate: term plans, endowment and money-back plans, whole life, unit linked insurance plans (ULIPs), pension and annuity plans, and riders. It also covers participating versus non-participating policies, surrender value, paid-up value, nomination and assignment, and the Married Women's Property Act protection. When revising, compare products on cost, cover and returns, and learn why pure term cover plus separate investments often suits clients best.

Life Insurance Products questions

Question 1

Which life insurance policy provides cover for the whole of the insured's life rather than a fixed term?

  1. Term insurance policy
  2. Money-back policy
  3. Whole life policy
  4. Endowment policy of 15 years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Whole life policy

A whole life policy covers the insured for life (often up to age 99 or 100), paying on death whenever it occurs. Term, money-back and endowment plans have fixed terms.

Question 2

A participating (with-profit) policy differs from a non-participating policy because it:

  1. Offers guaranteed returns linked to the stock market
  2. Shares in the insurer's surplus via bonuses
  3. Has no surrender value at any time
  4. Cannot be bought by salaried individuals
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Shares in the insurer's surplus via bonuses

Participating policies receive bonuses from the insurer's surplus, which are not guaranteed in advance. Non-participating policies offer only contracted benefits.

Question 3

A policyholder transfers the rights under his life policy to a bank as security for a loan. This is called:

  1. Assignment
  2. Nomination
  3. Surrender
  4. Revival
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Assignment

Assignment transfers the policy's rights and benefits to another person or entity, such as a lender. A nominee only receives the claim money; ownership remains with the policyholder.

Question 4

Which rider would pay an additional sum if the insured dies in an accident?

  1. Waiver of premium rider
  2. Critical illness rider
  3. Hospital cash rider
  4. Accidental death benefit rider
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Accidental death benefit rider

An accidental death benefit rider pays an extra amount if death is caused by an accident. A waiver of premium rider stops future premiums on specified events.

Question 5

In a ULIP, the investment risk on the fund portion is borne by:

  1. The policyholder
  2. The insurance company
  3. IRDAI
  4. The fund manager personally
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The policyholder

ULIP premiums, after charges, buy units whose value depends on market performance. The policyholder bears the investment risk, unlike guaranteed traditional plans.

Question 6

A 30-year-old wants ₹1 crore of cover for 30 years at the lowest cost. Which product best meets this need?

  1. Endowment plan
  2. Whole life plan with bonuses
  3. Money-back plan
  4. Term insurance plan
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Term insurance plan

Term insurance provides pure protection with no savings element, so premiums are far lower for the same cover. Endowment and money-back plans are much costlier per rupee of cover.

Question 7

Under the free-look provision, a new life insurance policyholder can:

  1. Get a full refund at any time during the term
  2. Change the insurer without any formalities
  3. Return the policy within the free-look period
  4. Increase cover without medical tests
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Return the policy within the free-look period

The free-look period lets a new policyholder review terms and cancel the policy for a refund, after deductions for proportionate risk premium and expenses. IRDAI has extended this period to 30 days.

Question 8

A money-back policy is best described as a policy that:

  1. Pays only if the insured survives to 100
  2. Pays part of the sum assured at intervals
  3. Returns all premiums if no claim is made
  4. Pays the sum assured only on accidental death
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Pays part of the sum assured at intervals

A money-back policy pays survival benefits periodically during the term, with full sum assured payable on death. It is a variant of endowment assurance.
