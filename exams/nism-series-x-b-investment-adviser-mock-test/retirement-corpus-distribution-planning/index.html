Retirement Corpus & Distribution Planning: NISM Series X-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Retirement Corpus & Distribution Planning, from the NISM Series X-B (Investment Adviser (Level 2)) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how to calculate the corpus a client needs at retirement and how to draw it down safely. Topics include estimating first-year expenses, using real rates of return, present value of growing annuities, systematic withdrawal plans, annuities, bucket strategies, sustainable withdrawal rates and managing sequence risk. When revising, practise corpus and SIP calculations step by step, understand the trade-off between guaranteed income and flexibility, and know how to structure income from multiple sources.

Retirement Corpus & Distribution Planning questions

Question 1

A client needs ₹10 lakh in the first year of retirement, rising 6% a year, for 20 years, withdrawn at the start of each year. If the corpus earns 8%, the required corpus is closest to:

  1. ₹2.00 crore
  2. ₹1.06 crore
  3. ₹1.43 crore
  4. ₹1.68 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹1.68 crore

PV of a growing annuity due = 10 lakh × Σ (1.06/1.08)^t for t = 0 to 19 ≈ ₹1.68 crore. ₹2.00 crore ignores returns and ₹1.06 crore ignores inflation in withdrawals.

Question 2

A client wants ₹50 lakh in 15 years through a monthly SIP. Assuming 12% a year (1% a month), the monthly SIP needed is closest to:

  1. ₹27,778
  2. ₹8,200
  3. ₹10,008
  4. ₹12,500
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹10,008

Monthly SIP = FV × r ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1) = 50,00,000 × 0.01 ÷ (1.01^180 − 1) ≈ ₹10,008. ₹27,778 simply divides the target by 180 months and ignores returns.

Question 3

A retiree buys an annuity for ₹50 lakh at an annuity rate of 7% a year. Her annual annuity income is:

  1. ₹3,50,000
  2. ₹7,00,000
  3. ₹35,000
  4. ₹5,00,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹3,50,000

Annual annuity = purchase price × annuity rate = 50,00,000 × 7% = ₹3,50,000. This income is generally fixed and taxable as per slab, so it loses purchasing power over time.

Question 4

A systematic withdrawal plan (SWP) from a mutual fund helps a retiree by:

  1. Guaranteeing a fixed return for life
  2. Providing regular cash flow while staying invested
  3. Avoiding all taxes on withdrawals
  4. Locking money in for ten years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Providing regular cash flow while staying invested

An SWP redeems a set amount at intervals, offering regular income while the rest stays invested. Returns are not guaranteed, and each redemption may attract capital gains tax.

Question 5

Sequence-of-returns risk is highest:

  1. At the start of a career
  2. When the corpus is very small
  3. Just before and after retirement
  4. Only for clients with annuities
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Just before and after retirement

Poor returns soon after withdrawals begin can permanently deplete the corpus, since units are sold at low prices. This makes the years around retirement the most vulnerable.

Question 6

In a bucket strategy, the long-term bucket is usually invested in:

  1. Growth assets such as equity
  2. Savings bank account balances
  3. Overnight funds only
  4. Cash kept at home
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Growth assets such as equity

The long-term bucket is meant for expenses many years ahead, so it can hold growth assets that beat inflation. Near-term buckets hold safe, liquid assets.

Question 7

Compared with an annuity, an SWP-based retirement income plan offers:

  1. A guaranteed income for life
  2. Flexibility and access to capital
  3. Protection from all market risk
  4. Payments that never change
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Flexibility and access to capital

An SWP keeps capital accessible and can be adjusted, but income depends on market returns and the corpus can run out. Annuities give guaranteed lifelong income but lock in capital.

Question 8

A retiree withdraws 10% of his corpus each year while it earns 7%. What is the main risk?

  1. The corpus will grow without limit
  2. His tax liability will fall to zero
  3. Inflation will be eliminated
  4. The corpus may run out early
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The corpus may run out early

Withdrawing more than the corpus earns, especially after inflation, depletes capital each year and can exhaust it well before death. A sustainable withdrawal rate is needed.
