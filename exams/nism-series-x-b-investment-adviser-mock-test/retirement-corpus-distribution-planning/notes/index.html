Retirement Corpus & Distribution Planning: NISM Series X-B revision notes

This is the most calculation-heavy part of the retirement block. You must estimate the corpus needed at retirement, the monthly saving required to build it, and a sensible way to draw it down. Questions give a client's age, expenses, inflation and return assumptions and ask for a number, so practise every step with a calculator.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Step 1: first-year expense

Project today's continuing annual expenses to the retirement year. A client spending ₹3 lakh a year on continuing items, retiring in 25 years with 6% inflation, needs about ₹3 lakh × 1.06^25 ≈ ₹12.9 lakh in the first year of retirement.

2. Step 2: real rate during retirement

During retirement, expenses keep rising with inflation while the corpus earns a return. Combine them into a real rate: with 7% return and 5% inflation, real rate = 1.07 ÷ 1.05 − 1 ≈ 1.9%. Using this rate lets you treat rising withdrawals as a flat annuity.

3. Step 3: corpus as PV of an annuity

The corpus is the present value, at retirement, of the first-year expense for the retirement period at the real rate, usually as an annuity due because withdrawals start at the beginning of each year. For ₹6 lakh a year, 25 years and 2% real rate, the factor is about 19.9, giving a corpus of roughly ₹1.19 crore.

4. Step 4: saving required

Subtract the future value of existing retirement savings from the target corpus and calculate the monthly SIP needed for the gap. If the gap is ₹1 crore in 20 years at 10% a year, the required SIP is roughly ₹13,000 a month. A step-up SIP that rises with salary lowers the starting amount.

5. Systematic withdrawal plans

An SWP from mutual funds gives regular cash flows while the remaining units stay invested. Each withdrawal redeems units, so only the gain portion is taxed. SWPs offer flexibility and potential growth but no guarantee of lifelong income.

6. Annuities

Annuities give guaranteed lifelong income, removing longevity risk, but are inflexible, taxable as income and lock in prevailing rates. A common approach is to annuitise part of the corpus to cover essential expenses and keep the rest invested for growth and flexibility.

7. Bucket strategy

Split the corpus into buckets: a cash bucket for 1–3 years of expenses, an income bucket in short and medium-term debt for the next few years, and a growth bucket in equity for later years. Refill buckets periodically. This reduces the need to sell equity during market falls, managing sequence risk.

8. Sustainable withdrawal rate

The well-known 4% rule comes from US data. In India, higher inflation makes a lower or flexible initial withdrawal rate more prudent. Withdrawals should be reviewed annually against portfolio performance rather than fixed rigidly.

9. Multiple income sources

A retiree's income may combine EPS or other pensions, annuities, SCSS interest, rent, SWPs and dividends. The plan should sequence these to cover essential expenses with stable sources and discretionary expenses with market-linked sources.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Corpus = E × [1 − (1 + r)^−n] ÷ r × (1 + r), where E = first-year expense, r = real rate, n = retirement years.
  • Monthly SIP = FV × i ÷ [(1 + i)^m − 1], where i = monthly rate and m = number of months (end-of-month investment).

Common traps in the exam

  • Using the nominal return to discount retirement expenses. Use the inflation-adjusted real rate.
  • Treating withdrawals as starting at year end. Retirement withdrawals usually start immediately, so use an annuity due.
  • Assuming SWPs are guaranteed income. They depend on fund performance and can run out.
  • Applying the US 4% rule without adjustment. Indian inflation usually requires a more cautious rate.

Quick recap

Inflate today's expenses to retirement, convert return and inflation into a real rate, and compute corpus as PV of an annuity due. Find the SIP needed for the gap. Draw down using a mix of annuities for essentials and SWPs or buckets for growth. Review withdrawal rates yearly.
