Taxation of Investment Products: NISM Series X-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Taxation of Investment Products, from the NISM Series X-B (Investment Adviser (Level 2)) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how different investment products are taxed for resident individuals: equity shares and equity-oriented funds, debt funds, bonds, bank deposits, small savings, ELSS, ULIPs, NPS, gold and real estate. Key ideas are holding periods for short and long-term gains, applicable rates, exemption thresholds, tax deducted at source and treatment of dividends and interest. Rates have changed recently, so check the year in each question and focus on structure: what is taxed, when, and under which head.

Taxation of Investment Products questions

Question 1

A resident sells listed equity shares held for two years with a long-term gain of ₹2,00,000 in FY 2025-26, with no other gains. Ignoring cess, the tax is:

  1. ₹25,000
  2. ₹20,000
  3. ₹12,500
  4. ₹9,375
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹9,375

Long-term gains on listed equity above ₹1,25,000 are taxed at 12.5%: (2,00,000 − 1,25,000) × 12.5% = ₹9,375. ₹25,000 wrongly taxes the whole gain at 12.5%.

Question 2

Short-term capital gains on listed equity shares sold after 23 July 2024 are taxed at:

  1. 15%
  2. Slab rate
  3. 20%
  4. 10%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 20%

The Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024 raised the STCG rate on listed equity and equity-oriented funds, where STT is paid, from 15% to 20% for transfers on or after 23 July 2024.

Question 3

Interest on a bank fixed deposit is taxed for an individual:

  1. At a flat 10% as capital gains
  2. At slab rates under other sources
  3. Only when the deposit matures
  4. Not at all if below ₹10 lakh
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. At slab rates under other sources

FD interest is income from other sources taxed at slab rates, usually on an accrual basis each year. Banks deduct TDS above thresholds, but TDS is not the final tax.

Question 4

Which investment gives tax-free interest and maturity proceeds for an individual?

  1. Public Provident Fund
  2. Bank recurring deposit
  3. Corporate non-convertible debentures
  4. Senior Citizens' Savings Scheme
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Public Provident Fund

PPF is in the exempt-exempt-exempt category: interest and maturity amounts are tax-free. Interest on RDs, NCDs and SCSS is taxable at slab rates.

Question 5

Units of a debt mutual fund bought in 2024 are sold after three years at a gain. How is the gain taxed?

  1. At 12.5% as long-term gains
  2. At 20% with indexation
  3. Fully exempt after three years
  4. At slab rates, irrespective of holding period
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. At slab rates, irrespective of holding period

For specified funds with up to 35% in equity, units bought on or after 1 April 2023 have gains taxed at slab rates regardless of holding period, with no indexation.

Question 6

On sale of a residential house, what is the holding period after which the gain is long-term?

  1. More than 12 months
  2. More than 24 months
  3. More than 36 months
  4. More than 60 months
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. More than 24 months

Immovable property such as a house is a long-term capital asset if held for more than 24 months. Listed shares need more than 12 months.

Question 7

Dividends received by a resident individual from Indian companies are:

  1. Fully exempt
  2. Taxed at 10% flat
  3. Taxable at slab rates
  4. Taxed only in the company's hands
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Taxable at slab rates

Since the abolition of dividend distribution tax from April 2020, dividends are taxable in the hands of shareholders at their slab rates, with TDS above a threshold.

Question 8

Sovereign gold bonds held until maturity give investors:

  1. Exemption on redemption gains
  2. Taxable gains at 30% on redemption
  3. Tax-free interest every year
  4. A deduction for the purchase cost
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Exemption on redemption gains

Capital gains on redemption of SGBs at maturity are exempt for individuals. The annual interest is taxable at slab rates, and there is no deduction on purchase.
