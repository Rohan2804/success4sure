Taxation of Investment Products: NISM Series X-B revision notes

Post-tax return is what clients keep, so advisers must know how each product is taxed. This chapter covers holding periods, capital gains rates, exemptions and TDS across equity, debt, insurance, retirement, gold and real estate. Rates changed in July 2024 and the Income-tax Act, 2025 took effect in April 2026, so always note the year assumed in a question.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Listed equity and equity funds

For listed shares and equity-oriented funds, gains are long-term after 12 months. As of 2026, short-term gains are taxed at 20% and long-term gains at 12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 lakh a year, provided STT is paid (check current rates). There is no indexation.

2. Debt mutual funds

Units of specified debt funds bought on or after 1 April 2023 are taxed at slab rates regardless of holding period. This removed the earlier long-term benefit with indexation. Advisers should compare post-tax yields of debt funds and FDs accordingly.

3. Bonds and debentures

Interest is taxed at slab rates. For listed bonds, gains after 12 months are long-term at 12.5% without indexation; unlisted bonds and debentures are taxed at slab rates on gains. Tax-free bonds pay exempt interest but gains on sale are taxable.

4. Bank deposits and small savings

FD and RD interest is taxed at slab rates on an accrual basis, with TDS above a threshold. PPF has exempt-exempt-exempt status: contributions (old regime), interest and maturity are all exempt. NSC interest is taxable but deemed reinvested.

5. ELSS

Equity-linked savings schemes have a three-year lock-in and qualify for deduction under the old regime within the overall limit. Gains on redemption are taxed like other equity funds. Under the new regime, there is no deduction benefit.

6. ULIPs and life insurance

Maturity proceeds of life policies are exempt if conditions on premium relative to sum assured are met. For ULIPs issued after February 2021 with annual premium above ₹2.5 lakh, and non-ULIP policies above ₹5 lakh premium from April 2023, maturity proceeds are taxable. Death benefits remain exempt.

7. NPS

Contributions get deductions under specified provisions, including employer contributions under the new regime. At exit, the lump sum withdrawal up to the permitted limit is exempt, while annuity income is taxable as income. Tier II has no such tax benefits for most subscribers.

8. Gold and real estate

Physical gold, gold funds and real estate become long-term after 24 months and are taxed at 12.5% without indexation. For property bought before 23 July 2024, resident individuals may choose the older 20% with indexation route. Sovereign gold bonds held to maturity give exempt gains.

9. Dividends and TDS

Dividends are taxed at slab rates in the investor's hands, with TDS by the company or fund above a threshold. Buyback proceeds are now taxed as dividends in shareholders' hands (from October 2024). Interest TDS can be avoided by eligible investors submitting declarations.

Common traps in the exam

  • Assuming debt funds still get indexation. Units bought from April 2023 are taxed at slab rates.
  • Thinking all life insurance maturity proceeds are tax-free. High-premium policies lose the exemption.
  • Using a 36-month holding period for listed equity. It is 12 months; 24 months applies to gold, property and unlisted assets.
  • Treating the NPS annuity as tax-free. Only the permitted lump sum is exempt; annuity income is taxable.

Quick recap

Listed equity: long-term after 12 months, STCG 20%, LTCG 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh (as of 2026). Debt funds bought from April 2023: slab rates. Gold and property: 24 months, 12.5% without indexation. PPF is EEE. ELSS: three-year lock-in. High-premium ULIPs and policies lose exemption. Dividends at slab rates.
