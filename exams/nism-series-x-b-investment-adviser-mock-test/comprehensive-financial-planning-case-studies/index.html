Comprehensive Financial Planning & Case Studies: NISM Series X-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Comprehensive Financial Planning & Case Studies, from the NISM Series X-B (Investment Adviser (Level 2)) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter brings together all topics into comprehensive financial plans for clients. It covers gathering data, analysing the current position, prioritising goals, integrating insurance, investments, retirement, tax and estate planning, presenting recommendations and reviewing the plan. Case-study questions test judgement: what to address first, how to resolve conflicting goals and what to change when circumstances shift. When revising, practise full calculations and focus on logical, client-centred recommendations.

Comprehensive Financial Planning & Case Studies questions

Question 1

A 35-year-old client has no life insurance, ₹5 lakh credit card debt and plans to start equity SIPs. What should come first?

  1. Starting equity SIPs of the full surplus
  2. Buying a second home with a loan
  3. Investing in a new fund offer
  4. Clearing the card debt and buying term cover
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Clearing the card debt and buying term cover

Protecting dependants and eliminating expensive debt come before long-term investing. Card interest far exceeds expected investment returns, and lack of cover exposes the family.

Question 2

A client needs ₹30 lakh for a goal in 4 years. Which allocation is most suitable?

  1. Entirely in small-cap equity funds
  2. Entirely in a sector fund
  3. Mostly debt with limited equity
  4. Entirely in cryptocurrency
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Mostly debt with limited equity

Short horizons leave little time to recover from equity falls, so most of the money should be in debt, with only limited equity exposure if any.

Question 3

A married couple has a child. Which change should trigger an immediate plan review?

  1. A routine rise in the index
  2. The birth of the child
  3. A change in the bank's branch timings
  4. A new mobile phone
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The birth of the child

A new dependant changes insurance needs, cash flows, goals and estate plans. Routine market moves do not by themselves require a full plan review.

Question 4

A client has two goals competing for limited surplus: a car in two years and retirement in 25 years. A sensible approach is to:

  1. Fund retirement and size the car goal to fit
  2. Ignore retirement until the car is bought
  3. Take a large loan for both goals
  4. Put all savings into the car goal
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Fund retirement and size the car goal to fit

Long-term needs like retirement benefit from early saving, so they should not be neglected. The discretionary goal can be scaled down or delayed to fit the budget.

Question 5

A client in the highest tax bracket asks for a debt option. Which factor is most relevant to compare?

  1. Pre-tax returns only
  2. Post-tax returns
  3. The colour of the brochure
  4. The number of branches of the issuer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Post-tax returns

For high-bracket clients, tax treatment can change rankings significantly, so comparison should be on post-tax, risk-adjusted returns.

Question 6

A financial plan's recommendations are presented to the client. What should follow?

  1. Locking the plan with no changes ever
  2. Selling all existing investments at once
  3. Implementation and periodic monitoring
  4. Sending the plan only to the bank
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Implementation and periodic monitoring

After agreement, the plan is implemented and regularly monitored and revised. Plans must adapt to changes; wholesale selling may cause unnecessary costs.

Question 7

A client's education goal costs ₹15 lakh today and is 10 years away. At 8% education inflation, the future cost is closest to:

  1. ₹32.4 lakh
  2. ₹27.0 lakh
  3. ₹15.0 lakh
  4. ₹40.0 lakh
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹32.4 lakh

Future cost = 15 lakh × 1.08^10 ≈ ₹32.4 lakh. ₹27.0 lakh uses simple growth of 8% a year, which understates compounding inflation.

Question 8

Which action best shows client-centred advice in a case where the client is risk-averse but needs higher returns for a goal?

  1. Push him into high-risk products without discussion
  2. Promise him high returns with low risk
  3. Ignore the goal and plan only for tax
  4. Discuss trade-offs like saving more or delaying
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Discuss trade-offs like saving more or delaying

When risk tolerance and required return conflict, the adviser should explain options such as increasing savings, extending the timeline or revising the goal, rather than ignoring the conflict.
