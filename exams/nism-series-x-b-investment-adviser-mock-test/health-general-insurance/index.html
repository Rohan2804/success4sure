Health & General Insurance: NISM Series X-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Health & General Insurance, from the NISM Series X-B (Investment Adviser (Level 2)) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers non-life insurance for individuals: health insurance (indemnity hospitalisation policies, family floaters, critical illness, top-up and super top-up covers), and general insurance such as motor, home and personal accident covers. Key concepts include sum insured, deductibles, co-payment, room-rent limits, waiting periods, pre-existing diseases, cashless claims and no-claim bonus. When revising, practise claim calculations with deductibles and co-payments and know how standard rules on waiting periods and portability protect policyholders.

Health & General Insurance questions

Question 1

A top-up policy has a deductible of ₹5 lakh. A single hospital bill is ₹7 lakh. Ignoring other limits, how much will the top-up insurer pay?

  1. ₹7 lakh
  2. ₹5 lakh
  3. ₹2 lakh
  4. Nothing
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹2 lakh

A top-up pays only the amount above the deductible for a single claim: 7 lakh − 5 lakh = ₹2 lakh. The first ₹5 lakh must be met by the base policy or the insured.

Question 2

A critical illness policy differs from a hospitalisation policy because it:

  1. Reimburses actual hospital bills only
  2. Covers only outpatient consultation
  3. Pays only if the insured dies
  4. Pays a lump sum on diagnosis
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Pays a lump sum on diagnosis

Critical illness cover is a benefit policy paying a fixed sum on diagnosis of a listed illness, regardless of actual bills. Hospitalisation policies are indemnity covers.

Question 3

Portability in health insurance allows a policyholder to:

  1. Switch insurer while keeping waiting-period credit
  2. Transfer the policy to a friend
  3. Convert health cover into life cover
  4. Claim from two insurers for the same bill
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Switch insurer while keeping waiting-period credit

Portability lets a policyholder move to another insurer without losing credit for waiting periods already served. It does not allow transfer to another person or double recovery.

Question 4

A no-claim bonus in health insurance usually results in:

  1. A cash refund of all premiums paid
  2. Higher sum insured after claim-free years
  3. A lower deductible for life
  4. Removal of all waiting periods
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Higher sum insured after claim-free years

NCB rewards claim-free years, typically by increasing the sum insured (cumulative bonus) or lowering premium. It is not a refund of premiums.

Question 5

A personal accident policy typically covers:

  1. Hospital bills for any illness
  2. Damage to the insured's car
  3. Accidental death and disability
  4. Theft of household goods
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Accidental death and disability

Personal accident covers pay for death and permanent or temporary disability caused by accidents. Illness, motor damage and burglary need other covers.

Question 6

Under IRDAI's rules, the maximum waiting period that insurers can apply for pre-existing diseases in health policies is:

  1. 3 years
  2. 8 years
  3. 1 month
  4. 10 years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 3 years

IRDAI's 2024 health insurance norms cap the waiting period for pre-existing diseases at three years. This replaced the earlier maximum of four years.

Question 7

Which motor insurance cover pays for damage to the insured's own vehicle as well as third-party liability?

  1. Third-party liability only policy
  2. Personal accident cover for the owner-driver
  3. Engine protection add-on alone
  4. A comprehensive (package) policy
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A comprehensive (package) policy

A comprehensive policy combines own-damage cover with mandatory third-party liability. A standalone third-party policy does not pay for damage to one's own vehicle.

Question 8

A health policy has a room-rent limit of 1% of sum insured per day. Choosing a costlier room usually leads to:

  1. The insurer paying the full bill anyway
  2. Proportionate deduction from related charges
  3. Automatic cancellation of the policy
  4. A refund of the premium paid
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Proportionate deduction from related charges

With room-rent caps, insurers often reduce associated charges proportionately when a costlier room is taken, so the claim payout falls more than the room-rent difference.
