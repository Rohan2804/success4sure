Health & General Insurance: NISM Series X-B revision notes

Non-life insurance protects a household's savings from large medical bills and property losses. This chapter covers health covers, top-ups, critical illness and personal accident policies, plus motor and home insurance. The exam often asks for claim calculations involving deductibles, co-payments and room-rent limits, so practise these until they are easy.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Indemnity health policies

Standard hospitalisation policies reimburse actual medical expenses up to the sum insured, covering in-patient treatment, pre- and post-hospitalisation and day care procedures. They are indemnity contracts: the claim cannot exceed actual expenses. Cashless claims are settled directly with network hospitals via the TPA or insurer.

2. Individual vs family floater

An individual policy gives each member their own sum insured. A family floater shares one sum insured across the family, which is cheaper but can be exhausted by one big claim. Floaters suit young families; individual covers suit older members with higher claim probability.

3. Top-up vs super top-up

A top-up pays when a single claim exceeds the deductible. A super top-up pays when total claims in the policy year exceed the deductible. With a ₹3 lakh deductible and two claims of ₹2 lakh each, a top-up pays nothing, while a super top-up pays ₹1 lakh.

4. Co-payment and deductibles

Co-payment means the insured bears a fixed percentage of each admissible claim. With 20% co-pay on a ₹1,50,000 claim, the insurer pays ₹1,20,000. A deductible is a fixed amount the insured bears first. Both reduce premiums but increase out-of-pocket costs.

5. Room-rent limits

Some policies cap room rent, for example 1% of the sum insured per day. If a client chooses a costlier room, many associated charges are reduced proportionately, not just the room rent. This proportionate deduction is a common cause of reduced claims.

6. Waiting periods

Policies have an initial waiting period (often 30 days except accidents), specific-disease waiting periods, and pre-existing disease waiting periods. IRDAI rules cap PED waiting at 36 months and introduced a moratorium after 60 months of continuous cover, after which claims cannot be contested except for fraud (as of 2026).

7. Critical illness and personal accident

Critical illness policies pay a lump sum on diagnosis of listed illnesses, regardless of actual expenses; they are benefit, not indemnity, policies. Personal accident policies pay for accidental death and permanent or temporary disablement, often as a percentage of the sum insured.

8. No-claim bonus and portability

A claim-free year earns a cumulative bonus, increasing the sum insured or reducing premium. Portability lets policyholders move to another insurer while keeping credit for waiting periods already served. Continuity benefits make switching less risky than it seems.

9. Motor and home insurance

Third-party motor insurance is mandatory under the Motor Vehicles Act; own-damage cover is optional. Depreciation applies to parts unless a zero-depreciation add-on is bought. Home insurance covers the structure and contents against fire, natural perils and burglary, and should reflect reconstruction cost, not market value.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Insurer's payment with co-pay = admissible claim × (1 − co-pay %).
  • Super top-up payout = total annual claims − deductible (if positive), up to the super top-up sum insured.

Common traps in the exam

  • Confusing top-up and super top-up. Top-up looks at each claim; super top-up looks at the year's aggregate.
  • Thinking critical illness reimburses bills. It pays a fixed lump sum on diagnosis.
  • Ignoring proportionate deduction. A higher room category reduces many related charges, not only room rent.
  • Insuring a house at market value. Cover should be based on reconstruction cost; land is not insurable.

Quick recap

Indemnity health policies reimburse actual costs; floaters share one sum insured. Top-up works per claim, super top-up on aggregate. Co-pay is a percentage; deductible is a fixed amount. PED waiting capped at 36 months with a 60-month moratorium. Critical illness pays lump sums. Third-party motor cover is compulsory.
