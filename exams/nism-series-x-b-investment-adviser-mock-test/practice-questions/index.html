Free NISM Series X-B practice questions

Question 1 · Risk Management & Insurance Concepts

While buying a life policy, Kunal did not disclose that he was being treated for diabetes. Which insurance principle has he breached?

  1. Indemnity
  2. Subrogation
  3. Contribution
  4. Utmost good faith
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Utmost good faith

Insurance contracts require utmost good faith, under which the proposer must disclose all material facts; non-disclosure can lead to the claim being repudiated.

Question 2 · Life Insurance Products

What is the lock-in period applicable to a unit-linked insurance plan (ULIP)?

  1. 5 years
  2. 3 years
  3. 7 years
  4. 10 years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 5 years

IRDAI rules impose a five-year lock-in on ULIPs, during which money cannot be withdrawn even if the policy is discontinued.

Question 3 · Health & General Insurance

A health policy has a 20% co-payment clause. A policyholder makes an admissible hospitalisation claim of ₹2,00,000 within the sum insured. How much will the insurer pay?

  1. ₹2,00,000
  2. ₹40,000
  3. ₹1,60,000
  4. ₹1,80,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹1,60,000

With a 20% co-payment, the insured bears 20% (₹40,000) and the insurer pays 80% of ₹2,00,000 = ₹1,60,000.

Question 4 · Insurance Needs Analysis

Using the need-based approach: a client's outstanding home loan is ₹40 lakh, children's education needs ₹30 lakh (present value), family living-expense corpus needs ₹1.2 crore, existing investments are ₹50 lakh and existing life cover is ₹25 lakh. What additional life cover is required?

  1. ₹1.15 crore
  2. ₹1.90 crore
  3. ₹1.40 crore
  4. ₹1.65 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹1.15 crore

Total need = 40 + 30 + 120 = ₹190 lakh; less resources of 50 + 25 = ₹75 lakh; additional cover = ₹115 lakh = ₹1.15 crore.

Question 5 · Retirement Planning Concepts

A couple's current living expenses are ₹50,000 a month. With 6% annual inflation, what monthly amount will they need to maintain the same lifestyle in 25 years (approximately)?

  1. ₹2.15 lakh
  2. ₹1.25 lakh
  3. ₹1.60 lakh
  4. ₹3.05 lakh
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹2.15 lakh

Future expense = 50,000 × (1.06)^25 = 50,000 × 4.2919 ≈ ₹2,14,594, i.e. about ₹2.15 lakh a month.

Question 6 · Retirement Products & Pension Schemes

What is the basic tenure of a Public Provident Fund (PPF) account and the maximum deposit allowed in a financial year?

  1. 15 years; ₹1.5 lakh
  2. 10 years; ₹2 lakh
  3. 15 years; ₹2.5 lakh
  4. 21 years; ₹1.5 lakh
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 15 years; ₹1.5 lakh

A PPF account matures after 15 years (extendable in blocks of five years) and accepts up to ₹1.5 lakh per financial year.

Question 7 · Retirement Corpus & Distribution Planning

A client will need ₹6 lakh a year in today's terms after retirement for 25 years, withdrawn at the beginning of each year. If the corpus earns a real (inflation-adjusted) return of 2%, what corpus is needed at retirement in today's terms (approximately)?

  1. ₹1.50 crore
  2. ₹1.19 crore
  3. ₹1.06 crore
  4. ₹0.98 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹1.19 crore

PV of annuity due = 6,00,000 × [(1 − 1.02^−25) ÷ 0.02] × 1.02 = 6,00,000 × 19.524 × 1.02 ≈ ₹1.19 crore; ₹1.50 crore ignores discounting.

Question 8 · Income Tax Fundamentals

How does residential status affect the scope of an individual's taxable income in India?

  1. Both residents and non-residents are taxed only on salary income
  2. A resident is generally taxed on global income, while a non-resident is taxed only on income received, accruing or arising in India
  3. Non-residents are taxed on global income and residents only on Indian income
  4. Residential status matters only for companies
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A resident is generally taxed on global income, while a non-resident is taxed only on income received, accruing or arising in India

Residential status determines scope: residents are taxed on worldwide income, whereas non-residents are taxed only on India-sourced or India-received income.

Question 9 · Taxation of Investment Products

How are capital gains taxed on a pure debt mutual fund (investing almost entirely in bonds and money market instruments) bought after 1 April 2023?

  1. At a concessional long-term rate after 36 months with indexation
  2. Fully exempt if held for more than 3 years
  3. At a flat 10% without indexation
  4. At the investor's applicable slab rate, regardless of holding period
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. At the investor's applicable slab rate, regardless of holding period

Since 1 April 2023, gains on such debt funds are deemed short-term and taxed at slab rates whatever the holding period.

Question 10 · Wealth Management & Estate Planning Concepts

What is the primary objective of estate planning?

  1. Maximising short-term portfolio returns
  2. Orderly transfer of a person's assets to intended beneficiaries with minimum disputes, delay and cost
  3. Avoiding all taxes legally payable during one's lifetime
  4. Increasing the person's borrowing capacity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Orderly transfer of a person's assets to intended beneficiaries with minimum disputes, delay and cost

Estate planning arranges how wealth passes on death or incapacity so that beneficiaries receive it smoothly as intended.

Question 11 · Wills, Succession & Trusts

Which of the following is required for a will to be validly executed in India (other than a privileged will)?

  1. Compulsory registration with the sub-registrar
  2. Execution on stamp paper of a specified value
  3. Signature of the testator, attested by at least two witnesses
  4. Approval by all legal heirs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Signature of the testator, attested by at least two witnesses

An unprivileged will must be signed by the testator and attested by two or more witnesses; registration and stamp paper are not mandatory.

Question 12 · Comprehensive Financial Planning & Case Studies

Amit, 40, earns ₹24 lakh a year, his wife is a homemaker and they have two school-going children. He has a ₹50 lakh home loan, ₹20 lakh in mutual funds and a ₹50 lakh term cover. What is the most pressing gap in his plan?

  1. He holds too little gold
  2. He should prepay the home loan using all mutual funds
  3. He should stop investing for retirement
  4. His life cover is inadequate relative to his liabilities and family's future needs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. His life cover is inadequate relative to his liabilities and family's future needs

As the sole earner with a large loan and dependants, a ₹50 lakh cover barely matches the loan; protection must be fixed before growth goals.

Question 13 · Risk Management & Insurance Concepts

Why does the principle of indemnity generally not apply to life insurance?

  1. Because life insurance is not a contract
  2. Because the value of a human life cannot be precisely measured, so a fixed sum assured is paid
  3. Because life insurance claims are always paid in instalments
  4. Because life insurance does not require insurable interest
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Because the value of a human life cannot be precisely measured, so a fixed sum assured is paid

Indemnity restores the insured to the pre-loss position, which works for measurable property losses; life insurance is a benefit policy paying a pre-agreed sum.

Question 14 · Life Insurance Products

A policyholder stops paying premiums on a traditional endowment policy after it has acquired a surrender value and does not surrender it. What generally happens?

  1. The policy lapses with no benefit at all
  2. The insurer refunds all premiums with interest
  3. The policy continues as a paid-up policy with a reduced sum assured
  4. The full original sum assured stays in force without further premiums
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The policy continues as a paid-up policy with a reduced sum assured

Once a traditional policy acquires surrender value, non-payment converts it into a paid-up policy whose sum assured is reduced in proportion to premiums paid.

Question 15 · Health & General Insurance

How does a super top-up health policy differ from a regular top-up policy?

  1. A super top-up has no deductible at all
  2. A regular top-up covers only critical illnesses
  3. A super top-up applies its deductible to the total of claims in a policy year, not to each claim separately
  4. A super top-up can be bought only by senior citizens
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A super top-up applies its deductible to the total of claims in a policy year, not to each claim separately

A regular top-up pays only when a single claim exceeds the deductible; a super top-up pays once aggregate claims in the year cross the deductible.
