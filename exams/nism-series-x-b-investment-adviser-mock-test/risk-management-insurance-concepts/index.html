Risk Management & Insurance Concepts: NISM Series X-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Risk Management & Insurance Concepts, from the NISM Series X-B (Investment Adviser (Level 2)) syllabus.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces personal risk management and the principles of insurance. It covers identifying risks, methods of handling them (avoidance, reduction, retention and transfer), pooling of risk, and legal principles such as utmost good faith, insurable interest, indemnity, subrogation, contribution and proximate cause. When revising, match each principle to a claim scenario, understand why some risks are better retained and others transferred, and know how insurance fits into a client's overall financial plan.

Risk Management & Insurance Concepts questions

Question 1

A client keeps a large emergency fund to meet small medical bills himself instead of buying a low-deductible policy. This method of handling risk is:

  1. Risk avoidance
  2. Risk transfer
  3. Risk elimination
  4. Risk retention
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Risk retention

Bearing small, predictable losses from one's own resources is risk retention. Insurance would be risk transfer, while avoidance means not undertaking the risky activity at all.

Question 2

After paying a car damage claim, an insurer recovers the amount from the third party who caused the accident. This is based on the principle of:

  1. Contribution
  2. Utmost good faith
  3. Subrogation
  4. Insurable interest
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Subrogation

Subrogation lets the insurer, after paying a claim, step into the insured's shoes to recover from the party responsible. It prevents the insured from being paid twice.

Question 3

A house worth ₹50 lakh is insured with two insurers for ₹50 lakh each. A fire causes ₹10 lakh of damage. Under the principle of contribution, the owner can recover:

  1. ₹10 lakh in total, shared by insurers
  2. ₹20 lakh, ₹10 lakh from each insurer
  3. ₹10 lakh from each insurer separately
  4. Nothing, because double insurance is illegal
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹10 lakh in total, shared by insurers

Contribution ensures the insured recovers only the actual loss, with insurers sharing it rateably. Indemnity prevents profit from insurance, so ₹20 lakh cannot be recovered.

Question 4

Which risk is generally considered insurable?

  1. Loss from a fall in share prices
  2. Accidental fire damage to a factory
  3. Loss due to a business decision going wrong
  4. A certain loss that will definitely happen
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Accidental fire damage to a factory

Insurable risks are pure, accidental, measurable and affect many similar exposures. Speculative risks like share price falls and certain losses are not insurable.

Question 5

Pooling of risk in insurance works because:

  1. Every policyholder is guaranteed to make a claim
  2. The insurer invests only in fixed deposits
  3. Losses of a few are paid from many premiums
  4. Premiums are refunded if no claim is made
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Losses of a few are paid from many premiums

Insurance pools premiums from many people facing similar risks; only some suffer losses, which are paid from the common fund. The law of large numbers makes losses predictable.

Question 6

A client installs smoke detectors and fire extinguishers in his shop. This is an example of:

  1. Risk transfer
  2. Risk retention
  3. Risk avoidance
  4. Risk reduction
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Risk reduction

Taking steps to lower the likelihood or severity of a loss is risk reduction or loss control. It complements insurance but does not transfer the risk to an insurer.

Question 7

A death occurred because an accident led to an injury that caused fatal infection. The principle used to decide whether the accident cover applies is:

  1. Proximate cause
  2. Indemnity
  3. Contribution
  4. Subrogation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Proximate cause

Proximate cause identifies the dominant, effective cause in an unbroken chain of events. If the accident set the chain in motion, the accident cover typically responds.

Question 8

Which statement about utmost good faith is correct?

  1. Only the insurer must disclose facts
  2. Both proposer and insurer must disclose material facts
  3. Only facts asked in the form need disclosure
  4. It applies only to motor insurance
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Both proposer and insurer must disclose material facts

Utmost good faith requires full disclosure of all material facts by both parties. Non-disclosure by the proposer can make the policy voidable, and insurers must also explain terms fairly.
