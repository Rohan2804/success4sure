Wealth Management & Estate Planning Concepts: NISM Series X-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Wealth Management & Estate Planning Concepts, from the NISM Series X-B (Investment Adviser (Level 2)) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces wealth management for high-net-worth clients and the basics of estate planning: ways of transferring wealth, nomination versus inheritance, joint holding, gifts, powers of attorney, wills and trusts, and the importance of documentation and record keeping. When revising, understand the different roles of nominee, legal heir, executor and trustee, how various tools fit different family situations, and why estate planning is part of every financial plan, not only for the very wealthy.

Wealth Management & Estate Planning Concepts questions

Question 1

Under the Companies Act, a nominee of shares receives the shares on the holder's death. According to the Supreme Court, the nominee:

  1. Becomes the absolute owner in all cases
  2. Must sell them within one year
  3. Has no right to receive them
  4. Holds them for the legal heirs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Holds them for the legal heirs

The Supreme Court has held that nomination does not override succession laws; the nominee receives the securities but holds them for the legal heirs or will beneficiaries.

Question 2

A client holds a bank account jointly with 'either or survivor' mandate. On his death, the bank will:

  1. Freeze the account until a probate is obtained
  2. Pay the surviving joint holder
  3. Transfer the balance to the government
  4. Pay only the legal heirs named in a will
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Pay the surviving joint holder

With an either-or-survivor mandate, the survivor can operate the account and receive the balance. The survivor may still hold it for heirs under succession law.

Question 3

Which document lets a person appoint someone to manage his financial affairs while he is alive?

  1. Will
  2. Probate
  3. Power of attorney
  4. Succession certificate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Power of attorney

A power of attorney authorises an agent to act on the principal's behalf during his lifetime. A will operates only after death, and probate and succession certificates are court documents.

Question 4

Why is keeping an updated list of assets, accounts and policies important in estate planning?

  1. It helps heirs trace and claim assets
  2. It reduces the income tax rate payable
  3. It replaces the need for a will
  4. It is required to open a demat account
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. It helps heirs trace and claim assets

Heirs often struggle to locate assets, leading to unclaimed deposits and policies. A clear record speeds up transmission and reduces disputes.

Question 5

Which is a common objective of wealth management for an HNI family?

  1. Avoiding all forms of investment risk
  2. Holding only cash in savings accounts
  3. Maximising the number of bank accounts
  4. Preserving and transferring wealth across generations
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Preserving and transferring wealth across generations

Wealth management integrates investment, tax, risk and estate planning to grow, protect and pass on wealth. Avoiding all risk or holding only cash would erode real value.

Question 6

A gift of money from a father to his daughter is:

  1. Taxable as her income above ₹50,000
  2. Exempt in her hands, as she is a relative
  3. Taxable in the father's hands at 30%
  4. Prohibited under Indian law
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Exempt in her hands, as she is a relative

Gifts received from specified relatives, including parents, are not taxable in the recipient's hands. However, income later earned on certain gifts may be clubbed in specific cases, such as gifts to a spouse.

Question 7

Estate planning for a client with a child having special needs often uses:

  1. A trust to manage assets for the child
  2. Only a joint bank account with the child
  3. A nomination in favour of a minor sibling
  4. No planning, as courts will decide
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A trust to manage assets for the child

A trust can provide professional management and ongoing support for a dependent who cannot manage money, with clear instructions on use of funds.

Question 8

An executor of a will is responsible for:

  1. Writing the will on behalf of the testator
  2. Challenging the will in court
  3. Carrying out the will's instructions
  4. Collecting income tax from the heirs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Carrying out the will's instructions

The executor administers the estate after death: collecting assets, paying debts and distributing property as per the will, and may apply for probate where needed.
