Issue Management – Important Terms: NISM Series IX revision notes

Questions in this chapter test precise vocabulary used under the SEBI ICDR Regulations. Many options differ only in a definition, so learn exactly what each term means, who each investor category includes, how price discovery works in book building and what each offer document contains. This chapter carries a large share of marks.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Fixed price vs book building

In a fixed price issue, the price is stated upfront in the offer document. In book building, the issuer announces a price band and investors bid within it; the final price is discovered based on demand. Most Indian IPOs use book building, and demand is shown on exchange websites during the bidding period.

2. Price band

The floor price is the lower end and the cap price the upper end of the band. Under ICDR, the cap must not exceed 120% of the floor, and the cap must be at least 105% of the floor (as of 2026). For example, with a floor of ₹200, the cap can be anywhere from ₹210 to ₹240.

3. Cut-off price

Retail investors and employees can bid at 'cut-off', meaning they agree to buy at whatever final price is discovered within the band. Their bid amount is blocked at the cap price. QIBs and non-institutional investors cannot bid at cut-off and must specify a price.

4. Investor categories

QIBs include mutual funds, FPIs (other than individuals and family offices), banks, insurers, pension and provident funds, AIFs and similar institutions. Non-institutional investors bid above ₹2 lakh; retail individual investors bid up to ₹2 lakh. Employee and shareholder reservations may also exist.

5. Anchor investors

Anchor investors are QIBs who bid one working day before the issue opens, applying for at least ₹10 crore. Up to 60% of the QIB portion can go to anchors. Half the anchor shares are locked in for 30 days and the rest for 90 days after allotment (check current rules, as SEBI revised anchor reservations in 2025).

6. Offer documents

The draft red herring prospectus is filed with SEBI for observations. The red herring prospectus contains all details except price and issue size and is filed with the Registrar of Companies before the issue opens. The prospectus is filed after pricing. An abridged prospectus accompanies application forms.

7. Fresh issue and offer for sale

A fresh issue creates new shares and brings money to the company, diluting existing holders. An offer for sale is the sale of existing shares by promoters or shareholders, bringing no money to the company. Many IPOs combine both.

8. Green shoe option

The green shoe or over-allotment option allows allotting up to 15% more shares than the issue size, with a stabilising agent using the extra proceeds to buy shares in the market if the price falls below issue price after listing. It supports post-listing price stability.

9. ASBA and UPI

All public issue applications must use ASBA, which blocks funds in the investor's account. Individual bids up to the prescribed limit can use the UPI mechanism through a sponsor bank, with the investor approving the mandate in their UPI app.

10. Basis of allotment

After the issue closes, the registrar, lead managers and the designated exchange finalise the basis of allotment. In retail oversubscription, the minimum bid lot is allotted to as many applicants as possible by lottery. Listing must occur within the timeline prescribed, now T+3 after issue closure.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Maximum cap price = 1.20 × floor price; minimum cap = 1.05 × floor price.

Common traps in the exam

  • Allowing QIBs or NIIs to bid at cut-off. Only retail and employee bidders can.
  • Getting the price band rule wrong. The cap can be at most 120% of the floor, not 120% higher.
  • Treating the RHP as having the final price. The RHP lacks price and size; the prospectus has them.
  • Confusing OFS and fresh issue. Only a fresh issue brings money to the company.

Quick recap

Book building: price band with cap up to 120% of floor; final price discovered. Retail and employees can bid at cut-off. QIBs are institutions; NIIs bid above ₹2 lakh; retail up to ₹2 lakh. Anchors bid a day early, minimum ₹10 crore, up to 60% of QIB book. DRHP to SEBI, RHP without price, prospectus with price. Green shoe up to 15%.
