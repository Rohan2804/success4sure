What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the vocabulary of public issues under the SEBI (ICDR) Regulations: book building, fixed price issue, price band, floor price, cap price, cut-off price, anchor investors, QIBs, non-institutional and retail investors, red herring prospectus, abridged prospectus, offer for sale, fresh issue, green shoe option, ASBA, UPI mandate, basis of allotment and listing. When revising, be precise about who each investor category includes, how prices are discovered in book building and what each document contains.

Issue Management – Important Terms questions

Question 1

In a book-built issue, the price band is announced and the floor price is ₹400. What is the maximum cap price permitted?

  1. ₹440
  2. ₹480
  3. ₹500
  4. ₹420
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹480

The cap of the price band cannot exceed 120% of the floor price, so the maximum cap = 1.2 × ₹400 = ₹480. ₹500 would be 125% of the floor and is not permitted.

Question 2

Which document contains the final issue price and number of securities after book building is completed?

  1. Prospectus filed with the Registrar of Companies
  2. Draft red herring prospectus
  3. Abridged prospectus attached to the bid form
  4. Price band advertisement
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Prospectus filed with the Registrar of Companies

After bids close, the final price and issue size are included in the prospectus filed with the RoC. The DRHP and RHP do not contain the final price, and the price band advertisement only states the range.

Question 3

Bidders applying for more than ₹2 lakh in a public issue who are not QIBs fall under the category of:

  1. Retail individual investors
  2. Anchor investors
  3. Qualified institutional buyers
  4. Non-institutional investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Non-institutional investors

Applicants who are not QIBs and bid above ₹2 lakh are non-institutional investors (NIIs). Retail individual investors bid up to ₹2 lakh, while anchors and QIBs are institutional categories.

Question 4

Which of the following is an example of a qualified institutional buyer (QIB) under ICDR?

  1. A resident individual with a large portfolio
  2. A Hindu Undivided Family investing ₹5 lakh
  3. A SEBI-registered mutual fund
  4. A partnership firm dealing in textiles
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A SEBI-registered mutual fund

QIBs include mutual funds, banks, insurers, pension funds, FPIs (other than certain categories) and similar institutions. Individuals, HUFs and ordinary partnership firms are not QIBs, regardless of investment size.

Question 5

In the 'basis of allotment' for an oversubscribed retail portion of a main-board IPO, the general principle is to:

  1. Allot shares only to the largest retail applicants first
  2. Allot shares in proportion to each applicant's annual income
  3. Give the minimum bid lot to as many applicants as possible
  4. Allot shares only to applicants who bid above the cut-off
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Give the minimum bid lot to as many applicants as possible

ICDR requires that, where possible, each retail applicant gets at least the minimum bid lot, with draw of lots if needed. Size priority or income has no role, and cut-off bids are valid retail bids.

Question 6

The minimum bid lot in a main-board IPO is designed so that the minimum application value falls:

  1. Above ₹2 lakh, to exclude retail investors
  2. Within a band prescribed by SEBI
  3. At exactly ₹1 lakh for every issue
  4. At whatever value the anchor investors decide
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Within a band prescribed by SEBI

SEBI prescribes a band for the minimum application size so that small investors can participate. The issuer and lead manager set the lot within that band; it is not fixed at a single value or decided by anchors.

Question 7

An 'abridged prospectus' is:

  1. A summary of salient features sent with the bid form
  2. A prospectus filed only with the stock exchange and not with SEBI
  3. A document issued only for debt securities
  4. The final prospectus after deletion of risk factors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A summary of salient features sent with the bid form

The abridged prospectus summarises key information and must accompany application forms so investors see the essentials. Risk factors cannot be dropped; it complements the full offer document.

Question 8

In a book-built issue, what does 'cut-off price' mean for a retail bidder?

  1. Bidding at the floor price only
  2. Bidding at a price below the floor price
  3. Withdrawing the bid if the price is above the floor
  4. Accepting the final price discovered within the band
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Accepting the final price discovered within the band

A cut-off bid means the investor accepts the final issue price determined within the band. Such bidders block funds at the cap price so that their bid remains valid whatever the final price.
