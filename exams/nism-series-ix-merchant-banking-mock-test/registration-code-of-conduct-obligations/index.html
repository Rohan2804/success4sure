Registration, Code of Conduct & Obligations: NISM Series IX practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Registration, Code of Conduct & Obligations, from the NISM Series IX (Merchant Banking) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how a merchant banker gets registered with SEBI, the eligibility and capital adequacy requirements, the fit and proper person criteria, the code of conduct, general obligations and responsibilities, record keeping, appointment of a compliance officer and SEBI's powers of inspection and action. Requirements are amended from time to time, so focus on principles: who may register, what conduct is expected, which conflicts are prohibited and what happens on breach. Expect scenario questions on ethics and conflicts of interest.

Registration, Code of Conduct & Obligations questions

Question 1

A merchant banker wants to lead manage an issue in which it holds a significant stake through an associate. Which concern does this mainly raise?

  1. Insufficient number of employees for the issue
  2. A conflict of interest
  3. Failure to pay the stamp duty on the issue
  4. Breach of minimum public shareholding norms
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A conflict of interest

SEBI rules restrict a merchant banker from lead managing an issue of an associated entity because its objectivity in due diligence is compromised. The concern is conflict of interest, not staffing or public shareholding.

Question 2

Which is an essential condition for SEBI to grant a certificate of registration to a merchant banker?

  1. It meets the 'fit and proper person' criteria
  2. The applicant has already completed at least ten public issues
  3. The applicant is listed on a recognised stock exchange
  4. The applicant is owned by a public sector bank
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. It meets the 'fit and proper person' criteria

SEBI assesses integrity, reputation, competence and financial soundness under the fit and proper criteria. Prior issue experience, listing or public sector ownership are not preconditions for registration.

Question 3

A merchant banker's employee prepares marketing material claiming the IPO 'will surely list at a 50% premium'. Under the code of conduct this is:

  1. Permitted if the grey market premium supports it
  2. Permitted if the issuer approves the statement
  3. Not permitted, as it is a misleading assurance of returns
  4. Permitted for issues reserved for retail investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Not permitted, as it is a misleading assurance of returns

The code of conduct prohibits misleading statements and assurances of returns or listing gains. Grey market indications or issuer approval do not make such promises acceptable.

Question 4

For how long must a merchant banker generally preserve books of account, records and documents?

  1. Only until the issue gets listed
  2. Only for the current financial year
  3. Until the next SEBI inspection is completed
  4. For the minimum period set by SEBI regulations
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. For the minimum period set by SEBI regulations

Merchant bankers must maintain prescribed books and records and preserve them for the minimum period laid down in the regulations, so SEBI can inspect past issues. Discarding records after listing or one year would breach this.

Question 5

SEBI wants to examine whether a merchant banker's records comply with the regulations. Which power can it use?

  1. Revocation of the company's incorporation certificate
  2. Freezing of the merchant banker's employees' salaries
  3. Inspection of the merchant banker's books and records
  4. Taking over the management of the issuer company
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Inspection of the merchant banker's books and records

SEBI can inspect a merchant banker's books, records and documents and follow up with directions or penalties. Company incorporation is under the Registrar of Companies, and SEBI does not take over issuer management in such cases.

Question 6

A merchant banker is asked by a client to suppress adverse information from an offer document. What should it do?

  1. Refuse, because full and true disclosure is its obligation
  2. Agree, provided the client pays a higher fee
  3. Agree, but mention the matter only to SEBI orally
  4. Agree if the information is less than one year old
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Refuse, because full and true disclosure is its obligation

Merchant bankers must ensure the offer document contains all material information and must not suppress facts. Fee levels or informal disclosure to SEBI cannot justify misleading investors.

Question 7

Which of the following is a duty of the compliance officer of a merchant banker?

  1. Deciding the price band of every issue managed by the firm
  2. Approving the issuer's annual financial statements
  3. Allotting shares to investors in every public issue
  4. Monitoring compliance and reporting lapses to SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Monitoring compliance and reporting lapses to SEBI

The compliance officer monitors adherence to SEBI rules, circulars and regulations and must report any non-compliance observed to SEBI. Pricing, audit approval and allotment are handled by others.

Question 8

A merchant banker receives an order from SEBI imposing a penalty. If aggrieved, it may appeal to:

  1. The National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission
  2. The Securities Appellate Tribunal
  3. The Insurance Ombudsman
  4. The Reserve Bank of India
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The Securities Appellate Tribunal

Appeals against SEBI orders lie with the Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT), and further to the Supreme Court. Consumer forums, the Insurance Ombudsman and RBI do not hear appeals against SEBI orders.
