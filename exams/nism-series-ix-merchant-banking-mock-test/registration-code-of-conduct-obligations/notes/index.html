Registration, Code of Conduct & Obligations: NISM Series IX revision notes

This chapter deals with the rules a merchant banker must meet to enter and remain in business. It covers SEBI registration, eligibility and capital requirements, the fit and proper criteria, the code of conduct, general obligations, record keeping and SEBI's supervisory powers. Requirements change, so learn principles and the reasons behind them.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Registration

No person can act as a merchant banker without a certificate of registration from SEBI. SEBI considers the applicant's infrastructure, experience of key personnel, past conduct, net worth and whether it is a fit and proper person. Registration is permanent unless suspended or cancelled, subject to periodic fees.

2. Net worth and categories

Net worth requirements were increased substantially by the December 2024 amendments, which also replaced the old four-category structure with categories based on the activities undertaken; entities managing main-board equity issues need the highest net worth. Existing merchant bankers get a phased timeline to comply. Check the current figures on SEBI's website.

3. Fit and proper person

Under the SEBI (Intermediaries) Regulations, the applicant, its principal officers and directors must have integrity, reputation and character, and must not be subject to disqualifications such as convictions, insolvency or debarment orders. This test applies at registration and continuously thereafter.

4. Code of conduct

A merchant banker must act with integrity, dignity and fairness, render the best possible advice, avoid conflicts or disclose them, not make misleading statements, maintain confidentiality, ensure adequate disclosure in offer documents and not indulge in unfair or manipulative practices. It must also cooperate with SEBI.

5. Restrictions on dealings

A merchant banker cannot deal in securities of an issuer it is advising on the basis of unpublished price-sensitive information, and must disclose its holdings in the issuer. It cannot act as lead manager to an issue of an associate company, which protects independence.

6. Responsibilities to the issue

A merchant banker must enter into an agreement with the issuer defining responsibilities, submit due diligence certificates, ensure proper underwriting arrangements, and report to SEBI on the issues it manages. It must not accept more lead manager assignments than it can handle properly.

7. Compliance officer

Every merchant banker appoints a compliance officer who monitors compliance with laws, regulations and circulars, reports non-compliance to SEBI, and handles investor grievances. The compliance officer is a key point of contact during inspections.

8. Books and records

Merchant bankers must maintain books of account, records and documents, including balance sheets, profit and loss accounts and records of issues managed, for the prescribed period (generally at least five years). SEBI must be informed where they are kept.

9. Inspection and action

SEBI can inspect a merchant banker's books, records and systems, either routinely or on complaints. Defaults can lead to warnings, monetary penalties under the SEBI Act, suspension or cancellation of registration, or debarment. Enforcement depends on the seriousness and repetition of breaches.

Common traps in the exam

  • Assuming registration has to be renewed every few years. It is permanent, subject to fees and continued compliance.
  • Thinking fit and proper is checked only at registration. It is a continuing requirement.
  • Believing a merchant banker can lead-manage an associate's issue with disclosure. It is prohibited from acting as lead manager for an associate.
  • Ignoring the 2024 changes. The old Category I to IV structure was replaced and net worth requirements were raised.

Quick recap

SEBI registration is mandatory and permanent subject to fees. Net worth and activity-based categories were overhauled in 2024. Fit and proper is a continuing test. Code of conduct: integrity, best advice, disclose conflicts, confidentiality, no misleading statements. No lead managing an associate's issue. Compliance officer, records for at least five years, SEBI inspection powers.
