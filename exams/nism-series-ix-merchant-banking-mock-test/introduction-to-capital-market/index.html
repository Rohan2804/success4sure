What this chapter covers

This chapter sets the context for merchant banking by describing the Indian capital market: primary and secondary markets, types of securities, market participants and intermediaries, and the regulators and laws that govern them. Learn the roles of SEBI, stock exchanges, depositories, clearing corporations, registrars and bankers to an issue, and the different ways companies raise capital such as IPOs, FPOs, rights issues, QIPs and private placements. Expect straightforward questions on terms, roles and the flow of funds between issuers and investors.

Introduction to Capital Market questions

Question 1

Which method allows a listed company to raise capital by issuing shares only to qualified institutional buyers, without a full public offer?

  1. Rights issue to all existing shareholders
  2. Follow-on public offer through book building
  3. Qualified Institutions Placement
  4. Bonus issue out of free reserves
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Qualified Institutions Placement

A QIP lets a listed company place equity or convertible securities with QIBs quickly, with lighter documentation than a public issue. A rights issue goes to existing holders and a bonus issue raises no fresh money.

Question 2

A company issues new shares to existing shareholders in proportion to their holdings, at a price usually below market. This is a:

  1. Rights issue
  2. Preferential allotment to a strategic investor
  3. Initial public offer on the main board
  4. Offer for sale by promoters
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Rights issue

A rights issue offers shares to existing shareholders pro rata, often at a discount, and the rights can usually be renounced. A preferential allotment goes to selected persons, and an offer for sale involves existing shares.

Question 3

Which intermediary maintains the register of security holders and processes applications and allotment on behalf of an issuer?

  1. Credit rating agency for the issuer's debt
  2. Registrar to an issue and share transfer agent
  3. Custodian of a foreign portfolio investor
  4. Debenture trustee for secured bonds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Registrar to an issue and share transfer agent

The registrar (RTA) handles applications, finalises the basis of allotment with the exchange, credits securities and maintains holder records. Rating agencies rate debt, custodians hold assets for clients, and debenture trustees protect debenture holders.

Question 4

A company's shares change hands between two investors on a stock exchange. What does the company receive from this trade?

  1. The full sale value of the shares
  2. The difference between issue price and sale price
  3. A fee equal to the stamp duty paid
  4. Nothing, as the trade is between investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Nothing, as the trade is between investors

Secondary market trades transfer existing shares between investors; the issuer receives no money. Companies raise funds only when they issue securities in the primary market.

Question 5

The main purpose of the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956 is to:

  1. Regulate the issue of bank notes and coins by the government
  2. Prevent undesirable transactions in securities
  3. Govern the incorporation and winding up of companies
  4. Regulate insurance and pension products sold to the public
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Prevent undesirable transactions in securities

The SCRA regulates recognition of stock exchanges, contracts in securities and listing, to prevent undesirable transactions. Currency issue is under the RBI Act, company formation under the Companies Act, and insurance under the Insurance Act.

Question 6

Which of the following is a debt instrument commonly issued by companies to raise long-term funds?

  1. Equity shares with voting rights
  2. Sweat equity shares
  3. Units of an equity mutual fund
  4. Non-convertible debentures
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Non-convertible debentures

Non-convertible debentures are debt securities that pay interest and are repaid at maturity. Equity and sweat equity shares represent ownership, and mutual fund units are issued by funds, not companies borrowing money.

Question 7

Why is a depository important for a public issue in India?

  1. It decides the price band for the issue
  2. It underwrites the unsubscribed portion of the issue
  3. Allotted shares are credited in demat form
  4. It grants SEBI's approval to the offer document
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Allotted shares are credited in demat form

Securities in public issues must be issued in dematerialised form, so depositories (NSDL and CDSL), through participants, credit allotted shares to investors. Pricing is with the issuer and lead manager, and SEBI issues observations.

Question 8

A company listed on the main board makes a further public issue of fresh shares. This is called a:

  1. Follow-on public offer
  2. Initial public offer
  3. Qualified institutions placement to QIBs only
  4. Buyback of securities
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Follow-on public offer

An FPO is a public issue by a company that is already listed. An IPO is the first public offer by an unlisted company, a QIP is restricted to QIBs, and a buyback returns money to shareholders.
