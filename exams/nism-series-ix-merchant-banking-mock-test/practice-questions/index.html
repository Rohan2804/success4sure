Free NISM Series IX practice questions

Question 1 · Introduction to Capital Market

Under which statute did SEBI acquire statutory powers to regulate the Indian securities market?

  1. The Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956
  2. The Depositories Act, 1996
  3. The Securities and Exchange Board of India Act, 1992
  4. The Companies Act, 2013
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The Securities and Exchange Board of India Act, 1992

SEBI was set up in 1988 as a non-statutory body and received statutory powers through the SEBI Act, 1992.

Question 2 · Introduction to Merchant Banking

Which of these services is typically offered by a merchant banker?

  1. Advising a company on the structure and timing of a capital-raising issue
  2. Issuing currency notes on behalf of the government
  3. Acting as the depository for a listed company's shares
  4. Running the clearing and settlement system of a stock exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Advising a company on the structure and timing of a capital-raising issue

Merchant bankers advise on and manage capital-raising; currency issue, depository and clearing functions belong to other institutions.

Question 3 · Registration, Code of Conduct & Obligations

A merchant banker discovers that it is an associate of the issuer whose IPO it has been asked to lead manage. What should it do?

  1. Act as lead manager after disclosing the relationship in the red herring prospectus
  2. Act as lead manager if the issue size is small
  3. Act as lead manager after taking approval from the stock exchange
  4. Decline to act as lead manager, because a merchant banker cannot lead manage an issue of an associate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Decline to act as lead manager, because a merchant banker cannot lead manage an issue of an associate

The regulations bar a merchant banker from lead managing an issue where it is an associate of the issuer, to avoid conflicts of interest; disclosure does not cure this.

Question 4 · Issue Management – Important Terms

In a book-built public issue, who is permitted to bid at the 'cut-off' price?

  1. Qualified institutional buyers
  2. Non-institutional investors applying above ₹2 lakh
  3. Anchor investors
  4. Retail individual investors (and eligible employee/shareholder reservation categories)
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Retail individual investors (and eligible employee/shareholder reservation categories)

The cut-off option is available only to retail individual investors and certain reserved categories; QIBs and NIIs must bid at a specific price.

Question 5 · Issue Management – Process & Underwriting

An unlisted company does not meet the profitability track record test of Regulation 6(1). Under Regulation 6(2) it can still do an IPO if:

  1. Its promoters contribute 50% of the post-issue capital
  2. It lists only on a regional stock exchange
  3. At least 75% of the net offer is allotted to qualified institutional buyers
  4. It obtains a guarantee from a scheduled commercial bank
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. At least 75% of the net offer is allotted to qualified institutional buyers

Regulation 6(2) allows issuers not meeting 6(1) to make an IPO through book building if at least 75% of the net offer goes to QIBs.

Question 6 · Issue Management – Obligations & Due Diligence

The due diligence certificate in a public issue is submitted to SEBI by:

  1. The registrar to the issue
  2. The lead manager(s) to the issue
  3. The bankers to the issue
  4. The stock exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The lead manager(s) to the issue

The lead manager files a due diligence certificate confirming that disclosures in the offer document are true, fair and adequate.

Question 7 · Mergers, Acquisitions & Takeovers

Under the SEBI (SAST) Regulations, 2011, an acquirer must make an open offer when it acquires shares or voting rights entitling it to:

  1. 10% or more of the voting rights
  2. 51% or more of the voting rights
  3. 5% or more of the voting rights
  4. 25% or more of the voting rights in the target company
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 25% or more of the voting rights in the target company

Regulation 3(1) of SAST triggers a mandatory open offer when an acquirer crosses 25% of voting rights.

Question 8 · Disinvestment & Buyback

Under the Companies Act, 2013, a buyback approved only by the board of directors (without a special resolution) is limited to:

  1. 25% of the total paid-up equity capital and free reserves
  2. 5% of the paid-up equity capital only
  3. 50% of the free reserves
  4. 10% of the total paid-up equity capital and free reserves
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 10% of the total paid-up equity capital and free reserves

Section 68 allows a board-approved buyback up to 10% of paid-up equity capital and free reserves; above that, a special resolution is needed (overall cap 25%).

Question 9 · Introduction to Capital Market

A company already listed on a stock exchange offers fresh shares to the public. What is this issue called?

  1. Initial public offer (IPO)
  2. Further public offer (FPO)
  3. Private placement
  4. Bonus issue
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Further public offer (FPO)

A public issue of fresh securities by an issuer that is already listed is a further public offer; an IPO is by an unlisted issuer.

Question 10 · Introduction to Merchant Banking

A merchant banker that acts as the lead manager to a public issue is commonly called the:

  1. Registrar to the issue
  2. Sponsor bank
  3. Debenture trustee
  4. Book running lead manager (BRLM)
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Book running lead manager (BRLM)

The lead manager that manages the book-building process is the BRLM; the registrar, sponsor bank and trustee perform separate roles.

Question 11 · Registration, Code of Conduct & Obligations

The SEBI-mandated code of conduct requires a merchant banker to:

  1. Act with integrity, due skill, care and diligence and avoid conflicts of interest
  2. Promise investors a guaranteed listing gain
  3. Share confidential client information with other clients to improve pricing
  4. Recommend only issues on which it earns the highest fee
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Act with integrity, due skill, care and diligence and avoid conflicts of interest

The code of conduct centres on integrity, diligence, fairness to clients and managing conflicts; promising returns or misusing information is prohibited.

Question 12 · Issue Management – Important Terms

As per SEBI ICDR, a 'retail individual investor' is an individual who applies for securities of value:

  1. Not more than ₹5 lakh
  2. Not more than ₹10 lakh
  3. Not more than ₹2 lakh
  4. Not more than ₹1 lakh
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Not more than ₹2 lakh

ICDR defines a retail individual investor as one applying for not more than ₹2 lakh in an issue; applications above that fall in the non-institutional category.

Question 13 · Issue Management – Process & Underwriting

Under ICDR, the minimum promoter contribution in an IPO is:

  1. 20% of the post-issue capital
  2. 10% of the pre-issue capital
  3. 51% of the post-issue capital
  4. 25% of the issue size
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 20% of the post-issue capital

Promoters must hold at least 20% of the post-issue capital as minimum promoter contribution, which is subject to lock-in.

Question 14 · Issue Management – Obligations & Due Diligence

The draft offer document filed with SEBI is made public for comments for at least:

  1. 7 days
  2. 45 days
  3. 90 days
  4. 21 days
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 21 days

ICDR requires the draft offer document to be kept open for public comments for a period of at least 21 days from filing.

Question 15 · Mergers, Acquisitions & Takeovers

An acquirer already holds 30% of a listed company. Without making an open offer, how much more can it acquire in a financial year under the creeping acquisition route?

  1. Up to 10% of the voting rights
  2. Up to 15% of the voting rights
  3. Up to 5% of the voting rights
  4. Up to 25% of the voting rights
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Up to 5% of the voting rights

An acquirer holding between 25% and the maximum permissible non-public shareholding can acquire up to 5% per financial year without an open offer.
