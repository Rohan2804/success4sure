Issue Management – Process & Underwriting: NISM Series IX revision notes

This chapter follows a public issue from appointing intermediaries to listing. It is the most heavily weighted part of the Merchant Banking exam and mixes sequencing questions with numbers you must recall exactly: eligibility under regulation 6(1) and 6(2), allocation percentages, minimum subscription, promoter contribution and lock-in. Work through numerical examples until the figures stick.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Sequence of steps

Typical sequence: appoint lead managers and other intermediaries; due diligence; board and shareholder approvals; file DRHP with SEBI and exchanges; obtain in-principle approval and SEBI observations; file RHP with RoC; roadshows and anchor bidding; announce price band; bid/issue period; pricing; file prospectus; basis of allotment; credit of shares, unblocking of funds; listing. Questions often scramble these steps.

2. Eligibility under regulation 6(1)

A company can make an IPO under 6(1) if it meets track-record tests: net tangible assets of at least ₹3 crore in each of the preceding three full years, average operating profit of at least ₹15 crore in the preceding three years with profit in each, and net worth of at least ₹1 crore in each of the preceding three years, among other conditions (as of 2026).

3. Regulation 6(2) route

A company that does not meet 6(1) tests can still do an IPO through book building if at least 75% of the net offer is allotted to QIBs. This route is used by younger, loss-making technology companies. The higher QIB share is meant to ensure informed investors drive pricing.

4. Allocation percentages

Under 6(1): QIBs not more than 50% of the net offer, NIIs not less than 15% and retail not less than 35%. Under 6(2): QIBs at least 75%, NIIs not more than 15% and retail not more than 10%. Within QIB, 5% of the non-anchor portion is reserved for mutual funds. The NII portion is split one-third for bids of ₹2–10 lakh and two-thirds for bids above ₹10 lakh.

5. Issue period

A public issue must stay open for at least three working days and at most ten working days. If the price band is revised, the issue must be extended by at least three working days, subject to the ten-day maximum. Revisions must be widely publicised.

6. Minimum subscription

If the issue does not receive minimum subscription of 90% of the fresh issue, all application money must be unblocked or refunded within the prescribed time. Underwriting can be used to ensure this threshold is met.

7. Promoter contribution and lock-in

Promoters must hold at least 20% of the post-issue capital as minimum promoter contribution, locked in for 18 months from allotment, or three years if the issue proceeds are mainly for capital expenditure. Promoter holding in excess of 20% is locked in for six months (one year in the capex case). Pre-IPO shares of other holders are generally locked in for six months.

8. Underwriting

The issuer signs an underwriting agreement with the book running lead managers and syndicate members after pricing. Underwriters commit to subscribe to any shortfall in their share. An underwriter's total commitments must stay within its capacity; under 6(2) issues, the QIB requirement cannot be met by underwriters' own bids.

9. Basis of allotment and listing

After closure, the registrar and lead manager finalise allotment with the designated exchange. For retail oversubscription, as many retail bidders as possible get the minimum lot through a lottery. Allotment and listing must be completed on T+3, where T is the issue closing day.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Minimum promoter contribution = 20% × post-issue paid-up capital; e.g. post-issue capital of 10 crore shares needs at least 2 crore promoter shares locked in.
  • Retail allocation under 6(1) ≥ 35% × net offer; e.g. net offer ₹1,000 crore gives retail at least ₹350 crore.

Common traps in the exam

  • Swapping 6(1) and 6(2) percentages. Under 6(1), QIBs are capped at 50%; under 6(2), QIBs must get at least 75%.
  • Forgetting the capex exception. Lock-in of minimum promoter contribution is three years when proceeds are mainly for capital expenditure.
  • Thinking minimum subscription is 100%. It is 90% of the fresh issue.
  • Assuming the issue period can be shortened after a price revision. It must be extended by at least three working days.

Quick recap

Steps: intermediaries, due diligence, DRHP, SEBI observations, RHP, anchors, price band, bidding, pricing, prospectus, allotment, listing on T+3. 6(1): QIB ≤50%, NII ≥15%, retail ≥35%. 6(2): QIB ≥75%, NII ≤15%, retail ≤10%. Issue open 3–10 working days. Minimum subscription 90%. Promoters 20% locked in 18 months (3 years for capex).
