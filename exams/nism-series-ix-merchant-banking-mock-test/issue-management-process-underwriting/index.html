Issue Management – Process & Underwriting: NISM Series IX practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Issue Management – Process & Underwriting, from the NISM Series IX (Merchant Banking) syllabus.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter describes the end-to-end process of a public issue under ICDR: appointment of intermediaries, eligibility norms, due diligence, filing the draft offer document, SEBI observations, roadshows, price band announcement, bidding through ASBA and UPI, allocation among investor categories, basis of allotment, refunds and listing. It also covers underwriting, minimum subscription and promoter contribution. Revise the sequence of steps, the reservation percentages under regulation 6(1) and 6(2), and the rules on promoter contribution and lock-in, using numerical examples.

Issue Management – Process & Underwriting questions

Question 1

A company's post-issue paid-up equity capital will be ₹50 crore. What is the minimum promoter contribution required in its IPO under ICDR?

  1. ₹5 crore
  2. ₹12.5 crore
  3. ₹25 crore
  4. ₹10 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹10 crore

Promoters must contribute at least 20% of the post-issue capital: 20% × ₹50 crore = ₹10 crore. ₹12.5 crore would be 25%, which reflects confusion with the minimum public shareholding norm.

Question 2

In a book-built IPO under regulation 6(1) of ICDR, what is the minimum share of the issue to be allocated to retail individual investors?

  1. 10%
  2. 35%
  3. 15%
  4. 50%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 35%

Under regulation 6(1), not less than 35% goes to retail investors, not less than 15% to non-institutional investors and not more than 50% to QIBs. 10% retail applies to issuers under regulation 6(2).

Question 3

Which step must occur before the red herring prospectus is filed with the Registrar of Companies?

  1. Listing of the shares on the stock exchange
  2. Finalisation of the basis of allotment
  3. Receipt of SEBI's observations on the draft offer document
  4. Credit of shares to the investors' demat accounts
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Receipt of SEBI's observations on the draft offer document

The DRHP is filed with SEBI and the issue proceeds only after SEBI's observations are incorporated. Basis of allotment, credit of shares and listing all happen after the issue closes.

Question 4

An issuer revises the price band during the bidding period. What must it do as a result?

  1. Extend the bidding period by some working days
  2. Cancel all bids already received and invite fresh bids
  3. Refile the draft offer document with SEBI
  4. Reduce the issue size by half
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Extend the bidding period by some working days

On revision of the price band, the bid period must be extended by at least three working days, subject to the overall maximum, with wide publicity. Existing bids are not cancelled and refiling is not required.

Question 5

Under UPI-based ASBA, what happens to a retail investor's money when he bids?

  1. The amount is blocked in his bank account until allotment is finalised
  2. The amount is debited and paid to the issuer immediately
  3. The amount is transferred to the lead manager's own account
  4. Nothing happens until shares are listed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The amount is blocked in his bank account until allotment is finalised

Under ASBA, funds stay in the investor's account but are blocked through a UPI mandate. On allotment, only the amount for allotted shares is debited and the rest unblocked; there is no transfer to the lead manager.

Question 6

In a firm underwriting arrangement, the underwriter:

  1. Subscribes only if the issue is fully subscribed by the public
  2. Has no obligation if the issue fails
  3. Is paid only if the shares list at a premium
  4. Commits to take a fixed number of shares in any case
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Commits to take a fixed number of shares in any case

In firm underwriting, the underwriter commits to take up a specified number of securities irrespective of subscription by the public, in addition to any devolvement obligation. Its commission does not depend on listing gains.

Question 7

A company does not satisfy the track record conditions under regulation 6(1) and comes with an IPO under regulation 6(2). Which allocation rule applies?

  1. At least 75% of the net offer must be allotted to retail investors
  2. At least 75% of the net offer must be allotted to QIBs
  3. At most 25% of the net offer can be allotted to QIBs
  4. The entire issue must be allotted to anchor investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. At least 75% of the net offer must be allotted to QIBs

Issuers coming under regulation 6(2) must allot at least 75% of the net offer to QIBs, with not more than 15% to NIIs and not more than 10% to retail investors, since such companies carry higher risk.

Question 8

Which is the correct order of these IPO steps?

  1. Bidding, filing of DRHP, allotment, SEBI observations, filing of RHP
  2. Filing of RHP, bidding, filing of DRHP, SEBI observations, allotment
  3. Filing of DRHP, SEBI observations, filing of RHP, bidding, allotment
  4. SEBI observations, filing of DRHP, allotment, bidding, filing of RHP
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Filing of DRHP, SEBI observations, filing of RHP, bidding, allotment

The draft is filed with SEBI first, SEBI gives observations, the RHP is filed with the RoC, bidding opens, and allotment follows issue closure. Bidding cannot precede the offer document.
