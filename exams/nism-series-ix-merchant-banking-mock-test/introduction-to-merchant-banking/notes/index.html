Introduction to Merchant Banking: NISM Series IX revision notes

This chapter explains what merchant bankers do and how their role developed in India. Expect questions on the range of services, which activities need SEBI registration, the core functions of a lead manager and how merchant bankers protect investors through due diligence. Keep the distinction between regulated issue management and other advisory work clear.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Meaning

A merchant banker is any person engaged in issue management, either by making arrangements for selling, buying or subscribing to securities, or acting as manager, consultant or adviser in relation to such issue management. The definition in the SEBI (Merchant Bankers) Regulations, 1992 makes issue management the central regulated activity.

2. Evolution in India

Merchant banking in India began in the late 1960s and 1970s with banks such as Grindlays and SBI setting up merchant banking divisions. Before SEBI, issues were priced under the Controller of Capital Issues. After the 1992 reforms, free pricing and SEBI regulation made due diligence and disclosure the merchant banker's main responsibilities.

3. Issue management

The lead manager structures the issue, prepares the offer document, coordinates due diligence, files documents with SEBI and exchanges, appoints and coordinates other intermediaries, markets the issue through roadshows, manages book building and oversees allotment and listing. This is the core regulated function.

4. Underwriting

Merchant bankers often act as underwriters, committing to subscribe to any unsubscribed portion of an issue for a commission. Underwriting gives the issuer certainty of funds and signals confidence. Underwriting obligations must be within the underwriter's capital adequacy.

5. Corporate advisory

Merchant bankers advise companies on capital structure, fundraising options, mergers, acquisitions, takeovers, buybacks, delisting and restructuring. Acting as manager to an open offer, buyback or delisting offer is regulated and requires a registered merchant banker.

6. Project counselling and loan syndication

Merchant bankers may help prepare project reports, assess feasibility and arrange loans from banks and financial institutions. These activities do not by themselves require merchant banker registration, but registered entities often provide them alongside issue work.

7. Coordination of intermediaries

In a public issue, the lead manager coordinates the registrar, bankers, syndicate members, legal advisers, auditors, advertising agencies and printers. The lead manager is responsible for ensuring each performs on time and that investors' interests are protected throughout.

8. Investor protection role

Because investors rely on the offer document, the merchant banker's due diligence is the main safeguard against false or misleading disclosures. The lead manager must independently verify information, not merely compile what the issuer provides, and certify to SEBI that disclosures are true, fair and adequate.

9. Regulatory framework

The SEBI (Merchant Bankers) Regulations, 1992, substantially amended in December 2024, govern registration, net worth, permitted activities, code of conduct and obligations. Merchant bankers also follow ICDR, SAST, Buy-back and Delisting regulations when acting as managers. The 2024 amendments tightened eligibility and limited activities outside SEBI-regulated work.

Common traps in the exam

  • Treating loan syndication as a regulated merchant banking activity. Issue management is the core regulated activity; syndication alone does not need registration.
  • Thinking the lead manager just compiles issuer information. It must verify and certify disclosures through due diligence.
  • Assuming any adviser can manage an open offer or buyback. Only a SEBI-registered merchant banker can act as manager.
  • Confusing underwriting with issue management. Underwriting is a commitment to subscribe; management covers the whole process.

Quick recap

Merchant banker = issue manager under SEBI's 1992 regulations (amended 2024). Core functions: issue management, underwriting, managing open offers, buybacks and delisting, plus advisory. Lead managers coordinate intermediaries, perform due diligence and certify disclosures. Project counselling and loan syndication alone do not need registration.
