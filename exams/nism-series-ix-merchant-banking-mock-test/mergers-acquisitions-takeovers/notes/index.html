Mergers, Acquisitions & Takeovers: NISM Series IX revision notes

This chapter covers corporate restructuring and the Takeover Regulations, 2011. You need to know types of mergers, how schemes of arrangement are approved, and the key SAST concepts: triggers for an open offer, creeping acquisition, offer size, offer price, disclosures and the manager's role. Thresholds and timelines are tested exactly, so revise them carefully.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Types of combinations

A horizontal merger joins competitors in the same business; a vertical merger joins firms at different stages of the value chain; a conglomerate merger joins unrelated businesses. In a reverse merger, a larger or unlisted company merges into a smaller listed one. A demerger separates a business into a new company, with shareholders getting shares in it.

2. Schemes of arrangement

Mergers and demergers are done through schemes under sections 230 to 232 of the Companies Act, 2013, approved by shareholders and creditors and sanctioned by the NCLT. Listed companies need stock exchange observations and SEBI's no-objection before filing with NCLT. Fast-track mergers under section 233 apply to small companies and wholly owned subsidiaries.

3. Valuation and swap ratio

Swap ratios are based on valuations using methods such as net asset value, earnings and market price. If Company A is valued at ₹300 per share and B at ₹100 per share, a fair swap ratio might be one A share for three B shares. Valuation reports by registered valuers and fairness opinions by merchant bankers are required for listed companies.

4. Open offer triggers

An acquirer who, with persons acting in concert, acquires 25% or more of the voting rights of a listed company must make an open offer. Acquiring control, directly or indirectly, also triggers an open offer regardless of the percentage. Indirect acquisitions through an overseas parent can trigger offers too.

5. Creeping acquisition

An acquirer already holding 25% or more but less than the maximum permissible non-public shareholding can acquire up to 5% additional voting rights in a financial year without an open offer. Crossing 5% in a year triggers an offer. Gross acquisitions are counted, ignoring intermediate sales.

6. Offer size

A mandatory open offer must be for at least 26% of the total shares of the target company. A voluntary offer by an acquirer holding at least 25% must be for at least 10%. If acceptances exceed the offer size, shares are accepted proportionately.

7. Offer price

The offer price must be the highest of: the negotiated price under the agreement triggering the offer; the volume-weighted average price paid by the acquirer in the preceding 52 weeks; the highest price paid in the preceding 26 weeks; and, for frequently traded shares, the 60-trading-day volume-weighted average market price. This protects minority shareholders from being underpaid.

8. Disclosures

Anyone acquiring 5% or more of a listed company's shares must disclose it, and further changes of 2% or more must also be disclosed, within two working days to the company and exchanges. Promoters disclose encumbrances such as pledges of their shares.

9. Manager and escrow

The acquirer must appoint a SEBI-registered merchant banker, not an associate of the acquirer, as manager to the open offer. An escrow account must be created to secure the offer: 25% of the first ₹500 crore of consideration and 10% of the balance. The manager can forfeit escrow if obligations are not met.

10. Competing offers and withdrawal

Another person can make a competing offer within 15 working days of the detailed public statement of the first offer. An open offer can be withdrawn only in limited cases, such as statutory approvals being refused or the acquirer's death.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Escrow = 25% × first ₹500 crore + 10% × consideration above ₹500 crore; e.g. ₹800 crore offer needs ₹125 crore + ₹30 crore = ₹155 crore.

Common traps in the exam

  • Thinking the open offer must be for 25%. The trigger is 25%; the minimum offer size is 26%.
  • Counting net instead of gross acquisitions for creeping limits. Gross acquisitions within the year are counted.
  • Choosing a single price for the offer. The offer price is the highest of several benchmarks.
  • Allowing an associate merchant banker to manage the offer. The manager must not be an associate of the acquirer.

Quick recap

Mergers through NCLT schemes under sections 230–232; fast-track under 233. SAST: 25% or control triggers an open offer; creeping 5% a year for holders between 25% and the maximum non-public holding. Offer size at least 26% (voluntary 10%). Offer price = highest of four benchmarks. Disclose at 5% and every 2%. Escrow 25% of first ₹500 crore plus 10% above.
