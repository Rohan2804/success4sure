Mergers, Acquisitions & Takeovers: NISM Series IX practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Mergers, Acquisitions & Takeovers, from the NISM Series IX (Merchant Banking) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers corporate restructuring and takeovers: types of mergers, amalgamations and demergers, schemes of arrangement under the Companies Act, valuation in M&A, and the SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011. Key SAST concepts are triggers for open offers, creeping acquisitions, offer size, offer price, disclosures, voluntary and competing offers, withdrawal and the role of the manager to the open offer. Revise thresholds and timelines carefully and practise simple calculations on offer size and creeping acquisition.

Mergers, Acquisitions & Takeovers questions

Question 1

A listed company has 10 crore shares. An acquirer crosses the 25% threshold and must make a mandatory open offer for the minimum size. How many shares must the offer cover?

  1. 2.5 crore shares
  2. 1.0 crore shares
  3. 5.1 crore shares
  4. 2.6 crore shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 2.6 crore shares

The minimum open offer size is 26% of the total shares: 26% × 10 crore = 2.6 crore shares. 2.5 crore reflects the trigger level, not the offer size.

Question 2

An acquirer holds 40% of a listed company. Under the creeping acquisition route, what is the maximum holding it can reach by acquisitions in one financial year without an open offer?

  1. 45%
  2. 50%
  3. 42%
  4. 75%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 45%

An acquirer holding 25% or more but below the maximum permissible non-public shareholding may acquire up to 5% more in a financial year without an open offer: 40% + 5% = 45%.

Question 3

A merger in which two companies in the same industry and at the same stage of production combine is a:

  1. Vertical merger
  2. Horizontal merger
  3. Conglomerate merger
  4. Reverse merger
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Horizontal merger

A horizontal merger joins competitors in the same line of business. A vertical merger joins firms at different stages of the supply chain, and a conglomerate merger joins unrelated businesses.

Question 4

An acquirer who already holds 30% of a target makes an open offer voluntarily to consolidate control. What is the minimum size of such a voluntary offer?

  1. 26% of the total shares
  2. 5% of the total shares
  3. 10% of the total shares
  4. 51% of the total shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 10% of the total shares

A voluntary open offer by an acquirer holding 25% or more must be for at least 10% of the total shares, without exceeding the maximum permissible non-public shareholding. The 26% minimum applies to mandatory offers.

Question 5

Which of the following can trigger an open offer obligation under SAST even without crossing a shareholding threshold?

  1. Purchase of 1% of shares by a mutual fund
  2. Receipt of a bonus issue by an existing holder
  3. Grant of ESOPs to employees
  4. Acquisition of control over the target company
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Acquisition of control over the target company

SAST requires an open offer when an acquirer acquires control, directly or indirectly, regardless of the percentage acquired. Small purchases, bonus issues and ESOP grants do not by themselves trigger an open offer.

Question 6

A company transfers one of its divisions to a new company, whose shares are issued to the original company's shareholders. This is a:

  1. Horizontal merger
  2. Buyback of shares
  3. Demerger
  4. Rights issue
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Demerger

In a demerger, an undertaking is transferred to a resulting company, which issues shares to the demerged company's shareholders. It splits a business rather than combining two businesses.

Question 7

After an acquirer announces an open offer, another person announces a competing offer for the same target within the permitted time. What is the general effect?

  1. The first offer is automatically cancelled
  2. Shareholders may choose either offer
  3. SEBI decides which offer shareholders must accept
  4. The target company must reject both offers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Shareholders may choose either offer

SAST allows competing offers within a specified period. Both offers proceed on a common schedule, acquirers may revise their offers, and shareholders decide which to tender into.

Question 8

Under SAST, the offer price in a mandatory open offer is broadly set as:

  1. The highest of several benchmark prices specified in the regulations
  2. Any price the acquirer chooses, without reference to the market
  3. The face value of the shares
  4. The lowest price at which the stock traded in the last week
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The highest of several benchmark prices specified in the regulations

The offer price must be at least the highest of benchmarks such as the negotiated price, the highest price paid by the acquirer in a look-back period and the volume-weighted average market price, protecting public shareholders.
