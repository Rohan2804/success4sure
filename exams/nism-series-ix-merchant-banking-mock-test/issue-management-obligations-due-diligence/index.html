Issue Management – Obligations & Due Diligence: NISM Series IX practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Issue Management – Obligations & Due Diligence, from the NISM Series IX (Merchant Banking) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the responsibilities of the lead manager before, during and after a public issue: due diligence on the issuer, promoters and disclosures, the due diligence certificates, coordination with the monitoring agency, advertisement norms, post-issue monitoring, handling of investor grievances, refunds and reporting to SEBI. Key ideas are that the lead manager must verify, not just compile, information; must ensure advertisements are fair; and continues to be responsible after the issue closes. Expect scenario questions on red flags discovered during due diligence.

Issue Management – Obligations & Due Diligence questions

Question 1

While reviewing a draft offer document, the lead manager finds that a promoter director has been declared a wilful defaulter. What is the correct approach?

  1. Ignore it, since only the company's own record matters
  2. Disclose it only after listing to avoid affecting demand
  3. Remove the director's name from the offer document
  4. Treat it as material, as such issuers face ICDR curbs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Treat it as material, as such issuers face ICDR curbs

ICDR restricts issuers whose promoters or directors are wilful defaulters or fugitive economic offenders from making public issues. The lead manager must treat this as material; hiding it or deferring disclosure would breach due diligence obligations.

Question 2

Issue advertisements prepared by the issuer and lead manager must:

  1. Highlight expected listing gains to attract investors
  2. Mention only the positive aspects of the issuer
  3. Be truthful, fair and consistent with the offer document
  4. Avoid any reference to risk factors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Be truthful, fair and consistent with the offer document

ICDR advertisement norms require advertisements to be fair, not misleading, and consistent with the offer document; they cannot promise returns or omit material risks. Selective positive messaging would mislead investors.

Question 3

After the issue, investors complain of delays in unblocking funds for unallotted bids. Who is primarily responsible for coordinating the redress?

  1. The stock exchange's listing department alone
  2. The lead manager, with the registrar and SCSBs
  3. The credit rating agency that rated the issuer
  4. SEBI's chairman personally
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The lead manager, with the registrar and SCSBs

The lead manager continues to coordinate post-issue activities, including refunds, unblocking and grievance redress, with the registrar and SCSBs. Rating agencies have no role, and SEBI oversees rather than handles each complaint.

Question 4

A monitoring agency appointed for an IPO reports that issue proceeds are being used for purposes not stated in the offer document. This deviation:

  1. Must be disclosed, and a change in objects needs shareholder approval
  2. Can be ignored if the amount is below 50% of the issue
  3. Is permitted without any approval within three years
  4. Must be reported only to the company's auditors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Must be disclosed, and a change in objects needs shareholder approval

Proceeds must be used for stated objects. Any variation requires shareholders' approval by special resolution and appropriate disclosures, with exit options where applicable. Deviations cannot simply be ignored.

Question 5

In due diligence, which approach reflects a merchant banker's proper standard?

  1. Independently verifying key claims made by management
  2. Copying management's statements into the offer document without checks
  3. Relying only on the previous year's newspaper reports
  4. Verifying information only if SEBI specifically asks for it
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Independently verifying key claims made by management

Due diligence requires reasonable independent verification through documents, site visits, legal and financial checks and confirmations. Mere reproduction of management claims does not meet the standard expected of a lead manager.

Question 6

Which of the following must be disclosed as a risk factor if material?

  1. The name of the advertising agency for the issue
  2. The colour scheme of the company's logo
  3. The personal hobbies of the company's directors
  4. Pending litigation that could hurt operations
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Pending litigation that could hurt operations

Material litigation, regulatory actions and business risks must be disclosed so investors can assess the issue. Trivial details such as advertising agencies or logos are not risk factors.

Question 7

When must a lead manager file a due diligence certificate with SEBI in an IPO?

  1. Only after the shares have listed
  2. Only if SEBI raises a query on the issue
  3. With the draft offer document and at later stages
  4. Only when the issue is undersubscribed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. With the draft offer document and at later stages

Due diligence certificates are filed with the draft offer document and at specified later stages, such as filing with the RoC, confirming continued accuracy. They are not optional or triggered only by queries.

Question 8

A lead manager learns of a material adverse development about the issuer after the RHP is filed but before bidding closes. It should:

  1. Ignore it, since the RHP has already been filed
  2. Ensure it is disclosed before the issue closes
  3. Disclose it only in the next annual report
  4. Inform only anchor investors privately
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Ensure it is disclosed before the issue closes

The lead manager must ensure investors receive updated material information, typically through an addendum or public notice, before they commit. Selective disclosure to anchors would be unfair and violate disclosure norms.
