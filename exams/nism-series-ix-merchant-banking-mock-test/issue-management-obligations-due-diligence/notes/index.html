Issue Management – Obligations & Due Diligence: NISM Series IX revision notes

The lead manager's responsibility does not stop at filing an offer document; it covers verifying disclosures, controlling publicity, monitoring the use of funds and handling post-issue matters. This chapter's scenario questions often describe a red flag during due diligence and ask what the merchant banker should do. The guiding principle is verification, not compilation.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Meaning of due diligence

Due diligence is the independent investigation of the issuer's business, financials, legal matters, promoters, litigation, material contracts and risk factors to ensure the offer document is true, fair and adequate. The lead manager must check source documents, meet management, visit sites and consult experts where needed.

2. Due diligence certificates

The lead manager submits due diligence certificates to SEBI at key stages, such as filing the draft offer document, before the issue opens and after the issue. These certify that disclosures are adequate and that the issuer has complied with applicable laws. A false certificate exposes the merchant banker to action.

3. Responding to red flags

If the lead manager discovers undisclosed litigation, unusual related-party transactions, doubtful assets or inconsistent figures, it must insist on full disclosure or corrections. If the issuer refuses, the lead manager should not proceed. Signing off on known gaps is a breach of its obligations.

4. Expert reliance

Lead managers may rely on statutory auditors, legal counsel and other experts for specialised matters, but they must apply professional judgement and cannot blindly accept expert reports when there are warning signs. Consent of experts named in the offer document must be obtained.

5. Monitoring agency

For issues with fresh issue size above a threshold (₹100 crore as of 2026), the issuer must appoint a monitoring agency, which must be a SEBI-registered credit rating agency, to report on the use of proceeds. Reports go to the audit committee and stock exchanges, and deviations from the stated objects require shareholder approval.

6. Advertisement norms

Issue advertisements must be truthful, fair and clear, must not contain promises of returns, misleading claims or information not in the offer document, and must include key risk disclosures. Publicity through the issuer's normal business advertising should not be used to promote the issue indirectly.

7. Research and quiet periods

During the issue period, research reports or statements by syndicate members about the issuer must be consistent with the offer document. Communications that could influence investors outside the offer document are restricted until listing.

8. Post-issue responsibilities

After the issue closes, the lead manager oversees finalisation of allotment, credit of shares, unblocking of funds, listing and trading approvals. It submits post-issue reports to SEBI and handles investor complaints, coordinating with the registrar to resolve them promptly.

9. Continuing liability

Liability for misstatements in the offer document can arise under the Companies Act and SEBI regulations even after listing. Directors, promoters, experts and the lead manager may face penalties and civil liability. This is why thorough documentation of due diligence work is essential.

Common traps in the exam

  • Thinking the lead manager can simply rely on the issuer's statements. It must independently verify information.
  • Assuming responsibilities end at listing. Post-issue reporting and grievance handling continue afterwards.
  • Treating issue advertisements like normal product ads. They cannot carry information not in the offer document or misleading claims.
  • Believing any agency can be the monitoring agency. It must be a SEBI-registered credit rating agency.

Quick recap

Due diligence means verifying, not compiling. Certificates go to SEBI at key stages. Red flags must be disclosed or the issue should not proceed. Monitoring agency (a CRA) tracks use of proceeds above the threshold. Advertisements must match the offer document and avoid return promises. Post-issue allotment, listing, reporting and grievances remain the lead manager's job.
