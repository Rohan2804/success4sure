SSE: Introduction, Funding Structures & Instruments: NISM Series XXIII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SSE: Introduction, Funding Structures & Instruments, from the NISM Series XXIII (Social Impact Assessors) syllabus.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces the Social Stock Exchange (SSE), a separate segment of recognised stock exchanges created under SEBI's framework to help social enterprises raise funds. It covers who qualifies as a Social Enterprise, the eligible social activities, the requirement to target underserved populations, the exclusion list, and the instruments available — Zero Coupon Zero Principal instruments, donations through mutual fund schemes where permitted, Social Impact Funds and, for for-profit enterprises, equity and debt. Focus on matching each type of enterprise with the instruments it can use.

SSE: Introduction, Funding Structures & Instruments questions

Question 1

A for-profit social enterprise that wants to raise money through the SSE framework may issue:

  1. Zero Coupon Zero Principal instruments meant only for NPOs
  2. Insurance policies with guaranteed maturity benefits
  3. Units of a mutual fund scheme managed by itself
  4. Equity shares or debt securities
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Equity shares or debt securities

For-profit social enterprises raise capital through regular securities such as equity or debt under SEBI rules, subject to SSE conditions. ZCZP instruments are available only to NPOs.

Question 2

Investors in a Zero Coupon Zero Principal (ZCZP) instrument should understand that:

  1. They receive a fixed coupon every year and principal at maturity
  2. Their principal is guaranteed by the stock exchange
  3. They can trade the instrument daily on the equity segment
  4. They receive neither interest nor principal back
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. They receive neither interest nor principal back

ZCZP instruments work like donations through the exchange: no interest and no repayment of principal. The return to investors is the social impact the NPO delivers.

Question 3

A listed company's corporate foundation, which runs health camps in villages, wants to raise ZCZP funds on the SSE. What is the position?

  1. It may register, provided its parent company guarantees the ZCZP issue
  2. Corporate foundations are excluded from the SSE framework
  3. It may register only if it first converts into a for-profit social enterprise
  4. It may register, since every health activity automatically qualifies
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Corporate foundations are excluded from the SSE framework

SEBI's framework specifically excludes corporate foundations, along with political or religious organisations and professional or trade associations, even if their activities are social. Independent NPOs serving target groups may qualify.

Question 4

Social Impact Funds under the SEBI AIF Regulations are a sub-category of:

  1. Category III AIFs that use leverage to trade listed securities
  2. Mutual fund schemes open to all retail investors
  3. Portfolio management services for high-net-worth clients
  4. Category I AIFs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Category I AIFs

Social Impact Funds (formerly Social Venture Funds) are Category I AIFs investing in social enterprises. They can also receive grants and may route funds to SSE-registered entities.

Question 5

To qualify as a Social Enterprise, an entity must show that a substantial share of its activities targets underserved populations. The test is commonly measured using:

  1. The number of board members who are retired government officers
  2. Revenue, expenditure or beneficiaries from target groups
  3. The size of its head office and number of branches
  4. Its share price performance in the past three years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Revenue, expenditure or beneficiaries from target groups

SEBI's framework requires a minimum proportion (currently 67%) of activities, measured through revenue, expenditure or customer/beneficiary base, to come from or serve the target population.

Question 6

The SSE in India operates as:

  1. A separate segment of a recognised stock exchange
  2. An independent exchange regulated by the RBI rather than SEBI
  3. A department of the Ministry of Corporate Affairs
  4. A private platform owned by a group of NGOs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A separate segment of a recognised stock exchange

SEBI allowed recognised stock exchanges such as NSE and BSE to set up SSE segments. They are regulated by SEBI like other exchange segments.

Question 7

An NPO wants to raise funds through the SSE without offering any financial return. The suitable instrument is:

  1. A listed non-convertible debenture paying 9% interest
  2. An IPO of equity shares on the main board
  3. A commercial paper issue placed with banks
  4. A ZCZP issue
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A ZCZP issue

Most NPOs cannot issue shares or pay returns. ZCZP lets them raise donation-like funds through the exchange with SSE disclosure and impact reporting.

Question 8

Compared with direct donations, raising funds through the SSE is expected to give donors:

  1. A guaranteed financial return on their contribution
  2. Exemption from all KYC requirements
  3. Greater transparency through mandatory disclosures
  4. Control over the NPO's board decisions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Greater transparency through mandatory disclosures

SSE-listed entities must make regular financial and impact disclosures, including independent social impact assessment. This gives donors more information than typical direct giving.
