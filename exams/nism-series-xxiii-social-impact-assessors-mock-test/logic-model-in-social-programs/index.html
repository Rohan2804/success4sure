Logic Model in Social Programs: NISM Series XXIII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Logic Model in Social Programs, from the NISM Series XXIII (Social Impact Assessors) syllabus.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains the logic model and theory of change as tools to design, manage and evaluate social programmes. It covers the chain of inputs, activities, outputs, outcomes and impact; the role of assumptions and external factors; designing SMART indicators at each level; and how theory of change works backwards from long-term goals to preconditions. Expect questions that ask you to classify a given result (output, outcome or impact) or to identify a weak link in a programme's logic.

Logic Model in Social Programs questions

Question 1

In a girls' education programme, 'number of bicycles distributed to girls' is best classified as:

  1. An input to the programme
  2. A long-term impact on society
  3. An external assumption
  4. An output
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. An output

Outputs are the direct products of activities, such as bicycles distributed. The resulting change, like higher attendance, is an outcome, while long-term change is impact.

Question 2

Funds, staff and training materials used by a programme are its:

  1. Outcomes
  2. Impacts
  3. Inputs
  4. Outputs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Inputs

Inputs are the resources put into a programme. Activities use inputs to produce outputs, which lead to outcomes and, ultimately, impact.

Question 3

A logic model's 'assumptions' are best described as:

  1. The final results the programme guarantees to deliver to donors
  2. Conditions that must hold for each step to lead to the next
  3. Budget lines that list how money will be spent
  4. Names of staff responsible for each activity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Conditions that must hold for each step to lead to the next

Assumptions are beliefs about conditions, like 'trained farmers will have access to water'. If they fail, the expected chain from outputs to outcomes may break.

Question 4

Which indicator is the best example of an outcome indicator for a maternal health programme?

  1. Number of awareness sessions held in the villages
  2. Share of births attended by skilled health workers
  3. Amount of money spent on printing posters
  4. Number of field staff hired during the year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Share of births attended by skilled health workers

Outcomes measure changes in behaviour or conditions among beneficiaries. Sessions held, money spent and staff hired describe activities or inputs.

Question 5

Theory of change is typically developed by:

  1. Listing all activities and hoping they create impact
  2. Working backwards from the long-term goal
  3. Copying the budget of a similar organisation
  4. Starting from the funds available and ignoring goals
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Working backwards from the long-term goal

Theory of change begins with the desired long-term change and maps the preconditions needed to reach it, making causal links and assumptions explicit.

Question 6

A programme trains 1,000 farmers, but crop yields do not improve because farmers cannot afford the recommended seeds. This reveals:

  1. A failed assumption between output and outcome
  2. That the programme's inputs were too large
  3. That training is never useful for farmers
  4. That impact always follows outputs automatically
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A failed assumption between output and outcome

The training (output) was delivered, but the expected outcome needed an unmet condition — access to affordable seeds. Logic models help spot such gaps early.

Question 7

A SMART indicator is one that is:

  1. Simple, monetary, annual, regulatory and tax-related
  2. Specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound
  3. Social, moral, accountable, reliable and transparent
  4. Standard, mandatory, audited, reported and tracked
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound

SMART indicators are precisely defined, can be measured, are realistic, relate to the programme's goals and have a time frame, making progress tracking meaningful.

Question 8

Long-term, broad changes in a community, such as a reduction in district child mortality, are classified as:

  1. Inputs
  2. Impact
  3. Activities
  4. Outputs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Impact

Impact refers to long-term, wider changes that programmes contribute to, often alongside other factors. Attributing impact to one programme requires careful evaluation.
