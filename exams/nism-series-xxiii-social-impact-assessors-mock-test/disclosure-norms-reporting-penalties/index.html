Disclosure Norms, Reporting & Penalties: NISM Series XXIII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Disclosure Norms, Reporting & Penalties, from the NISM Series XXIII (Social Impact Assessors) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the ongoing obligations of Social Enterprises registered or listed on the SSE. NPOs must make annual disclosures on governance, finances, programmes and impact, and, if they have raised funds, periodic statements of fund utilisation and an Annual Impact Report assessed by a social auditor. For-profit social enterprises follow listing obligations plus impact reporting. The chapter also covers consequences of non-compliance and misconduct for entities and social auditors, including SEBI action and disciplinary proceedings. Focus on who must disclose what and why.

Disclosure Norms, Reporting & Penalties questions

Question 1

An NPO that has raised funds through the SSE must disclose periodically:

  1. A statement of how the funds have been used
  2. Its daily bank balance on the exchange website
  3. The personal income tax returns of its trustees
  4. A list of every beneficiary with their phone numbers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A statement of how the funds have been used

NPOs must report utilisation of funds raised against the objects stated in the fund-raising document, with any deviations explained. Personal data of beneficiaries must not be published.

Question 2

Annual disclosures by SSE-registered NPOs typically cover:

  1. Governance, finances, programmes and impact
  2. Only the names of the NPO's donors and their addresses
  3. The share price of its corporate donors
  4. Its marketing plans for the next five years only
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Governance, finances, programmes and impact

Annual disclosures give stakeholders information on the governance structure, financial statements, programme activities and social impact, supporting transparency and accountability to donors.

Question 3

A social auditor knowingly certifies an impact report containing false information. The auditor may face:

  1. No consequences, since impact reports are voluntary
  2. Only a requirement to rewrite the report
  3. Disciplinary action by the SRO and action by SEBI
  4. Promotion to a senior position at the SRO
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Disciplinary action by the SRO and action by SEBI

Social auditors are accountable to their SRO and, under the SSE framework, to SEBI. Knowingly false certification is professional misconduct and may also breach securities law.

Question 4

Why do disclosure norms require reporting both deviations in fund utilisation and their reasons?

  1. So donors can judge whether changes were justified
  2. To allow the NPO to avoid future impact reports
  3. To give the exchange a reason to raise fees
  4. To replace the need for annual financial audits
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. So donors can judge whether changes were justified

Explaining deviations — such as budget shifts due to a natural disaster — lets stakeholders assess management decisions. It does not replace audits or impact reports.

Question 5

Primary responsibility for the accuracy of an SSE-registered entity's disclosures lies with the:

  1. Stock exchange where it is registered
  2. Entity and its management
  3. Social auditor alone
  4. Donors who contributed funds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Entity and its management

The entity prepares and owns its disclosures. Social auditors give an opinion on impact reports, and exchanges monitor compliance, but neither replaces management's responsibility.

Question 6

A for-profit social enterprise listed on an exchange through the SSE route must comply with:

  1. Normal listing obligations plus impact reporting
  2. Only impact reporting and no financial disclosures
  3. No obligations once its shares are listed
  4. Only the disclosure rules for mutual funds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Normal listing obligations plus impact reporting

For-profit social enterprises are listed companies, so they must follow SEBI's listing regulations in full, along with the additional SSE-specific social impact disclosures.

Question 7

Failure to submit required disclosures on time may lead to:

  1. Automatic increase in the NPO's grant funding
  2. Exemption from future disclosure requirements
  3. Transfer of the NPO's assets to the exchange
  4. Exchange action such as fines or suspension
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Exchange action such as fines or suspension

Exchanges monitor compliance and, under SEBI's framework, may impose fines, suspend trading or registration, or delist entities for persistent non-compliance with disclosure norms.

Question 8

Timely and regular disclosures by social enterprises benefit the SSE ecosystem mainly by:

  1. Guaranteeing that every project will succeed
  2. Building trust and informing funding decisions
  3. Allowing NPOs to pay dividends to donors
  4. Removing the need for social auditors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Building trust and informing funding decisions

Regular disclosures reduce information gaps between enterprises and funders, encouraging more and better-targeted funding over time. They improve accountability but do not guarantee success.
