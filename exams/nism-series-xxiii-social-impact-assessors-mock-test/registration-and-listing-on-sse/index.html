Registration and Listing on SSE: NISM Series XXIII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Registration and Listing on SSE, from the NISM Series XXIII (Social Impact Assessors) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how social enterprises access the SSE. NPOs must first register on the SSE after meeting eligibility conditions such as legal registration, valid income-tax registrations, minimum operating history and fund-raising and spending levels; they may then raise funds through ZCZP or other permitted routes by filing the required offer documents. For-profit social enterprises list securities on the main board, SME platform or Innovators Growth Platform following SEBI's issue norms plus SSE requirements. Focus on the sequence — registration, issue, listing — and on the documents and disclosures needed at each stage.

Registration and Listing on SSE questions

Question 1

Which statement correctly describes the relationship between SSE registration and fund-raising for an NPO?

  1. Registration is a precondition but does not oblige it to raise funds
  2. Registration automatically lists ZCZP instruments for the NPO
  3. Registration is optional for NPOs wishing to issue ZCZP
  4. Registration permits the NPO to issue equity shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Registration is a precondition but does not oblige it to raise funds

An NPO must register on the SSE before raising funds there, but it may remain registered without raising money, subject to annual disclosure requirements.

Question 2

A typical eligibility condition for an NPO seeking SSE registration is that it:

  1. Has been incorporated as a listed public limited company
  2. Has at least 10,000 employees on its rolls
  3. Has made a profit in each of the last five years
  4. Holds valid tax-exemption registrations
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Holds valid tax-exemption registrations

NPOs must be legally registered, hold valid registrations for income-tax exemption and donor deductions, and meet operating history and financial conditions. Profit or size is not required.

Question 3

A for-profit social enterprise wishes to list equity through the SSE route. It must comply with:

  1. Only the rules of the Ministry of Social Justice
  2. No rules, as social enterprises are exempt
  3. SEBI's ICDR issue norms plus SSE conditions
  4. Only the requirements of the Companies Act on CSR
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. SEBI's ICDR issue norms plus SSE conditions

For-profit social enterprises follow the regular ICDR requirements for the chosen platform, plus additional SSE disclosures such as social intent and impact reporting.

Question 4

An NPO raising funds through a ZCZP issue must file a document disclosing its projects, use of funds and risks. This document is the:

  1. Red herring prospectus for an equity IPO on the main board
  2. Scheme information document of a mutual fund
  3. GST return for the previous quarter
  4. Fund-raising document filed with the SSE
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Fund-raising document filed with the SSE

NPOs file a draft and final fund-raising document for ZCZP issues, describing the project, target segment, fund utilisation and risks. This is lighter than an equity prospectus.

Question 5

If a ZCZP issue fails to collect its minimum subscription, the NPO should:

  1. Refund the money to the subscribers
  2. Keep the money and use it for any other project it chooses
  3. Transfer the money to the stock exchange's general account
  4. Convert the money into a loan repayable after ten years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Refund the money to the subscribers

Like other public issues, a ZCZP issue that does not meet its minimum subscription cannot proceed, and funds must be refunded to subscribers.

Question 6

Why do SSE norms require NPOs to show a minimum operating history before registration?

  1. To ensure the NPO has paid dividends to its members
  2. To prove that the NPO is owned by a listed company
  3. To show a track record of programmes and governance
  4. To check whether the NPO has listed equity shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. To show a track record of programmes and governance

A track record gives donors evidence of the NPO's experience and credibility. NPOs do not pay dividends, and ownership by a listed company is not a criterion.

Question 7

Funds raised by an NPO through the SSE must be utilised:

  1. For any purpose decided later by the NPO's founder
  2. To repay the founders' personal loans
  3. To buy listed shares for capital gains
  4. For the projects stated in the fund-raising document
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. For the projects stated in the fund-raising document

Utilisation must follow the objects disclosed to donors, and the NPO must report utilisation periodically. Diverting funds breaches SSE norms and may attract action.

Question 8

An NPO's SSE registration may lapse or be withdrawn if it:

  1. Fails to meet ongoing disclosure obligations
  2. Achieves more beneficiaries than it originally planned
  3. Receives donations from many small donors
  4. Opens a new office in another state
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Fails to meet ongoing disclosure obligations

Registered entities must keep complying with disclosure and eligibility conditions. Persistent non-compliance can lead to suspension or withdrawal of registration.
