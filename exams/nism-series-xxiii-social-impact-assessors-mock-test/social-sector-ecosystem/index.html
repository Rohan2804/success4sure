Social Sector Ecosystem: NISM Series XXIII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Social Sector Ecosystem, from the NISM Series XXIII (Social Impact Assessors) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter maps India's social sector: not-for-profit organisations (trusts, societies, Section 8 companies), for-profit social enterprises, government programmes, CSR by companies, philanthropy, impact investors, development finance institutions and multilateral agencies. It introduces the Sustainable Development Goals, the main funding sources and their constraints, and the shift towards outcome-based funding. Learners should understand who the key actors are, how money flows to social causes, and why credible measurement of impact has become essential for donors and investors alike.

Social Sector Ecosystem questions

Question 1

Which of the following is a for-profit model in the social sector?

  1. A public charitable trust registered under a state trusts law
  2. A social enterprise selling solar lamps at affordable prices
  3. A society running a free school under the Societies Registration Act
  4. A Section 8 company that cannot distribute profits to members
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A social enterprise selling solar lamps at affordable prices

A social enterprise can earn and distribute profits while pursuing social goals. Trusts, societies and Section 8 companies are not-for-profit forms that cannot distribute surplus to members.

Question 2

Outcome-based funding, such as a development impact bond, links payments to:

  1. The number of staff the NGO employs during the year
  2. The size of the NGO's office and its infrastructure
  3. Achievement of agreed social results
  4. The amount of money spent, regardless of what it achieved
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Achievement of agreed social results

In outcome-based funding, investors provide upfront capital and outcome funders pay only when independently verified results are achieved. It shifts the focus from inputs to outcomes.

Question 3

Corporate Social Responsibility spending under the Companies Act, 2013 is based on:

  1. Total revenue of the company in the current financial year only
  2. The market capitalisation of the company on the last trading day
  3. The number of employees the company has in India
  4. Average net profits of the preceding three years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Average net profits of the preceding three years

Companies meeting the CSR thresholds must spend at least 2% of their average net profit for the three immediately preceding financial years on eligible CSR activities.

Question 4

The UN Sustainable Development Goals are primarily intended to:

  1. Replace national laws on charities and social enterprises
  2. Set binding tax rates for NGOs in member countries
  3. Provide a shared global framework for development
  4. Rate individual NGOs for donors on a scale of 1 to 10
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Provide a shared global framework for development

The SDGs set 17 goals with targets to be achieved by 2030. Governments, companies and NGOs use them to align and communicate their work, but they are not laws or ratings.

Question 5

A development finance institution (DFI) differs from a typical philanthropic foundation mainly because a DFI:

  1. Usually expects its capital to be repaid or earn a return
  2. Never invests in private sector companies of any kind
  3. Gives only small grants to individual households
  4. Is not owned or backed by any government
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Usually expects its capital to be repaid or earn a return

DFIs, often government-backed, provide loans, equity and guarantees to projects with development goals, and generally expect repayment or returns. Foundations typically give grants.

Question 6

Which statement about 'blended finance' is correct?

  1. It combines concessional and commercial capital
  2. It refers to NGOs mixing cash and in-kind donations in one account
  3. It is a type of loan given only by banks to individuals
  4. It means investing only in listed equity shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. It combines concessional and commercial capital

Blended finance uses philanthropic or public money, often taking first loss or giving guarantees, to attract private investors into projects that would otherwise seem too risky.

Question 7

Why has credible impact measurement become important in the social sector?

  1. Because SEBI requires all NGOs in India to list on stock exchanges
  2. Because impact reports replace the need for financial audits
  3. Because measurement guarantees that every programme will succeed
  4. Funders want evidence that money creates real change
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Funders want evidence that money creates real change

Donors, CSR teams and impact investors increasingly allocate funds based on demonstrated results. Impact assessment complements, but does not replace, financial audits.

Question 8

An impact investor is best distinguished from a traditional investor by its intention to:

  1. Avoid any financial return and give money away as grants
  2. Generate measurable social impact alongside returns
  3. Invest only in government bonds with guaranteed returns
  4. Earn the highest return possible regardless of social effects
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Generate measurable social impact alongside returns

Impact investors intentionally seek measurable positive social or environmental outcomes together with financial returns, which may range from below-market to market rates.
