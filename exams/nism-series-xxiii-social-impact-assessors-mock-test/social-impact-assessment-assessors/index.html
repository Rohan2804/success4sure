Social Impact Assessment & Assessors: NISM Series XXIII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Social Impact Assessment & Assessors, from the NISM Series XXIII (Social Impact Assessors) syllabus.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains the purpose of social impact assessment (SIA) and the role of social auditors under the SSE framework. It covers who may act as a social auditor (individuals with the specified NISM certification who are registered with a recognised self-regulatory body), the auditor's duties, independence and conflict-of-interest requirements, professional ethics, confidentiality and data protection, and how SIA differs from a financial audit. Focus on ethical scenarios and on what makes an assessment credible.

Social Impact Assessment & Assessors questions

Question 1

To carry out social impact assessments for SSE-registered entities, an individual social auditor must generally:

  1. Hold the NISM certification and be registered with a recognised SRO
  2. Be an employee of the NPO being assessed for at least three years
  3. Hold only a degree in social work without any registration
  4. Be approved by the NPO's largest donor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Hold the NISM certification and be registered with a recognised SRO

SEBI's framework requires social auditors to hold the NISM Series XXIII certification and be registered with a self-regulatory organisation under a specified professional institute.

Question 2

A social auditor's main responsibility in an SIA is to:

  1. Raise funds for the NPO from new donors and investors
  2. Prepare the NPO's financial statements and tax returns
  3. Give an independent opinion on reported impact
  4. Decide which beneficiaries the NPO should serve
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Give an independent opinion on reported impact

The social auditor independently examines the evidence behind claimed outputs and outcomes and gives an assurance opinion. Fund-raising, accounting and programme design are the NPO's job.

Question 3

A social auditor previously worked as a paid consultant designing the same programme she is now asked to assess. She should:

  1. Accept, since her knowledge of the programme is an advantage
  2. Accept, but not tell the NPO's donors about her past role
  3. Disclose the conflict and decline if independence is impaired
  4. Ask the NPO to pay a higher fee to compensate for the risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Disclose the conflict and decline if independence is impaired

Assessing one's own work creates a self-review threat. Independence rules require disclosure and, where the threat cannot be managed, declining the engagement.

Question 4

Which is a key difference between an SIA and a statutory financial audit?

  1. SIA verifies the NPO's tax payments and GST returns
  2. SIA examines social outcomes, not financial statements
  3. SIA is conducted only by the income-tax department
  4. SIA checks share prices of listed social enterprises
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. SIA examines social outcomes, not financial statements

A financial audit gives an opinion on financial statements. SIA assesses whether claimed social results are real, measured credibly and attributable to the programme.

Question 5

While collecting beneficiary data, a social auditor should:

  1. Publish beneficiaries' names and addresses to prove authenticity
  2. Collect as much personal information as possible for future use
  3. Share the data with marketing companies to fund the audit
  4. Obtain informed consent and protect personal data
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Obtain informed consent and protect personal data

Ethical assessment requires informed consent, data minimisation and confidentiality, consistent with India's data protection law. Publishing personal details can harm vulnerable people.

Question 6

The NPO pressures the social auditor to remove a finding of poor outcomes from the report. The auditor should:

  1. Retain the finding if supported by evidence
  2. Remove it, since the NPO is paying for the assessment
  3. Replace it with a positive finding from another NPO
  4. Delete all data so that no one can verify it
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Retain the finding if supported by evidence

Objectivity requires reporting supported findings even if unwelcome. Bowing to client pressure undermines the purpose of SIA and may lead to disciplinary action.

Question 7

Under the SSE framework, the social impact assessment of an entity is meant to give comfort mainly to:

  1. Donors, investors and other stakeholders
  2. The NPO's competitors seeking to copy its programmes
  3. The tax department in computing income tax
  4. The stock exchange's trading members alone
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Donors, investors and other stakeholders

SIA gives stakeholders independent assurance that funds raised through SSE are creating the social impact claimed, improving trust and accountability.

Question 8

Which professional skill is most important for a social auditor when interviewing beneficiaries in a rural setting?

  1. Using leading questions to confirm what the NPO has claimed
  2. Interviewing only those beneficiaries chosen by the NPO staff
  3. Using culturally sensitive, unbiased questioning
  4. Conducting interviews in the presence of programme staff to save time
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Using culturally sensitive, unbiased questioning

Unbiased, context-appropriate questioning gives reliable data. Leading questions, NPO-selected respondents or staff presence can bias answers and weaken the assessment.
