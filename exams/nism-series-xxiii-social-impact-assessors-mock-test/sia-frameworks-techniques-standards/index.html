SIA Frameworks, Techniques & Standards: NISM Series XXIII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SIA Frameworks, Techniques & Standards, from the NISM Series XXIII (Social Impact Assessors) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the methods and standards used in social impact assessment. It includes evaluation designs (randomised control trials, quasi-experimental designs such as difference-in-differences and propensity score matching, before–after studies), qualitative methods (interviews, focus groups, Most Significant Change, participatory approaches), SROI and cost-effectiveness analysis, adjustments such as deadweight, attribution, displacement and drop-off, and frameworks like the IMP five dimensions, IRIS+, OECD-DAC criteria, Social Value Principles and ICAI's social audit standards. Focus on choosing the right method for a given situation.

SIA Frameworks, Techniques & Standards questions

Question 1

In a randomised control trial (RCT), the key feature that supports causal claims is that:

  1. Only the most motivated villages are selected for the programme
  2. Participants are randomly assigned to treatment or control
  3. Results are measured only for those who complete the programme
  4. The NPO chooses which beneficiaries receive services based on need
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Participants are randomly assigned to treatment or control

Random assignment makes treatment and control groups similar on average, so differences in outcomes can be attributed to the programme rather than selection effects.

Question 2

A difference-in-differences design compares:

  1. Only post-programme outcomes of beneficiaries with national averages
  2. Changes over time in programme and comparison groups
  3. Budgets of two NPOs working in different states
  4. Responses from staff before and after training
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Changes over time in programme and comparison groups

DiD subtracts the comparison group's change from the programme group's change, removing common trends. It assumes both groups would have followed parallel trends without the programme.

Question 3

An NPO invests ₹40 lakh, and the present value of social benefits, after adjustments, is ₹1.4 crore. Its SROI ratio is:

  1. 1.4 : 1
  2. 0.29 : 1
  3. 3.5 : 1
  4. 4.0 : 1
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 3.5 : 1

SROI = present value of benefits ÷ investment = 140 ÷ 40 = 3.5. Every rupee invested generates an estimated ₹3.50 of social value, after adjustments like deadweight and attribution.

Question 4

A job-training programme places trainees in local jobs that would otherwise have gone to other unemployed residents of the same town. Which SROI adjustment captures this?

  1. Displacement
  2. Deadweight, since some trainees would have found jobs anyway
  3. Drop-off, since job retention falls over the years
  4. Attribution to other organisations working in the town
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Displacement

Displacement occurs when a programme's benefit shifts outcomes from elsewhere, such as trainees taking jobs others would have got. Deadweight, drop-off and attribution are different adjustments.

Question 5

The Impact Management Project's five dimensions of impact are:

  1. What, Who, How Much, Contribution and Risk
  2. Input, Output, Outcome, Impact and Budget
  3. Plan, Do, Check, Act and Report
  4. Relevance, Efficiency, Tax, Audit and Compliance
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. What, Who, How Much, Contribution and Risk

The IMP five dimensions describe what outcome occurs, who experiences it, how much change happens, the enterprise's contribution, and the risk that impact differs from expectations.

Question 6

The 'Most Significant Change' technique is primarily:

  1. A statistical test of the difference between two group means
  2. A financial ratio comparing costs and benefits
  3. A qualitative method collecting stories of change
  4. A sampling formula for household surveys
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A qualitative method collecting stories of change

Most Significant Change gathers stakeholders' stories and systematically selects the most significant ones, capturing unexpected and qualitative outcomes that indicators may miss.

Question 7

Propensity score matching is used when:

  1. The programme has no beneficiaries and no data are available
  2. The assessor wants to avoid using any comparison group
  3. Random assignment is not possible and a matched comparison group is needed
  4. All beneficiaries are identical in every characteristic
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Random assignment is not possible and a matched comparison group is needed

PSM matches participants with similar non-participants based on observed characteristics, creating a comparison group that approximates what would have happened without the programme.

Question 8

OECD-DAC's 'sustainability' criterion asks whether:

  1. Benefits are likely to continue after support ends
  2. The NPO uses recycled paper in its offices
  3. The programme cost less than its budget
  4. The programme reached the planned number of people
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Benefits are likely to continue after support ends

Sustainability looks at whether net benefits persist after the intervention or funding ends. Cost and reach are covered under efficiency and effectiveness.
