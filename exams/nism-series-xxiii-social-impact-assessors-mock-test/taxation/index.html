Taxation: NISM Series XXIII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Taxation, from the NISM Series XXIII (Social Impact Assessors) syllabus.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter outlines the tax framework relevant to social enterprises and their funders. It covers income-tax registration of charitable trusts, societies and Section 8 companies, conditions for exemption (application of income to charitable purposes, accumulation rules, restrictions on business activities and anonymous donations), donor deductions, tax treatment of CSR spending, GST considerations for NPOs, and taxation of for-profit social enterprises. The law changes frequently, and India's new Income-tax Act renumbers provisions, so focus on principles rather than section numbers or rates.

Taxation questions

Question 1

The main tax benefit of registration of a charitable trust as an approved charitable institution is that:

  1. Income applied for charitable purposes is exempt, subject to conditions
  2. The trust can distribute its surplus to trustees tax-free
  3. All business income of the trust becomes fully exempt without conditions
  4. The trust no longer needs to maintain any accounts
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Income applied for charitable purposes is exempt, subject to conditions

Registered charitable institutions can claim exemption on income applied to their objects, if they meet conditions such as application of income, accounts and audit. Surplus cannot be distributed.

Question 2

A donor contributing to an NPO with valid donor-deduction approval can generally:

  1. Receive a refund of the full donation from the government
  2. Claim a deduction even for cash donations of any size
  3. Claim a deduction for eligible donations
  4. Avoid filing an income tax return for that year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Claim a deduction for eligible donations

Eligible donations to approved institutions qualify for a deduction, subject to limits and conditions. Large cash donations above a threshold are not eligible, so donors should use banking channels.

Question 3

Anonymous donations received by a charitable trust are generally:

  1. Always fully exempt, regardless of amount
  2. Required to be returned to the donor
  3. Treated as the trustee's personal income
  4. Subject to special tax rules beyond limits
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Subject to special tax rules beyond limits

Tax law restricts anonymous donations to discourage misuse, taxing them above specified limits, with some exceptions for wholly religious bodies. Proper donor records are essential.

Question 4

For a company, CSR expenditure under the Companies Act is, for income-tax purposes, generally:

  1. Fully deductible as a business expense in all cases
  2. Eligible for a deduction of twice the amount spent
  3. Not allowed as a business expense
  4. Exempt from GST and income tax in the hands of all recipients
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Not allowed as a business expense

Income-tax law disallows CSR spending as a business expense, though some CSR contributions may qualify for separate donation deductions if conditions are met.

Question 5

A for-profit social enterprise listed through the SSE framework is taxed:

  1. At zero rate because it has social objectives
  2. Like any other company on its profits
  3. Only on dividends paid to shareholders
  4. Only if it raises money from foreign investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Like any other company on its profits

Social intent does not change a company's tax status. For-profit social enterprises pay corporate tax on profits under normal rules.

Question 6

A charitable trust that carries on a business incidental to its objects must take care that:

  1. It distributes the business profits to its trustees
  2. It stops filing income tax returns
  3. Business receipts stay within prescribed limits
  4. It converts into a partnership firm
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Business receipts stay within prescribed limits

Charitable institutions may undertake activities in the nature of trade only within limits and conditions. Exceeding them can jeopardise exemption.

Question 7

Why must an NPO apply most of its income towards its objects in the year of receipt or accumulate it as permitted?

  1. To retain its tax exemption
  2. To qualify for a higher interest rate on deposits
  3. To be listed on the main board of the exchange
  4. To avoid preparing a balance sheet
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. To retain its tax exemption

Exemption depends on applying a prescribed share of income to charitable purposes or accumulating it for specified purposes with intimation. Failure can lead to tax on unapplied income.

Question 8

An adviser should avoid quoting income-tax section numbers to NPOs from memory mainly because:

  1. The new Income-tax Act has changed numbering and rules
  2. NPOs are not covered by any tax law in India
  3. Section numbers are confidential
  4. SEBI prohibits mention of tax law
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The new Income-tax Act has changed numbering and rules

India's new Income-tax Act has reorganised provisions, and Finance Acts regularly amend charity rules. Advice should be based on current law and professional verification.
