SIA Reporting: NISM Series XXIII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SIA Reporting, from the NISM Series XXIII (Social Impact Assessors) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how social impact findings are reported. It covers the Annual Impact Report (AIR) required of SSE-registered entities, the social auditor's assessment report, the elements of a good impact report (scope, methodology, data sources, limitations, positive and negative outcomes, comparison with targets), standards of transparency and balance, and how to present quantitative and qualitative evidence. Focus on the principles of accuracy, completeness, materiality and verifiability and how they apply in practice.

SIA Reporting questions

Question 1

A good social impact report should state its limitations because:

  1. Limitations make the NPO's results look more impressive
  2. SEBI requires reports to hide all methodological problems
  3. Limitations replace the need to describe the methodology
  4. Readers need to judge how far findings can be relied on
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Readers need to judge how far findings can be relied on

Stating limits — such as small samples or no comparison group — helps readers interpret findings correctly. It is a mark of credibility, not weakness.

Question 2

The Annual Impact Report (AIR) of an SSE-registered entity must be:

  1. Prepared by the stock exchange on the entity's behalf
  2. Kept confidential and shared only with the founders
  3. Submitted only once every five years
  4. Assessed by a social auditor and disclosed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Assessed by a social auditor and disclosed

The AIR describes the entity's impact against its strategy and targets and must be assessed by an eligible social auditor and disclosed to the SSE for stakeholders.

Question 3

An impact report shows only success stories and omits a project that missed its targets. This violates the principle of:

  1. Confidentiality of beneficiary data
  2. Completeness and balance
  3. Timeliness of reporting
  4. Use of local language
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Completeness and balance

Reports must present a fair picture, including shortfalls and negative or unintended effects. Selective reporting misleads donors and investors and damages the credibility of the whole report.

Question 4

In an impact report, materiality means focusing on:

  1. Only outcomes that are easiest to measure
  2. Only figures that look largest
  3. Outcomes that matter most to stakeholders' decisions
  4. Outcomes the NPO's founder personally prefers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Outcomes that matter most to stakeholders' decisions

Material information is what could influence stakeholders' decisions about funding or support. Reports should prioritise significant outcomes, not just convenient or flattering ones.

Question 5

Which element best supports the verifiability of a reported outcome?

  1. A photograph of the NPO's office building
  2. A quote from the NPO's chairperson praising the programme
  3. A clear audit trail to source data and method
  4. A list of awards the NPO has received
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A clear audit trail to source data and method

Verifiable reporting allows a third party to trace numbers back to data and methods. Photos, quotes and awards do not substantiate outcomes on their own.

Question 6

A social auditor cannot obtain enough evidence to support some of the NPO's claims. In the assessment report, the auditor should:

  1. Issue a clean opinion to avoid upsetting donors
  2. Qualify the opinion and explain the limitation
  3. Delete the claims from the NPO's own report without telling anyone
  4. Refuse to issue any report and keep the fee
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Qualify the opinion and explain the limitation

When evidence is insufficient, the auditor should modify or qualify the opinion and explain the reason, so readers understand what has and has not been assured.

Question 7

Reporting progress against baseline values in an impact report helps readers to:

  1. Know the NPO's bank balance at the start of the year
  2. See how much has changed since the programme began
  3. Compare the NPO's rent with other NPOs
  4. Identify the auditor's personal qualifications
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. See how much has changed since the programme began

A baseline sets the starting point for each indicator. Comparing endline with baseline, and ideally with a comparison group, shows the scale of change.

Question 8

Which presentation is most appropriate for reporting a sensitive outcome like reduced domestic violence?

  1. Names and photos of survivors as proof
  2. Detailed addresses of households surveyed
  3. Unverified anecdotes from programme staff only
  4. Aggregated, anonymised data with context
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Aggregated, anonymised data with context

Sensitive outcomes need strong protection of respondents. Aggregated, anonymised data with qualitative context conveys the change achieved without risking harm or stigma to individuals.
