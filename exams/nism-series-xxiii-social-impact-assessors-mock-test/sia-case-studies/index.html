SIA Case Studies: NISM Series XXIII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SIA Case Studies, from the NISM Series XXIII (Social Impact Assessors) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter applies impact assessment concepts to practical cases from sectors such as education, health, livelihoods, water and sanitation, and women's empowerment. Cases typically test whether outputs translate into outcomes, whether results can be attributed to the programme, how to adjust for deadweight and drop-off, how to handle unreliable data, and how to frame findings for stakeholders. Read each scenario carefully, identify the logic-model level and the measurement issue involved, and choose the most defensible assessment approach.

SIA Case Studies questions

Question 1

A skilling NGO reports that 80% of trainees found jobs. The assessor finds that local job demand doubled because a large factory opened nearby. The key issue is:

  1. Drop-off — the jobs will certainly disappear next year
  2. Displacement — trainees took jobs from people abroad
  3. Inputs — the NGO spent too little on training
  4. Attribution — part of the result may be due to the factory
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Attribution — part of the result may be due to the factory

External factors like a new factory can explain outcomes. The assessor should estimate the programme's contribution, for example using a comparison group from areas without the factory.

Question 2

A sanitation programme built 2,000 toilets, but a survey shows only 900 are regularly used. In the report, the assessor should present:

  1. 2,000 toilets as the final impact achieved
  2. Toilets built as output and regular use as outcome
  3. Only the 900 figure and hide the output
  4. Neither figure, since data differ
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Toilets built as output and regular use as outcome

Toilets built is an output; regular use is the behavioural outcome that leads to health impact. Reporting both shows the gap and areas for improvement.

Question 3

An NPO's school programme compares test scores of its students with district averages, but its schools are in wealthier blocks. The main problem is:

  1. Too many students in the sample
  2. Use of test scores as an indicator
  3. Insufficient project budget
  4. Selection bias in the comparison
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Selection bias in the comparison

Students in wealthier blocks may score higher regardless of the programme. A credible comparison needs groups with similar backgrounds, such as matched schools.

Question 4

A women's self-help group programme shows income gains in year 1 that decline by half by year 3. In SROI, this pattern is captured through:

  1. Drop-off
  2. Deadweight
  3. Displacement
  4. Leverage
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Drop-off

Drop-off accounts for the decline of outcomes over time. Deadweight is what would have happened anyway, and displacement is benefit shifted from others.

Question 5

An assessor finds that attendance registers in a health programme were filled in by staff days later in the same handwriting. The best response is to:

  1. Treat the data as unreliable and seek other evidence
  2. Use the registers as they are, since they are official records
  3. Ask staff to rewrite them neatly before the assessment
  4. Ignore attendance altogether in the report without mention
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Treat the data as unreliable and seek other evidence

Signs of fabricated or back-filled records undermine reliability. The assessor should triangulate with other sources, such as beneficiary interviews, and report the data quality issue.

Question 6

A nutrition programme reduces child stunting from 40% to 30%, while a comparison area fell from 40% to 36%. The impact attributable to the programme is about:

  1. 10 percentage points
  2. 4 percentage points
  3. 6 percentage points
  4. 30 percentage points
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 6 percentage points

Difference-in-differences: programme change (−10) minus comparison change (−4) = −6 percentage points. The other 4 points would likely have happened anyway.

Question 7

A microfinance programme reports high repayment rates, but interviews show many borrowers took new loans from moneylenders to repay. The assessor should conclude that:

  1. High repayment rates always prove that borrowers are better off
  2. Moneylender loans are unrelated to the programme
  3. The programme should stop collecting repayment data
  4. Repayment alone may hide negative outcomes like over-indebtedness
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Repayment alone may hide negative outcomes like over-indebtedness

Output metrics like repayment can mask harm. Balanced assessment looks at household debt and wellbeing outcomes and reports unintended negative effects.

Question 8

A livelihood NGO claims that it 'lifted 10,000 families out of poverty' based only on a count of families enrolled. The weakness is that:

  1. 10,000 is too small a number to report
  2. Poverty cannot be measured under any method
  3. Enrolment is an output, not evidence of reduced poverty
  4. The NGO should have reported a larger figure
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Enrolment is an output, not evidence of reduced poverty

Enrolment shows reach, not change. Claims about poverty reduction need outcome data such as income or consumption, compared against a baseline and ideally a counterfactual.
