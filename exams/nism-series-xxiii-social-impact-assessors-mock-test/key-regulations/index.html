Key Regulations: NISM Series XXIII practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Key Regulations, from the NISM Series XXIII (Social Impact Assessors) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the main laws and regulations affecting social enterprises in India: the Indian Trusts Act and state public trust laws, the Societies Registration Act, Section 8 of the Companies Act, 2013, CSR provisions and Schedule VII, the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act, 2010 and its amendments, SEBI's ICDR and LODR provisions for SSE, the AIF Regulations for Social Impact Funds, and data protection law. Focus on what each law governs and on key compliance requirements such as FCRA bank accounts and restrictions on Section 8 companies.

Key Regulations questions

Question 1

Under the FCRA as amended in 2020, foreign contributions must first be received in:

  1. Any savings account of the NPO in any bank of its choice
  2. The personal account of the NPO's chief executive
  3. A designated FCRA account at SBI's main New Delhi branch
  4. An account opened with a foreign bank outside India
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A designated FCRA account at SBI's main New Delhi branch

The 2020 amendment requires every FCRA-registered organisation to receive foreign contributions first in a designated account at the State Bank of India, New Delhi Main Branch.

Question 2

A Section 8 company is restricted from:

  1. Opening a bank account in its own name
  2. Paying dividends to its members
  3. Employing staff and paying salaries
  4. Receiving donations from Indian donors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Paying dividends to its members

Section 8 companies must apply profits to promote their objects and cannot pay dividends. They can open bank accounts, employ staff and receive donations.

Question 3

Schedule VII of the Companies Act, 2013 lists:

  1. Taxes payable by NPOs on business income
  2. Activities eligible as CSR spending
  3. Members of the board of the SSE
  4. Rules for issuing equity shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Activities eligible as CSR spending

Schedule VII sets out activities that qualify as CSR, such as education, health, environment and rural development. Companies must spend CSR funds on these activities.

Question 4

After the 2020 FCRA amendment, an NPO receiving foreign contribution may:

  1. Freely transfer it to any registered NPO in India
  2. Use all of it for administrative expenses without limit
  3. Deposit it into any bank chosen by the donor
  4. Not transfer it to another organisation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Not transfer it to another organisation

The 2020 amendment prohibits transfer of foreign contribution to any other person or organisation and lowered the cap on administrative expenses.

Question 5

Public charitable trusts in many states, such as Maharashtra, are governed by:

  1. The SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations
  2. The Banking Regulation Act
  3. The Insurance Act
  4. State public trust laws
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. State public trust laws

Public trusts are regulated by state laws, such as the Maharashtra Public Trusts Act. The Indian Trusts Act, 1882 mainly governs private trusts.

Question 6

SEBI's provisions on issue of securities and fund raising by Social Enterprises are mainly found in:

  1. The FCRA Rules issued by the Home Ministry
  2. SEBI ICDR Regulations
  3. The Income-tax Rules
  4. The Banking Ombudsman Scheme
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. SEBI ICDR Regulations

SEBI added a chapter on Social Stock Exchanges to the ICDR Regulations, covering registration, fund raising and related disclosures, with ongoing obligations under LODR.

Question 7

An NPO collecting beneficiaries' personal data must comply with:

  1. The SEBI (Portfolio Managers) Regulations
  2. The Negotiable Instruments Act
  3. The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023
  4. The Payment and Settlement Systems Act
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023

The DPDP Act governs processing of digital personal data, requiring lawful purpose, consent or legitimate use, and safeguards. It applies to NPOs collecting beneficiary data.

Question 8

A company's unspent CSR amount relating to an ongoing project must be:

  1. Distributed to shareholders as a special dividend
  2. Kept in the company's general reserves indefinitely
  3. Paid to the company's directors as a bonus
  4. Moved to a special unspent CSR account and spent within the set period
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Moved to a special unspent CSR account and spent within the set period

CSR rules require unspent amounts for ongoing projects to be transferred to an Unspent CSR Account and used within a specified period; other unspent sums go to specified funds.
