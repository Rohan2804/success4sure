Special Services – Pledge & Hypothecation: NISM Series VI revision notes

Demat securities can be used as collateral without any paperwork moving between banks. This chapter covers who initiates and who accepts each step of a pledge, how a pledge is closed or invoked, how hypothecation differs, and the margin pledge framework that replaced broker-held collateral. Questions mostly test sequence and rights.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Creating a pledge

The pledgor (the borrower) initiates the pledge through its DP, specifying the pledgee's account, ISIN and quantity. The pledgee (the lender, usually a bank or NBFC) then accepts or rejects it through its own DP. Only after acceptance are the securities marked as pledged; they stay in the pledgor's account but cannot be transferred.

2. Closing a pledge

When the loan is repaid, the pledgor initiates closure and the pledgee confirms it. The pledge mark is removed and the securities become free again. A pledgee can also initiate closure on its own when it is satisfied that the loan is closed.

3. Invoking a pledge

If the borrower defaults, the pledgee invokes the pledge, and the securities move from the pledgor's account to the pledgee's account. In a pledge, the pledgee can invoke without the pledgor's fresh consent, as per the loan agreement. This is the main protection a lender has.

4. Pledge vs hypothecation

In depository terms, hypothecation gives the lender weaker control: invocation requires the hypothecator's confirmation, whereas a pledgee can invoke unilaterally. Hypothecation is used where the lender is comfortable with a lighter charge, such as some loans where securities are additional comfort rather than primary security.

5. Margin pledge

Clients who want to give securities as margin for trading pledge them to their trading member in a margin pledge account. The trading member re-pledges to the clearing member, which re-pledges to the clearing corporation. The shares stay in the client's demat account throughout, ending the old practice of transferring them to the broker.

6. Benefits during pledge

Corporate benefits on pledged securities, such as dividends, bonus and rights, flow to the beneficial owner (pledgor), because ownership does not change. Bonus shares may also be pledged automatically if the agreement provides. Voting rights also stay with the pledgor unless invoked.

7. Loans against securities

Banks and NBFCs offer loans against shares, mutual fund units and bonds with a loan-to-value ratio, for example a loan of ₹5 lakh against shares worth ₹10 lakh (50% LTV). If the value falls, the lender makes a margin call; if not met, the pledge is invoked and shares sold. LTV caps are set by RBI for some categories.

8. Pledge alerts

Depositories send SMS and email alerts on creation and invocation of pledges, so the client knows immediately if a pledge was created without consent. Clients should match these alerts with their loan or margin records.

9. Locked-in and frozen securities

Locked-in securities can be pledged in specified cases, for example promoter shares pledged to banks for loans to the company, with the lock-in continuing. Frozen securities cannot be pledged until unfrozen.

Common traps in the exam

  • Getting the initiator wrong. The pledgor initiates creation; the pledgee accepts. The pledgee invokes.
  • Assuming pledged shares move to the lender's account at creation. They stay in the pledgor's account until invocation.
  • Treating pledge and hypothecation as identical. Hypothecation invocation needs the hypothecator's confirmation.
  • Thinking dividends on pledged shares go to the lender. They go to the beneficial owner, the pledgor.

Quick recap

Pledgor initiates, pledgee accepts, pledgee invokes on default; closure by pledgor confirmed by pledgee. Shares remain in the pledgor's account until invocation and corporate benefits stay with the pledgor. Hypothecation needs the hypothecator's confirmation to invoke. Margin pledges flow client to trading member to clearing member to clearing corporation.
