Special Services – Pledge & Hypothecation: NISM Series VI practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Special Services – Pledge & Hypothecation, from the NISM Series VI (Depository Operations) syllabus.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how demat securities are used as collateral. Revise the pledge process (initiation by the pledgor, acceptance by the pledgee), closure and invocation, the difference between pledge and hypothecation, the margin pledge and re-pledge framework for brokers and clearing corporations, and loans against securities. Understand who initiates each step and what happens to corporate benefits on pledged securities.

Special Services – Pledge & Hypothecation questions

Question 1

In a demat pledge, once the pledge is created, the securities:

  1. Move to the pledgee's account immediately
  2. Stay in the pledgor's account, marked as pledged
  3. Are rematerialised
  4. Are sold at market
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Stay in the pledgor's account, marked as pledged

A pledge marks a lien on the securities in the pledgor's account in favour of the pledgee. Ownership remains with the pledgor until invocation.

Question 2

A borrower repays a loan against shares. To release the shares, the next step is:

  1. Pledge invocation
  2. Transmission
  3. Transposition
  4. Pledge closure
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Pledge closure

On repayment, the pledgor requests closure and the pledgee accepts, removing the lien. Invocation is used only on default.

Question 3

Corporate benefits such as dividends on pledged shares go to:

  1. The pledgor
  2. The pledgee
  3. The DP
  4. The broker
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The pledgor

The pledgor remains the beneficial owner, so dividends and similar benefits are credited to the pledgor unless agreed otherwise.

Question 4

Under the margin pledge framework, a broker who receives a client's margin pledge may:

  1. Sell the shares freely whenever the broker needs funds
  2. Transfer them to its own account
  3. Re-pledge to the clearing member or corporation
  4. Lend them to other clients
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Re-pledge to the clearing member or corporation

Brokers can re-pledge client securities to the clearing member and clearing corporation to meet margin needs, keeping securities in the client's account.

Question 5

Hypothecation differs from pledge in that, in hypothecation:

  1. The securities move to the lender's account at once on creation
  2. No agreement is needed
  3. Only banks can be lenders
  4. Invocation needs the hypothecator's confirmation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Invocation needs the hypothecator's confirmation

In hypothecation, the lender cannot invoke unilaterally; invocation requires confirmation by the borrower (hypothecator). In a pledge, the pledgee can invoke on default.

Question 6

Which party initiates the creation of a pledge in the depository system?

  1. The pledgor, through its DP
  2. The pledgee, without the pledgor's knowledge
  3. SEBI
  4. The RTA
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The pledgor, through its DP

The pledgor (borrower) instructs its DP to create a pledge; the pledgee then accepts it through its own DP.

Question 7

A lender invokes a pledge after the borrower defaults. As a result:

  1. Securities are refunded to the pledgor
  2. The pledge is closed without transfer
  3. Securities move to the pledgee's account
  4. The ISIN is cancelled
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Securities move to the pledgee's account

Invocation transfers the pledged securities from the pledgor's account to the pledgee's account, so the lender can recover dues.

Question 8

Can a demat holding that is already pledged be sold on the exchange by the client?

  1. Yes, without any change
  2. Not until the pledge is released
  3. Yes, by the DP
  4. Only after transmission
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Not until the pledge is released

Pledged securities are under lien. They must be unpledged (or the lender must consent) before they can be delivered against a sale.
