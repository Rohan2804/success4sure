Investor Services: NISM Series VI revision notes

The final chapter of the Depository Operations syllabus focuses on what investors are entitled to and how they can protect themselves. It covers statements and alerts, grievance redress, the online dispute resolution mechanism, investor protection funds and safe practices. Questions usually ask for the right escalation path, timeline or safeguard in a given situation.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Consolidated Account Statement

Investors with a PAN get a single CAS from the depository that covers all their demat accounts and mutual fund folios. It is sent monthly when there are transactions, and half-yearly otherwise. The CAS shows holdings, transactions and the value of investments, making it easy to spot unauthorised activity.

2. Alerts

Depositories send SMS and email alerts directly to the investor for debits, pledges and changes in key details. The investor should immediately contact the DP and depository if an alert does not match their own instruction. Depositories also send alerts for credits in many cases.

3. Grievance escalation path

First, the investor complains to the DP. If unresolved, the investor can approach the depository. Next, the complaint can be lodged on SEBI's SCORES platform, which requires the entity to respond within prescribed timelines, currently 21 calendar days. Unresolved disputes can then go to online dispute resolution.

4. Online dispute resolution

SEBI's ODR framework allows investors to file disputes against market intermediaries on the Smart ODR portal. Disputes first go to online conciliation; if that fails, to online arbitration with arbitrators empanelled by ODR institutions. The process is time-bound and largely conducted online.

5. Investor protection fund

Each depository maintains an investor protection fund, financed partly from its profits and penalties, to promote investor education and protect investors against certain defaults. It complements exchange investor protection funds that compensate investors when brokers default.

6. Investor charter

Depositories and DPs publish an investor charter describing services provided, timelines, rights and obligations of investors, and grievance redress mechanisms. Displaying the charter and monthly complaint data is mandatory, which makes service quality visible.

7. Safe practices

Investors should never sign blank DIS, keep the DIS booklet safe, strike out unused portions of a slip, update contact details, check statements and alerts regularly, and avoid giving broad authorisations. Use DDPI only if needed and only for the specified purposes. Freezing the account is useful during long absences.

8. e-Voting and other services

Depositories provide e-voting platforms so beneficial owners can vote on company resolutions online without attending meetings. They also offer online access to holdings, e-DIS and portals for nomination and contact updates.

9. Investor education

Depositories conduct investor awareness programmes and publish FAQs and guides in Indian languages. Questions sometimes ask about the purpose of investor protection funds; investor education and awareness is one of their main uses.

Common traps in the exam

  • Going to SCORES before the DP. The first point of complaint is the intermediary; SCORES comes after.
  • Thinking ODR starts with arbitration. It starts with conciliation; arbitration follows only if conciliation fails.
  • Believing the CAS is sent only on request. It is sent automatically to PAN-linked investors.
  • Signing a blank DIS for convenience. This is the most common route for unauthorised transfers.

Quick recap

CAS covers all demat and MF holdings, monthly with transactions and half-yearly otherwise. Alerts go directly to investors. Escalate DP, then depository, then SCORES, then ODR (conciliation, then arbitration). Investor protection funds support education and protection. Never sign blank DIS and keep contact details updated.
