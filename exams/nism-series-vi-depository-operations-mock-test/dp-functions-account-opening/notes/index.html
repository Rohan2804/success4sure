DP Functions – Account Opening: NISM Series VI revision notes

Account opening is one of the most heavily tested areas of the Depository Operations exam. You need to know who can open which type of account, what documents and verification are needed, how many holders are permitted, and what the Basic Services Demat Account offers. Questions are often set as small scenarios: a minor, an HUF, an NRI or a company wants an account — what applies?

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Types of beneficial owner accounts

Accounts can be opened for resident individuals, NRIs (repatriable and non-repatriable), HUFs, minors, corporates, trusts, partnership firms, FPIs, banks and clearing members. Each type has its own sub-type in the depository system, which matters for tax, repatriation and settlement. A clearing member account is a business account, not a beneficial owner account.

2. KYC and in-person verification

Every holder must complete KYC: PAN (mandatory except specified exemptions), proof of identity and address, and in-person verification by the DP's staff or via video-based KYC. KYC records are uploaded to a KRA so the investor need not repeat KYC with other intermediaries. Original documents must be verified against copies.

3. Joint accounts

A demat account can have a maximum of three holders. The order of names matters: holdings in 'A and B' are different from 'B and A', so shares held in a different order cannot be credited without transposition. All holders sign the account opening form, and instructions generally need all holders' signatures unless a holder authorises otherwise.

4. Minor accounts

A minor can only be the sole holder; joint holding is not allowed. The account is operated by a natural or court-appointed guardian whose details and KYC are recorded. A minor cannot nominate. When the minor turns 18, the account must be converted with fresh KYC of the now-major holder.

5. HUF, corporate and NRI accounts

An HUF account is opened in the name of the HUF and operated by the Karta; coparceners cannot be joint holders. Corporates provide board resolutions and authorised signatories. NRIs need a repatriable or non-repatriable bank account (NRE or NRO) linked appropriately, and FEMA-compliant documentation. Partnership firms often hold through partners' names because a firm is not a legal person.

6. Basic Services Demat Account

BSDA is a low-cost account for small individual investors with only one demat account across depositories, as sole or first holder. As of 2026, holdings up to ₹4 lakh attract no AMC and holdings between ₹4 lakh and ₹10 lakh a capped AMC (check current SEBI circular). The DP must offer BSDA to eligible clients and convert accounts automatically if eligibility changes.

7. Nomination at opening

Individual sole or joint holders must either nominate or explicitly opt out through a declaration (with verification). SEBI's 2025 nomination framework allows multiple nominees (up to 10 as of 2026, check current rules) with percentage shares. Non-individuals such as companies, HUFs and societies cannot nominate.

8. Documents given to the client

After opening, the DP gives the client a copy of the account opening form, rights and obligations document, tariff sheet, Do's and Don'ts and the client master report showing account details. A DIS booklet is issued on request only. Clients should verify their client master for correct bank details and nominee information.

9. Linking bank account and contact details

Bank details are captured so that cash corporate benefits such as dividends reach the right account. Mobile numbers and email IDs are mandatory for alerts; SEBI requires them to be unique to a family. Incorrect contact details are a common cause of missed alerts and fraud, which is why DPs validate them at opening.

Common traps in the exam

  • Allowing a minor as a joint holder. A minor can only be the sole holder through a guardian.
  • Thinking an HUF can nominate or that coparceners are joint holders. The Karta operates the account and nomination is not available.
  • Believing holdings in 'A and B' and 'B and A' are interchangeable. The order of names is part of the account identity.
  • Assuming BSDA is available to anyone. It requires only one demat account, as sole or first holder, and holding value within the SEBI limit.

Quick recap

Up to three holders; minors only as sole holder via guardian; HUF operated by Karta, no nomination. KYC with PAN and in-person or video verification, recorded with a KRA. BSDA for small investors with a single demat account and holdings within the SEBI limit. Individuals must nominate or opt out explicitly.
