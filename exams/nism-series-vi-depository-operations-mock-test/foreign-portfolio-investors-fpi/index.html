Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPI): NISM Series VI practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPI), from the NISM Series VI (Depository Operations) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how foreign portfolio investors access Indian securities. Revise FPI registration through Designated Depository Participants on behalf of SEBI, the two FPI categories, KYC norms, custodians' role in settlement, the investment limits for individual FPIs and FPI groups, and monitoring of these limits by depositories. Understand the steps when limits are breached and why FPIs need PAN and a demat account in India.

Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPI) questions

Question 1

FPI registration on behalf of SEBI is granted by:

  1. A Designated Depository Participant
  2. The Reserve Bank of India
  3. The stock exchange
  4. The investor's home regulator
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A Designated Depository Participant

SEBI has authorised DDPs (large custodians or banks approved by SEBI) to grant FPI registration on its behalf after due diligence.

Question 2

SEBI classifies FPIs into how many categories under the current regulations?

  1. Three
  2. Four
  3. Two
  4. Five
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Two

The current SEBI FPI Regulations have two categories, Category I and Category II, based on regulatory status and risk of the entity.

Question 3

Who monitors FPI holdings in listed companies against the prescribed limits?

  1. The FPI's home regulator
  2. The custodian alone
  3. The stock exchanges' IPF trust
  4. The depositories
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The depositories

Depositories monitor FPI investment limits using holding data and trigger alerts when limits approach, so breaches can be prevented or corrected.

Question 4

Which entity typically confirms an FPI's trades and settles them?

  1. The issuer company
  2. The FPI's custodian
  3. A mutual fund
  4. The RTA
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The FPI's custodian

Custodians handle settlement for FPIs: they confirm trades executed by brokers and ensure funds and securities pay-in and pay-out.

Question 5

Before an FPI can trade in India, it must have:

  1. Indian citizenship for its directors and fund managers
  2. A listed Indian parent
  3. PAN, demat and bank accounts in India
  4. An AMFI registration
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. PAN, demat and bank accounts in India

An FPI needs registration, a PAN, a demat account with a custodian DP and a bank account to settle trades and receive income.

Question 6

An FPI's holding in a company goes above the individual limit. The excess must be:

  1. Held indefinitely
  2. Transferred to SEBI
  3. Converted into debt
  4. Divested or reclassified as FDI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Divested or reclassified as FDI

If the limit is breached, the FPI must either divest the excess within the allowed time or have the investment reclassified as FDI, subject to rules.

Question 7

A Category I FPI typically includes:

  1. Unregulated individuals
  2. Government and regulated entities
  3. Indian residents
  4. Domestic mutual funds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Government and regulated entities

Category I covers government-related investors, central banks, pension funds and appropriately regulated entities, which carry lower KYC risk.

Question 8

Income such as dividends earned by an FPI on Indian shares is received:

  1. In its Indian bank account
  2. In cash at the stock exchange
  3. Through the RTA's account
  4. In gold
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. In its Indian bank account

Dividends are paid to the FPI's designated bank account in India and can then be repatriated after taxes as permitted.
