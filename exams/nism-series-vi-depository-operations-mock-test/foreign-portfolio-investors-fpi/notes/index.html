Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPI): NISM Series VI revision notes

Foreign portfolio investors are a major source of investment in Indian markets, and depositories play a key role in watching their limits. This chapter covers how FPIs register, their categories, the role of custodians and designated depository participants, and investment limits. Expect conceptual questions with a few numbers that you must recall exactly.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. What an FPI is

An FPI is a non-resident entity that invests in Indian securities as a portfolio investor rather than taking a controlling stake. Investment below 10% of a listed company's paid-up equity on a fully diluted basis is portfolio investment; at 10% or above, it is treated as foreign direct investment.

2. Registration through DDPs

FPIs register with a Designated Depository Participant, which grants registration on behalf of SEBI. DDPs are SEBI-approved custodians that also act as DPs. Registration is permanent unless suspended or surrendered, subject to payment of fees at the prescribed intervals. The DDP also conducts KYC and ongoing due diligence.

3. Categories

There are two categories. Category I includes government and government-related investors such as central banks and sovereign wealth funds, multilateral bodies, and appropriately regulated entities like pension funds, banks, insurers and asset managers from eligible jurisdictions. Category II covers all others, such as unregulated funds, family offices and individuals. KYC intensity is higher for Category II.

4. Investment limits

A single FPI, or an investor group with common ownership, must hold less than 10% of a company's total paid-up equity. The aggregate limit for all FPIs is now the sectoral cap applicable to that sector, unless a company lowers it with shareholder approval. Limits are as of 2026; check current SEBI and RBI rules.

5. Monitoring by depositories

Depositories monitor FPI holdings in each listed company and publish alerts when aggregate holdings approach the limit, after which further FPI purchases are restricted. When a single FPI group breaches 10%, it must divest the excess within a prescribed short period or have the investment reclassified as FDI.

6. Custodians and settlement

FPIs appoint a SEBI-registered custodian that holds their securities and settles trades on their behalf. FPIs trade through brokers, and their trades are confirmed by custodians before settlement. Custodians also handle corporate actions, tax deductions and reporting for their FPI clients.

7. PAN, bank and demat accounts

Every FPI needs a PAN, a demat account with a DP and a special non-resident rupee account with a designated bank for investment flows. Funds come in through banking channels in foreign currency and are converted to rupees; repatriation of sale proceeds and income is permitted after applicable taxes.

8. Permitted instruments

FPIs can invest in listed equity, unlisted equity within limits, government securities, corporate bonds, units of mutual funds, REITs and InvITs, and exchange-traded derivatives within prescribed limits. Debt investment is subject to RBI's macro-prudential limits, and some government securities are fully accessible under the fully accessible route.

9. Offshore derivative instruments

Some FPIs issue offshore derivative instruments such as participatory notes to overseas investors with Indian securities as underlying. Only Category I FPIs can issue ODIs, subject to strict KYC and reporting. This is a frequent exam detail.

Common traps in the exam

  • Confusing FPI and FDI thresholds. Below 10% is portfolio investment; 10% and above is FDI.
  • Thinking SEBI processes FPI registration directly. DDPs grant registration on SEBI's behalf.
  • Assuming any FPI can issue participatory notes. Only Category I FPIs, subject to conditions.
  • Forgetting the aggregate limit now defaults to the sectoral cap, not the older 24% default.

Quick recap

FPIs register through DDPs on SEBI's behalf; two categories, with Category I for government and regulated entities. Individual FPI or group holdings must stay below 10% of paid-up equity, aggregate up to the sectoral cap. Depositories monitor limits; breaches must be divested or reclassified as FDI. Custodians settle FPI trades; FPIs need PAN and demat accounts.
