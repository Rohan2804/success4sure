Special Services – Public Offering / Corporate Actions: NISM Series VI revision notes

Public issue allotments and corporate actions reach investors' demat accounts automatically through the depository. The exam tests how this happens, the role of the RTA and record date, and simple calculations on bonus issues and splits. Remember a simple rule: securities benefits come to the demat account, cash benefits go to the bank account linked to it.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. IPO allotment credit

After the basis of allotment is finalised, the issuer's RTA uploads the allotment as a corporate action and the depository credits shares directly to successful applicants' demat accounts. The investor's DP ID and client ID from the bid are used, so incorrect details lead to failed credits. Listing usually follows on T+3 from issue closure (as of 2026).

2. Record date

The record date is fixed by the company to determine which beneficial owners are entitled to a corporate benefit. The depository provides the RTA a list of beneficial owners as at the end of the record date. Because of T+1 settlement, an investor must buy at least one trading day before the record date to be on the list.

3. Bonus issue

A bonus issue gives free shares from reserves. In a 1:2 bonus, a holder gets one new share for every two held: 100 shares become 150. Fractional entitlements are usually consolidated and sold, with proceeds paid in cash. Bonus shares come to the demat account; the share price adjusts downward proportionally.

4. Stock split

A split reduces face value and increases the number of shares without changing total value. If a ₹10 face value share is split into ₹2, each share becomes five: 100 shares become 500, and price per share falls to roughly one-fifth. The old ISIN is replaced with a new one for the new face value.

5. Rights issue and rights entitlements

In a rights issue, existing shareholders get the right to buy new shares, usually at a discount. Rights entitlements are credited in demat form with a separate ISIN, can be traded on exchanges or renounced off-market, and lapse if not exercised or sold before the deadline. Applications are made through ASBA.

6. Mergers and amalgamations

When companies merge, shares of the transferor company are extinguished and the transferee's shares credited per the swap ratio, for example 3 shares of B for every 5 of A. The RTA executes this as a corporate action, and fractions are dealt with as per the scheme.

7. Buyback through tender

In a tender offer buyback, shareholders tender shares through their broker; shares are blocked in the demat account, and accepted shares are debited while unaccepted shares are released. Cash goes to the linked bank account.

8. Cash benefits

Dividends, interest and redemption amounts are paid through electronic transfer to the bank account linked to the demat account as per records on the record date. Updating the bank account in the demat client master is therefore essential; dividends are not credited to demat accounts.

9. Debit corporate actions

Some corporate actions debit securities, such as redemption of debentures, reduction of capital or a reverse split (consolidation of shares). These are executed by the depository on RTA instructions, with the debit and corresponding payment or new credit happening together.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Bonus shares = shares held × (bonus number ÷ base number); e.g. 200 × 1/4 = 50 new shares.
  • Shares after split = shares held × (old face value ÷ new face value); e.g. 100 × 10/1 = 1,000.

Common traps in the exam

  • Thinking dividends are credited to the demat account. They go to the linked bank account; only securities go to the demat account.
  • Miscalculating bonus: a 1:2 bonus means one new for two held, so 100 becomes 150, not 300.
  • Buying on the record date and expecting the benefit. With T+1, you must buy at least one trading day before the record date.
  • Assuming rights entitlements cannot be traded. They are credited in demat form and can be sold or renounced before expiry.

Quick recap

RTA executes allotments and corporate actions; the depository credits or debits accounts. Record date decides who is entitled; under T+1 buy a day earlier. 1:2 bonus = one free share per two held; split multiplies shares by old FV/new FV. Rights entitlements are tradeable demat credits. Cash goes to the bank account.
