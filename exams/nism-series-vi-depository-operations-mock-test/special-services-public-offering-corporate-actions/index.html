Special Services – Public Offering / Corporate Actions: NISM Series VI practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Special Services – Public Offering / Corporate Actions, from the NISM Series VI (Depository Operations) syllabus.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how depositories support public issues and corporate actions. Revise how IPO allotments are credited through corporate actions by the RTA, the role of record dates, and processing of bonus issues, stock splits, rights issues (including tradeable rights entitlements), mergers and buybacks. Practise simple bonus and split calculations, and know that cash benefits like dividends go to bank accounts while securities benefits go to demat accounts.

Special Services – Public Offering / Corporate Actions questions

Question 1

A company splits its shares from face value ₹10 to ₹2. An investor held 200 shares. After the split, she holds:

  1. 400 shares
  2. 40 shares
  3. 1,000 shares
  4. 200 shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 1,000 shares

Each ₹10 share becomes five ₹2 shares, so 200 × 5 = 1,000 shares. The total face value and the investor's value stay the same.

Question 2

Rights entitlements (REs) in a rights issue are:

  1. Credited to demat and can be traded
  2. Sent as physical letters only
  3. Never transferable
  4. Credited to the bank account
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Credited to demat and can be traded

REs are credited in demat form to eligible shareholders. They can be traded on exchanges or renounced off-market, and are used to apply in the issue.

Question 3

A company announces a 2:5 bonus. An investor holds 500 shares on the record date. The bonus shares credited are:

  1. 250
  2. 200
  3. 1,250
  4. 100
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 200

Two bonus shares for every five held: 500 × 2/5 = 200 shares. 250 would be a 1:2 bonus.

Question 4

Credit of shares allotted in an IPO to investors' demat accounts is done through:

  1. Off-market transfers by the issuer
  2. Investors' DPs on their own
  3. SEBI
  4. A corporate action by the RTA
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A corporate action by the RTA

The issuer's RTA executes a corporate action file with the depository to credit allotted shares to investors' demat accounts.

Question 5

For a corporate action, the 'record date' is the date on which:

  1. Eligible holders are identified
  2. The shares are first listed
  3. The company pays income tax
  4. Shareholders must vote
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Eligible holders are identified

The depository provides the list of beneficial owners as on the record date; those holders receive the benefit.

Question 6

In a merger where holders of Company A get shares of Company B, the depository:

  1. Asks investors to buy B shares
  2. Debits A and credits B shares
  3. Sends physical B certificates
  4. Keeps both ISINs active
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Debits A and credits B shares

Through a corporate action, the RTA debits the old ISIN and credits the new shares in the swap ratio to beneficial owners.

Question 7

Dividend declared by a company is paid to shareholders':

  1. Demat accounts
  2. Broker accounts
  3. DP accounts
  4. Bank accounts
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Bank accounts

Cash benefits are paid to the bank account linked to the demat account. Only securities benefits such as bonus shares are credited to the demat account.

Question 8

In a buyback through the stock exchange tender route, shareholders tender shares by:

  1. Sending physical certificates to SEBI
  2. Transferring shares to the company directly by gift
  3. Blocking shares via their broker
  4. Pledging shares to the RTA
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Blocking shares via their broker

Tendered shares are blocked or earmarked in the shareholder's demat account through the broker, and are debited if accepted in the buyback.
