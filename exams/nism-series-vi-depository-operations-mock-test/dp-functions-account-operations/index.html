DP Functions – Account Operations: NISM Series VI practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on DP Functions – Account Operations, from the NISM Series VI (Depository Operations) syllabus.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers day-to-day operations of demat accounts: delivery instruction slips and their control, online instructions, freezing and unfreezing, change of details, transposition, consolidation of accounts, account closure, and transaction and holding statements. Revise safeguards against misuse of DIS, the difference between transposition and transmission, how to move holdings between accounts, and how a client can block debits. Questions are practical and scenario-based.

DP Functions – Account Operations questions

Question 1

A joint account is held as 'A, B'. The holders want it to be 'B, A'. This change is called:

  1. Transmission
  2. Transposition
  3. Dematerialisation
  4. Rematerialisation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Transposition

Transposition changes the order of names. The holders open a new account in the desired order and move securities to it. Transmission applies on the death of a holder.

Question 2

A client wants to merge holdings from two of his own demat accounts into one. The appropriate step is to:

  1. Ask the RTA to issue new physical certificates
  2. Rematerialise both holdings
  3. Invoke a pledge
  4. Transfer and close one account
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Transfer and close one account

Consolidation is done by an account transfer instruction from one account to the other in the same name, followed by closure of the empty account.

Question 3

To prevent misuse, a DP should issue DIS booklets:

  1. Only to the account holder on request
  2. In bulk to the client's broker for convenience
  3. Pre-signed by the DP official
  4. Without serial numbers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Only to the account holder on request

DIS booklets are issued only to account holders on a signed requisition, with pre-printed serial numbers, so that every instruction can be traced.

Question 4

A client wants to stop all debits from his demat account while travelling for three months. He can:

  1. Close the account and open a new one later
  2. Transfer holdings to the DP's pool
  3. Request a debit freeze
  4. Pledge them to a friend
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Request a debit freeze

A freeze can be applied for debits, credits or both, for the whole account or specific ISINs, at the client's request, and lifted later.

Question 5

Which facility lets clients submit delivery instructions electronically instead of physical slips?

  1. The rights entitlement window
  2. Rematerialisation
  3. Pledge closure
  4. Online instruction facilities
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Online instruction facilities

Depositories offer internet-based facilities (such as SPEED-e at NSDL and easiest at CDSL) through which clients submit instructions online securely.

Question 6

A DP receives a DIS where the signature differs from the one on record. The DP should:

  1. Reject the instruction
  2. Execute it if the client calls to confirm
  3. Execute it if the amount is small
  4. Forward it to SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Reject the instruction

A DP must verify signatures against its records. A mismatch is a ground for rejection, protecting the client against fraudulent debits.

Question 7

Before closing a demat account that still holds securities, the client must:

  1. Pay tax on all unrealised gains on the holdings
  2. Sell all securities to the DP
  3. Transfer the holdings to another own account
  4. Obtain a court order
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Transfer the holdings to another own account

The client can transfer balances to another demat account in the same name (or rematerialise them). The DP closes the account only after it has no holdings.

Question 8

A change of address request for a demat account should be processed:

  1. On a phone call from the client
  2. On written request with proof
  3. Only once in a lifetime
  4. Without any document
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. On written request with proof

Changes of address need a signed request with valid proof, often updated via KRA. This guards against fraudulent changes that could enable misuse.
