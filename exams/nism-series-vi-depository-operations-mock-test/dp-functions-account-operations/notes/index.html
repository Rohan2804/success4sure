DP Functions – Account Operations: NISM Series VI revision notes

Once an account is open, the DP handles a stream of instructions and changes. This chapter is practical and scenario-heavy: how delivery instruction slips are issued and checked, how a client can freeze an account, what transposition means, how accounts are closed and how often statements must be sent. Know the safeguard behind each rule.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Delivery instruction slips

A DIS is the client's written instruction to debit securities. DPs issue pre-numbered DIS booklets only on request, record the serial numbers against the account, and verify signatures and slip numbers before execution. Loose slips are given only with the account holder's personal presence or under strict controls, because blank slips are a classic fraud route.

2. Online instructions

Clients can submit debit instructions electronically through depository platforms such as NSDL's SPEED-e or CDSL's easiest, or the DP's app, with two-factor authentication. Sell transactions on exchanges now use the block mechanism and e-DIS or DDPI authorisation, so a client's holdings are debited only for valid obligations.

3. DDPI instead of POA

The Demat Debit and Pledge Instruction is a limited authorisation a client may give a broker, covering transfer for settlement of trades, pledges for margin, mutual fund transactions on exchange platforms and tendering in open offers. It replaced the broad power of attorney that earlier allowed misuse. DDPI is optional; clients can use e-DIS instead.

4. Freezing and unfreezing

A client can freeze an account for debits, credits or both, or freeze specific ISINs or quantities, for example while travelling. Regulators and courts can also order freezes, and accounts with incomplete KYC may be frozen by rule. Unfreezing requires a request from the same authority that froze it.

5. Change in details

Address, bank details and contact information can be changed on a signed request with supporting documents, after verification. Names can be changed after events such as marriage with proof. Changes are reflected in the client master and the client must be informed. A change of PAN is not normally permitted; PAN mismatch requires correction.

6. Transposition

Transposition changes the order of names in a joint holding when all holders are alive, for example moving shares from 'B and A' to an account in 'A and B'. Physical certificates can be transposed during dematerialisation with a transposition-cum-demat form. Transposition differs from transmission, which happens only on death.

7. Consolidation and closure

A client with multiple accounts in the same names and order can consolidate by transferring holdings, typically through an account closure request in which balances move to the target account. Closure with balances needs an instruction to transfer or rematerialise. Accounts with pending pledges, freezes or lock-in cannot be closed until these are cleared.

8. Statements

DPs must send a transaction statement in any month with a transaction and a holding statement periodically, now usually through the Consolidated Account Statement issued by depositories for investors with a PAN-mapped account. If there are no transactions, a holding statement is still sent periodically, half-yearly through the CAS for accounts without activity (check current depository rules). Statements can be electronic if the client agrees.

9. SMS and email alerts

Depositories send alerts directly to the registered mobile and email for every debit, pledge creation and certain other transactions. This lets the client catch unauthorised activity immediately. The client must keep contact details updated; using a broker's mobile number is not allowed.

Common traps in the exam

  • Confusing transposition with transmission. Transposition reorders living joint holders; transmission happens on a holder's death.
  • Assuming DDPI gives the broker unlimited control. It covers only specified purposes such as settlement and margin pledges.
  • Thinking DIS booklets are issued automatically with every account. They are issued on request, and their numbers are recorded against the account.
  • Believing an account can be closed while securities are pledged or frozen. These must be released first.

Quick recap

DIS booklets are pre-numbered, issued on request and checked before execution. DDPI is a limited authorisation that replaced broad POAs. Clients can freeze debits, credits or specific ISINs. Transposition changes the order of living joint holders. Closure requires transferring balances and clearing pledges. Statements go monthly when there are transactions, and periodically (through the CAS) otherwise.
