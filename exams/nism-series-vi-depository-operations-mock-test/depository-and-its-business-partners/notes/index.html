Depository and its Business Partners: NISM Series VI revision notes

A depository never deals with investors directly; it works through a network of partners. This chapter explains each partner's job and how the depository keeps watch over them. Questions are typically 'who does this?' — who opens accounts, who confirms demat requests, who credits allotments, who supervises DPs — so build a clear mental map of the flows.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Depository participant

A DP is the depository's agent and the investor's point of contact. It opens and maintains demat accounts, accepts instructions, sends statements and handles grievances. A DP must be registered with SEBI under the Depositories and Participants Regulations and admitted by the depository. One entity can be a DP of both NSDL and CDSL.

2. Who can be a DP

Eligible categories include banks, financial institutions, custodians, clearing corporations, stock brokers, NBFCs and RTAs meeting net worth criteria prescribed by SEBI. Stock brokers and NBFCs face higher net worth thresholds than banks because they are not otherwise prudentially regulated to the same degree. Figures change, so learn the category list rather than exact amounts.

3. Issuer and RTA

The issuer (the company) appoints a registrar and share transfer agent to maintain its records. The RTA is the depository's link to the issuer: it confirms dematerialisation and rematerialisation requests, executes corporate actions such as allotments and bonus credits, and reconciles the issuer's capital with the depository's records every quarter (reconciliation of share capital audit).

4. Tripartite agreement

Before its securities can be dematerialised, an issuer signs a tripartite agreement with the depository and its RTA. Once the agreement is in place and the security is admitted, investors can convert physical certificates or receive allotments directly in demat form. Listed public issues require this arrangement with both depositories.

5. Clearing corporation interface

The clearing corporation holds a pool or settlement account with each depository. On pay-in, securities move from the selling client's account (via block mechanism) to the clearing corporation; on pay-out, they move from the clearing corporation directly to the buying client's account. The depository simply executes these book entries on settlement instructions.

6. Banks and pledgees

Banks and NBFCs participate as pledgees when investors borrow against securities. They hold demat accounts and accept or invoke pledges. Banks also act as clearing banks and DPs. The depository records the pledge so the pledged securities cannot be transferred without the pledgee's consent.

7. Depository's supervision of DPs

Depositories inspect DPs periodically, require internal audits and concurrent audits of account opening and DIS controls, monitor complaints and can levy penalties or suspend DPs for violations. DPs must also submit compliance certificates and net worth certificates. SEBI in turn inspects the depositories.

8. Rights and obligations document

The relationship between the beneficial owner and DP is governed by a standard rights and obligations document prescribed by SEBI, given to the client at account opening. It sets out charges, communication of changes, statements, grievance redress and the DP's duties. Fee changes must be notified to clients in advance.

9. Information flow summary

Investor gives instruction to DP; DP enters it into the depository system; depository executes book entry; RTA confirms anything that changes issuer records; clearing corporation sends settlement obligations. Remembering this chain helps solve most 'who acts next' questions.

Common traps in the exam

  • Saying the investor deals directly with the depository. All requests go through the DP, which acts as the depository's agent.
  • Confusing the RTA's role with the DP's role in dematerialisation. The DP forwards the request; the RTA verifies certificates and confirms the credit.
  • Assuming a DP is registered only with the depository. SEBI registration is required, and admission by the depository is separate.
  • Forgetting that the pledgee, not the pledgor, accepts a pledge and can later invoke it.

Quick recap

Depository works through partners: DPs face investors, RTAs represent issuers, clearing corporations route settlement, banks act as pledgees and clearing banks. DPs need SEBI registration and depository admission, follow the rights and obligations document and are audited and inspected by the depository. Issuers join through a tripartite agreement.
