DP Functions – Transmission and Nomination: NISM Series VI practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on DP Functions – Transmission and Nomination, from the NISM Series VI (Depository Operations) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers what happens to demat holdings when a holder dies, and how nomination simplifies the process. Revise who can nominate and be nominated, multiple nominees and their shares, procedures when a nominee exists or does not, transmission in joint accounts, documents required, and the role of minors as nominees. Understand the difference between transmission and transposition, and why nomination is strongly encouraged for individual accounts.

DP Functions – Transmission and Nomination questions

Question 1

In a sole-holder account with two nominees sharing 60:40, the holder dies. The securities will be transmitted:

  1. Entirely to the first nominee, who then shares with the second
  2. To the legal heirs only
  3. To both nominees in the 60:40 ratio
  4. To the DP
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. To both nominees in the 60:40 ratio

When multiple nominees are named with specified shares, the securities are transmitted to each nominee in the stated proportion on submission of required documents.

Question 2

A sole holder dies without a nominee. To claim the securities, the legal heir must generally submit:

  1. Documents establishing succession
  2. Only a copy of the deceased's PAN card
  3. A new DIS signed by the heir
  4. A pledge closure form
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Documents establishing succession

Without a nominee, the claimant must show legal entitlement, through documents such as a succession certificate, probate or, within limits, affidavits and indemnity.

Question 3

Which of these can be a nominee for a demat account?

  1. A partnership firm registered under the Partnership Act
  2. An individual, including a minor
  3. A private limited company
  4. A Hindu Undivided Family
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. An individual, including a minor

Only individuals can be nominees. A minor can be nominated, with a guardian named. Bodies corporate, firms and HUFs cannot be nominees.

Question 4

In a joint demat account, the first holder dies. The securities will be:

  1. Transmitted to the first holder's nominee
  2. Frozen permanently
  3. Sold by the DP
  4. Transmitted to the surviving holders
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Transmitted to the surviving holders

On the death of one joint holder, holdings pass to the surviving holders. The nominee comes into the picture only after all joint holders have died.

Question 5

Transmission of securities differs from transposition because transmission:

  1. Occurs on the death of a holder
  2. Changes only the order of names
  3. Requires no documents
  4. Happens only for physical certificates
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Occurs on the death of a holder

Transmission transfers securities to nominees, survivors or heirs after a holder's death. Transposition is a change in the order of names among living joint holders.

Question 6

A client wants to change the nominee in her demat account. She can do this:

  1. Only once in the life of the account
  2. Any time by submitting a fresh form
  3. Only through a court order
  4. Only with the nominee's consent
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Any time by submitting a fresh form

Nomination can be changed or cancelled at any time by the account holder through a fresh nomination form or the online facility, without the nominee's consent.

Question 7

All joint holders of a demat account die together. The securities will be transmitted to:

  1. The DP
  2. The government
  3. The broker
  4. The nominee
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The nominee

The nominee is entitled after all joint holders have died. Joint holders nominate together, and the nominee claims with the death certificates.

Question 8

A minor nominee is entitled to securities after the holder's death. Until the minor turns 18, the account will be:

  1. Operated by the minor
  2. Closed
  3. Operated by the guardian
  4. Operated by the DP
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Operated by the guardian

The securities are credited to a demat account in the minor's name, which is operated by the guardian named in the nomination (or appointed) until the minor attains majority.
