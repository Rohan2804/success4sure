DP Functions – Transmission and Nomination: NISM Series VI revision notes

When an account holder dies, the securities must pass to the right person with minimum delay. This chapter covers how nomination works, how transmission is processed in different situations and the documents required. Exam questions often describe a family situation — a sole holder with a nominee, a joint account where one holder dies, a minor nominee — and ask what happens.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Who can nominate

Only individuals holding accounts singly or jointly can nominate. Non-individuals such as companies, HUFs, trusts, partnership firms and societies cannot. A guardian cannot nominate on behalf of a minor account holder. In joint accounts, all holders jointly make the nomination, and it applies only after the death of all joint holders.

2. Who can be a nominee

Only an individual can be a nominee, including a minor represented by a guardian. A trust, company, society, HUF or Karta in that capacity cannot be a nominee. An NRI can be a nominee subject to FEMA rules. Under SEBI's 2025 framework, up to 10 nominees can be named with percentage shares (check current rules).

3. Nominee's position

A nominee receives the securities on the holder's death, which gives the depository and DP a clean discharge. Whether the nominee is the ultimate owner or a trustee for legal heirs depends on succession law, and courts have generally treated the nominee as holding for heirs. Nomination is therefore a transfer mechanism, not a substitute for a will.

4. Transmission with a nominee

The nominee submits a transmission request form, a copy of the death certificate attested appropriately and their own KYC. The DP verifies documents and transfers the holdings to the nominee's demat account, which must be in the nominee's name. No legal heirship documents are needed when a valid nomination exists.

5. Joint account, one holder dies

If one of the joint holders dies, the securities are transmitted to the surviving holders by closing the account and moving balances to a new account in the survivors' names. The survivors submit the death certificate and a transmission request; nomination is not triggered because some holders remain alive.

6. Sole holder without a nominee

When there is no nomination, the claimant must prove entitlement. Depending on value, depositories prescribe documents such as succession certificate, probate or letter of administration, or for smaller values an affidavit, indemnity and no-objection from other legal heirs. Value thresholds for simplified documentation are set by SEBI and depositories.

7. Minor as nominee

A minor nominee is represented by a guardian, whose details are recorded at nomination. On transmission, securities are credited to the minor's demat account operated by the guardian. When the minor becomes major, the account must be updated with fresh KYC.

8. Changing or cancelling nomination

Nomination can be changed or cancelled any number of times by the holders while alive, through a fresh nomination form. The latest valid nomination prevails. DPs must record nominations promptly and confirm them to clients, since an unrecorded change can lead to disputes.

9. Transmission vs transposition

Transmission moves securities due to death of a holder, to nominees, survivors or legal heirs. Transposition reorders names among living holders. A common exam scenario mixes the two: if all holders are alive and only the order differs, it is transposition, not transmission.

Common traps in the exam

  • Allowing a company, trust or HUF to be a nominee. Only individuals can be nominees.
  • Assuming nomination in a joint account applies when the first holder dies. It operates only after all joint holders die; survivors take first.
  • Thinking a nominee needs a succession certificate. A valid nomination avoids those documents; they are needed when there is no nomination.
  • Treating a guardian of a minor account holder as able to nominate. A minor's account cannot have a nomination.

Quick recap

Only individuals nominate and only individuals (including minors via guardian) can be nominees. Joint accounts: survivors get the holdings; nominees come in only after all holders die. With a nominee, a death certificate and KYC suffice; without one, legal heirship documents apply based on value. Transmission is on death; transposition is reordering living holders.
