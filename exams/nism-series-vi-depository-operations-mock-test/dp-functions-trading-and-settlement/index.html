DP Functions – Trading and Settlement: NISM Series VI practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on DP Functions – Trading and Settlement, from the NISM Series VI (Depository Operations) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how depository accounts support settlement of trades on stock exchanges under the T+1 cycle: pay-in and pay-out of securities, early pay-in, direct pay-out to client accounts, the block mechanism for sell orders, off-market transfers and their reasons, and inter-depository transfers. Revise the settlement timeline, what happens on short delivery, the role of DDPI, and the purpose of the clearing member account. Expect scenario questions on dates and instructions.

DP Functions – Trading and Settlement questions

Question 1

Under T+1, shares bought on a Tuesday (no holidays) are credited to the buyer's demat account by:

  1. Wednesday
  2. Thursday
  3. Friday
  4. The next Monday
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Wednesday

Under T+1, settlement happens one working day after the trade. Pay-out on Wednesday credits securities to the buyer's demat account the same day.

Question 2

The block mechanism for sell orders means that:

  1. Shares are moved to the broker's own account before selling
  2. Shares are frozen permanently
  3. Shares are blocked in the seller's own account till settlement
  4. The DP sells the shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Shares are blocked in the seller's own account till settlement

Under the block mechanism, securities meant for delivery are blocked in the client's demat account, then debited directly at pay-in, avoiding transfer to the broker's account.

Question 3

An investor gifts shares to a relative through the depository. This is a(n):

  1. On-market trade through the exchange
  2. Inter-depository pledge
  3. Corporate action
  4. Off-market transfer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Off-market transfer

Gifts are transfers outside the exchange, so they are off-market. The reason must be stated in the instruction, as depositories require.

Question 4

A transfer from a CDSL account to an NSDL account of the same investor is called:

  1. A rematerialisation of holdings
  2. An inter-depository transfer
  3. A transmission
  4. A pledge
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. An inter-depository transfer

Transfers between accounts in different depositories are inter-depository transfers, routed through a link between NSDL and CDSL.

Question 5

A client gives DDPI to the broker. This authorises the broker to:

  1. Withdraw the client's bank balance
  2. Change the client's nominee
  3. Debit securities for settlement and margin pledges
  4. Close the demat account
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Debit securities for settlement and margin pledges

DDPI is limited to specified purposes such as settlement of trades, margin pledges and certain transactions, unlike the broader power of attorney used earlier.

Question 6

A seller fails to deliver shares at pay-in. The clearing corporation will usually:

  1. Cancel all trades of the day
  2. Ask SEBI to deliver
  3. Freeze the buyer's account
  4. Conduct an auction
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Conduct an auction

Short delivery is handled through an auction to buy the shares for the buyer; if the auction fails, the trade is closed out at a set price.

Question 7

Early pay-in of securities by a seller is useful because:

  1. It gets the seller a better sale price than the market
  2. It reduces the seller's margin requirement
  3. It postpones settlement
  4. It cancels the trade
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. It reduces the seller's margin requirement

When a seller delivers securities before the settlement deadline, margin exposure on the sale falls, so the clearing corporation gives margin benefit.

Question 8

Pay-out of securities to a buying client under current norms is made:

  1. Directly to the client's demat account
  2. Always to the broker's pool account first
  3. To the RTA
  4. To the issuer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Directly to the client's demat account

SEBI requires clearing corporations to pay out securities directly to clients' demat accounts, reducing the risk of misuse by brokers.
