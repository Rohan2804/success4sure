DP Functions – Trading and Settlement: NISM Series VI revision notes

This chapter links the depository to the stock exchange. You must understand what happens in the demat account between the day a client sells or buys and the day settlement completes under T+1. Questions test timelines, the block mechanism for sell orders, direct pay-out, off-market transfer rules and inter-depository transfers, usually through short practical scenarios.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. T+1 cycle

Equity trades on Indian exchanges settle on T+1: a trade on Monday settles on Tuesday, skipping weekends and settlement holidays. A trade on Friday settles on Monday. An optional same-day (T+0) settlement has also been introduced for a limited set of stocks; the rolling T+1 cycle remains the default for most trades.

2. Block mechanism for sales

When a client sells, the shares are blocked in the client's own demat account in favour of the clearing corporation, either through e-DIS (validated by the client) or through DDPI. On the pay-in day, the blocked shares are debited directly to the clearing corporation. If the trade is not executed, the block is released. This prevents misuse of client securities in a broker pool account.

3. Pay-in and pay-out

Pay-in is when sellers deliver securities and buyers deliver funds to the clearing corporation. Pay-out is when the clearing corporation delivers securities to buyers and funds to sellers. Under direct pay-out, securities are credited straight to the buying client's demat account instead of passing through the broker's pool.

4. Early pay-in

A seller can deliver securities to the clearing corporation before the normal pay-in, usually on trade day itself. Early pay-in reduces risk for the clearing corporation, so margins on that sale are exempted. With block mechanism, early pay-in is largely automatic.

5. Unpaid securities

If a buying client has not paid in full, the broker may hold the securities in a client unpaid securities pledgee account and later pledge them as margin trading collateral or sell them after the prescribed period. The broker cannot simply keep client securities in its own account indefinitely.

6. Off-market transfers

Transfers between two demat accounts that do not go through an exchange are off-market. The client must mention a reason code such as gift, sale outside exchange or transfer between own accounts, and stamp duty may apply. Off-market sale consideration must be disclosed, and DPs watch high-value or unusual transfers for suspicious activity.

7. Inter-depository transfers

Securities can be moved between an NSDL account and a CDSL account through an inter-depository transfer instruction. The two depositories are linked electronically, so the credit happens on the same day. The client fills the target depository's DP ID and client ID correctly; mistakes are hard to reverse.

8. Short delivery

If a seller fails to deliver, the clearing corporation conducts an auction to buy the shares for the buyer. If the auction fails, the trade is closed out at a price set by rules, generally the higher of the highest price in the period or a premium over the closing price. The defaulting seller bears the cost.

9. Clearing member accounts

Clearing members hold pool and settlement accounts with depositories to handle settlement flows. Client securities must not be mixed with the broker's own holdings. Since the block mechanism and direct pay-out, client shares spend very little time outside the client's own account.

Common traps in the exam

  • Counting calendar days instead of trading days for T+1. Saturdays, Sundays and settlement holidays are skipped.
  • Assuming the broker's pool account receives client sale shares days in advance. Under block mechanism shares stay in the client's account until pay-in.
  • Treating an off-market transfer as needing no reason. A reason code is mandatory and stamp duty may apply.
  • Believing early pay-in increases margin. It reduces risk, so margin on that position is exempted.

Quick recap

T+1 is the default cycle. Sales use block mechanism via e-DIS or DDPI; securities are debited at pay-in. Buyers get direct pay-out to their own accounts. Early pay-in exempts margins. Off-market transfers need a reason code and may attract stamp duty. Short deliveries go to auction, then close-out at the defaulter's cost.
