Introduction to Depository: NISM Series VI practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Introduction to Depository, from the NISM Series VI (Depository Operations) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains what a depository is and why it matters. Revise the two depositories (NSDL and CDSL), the legal framework (Depositories Act, 1996 and SEBI Depositories and Participants Regulations), the concepts of registered owner and beneficial owner, fungibility, ISIN, and the benefits of holding securities in demat form compared with physical certificates. Understand how a depository is similar to and different from a bank, and which securities can be held in a demat account.

Introduction to Depository questions

Question 1

In the depository system, the depository is shown in the issuer's records as the:

  1. Beneficial owner entitled to all voting rights and dividends
  2. Pledgee
  3. Registered owner
  4. Nominee
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Registered owner

The depository is the registered owner of dematerialised securities for transfer purposes, while the investor is the beneficial owner, entitled to all rights and benefits.

Question 2

The two depositories operating in India are:

  1. NSDL and CDSL
  2. NSE and BSE
  3. CCIL and NSCCL
  4. CAMS and KFintech
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. NSDL and CDSL

National Securities Depository Ltd and Central Depository Services (India) Ltd are the two SEBI-registered depositories. NSE and BSE are stock exchanges, and CAMS and KFintech are RTAs.

Question 3

Securities held in a depository are fungible. This means:

  1. Each share keeps its own distinctive number that the investor can trace
  2. Units of the same ISIN are interchangeable
  3. They cannot be transferred
  4. They carry different dividends
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Units of the same ISIN are interchangeable

Dematerialised securities lose distinctive numbers. Any unit of the same ISIN is identical to any other, which simplifies transfer and settlement.

Question 4

An ISIN is a code that:

  1. Uniquely identifies an investor across all depositories and banks
  2. Identifies a stock broker
  3. Identifies a bank branch
  4. Uniquely identifies a security
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Uniquely identifies a security

The International Securities Identification Number is a 12-character code that identifies a specific security. Separate ISINs are used for, say, fully paid and partly paid shares of the same company.

Question 5

Which is NOT a benefit of holding securities in demat form?

  1. Assured protection against falls in market price
  2. Elimination of risks like loss, theft and forgery
  3. Faster settlement of trades
  4. Automatic credit of bonus shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Assured protection against falls in market price

Demat removes risks linked to physical paper and speeds up settlement and corporate actions. It does not protect the investor from market price declines.

Question 6

A depository is similar to a bank mainly because both:

  1. Pay interest on the balances held in their accounts every quarter
  2. Hold assets in accounts and transfer them on instruction
  3. Lend money to account holders
  4. Are regulated by RBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Hold assets in accounts and transfer them on instruction

A bank holds money and a depository holds securities, each in accounts and each transferring balances on the holder's instruction. Depositories do not pay interest or lend.

Question 7

Which of these laws provides the legal basis for depositories in India?

  1. Banking Regulation Act, 1949
  2. Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007
  3. Indian Partnership Act, 1932
  4. Depositories Act, 1996
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Depositories Act, 1996

The Depositories Act, 1996 enabled electronic holding and transfer of securities and recognised the depository as registered owner, with investors as beneficial owners.

Question 8

An investor asks her DP which of her papers she can convert into electronic holdings. Which one qualifies?

  1. The title deed of her residential flat
  2. A bank fixed deposit receipt
  3. Units of a mutual fund
  4. A life insurance premium receipt
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Units of a mutual fund

Securities such as shares, bonds, mutual fund units and government securities can be held in demat form. Property deeds and FD receipts are not securities.
