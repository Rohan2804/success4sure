Introduction to Depository: NISM Series VI revision notes

This chapter answers a simple question: what exactly does a depository do and why did India need one? Exam questions test the legal framework, the two depositories, the difference between registered and beneficial ownership, ISIN and fungibility, and the comparison with a bank. Learn the vocabulary precisely, because options often differ by a single word.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Definition

A depository is an organisation that holds securities of investors in electronic form, at the investor's request, through registered depository participants. It also transfers ownership by book entry. The analogy is a bank: a bank holds money and transfers it between accounts; a depository holds securities and transfers them, without any physical movement of paper.

2. The two depositories

India has two depositories: National Securities Depository Ltd (NSDL), set up in 1996 and promoted by IDBI, UTI and NSE, and Central Depository Services (India) Ltd (CDSL), promoted by BSE and banks and operational from 1999. Investors can hold accounts with either through any DP; inter-depository transfers are seamless.

3. Legal framework

The Depositories Act, 1996 provides the legal basis, read with the SEBI (Depositories and Participants) Regulations, now the 2018 version, and each depository's bye-laws and business rules. The Act made ownership transfers by book entry legal and removed stamp duty on demat transfers at that time. Depositories must be registered with SEBI.

4. Registered owner vs beneficial owner

For dematerialised securities, the depository is shown as the registered owner in the issuer's register of members, but only to effect transfers. The investor whose name appears in the depository's records is the beneficial owner and enjoys all rights: voting, dividends, bonus and rights entitlements. Exam options that give voting rights to the depository are wrong.

5. ISIN

Each security admitted to a depository has a 12-character International Securities Identification Number, such as INE002A01018. In India, ISINs start with IN; the next character indicates the issuer type (E for companies, F for mutual funds). Different types of securities of the same company, such as partly paid shares, carry different ISINs.

6. Fungibility

Securities in demat form are fungible: they have no distinctive numbers or certificate numbers, so any 100 shares of a company with the same ISIN are identical. This is why the depository can settle trades by changing balances without tracking specific certificates, and why distinctive numbers disappear after dematerialisation.

7. Benefits over physical holding

Demat eliminates risks of loss, theft, mutilation, forgery and bad delivery; removes odd-lot problems; speeds up transfers and credits of corporate actions; and allows pledging with a few instructions. Investors can hold all securities in one account and get a single consolidated account statement. Costs such as courier and handling also fall.

8. Depository vs bank

Both hold assets in accounts and transfer by instruction, but a depository cannot use, lend or pay interest on the securities it holds; it has no claim on them. A bank lends deposits and pays interest. Depository accounts can never be overdrawn, while banks permit overdrafts. Securities balances are held in trust for the investor.

9. What can be held in demat

Equity and preference shares, debentures and bonds, government securities, mutual fund units, ETFs, commercial paper, certificates of deposit, REIT and InvIT units, sovereign gold bonds and warrants can all be held in demat form. Listed companies' shares must be in demat form for transfer, so physical shares need to be dematerialised before they can be sold on an exchange.

Common traps in the exam

  • Treating the depository as the beneficial owner. It is only the registered owner for transfer purposes; all economic and voting rights stay with the investor.
  • Assuming one ISIN per company. Each distinct class (fully paid, partly paid, different debenture series) gets its own ISIN.
  • Believing a depository can lend or invest client securities like a bank lends deposits. It merely records and transfers them.
  • Mixing up promoters: NSDL is associated with NSE, IDBI and UTI; CDSL with BSE.

Quick recap

Depository = electronic custodian of securities, governed by the Depositories Act 1996 and SEBI regulations. Two depositories: NSDL and CDSL. Depository is registered owner, investor is beneficial owner with all rights. ISIN is the 12-character identifier; demat securities are fungible. Unlike a bank, a depository cannot lend or use holdings.
