DP Functions – Dematerialisation: NISM Series VI revision notes

Dematerialisation converts physical certificates into electronic balances; rematerialisation does the reverse. The exam tests the sequence of steps, which party performs each step and the common reasons requests get rejected. Learn the process as a flow chart, because many questions describe a step and ask who acts next or what goes wrong.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Starting a demat request

The client fills a Dematerialisation Request Form (DRF) and hands it to the DP with the physical certificates. The holder's names and their order on the certificates must match the demat account exactly. Separate DRFs are needed for different ISINs, for locked-in and free securities, and for fully and partly paid shares.

2. Defacing the certificates

Before forwarding, the DP or client marks each certificate 'Surrendered for Dematerialisation' so that it cannot be misused if lost in transit. The DP checks the certificates, the DRF and the signatures, then generates a demat request number in the depository system.

3. Electronic and physical legs

The DP sends the electronic request through the depository system and dispatches the physical DRF and certificates to the issuer's RTA. Both legs must reach the RTA; the electronic request alone cannot be confirmed without the certificates.

4. RTA verification

The RTA verifies that the certificates are genuine, that the holder's name matches the register, that there are no stop-transfer orders and that the distinctive numbers are valid. On approval, it confirms the request electronically and the depository credits the holder's demat account. The RTA then updates the register to show the depository as registered owner.

5. Rejection reasons

Common reasons are signature mismatch, name or order mismatch, certificates reported lost or under court order, fake certificates, wrong ISIN or a mismatch between the DRF and certificates. On rejection, the certificates are returned so the client can correct the problem and resubmit.

6. Locked-in and partly paid shares

Locked-in shares can be dematerialised, but they are credited under a lock-in status with the lock-in release date and cannot be transferred until then. Partly paid shares carry a separate ISIN, so they cannot be clubbed with fully paid shares on the same DRF.

7. Rematerialisation

A client wanting physical certificates submits a Remat Request Form to the DP. The DP blocks the quantity and forwards the request electronically; the RTA prints the certificates and dispatches them to the client, after which the depository debits the balance. Since transfers of listed shares require demat form, remat is uncommon.

8. Timelines

SEBI and depositories prescribe tight timelines for each leg: the DP must forward documents promptly and the RTA must process within the prescribed period, typically measured in days. Delays are monitored through pending demat request reports and are a frequent grievance category.

9. Transmission-cum-demat

Where a physical holder has died, the claimant can apply for transmission and dematerialisation in one step using a transmission-cum-demat form, saving the step of first getting physical transmission done. Similarly, a transposition-cum-demat form handles a different order of names.

Common traps in the exam

  • Thinking the DP confirms the demat credit. Only the RTA can confirm after verifying the certificates.
  • Clubbing locked-in and free shares, or fully and partly paid shares, in one DRF. Separate forms are needed because they are distinct ISINs or statuses.
  • Believing certificates need not be defaced. Defacing is done before dispatch to prevent misuse.
  • Assuming a name order mismatch can be ignored. Certificates in 'B and A' cannot be credited to an account in 'A and B' without transposition.

Quick recap

Demat flow: client submits DRF and defaced certificates to DP, DP sends electronic request and physical documents to RTA, RTA verifies and confirms, depository credits the account. Names and order must match. Locked-in and partly paid shares go on separate forms. Remat reverses the flow with the RTA printing certificates.
