DP Functions – Dematerialisation: NISM Series VI practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on DP Functions – Dematerialisation, from the NISM Series VI (Depository Operations) syllabus.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how physical certificates are converted to electronic form and back. Revise the dematerialisation process (DRF submission, defacing, DP checks, request to depository, RTA verification and confirmation), common rejection reasons, treatment of locked-in and partly paid shares, rematerialisation, and timelines. Understand the role of each party and why names on certificates must match the demat account. Questions often describe a step and ask who acts next.

DP Functions – Dematerialisation questions

Question 1

The form an investor submits to the DP along with physical certificates for demat is the:

  1. Delivery Instruction Slip with the certificate numbers
  2. Remat Request Form
  3. Pledge Request Form
  4. Dematerialisation Request Form (DRF)
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Dematerialisation Request Form (DRF)

The DRF, along with defaced certificates, starts the demat process. A DIS is used for transfers; an RRF for rematerialisation.

Question 2

Before sending physical certificates to the RTA for demat, they are marked:

  1. 'Cancelled by order of SEBI'
  2. 'Surrendered for Dematerialisation'
  3. 'Duplicate certificate'
  4. 'Pledged'
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 'Surrendered for Dematerialisation'

Certificates are defaced with this endorsement so they cannot be misused or traded in physical form while the demat request is processed.

Question 3

Physical shares in the names 'X and Y' can be dematerialised into:

  1. X's sole account only
  2. Any account opened by Y
  3. A joint account in the names X and Y in the same order
  4. The broker's account
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A joint account in the names X and Y in the same order

Names and their order on certificates must match the demat account. If the order differs, transposition-cum-demat is used.

Question 4

Partly paid-up shares of a company in demat form:

  1. Have a separate ISIN
  2. Share the ISIN of fully paid shares
  3. Cannot be dematerialised
  4. Are converted to fully paid on demat
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Have a separate ISIN

Partly paid shares differ in rights and liability, so they are given a separate ISIN. They merge into the fully paid ISIN after calls are paid.

Question 5

After the RTA verifies a demat request and confirms it, the depository:

  1. Issues new physical certificates
  2. Credits the investor's demat account
  3. Sends a refund to the investor
  4. Locks the shares permanently
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Credits the investor's demat account

Once the RTA confirms, the depository credits the electronic balance to the investor's account, and the physical certificates are cancelled.

Question 6

An investor wants physical certificates back for shares held in demat. This process is:

  1. Transposition
  2. Dematerialisation
  3. Rematerialisation
  4. Transmission
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Rematerialisation

Remat converts electronic holdings into physical certificates, which the RTA prints and dispatches after the depository debits the account.

Question 7

A demat request is rejected because the certificates were reported stolen. The RTA will:

  1. Reject it and follow the issuer's procedure
  2. Credit the shares anyway
  3. Issue duplicate certificates to the DP
  4. Transfer them to the broker
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Reject it and follow the issuer's procedure

Reported stolen or fake certificates are rejected, and the RTA follows the issuer's and regulatory procedures, as such securities cannot enter the depository.

Question 8

Locked-in shares that are dematerialised will:

  1. Become freely tradable from the date of credit
  2. Be sold automatically
  3. Lose voting rights
  4. Remain locked-in until expiry
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Remain locked-in until expiry

Lock-in status is carried into the demat account with the lock-in expiry date; the shares become free only after the lock-in period ends.
