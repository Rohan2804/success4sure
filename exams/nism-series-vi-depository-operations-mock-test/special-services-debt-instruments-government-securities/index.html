Special Services – Debt Instruments & Government Securities: NISM Series VI practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Special Services – Debt Instruments & Government Securities, from the NISM Series VI (Depository Operations) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers debt securities in the depository system: corporate bonds and debentures, commercial paper, certificates of deposit and government securities. Revise which instruments must be issued in demat form, how interest and redemption are processed, how retail investors hold G-secs through depositories and the RBI Retail Direct scheme, and features of T-bills and dated securities. Understand that redemption of debt is processed as a corporate action that debits securities and pays money.

Special Services – Debt Instruments & Government Securities questions

Question 1

Retail investors can buy government securities directly from RBI's primary auctions through:

  1. SCORES
  2. MF Utility
  3. BSE StAR MF
  4. RBI Retail Direct
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. RBI Retail Direct

RBI Retail Direct lets individuals open a gilt securities account with RBI to invest in G-secs directly in primary auctions and secondary trading.

Question 2

When a bond is redeemed at maturity, the depository system:

  1. Converts them to equity shares
  2. Debits the bonds and pays proceeds to the bank
  3. Leaves them in the account
  4. Transfers them to the RTA's account for resale
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Debits the bonds and pays proceeds to the bank

Redemption is a corporate action: bonds are extinguished from the holder's demat account and the principal is paid to the linked bank account.

Question 3

Which instrument is issued by state governments?

  1. Treasury bill
  2. Cash Management Bill
  3. State Development Loan
  4. Commercial paper
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. State Development Loan

SDLs are bonds issued by state governments through RBI. T-bills and CMBs are central government instruments, and CPs are issued by companies.

Question 4

Interest on a corporate bond held in demat form is paid to:

  1. The beneficial owner on record date
  2. Whoever bought the bond first
  3. The DP
  4. The stock exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The beneficial owner on record date

The issuer pays interest to beneficial owners as per the depository's list on the record date, crediting their bank accounts.

Question 5

Which statement about T-bills is correct?

  1. Pay coupons half-yearly
  2. Issued at a discount, no coupon
  3. Issued by companies
  4. Have a 10-year tenure
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Issued at a discount, no coupon

T-bills are short-term central government securities issued at a discount and redeemed at face value; the difference is the investor's return.

Question 6

A Certificate of Deposit can be issued by:

  1. Retail investors
  2. Mutual funds
  3. Scheduled commercial banks
  4. Stock brokers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Scheduled commercial banks

CDs are money market instruments issued by banks and select financial institutions, and must be issued in demat form.

Question 7

Government securities held by retail investors in a demat account are kept in RBI's books through:

  1. A Constituent SGL account
  2. The investor's own SGL account with SEBI
  3. The company's RTA
  4. The broker's pool account
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A Constituent SGL account

Depositories hold G-secs for their clients in a Constituent Subsidiary General Ledger account with RBI, while investors see holdings in their demat accounts.

Question 8

Which of these debt instruments is a zero-coupon instrument by design?

  1. A 7-year corporate bond paying 8%
  2. A floating-rate G-sec
  3. A perpetual bond
  4. Commercial paper
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Commercial paper

Commercial paper is issued at a discount and redeemed at face value, with no periodic coupon. The others pay interest periodically.
