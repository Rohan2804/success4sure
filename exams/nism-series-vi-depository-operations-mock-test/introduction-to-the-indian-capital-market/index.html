Introduction to the Indian Capital Market: NISM Series VI practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Introduction to the Indian Capital Market, from the NISM Series VI (Depository Operations) syllabus.

What this chapter covers

This chapter sets the scene for depository operations: the structure of India's capital market, the difference between primary and secondary markets, the main market infrastructure institutions (stock exchanges, clearing corporations, depositories), the role of SEBI, and the main intermediaries. Revise how money is raised through public issues using ASBA and UPI, the instruments traded (equity, debt, hybrid), and why depositories are central to settlement. Questions are mostly conceptual and test who does what.

Introduction to the Indian Capital Market questions

Question 1

Under ASBA, the application money of an IPO investor is:

  1. Transferred immediately to the issuer's escrow account and refunded later if shares are not allotted
  2. Blocked in the investor's bank account until allotment
  3. Paid in cash to the broker
  4. Debited from the demat account
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Blocked in the investor's bank account until allotment

ASBA (Application Supported by Blocked Amount) blocks funds in the investor's account. Only the allotted amount is debited; the rest is unblocked, so investors do not lose interest on refunds.

Question 2

Which institution regulates the Indian securities market, including depositories and DPs?

  1. Reserve Bank of India
  2. Ministry of Corporate Affairs
  3. NSDL
  4. SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. SEBI

SEBI regulates the securities market under the SEBI Act, 1992, including market infrastructure institutions like depositories and intermediaries like DPs.

Question 3

Which of the following is a market infrastructure institution (MII)?

  1. A clearing corporation
  2. A stock broker registered with SEBI
  3. A mutual fund distributor
  4. A portfolio manager
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A clearing corporation

MIIs are the core systemic institutions of the market: stock exchanges, clearing corporations and depositories. Brokers, distributors and portfolio managers are intermediaries.

Question 4

A company issues new shares to the public for the first time. This takes place in the:

  1. Secondary market
  2. Money market
  3. Primary market
  4. Derivatives market
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Primary market

The primary market is where securities are first issued to raise capital. Subsequent trading between investors happens in the secondary market.

Question 5

A retail investor bidding in an IPO through UPI can block funds using:

  1. A physical cheque handed to the registrar with the application form and a copy of PAN
  2. A demand draft
  3. Cash at the exchange
  4. A UPI mandate approved in the UPI app
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A UPI mandate approved in the UPI app

Retail investors can apply in public issues through brokers or apps using a UPI ID. They approve a mandate that blocks funds in their bank account until allotment.

Question 6

Which statement about the secondary market is correct?

  1. It provides liquidity to investors
  2. It allows companies to raise fresh capital each time their shares are traded between investors
  3. It deals only in government securities
  4. It operates without a regulator
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. It provides liquidity to investors

The secondary market lets investors buy and sell existing securities, giving liquidity and price discovery. Companies do not receive money from these trades.

Question 7

Since April 2019, a transfer of shares of a listed company, other than transmission or transposition, can be done only if the shares are:

  1. Held in physical certificates registered with the company's RTA and stamped by a notary
  2. Pledged with a bank
  3. In dematerialised form
  4. Held by a promoter
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. In dematerialised form

SEBI mandated that transfers of listed securities be processed only in demat form (transmission and transposition were exempted), pushing investors to dematerialise holdings.

Question 8

Which type of instrument gives the holder a fixed claim on interest and principal rather than ownership?

  1. An equity share
  2. A debenture
  3. A rights entitlement on equity shares
  4. A sweat equity share
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A debenture

Debentures and bonds are debt instruments: the issuer owes interest and principal. Equity shares and their rights entitlements represent ownership.
