Free NISM Series XIX-B practice questions

Question 1 · Overview of Alternative Investments

Compared with a long-only equity mutual fund, a typical hedge fund strategy is more likely to:

  1. Be restricted to large-cap stocks
  2. Offer daily liquidity with no lock-in
  3. Use short selling, derivatives and leverage
  4. Be sold to retail investors with ₹500 SIPs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Use short selling, derivatives and leverage

Hedge strategies rely on flexible tools like shorting, derivatives and leverage that mutual funds use only in limited ways.

Question 2 · Growth of AIFs in India and Suitability of Category III AIFs

Category III AIFs, unlike Category I and II AIFs, may be structured as:

  1. Only close-ended
  2. Only interval schemes
  3. Only exchange-traded funds
  4. Open-ended or close-ended
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Open-ended or close-ended

Category I and II AIFs must be close-ended, while Category III may be either.

Question 3 · Introduction to Category III AIF Ecosystem

Which entity is responsible for investment decisions and day-to-day management of a Category III AIF?

  1. The trustee
  2. The distributor
  3. The investment manager
  4. The valuer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The investment manager

The manager runs the portfolio within the PPM; the trustee oversees, and others provide services.

Question 4 · Category III AIF: Fund Structures and Service Providers

Why must a Category III AIF appoint a custodian?

  1. To guarantee investor returns
  2. To safekeep the fund's securities independently of the manager
  3. To approve investment decisions
  4. To act as the fund's distributor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. To safekeep the fund's securities independently of the manager

Independent custody reduces the risk of misuse of assets; SEBI requires AIFs to appoint a custodian.

Question 5 · Regulatory Framework

A Category III AIF has NAV of ₹150 crore. What is the maximum gross exposure it can take under the leverage cap?

  1. ₹150 crore
  2. ₹300 crore
  3. ₹225 crore
  4. ₹450 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹300 crore

Maximum gross exposure = 2 × NAV = 2 × ₹150 crore = ₹300 crore.

Question 6 · Fees Structure, Fund Performance and Benchmarking

A Category III fund's NAV rose from ₹100 to ₹120 in a year. The performance fee is 20% of gains above the high-water mark of ₹100, with no hurdle. Fee per unit is:

  1. ₹20
  2. ₹4
  3. ₹2
  4. ₹24
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹4

Gain above HWM = 120 − 100 = ₹20; 20% × ₹20 = ₹4 per unit.

Question 7 · Category III AIF Investment Strategies and Due Diligence Process

A fund buys shares in the cash market and simultaneously sells the same quantity in futures to capture the price difference. This is:

  1. Cash-futures arbitrage
  2. Directional long-only investing
  3. Global macro trading
  4. Venture debt
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Cash-futures arbitrage

Buying spot and selling futures locks in the basis, earning a near-risk-free spread if held to expiry.

Question 8 · Valuation

The NAV per unit of a Category III AIF with total assets of ₹105 crore, liabilities of ₹5 crore and 1 crore units outstanding is:

  1. ₹105
  2. ₹100
  3. ₹110
  4. ₹95
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹100

NAV = (105 − 5) / 1 crore units = ₹100 per unit.

Question 9 · Taxation

How is a Category III AIF set up as a trust generally taxed in India?

  1. All income passes through to investors
  2. It is fully tax exempt
  3. It does not get pass-through status; income is generally taxed at the fund level
  4. Only the distributor pays tax
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. It does not get pass-through status; income is generally taxed at the fund level

Statutory pass-through is limited to Category I and II, so Category III income is taxed at the trust level.

Question 10 · Good Practices

A distributor promises a client that a Category III long-short fund 'cannot lose money because it is hedged'. This is:

  1. Mis-selling, because hedged strategies can still lose money
  2. Acceptable, since hedging removes all risk
  3. Acceptable if the client is wealthy
  4. Required disclosure
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Mis-selling, because hedged strategies can still lose money

No strategy is risk-free; implying guaranteed outcomes is misleading.

Question 11 · Overview of Alternative Investments

Which statement about the risk of alternative investments is correct?

  1. Alternatives always have lower risk than equities
  2. Alternative assets cannot lose capital
  3. Risk in alternatives is measured only by credit rating
  4. Reported volatility may understate true risk when assets are illiquid or infrequently priced
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Reported volatility may understate true risk when assets are illiquid or infrequently priced

Smoothed pricing of illiquid holdings can make returns look less volatile than economic reality.

Question 12 · Growth of AIFs in India and Suitability of Category III AIFs

Which investor is generally most suitable for a leveraged long-short Category III AIF?

  1. A first-time investor seeking capital protection
  2. A retiree depending on the investment for monthly expenses
  3. An investor who needs the money within three months
  4. A financially sophisticated investor with a high risk appetite and investable surplus well beyond the minimum ticket
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A financially sophisticated investor with a high risk appetite and investable surplus well beyond the minimum ticket

Leverage and complex strategies suit investors who understand them and can absorb losses without affecting their essential needs.

Question 13 · Introduction to Category III AIF Ecosystem

A prime broker typically provides a Category III AIF with:

  1. Distribution of fund units to retail investors
  2. Statutory audit of accounts
  3. Securities lending, financing, trade clearing and custody-related services
  4. Registration of the fund with SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Securities lending, financing, trade clearing and custody-related services

Prime brokers support hedge-fund strategies with leverage, stock borrowing and consolidated clearing.

Question 14 · Category III AIF: Fund Structures and Service Providers

The fund accountant or fund administrator of a Category III AIF is mainly responsible for:

  1. Sourcing new investors
  2. Choosing the fund's benchmark index
  3. Approving investment committee decisions
  4. Maintaining books of accounts and computing NAV
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Maintaining books of accounts and computing NAV

Fund administration covers accounting, NAV computation and investor records.

Question 15 · Regulatory Framework

The sponsor or manager of a Category III AIF must maintain a continuing interest of:

  1. The lower of 2.5% of the corpus or ₹5 crore
  2. Exactly 1% of the corpus
  3. Nothing, as Category III is exempt
  4. The lower of 5% of the corpus or ₹10 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The lower of 5% of the corpus or ₹10 crore

Category III has a higher skin-in-the-game requirement than Category I and II.
