Growth of AIFs in India and Suitability of Category III AIFs: NISM Series XIX-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Growth of AIFs in India and Suitability of Category III AIFs, from the NISM Series XIX-B (Alternative Investment Funds (Category III) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter traces the growth of the AIF industry in India and the position of Category III AIFs within it. It covers drivers of growth such as rising HNI and family-office wealth, the search for differentiated strategies and the regulatory framework introduced in 2012. It then explains how to judge whether a Category III fund suits an investor: risk profile, investable surplus, liquidity needs, understanding of complex strategies, time horizon and portfolio concentration. It also compares Category III AIFs with PMS and mutual funds. Focus on suitability scenarios and product comparisons.

Growth of AIFs in India and Suitability of Category III AIFs questions

Question 1

A client wants a liquid, low-cost diversified equity product with no minimum ticket. Compared with a Category III AIF, the more suitable choice is:

  1. A leveraged long-short Category III AIF
  2. A Category II private equity AIF
  3. An angel fund
  4. An equity mutual fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. An equity mutual fund

Mutual funds offer liquidity, low minimums and diversification. Category III AIFs require ₹1 crore (except accredited investors) and use complex strategies.

Question 2

Which factor is MOST important in assessing a client's suitability for a Category III AIF?

  1. The client's preferred bank branch
  2. Understanding of complex strategies and ability to bear losses
  3. Whether the client already holds a credit card issued by the sponsor's bank
  4. The client's city of residence
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Understanding of complex strategies and ability to bear losses

Category III AIFs may use leverage, derivatives and short selling; investors must understand these and be able to absorb losses. Banking preferences are irrelevant.

Question 3

Compared with a PMS, a key difference of a Category III AIF is that:

  1. Investors hold units of a pooled fund
  2. Investors hold securities directly in their own demat accounts
  3. There is no minimum investment
  4. It cannot use derivatives
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Investors hold units of a pooled fund

In an AIF, investors own units of a pooled vehicle, while in a PMS the securities are held in the client's own name. Category III AIFs can use derivatives.

Question 4

An investor's total investable portfolio is ₹20 crore and she has a high risk appetite. Investing ₹2 crore in a Category III AIF would most likely be viewed as:

  1. Unsuitable, because she exceeds the minimum ticket
  2. Prohibited, as AIF investments must exceed 50% of wealth
  3. Unsuitable, since high-risk investors cannot invest in AIFs
  4. A reasonable satellite allocation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A reasonable satellite allocation

A 10% allocation for an experienced, high-risk investor with ample wealth is a reasonable satellite position. There is no rule requiring a minimum share of wealth.

Question 5

Which development has supported the growth of Category III AIFs in India?

  1. A ban on equity mutual funds for HNIs
  2. More HNIs seeking differentiated strategies
  3. Guaranteed returns offered by Category III funds
  4. Exemption of Category III AIFs from SEBI regulation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. More HNIs seeking differentiated strategies

Growing wealth and demand for strategies beyond long-only funds have driven growth. Category III AIFs are SEBI-regulated and do not guarantee returns.

Question 6

A client needs to withdraw her money at short notice for a property purchase next month. A close-ended Category III AIF is:

  1. Unsuitable, as money may be locked in
  2. Ideal, as Category III funds offer daily redemption by law
  3. Suitable only if the fund is long-only
  4. Suitable because AIFs guarantee capital
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Unsuitable, as money may be locked in

Close-ended funds do not allow early redemption, and even open-ended ones may have notice periods. Short-term liquidity needs are best met elsewhere.

Question 7

Accredited investors may invest in Category III AIFs below the usual minimum. Accreditation primarily reflects the investor's:

  1. Years of experience with the same bank
  2. Age above 60
  3. Income or net worth meeting SEBI's criteria
  4. Possession of a NISM certificate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Income or net worth meeting SEBI's criteria

Accreditation is based on income, net worth or financial assets as set by SEBI, verified by an accreditation agency. Age and certifications are not criteria.

Question 8

A client invests in a Category III fund because last year it returned 45%. The distributor should highlight that:

  1. Past returns may not repeat and risks matter
  2. A 45% return guarantees similar results next year
  3. SEBI caps losses of Category III funds
  4. High returns mean the fund is risk-free
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Past returns may not repeat and risks matter

Past performance is not indicative of future results, especially for leveraged or concentrated strategies. The investor should consider risk, strategy and drawdowns.
