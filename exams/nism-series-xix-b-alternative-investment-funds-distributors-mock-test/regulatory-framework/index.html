Regulatory Framework: NISM Series XIX-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Regulatory Framework, from the NISM Series XIX-B (Alternative Investment Funds (Category III) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers SEBI's rules for Category III AIFs. Key topics are registration and the PPM, minimum corpus and minimum investment, accredited investors, number of investors, continuing interest of the sponsor or manager, leverage limits and the method for computing exposure, concentration limits, permitted investments including derivatives and commodity derivatives, disclosure and reporting, NAV computation and disclosure, risk management, the direct plan and distribution fee rules, investor charter, grievance redressal and penalties. Focus on the numerical thresholds that are stable and the principles behind limits that SEBI revises from time to time.

Regulatory Framework questions

Question 1

A Category III AIF has a corpus of ₹300 crore. The minimum continuing interest of the sponsor or manager is:

  1. ₹15 crore
  2. ₹7.5 crore
  3. ₹5 crore
  4. ₹10 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹10 crore

For Category III, continuing interest is the lower of 5% of corpus or ₹10 crore. 5% of ₹300 crore is ₹15 crore, so the requirement is ₹10 crore.

Question 2

A Category III AIF has NAV of ₹80 crore. Under SEBI's leverage cap, its maximum gross exposure is:

  1. ₹80 crore
  2. ₹240 crore
  3. ₹120 crore
  4. ₹160 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹160 crore

Gross exposure of a Category III AIF cannot exceed two times its NAV. 2 × ₹80 crore = ₹160 crore.

Question 3

While computing leverage for a Category III AIF, positions taken purely for hedging that meet SEBI's conditions:

  1. Must be doubled in exposure computation
  2. May be offset in computing exposure
  3. Are always prohibited
  4. Are ignored only for open-ended funds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. May be offset in computing exposure

SEBI's methodology allows offsetting of eligible hedging and portfolio-rebalancing positions when calculating exposure, so genuine hedges do not consume the leverage limit.

Question 4

Category III AIFs may participate in exchange-traded commodity derivatives. This is permitted:

  1. Without any limits
  2. Subject to SEBI's conditions and limits
  3. Only for agricultural commodities notified by the government
  4. Only if the fund is close-ended
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Subject to SEBI's conditions and limits

SEBI allows Category III AIFs to trade commodity derivatives within prescribed conditions, including concentration limits per commodity. It is not unlimited.

Question 5

Which body issues accreditation certificates to individuals wishing to be accredited investors?

  1. Accreditation agencies recognised by SEBI
  2. The AIF manager itself
  3. The Reserve Bank of India
  4. Any practising chartered accountant who certifies net worth
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Accreditation agencies recognised by SEBI

Accreditation is granted by SEBI-recognised accreditation agencies, typically subsidiaries of depositories or exchanges. Managers and CAs cannot accredit investors.

Question 6

The minimum corpus requirement for each scheme of a Category III AIF is:

  1. ₹10 crore
  2. ₹100 crore
  3. ₹20 crore
  4. ₹1 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹20 crore

Each AIF scheme, including Category III, must have a minimum corpus of ₹20 crore. ₹1 crore is the minimum per-investor investment.

Question 7

A Category III AIF wants to make a material change to its PPM, such as a change in strategy. Investors who disagree should generally be:

  1. Bound by the change without recourse
  2. Charged an exit penalty
  3. Given an exit option before the change
  4. Required to double their investment
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Given an exit option before the change

SEBI requires material changes to be communicated with an exit option for dissenting investors, protecting them from unwanted changes.

Question 8

Which is an obligation of a Category III AIF manager under SEBI's framework?

  1. Disclosing NAV to investors at prescribed frequency
  2. Guaranteeing a minimum return to investors
  3. Distributing all realised profits to investors every month as dividends
  4. Listing the units on a stock exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Disclosing NAV to investors at prescribed frequency

Managers must compute and disclose NAV periodically as prescribed. Guaranteeing returns or monthly payouts is not required, and listing is not mandatory.
