Category III AIF: Fund Structures and Service Providers: NISM Series XIX-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Category III AIF: Fund Structures and Service Providers, from the NISM Series XIX-B (Alternative Investment Funds (Category III) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how Category III AIFs are legally set up and the service providers that support them. It explains trust, company and LLP structures, the trust deed, investment management agreement and PPM, open-ended versus close-ended structures, master-feeder and side-by-side structures for foreign investors, unit classes, and the roles of custodian, prime broker, administrator, valuer and auditor. It also covers dematerialisation of units and the importance of operational infrastructure for trading-intensive strategies. Focus on structural choices and the work each provider performs.

Category III AIF: Fund Structures and Service Providers questions

Question 1

An open-ended Category III AIF differs from a close-ended one in that it:

  1. Allows periodic subscriptions and redemptions
  2. Can never use leverage
  3. Has a fixed maturity date of three years that cannot be extended by investors
  4. Is exempt from SEBI regulation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Allows periodic subscriptions and redemptions

Open-ended funds accept subscriptions and allow redemptions at specified intervals, often with notice periods. Category III funds of either type may use leverage within limits.

Question 2

In a master-feeder structure, the feeder fund:

  1. Trades securities directly while the master only collects fees
  2. Acts as the custodian of the master fund
  3. Pools investors and invests into the master
  4. Calculates NAV for the regulator
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Pools investors and invests into the master

Feeders collect money from particular investor groups, such as offshore investors, and invest in a master fund that holds the portfolio. Trading happens at master level.

Question 3

Why is a trust structure common for Category III AIFs in India?

  1. Trusts pay no tax on any income they earn, whatever its nature or source
  2. It is flexible and familiar to Indian investors
  3. Only trusts can use derivatives
  4. Trusts are not regulated by SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. It is flexible and familiar to Indian investors

Trusts are simple to establish and wind up and are widely used. They are taxed under trust-taxation rules, can be used for any category, and remain SEBI-regulated.

Question 4

A Category III fund issues a separate unit class with a lower management fee for investors committing more than ₹25 crore. This is permissible if:

  1. It is disclosed and within SEBI's rules
  2. It is kept confidential from smaller investors
  3. The sponsor guarantees returns to that class
  4. The class is not reported to SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. It is disclosed and within SEBI's rules

Differential fee classes are allowed when disclosed and consistent with SEBI's framework on investor rights. Secrecy or guarantees are not permitted.

Question 5

Units of AIF schemes are required to be held in dematerialised form mainly to:

  1. Allow units to be traded on stock exchanges daily
  2. Improve transparency and record keeping
  3. Remove the minimum investment requirement
  4. Avoid KYC
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Improve transparency and record keeping

Demat holding improves record keeping, transferability and regulatory oversight. It does not make units exchange-traded or waive KYC and minimums.

Question 6

A side-by-side structure means that:

  1. The AIF has two managers making separate decisions
  2. Two Indian AIFs merge into one
  3. An offshore fund invests alongside the Indian AIF in the same deals
  4. The fund invests side by side with a mutual fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. An offshore fund invests alongside the Indian AIF in the same deals

In a side-by-side structure, a parallel offshore fund invests alongside the domestic AIF, typically in the same proportions, under common management.

Question 7

Which service provider is most critical for executing a high-frequency quantitative strategy?

  1. A real estate valuer
  2. A legal adviser on property titles
  3. An insurance surveyor
  4. A broker with robust execution technology
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A broker with robust execution technology

Quantitative strategies depend on reliable, low-latency execution and connectivity from brokers. Property-related service providers are irrelevant.

Question 8

The valuer of a Category III AIF is most important for:

  1. Actively traded large-cap shares
  2. Cash balances
  3. Illiquid or thinly traded positions
  4. Exchange-traded futures with daily settlement prices
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Illiquid or thinly traded positions

Liquid securities have observable prices; valuers add most value where prices are not readily available and judgement is required.
