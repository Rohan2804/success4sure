Category III AIF Investment Strategies and Due Diligence Process: NISM Series XIX-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Category III AIF Investment Strategies and Due Diligence Process, from the NISM Series XIX-B (Alternative Investment Funds (Category III) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the main strategies used by Category III AIFs and how investors evaluate them. Strategies include long-only concentrated equity, long-short equity, market-neutral, arbitrage (cash-futures, merger, statistical), event-driven, quantitative and systematic, volatility and options-based, multi-strategy and global macro. Due diligence covers investment process, risk management, liquidity terms, leverage, style drift, operational controls, valuation, service providers and team. Revise how each strategy makes money, its typical risks, and the questions an investor or distributor should ask in due diligence.

Category III AIF Investment Strategies and Due Diligence Process questions

Question 1

A fund holds long positions of ₹120 crore and short positions of ₹80 crore with NAV of ₹100 crore. Its gross and net exposures are:

  1. Gross 40%, net 200% long
  2. Gross 120%, net 80% long
  3. Gross 200%, net 40% long
  4. Gross 200%, net 80% short
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Gross 200%, net 40% long

Gross exposure = 120 + 80 = 200 crore (200% of NAV). Net = 120 − 80 = 40 crore long (40% of NAV).

Question 2

In merger arbitrage, the main risk is that:

  1. Interest rates on bank deposits fall
  2. The acquirer pays a dividend
  3. The deal fails and the spread widens
  4. Index futures expire
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The deal fails and the spread widens

Merger arbitrage profits from the spread between the target's price and the deal value; if the deal fails, the target's price usually falls sharply.

Question 3

A fund holds long stocks of ₹100 crore with beta 1.2 and short index futures of ₹80 crore (beta 1.0). Its beta-adjusted net exposure is about:

  1. ₹20 crore long
  2. ₹40 crore long
  3. ₹180 crore long
  4. ₹40 crore short
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹40 crore long

Beta-adjusted long = 100 × 1.2 = 120; short = 80 × 1.0 = 80. Net = 120 − 80 = ₹40 crore long.

Question 4

A statistical arbitrage strategy typically:

  1. Takes large concentrated bets on single companies
  2. Invests in real estate projects
  3. Holds stocks for many years without trading
  4. Trades many small pricing anomalies using models
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Trades many small pricing anomalies using models

Statistical arbitrage uses quantitative models to exploit short-term mispricings across many securities, relying on diversification and execution efficiency.

Question 5

Which due diligence finding most indicates style drift?

  1. A long-only fund holding mostly equities
  2. An arbitrage fund holding cash-futures positions
  3. A quant fund using models
  4. A 'market-neutral' fund running large net long exposure
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A 'market-neutral' fund running large net long exposure

A market-neutral fund should have little net exposure; persistent large net long positions show it has drifted from its stated strategy.

Question 6

Why should investors check redemption terms such as notice periods and gates in an open-ended Category III fund?

  1. They decide the fund's tax rate
  2. They are irrelevant for liquid strategies because such funds never restrict exits
  3. They determine how quickly money can be withdrawn
  4. They determine the fund's benchmark
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. They determine how quickly money can be withdrawn

Notice periods, lock-ins and gates limit redemptions, especially in stress. Investors need to match these with their liquidity needs.

Question 7

An options-selling volatility strategy shows steady small gains for years. The main due diligence concern is:

  1. Too much diversification
  2. Lack of any premium income
  3. Exposure to rare but severe losses
  4. High dividend payouts
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Exposure to rare but severe losses

Selling options earns premiums regularly but can produce large losses during sharp market moves. Due diligence should test stress scenarios and risk limits.

Question 8

Which question is most relevant when assessing a manager's risk management framework?

  1. Who sets and monitors risk limits independently?
  2. How many branches does the manager have?
  3. What is the manager's advertising budget?
  4. Does the manager sponsor sports events?
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Who sets and monitors risk limits independently?

Independent setting and monitoring of position, leverage and loss limits is central to risk management. Marketing activities do not indicate risk discipline.
