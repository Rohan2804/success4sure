Overview of Alternative Investments: NISM Series XIX-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Overview of Alternative Investments, from the NISM Series XIX-B (Alternative Investment Funds (Category III) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces alternative investments with an emphasis on hedge-fund style strategies, which Category III AIFs resemble. It contrasts traditional long-only investing with absolute-return approaches using short selling, derivatives, arbitrage and leverage, and outlines the broader universe of alternatives — private equity, private credit, real estate and commodities. Learners should understand the objectives of alternatives (diversification, absolute returns, downside management), their distinctive risks (leverage, liquidity, model and counterparty risk) and why reported statistics can mislead. Focus on matching strategy descriptions to their likely risk and return behaviour.

Overview of Alternative Investments questions

Question 1

A 'long-short equity' strategy typically aims to:

  1. Profit from both rising and falling stocks
  2. Hold only cash during market downturns
  3. Track a market index as closely as possible
  4. Invest only in government bonds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Profit from both rising and falling stocks

Long-short managers buy stocks expected to outperform and short those expected to underperform, so they can earn from stock selection in both directions. Index tracking is a passive approach.

Question 2

Which risk is specifically introduced when a fund uses leverage?

  1. Magnified losses and margin calls
  2. Lower expected returns in all market conditions
  3. Elimination of market risk
  4. Removal of counterparty risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Magnified losses and margin calls

Leverage enlarges both gains and losses and can force selling when margins are called. It does not remove market or counterparty risk.

Question 3

A global macro strategy primarily takes positions based on:

  1. Detailed analysis of a single small-cap company
  2. Views on economies, rates and currencies
  3. Merger announcements only
  4. Pairs of closely related stocks
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Views on economies, rates and currencies

Global macro funds trade interest rates, currencies, commodities and indices based on macroeconomic views. Merger arbitrage and pair trades are different strategies.

Question 4

Why might the historical correlation of a hedge-fund strategy with equities rise during a market crisis?

  1. Liquidity stress makes many assets fall together
  2. Hedge funds are required by law to buy equities in a crisis
  3. Short positions automatically become long positions
  4. Correlations are fixed and cannot change
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Liquidity stress makes many assets fall together

In crises, deleveraging and liquidity shortages lead to simultaneous selling, raising correlations precisely when diversification is most needed.

Question 5

Compared with traditional investments, alternative investments usually have:

  1. Lower fees and daily holdings disclosure
  2. Higher fees and less transparency
  3. No minimum investment
  4. Guaranteed liquidity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Higher fees and less transparency

Alternatives generally carry management and performance fees, limited disclosure and higher minimums. Liquidity is often restricted.

Question 6

An investor says a fund with high average returns and low volatility is 'obviously safe'. What should an adviser point out?

  1. Low volatility always means low risk, so the fund can replace fixed deposits
  2. Strategies selling options can hide rare large losses
  3. Average returns guarantee future returns
  4. Hedge funds cannot have losses above 10%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Strategies selling options can hide rare large losses

Strategies that sell insurance-like options can earn steady returns until a rare event causes large losses. Volatility does not capture such tail risk.

Question 7

A 'relative value' strategy seeks to profit from:

  1. An overall rise in the stock market
  2. Long-term holding of real estate
  3. Mispricing between related securities
  4. Interest on bank deposits
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Mispricing between related securities

Relative value strategies buy the cheaper and sell the dearer of related instruments, aiming to profit as prices converge, with limited directional exposure.

Question 8

Which statement about 'absolute return' funds is correct?

  1. They guarantee positive returns every year
  2. They aim for positive returns but do not guarantee them
  3. They must always outperform the Nifty 50 index in every calendar year
  4. They are prohibited from using derivatives
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. They aim for positive returns but do not guarantee them

An absolute-return objective targets positive returns regardless of the market, but it is an aim, not a guarantee. Such funds commonly use derivatives.
