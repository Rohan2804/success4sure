Fees Structure, Fund Performance and Benchmarking: NISM Series XIX-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Fees Structure, Fund Performance and Benchmarking, from the NISM Series XIX-B (Alternative Investment Funds (Category III) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how Category III AIFs charge fees and how their performance is measured and compared. It explains management fees, performance fees, high-water marks, hurdle rates (hard and soft), crystallisation, and the impact of fees on net returns. It covers performance measures such as absolute and annualised returns, standard deviation, Sharpe, Sortino, Treynor and information ratios, maximum drawdown, alpha and beta, and SEBI's performance benchmarking framework. Practise numerical questions carefully and understand why benchmarks must reflect a fund's strategy and net exposure.

Fees Structure, Fund Performance and Benchmarking questions

Question 1

A fund returned 16% with standard deviation of 12%; the risk-free rate is 7%. Its Sharpe ratio is:

  1. 0.75
  2. 1.33
  3. 0.58
  4. 0.44
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 0.75

Sharpe = (16 − 7) ÷ 12 = 0.75. 1.33 divides the return by volatility without subtracting the risk-free rate.

Question 2

A fund returned 14% with downside deviation of 10%; the risk-free rate is 6%. Its Sortino ratio is:

  1. 1.40
  2. 0.60
  3. 0.80
  4. 2.33
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 0.80

Sortino = (14 − 6) ÷ 10 = 0.80. Unlike Sharpe, it penalises only downside volatility.

Question 3

A unit's NAV is ₹125 at the year-end. The high-water mark is ₹115. With a 20% performance fee and no hurdle, the fee per unit is:

  1. ₹5
  2. ₹2
  3. ₹3
  4. ₹25
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹2

Fee applies only to gains above the high-water mark: (125 − 115) × 20% = ₹2. ₹5 would apply the fee to gains from ₹100.

Question 4

NAV rises from ₹100 to ₹125 in a year. There is a 10% hard hurdle and a 20% performance fee. The fee per unit is:

  1. ₹3
  2. ₹5
  3. ₹2.5
  4. ₹2
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹3

With a hard hurdle, the fee is only on returns above ₹110: (125 − 110) × 20% = ₹3. With a soft hurdle and full catch-up, the fee would be ₹5.

Question 5

A fund's gross return is 18%. It charges a 2% management fee and then 20% of the remaining gain as performance fee. The approximate net return is:

  1. 14.4%
  2. 12.8%
  3. 16.0%
  4. 12.0%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 12.8%

After management fee, return = 16%. Performance fee = 20% × 16% = 3.2%. Net = 16 − 3.2 = 12.8%.

Question 6

A fund's active return over its benchmark is 4% and its tracking error is 5%. Its information ratio is:

  1. 1.25
  2. 0.2
  3. 0.8
  4. 9.0
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 0.8

Information ratio = active return ÷ tracking error = 4 ÷ 5 = 0.8. It measures consistency of outperformance per unit of active risk.

Question 7

Over the last two years, a hedge-style fund's unit value slid from its high of ₹200 to a low of ₹150 and then recovered. What maximum drawdown should be reported?

  1. 25%
  2. 33.3%
  3. 50%
  4. 20%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 25%

Drawdown = (200 − 150) ÷ 200 = 25%. 33.3% divides the fall by the trough instead of the peak.

Question 8

Why should an investor care about how often a Category III fund crystallises its performance fee?

  1. It decides the leverage limit SEBI allows the fund to use in a given year
  2. More frequent crystallisation can raise total fees
  3. It sets the minimum investment
  4. It has no effect on investor returns
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. More frequent crystallisation can raise total fees

If fees crystallise often, managers may earn fees on interim gains that later reverse, raising total fees in volatile periods. Annual crystallisation is generally more investor-friendly.
