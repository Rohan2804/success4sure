Valuation: NISM Series XIX-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Valuation, from the NISM Series XIX-B (Alternative Investment Funds (Category III) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how Category III AIFs value their holdings and compute NAV. It explains the valuation of listed equities, derivatives, debt instruments, unlisted or thinly traded securities and suspended stocks, the use of independent valuers and fair value principles, NAV computation (assets less liabilities including accrued fees and expenses, divided by units), frequency of NAV disclosure for open and close-ended funds, and the consequences of mispricing for fees and investor fairness. Practise NAV calculations and understand the treatment of accrued expenses and illiquid holdings.

Valuation questions

Question 1

A Category III fund has total assets of ₹212 crore, liabilities of ₹12 crore and 1.6 crore units. Its NAV per unit is:

  1. ₹132.50
  2. ₹125
  3. ₹117.50
  4. ₹140
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹125

NAV = (212 − 12) ÷ 1.6 = ₹125. ₹132.50 ignores liabilities (212 ÷ 1.6).

Question 2

A stock held by the fund is suspended from trading. Its valuation should be:

  1. The last traded price before suspension, indefinitely and without review
  2. Zero in all cases
  3. Fair value determined by an approved policy
  4. The price at which the fund bought it
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Fair value determined by an approved policy

Suspended securities should be valued at fair value in line with the valuation policy, considering all available information. Mechanically using old prices or zero may misstate NAV.

Question 3

Exchange-traded futures held by a Category III AIF are usually valued at:

  1. The exchange's daily settlement price
  2. The price at which the contract was opened
  3. The underlying stock's 52-week high
  4. The strike price of related options
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The exchange's daily settlement price

Futures are marked to the exchange settlement price each day, which reflects current market value.

Question 4

Accrued but unpaid management fees should be treated in NAV computation as:

  1. An asset that increases NAV
  2. Ignored until paid
  3. Part of investors' capital
  4. A liability that reduces NAV
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A liability that reduces NAV

Accrued fees are obligations of the fund and must be deducted as liabilities to present the true NAV.

Question 5

Overvaluing illiquid holdings in an open-ended fund harms:

  1. Remaining investors, as redeemers exit at inflated NAV
  2. Only the manager, as its fees fall whenever the portfolio is overvalued
  3. No one, as values eventually adjust
  4. Only new investors who buy at low NAV
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Remaining investors, as redeemers exit at inflated NAV

Redeeming investors are paid more than the true value, leaving remaining investors with the shortfall. Overvaluation also inflates fees.

Question 6

A thinly traded corporate bond with no recent trades is best valued by:

  1. Its face value
  2. The coupon rate
  3. The issuer's share price
  4. Using yields of similar bonds with spreads
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Using yields of similar bonds with spreads

Without trades, fair value can be estimated from yields on comparable bonds adjusted for credit and liquidity differences.

Question 7

Why should NAV be computed by an independent administrator rather than the manager alone?

  1. Because managers are not allowed to see NAV
  2. Because independent NAV is always higher
  3. To avoid all audit requirements
  4. To reduce the risk of manipulating NAV
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. To reduce the risk of manipulating NAV

Independent computation provides a check on the manager, whose fees depend on NAV. It does not remove audit requirements.

Question 8

Options held by a fund that are deep out of the money and illiquid should be valued:

  1. At the premium originally paid
  2. At zero always
  3. At their strike price
  4. Using a model consistent with market inputs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Using a model consistent with market inputs

Illiquid options are valued using recognised pricing models with current inputs like volatility. Original premium or strike values do not reflect fair value.
