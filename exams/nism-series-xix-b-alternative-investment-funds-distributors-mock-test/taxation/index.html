Taxation: NISM Series XIX-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Taxation, from the NISM Series XIX-B (Alternative Investment Funds (Category III) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the taxation of Category III AIFs and their investors in India. It explains that Category III funds lack statutory pass-through, the taxation of trusts (determinate and indeterminate) at fund level, characterisation of income as capital gains or business income, tax on derivatives trading, treatment of distributions in investors' hands, GST on fees, and the tax position of non-resident investors. It also compares the tax outcome of Category III AIFs with PMS and mutual funds. As tax law has been restructured, concentrate on principles rather than section numbers or rates.

Taxation questions

Question 1

A Category III AIF trust where each beneficiary's share is clearly determinable is generally taxed:

  1. At zero rate
  2. Only when investors redeem their units, at the rate applicable to each investor
  3. Only on dividends received
  4. As a representative assessee on beneficiaries' shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. As a representative assessee on beneficiaries' shares

A determinate trust is taxed in a representative capacity, in the same manner as the beneficiaries would be on their shares. Indeterminate trusts may face higher rates.

Question 2

A Category III fund frequently trades listed shares within days. Its gains are likely to be characterised as:

  1. Long-term capital gains
  2. Exempt income
  3. Income from house property
  4. Business income
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Business income

High-frequency trading indicates a trading business, so gains may be treated as business income. Long-term capital gains need longer holding periods.

Question 3

When a Category III AIF distributes income on which it has already paid tax, investors are generally:

  1. Taxed again at slab rates
  2. Taxed at 30% flat
  3. Not taxed again on it
  4. Liable for GST on it
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Not taxed again on it

Since tax is paid at fund level, distributions are generally not taxed again in investors' hands, avoiding double taxation.

Question 4

Compared with a PMS, a Category III AIF investor typically:

  1. Is not taxed on each underlying trade directly
  2. Must report every trade in her own return
  3. Pays no tax at any stage
  4. Must pay STT separately on each trade
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Is not taxed on each underlying trade directly

In a PMS, the client is taxed on each transaction in her account. In a Category III AIF, tax is mostly paid at the fund level.

Question 5

GST on management and performance fees in a Category III AIF is:

  1. Paid by SEBI
  2. Not applicable to fund managers because AIFs are regulated by SEBI
  3. Charged to the fund and reduces net returns
  4. Payable only by the distributor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Charged to the fund and reduces net returns

Fees are a taxable service, and GST on them is a cost borne by the fund, reducing net returns.

Question 6

Why might a Category III fund's high tax rate at the trust level matter to a low-income investor?

  1. She gets a refund of all tax paid by the fund
  2. She cannot use her lower slab on fund income
  3. Her income becomes exempt
  4. She pays tax twice on every rupee
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. She cannot use her lower slab on fund income

Because tax is paid at fund level, often at the maximum marginal rate, a low-bracket investor cannot benefit from her lower personal rate.

Question 7

A Category III fund earns income from derivatives traded on recognised exchanges. Such income is generally treated as:

  1. Speculative income
  2. Non-speculative business income
  3. Exempt income
  4. Capital gains in all cases
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Non-speculative business income

Eligible derivative transactions on recognised exchanges are excluded from speculative transactions, so income is generally non-speculative business income.

Question 8

Securities Transaction Tax on the fund's trades in listed shares is:

  1. Paid directly by each investor
  2. Refunded by the exchange at year-end
  3. Borne by the fund as a cost
  4. Not applicable to AIFs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Borne by the fund as a cost

STT applies to trades on recognised exchanges, and the fund pays it as a transaction cost, reducing returns.
