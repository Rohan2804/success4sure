Introduction to Category III AIF Ecosystem: NISM Series XIX-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Introduction to Category III AIF Ecosystem, from the NISM Series XIX-B (Alternative Investment Funds (Category III) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter describes the participants in the Category III AIF ecosystem and their roles: sponsor, investment manager, trustee, custodian, prime broker, fund accountant or administrator, registrar and transfer agent, auditor, valuer, legal and tax advisers, distributors and investors. It also covers how money and securities flow among them, the importance of independent service providers for investor protection, and the responsibilities of each party under SEBI's framework. Revise who does what, and which roles must be independent of the manager, using scenario questions about errors or conflicts.

Introduction to Category III AIF Ecosystem questions

Question 1

Which party in a Category III AIF is responsible for independently auditing the fund's financial statements?

  1. Statutory auditor
  2. Prime broker
  3. Investment manager
  4. Distributor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Statutory auditor

The statutory auditor gives an independent opinion on the fund's accounts. The prime broker provides financing and trading services, and the manager runs the portfolio.

Question 2

The registrar and transfer agent of a Category III AIF mainly handles:

  1. Trading decisions and risk limits
  2. Securities lending to the fund and financing of its leveraged positions
  3. Selection of stocks
  4. Investor records and unit transactions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Investor records and unit transactions

The RTA maintains investor records, processes subscriptions and redemptions and services investors. Trading decisions rest with the manager.

Question 3

Why should the custodian of a Category III AIF be independent of the manager?

  1. To allow the manager to trade without records
  2. To protect assets from misuse by the manager
  3. Because the custodian decides investments
  4. Because custodians earn performance fees
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. To protect assets from misuse by the manager

An independent custodian holds assets separately and reconciles positions, reducing the risk of misappropriation. It does not take investment decisions.

Question 4

A Category III fund relies on its prime broker for leverage and stock borrowing. A key risk for investors is:

  1. Counterparty risk if the prime broker fails
  2. Inflation risk on the government bonds the fund holds for margin purposes
  3. Currency risk on rupee holdings
  4. Absence of any market risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Counterparty risk if the prime broker fails

Heavy reliance on a prime broker exposes the fund to that broker's credit and operational risk; collateral may be at risk if it fails.

Question 5

Which of these is a function of the fund administrator?

  1. Marketing the fund to new investors
  2. Deciding the fund's strategy
  3. Granting accreditation to investors
  4. Independent NAV calculation and accounting
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Independent NAV calculation and accounting

Administrators calculate NAV, maintain books and reconcile positions independently. Marketing is done by distributors, and accreditation by recognised agencies.

Question 6

The sponsor of a Category III AIF differs from the investment manager in that the sponsor:

  1. Makes the daily trading decisions and sets position limits for the portfolio
  2. Holds custody of securities
  3. Audits the fund's accounts
  4. Sets up the AIF and keeps continuing interest
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Sets up the AIF and keeps continuing interest

The sponsor establishes the fund and maintains the required continuing interest; the manager takes investment decisions. The sponsor and manager may sometimes be the same entity.

Question 7

A distributor of a Category III AIF should:

  1. Promise investors a minimum return
  2. Manage the fund's portfolio
  3. Act as the custodian of investor units
  4. Hold the required NISM certification
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Hold the required NISM certification

AIF distributors must hold the relevant NISM certification and assess suitability. They cannot promise returns or perform the manager's or custodian's functions.

Question 8

Which service provider would most likely detect a mismatch between the manager's reported positions and actual holdings?

  1. The fund's marketing agency
  2. The investor relations team of the manager, while preparing quarterly letters
  3. The distributor
  4. The administrator reconciling with custodian records
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The administrator reconciling with custodian records

Independent administrators reconcile manager records with custodian and broker statements, flagging discrepancies. Marketing and distribution functions do not do this.
