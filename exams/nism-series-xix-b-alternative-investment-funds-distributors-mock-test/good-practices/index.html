Good Practices: NISM Series XIX-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Good Practices, from the NISM Series XIX-B (Alternative Investment Funds (Category III) Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the conduct expected of Category III AIF distributors. It explains SEBI's rules on distribution — NISM certification, direct plans, payment of distribution commission only on an equal-trail basis, disclosure of commissions — and good practices including suitability and appropriateness assessment, clear explanation of strategy, leverage, liquidity and fee terms, avoiding misleading claims about hedging or returns, KYC and AML compliance, handling complaints and maintaining records. Learners should be able to recognise mis-selling and conflicts and know what information must be given to investors before they invest.

Good Practices questions

Question 1

How may a Category III AIF pay commission to the distributors who place its units with investors?

  1. Fully upfront at the time of investment
  2. Only as a one-time fee on exit
  3. Only on an equal-trail basis
  4. As a share of the manager's performance fee
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Only on an equal-trail basis

SEBI requires Category III distribution fees to be paid only as equal trail, with no upfront payment, to discourage churning.

Question 2

While pitching a leveraged Category III fund, a distributor shows the client only its best annual returns and does not mention its past drawdowns. This conduct is:

  1. Acceptable marketing practice
  2. Required by SEBI
  3. Incomplete disclosure amounting to mis-selling
  4. Only a minor lapse if returns are high
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Incomplete disclosure amounting to mis-selling

Investors must be told about risks, including leverage and drawdowns. Highlighting only returns is misleading.

Question 3

A client's risk profile shows low risk tolerance, but he insists on a high-risk Category III AIF. A good distributor should:

  1. Document the mismatch and explain the risks
  2. Hide the risk profile result
  3. Change the risk profile to match the product
  4. Promise to cover losses
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Document the mismatch and explain the risks

The distributor should explain the mismatch, record the client's informed decision and not alter the profile. Promising to cover losses is prohibited.

Question 4

A distributor receives complaints from a client about delayed redemption. The distributor should:

  1. Ignore it, as it concerns the manager
  2. Ignore it, as redemption issues concern only the manager and not the distributor
  3. Withdraw the client's units personally
  4. Help escalate it to the manager and SEBI channels
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Help escalate it to the manager and SEBI channels

Distributors should help clients raise grievances with the manager and, if needed, through SEBI's SCORES and ODR mechanisms.

Question 5

Which statement to a client is appropriate for a long-short fund?

  1. The fund cannot lose money because all its positions are fully hedged
  2. SEBI guarantees the fund's capital
  3. Returns are fixed at 12% a year
  4. Hedging reduces some risk but losses are possible
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Hedging reduces some risk but losses are possible

Hedged funds can still lose money from stock selection, leverage or basis risk. No return or capital is guaranteed.

Question 6

Why should a distributor assess a client's entire portfolio before recommending a Category III AIF?

  1. To compute the fund's NAV
  2. To avoid over-concentration in risky products
  3. To decide the fund's leverage
  4. To decide the leverage the fund will use for that particular client's units
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. To avoid over-concentration in risky products

Looking at the full portfolio shows whether the new investment leads to excessive concentration or illiquidity.

Question 7

A distributor recommends switching a client from one Category III fund to another every year without clear reasons. This may indicate:

  1. Normal portfolio rebalancing
  2. Churning for commissions
  3. Mandatory regulatory action
  4. Tax-loss harvesting required by law
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Churning for commissions

Frequent unexplained switching benefits the distributor more than the client and may be churning. Recommendations must be in the client's interest.

Question 8

A distributor must keep client information confidential. Sharing it is permitted:

  1. With any other fund manager
  2. With friends who are prospective clients
  3. With client consent or as required by law
  4. Whenever the distributor wishes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. With client consent or as required by law

Client data may be shared only with consent or as legally required, such as to regulators. Casual sharing breaches confidentiality.
