Legal and Regulatory Framework: NISM Series V-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Legal and Regulatory Framework, from the NISM Series V-A (Mutual Fund Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the rules that govern how mutual funds invest, advertise and treat investors. Revise SEBI's investment restrictions (single-issuer limits, ownership limits in a company, borrowing limits), advertisement rules and standard warnings, treatment of unclaimed amounts, and the duties placed on distributors under the AMFI code of conduct and SEBI norms such as commission disclosure. Also know the source of SEBI's powers and how investor complaints move from the AMC to SCORES and then online dispute resolution.

Legal and Regulatory Framework questions

Question 1

Excluding index and sector-specific schemes, a mutual fund scheme cannot invest more than what share of its NAV in the equity shares of a single company?

  1. 10%
  2. 5%
  3. 15%
  4. 25%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 10%

SEBI caps investment in equity of any one company at 10% of a scheme's NAV, to ensure diversification. Index funds and ETFs may follow index weights, and sectoral funds have separate treatment.

Question 2

All schemes of a mutual fund taken together cannot own more than what share of any company's paid-up capital carrying voting rights?

  1. 5%
  2. 20%
  3. 10%
  4. 26%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 10%

The 10% fund-house-level limit on voting capital prevents a mutual fund from gaining control or undue influence over a company it invests in.

Question 3

A mutual fund may borrow money:

  1. Freely and for any tenure, to increase the scheme's exposure to equity markets beyond its net assets
  2. Only from its sponsor, without any limit or tenure
  3. Never, as borrowing of any kind is fully prohibited
  4. Only for temporary liquidity needs, up to 20% of net assets for 6 months
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Only for temporary liquidity needs, up to 20% of net assets for 6 months

Borrowing is allowed only for temporary liquidity needs, such as redemptions or IDCW payouts, and is capped at 20% of net assets for a maximum of six months. Leveraging to buy securities is not permitted.

Question 4

Which statement about mutual fund advertisements is correct?

  1. They may promise a minimum return for equity schemes if the AMC has a long and consistent track record
  2. They must carry the standard risk warning and show performance against the benchmark
  3. They may use performance of only the best year
  4. They do not need any disclosure if shown online
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. They must carry the standard risk warning and show performance against the benchmark

SEBI's advertisement code requires the standard warning about market risks and, where performance is shown, comparison with the benchmark over standard periods. Selective or assured-return claims are not allowed.

Question 5

Unclaimed redemption and IDCW amounts lying with a mutual fund are:

  1. Transferred to the AMC's own profit and loss account after one full year of remaining unclaimed
  2. Kept idle in a current account without any return
  3. Invested in special plans of liquid or money market schemes
  4. Distributed among the other unitholders of the scheme
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Invested in special plans of liquid or money market schemes

SEBI requires unclaimed amounts to be invested in specially created plans of liquid, overnight or money market schemes, so they keep earning returns until claimed by the rightful investors.

Question 6

SEBI derives its statutory powers to regulate mutual funds primarily from the:

  1. Companies Act, 2013
  2. Indian Trusts Act, 1882
  3. Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934
  4. SEBI Act, 1992
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. SEBI Act, 1992

The SEBI Act, 1992 established SEBI and gave it powers to regulate collective investment vehicles including mutual funds. The SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996 are framed under this Act.

Question 7

A distributor tells a client that a credit risk debt fund is 'as safe as a bank fixed deposit'. This is:

  1. Acceptable if the fund has never defaulted
  2. A misrepresentation that breaches the code of conduct
  3. Acceptable because both are debt products
  4. Required to simplify things for the investor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A misrepresentation that breaches the code of conduct

Mutual fund schemes are market-linked and carry credit and interest rate risk; they carry no deposit insurance. Comparing a credit risk fund to an FD misleads the investor about risk.

Question 8

When recommending schemes, a mutual fund distributor is required to disclose to the investor:

  1. The commissions it receives from the AMCs for the schemes recommended
  2. Its personal income tax returns and bank statements for the last three financial years
  3. The fund manager's salary details
  4. A guarantee of the scheme's future performance
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The commissions it receives from the AMCs for the schemes recommended

SEBI requires distributors to disclose all commissions, such as trail payments, received from competing schemes they recommend. This helps investors see any conflicts of interest.
