NAV, TER and Pricing of Units: NISM Series V-A revision notes

This numerical chapter covers how NAV is calculated, what expenses can be charged to a scheme, how loads affect the price an investor gets and which day's NAV applies to a transaction. Expect two or three calculation questions on NAV or exit load plus rule questions on cut-off times and valuation.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. NAV calculation

NAV per unit = (market value of investments + other assets + accrued income - liabilities - accrued expenses) / units outstanding. A scheme with assets of ₹120 crore, liabilities of ₹2 crore and 8 crore units has NAV of ₹14.75.

2. Valuation principles

Securities are valued at market prices; thinly traded or non-traded securities follow SEBI's fair valuation norms, using prices from valuation agencies for debt. Accrued interest and dividends declared but not received count as income on an accrual basis. Valuation policies are approved by the AMC's board and the trustees.

3. Total expense ratio

TER is the annual percentage of daily net assets charged for management fees, distribution, registrar, audit and marketing costs. SEBI caps TER in slabs that fall as AUM rises, with lower caps for debt and index schemes. TER limits are revised periodically - check current SEBI rules.

4. Effect of TER on returns

TER is deducted daily from the scheme's assets, so NAV is already net of expenses. Two identical portfolios earning 12% gross with TERs of 0.5% and 1.5% deliver roughly 11.5% and 10.5% to investors. Over 20 years, that one-percentage-point gap can reduce the final corpus by roughly a fifth.

5. Entry and exit loads

Entry load is banned since 2009. Exit load is charged when units are redeemed within a specified period - for example 1% if redeemed within one year. Exit load is credited back to the scheme, not kept by the AMC.

6. Repurchase price

Repurchase price = NAV x (1 - exit load). With NAV of ₹50 and 1% exit load, the investor receives ₹49.50 per unit. Sale price for purchases equals the applicable NAV, as no entry load is allowed. Exit loads usually lapse after a stated period.

7. Cut-off timings

For most schemes, applications received by 3 pm get the same day's NAV for redemptions. For purchases, NAV applies based on when funds are realised in the scheme's account, irrespective of amount. Liquid and overnight funds have a 1:30 pm cut-off for purchases and use the previous day's NAV.

8. Stamp duty

A stamp duty of 0.005% applies on purchase of mutual fund units, including SIP instalments and switch-ins, reducing the units allotted slightly. Redemptions do not attract stamp duty. On a ₹1,00,000 purchase, it works out to ₹5, so the units allotted are calculated on ₹99,995 rather than the full amount.

9. Swing pricing

Swing pricing adjusts NAV for certain debt schemes during large outflows so that exiting investors bear the cost of liquidating assets, protecting those who stay. It applies mainly in stressed market conditions. The swing factor and the conditions that trigger it are disclosed in the scheme documents.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • NAV per unit = (Assets - Liabilities) / Units outstanding
  • Redemption amount = Units x NAV x (1 - exit load %)
  • Units allotted = (Amount - stamp duty) / Applicable NAV

Common traps in the exam

  • Thinking exit load goes to the AMC as income - it is credited to the scheme.
  • Believing a lower NAV means a 'cheaper' or better fund; NAV level says nothing about value.
  • Applying the 3 pm cut-off rule blindly to liquid fund purchases - their cut-off is 1:30 pm.
  • Forgetting that NAV is already net of TER; expenses are not deducted again from redemption proceeds.

Quick recap

NAV = net assets / units. TER is charged daily and capped in slabs by SEBI. No entry load; exit load returns to the scheme. Repurchase price = NAV less exit load. Cut-offs: 3 pm generally, 1:30 pm for liquid and overnight purchases; purchase NAV depends on realisation of funds. Stamp duty of 0.005% applies on purchases.
