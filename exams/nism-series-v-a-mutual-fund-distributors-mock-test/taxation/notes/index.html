Taxation: NISM Series V-A revision notes

Taxation questions in V-A test the classification of schemes, holding periods for long-term status, how capital gains and IDCW are taxed, and STT. Tax rates change through Union Budgets, so understand the structure and check current rates with the income tax department. The figures below reflect rules as of 2026.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Equity-oriented fund

For tax purposes an equity-oriented fund invests at least 65% of its assets in equity shares of domestic listed companies. Equity fund of funds can also qualify if they meet the specified thresholds. Arbitrage funds qualify because their hedged equity counts as equity.

2. Equity fund capital gains

Units held for more than 12 months are long-term. As of 2026, LTCG above ₹1.25 lakh a year is taxed at 12.5% and STCG at 20%, following the July 2024 changes. Rates change through Budgets - check the latest Finance Act.

3. Specified (debt) mutual funds

Funds with more than 65% in debt and money market instruments, bought on or after 1 April 2023, are taxed at the investor's slab rate regardless of holding period. There is no indexation benefit. Older investments follow transition rules. Holding period no longer matters for these.

4. Other funds

Schemes that are neither equity-oriented nor specified debt funds - such as some gold ETFs, international funds and certain hybrids - have their own holding period (generally 24 months for unlisted units, 12 for listed) and rates. Questions usually test the concept rather than every case.

5. IDCW taxation

Income distributed under IDCW is taxed in the investor's hands at slab rates. The fund deducts TDS for resident individuals above the threshold set in the Act (raised to ₹10,000 a year from April 2025). Growth option investors are taxed only when they redeem.

6. Securities Transaction Tax

STT applies on redemption of equity-oriented fund units (paid by the seller) and on purchase or sale of equity ETF units on the exchange. Debt fund transactions do not attract STT. STT is a transaction levy and is separate from capital gains tax, which is computed on the gain itself.

7. Set-off of losses

Short-term capital losses can be set off against both short-term and long-term gains; long-term losses only against long-term gains. Unabsorbed losses can be carried forward for eight years if the return is filed on time. Capital losses cannot be set off against salary income.

8. ELSS and deduction

ELSS investments qualify for a deduction (up to ₹1.5 lakh with other eligible items) only under the old tax regime. Each SIP instalment has its own three-year lock-in. ELSS gains are taxed like any equity fund on redemption. The new regime offers no such deduction.

9. Switches and SWP

A switch between schemes, or between plans of the same scheme, is a redemption plus a fresh purchase and triggers capital gains. Each SWP instalment is partly capital and partly gain, and only the gain portion is taxed. Exit loads may apply too.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Equity-oriented: at least 65% in domestic listed equity; long term after 12 months
  • Capital gain = Redemption value - Cost of acquisition (units x purchase NAV)

Common traps in the exam

  • Assuming all debt funds get long-term treatment after three years - specified debt funds bought after 1 April 2023 are always taxed at slab.
  • Thinking a switch within the same fund house is tax-free; it is a sale and purchase.
  • Forgetting each ELSS SIP instalment has its own three-year lock-in.
  • Quoting tax rates as permanent - rates change through Budgets, so learn the structure and check current rates.

Quick recap

Equity-oriented = 65%+ domestic equity; long term after 12 months; LTCG beyond the annual exemption at a concessional rate (12.5% as of 2026). Debt funds bought after April 2023 are taxed at slab. IDCW is taxed at slab with TDS above a threshold. Switches are taxable. STT applies on equity fund redemptions.
