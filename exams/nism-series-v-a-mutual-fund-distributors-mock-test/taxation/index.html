Taxation: NISM Series V-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Taxation, from the NISM Series V-A (Mutual Fund Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how mutual fund investments are taxed in India. Revise how equity-oriented schemes are defined, the holding periods for short- and long-term gains, the current rates on equity gains (20% short-term, 12.5% long-term above the annual exemption of ₹1.25 lakh), slab-rate taxation of most debt fund gains on units bought after March 2023, treatment of IDCW, set-off rules for capital losses, grandfathering of pre-2018 equity gains, and why switches are taxable events. Because limits change in Budgets, read the latest rules before the exam.

Taxation questions

Question 1

Short-term capital gains on units of an equity-oriented fund, sold on or after 23 July 2024, are taxed at:

  1. 20%
  2. 15%
  3. 10%
  4. The investor's slab rate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 20%

Since 23 July 2024, STCG on equity-oriented fund units (held 12 months or less) on which STT is paid is taxed at 20%, up from the earlier 15%.

Question 2

Long-term capital gains on equity-oriented fund units are currently taxed at:

  1. 10% on gains above ₹1 lakh in a financial year
  2. 20% with indexation benefit
  3. 12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 lakh in a financial year
  4. Nil, as long-term equity gains are exempt
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 lakh in a financial year

From 23 July 2024, LTCG on equity-oriented funds is taxed at 12.5% on gains exceeding ₹1.25 lakh in a year. The earlier rule was 10% on gains above ₹1 lakh.

Question 3

An investor bought units of a debt fund (investing more than 65% in debt) in 2024 and sells them after four years. The gain is:

  1. Long-term, taxed at 20% with indexation
  2. Long-term, taxed at 12.5% without indexation
  3. Fully exempt after three years
  4. Treated as short-term and taxed at the investor's slab rate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Treated as short-term and taxed at the investor's slab rate

For specified debt-oriented funds bought after 1 April 2023, gains are deemed short-term irrespective of holding period and taxed at slab rates. Indexation benefits do not apply.

Question 4

An investor switches from the regular plan to the direct plan of the same scheme. For tax purposes, this switch is:

  1. Ignored, as the scheme remains the same
  2. A redemption plus a fresh purchase, so gains may be taxed
  3. Treated as a gift between plans
  4. Tax-exempt only for equity schemes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A redemption plus a fresh purchase, so gains may be taxed

Regular and direct plans are separate plans with different NAVs, so a switch is a sale of units in one plan and a purchase in another. Any gain is taxable and exit load may apply.

Question 5

For equity fund units bought before 1 February 2018 and sold later, the cost of acquisition for computing LTCG is:

  1. Always the actual purchase cost paid, without considering any market value adjustment
  2. The NAV on 1 April 2001
  3. Higher of actual cost and 31 Jan 2018 FMV (capped at sale value)
  4. Zero, since gains up to 2018 are taxable
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Higher of actual cost and 31 Jan 2018 FMV (capped at sale value)

Under grandfathering, gains accrued up to 31 January 2018 are protected. Cost is taken as the higher of actual cost and the lower of FMV on 31 January 2018 and the sale value.

Question 6

A short-term capital loss on mutual fund units can be set off against:

  1. Only short-term capital gains
  2. Salary income in the same year
  3. Any income, including interest
  4. Both short-term and long-term capital gains
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Both short-term and long-term capital gains

Short-term capital losses can be set off against both STCG and LTCG. Long-term capital losses can be set off only against LTCG. Capital losses cannot be set off against salary.

Question 7

An investor's LTCG on equity fund units in a financial year is ₹2,00,000 and there are no other equity gains. Ignoring cess and surcharge, the tax payable is:

  1. ₹25,000
  2. ₹9,375
  3. ₹15,000
  4. ₹20,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹9,375

Taxable gain = 2,00,000 − 1,25,000 exemption = ₹75,000. Tax at 12.5% = ₹9,375. ₹25,000 wrongly applies 12.5% to the whole gain.

Question 8

For a resident investor, IDCW received from a mutual fund is:

  1. Added to income and taxed at the investor's applicable slab rate
  2. Fully tax-free in the investor's hands
  3. Taxed at a flat 10% irrespective of the investor's total income for the year
  4. Taxed only when units are finally redeemed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Added to income and taxed at the investor's applicable slab rate

Since the abolition of dividend distribution tax in 2020, IDCW is taxable in the hands of the investor at slab rates, with TDS deducted by the fund above the prescribed threshold.
