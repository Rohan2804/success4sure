Risk, Return and Performance of Funds: NISM Series V-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Risk, Return and Performance of Funds, from the NISM Series V-A (Mutual Fund Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how mutual fund returns are measured and how risk is assessed. Revise absolute, annualised and compounded returns, and risk measures such as standard deviation, beta, modified duration, credit rating and downside deviation. Learn the risk-adjusted measures: Sharpe, Treynor, Sortino and Jensen's alpha, and which risk each one uses. Understand interest rate, credit, reinvestment and liquidity risk in debt funds, and practise short calculations of risk-adjusted performance.

Risk, Return and Performance of Funds questions

Question 1

A fund returned 13% while the risk-free rate was 6%. Its beta is 1.4. What is its Treynor ratio?

  1. 9.3
  2. 4.3
  3. 5.0
  4. 7.0
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 5.0

Treynor ratio = (fund return − risk-free rate) / beta = (13 − 6) / 1.4 = 5.0. It measures excess return per unit of systematic risk, unlike Sharpe, which uses total risk.

Question 2

A fund returned 15%, the market returned 12%, the risk-free rate was 6% and the fund's beta is 1.2. What is the fund's Jensen's alpha?

  1. 1.8%
  2. 3.0%
  3. 1.2%
  4. 0.6%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 1.8%

Expected return (CAPM) = 6 + 1.2 × (12 − 6) = 13.2%. Alpha = 15 − 13.2 = 1.8%. Simply comparing with the market (3%) ignores the fund's higher beta.

Question 3

Which measure best indicates how sensitive a debt fund's NAV is to a change in interest rates?

  1. Standard deviation of equity returns
  2. Modified duration
  3. Credit rating of the portfolio
  4. Expense ratio of the scheme
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Modified duration

Modified duration estimates the percentage change in price for a 1% change in yield. A higher modified duration means larger NAV swings when interest rates move.

Question 4

The Sortino ratio differs from the Sharpe ratio because it uses:

  1. Beta instead of standard deviation
  2. The benchmark return instead of the risk-free rate
  3. Gross return before deducting expenses
  4. Only downside deviation as the measure of risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Only downside deviation as the measure of risk

Sortino penalises only harmful volatility, using downside deviation below a target return. Sharpe uses total standard deviation, which also counts upside volatility as risk.

Question 5

Reinvestment risk for a debt investor is highest when:

  1. Rates fall and coupons are reinvested at lower yields
  2. Interest rates are rising steadily and new bonds are being issued at higher coupons
  3. The bond is a zero-coupon bond held to maturity
  4. Inflation is falling with stable interest rates
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Rates fall and coupons are reinvested at lower yields

Reinvestment risk is the risk that cash flows received are reinvested at lower rates. It bites when rates fall. A zero-coupon bond held to maturity has no interim cash flows, so it has no reinvestment risk.

Question 6

The 'maximum drawdown' of a fund refers to:

  1. The highest NAV the fund has ever reached
  2. The largest fall in value from a peak to a subsequent trough
  3. The largest single-day redemption by investors
  4. The maximum exit load the fund may charge
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The largest fall in value from a peak to a subsequent trough

Maximum drawdown measures the worst peak-to-trough decline over a period. It shows how much an investor could have lost by buying at the top, which standard deviation alone does not reveal.

Question 7

A fund's NAV rose from ₹10 to ₹13 in six months. What is its simple annualised return?

  1. 30%
  2. 69%
  3. 15%
  4. 60%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 60%

Absolute return = 3/10 = 30% over six months. Simple annualised return = 30% × (12/6) = 60%. The compounded annualised figure would be 69%, which is a different measure.

Question 8

Credit risk in a debt fund is best gauged by looking at:

  1. The scheme's modified duration
  2. The number of investors in the scheme
  3. The credit ratings of the securities in the portfolio
  4. The scheme's beta relative to Nifty 50
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The credit ratings of the securities in the portfolio

Credit ratings indicate the likelihood of issuers paying interest and principal on time. Lower-rated holdings mean higher credit risk; duration measures interest rate risk instead.
