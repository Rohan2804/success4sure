Mutual Fund Scheme Performance: NISM Series V-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Mutual Fund Scheme Performance, from the NISM Series V-A (Mutual Fund Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how to evaluate a scheme's performance fairly. Revise the use of appropriate benchmarks (TRI for equity, relevant debt indices for debt), peer-group comparison within the same category, point-to-point versus rolling returns, CAGR calculation, tracking difference and tracking error for index funds and ETFs, and pitfalls such as survivorship bias. For debt funds, understand how portfolio yield and expenses indicate potential returns. Expect numerical questions on CAGR and tracking difference.

Mutual Fund Scheme Performance questions

Question 1

When comparing a scheme's performance with other schemes, the peer group should consist of:

  1. All mutual fund schemes in the industry
  2. Schemes managed by the same fund manager
  3. Schemes launched in the same calendar year
  4. Schemes in the same SEBI category with similar objectives
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Schemes in the same SEBI category with similar objectives

Peer comparison is meaningful only when schemes share objectives, asset allocation and risk, which is why SEBI categories are used. Comparing across categories mixes very different risks.

Question 2

Why are rolling returns considered more reliable than a single point-to-point return?

  1. They always show higher returns than point-to-point figures
  2. They reduce the effect of the chosen start and end dates
  3. They are calculated only during bull markets
  4. They exclude the impact of the expense ratio
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. They reduce the effect of the chosen start and end dates

Point-to-point returns depend heavily on the chosen dates. Rolling returns average performance over many overlapping periods, giving a better picture of consistency.

Question 3

The most suitable benchmark for a liquid fund is:

  1. The Nifty 50 Total Return Index
  2. A 10-year government bond index
  3. A liquid or money market debt index
  4. The price of gold in rupees
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A liquid or money market debt index

A liquid fund invests in very short-term debt, so its benchmark should be a liquid or money market index with similar maturity and credit profile.

Question 4

An index fund returned 11.4% in a year while its benchmark TRI returned 12.0%. The tracking difference is:

  1. −0.6%
  2. +0.6%
  3. −1.2%
  4. 23.4%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. −0.6%

Tracking difference = fund return − index return = 11.4 − 12.0 = −0.6%. It largely reflects expenses and cash drag. Tracking error is a different measure: the variability of these differences.

Question 5

When an AMC presents a scheme's past performance in its communications, it must show it:

  1. Only for the period in which returns were highest
  2. Alongside its benchmark's returns for the same periods
  3. Without any comparison, to avoid confusing investors
  4. Along with a promise of similar future returns
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Alongside its benchmark's returns for the same periods

SEBI requires performance to be shown against the benchmark (and an additional benchmark where specified) for standard periods, so investors can judge the scheme's value addition.

Question 6

Category average returns can look better than reality because schemes that were merged or closed due to poor performance are dropped from the data. This is called:

  1. Recency bias
  2. Confirmation bias
  3. Survivorship bias
  4. Home bias
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Survivorship bias

Survivorship bias arises when only surviving funds are counted. Since weak funds tend to be closed or merged, the average of survivors overstates what investors actually earned.

Question 7

A scheme's NAV grew from ₹10 to ₹20 over exactly 5 years. Its CAGR is closest to:

  1. 14.87%
  2. 20.00%
  3. 15.00%
  4. 12.25%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 14.87%

CAGR = (20/10)^(1/5) − 1 = 14.87%. Dividing the 100% absolute gain by 5 gives 20%, which ignores compounding.

Question 8

For a debt scheme that holds bonds to maturity, a good indicator of the likely return is:

  1. The scheme's highest one-day return
  2. The past one-month return annualised
  3. The number of bonds in the portfolio
  4. The portfolio yield to maturity minus the expense ratio
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The portfolio yield to maturity minus the expense ratio

If securities are held to maturity, investors broadly earn the portfolio YTM, less expenses. Short-term past returns can be distorted by price changes from rate movements.
