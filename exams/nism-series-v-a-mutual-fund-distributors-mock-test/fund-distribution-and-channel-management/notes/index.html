Fund Distribution and Channel Management: NISM Series V-A revision notes

This chapter explains how mutual funds reach investors: the types of distributors, the registration process, how distributors are paid and the rules that protect investors from mis-selling. Questions frequently test ARN and EUIN, upfront versus trail commission, the direct plan and the role of stock exchange and online platforms.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Distribution channels

Funds are sold by individual distributors, banks, national distributors, stock brokers, online platforms and directly by AMCs. Exchange platforms such as NSE's NMF II and BSE StAR MF let brokers process transactions; MF Central and MF Utility offer common transaction services.

2. Becoming a distributor

An individual must pass NISM Series V-A, then obtain an AMFI Registration Number (ARN) through KYD (Know Your Distributor) formalities. Employees of corporate distributors who interact with investors need an Employee Unique Identification Number (EUIN). The ARN must be quoted on every application form so that commission is correctly tracked and paid.

3. Validity and CPE

The NISM certificate is valid for three years. To renew, distributors either retake the exam or attend Continuing Professional Education (CPE) as specified. An expired ARN means the distributor cannot earn commission on new business. Distributors should track the expiry date of both the certificate and the ARN, since lapses interrupt trail commission on new investments.

4. Commission structure

SEBI has banned upfront commission paid out of scheme expenses; distributors earn trail commission, a percentage of the assets they bring, paid as long as the investor remains invested. Commission is part of the regular plan's expense ratio. Trail is paid monthly or quarterly by the AMC.

5. Commission disclosure

Distributors must disclose commissions received from competing schemes. The Consolidated Account Statement shows the commission paid to the distributor on the investor's holdings, helping investors see what they pay. This transparency lets investors compare the cost of using a distributor with investing directly in the scheme's direct plan.

6. Direct plans

Every scheme has a direct plan for investors who invest without a distributor. Its expense ratio is lower because no commission is paid, so its NAV is typically higher than the regular plan's over time. Investors can buy direct plans through AMC websites, MF Central or RTA offices.

7. Mutual Fund Distributor vs RIA

A distributor earns commission from AMCs and may give incidental advice. A SEBI-registered investment adviser charges fees to clients and cannot also earn commission on the same client. Distributors may not call themselves 'independent financial advisers'. Relatives and group entities are also checked for this separation of roles.

8. Due diligence and churning

AMCs perform due diligence on large distributors. Churning - switching a client between schemes to earn more commission - and rebating commission to clients are prohibited. Distributors must sell only suitable products after assessing the client's needs. Every recommendation should be documented.

9. Payment rules

Cheques must be drawn in favour of the scheme, not the distributor. Third-party payments are not accepted except limited cases such as a parent investing for a minor, an employer for employees or a custodian for an FPI. Such exceptions require prescribed declarations.

Common traps in the exam

  • Thinking distributors still earn upfront commission from scheme expenses - SEBI banned this; trail is the main mode.
  • Confusing ARN (for the distributor firm or individual) with EUIN (for an employee of a distributor).
  • Assuming a direct plan has a different portfolio; the portfolio is identical, only the expense ratio differs.
  • Writing cheques to the distributor's name - payments always go to the scheme.

Quick recap

Pass NISM V-A, get an ARN from AMFI; employees need an EUIN. Certificates last three years; renew by exam or CPE. Distributors earn trail commission, shown in the CAS. Direct plans have no commission and a lower TER. No churning, no rebating, no third-party cheques except permitted cases.
