Legal Structure of Mutual Funds: NISM Series V-A revision notes

Indian mutual funds are set up as trusts with a three-tier structure of sponsor, trustees and asset management company, supported by service providers. Exam questions ask who does what: who creates the fund, who protects investors, who manages money and who holds the securities. Learn the roles and the eligibility conditions SEBI sets for each entity.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Trust structure

A mutual fund in India is constituted as a public trust under the Indian Trusts Act, 1882. The sponsor creates the trust through a trust deed registered under the Indian Registration Act, 1908. The trust is the mutual fund; the schemes are launched under it.

2. Sponsor

The sponsor is like a promoter of a company. SEBI's eligibility criteria include a sound track record, profits in at least three of the last five years and positive net worth, with an alternative route for certain private equity sponsors. The sponsor contributes to the AMC's net worth and appoints the trustees.

3. Trustees

Trustees are the investors' watchdogs. They may be individuals forming a board of trustees or a trustee company. At least two-thirds of trustees must be independent of the sponsor. They approve schemes, monitor the AMC and ensure compliance with SEBI regulations.

4. Asset Management Company

The AMC is the investment manager appointed by the trustees. It designs schemes, manages portfolios, appoints most service providers and handles sales. It must maintain a minimum net worth of ₹50 crore and at least 50% of its board must be independent directors.

5. Custodian

The custodian holds the scheme's securities in safekeeping, settles trades and tracks corporate actions such as dividends and bonus issues. It must be registered with SEBI. Independence matters: a sponsor and its associates cannot hold 50% or more of the custodian's voting rights if the custodian services that fund.

6. Registrar and Transfer Agent

The RTA (for example CAMS or KFintech) processes purchase and redemption applications, maintains unit holder records and sends account statements. A change of address or bank mandate usually goes through the RTA or AMC, not the custodian. Investors can also submit transactions at RTA service centres.

7. Other service providers

Fund accountants compute NAV, auditors audit scheme accounts (separately from the AMC's auditor), bankers handle collections and payments, and distributors bring in investors. Many functions can be outsourced, but responsibility stays with the AMC. The trustees review whether these outsourced arrangements protect unit holders adequately.

8. AMFI

The Association of Mutual Funds in India is the industry body of SEBI-registered AMCs, not a regulator. It registers distributors (ARN), issues the code of conduct, runs investor awareness campaigns and publishes industry data and the market-cap list. Certification exams for distributors are conducted by NISM, not by AMFI.

9. Separation of roles

The structure deliberately separates ownership of assets (trust, held by custodian), management (AMC) and oversight (trustees). This protects investors if the AMC fails: scheme assets do not belong to the AMC and cannot be used to pay its creditors. Each entity's accounts are audited independently.

Common traps in the exam

  • Mixing up trustees and the AMC: trustees supervise and protect investors, the AMC manages the money.
  • Calling AMFI a regulator - SEBI regulates; AMFI is a self-regulatory industry association.
  • Assuming the custodian maintains investor records; that is the RTA's job. The custodian holds securities.
  • Forgetting the ₹50 crore AMC net worth requirement and the two-thirds independence rule for trustees.

Quick recap

Sponsor creates the trust; trustees (two-thirds independent) guard investors; the AMC (₹50 crore net worth, half the board independent) manages money; the custodian holds securities; the RTA keeps unit holder records. AMFI is the industry body that registers distributors. Scheme assets are held in trust, separate from the AMC's own money.
