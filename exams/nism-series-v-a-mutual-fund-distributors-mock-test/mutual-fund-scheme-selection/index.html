Mutual Fund Scheme Selection: NISM Series V-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Mutual Fund Scheme Selection, from the NISM Series V-A (Mutual Fund Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter is about matching schemes to investor needs. Revise how goal, time horizon, risk profile and liquidity needs point to suitable categories, how to choose between active and passive funds, direct and regular plans, and growth, IDCW and SWP options. Also consider fund-level factors such as consistency of performance, expense ratio, fund size relative to its category, and portfolio overlap when holding several schemes. Questions are mostly scenario-based, asking for the most suitable recommendation.

Mutual Fund Scheme Selection questions

Question 1

An investor wants to build an emergency fund that must be accessible within a day, with very low risk. The most suitable scheme is:

  1. A liquid fund
  2. A small cap fund
  3. A credit risk fund
  4. A sectoral equity fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A liquid fund

Liquid funds invest in instruments maturing within 91 days, have low volatility and process redemptions quickly, which suits emergency money. Equity and credit risk funds are too volatile.

Question 2

A cost-conscious investor who believes most active managers will not beat the market over time would prefer:

  1. A thematic fund focused on one theme
  2. A fund with the highest past one-year return
  3. An index fund or ETF tracking a broad index
  4. A focused fund holding 25 stocks
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. An index fund or ETF tracking a broad index

Passive funds track an index at low cost, avoiding the risk of a manager underperforming. Chasing last year's top performer or thematic bets adds manager and concentration risk.

Question 3

When choosing between two equity funds in the same category, which factor is the most reliable guide?

  1. Which one topped the one-month return chart
  2. Which one has the newest launch date
  3. Which one has the lowest NAV per unit
  4. Consistent performance versus benchmark across cycles
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Consistent performance versus benchmark across cycles

Consistent performance over full cycles reflects process, not luck. A low NAV does not make a fund cheaper, and short-term rankings change frequently.

Question 4

A moderate-risk investor needs the money in about three years for a home down payment. Which choice is most appropriate?

  1. A small cap fund for higher growth
  2. A short duration debt fund or conservative hybrid fund
  3. A sector fund that has done well recently
  4. A 10-year gilt fund with constant maturity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A short duration debt fund or conservative hybrid fund

A three-year goal with moderate risk calls for limited equity and moderate interest rate risk. Small cap and sectoral funds are too volatile, and a 10-year gilt fund carries high duration risk.

Question 5

An investor who wants guidance on scheme selection and periodic review from a mutual fund distributor will typically invest in:

  1. The direct plan of the scheme
  2. A closed-ended plan only
  3. The regular plan of the scheme
  4. An ETF through the AMC office
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The regular plan of the scheme

Distributors are paid through trail commission included in the regular plan's TER. Investors who want a distributor's service therefore invest in the regular plan; direct plans carry no commission.

Question 6

Why might an investor prefer the growth option with an SWP over the IDCW option for regular cash flow?

  1. IDCW payouts are guaranteed by the AMC every month, whereas SWP amounts can never be fixed
  2. SWP withdrawals are always fully tax-free for every investor
  3. The IDCW option does not allow redemption at any time
  4. SWP controls amount and timing; only the gain part is taxed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. SWP controls amount and timing; only the gain part is taxed

IDCW amounts depend on the AMC's decision and are fully taxable at slab rates. SWP lets the investor fix the amount and date, and tax applies only to the capital gain component.

Question 7

A very large AUM can be a concern mainly for which type of scheme?

  1. A liquid fund
  2. A small cap fund
  3. A Nifty 50 index fund
  4. An overnight fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A small cap fund

Small cap stocks have limited liquidity. A very large small cap fund may struggle to buy or sell without moving prices, which can hurt returns. Large-cap index funds and liquid funds handle size more easily.

Question 8

An investor holds three different large cap funds. The main drawback of this choice is:

  1. High portfolio overlap, giving limited extra diversification
  2. A higher tax rate that applies once an investor holds more than one large cap fund
  3. A lock-in on each additional large cap fund
  4. SEBI's ban on holding more than one large cap fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. High portfolio overlap, giving limited extra diversification

Large cap funds draw from the same top 100 stocks, so three such funds often hold similar shares. The investor adds complexity and cost without much extra diversification.
