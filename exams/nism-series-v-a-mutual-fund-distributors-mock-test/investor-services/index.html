Investor Services: NISM Series V-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Investor Services, from the NISM Series V-A (Mutual Fund Distributors) syllabus.

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers what happens after an investor decides to invest: NFO timelines and allotment, cut-off times and applicable NAV, systematic plans (SIP, STP, SWP), statements, KYC and PAN requirements, nomination and transmission, minors' folios, and NRI investments. Revise the special NAV rule for liquid and overnight funds, the treatment of micro investments, timelines for statements and the steps when a minor turns major. Investor Services questions

Question 1

Units of a new fund offer must be allotted to investors within how many business days of the NFO closing?

  1. 15 business days
  2. 5 business days
  3. 30 business days
  4. 1 business day
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 5 business days

SEBI requires allotment of units and dispatch of statements within five business days of the NFO closure, so investors can begin transacting soon after.

Question 2

An investor invests in a liquid fund at 1:00 pm on a Friday; funds are credited to the scheme before the cut-off. Monday is a business day. Which NAV applies?

  1. NAV of Friday
  2. NAV of Monday
  3. NAV of Thursday
  4. NAV of Sunday, the day before the next business day
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. NAV of Sunday, the day before the next business day

For liquid and overnight funds, an application before 1:30 pm with funds realised gets the NAV of the day immediately preceding the next business day. Here that is Sunday, so the investor earns weekend returns.

Question 3

The sole holder of a folio has died and a nominee is registered. To get the units, the nominee should normally submit:

  1. A transmission request with the death certificate and the nominee's KYC
  2. A fresh NFO application in the deceased's name
  3. A court succession certificate and indemnity bond in every case, regardless of nominee
  4. A redemption request signed by the deceased
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A transmission request with the death certificate and the nominee's KYC

Where a valid nominee exists, transmission is simple: the nominee submits the transmission form, death certificate and own KYC. Succession certificates are needed only when there is no nominee and amounts are large.

Question 4

When a minor unitholder attains majority, what must happen to the folio?

  1. The units are automatically redeemed and paid to the guardian
  2. The guardian continues to operate it for five more years
  3. It must be changed to major status; until then it is frozen
  4. Nothing changes; the guardian keeps operating the folio
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. It must be changed to major status; until then it is frozen

On attaining majority, the investor must submit the change-of-status request with own KYC and bank details. Until then the folio is frozen for transactions, and SIPs registered by the guardian stop.

Question 5

An investor holds mutual fund units but has made no transaction for several months. How often will a Consolidated Account Statement still be sent?

  1. Only when the investor asks for it in writing
  2. Every week, by email only
  3. Never, until a new transaction is made
  4. Every half-year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Every half-year

Investors who transact receive a CAS for that month. Those without any transaction still receive a CAS every half-year showing holdings and their value, so records stay up to date.

Question 6

An NRI wants to invest so that both principal and gains can be taken abroad. The investment should be made through:

  1. An NRE account
  2. An NRO account
  3. A resident savings account
  4. A cash payment at the AMC office
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. An NRE account

Investments routed through the NRE account (or FCNR) are repatriable. Investments from an NRO account are generally non-repatriable, subject to permitted annual remittance limits.

Question 7

Small SIP investments up to ₹50,000 a year per investor per fund house can be made without PAN through:

  1. A cash deposit with any bank branch
  2. A simple self-declaration of income on the form
  3. PAN-exempt KYC (PEKRN) registration under the micro investment route
  4. The investor's Aadhaar number printed on the cheque
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. PAN-exempt KYC (PEKRN) registration under the micro investment route

Micro investments up to ₹50,000 a year (including micro SIPs) are exempt from PAN if the investor completes PAN-exempt KYC and obtains a PEKRN. Other KYC norms continue to apply.

Question 8

An investor puts ₹2,000 a month through a SIP for three months at NAVs of ₹16, ₹20 and ₹25. What is the average cost per unit (to two decimals)?

  1. ₹20.33
  2. ₹19.67
  3. ₹20.00
  4. ₹19.25
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹19.67

Units bought = 125 + 100 + 80 = 305. Average cost = 6,000 / 305 = ₹19.67, below the simple average NAV of ₹20.33 because more units are bought when NAV is low.
