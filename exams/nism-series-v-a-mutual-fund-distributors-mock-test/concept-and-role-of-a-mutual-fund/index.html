Concept and Role of a Mutual Fund: NISM Series V-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Concept and Role of a Mutual Fund, from the NISM Series V-A (Mutual Fund Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains what a mutual fund is, how it pools investor money, and the benefits and limitations of investing through one. It covers open-ended, closed-ended and interval schemes, and SEBI's categorisation of equity, debt, hybrid, solution-oriented and other schemes, including the investment rules that define each category. Revise distinctive features such as focused funds' stock limit, the value/contra choice, credit risk funds' rating rule and lock-ins for ELSS and solution-oriented schemes.

Concept and Role of a Mutual Fund questions

Question 1

An interval scheme is one that:

  1. Allows purchase and redemption with the AMC on every single business day of the year
  2. Never allows redemption until its final maturity
  3. Allows purchase and redemption only during specified transaction periods
  4. Pays interest to unitholders at fixed intervals
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Allows purchase and redemption only during specified transaction periods

Interval schemes combine features of open and closed-ended schemes. Units can be bought or redeemed only during pre-announced intervals; at other times they are traded on a stock exchange.

Question 2

A Fund of Funds (FoF) scheme primarily invests in:

  1. Units of other mutual fund schemes
  2. Shares of asset management companies
  3. Bank fixed deposits of different tenors
  4. Government securities only
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Units of other mutual fund schemes

An FoF builds its portfolio from units of other schemes, domestic or overseas. Investors get diversification across funds but bear an additional layer of expenses.

Question 3

Under SEBI categorisation, a focused fund may hold a maximum of:

  1. 20 stocks
  2. 30 stocks
  3. 50 stocks
  4. 100 stocks
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 30 stocks

A focused fund invests in a concentrated portfolio of at most 30 stocks. It must state the market cap focus (large, mid, small or multi) and invest at least 65% in equity.

Question 4

Under SEBI categorisation, an AMC may offer:

  1. Both a value fund and a contra fund at any time
  2. A contra fund only if it also runs a value fund
  3. Neither, as these categories were withdrawn
  4. Either a value fund or a contra fund, but not both
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Either a value fund or a contra fund, but not both

SEBI permits one scheme per category. Value and contra funds are treated as alternatives within one slot, so a fund house may offer only one of the two.

Question 5

A credit risk fund must invest at least 65% of its assets in:

  1. Corporate bonds rated AA and below, excluding AA+
  2. AAA-rated public sector bonds only
  3. Government securities of any maturity
  4. Money market instruments maturing within 91 days
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Corporate bonds rated AA and below, excluding AA+

Credit risk funds seek extra yield by taking lower-rated paper: at least 65% in corporate bonds rated AA and below (excluding AA+). This raises both potential return and default risk.

Question 6

Which is a limitation of investing through a mutual fund compared with holding shares directly?

  1. The investor gets no professional management at all
  2. The investor cannot tailor the portfolio to personal preferences
  3. The investor cannot redeem units in an open-ended scheme
  4. The investor must buy at least one full share of each company
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The investor cannot tailor the portfolio to personal preferences

A scheme follows its stated mandate for all investors, so individual choices on specific stocks cannot be accommodated. Professional management and liquidity are benefits, not limitations.

Question 7

Retirement and children's funds (solution-oriented schemes) carry a lock-in of:

  1. Exactly 3 years from the date of each individual investment
  2. No lock-in, but an exit load of 1%
  3. 10 years for all investors
  4. At least 5 years, or till retirement or majority if earlier
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. At least 5 years, or till retirement or majority if earlier

Solution-oriented schemes have a lock-in of at least 5 years or until the retirement age or the child attaining majority, whichever is earlier, to encourage goal-based investing.

Question 8

A balanced advantage (dynamic asset allocation) fund differs from an aggressive hybrid fund mainly because it:

  1. Must always hold between 65% and 80% of its assets in equity instruments
  2. Invests only in government securities
  3. Can vary its equity–debt mix dynamically within a wide range
  4. Is a closed-ended scheme with fixed maturity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Can vary its equity–debt mix dynamically within a wide range

Balanced advantage funds shift between equity and debt (0–100%) based on a model or view. Aggressive hybrid funds must keep equity between 65% and 80% of assets.
