Scheme Related Information: NISM Series V-A revision notes

Investors receive several offer documents and periodic disclosures. This chapter explains what each document contains, which must accompany an application form, how the riskometer works and what counts as a fundamental attribute. Exam questions often describe a piece of information and ask which document contains it.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Scheme Information Document

The SID contains scheme-specific details: investment objective, asset allocation pattern, investment strategy, risk factors, benchmark, fund manager, loads, minimum investment and plans and options. It is the main document for judging whether a scheme fits an investor. It is updated at least once a year.

2. Statement of Additional Information

The SAI holds information common to all schemes of the fund house: the sponsor, AMC, trustees, service providers, legal and tax information, and the AMC's general policies. It is incorporated into the SID by reference and updated periodically. One SAI serves every scheme of the fund.

3. Key Information Memorandum

The KIM is a short summary of the SID and must accompany every application form. It gives the essential facts - objective, asset allocation, risk, expenses, loads and past performance - so investors can decide without reading the full SID.

4. Riskometer

SEBI's riskometer has six levels: Low, Low to Moderate, Moderate, Moderately High, High and Very High. It is computed from the portfolio's risk value and reviewed monthly, so a scheme's risk level can change. Benchmarks also carry a riskometer. Changes must be disclosed to unit holders.

5. Fundamental attributes

Fundamental attributes are the type of scheme, investment objective, investment pattern and terms such as liquidity provisions and fees. Changing them needs trustees' and SEBI's approval, written communication to unit holders and an exit window without exit load. The exit window is typically at least 30 days.

6. Plans and options

Every scheme has a direct plan (no distributor commission, lower expense ratio) and a regular plan. Options include Growth, where gains stay in the NAV, and IDCW (income distribution cum capital withdrawal), where payouts come out of the investor's own capital and gains.

7. Periodic disclosures

Schemes disclose NAV daily on the AMC and AMFI websites, portfolios at least monthly or fortnightly as required, half-yearly financial results and an annual report. These let investors check that the portfolio matches the stated objective. Fund factsheets add monthly details such as expense ratio, portfolio turnover and fund manager names.

8. New Fund Offer

An NFO is the first offer of a scheme's units. Most NFOs stay open for no more than 15 days (ELSS can differ). Units are allotted at the face value plus any applicable charges, and the scheme must reopen for continuous sale and repurchase within a few business days.

Common traps in the exam

  • Thinking the KIM replaces the SID legally - the KIM summarises, while the SID remains the detailed document.
  • Placing AMC and sponsor details in the SID; they are in the SAI.
  • Assuming the riskometer is fixed at launch; it is evaluated monthly and can move up or down.
  • Calling IDCW a bonus or extra income; it is paid out of the NAV, which falls by the payout amount.

Quick recap

SID = scheme-specific facts; SAI = fund-house facts; KIM = summary that goes with every form. The riskometer has six levels, from Low to Very High, reviewed monthly. Changing a fundamental attribute needs approvals, notice and a load-free exit. Direct plans skip commission; IDCW payouts reduce NAV.
