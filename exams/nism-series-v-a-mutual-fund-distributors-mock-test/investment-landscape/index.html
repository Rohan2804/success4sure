Investment Landscape: NISM Series V-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Investment Landscape, from the NISM Series V-A (Mutual Fund Distributors) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces why people invest and the choices available to them: physical versus financial assets, the risk-return trade-off, the main types of investment risk, and how inflation erodes purchasing power. It also covers asset allocation (strategic and tactical), the stages of an investor's life and wealth cycle, and common behavioural biases such as herding and loss aversion. Practise real-return and compounding calculations, and be able to identify which risks can and cannot be diversified away.

Investment Landscape questions

Question 1

A bank deposit earns 9% a year while inflation is 5%. Using the exact (Fisher) method, the real rate of return is closest to:

  1. 4.00%
  2. 3.81%
  3. 14.45%
  4. 4.20%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 3.81%

Real return = (1 + nominal) / (1 + inflation) − 1 = 1.09 / 1.05 − 1 = 3.81%. Simply subtracting gives 4.00%, a rough approximation that slightly overstates the real return.

Question 2

Which of the following is a financial asset?

  1. Gold jewellery kept in a locker at home
  2. Agricultural land held for farming
  3. A commercial shop let out on rent
  4. Units of a gold exchange traded fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Units of a gold exchange traded fund

A gold ETF unit is a claim on a fund's assets, which makes it a financial asset even though the fund holds gold. Jewellery, land and property are real (physical) assets.

Question 3

An investor buys a small cap fund only because colleagues and relatives have recently bought it. This behaviour is best described as:

  1. Herd mentality
  2. Anchoring
  3. Loss aversion
  4. Mental accounting
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Herd mentality

Herding means following what others do rather than independent analysis. Anchoring is over-reliance on a reference number, and loss aversion is feeling losses more than equivalent gains.

Question 4

A planner temporarily raises equity from the long-term 60% to 70% because valuations look attractive, planning to return to 60% later. This is:

  1. Strategic asset allocation
  2. Rupee cost averaging
  3. Tactical asset allocation
  4. Portfolio rebalancing to target
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Tactical asset allocation

Tactical allocation is a short-term deviation from the strategic mix to exploit market conditions. Strategic allocation is the long-term mix based on goals and risk profile, which the planner intends to restore.

Question 5

A retired person drawing regular income from accumulated savings, with little new saving, is in which stage of the wealth cycle?

  1. Accumulation stage
  2. Transition stage
  3. Inter-generational transfer stage
  4. Distribution (reaping) stage
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Distribution (reaping) stage

In the distribution stage the investor lives off the corpus built earlier. Accumulation is the earning-and-saving phase, and transition is the period just before retirement when goals come close.

Question 6

Which risk cannot be reduced simply by adding more stocks to an equity portfolio, however many are added?

  1. Market (systematic) risk
  2. Company-specific risk
  3. Management risk of one firm
  4. Sector concentration risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Market (systematic) risk

Systematic risk comes from factors like interest rates, inflation or recessions that affect the whole market, so diversification within equities cannot remove it. Company and sector risks fall as holdings are spread.

Question 7

Which asset is generally the least liquid for an individual investor?

  1. Units of a liquid fund
  2. Shares of a large listed company
  3. A residential flat
  4. A savings bank deposit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A residential flat

Selling property takes time, involves negotiation, legal work and transaction costs, and may need a price cut. Liquid fund units, large-cap shares and savings deposits can be converted to cash within a day or two.

Question 8

₹5,00,000 is invested at 8% a year, compounded annually. What will it be worth after 5 years (nearest rupee)?

  1. ₹7,00,000
  2. ₹7,34,664
  3. ₹7,20,000
  4. ₹7,93,437
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹7,34,664

Future value = 5,00,000 × (1.08)^5 = ₹7,34,664. ₹7,00,000 is simple interest for 5 years, while ₹7,93,437 is the compounded value after 6 years.
