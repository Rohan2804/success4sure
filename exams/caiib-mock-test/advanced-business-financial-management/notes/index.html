Advanced Business & Financial Management: CAIIB revision notes

Advanced Business and Financial Management combines strategic management with corporate finance and valuation. Bankers need it to understand borrowers' business models, evaluate projects and assess mergers. Questions include strategy concepts, ratio and cost of capital calculations, leverage measures, valuation methods and merger classification. Formula-based items are common, so practise DuPont, WACC and leverage computations.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Strategic analysis tools

SWOT separates internal factors (strengths, weaknesses) from external ones (opportunities, threats). PESTLE scans political, economic, social, technological, legal and environmental factors. Porter's five forces assess industry attractiveness: rivalry, threat of new entrants, substitutes, bargaining power of buyers and of suppliers.

2. Portfolio and growth strategy

The BCG matrix classifies businesses as stars (high growth, high share), cash cows (low growth, high share), question marks (high growth, low share) and dogs (low growth, low share). Ansoff's matrix covers market penetration, market development, product development and diversification.

3. DuPont analysis

ROE = net profit margin x asset turnover x equity multiplier. A company with 5% margin, asset turnover of 2 and equity multiplier of 2 has ROE of 5% x 2 x 2 = 20%. DuPont shows whether ROE comes from profitability, efficiency or leverage.

4. Cost of capital

Cost of equity can be estimated with CAPM: Ke = Rf + beta x (Rm - Rf). Cost of debt after tax = interest rate x (1 - tax rate). WACC weights each source by its market value proportion. Projects should earn more than WACC to create value.

5. Leverage

Operating leverage (DOL = contribution / EBIT) measures how EBIT responds to sales changes. Financial leverage (DFL = EBIT / EBT) measures how EPS responds to EBIT changes. Combined leverage = DOL x DFL. High combined leverage means high risk for lenders.

6. Capital structure

Capital structure theories include net income and net operating income approaches and Modigliani-Miller. With taxes, debt adds value through the interest tax shield, but bankruptcy costs limit how much debt is optimal. Bankers look at debt-equity and interest coverage limits.

7. Dividend policy

Walter's model says retain earnings if return on investment exceeds cost of equity. Gordon's growth model values shares as D1 / (Ke - g). Modigliani-Miller argue dividend policy is irrelevant in perfect markets. In practice, stable dividends signal confidence.

8. Business valuation

Valuation methods include discounted cash flow (present value of free cash flows), relative valuation using multiples (P/E, EV/EBITDA), and asset-based valuation (net asset value). DCF is sensitive to growth and discount rate assumptions.

9. Mergers and acquisitions

A horizontal merger joins firms in the same line of business at the same stage, such as two banks offering similar services. Vertical mergers join different stages of a supply chain. Conglomerate mergers join unrelated businesses. Synergy, cost savings and market power are common motives; integration risk is the main danger.

10. Working capital and project finance

Working capital management balances liquidity and profitability through inventory, receivables and payables policies. Project finance relies on project cash flows, with appraisal using NPV, IRR and DSCR. Debt service coverage ratio = cash available for debt service / debt service due.

11. Emerging areas

Venture capital funds early-stage businesses in exchange for equity; private equity invests in more mature firms. Start-ups often use convertible instruments. Bankers increasingly lend to new-age businesses with cash-flow based assessment.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • ROE = (net profit / sales) x (sales / assets) x (assets / equity).
  • CAPM: Ke = Rf + beta x (Rm - Rf); after-tax Kd = interest x (1 - t).
  • DOL = contribution / EBIT; DFL = EBIT / EBT; DCL = DOL x DFL.
  • Gordon: P0 = D1 / (Ke - g).

Common traps in the exam

  • In SWOT, strengths and weaknesses are internal; opportunities and threats are external.
  • Two banks merging is horizontal, not conglomerate.
  • DFL uses EBIT / EBT, not EBIT / sales.
  • Cost of debt must be taken after tax when computing WACC.

Quick recap

Strategy: SWOT (internal vs external), PESTLE, Porter's five forces, BCG and Ansoff matrices. Finance: DuPont ROE = margin x turnover x equity multiplier; WACC with CAPM and after-tax debt; DOL, DFL, DCL. Capital structure (MM with taxes) and dividend models (Walter, Gordon, MM). Valuation: DCF, multiples, NAV. M&A: horizontal, vertical, conglomerate. DSCR for project loans.
