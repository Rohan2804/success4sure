Bank Financial Management: CAIIB practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Bank Financial Management, from the CAIIB (Certified Associate of the Indian Institute of Bankers (per paper)) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This paper covers international banking, risk management and balance sheet management. Revise foreign exchange (cross rates, forward premium), trade finance (types of letters of credit, UCP 600), Basel III capital and liquidity norms (CRAR, LCR, NSFR, leverage ratio), interest rate risk measurement (gap and duration gap), and credit risk concepts such as expected loss using PD, LGD and EAD. Numerical questions are common, so practise each formula with simple figures.

Bank Financial Management questions

Question 1

A bank has total capital of ₹1,250 crore and risk-weighted assets of ₹10,000 crore. Its CRAR is:

  1. 8.0%
  2. 10.0%
  3. 12.5%
  4. 11.5%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 12.5%

CRAR = capital / RWA × 100 = 1,250 / 10,000 × 100 = 12.5%. This is compared with the regulatory minimum including buffers.

Question 2

A bank has rate-sensitive assets of ₹800 crore and rate-sensitive liabilities of ₹600 crore. If rates rise by 1%, net interest income will:

  1. Rise by about ₹2 crore
  2. Fall by about ₹2 crore
  3. Rise by about ₹14 crore
  4. Remain unchanged
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Rise by about ₹2 crore

Gap = RSA − RSL = +₹200 crore (positive gap). A 1% rise increases NII by about 200 × 1% = ₹2 crore.

Question 3

If GBP/USD is 1.27 and USD/INR is 85.00, the GBP/INR cross rate is:

  1. 66.93
  2. 107.95
  3. 86.27
  4. 83.73
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 107.95

GBP/INR = GBP/USD × USD/INR = 1.27 × 85.00 = 107.95. Dividing instead of multiplying gives 66.93.

Question 4

A loan exposure of ₹100 crore has a probability of default of 2% and loss given default of 45%. The expected loss is:

  1. ₹2 crore
  2. ₹45 crore
  3. ₹4.5 crore
  4. ₹0.9 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹0.9 crore

Expected loss = PD × LGD × EAD = 0.02 × 0.45 × 100 = ₹0.9 crore. It is covered by pricing and provisions, while unexpected loss is covered by capital.

Question 5

The Net Stable Funding Ratio under Basel III requires that:

  1. Available stable funding is at least 100% of required stable funding
  2. High-quality liquid assets cover net cash outflows over a 30-day stress period
  3. Tier 1 capital is at least 4% of exposure
  4. Banks hold 4% of deposits as CRR
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Available stable funding is at least 100% of required stable funding

NSFR ensures a stable funding profile over a one-year horizon. The 30-day stress test is the LCR, and the 4% exposure measure is the leverage ratio.

Question 6

An exporter receives an LC and uses it as security to have his bank open a second LC in favour of his supplier. The second LC is a:

  1. Revolving LC
  2. Back-to-back LC
  3. Red clause LC
  4. Standby LC
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Back-to-back LC

A back-to-back LC is opened on the strength of an export LC, typically for an intermediary who needs to pay a supplier. A red clause LC allows pre-shipment advances.

Question 7

A bank's assets have a duration of 4 years and its liabilities 2 years, with liabilities at 90% of assets. If rates rise, the bank's equity value will most likely:

  1. Rise
  2. Remain unchanged
  3. Double
  4. Fall
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Fall

Duration gap = 4 − 0.9 × 2 = 2.2 years (positive). With a positive gap, assets lose more value than liabilities when rates rise, so equity falls.

Question 8

Under UCP 600, banks examining documents under an LC must determine compliance on the basis of:

  1. Physical inspection of the goods
  2. The buyer's verbal confirmation
  3. The documents alone, on their face
  4. The seller's credit rating
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The documents alone, on their face

UCP 600 says banks deal with documents, not goods. They check whether documents appear on their face to comply with the credit's terms.
