Advanced Bank Management: CAIIB revision notes

Advanced Bank Management is a compulsory CAIIB paper with four broad areas: statistics, human resource management, credit management and compliance with corporate governance. The statistics and credit modules carry most calculation questions: mean and variance, normal distribution, correlation, time series and working capital assessment. The HRM and governance modules are conceptual. Practise numerical items with small data sets.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Measures of central tendency

Mean is the arithmetic average, median is the middle value when data are ordered, and mode is the most frequent value. For skewed data such as incomes, the median represents the typical value better than the mean. For 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10, mean and median are both 6.

2. Dispersion

Variance measures average squared deviation from the mean; standard deviation is its square root. For 2, 4, 6, 8, 10: deviations squared are 16, 4, 0, 4, 16, total 40, so population variance = 40/5 = 8 and SD is about 2.83. Sample variance divides by n - 1. Coefficient of variation = SD / mean.

3. Normal distribution

The normal curve is symmetric and bell-shaped, with mean, median and mode equal. About 68% of observations lie within one SD of the mean, 95% within two and 99.7% within three. Banks use this logic in value at risk and credit scoring models.

4. Correlation and regression

Correlation (from -1 to +1) measures the strength and direction of a linear relationship. Regression estimates one variable from another, for example deposits from income. Correlation does not prove causation, a favourite trap.

5. Time series

A time series has four components: trend (long-term movement), seasonal (regular within-year patterns like festival deposits each October), cyclical (business cycles over several years) and irregular (random shocks such as a pandemic). Forecasting separates these components.

6. Probability and sampling

Probability ranges from 0 to 1. Independent events multiply probabilities. Sampling methods include simple random, stratified, systematic and cluster sampling. Larger samples reduce sampling error. Banks use sampling in audits and customer surveys.

7. Working capital assessment: turnover method

Under the Nayak Committee approach for MSME borrowers, working capital requirement is taken as 25% of projected annual turnover, of which at least 20% of turnover is financed by the bank and 5% is the borrower's margin. For projected turnover of ₹2 crore, bank finance is at least ₹40 lakh.

8. MPBF methods

The Tandon Committee's first method sets maximum permissible bank finance at 75% of the working capital gap (current assets minus current liabilities other than bank borrowings). The second method sets it at 75% of current assets minus current liabilities, giving a lower figure because the borrower funds 25% of all current assets.

9. Cash budget and other methods

For seasonal industries or borrowers with uneven cash flows, banks use the cash budget method, financing the peak deficit. Large borrowers are assessed on projected balance sheets, with loan component and cash credit split as per RBI's guidelines.

10. Credit monitoring and NPA management

Monitoring uses stock statements, unit visits, account conduct, financial covenants and early warning signals. Stressed accounts are classified as SMA-0, SMA-1 and SMA-2 based on overdue days before becoming NPAs. Resolution options include restructuring, compromise settlements, SARFAESI and IBC.

11. Human resource management

HRM covers manpower planning, recruitment, training, performance appraisal, compensation and employee relations. Motivation theories: Maslow's hierarchy of needs and Herzberg's hygiene and motivator factors. Banks increasingly use competency-based appraisal and digital learning.

12. Compliance and governance

Banks have an independent chief compliance officer, risk-based compliance testing and board committees such as audit, risk management and nomination and remuneration. RBI guidelines govern board composition, tenure of CEOs and directors, and the fit and proper status of directors.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • Population variance = sum of (x - mean)^2 / N; sample variance divides by n - 1.
  • Coefficient of variation = SD / mean x 100.
  • Turnover method: bank finance = 20% of projected turnover; total requirement = 25%.
  • MPBF Method II = 0.75 x current assets - current liabilities (excluding bank borrowings).

Common traps in the exam

  • Population variance divides by N; sample variance divides by n - 1.
  • Recurring festival-time deposit peaks are seasonal, not cyclical.
  • In the turnover method, the 5% margin is from the borrower; bank finance is 20% of turnover.
  • Tandon Method II gives lower MPBF than Method I because the borrower funds 25% of all current assets.

Quick recap

Statistics: mean, median, mode; variance and SD (2,4,6,8,10 gives variance 8); normal curve 68-95-99.7; correlation is not causation; time series has trend, seasonal, cyclical, irregular parts. Credit: turnover method gives 20% bank finance plus 5% margin; Tandon Methods I and II; cash budget for seasonal units; SMA categories before NPA. HRM theories and governance structures.
