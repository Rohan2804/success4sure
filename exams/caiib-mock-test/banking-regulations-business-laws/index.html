Banking Regulations & Business Laws: CAIIB practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Banking Regulations & Business Laws, from the CAIIB (Certified Associate of the Indian Institute of Bankers (per paper)) syllabus.

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This paper covers the laws that govern banks and their business dealings. Revise key provisions of the Banking Regulation Act and RBI Act (licensing, CRR, SLR, RBI powers over management), recovery laws (SARFAESI, DRT, IBC), the Negotiable Instruments Act for cheque dishonour, the Indian Contract Act (guarantee, indemnity, bailment), consumer protection, and evidence of bank records. Know the purpose of each provision and apply it to practical scenarios.

Banking Regulations & Business Laws questions

Question 1

RBI's power to remove a managerial person or director of a banking company in public interest is under:

  1. Section 36AA of the Banking Regulation Act
  2. Section 42 of the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934
  3. Section 13 of SARFAESI Act
  4. Section 138 of the NI Act
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Section 36AA of the Banking Regulation Act

Section 36AA empowers RBI to remove chairmen, directors or other managerial personnel of banking companies where necessary in public interest or to protect depositors.

Question 2

The Cash Reserve Ratio that scheduled banks maintain with RBI is prescribed under:

  1. Section 24 of the Banking Regulation Act
  2. Section 35A of the Banking Regulation Act
  3. Section 42 of the RBI Act, 1934
  4. Section 5 of the Banking Regulation Act
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Section 42 of the RBI Act, 1934

CRR is maintained under Section 42 of the RBI Act. SLR, by contrast, is prescribed under Section 24 of the Banking Regulation Act.

Question 3

A complaint for dishonour of a cheque under Section 138 of the NI Act must be filed within:

  1. Three months of the cheque date
  2. One year of dishonour
  3. Seven days of the notice
  4. One month of the date the cause of action arises
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. One month of the date the cause of action arises

The cause of action arises when the drawer fails to pay within 15 days of receiving notice. The complaint must then be filed within one month, though courts can condone delay.

Question 4

Under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, the District Commission hears complaints where the value of goods or services paid is up to:

  1. ₹1 crore
  2. ₹50 lakh
  3. ₹10 lakh
  4. ₹10 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹50 lakh

Following the 2021 revision of pecuniary limits, District Commissions handle complaints up to ₹50 lakh; State Commissions handle ₹50 lakh to ₹2 crore, and the National Commission above that.

Question 5

Under SARFAESI, a secured creditor facing resistance in taking possession of secured assets can seek assistance from the:

  1. Civil court of the area
  2. Banking Ombudsman
  3. Chief Metropolitan Magistrate or District Magistrate
  4. Registrar of Companies
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Chief Metropolitan Magistrate or District Magistrate

Section 14 of SARFAESI allows a secured creditor to request the CMM or DM to take possession of secured assets and documents and hand them over.

Question 6

A contract of guarantee under the Indian Contract Act involves:

  1. Two parties only: the indemnifier and the indemnity holder, with no third party
  2. Only the creditor and the debtor
  3. Four parties including a trustee
  4. Three parties: creditor, principal debtor and surety
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Three parties: creditor, principal debtor and surety

A guarantee has three parties: the creditor, the principal debtor and the surety. A contract of indemnity has two parties.

Question 7

Under the Bankers' Books Evidence Act, entries in a bank's books can be proved in court by:

  1. Producing the original ledger in court in every single case
  2. Certified copies of the entries
  3. Oral evidence of the branch manager only
  4. The customer's passbook alone
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Certified copies of the entries

The Act allows certified copies of entries in bankers' books to be accepted as prima facie evidence, so banks need not produce original books in court.

Question 8

A bank wants to start insolvency proceedings against a corporate borrower under the IBC. The application is filed before the:

  1. National Company Law Tribunal
  2. Debts Recovery Tribunal
  3. High Court
  4. District Consumer Commission
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. National Company Law Tribunal

Corporate insolvency resolution under the IBC is initiated before the NCLT, which is the adjudicating authority for companies. The DRT handles individual and partnership insolvency.
