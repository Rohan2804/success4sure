Banking Regulations & Business Laws: CAIIB revision notes

Banking Regulations and Business Laws is the fourth compulsory CAIIB paper. It covers the RBI Act and Banking Regulation Act, anti-money laundering rules, recovery laws such as SARFAESI, DRT and IBC, and general business laws including contracts, companies and negotiable instruments. Questions are section and timeline based, so memorise key numbers and confirm any that have been amended recently.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. RBI Act, 1934

The RBI Act constitutes RBI and gives it powers over currency issue, monetary policy, and regulation of NBFCs (Chapter III-B). Section 42 requires scheduled banks to maintain CRR with RBI. Chapter IIIF (Sections 45Z onwards) sets the monetary policy framework, including the inflation target and the MPC.

2. Banking Regulation Act, 1949

Section 5(b) defines banking as accepting deposits from the public for lending or investment, repayable on demand or otherwise and withdrawable by cheque or other means. Section 6 lists permitted businesses. Section 22 requires a licence from RBI to carry on banking business in India. Section 24 prescribes SLR.

3. Supervisory powers

Section 35 empowers RBI to inspect banks; Section 35A to issue directions in public interest; Section 36ACA to supersede the board of a bank. Section 45 allows the central government, on RBI's application, to impose a moratorium and RBI to prepare a scheme of amalgamation or reconstruction.

4. Other BR Act provisions

Section 17 requires banks to transfer at least 20% of profit to a reserve fund before dividends. Section 20 restricts loans to directors and their interested entities. Section 10B requires a managing director or whole-time chairman. Banks must also follow RBI rules on ownership and voting rights.

5. PMLA obligations

Banks are reporting entities under PMLA. Cash transaction reports (above ₹10 lakh) are filed monthly, by the 15th of the next month; suspicious transaction reports within 7 days of the principal officer concluding suspicion; counterfeit currency reports and cross-border wire transfer reports as prescribed. Records are kept for five years.

6. SARFAESI Act, 2002

Secured creditors can enforce security without court intervention when the account is an NPA. Section 13(2) requires a 60-day demand notice; if unpaid, the bank can take possession under Section 13(4) and sell the asset. The Act does not apply to loans below ₹1 lakh or where the outstanding is less than 20% of principal and interest. Borrowers can appeal to the DRT under Section 17 within 45 days.

7. DRT and recovery

The Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act, 1993 created Debt Recovery Tribunals for bank claims of ₹20 lakh and above, with appeals to the Debt Recovery Appellate Tribunal. Lok Adalats help settle smaller dues.

8. Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016

A financial or operational creditor can initiate the corporate insolvency resolution process before the NCLT when default is at least ₹1 crore. CIRP must be completed within 180 days, extendable once by 90 days, and overall within 330 days including litigation. A resolution plan needs approval of at least 66% of the committee of creditors by voting share. A moratorium under Section 14 halts recovery actions.

9. Contract and agency law

The Indian Contract Act governs loan agreements, guarantees (Sections 126 onwards: principal debtor, creditor, surety), indemnity and bailment. A surety's liability is co-extensive with the principal debtor's unless the contract says otherwise. Pledge is a special bailment of goods as security.

10. Companies, partnership and LLP

Bankers must verify borrowing powers of companies under the Companies Act and the articles, register charges with the Registrar of Companies within the time allowed, and know partners' implied authority under the Partnership Act. LLPs are separate legal entities with limited liability.

11. Other laws

The Negotiable Instruments Act governs cheques and Section 138 dishonour; the Consumer Protection Act covers deficiency in service; the IT Act gives legal recognition to electronic records and signatures; the Right to Information Act applies to public sector banks; the DICGC Act insures deposits up to ₹5 lakh.

Formulas and rules of thumb

  • SARFAESI threshold: outstanding at least 20% of principal plus interest, and loan of ₹1 lakh or more.
  • IBC timeline: 180 days + one 90-day extension; outer limit 330 days including litigation.

Common traps in the exam

  • Section 22 of the BR Act is about licensing; Section 5(b) defines banking.
  • SARFAESI notice period is 60 days; the borrower's appeal to DRT is within 45 days.
  • IBC threshold for default is ₹1 crore, raised from ₹1 lakh in 2020.
  • STR is due within 7 days of concluding suspicion; CTR is monthly by the 15th.
  • A surety's liability is co-extensive with the principal debtor's, not secondary by default.

Quick recap

RBI Act: currency, monetary policy, NBFCs, CRR under Section 42. BR Act: 5(b) definition, 6 businesses, 22 licence, 24 SLR, 35A directions, 45 moratorium, 17 reserve fund 20%. PMLA: CTR monthly, STR within 7 days, five-year records. SARFAESI: 60-day notice, possession, DRT appeal in 45 days. DRT for ₹20 lakh+. IBC: ₹1 crore default, 180+90 days, 330 days outer, 66% CoC.
