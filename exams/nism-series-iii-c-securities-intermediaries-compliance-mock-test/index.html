About the NISM Series III-C exam

NISM Series III-C covers compliance for fund-based intermediaries — the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, AIF, REIT and InvIT regulations, insider trading, PMLA and governance duties of trustees and AMCs.

Who should take it: Compliance officers and compliance staff at AMCs, AIF managers, REIT/InvIT managers and other fund intermediaries.

Sample 1 · Introduction to the Financial System

Which of the following is an example of a financial intermediary?

  1. A household buying gold jewellery
  2. A government department collecting taxes
  3. A mutual fund pooling money from investors
  4. A company issuing its own shares directly
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A mutual fund pooling money from investors

Mutual funds pool savings and invest them, linking savers with users of capital.

Sample 2 · Regulatory Framework – General View

Which body regulates banks and non-banking financial companies in India?

  1. IRDAI
  2. PFRDA
  3. Reserve Bank of India
  4. SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Reserve Bank of India

The RBI is the banking regulator and also supervises NBFCs.

Sample 3 · Introduction to Compliance

Which of the following is the best example of compliance risk for an AMC?

  1. A rise in interest rates
  2. Being penalised for failing to follow SEBI's scheme investment restrictions
  3. A competitor launching a similar scheme
  4. A fall in the stock market
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Being penalised for failing to follow SEBI's scheme investment restrictions

Compliance risk is the risk of sanctions or loss from not complying with laws and regulations.

Sample 4 · SEBI Act, 1992

A person aggrieved by an order of a SEBI adjudicating officer may appeal to SAT within:

  1. 7 days
  2. 90 days
  3. 45 days from receipt of the order
  4. One year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 45 days from receipt of the order

Appeals to SAT must be filed within 45 days, extendable if sufficient cause is shown.

Sample 5 · SCRA, 1956 and SCRR, 1957

Under the SCRA, 1956, a company's shares can be traded on a stock exchange only if:

  1. The company is a government company
  2. They are listed on that stock exchange or permitted to trade there
  3. SEBI approves each trade
  4. The company is profitable
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. They are listed on that stock exchange or permitted to trade there

Securities must be listed or permitted to trade on a recognised stock exchange.
