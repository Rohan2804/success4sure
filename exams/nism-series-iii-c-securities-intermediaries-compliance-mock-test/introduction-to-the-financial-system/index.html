Introduction to the Financial System: NISM Series III-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Introduction to the Financial System, from the NISM Series III-C (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Fund)) syllabus.

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how the Indian financial system moves savings from surplus units (households) to deficit units (businesses and government). It covers financial markets (money and capital, primary and secondary), financial instruments (debt, equity, hybrids), financial intermediaries and market infrastructure institutions such as stock exchanges, clearing corporations and depositories. When revising, be clear on the difference between direct and intermediated finance, the role of the secondary market in liquidity and price discovery, the maturity boundary between money and capital markets, and the priority of claims of creditors over shareholders.

Introduction to the Financial System questions

Question 1

A company raises fresh capital by offering its shares to the public for the first time through an IPO. In which market does this transaction take place?

  1. Over-the-counter derivatives market
  2. Secondary market
  3. Primary market
  4. Money market
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Primary market

An IPO is a fresh issue of securities by the company itself, so the money flows to the issuer. That is the primary market. The secondary market only involves trading of already-issued securities between investors.

Question 2

Which of the following is a money market instrument?

  1. Ten-year government dated security
  2. Equity share of a listed company
  3. Unit of an open-ended equity mutual fund scheme
  4. Certificate of deposit issued by a bank
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Certificate of deposit issued by a bank

Money market instruments have original maturities of up to one year and include treasury bills, commercial paper and certificates of deposit. A ten-year G-sec and equity shares are capital market instruments.

Question 3

What is the core economic function of a financial system?

  1. Collecting direct and indirect taxes for the government
  2. Printing currency notes as required by businesses
  3. Channelling savings from surplus units to deficit units
  4. Fixing the prices of goods and services in the economy
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Channelling savings from surplus units to deficit units

A financial system mobilises savings from those with surplus funds and allocates them to those who need funds for investment or consumption. Price-setting, taxation and currency issue are not its core function.

Question 4

A bank accepts one-year fixed deposits from households and uses them to give five-year term loans to small businesses. This is best described as:

  1. Maturity transformation by a financial intermediary
  2. Direct finance raised through the capital market by issuers
  3. Price discovery in the secondary market
  4. Disinvestment of public sector holdings
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Maturity transformation by a financial intermediary

The bank converts short-term deposits into longer-term loans, which is maturity transformation, a key service of intermediaries. Direct finance would involve the business raising money from investors without the bank in between.

Question 5

A large company sells non-convertible debentures directly to investors through a public issue instead of taking a bank loan. This is an example of:

  1. Direct (market-based) finance
  2. Financial repression by the regulator
  3. Money market borrowing by the issuer
  4. Indirect finance through a bank
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Direct (market-based) finance

When the borrower raises funds straight from investors by issuing securities, it is direct or market-based finance. A bank loan would be indirect finance because the bank stands between saver and borrower.

Question 6

Which of the following is the most important benefit that an active secondary market gives to investors?

  1. Protection from all market price risk
  2. Fresh capital for the issuing company
  3. A guaranteed minimum return on every investment made
  4. Liquidity and continuous price discovery
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Liquidity and continuous price discovery

A secondary market lets investors exit by selling to other investors and reveals fair prices through trading. It does not raise new capital for the issuer, nor does it guarantee returns or remove price risk.

Question 7

Which of the following is NOT a market infrastructure institution (MII) in the Indian securities market?

  1. A recognised clearing corporation
  2. An asset management company
  3. A depository
  4. A recognised stock exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. An asset management company

Stock exchanges, clearing corporations and depositories are MIIs because they provide the core trading, settlement and holding infrastructure. An asset management company is a market intermediary that manages mutual fund schemes, not an MII.

Question 8

A company is being wound up. In what order are the following generally paid out of its assets?

  1. Preference and equity shareholders share equally
  2. Secured creditors first, equity shareholders last
  3. All claimants are paid in proportion to holdings
  4. Equity shareholders first, debenture holders last
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Secured creditors first, equity shareholders last

Debt holders have a contractual, prior claim, with secured creditors ranking ahead of unsecured ones. Equity shareholders are residual claimants and receive only what remains after all creditors and preference holders are paid.
