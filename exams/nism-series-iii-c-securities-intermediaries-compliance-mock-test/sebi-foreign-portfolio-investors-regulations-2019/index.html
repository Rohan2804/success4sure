SEBI (Foreign Portfolio Investors) Regulations, 2019: NISM Series III-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SEBI (Foreign Portfolio Investors) Regulations, 2019, from the NISM Series III-C (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the SEBI (Foreign Portfolio Investors) Regulations, 2019, which govern portfolio investment in India by foreign entities. Revise the two categories of FPIs and their eligibility, who cannot register as an FPI, the role of Designated Depository Participants, jurisdictional requirements relating to IOSCO and FATF, the investment limit for a single FPI or investor group and the consequences of breaching it, and the rules on issue of offshore derivative instruments (ODIs). Focus on classification scenarios and the 10% investment limit.

SEBI (Foreign Portfolio Investors) Regulations, 2019 questions

Question 1

Under the SEBI (FPI) Regulations, 2019, foreign portfolio investors are classified into how many categories?

  1. Three
  2. Five
  3. Two
  4. Four
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Two

The 2019 regulations simplified the earlier three-category system into two categories, Category I and Category II, based on the nature and regulation of the investor. Categorisation affects KYC and some investment conditions.

Question 2

A single FPI, together with its investor group, must hold less than what share of the total paid-up equity capital of an Indian company on a fully diluted basis?

  1. 49%
  2. 10%
  3. 24%
  4. 5%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 10%

Investment by a single FPI including its investor group must be below 10% of the company's total paid-up equity capital on a fully diluted basis. If this is breached, the FPI must divest within the permitted time or the holding is reclassified as FDI.

Question 3

An FPI's holding in a listed company crosses the individual investment limit. What are its options under the regulations?

  1. Keep the excess holding indefinitely if the investee company's board does not object
  2. Divest the excess within five trading days or have it reclassified as FDI
  3. Convert the excess shares into a bank fixed deposit
  4. Transfer the excess shares to a resident Indian relative
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Divest the excess within five trading days or have it reclassified as FDI

If the limit is breached, the FPI may divest the excess holding within five trading days of the settlement of the trades or the investment is treated as FDI, subject to FDI conditions. Other options listed are not permitted.

Question 4

Who among the following CANNOT register as a foreign portfolio investor?

  1. A non-resident Indian investing in his individual capacity
  2. A regulated foreign mutual fund
  3. A foreign pension fund that is appropriately regulated in its home country
  4. A foreign central bank
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A non-resident Indian investing in his individual capacity

NRIs and OCIs investing as individuals have their own investment routes and cannot register as FPIs, though they may be constituents of an FPI within limits. Foreign pension funds, central banks and regulated funds can register.

Question 5

Offshore derivative instruments (such as participatory notes) with Indian securities as underlying can be issued only by:

  1. Indian stock brokers to foreign clients
  2. Indian mutual funds through their overseas offices
  3. Category I FPIs, subject to SEBI conditions
  4. Any Category II FPI without restriction
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Category I FPIs, subject to SEBI conditions

ODIs can be issued only by Category I FPIs (other than certain excluded types), subject to KYC, reporting and other conditions. Category II FPIs, Indian brokers and mutual funds are not allowed to issue them.

Question 6

To be eligible for FPI registration, an applicant should generally be a resident of a country whose securities market regulator:

  1. Has been in existence for at least 50 years
  2. Is a member of the Indian Banks' Association and has an office in India
  3. Has a permanent office located in Mumbai
  4. Is a signatory to the IOSCO MMoU or has a bilateral MoU with SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Is a signatory to the IOSCO MMoU or has a bilateral MoU with SEBI

An FPI applicant must be from a jurisdiction whose securities regulator is a signatory to the IOSCO Multilateral MoU or has a bilateral MoU with SEBI, and it must not be from a jurisdiction identified by FATF as high-risk.

Question 7

A Designated Depository Participant (DDP) under the FPI framework is generally:

  1. A custodian or bank that is a depository participant approved by SEBI
  2. A credit rating agency registered with SEBI
  3. A stock exchange or clearing house located in the investor's home country
  4. Any individual chartered accountant in India
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A custodian or bank that is a depository participant approved by SEBI

DDPs are SEBI-approved entities, typically custodians that are also authorised dealer banks and depository participants. They grant FPI registration on SEBI's behalf and carry out KYC and monitoring.

Question 8

A sovereign wealth fund and an unregulated family office both apply for FPI registration. Which is the correct classification?

  1. Sovereign wealth fund as Category II; family office as Category I
  2. Both as Category II
  3. Both as Category I
  4. Sovereign wealth fund as Category I; family office as Category II
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Sovereign wealth fund as Category I; family office as Category II

Government and government-related investors such as sovereign wealth funds fall in Category I. Unregulated family offices and individuals fall in Category II, which carries different KYC requirements.
