SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations: NISM Series III-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, from the NISM Series III-C (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015. Revise the definitions of insider, connected person, generally available information and UPSI, the restrictions on communicating or procuring UPSI and the 'legitimate purposes' exception, trading plans and their approval and disclosure, the trading window, and the code of conduct for designated persons including pre-clearance and the contra trade restriction. Also learn the disclosure regime (initial and continual disclosures), the structured digital database, the Chief Investor Relations Officer under the fair disclosure code, and the consequences of violations.

SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations questions

Question 1

A person is treated as a 'connected person' if, in the company's context, he was associated with it during what period before the concerned act?

  1. Three years
  2. One year
  3. Six months
  4. One month
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Six months

The PIT Regulations treat a person as connected if, during the six months prior to the concerned act, he was associated with the company in a way that gives or may reasonably be expected to give access to UPSI.

Question 2

Which of the following is 'generally available information' under the PIT Regulations?

  1. Draft results shared with the statutory auditor for review
  2. Board discussions on a proposed acquisition that is not yet announced
  3. A merger proposal circulated among the deal team only
  4. Quarterly results disseminated through the stock exchange platform
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Quarterly results disseminated through the stock exchange platform

Generally available information is information accessible to the public on a non-discriminatory basis, such as results published through the exchange. Draft results and unannounced deal discussions are still UPSI.

Question 3

A company plans to share UPSI with a merchant banker for due diligence on a proposed acquisition. Under the regulations, this sharing is permitted only if it is:

  1. Approved verbally by any director of the company
  2. For legitimate purposes, performance of duties or discharge of legal obligations
  3. Communicated only through a private messaging app
  4. Paid for by the merchant banker at a fair price
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. For legitimate purposes, performance of duties or discharge of legal obligations

UPSI may be communicated only in furtherance of legitimate purposes, performance of duties or discharge of legal obligations, and recipients must be bound by confidentiality. Approval by any director or the mode of communication does not make it lawful.

Question 4

An insider wants to trade under a trading plan. Under the PIT Regulations, the trading plan must be:

  1. Approved by the auditor and published in newspapers
  2. Registered with the depository and kept with the DP
  3. Approved by the compliance officer and disclosed to the stock exchanges
  4. Approved by SEBI and kept strictly confidential by the company until expiry
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Approved by the compliance officer and disclosed to the stock exchanges

A trading plan is presented to the compliance officer for approval and, once approved, is notified to the stock exchanges where the securities are listed. SEBI, the auditor and the DP do not approve trading plans.

Question 5

A designated person buys shares of his company. Under the code of conduct, he generally cannot sell them (enter a contra trade) within the next:

  1. One month
  2. Six months
  3. One year
  4. Three months
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Six months

Designated persons must not execute a contra trade within six months of a trade. If one is executed in violation, the profits are liable to be disgorged and remitted to the Investor Protection and Education Fund.

Question 6

A director of a listed company trades its shares worth a total of ₹12 lakh during a calendar quarter. Under continual disclosure, he must inform the company because the value exceeds:

  1. ₹10 lakh in a calendar quarter
  2. ₹5 lakh in a single day
  3. ₹1 crore in a calendar quarter
  4. ₹25 lakh in a financial year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹10 lakh in a calendar quarter

Promoters, directors, KMP and designated persons must disclose trades whose aggregate value exceeds ₹10 lakh in a calendar quarter within two trading days. The company then informs the stock exchanges within two trading days.

Question 7

The board of a listed company must ensure maintenance of which record of persons with whom UPSI is shared?

  1. A newspaper notice listing all such recipients
  2. A physical register signed only by the Chairman
  3. A list of names handed over to the statutory auditor once every financial year
  4. A structured digital database with names and PAN or other identifiers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A structured digital database with names and PAN or other identifiers

The PIT Regulations require a structured digital database containing the nature of UPSI shared and names and PAN (or other identifiers) of persons with whom it is shared, with adequate internal controls such as time-stamping and audit trails.

Question 8

Under the Code of Practices and Procedures for Fair Disclosure, which official deals with universal dissemination and disclosure of UPSI?

  1. Chief Investor Relations Officer
  2. Chief Risk Officer
  3. Registrar and transfer agent
  4. Company's statutory auditor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Chief Investor Relations Officer

Listed companies must designate a senior officer as Chief Investor Relations Officer to deal with dissemination of information and disclosure of UPSI. The RTA and auditor have no role in fair disclosure of UPSI.
