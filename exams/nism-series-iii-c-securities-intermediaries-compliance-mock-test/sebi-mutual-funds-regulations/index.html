SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations: NISM Series III-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, from the NISM Series III-C (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996 and key circulars. Revise the structure of a mutual fund (sponsor, trust, trustees, AMC, custodian, RTA), eligibility and obligations of each party, scheme launch and documents, investment and borrowing restrictions, valuation and NAV, expense rules, unitholder rights including winding up and exit options, and recent reforms such as 'skin in the game', swing pricing, IDCW nomenclature and redemption timelines. Focus on the role and duties of trustees and AMCs, as compliance questions frequently test who is responsible for what.

SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations questions

Question 1

In a mutual fund structure, which entity holds the scheme's securities in safe custody?

  1. The custodian
  2. The registrar and transfer agent
  3. The mutual fund distributor
  4. The sponsor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The custodian

A SEBI-registered custodian holds and safeguards the scheme's securities and handles related settlement and corporate actions. The RTA maintains investor records, and the sponsor sets up the fund but does not hold its assets.

Question 2

Since April 2021, the dividend option in mutual fund schemes is required to be named:

  1. Guaranteed Income Payout Option (GIPO)
  2. Bonus Reinvestment Option (BRO)
  3. Systematic Profit Distribution (SPD)
  4. Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal (IDCW)
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal (IDCW)

SEBI required dividend options to be renamed IDCW so that investors understand that distributions may be paid partly out of their own capital. The other names do not exist in the regulations.

Question 3

Under the MF Regulations, a scheme may be wound up if unitholders pass a resolution to that effect. What proportion of unitholders is required?

  1. 51% of the unitholders
  2. 75% of the unitholders
  3. 90% of the unitholders
  4. 66% of the unitholders
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 75% of the unitholders

A close-ended or open-ended scheme can be wound up if 75% of its unitholders pass a resolution to that effect, among other grounds such as the trustees' opinion or a SEBI direction.

Question 4

All schemes of a mutual fund together cannot own more than what percentage of the paid-up capital carrying voting rights of any single company?

  1. 15%
  2. 5%
  3. 10%
  4. 25%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 10%

The Seventh Schedule of the MF Regulations prohibits a mutual fund, across all its schemes, from owning more than 10% of any company's paid-up capital carrying voting rights. This prevents mutual funds from gaining control of companies.

Question 5

Under the 'skin in the game' framework, what must AMCs do with part of the compensation of their key employees?

  1. Pay it entirely in cash in advance every year
  2. Pay it into a fixed deposit with the sponsor's bank for three years
  3. Pay it in units of the schemes they manage, subject to a lock-in
  4. Pay it into the trustees' personal accounts
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Pay it in units of the schemes they manage, subject to a lock-in

SEBI requires a portion of the compensation of designated AMC employees to be paid in units of the schemes they oversee, locked in for a specified period. This aligns their interests with unitholders.

Question 6

During severe market stress, a debt scheme applies an adjustment that lowers the NAV for redeeming investors so that they bear part of the liquidity cost. This mechanism is called:

  1. Exit load
  2. Swing pricing
  3. Side pocketing
  4. NAV smoothing
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Swing pricing

Swing pricing adjusts NAV to pass on the cost of large redemptions to the investors causing them, protecting remaining unitholders. Side pocketing segregates a stressed security, and an exit load is a fixed charge on redemption.

Question 7

Within how many working days of a valid redemption request must an AMC generally transfer redemption proceeds to the investor?

  1. Fifteen working days
  2. Ten working days
  3. Seven working days
  4. Three working days
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Three working days

SEBI has shortened the redemption timeline so that proceeds must generally be paid within three working days, with limited exceptions for schemes investing significantly overseas or in other specified situations.

Question 8

A mutual fund scheme wants to borrow money. Under the MF Regulations, it may borrow only:

  1. To meet temporary liquidity needs for redemptions or dividend payouts, within limits
  2. To fund the AMC's office expenses
  3. To take leveraged positions to boost returns
  4. To lend money at attractive interest rates to the sponsor's group companies
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. To meet temporary liquidity needs for redemptions or dividend payouts, within limits

Schemes may borrow only to meet temporary liquidity needs for repurchase, redemption or payout, capped at 20% of net assets and for a maximum of six months. Borrowing for leverage or for the AMC or sponsor is not allowed.
