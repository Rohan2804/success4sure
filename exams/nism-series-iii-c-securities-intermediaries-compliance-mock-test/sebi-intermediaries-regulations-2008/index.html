SEBI (Intermediaries) Regulations, 2008: NISM Series III-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SEBI (Intermediaries) Regulations, 2008, from the NISM Series III-C (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

These regulations give a common framework for registration and supervision of SEBI intermediaries such as brokers, depository participants, merchant bankers and portfolio managers. Revise the registration process and validity of the certificate, the 'fit and proper person' criteria and who must satisfy them, the general obligations and code of conduct, appointment of a compliance officer, investor grievance redressal through SCORES, and the procedure for action in case of default, including the designated authority, show cause notices, suspension and cancellation. Learn what an intermediary must do for its clients when registration is cancelled.

SEBI (Intermediaries) Regulations, 2008 questions

Question 1

Under the Intermediaries Regulations, the 'fit and proper person' criteria apply to:

  1. Only employees in the sales and marketing team
  2. Only the statutory auditor appointed by the intermediary
  3. The applicant and its principal officer, directors, promoters and key managerial persons
  4. Only the individual clients who open trading or demat accounts with the intermediary
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The applicant and its principal officer, directors, promoters and key managerial persons

Fit and proper criteria apply to the applicant or intermediary as well as its principal officer, directors, promoters, persons in control and key managerial persons. Clients, auditors or sales staff are not the subject of these criteria.

Question 2

At what stage must an intermediary satisfy the 'fit and proper person' criteria?

  1. Only at the time of applying for initial registration
  2. At the time of registration and on a continuous basis thereafter
  3. Only when SEBI conducts a periodic inspection of the intermediary
  4. Only when the intermediary renews its office lease
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. At the time of registration and on a continuous basis thereafter

Being fit and proper is a continuing requirement, not a one-time check. If a person later ceases to meet the criteria, SEBI can take action against the intermediary.

Question 3

Which online platform does SEBI use for receiving and tracking investor complaints against registered intermediaries?

  1. MCA21
  2. CERSAI
  3. SCORES
  4. e-FRRO
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. SCORES

SCORES (SEBI Complaints Redress System) lets investors lodge complaints online, and intermediaries must respond within the timelines prescribed. MCA21 is the corporate affairs portal and CERSAI maintains CKYC and security interest records.

Question 4

In proceedings for action in case of default, which officer examines the matter and submits a report with recommendations to SEBI?

  1. The designated authority
  2. The statutory auditor
  3. The intermediary's compliance officer
  4. The Securities Appellate Tribunal
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The designated authority

The Intermediaries Regulations provide for a designated authority to issue a show cause notice, consider replies and submit a report recommending action such as suspension or cancellation. SAT only hears appeals after an order is passed.

Question 5

SEBI has cancelled the registration of a stock broker. What is the broker required to do in respect of its clients?

  1. Transfer client securities to its promoters for safe custody
  2. Keep client money until the appeal period is over, without exception
  3. Continue trading for existing clients until their contracts expire
  4. Stop the activity and allow clients to withdraw or transfer their funds and securities
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Stop the activity and allow clients to withdraw or transfer their funds and securities

On cancellation, the intermediary must cease the activity, allow clients to withdraw or transfer their securities and funds, and hand over records as directed. Continuing to trade or moving assets to promoters would be a violation.

Question 6

Under the Intermediaries Regulations, a certificate of registration once granted is generally:

  1. Valid only until the intermediary changes its auditor
  2. Permanent, unless suspended or cancelled, subject to payment of fees
  3. Valid for five years and automatically lapses after that period ends
  4. Valid for one year and renewable every financial year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Permanent, unless suspended or cancelled, subject to payment of fees

The regulations provide for a permanent certificate of registration, subject to payment of applicable fees and continued compliance. It remains valid unless SEBI suspends or cancels it or the intermediary surrenders it.

Question 7

An intermediary wishes to give up its registration voluntarily. What should it ensure before SEBI accepts the surrender?

  1. Pending client obligations and complaints are settled or suitably provided for
  2. Its employees are transferred to another SEBI intermediary
  3. Its shares are listed on a recognised stock exchange first
  4. Its competitors and industry associations are informed about the surrender in writing
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Pending client obligations and complaints are settled or suitably provided for

SEBI accepts surrender after it is satisfied that client interests are protected, for example that pending obligations, assets and complaints are dealt with. Informing competitors or listing shares has no bearing on surrender.

Question 8

Who is generally responsible within an intermediary for monitoring adherence to securities laws and for redressal of investor grievances?

  1. The SEBI regional office in that city
  2. The statutory auditor appointed by the intermediary's shareholders
  3. The largest client of the intermediary
  4. The compliance officer appointed by the intermediary
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The compliance officer appointed by the intermediary

Intermediaries must appoint a compliance officer who monitors compliance with the Act, rules, regulations and circulars and handles investor grievances. The auditor reviews accounts, and SEBI's regional office is the regulator, not an internal officer.
