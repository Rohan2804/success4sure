Depositories Act, 1996: NISM Series III-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Depositories Act, 1996, from the NISM Series III-C (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the Depositories Act, 1996, which enabled holding and transfer of securities in electronic (dematerialised) form. Revise the roles of the depository, depository participant and issuer, the distinction between registered owner and beneficial owner, the investor's option to hold securities in physical or demat form, rematerialisation, pledge and hypothecation of demat securities, and the depository's liability to indemnify investors for losses due to negligence. Also note SEBI's requirement that transfers of listed securities be done only in demat form and the two depositories in India.

Depositories Act, 1996 questions

Question 1

Which are the two depositories currently operating in India?

  1. NSE and BSE
  2. NSCCL and ICCL
  3. CERSAI and CKYCR
  4. NSDL and CDSL
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. NSDL and CDSL

National Securities Depository Ltd and Central Depository Services (India) Ltd are India's two depositories. NSE and BSE are stock exchanges, NSCCL and ICCL are clearing corporations, and CERSAI maintains CKYCR.

Question 2

In the depository system, a depository participant (DP) acts as:

  1. The issuer of the securities held by investors
  2. An agent of the depository providing services to investors
  3. A regulator of the depository on behalf of SEBI
  4. The registered owner of all the securities held in the depository system
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. An agent of the depository providing services to investors

A DP is an agent of the depository through which investors open demat accounts and access depository services. The depository is the registered owner, and SEBI, not the DP, regulates depositories.

Question 3

In the books of the issuer, the depository is shown as the registered owner of demat securities. Which rights does the depository have in this capacity?

  1. The right to receive dividends on the securities
  2. The right to sell the securities to recover fees
  3. Only the right to effect transfers, without voting or economic rights
  4. Full voting rights at all general meetings and the right to appoint directors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Only the right to effect transfers, without voting or economic rights

Under the Act, the depository is deemed registered owner only for effecting transfer of ownership. It has no voting or other rights; all rights and benefits belong to the beneficial owner.

Question 4

An investor wishes to convert securities held in his demat account back into physical certificates. This process is called:

  1. Transmission
  2. Dematerialisation
  3. Rematerialisation
  4. Transposition
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Rematerialisation

Converting electronic holdings back to physical form is rematerialisation, the opposite of dematerialisation. Transmission is transfer on death, and transposition is a change in the order of joint holders' names.

Question 5

An investor wants to pledge shares held in demat form as security for a loan. How must the pledge be created?

  1. By signing a transfer deed in favour of the lender
  2. By handing over the delivery instruction slip book to the lender
  3. By informing the company's auditor in writing
  4. Through the depository system, with an entry recorded by the depository
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Through the depository system, with an entry recorded by the depository

Section 12 of the Depositories Act requires a pledge or hypothecation of demat securities to be recorded with the depository on intimation through the participant. Handing over slips or signing a transfer deed does not create a valid pledge.

Question 6

An investor suffers a loss because of negligence by his depository participant. Under the Depositories Act, who must indemnify him?

  1. The depository, which may then recover from the participant
  2. SEBI out of its own funds
  3. The issuer company whose shares were affected
  4. The stock exchange where the shares are listed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The depository, which may then recover from the participant

Section 16 makes the depository liable to indemnify the beneficial owner for losses caused by negligence of the depository or its participant. The depository can in turn seek recovery from the participant at fault.

Question 7

Since April 2019, SEBI requires transfers of securities of listed companies to be done:

  1. In either form at the investor's choice without restriction
  2. Only in demat form, except transmission or transposition
  3. Only through the company's registered office
  4. Only in physical form with a signed transfer deed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Only in demat form, except transmission or transposition

SEBI amended listing norms so that, from 1 April 2019, transfer of listed securities can be effected only in dematerialised form. Requests for transmission or transposition are an exception.

Question 8

Under the Depositories Act, an investor subscribing to securities of an issuer has the option to:

  1. Receive them in physical form or hold them through a depository
  2. Hold them only in the name of the depository participant chosen by the issuer
  3. Hold them only in physical form in all cases
  4. Hold them only with the registrar and transfer agent
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Receive them in physical form or hold them through a depository

Section 8 of the Act gives the investor the option to hold securities either in physical form or with a depository. However, separate SEBI rules now mandate demat for certain issues and for transfers of listed securities.
