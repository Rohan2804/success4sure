SEBI (InvIT) Regulations: NISM Series III-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SEBI (InvIT) Regulations, from the NISM Series III-C (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the SEBI (Infrastructure Investment Trusts) Regulations, 2014. Understand the InvIT structure as a trust holding infrastructure assets directly or through holding companies and SPVs, and the roles of the sponsor, investment manager, project manager and trustee. Revise investment conditions, holding requirements in SPVs, borrowing limits and the conditions for higher leverage, distribution of cash flows, listing of units, valuation, sponsor holding and lock-in, and unitholder rights. Questions often test which party is responsible for a function and what conditions apply before an action can be taken.

SEBI (InvIT) Regulations questions

Question 1

Under the InvIT Regulations, an InvIT is set up as:

  1. A private limited company incorporated under the Companies Act, 2013
  2. A trust registered under the Indian Trusts Act, 1882 and with SEBI
  3. A limited liability partnership under the LLP Act
  4. A cooperative society under State law
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A trust registered under the Indian Trusts Act, 1882 and with SEBI

An InvIT is created as a trust under the Indian Trusts Act, 1882, with a registered trust deed, and must be registered with SEBI. It is not a company, LLP or cooperative.

Question 2

In an InvIT, which party is responsible for executing and operating the infrastructure project as per the agreement?

  1. The project manager
  2. The valuer
  3. The trustee
  4. The unitholders
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The project manager

The project manager executes and operates the infrastructure projects or achieves their completion as per the project implementation agreement. The trustee oversees the InvIT for unitholders, and the investment manager handles investment decisions.

Question 3

Units of an InvIT offered to the public are required to be:

  1. Transferred only with the trustee's prior approval
  2. Held only in physical form by unitholders
  3. Listed on a recognised stock exchange
  4. Redeemable on demand at NAV like an open-ended fund
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Listed on a recognised stock exchange

Units of publicly offered InvITs must be listed on a recognised stock exchange so investors can trade them. InvIT units are not redeemable on demand like open-ended mutual fund units.

Question 4

Where an InvIT invests in an SPV through a holding company, the InvIT (directly or through the holdco) must generally hold at least what share of the SPV's equity?

  1. 100%
  2. 26%
  3. 51%
  4. 40%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 51%

The InvIT Regulations require the InvIT, directly or through a holdco, to hold a controlling interest and at least 51% of the equity share capital or interest in the SPV, except where restricted by law or concession terms.

Question 5

An InvIT wants its aggregate consolidated borrowings to exceed 25% of the value of its assets. Which conditions apply?

  1. No conditions apply, as InvITs can borrow without any limit
  2. Obtain a credit rating and approval of unitholders
  3. Approval of only the project manager
  4. Prior approval of RBI for every borrowing
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Obtain a credit rating and approval of unitholders

Borrowings beyond 25% of asset value require a credit rating and approval of unitholders, and higher levels are subject to additional conditions such as a strong rating and distribution track record. Borrowing is not unlimited.

Question 6

What is the main purpose of the requirement that the sponsor of an InvIT holds a minimum unitholding for a lock-in period after listing?

  1. To exempt the InvIT from distribution requirements
  2. To prevent unitholders from ever selling their units
  3. To allow the sponsor to control all of the trustee's decisions on distributions
  4. To keep the sponsor's 'skin in the game' and align it with investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. To keep the sponsor's 'skin in the game' and align it with investors

The sponsor's minimum holding and lock-in ensure that it remains committed to the assets it transferred, aligning its interests with unitholders. It does not restrict trading by other unitholders or override distribution norms.

Question 7

Which entity in an InvIT structure makes investment decisions and oversees the activities of the project manager?

  1. The stock exchange
  2. The sponsor
  3. The registrar to the issue
  4. The investment manager
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The investment manager

The investment manager takes investment decisions for the InvIT and is responsible for overseeing the project manager's performance. The sponsor sets up the InvIT, but day-to-day management sits with the investment manager.

Question 8

The trustee of an InvIT is primarily responsible for:

  1. Overseeing that the InvIT and its manager act in unitholders' interest
  2. Providing the bulk of the InvIT's borrowing
  3. Operating and maintaining the toll roads or power lines
  4. Setting the daily trading price of the InvIT's units on the stock exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Overseeing that the InvIT and its manager act in unitholders' interest

The trustee holds the InvIT assets in trust for unitholders and oversees the activities of the investment manager and others to ensure compliance and protect unitholders. Operations are with the project manager.
