SEBI (AIF) Regulations: NISM Series III-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SEBI (AIF) Regulations, from the NISM Series III-C (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the SEBI (Alternative Investment Funds) Regulations, 2012. Revise the three categories of AIFs and their sub-types, minimum corpus and investment amounts, limits on number of investors, continuing interest of the sponsor or manager, tenure and close-ended structure, leverage rules, investment concentration limits, accredited investors, filing of the private placement memorandum through a merchant banker, and restrictions on investing in associates. Practise classification of different fund strategies and calculations of the sponsor's continuing interest.

SEBI (AIF) Regulations questions

Question 1

A private equity fund that does not use leverage except for day-to-day operational needs and does not receive government incentives is usually registered as a:

  1. Category I AIF
  2. Category II AIF
  3. Venture capital undertaking
  4. Category III AIF
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Category II AIF

Category II covers AIFs such as private equity and debt funds that are not in Category I or III and do not use leverage other than for operational requirements. Category I is for funds with positive spillovers, and Category III for complex trading strategies.

Question 2

A Category II AIF scheme has a corpus of ₹300 crore. What is the minimum continuing interest the manager or sponsor must hold in the scheme?

  1. ₹5 crore
  2. ₹15 crore
  3. ₹10 crore
  4. ₹7.5 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹5 crore

For Category I and II AIFs, the continuing interest is 2.5% of the corpus or ₹5 crore, whichever is lower. Here 2.5% of ₹300 crore is ₹7.5 crore, so the lower figure of ₹5 crore applies.

Question 3

Which of the following statements about the structure of AIFs is correct?

  1. Category I AIFs may be open-ended with daily NAV, but Category III AIFs must always be close-ended
  2. Only Category II AIFs may be close-ended
  3. All AIFs must be open-ended with daily NAV
  4. Category I and II AIFs must be close-ended; Category III may be open-ended or close-ended
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Category I and II AIFs must be close-ended; Category III may be open-ended or close-ended

Category I and II AIFs must be close-ended with a minimum tenure of three years. Category III AIFs, which use trading strategies, can be either open-ended or close-ended.

Question 4

What is the minimum corpus required for each scheme of an AIF (other than an angel fund)?

  1. ₹20 crore
  2. ₹100 crore
  3. ₹5 crore
  4. ₹50 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹20 crore

Each AIF scheme, other than an angel fund, must have a corpus of at least ₹20 crore. This is different from the minimum investment per investor, which is generally ₹1 crore.

Question 5

A Special Situation Fund that invests in stressed loans and other special situation assets falls in which AIF category?

  1. It is not an AIF
  2. Category III
  3. Category II
  4. Category I
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Category I

SEBI introduced Special Situation Funds in 2022 as a sub-category of Category I AIFs to invest in stressed assets, including loans acquired under the relevant frameworks.

Question 6

Under the AIF Regulations, the private placement memorandum of an AIF scheme must be filed with SEBI through:

  1. The fund's statutory auditor
  2. A stock broker registered with an exchange
  3. A SEBI-registered merchant banker
  4. A credit rating agency
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A SEBI-registered merchant banker

SEBI requires the PPM of AIF schemes to be filed through a merchant banker, who carries out due diligence on the disclosures. Brokers, auditors and rating agencies do not perform this role.

Question 7

Under the AIF framework, which body issues the certificate that allows an investor to be treated as an 'accredited investor'?

  1. The Income Tax Department
  2. An accreditation agency recognised by SEBI
  3. The branch of the bank where the investor holds a savings account
  4. The AIF manager itself
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. An accreditation agency recognised by SEBI

Accreditation is granted by accreditation agencies, which are subsidiaries of depositories or stock exchanges recognised for this purpose, after verifying income or net worth. The fund manager cannot certify its own investors.

Question 8

A Category II AIF wants to invest in a company that is an associate of its manager. Under the AIF Regulations, such investment generally requires:

  1. Approval of the stock exchange where the units of the AIF scheme are listed
  2. Approval only of the manager's board of directors
  3. Approval of at least 75% of the investors by value of their investment
  4. No approval, since associates are always permitted
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Approval of at least 75% of the investors by value of their investment

Category I and II AIFs may invest in associates only with the approval of 75% of investors by value of their investment in the scheme, to address conflicts of interest. Manager approval alone is not sufficient.
