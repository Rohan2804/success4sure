SEBI Act, 1992: NISM Series III-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SEBI Act, 1992, from the NISM Series III-C (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the SEBI Act, 1992, which gave SEBI statutory status to protect investors and to develop and regulate the securities market. Revise SEBI's composition, its functions and powers under Sections 11, 11B and 11C, the requirement for intermediaries to register under Section 12, and the penalty framework under Chapter VI-A imposed by adjudicating officers. Also learn the settlement mechanism, the appellate route to the Securities Appellate Tribunal and then the Supreme Court, and where penalty amounts are credited. Questions often test who exercises a power and the appeal time limits.

SEBI Act, 1992 questions

Question 1

SEBI was set up in 1988 as a non-statutory body. In which year did it get statutory powers through an Act of Parliament?

  1. 1996
  2. 1992
  3. 1988
  4. 2002
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 1992

SEBI was constituted in 1988 by a government resolution and acquired statutory status under the SEBI Act, 1992. 1996 is the year of the Depositories Act, and 2002 saw major amendments to the SEBI Act.

Question 2

A firm wants to start business as a portfolio manager. Under the SEBI Act, what must it do before commencing activity?

  1. Obtain a certificate of registration from SEBI
  2. Register only with the Registrar of Companies
  3. Obtain a licence from the Reserve Bank of India
  4. Seek approval from a recognised stock exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Obtain a certificate of registration from SEBI

Section 12 of the SEBI Act prohibits any intermediary such as a portfolio manager, broker or investment adviser from dealing in securities without a certificate of registration from SEBI. ROC incorporation alone is not sufficient.

Question 3

One member of the SEBI board is nominated from which of the following institutions?

  1. Reserve Bank of India
  2. Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India
  3. Comptroller and Auditor General of India
  4. National Stock Exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Reserve Bank of India

The SEBI board includes a Chairman, two members from the Central Government ministries, one member from RBI and five other members. No member is nominated from IRDAI, a stock exchange or the CAG.

Question 4

SEBI wants to direct a mutual fund to refund money to investors to protect their interests. Under which provision does SEBI generally issue such directions?

  1. Section 12 of the SEBI Act
  2. Section 15Z of the SEBI Act
  3. Section 26 of the SEBI Act
  4. Section 11B of the SEBI Act
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Section 11B of the SEBI Act

Section 11B empowers SEBI to issue directions to any person or intermediary in the interest of investors or orderly development of the market. Section 12 deals with registration and Section 15Z with appeals to the Supreme Court.

Question 5

A broker is aggrieved by an order of the Securities Appellate Tribunal. Within how many days of communication of the order may it appeal to the Supreme Court?

  1. 30 days
  2. 60 days
  3. 45 days
  4. 90 days
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 60 days

Under Section 15Z, an appeal against a SAT decision lies to the Supreme Court within 60 days, extendable by up to another 60 days for sufficient cause. The 45-day limit applies to appeals from SEBI orders to SAT.

Question 6

A registered intermediary facing SEBI proceedings wants to resolve the matter without admitting or denying the findings. Which route can it use?

  1. Request the stock exchange to withdraw the case
  2. Surrender its registration to end the proceedings
  3. File a writ petition before the Securities Appellate Tribunal immediately
  4. Apply for settlement under the SEBI settlement proceedings framework
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Apply for settlement under the SEBI settlement proceedings framework

SEBI's settlement framework allows a noticee to settle administrative and civil proceedings by paying a settlement amount, without admission or denial of guilt. Surrendering registration does not automatically end proceedings already initiated.

Question 7

Under Chapter VI-A of the SEBI Act, monetary penalties for specified defaults are imposed by:

  1. The Securities Appellate Tribunal on its own
  2. The Central Government through a notification
  3. An adjudicating officer appointed by SEBI
  4. The stock exchange where the default occurred
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. An adjudicating officer appointed by SEBI

SEBI appoints adjudicating officers to hold inquiries and impose penalties under Chapter VI-A. SAT is an appellate body, and stock exchanges act under their own bye-laws, not Chapter VI-A.

Question 8

Penalty amounts realised by SEBI under the SEBI Act are credited to:

  1. The investor's demat account directly
  2. SEBI's own General Fund for salaries
  3. The Consolidated Fund of India
  4. The Settlement Guarantee Fund of the exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The Consolidated Fund of India

Section 15JA requires sums realised by way of penalties to be credited to the Consolidated Fund of India. Disgorged amounts are handled separately, for example through the Investor Protection and Education Fund.
