Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002: NISM Series III-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002, from the NISM Series III-C (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains the PMLA, 2002, the PML Rules and SEBI's AML/CFT guidelines applicable to intermediaries such as AMCs, brokers and depository participants. Learn the stages of money laundering (placement, layering, integration), the role of FIU-IND, client due diligence including beneficial ownership thresholds and enhanced due diligence for high-risk clients such as politically exposed persons, the duties of the Principal Officer and Designated Director, reporting obligations (CTR, STR) and their timelines, record retention, and the ban on tipping off. Scenario questions usually ask how to treat an unusual transaction pattern.

Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 questions

Question 1

In the money laundering process, moving illegal funds through a series of complex transactions to hide their source is known as:

  1. Placement
  2. Structuring
  3. Integration
  4. Layering
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Layering

Placement introduces illicit cash into the financial system, layering obscures its trail through multiple transactions, and integration brings it back as apparently legitimate money. Structuring is breaking amounts to avoid reporting thresholds.

Question 2

Once a reporting entity is satisfied that a transaction is suspicious, within what time must it file a Suspicious Transaction Report with FIU-IND?

  1. 24 hours
  2. 7 working days
  3. 30 days
  4. 15 days
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 7 working days

Under the PML Rules, an STR must be furnished within seven working days of the Principal Officer arriving at a conclusion that the transaction is suspicious. Delay in reaching that conclusion should also be avoided.

Question 3

Under the PMLA, records of transactions and client identification must generally be maintained for at least:

  1. Three years
  2. Ten years
  3. Five years
  4. One year
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Five years

Section 12 of the PMLA requires reporting entities to maintain transaction records and identity records for five years from the date of transaction or end of the business relationship, as applicable. Longer retention may apply if investigations are pending.

Question 4

Under the PML Rules, for a client that is a company, a natural person is a beneficial owner if he has a controlling ownership interest of more than:

  1. 10% of shares, capital or profits
  2. 5% of shares, capital or profits
  3. 51% of shares, capital or profits
  4. 25% of shares, capital or profits
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 10% of shares, capital or profits

Following the 2023 amendment to the PML Rules, the threshold for a company is ownership or entitlement of more than 10% of shares, capital or profits, or control through other means. The earlier threshold was 25%.

Question 5

FIU-IND, the central agency for receiving and analysing financial intelligence, functions under:

  1. Securities and Exchange Board of India
  2. Ministry of Home Affairs
  3. Reserve Bank of India
  4. Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance

The Financial Intelligence Unit – India is an independent body reporting to the Economic Intelligence Council and functions under the Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance. It is not part of SEBI or RBI.

Question 6

An AMC has filed an STR on a client. The client calls the AMC asking whether any report has been made about him. What should the staff do?

  1. Withdraw the STR if the client gives an explanation
  2. Confirm the report so the client can explain the transactions
  3. Not disclose the STR, as tipping off the client is prohibited
  4. Send the client a copy of the STR for his records
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Not disclose the STR, as tipping off the client is prohibited

Reporting entities and their staff must keep STRs strictly confidential and must not tip off the client. The STR, once filed, is not withdrawn because the client offers an explanation.

Question 7

A wealthy client who is a serving senior government official abroad wants to open a mutual fund folio. Under SEBI's AML guidelines, the intermediary should:

  1. Apply enhanced due diligence and obtain senior management approval
  2. Refuse to open the account automatically, whatever the source of funds or documents
  3. Open the account immediately without additional checks
  4. Accept the account only if the investment is in cash
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Apply enhanced due diligence and obtain senior management approval

Politically exposed persons are high-risk clients; intermediaries must apply enhanced due diligence, verify source of funds and obtain senior management approval. An automatic refusal is not required.

Question 8

Under the PML Rules, the person designated by a reporting entity to ensure overall compliance with PMLA obligations, usually a whole-time director, is the:

  1. Nodal Investor Officer
  2. Designated Director
  3. Compliance Auditor
  4. Principal Officer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Designated Director

The Designated Director ensures overall compliance with PMLA obligations and is typically the managing or a whole-time director. The Principal Officer is responsible for furnishing information and reports to FIU-IND.
