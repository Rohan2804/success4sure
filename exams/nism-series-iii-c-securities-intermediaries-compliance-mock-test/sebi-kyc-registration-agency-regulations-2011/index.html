SEBI (KYC Registration Agency) Regulations, 2011: NISM Series III-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SEBI (KYC Registration Agency) Regulations, 2011, from the NISM Series III-C (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the SEBI (KYC Registration Agency) Regulations, 2011 and the KYC framework for the securities market. Understand why KRAs were created (one-time KYC that can be used across intermediaries), the obligations of intermediaries to perform KYC and upload records, the obligations of KRAs to maintain, validate and protect data, interoperability between KRAs, and the link with the Central KYC Records Registry maintained by CERSAI. Revise the newer requirement for KRAs to validate key KYC attributes and the treatment of records whose details cannot be verified.

SEBI (KYC Registration Agency) Regulations, 2011 questions

Question 1

What is the main purpose of setting up KYC Registration Agencies in the securities market?

  1. To collect brokerage and other charges on behalf of all stock brokers
  2. To let a client complete KYC once and use it across intermediaries
  3. To issue PAN cards to new investors
  4. To settle trades between clearing members
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. To let a client complete KYC once and use it across intermediaries

KRAs centralise KYC records so that an investor who has completed KYC with one SEBI intermediary need not repeat the full process with others. Issuing PAN or settling trades is not their function.

Question 2

After completing the KYC of a new client, who is responsible for uploading the KYC details to the KRA system?

  1. The client himself through the stock exchange
  2. The depository with which the client later opens a demat account
  3. The SEBI-registered intermediary that performed the KYC
  4. SEBI's regional office in the client's state
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The SEBI-registered intermediary that performed the KYC

The intermediary that performs initial KYC, including verification, must upload the details and documents to the KRA within the prescribed time. The client and SEBI do not upload KYC records themselves.

Question 3

An investor who is already KYC-compliant on a KRA opens an account with a new broker. What can the broker generally do?

  1. Open the account without seeing any KYC record at all
  2. Refuse the account because KYC was done by another intermediary
  3. Insist on fresh in-person verification and a full set of new documents in all cases
  4. Fetch and rely on the KRA record, doing additional checks only where needed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Fetch and rely on the KRA record, doing additional checks only where needed

The KRA framework allows a new intermediary to download and rely on the existing KYC record, carrying out additional due diligence if its own risk policy requires. Repeating full KYC every time defeats the purpose of KRAs.

Question 4

Under the KRA framework, what happens when key attributes of a client's KYC record (such as PAN, address, mobile number or email) cannot be validated?

  1. The intermediary is fined automatically by the stock exchange for the error
  2. The record is permanently deleted without any notice
  3. The client's securities are automatically sold by the KRA
  4. The record may be placed 'on hold' until the attributes are verified
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The record may be placed 'on hold' until the attributes are verified

KRAs now validate key KYC attributes against official sources. Records that fail validation may be marked 'on hold', and the client must update or re-verify details before transacting. No automatic sale or deletion takes place.

Question 5

Which statement about interoperability among KRAs is correct?

  1. An intermediary can access a client's KYC record through any KRA
  2. A client must complete KYC with each KRA separately
  3. Each intermediary must register with every KRA separately for each client
  4. Only the KRA that first stored the record can ever view it
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. An intermediary can access a client's KYC record through any KRA

KRAs are required to be interoperable, so a KYC record uploaded to one KRA can be accessed by intermediaries connected to another KRA. The client does not need separate KYC for each KRA.

Question 6

A KRA wants to use the KYC data in its system to sell targeted marketing lists to a third party. Under the KRA Regulations, this is:

  1. Not permitted, as KRAs must maintain confidentiality of KYC data
  2. Permitted after informing the stock exchanges
  3. Permitted if the third party buying the data is a SEBI-registered intermediary
  4. Permitted if the data is more than five years old
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Not permitted, as KRAs must maintain confidentiality of KYC data

KRAs must keep KYC records confidential and use them only for the purposes permitted under the regulations. Selling client data for marketing breaches these obligations, regardless of the buyer.

Question 7

The Central KYC Records Registry (CKYCR) assigns each customer a KYC Identification Number (KIN) of how many digits?

  1. 16
  2. 14
  3. 12
  4. 10
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 14

CKYCR, maintained by CERSAI, issues a 14-digit KYC Identification Number that the customer can quote to other regulated entities. A 10-character code is a PAN and a 12-digit number is Aadhaar.

Question 8

Which of the following is a duty of an intermediary under the KRA Regulations?

  1. Prescribe the KYC forms and formats to be used across the entire securities market
  2. Approve the registration of other KRAs
  3. Carry out initial KYC and in-person verification before uploading
  4. Audit the KRA's systems every month
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Carry out initial KYC and in-person verification before uploading

The intermediary performs initial KYC, including in-person verification where required, and uploads the record. SEBI registers KRAs and prescribes KYC formats, while system audits of KRAs are not the intermediary's job.
