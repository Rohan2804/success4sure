SEBI (REIT) Regulations: NISM Series III-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SEBI (REIT) Regulations, from the NISM Series III-C (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the SEBI (Real Estate Investment Trusts) Regulations, 2014, including the 2024 framework for Small and Medium REITs. Learn the parties to a REIT (sponsor, manager, trustee, valuer), the trust structure, investment conditions focused on completed rent-generating properties in India, listing of units, related party transactions, valuation and independence of the valuer, distributions, and unitholder rights such as nominating a director on the manager's board. Revise what makes REITs attractive to retail investors and the safeguards that protect them.

SEBI (REIT) Regulations questions

Question 1

Which party is responsible for managing a REIT's assets and investments and for day-to-day operations?

  1. The manager
  2. The auditor
  3. The valuer
  4. The trustee
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The manager

The manager of a REIT makes investment decisions and manages the real estate assets, either directly or through property managers. The trustee oversees on behalf of unitholders, and the valuer only values assets.

Question 2

For a retail investor, which is a key benefit of investing in a listed REIT?

  1. Freedom from all market and interest rate risks
  2. Direct ownership of a specific office floor in the building that the investor can occupy personally
  3. A guaranteed fixed return set by SEBI each year
  4. Exposure to income-generating commercial real estate with small amounts and exchange liquidity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Exposure to income-generating commercial real estate with small amounts and exchange liquidity

REITs let investors own a share of a diversified portfolio of rent-generating properties with small ticket sizes and the ability to trade on exchanges. Returns are not guaranteed, and unitholders do not own specific floors.

Question 3

Under the REIT Regulations, a REIT may invest in real estate assets located:

  1. Anywhere in the world without restriction
  2. Only in Special Economic Zones abroad
  3. Only in India
  4. In India and any FATF member country
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Only in India

REITs registered with SEBI can invest only in real estate and related assets located in India. Overseas property investment is not permitted under the regulations.

Question 4

Under the framework introduced in 2024, a Small and Medium REIT (SM REIT) is meant for assets of value in the range of:

  1. ₹5 crore to ₹25 crore
  2. ₹50 crore to less than ₹500 crore
  3. Above ₹5,000 crore only
  4. ₹500 crore to below ₹1,000 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹50 crore to less than ₹500 crore

SEBI created SM REITs in 2024 for asset sizes from ₹50 crore up to less than ₹500 crore, bringing fractional ownership platforms under regulation. Larger portfolios continue under the main REIT framework.

Question 5

A REIT plans to buy a property from its sponsor. How should this related party transaction be handled?

  1. At arm's length, with valuation and disclosure, and unitholder approval where required
  2. Only with the oral consent of the sponsor's chairman
  3. Only after delisting the REIT units from the exchange and seeking approval of the sponsor's lenders
  4. Without any valuation since the sponsor is trusted
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. At arm's length, with valuation and disclosure, and unitholder approval where required

Related party transactions must be on an arm's length basis, in the unitholders' best interest, consistent with REIT strategy, supported by valuation reports and disclosed, with unitholder approval above specified thresholds.

Question 6

Under the REIT Regulations, a valuer cannot value the same property for more than how many consecutive years?

  1. Ten years
  2. Six years
  3. Four years
  4. Two years
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Four years

To preserve independence, a valuer may not undertake valuation of the same property for more than four consecutive years, and can be reappointed only after a gap. The valuer also must not be an associate of the sponsor, manager or trustee.

Question 7

Unitholders holding at least what percentage of a REIT's units have the right to nominate one director on the board of the manager?

  1. 1%
  2. 10%
  3. 26%
  4. 51%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 10%

SEBI's 2023 amendment gave eligible unitholders holding 10% or more of the total outstanding units the right to nominate one non-independent director on the manager's board, improving governance.

Question 8

Units of a REIT that are offered to the public must be:

  1. Held by only one institutional investor
  2. Issued only in physical certificate form
  3. Redeemable daily at NAV by the manager
  4. Listed on a recognised stock exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Listed on a recognised stock exchange

Publicly offered REIT units are mandatorily listed on a recognised stock exchange, which provides trading liquidity. REIT units are not redeemed daily like open-ended mutual fund units and are held in demat form.
