Introduction to Compliance: NISM Series III-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Introduction to Compliance, from the NISM Series III-C (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces compliance as a function that helps a regulated entity follow laws, regulations, internal policies and codes of conduct. Understand compliance risk (the risk of legal or regulatory sanctions, financial loss or loss of reputation), the importance of an independent compliance function with direct access to the board, the 'tone at the top', and the three lines of defence model. Revise how compliance policies, training, monitoring, testing and escalation work together, and the difference between preventive and detective controls. Expect scenario questions on what a compliance officer should do when a breach is found.

Introduction to Compliance questions

Question 1

Compliance risk is best described as the risk of:

  1. Legal or regulatory sanctions, financial loss or reputational damage from not following rules
  2. Loss due to changes in the market price of securities held in a portfolio
  3. Loss caused by a sudden lack of buyers for an asset being sold
  4. Default by a borrower or counterparty on its contractual obligations
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Legal or regulatory sanctions, financial loss or reputational damage from not following rules

Compliance risk arises from failing to comply with laws, regulations, codes and internal standards, leading to sanctions, financial loss or reputational harm. Market, credit and liquidity risk are separate risk types.

Question 2

Why should the compliance function of a fund intermediary be independent of its business teams?

  1. So that compliance staff can earn incentives linked to sales volumes
  2. So that compliance does not need to report to the board at all
  3. So it can assess and report breaches objectively without pressure from revenue targets
  4. So that business heads can review and approve all compliance reports before they are submitted
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. So it can assess and report breaches objectively without pressure from revenue targets

Independence lets compliance staff challenge business decisions and escalate breaches without conflict. Linking compliance pay to sales or routing reports through business heads would undermine that objectivity.

Question 3

In the 'three lines of defence' model, which function is generally regarded as the third line?

  1. Compliance and risk management
  2. Internal audit
  3. Business and operations teams
  4. External statutory auditors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Internal audit

Business units are the first line, compliance and risk management form the second line, and internal audit provides independent assurance as the third line. Statutory auditors are external to the model.

Question 4

Ultimate responsibility for ensuring that an asset management company complies with regulations rests with:

  1. The registrar and transfer agent
  2. The mutual fund distributors empanelled by the AMC
  3. Its board of directors and senior management
  4. Only the compliance officer personally
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Its board of directors and senior management

The compliance officer monitors and advises, but accountability for compliance lies with the board and senior management, who set the 'tone at the top'. Distributors and RTAs have their own obligations but do not bear the AMC's overall responsibility.

Question 5

A compliance officer finds that a scheme breached an investment limit. The business head asks her not to record it because the position has since been corrected. What should she do?

  1. Delete the record since the position is now within the limit
  2. Wait until the next audit to see if the auditor notices it
  3. Ask the fund manager to sign an undertaking and then quietly close the matter without any report
  4. Document the breach and escalate it as per policy and regulatory reporting requirements
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Document the breach and escalate it as per policy and regulatory reporting requirements

Even corrected breaches must be recorded, reported to the board or trustees and to the regulator where required. Hiding a breach is itself a serious compliance failure and damages the integrity of the function.

Question 6

Which of the following is a preventive compliance control rather than a detective one?

  1. Training employees on new SEBI circulars before they take effect
  2. Reviewing trade logs after month-end to find exceptions
  3. Investigating a complaint received through SCORES
  4. Conducting an internal audit of all of last year's completed transactions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Training employees on new SEBI circulars before they take effect

Preventive controls stop breaches before they occur, such as training, approvals and system checks. Post-facto reviews, audits and complaint investigations detect problems that have already happened.

Question 7

What does the phrase 'tone at the top' mean in the context of compliance?

  1. Top-performing sales staff being exempt from compliance checks
  2. Senior leadership visibly committing to ethical conduct and regulatory compliance
  3. Compliance officers being seated on the top floor of the head office building
  4. Regulators issuing directions only to large listed entities
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Senior leadership visibly committing to ethical conduct and regulatory compliance

'Tone at the top' refers to the ethical climate set by the board and senior managers. When leaders prioritise compliance, staff follow; exempting top performers from checks would send the opposite message.

Question 8

An AMC's compliance team checks a sample of completed transactions each quarter against internal policies and regulatory limits. This activity is called:

  1. Valuation of the scheme's assets
  2. Credit rating of the scheme portfolio
  3. Statutory audit of financial statements
  4. Compliance testing and monitoring
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Compliance testing and monitoring

Periodic sample checks against rules are compliance monitoring and testing, which help confirm that controls work. A statutory audit focuses on financial statements, while rating and valuation are different processes.
