Foreign Exchange Management Act: NISM Series III-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Foreign Exchange Management Act, from the NISM Series III-C (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999, which aims to facilitate external trade and payments and promote orderly development of India's foreign exchange market. Revise the definitions of 'person resident in India', current account and capital account transactions, the role of RBI and authorised persons, the Liberalised Remittance Scheme, FDI under automatic and approval routes, and the civil nature of contraventions with penalties, compounding and investigation by the Directorate of Enforcement. Practise classifying transactions and identifying the correct authority for each action.

Foreign Exchange Management Act questions

Question 1

Which of the following is a current account transaction under FEMA?

  1. A long-term loan raised from a foreign lender
  2. Payment for imported goods in the ordinary course of business
  3. Purchase of a residential flat abroad by a resident
  4. Acquisition of shares of a foreign company by a resident individual
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Payment for imported goods in the ordinary course of business

Current account transactions do not alter assets or liabilities outside India; payments for imports, travel and services are typical examples. Buying foreign property or shares and raising foreign loans are capital account transactions.

Question 2

Under FEMA, a person is generally a 'person resident in India' if he has resided in India for more than how many days in the preceding financial year?

  1. 365 days
  2. 90 days
  3. 182 days
  4. 120 days
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 182 days

FEMA treats a person as resident if he resided in India for more than 182 days during the preceding financial year, subject to conditions about the purpose of stay. Leaving India for employment or business can change this status.

Question 3

Under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme, a resident individual can currently remit up to what amount per financial year for permitted transactions?

  1. US$ 250,000
  2. US$ 100,000
  3. US$ 1,000,000
  4. US$ 500,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. US$ 250,000

The LRS allows resident individuals to remit up to US$ 250,000 per financial year for permitted current and capital account transactions, such as education, travel and overseas investment.

Question 4

Which agency investigates contraventions of FEMA?

  1. FIU-IND
  2. Serious Fraud Investigation Office
  3. Central Bureau of Investigation
  4. Directorate of Enforcement
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Directorate of Enforcement

The Directorate of Enforcement is empowered to investigate contraventions under FEMA and also enforces the PMLA. SFIO deals with corporate frauds under the Companies Act, and FIU-IND collects and analyses financial intelligence.

Question 5

Where the amount involved in a FEMA contravention is quantifiable, the maximum penalty is up to:

  1. Twice the sum involved
  2. ₹2 lakh irrespective of the amount
  3. Three times the sum involved
  4. The sum involved plus interest
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Three times the sum involved

Section 13 of FEMA provides a penalty of up to three times the sum involved where quantifiable, or up to ₹2 lakh where it is not, plus a further daily penalty for continuing contraventions.

Question 6

A bank authorised by RBI to deal in foreign exchange is known under FEMA as an:

  1. Approved intermediary
  2. Authorised person (authorised dealer)
  3. Designated depository participant
  4. Accredited investor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Authorised person (authorised dealer)

Under Section 10 of FEMA, RBI can authorise persons such as banks to deal in foreign exchange; banks so authorised are authorised dealers. DDPs and accredited investors are SEBI concepts, not FEMA terms.

Question 7

A foreign company invests in an Indian company in a sector open to 100% FDI under the automatic route. What approval is needed before the investment?

  1. Prior approval of SEBI's board
  2. Prior approval of the Union Cabinet in every case, irrespective of the amount invested
  3. Prior approval of the State Government
  4. No prior government approval; only post-investment reporting is required
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. No prior government approval; only post-investment reporting is required

Under the automatic route, no prior approval of the Government or RBI is needed; the investee company must report the investment within the prescribed timelines. The approval route applies only to specified sectors or limits.

Question 8

A resident realises he has made an inadvertent technical contravention of FEMA. Which mechanism lets him settle it by paying a sum instead of facing adjudication?

  1. Compounding of the contravention
  2. Applying under the SEBI settlement framework
  3. Registering as a foreign portfolio investor
  4. Filing a Suspicious Transaction Report
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Compounding of the contravention

FEMA contraventions can be compounded by RBI or the Directorate of Enforcement on payment of a compounding amount, closing the matter without adjudication. The SEBI settlement framework applies to securities-law proceedings.
