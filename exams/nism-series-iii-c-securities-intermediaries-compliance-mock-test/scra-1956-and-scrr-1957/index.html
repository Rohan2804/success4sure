SCRA, 1956 and SCRR, 1957: NISM Series III-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SCRA, 1956 and SCRR, 1957, from the NISM Series III-C (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956 and the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Rules, 1957, which regulate stock exchanges, securities contracts and listing. Revise the wide definition of 'securities', recognition of stock exchanges, corporatisation and demutualisation, the legality of spot delivery and derivative contracts, delisting and the right of appeal to SAT, and the minimum public shareholding requirement under the SCRR. Focus on what is and is not a security and on the powers to recognise and supervise exchanges and clearing corporations.

SCRA, 1956 and SCRR, 1957 questions

Question 1

Which of the following is NOT a 'security' as defined under the SCRA, 1956?

  1. Units issued by a mutual fund scheme to investors
  2. A government security
  3. An equity derivative contract
  4. A fixed deposit placed with a commercial bank
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A fixed deposit placed with a commercial bank

The SCRA definition includes shares, bonds, derivatives, units of mutual funds and collective investment schemes, government securities and similar instruments. A bank fixed deposit is a deposit, not a marketable security.

Question 2

Under the SCRR, 1957, a listed company must generally maintain public shareholding of at least:

  1. 25%
  2. 51%
  3. 35%
  4. 10%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 25%

Rule 19A of the SCRR requires every listed company (other than certain exempted public sector or specified cases) to maintain minimum public shareholding of 25%. If it falls below, the company must restore it within the specified time.

Question 3

Under the SCRA, a 'spot delivery contract' is one that provides for:

  1. Delivery on a fixed date one year after the trade date
  2. Delivery and payment on the same day or the next day
  3. Delivery within one month of the trade date
  4. Settlement by paying only the price difference
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Delivery and payment on the same day or the next day

Spot delivery contracts involve actual delivery of securities and payment of price on the same day or the next day. Contracts settled only by price differences or for future dates are forward or derivative contracts.

Question 4

What does 'demutualisation' of a stock exchange under the SCRA mean?

  1. Merger of two or more stock exchanges into one
  2. Conversion of the exchange into a department of the Ministry of Finance
  3. Closure of the exchange and transfer of its members
  4. Segregation of ownership and management from trading rights of members
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Segregation of ownership and management from trading rights of members

Demutualisation separates ownership and management of the exchange from the trading rights of its members, reducing conflicts of interest. Under a mutual structure, broker-members owned and ran the exchange.

Question 5

A recognised stock exchange delists the securities of a company. Where can the company appeal against this decision?

  1. Reserve Bank of India
  2. National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission
  3. Securities Appellate Tribunal
  4. Registrar of Companies
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Securities Appellate Tribunal

Section 21A of the SCRA permits a listed company or an aggrieved investor to appeal to SAT against a delisting decision of a recognised stock exchange. Consumer forums and the ROC have no role in delisting appeals.

Question 6

A recognised stock exchange is granted recognition under the SCRA. In practice, this power is exercised by:

  1. The exchange's own board of directors
  2. The Reserve Bank of India for all exchanges
  3. SEBI, to which the Central Government has delegated the power
  4. The Ministry of Corporate Affairs through the Registrar of Companies
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. SEBI, to which the Central Government has delegated the power

Recognition of stock exchanges under Section 4 is a Central Government power that has been delegated to SEBI. SEBI therefore grants, renews and can withdraw recognition of stock exchanges.

Question 7

Two parties enter into a derivative contract on a stock outside any recognised exchange and without any clearing arrangement. Under Section 18A of the SCRA, derivative contracts are legal and valid when they are:

  1. Approved individually by the Central Government in advance
  2. Traded on a recognised exchange and settled through its clearing house or corporation
  3. Registered with the Registrar of Companies within 30 days
  4. Signed by both parties on stamp paper before a notary public
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Traded on a recognised exchange and settled through its clearing house or corporation

Section 18A makes derivative contracts legal and valid if they are traded on a recognised stock exchange and settled on its clearing house or a recognised clearing corporation, as per its rules and bye-laws. Notarisation or ROC filing does not validate them.

Question 8

Under the SCRA, which body may make or amend the bye-laws of a recognised stock exchange, subject to approval?

  1. The recognised stock exchange itself, with SEBI's previous approval
  2. Any group of investors holding its shares, through a resolution passed at a meeting
  3. The listed companies through a joint resolution
  4. The Comptroller and Auditor General of India
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The recognised stock exchange itself, with SEBI's previous approval

A recognised stock exchange may make bye-laws for regulating contracts, subject to SEBI's previous approval, and SEBI can also make or amend bye-laws itself. Investors, listed companies or the CAG cannot do so.
