SEBI (PFUTP) Regulations, 2003: NISM Series III-C practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on SEBI (PFUTP) Regulations, 2003, from the NISM Series III-C (Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Fund)) syllabus.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the SEBI (Prohibition of Fraudulent and Unfair Trade Practices relating to Securities Market) Regulations, 2003. Learn the broad definition of 'fraud' and the list of prohibited practices: creating a false appearance of trading, price manipulation, circular and wash trades, front running, spreading false or misleading information, mis-selling of securities or services, and misuse of client funds or securities. Note that the prohibitions apply to any person, not only intermediaries, and revise the penalty and enforcement powers available to SEBI. Expect scenario questions where you must identify the specific prohibited practice.

SEBI (PFUTP) Regulations, 2003 questions

Question 1

A dealer at a broking firm learns of a large buy order from an institutional client and buys the same stock in his own account just before placing the client's order. This practice is called:

  1. Front running
  2. Bear raiding
  3. Insider trading
  4. Circular trading
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Front running

Trading ahead of a substantial client order using knowledge of that order is front running, specifically prohibited under PFUTP. Insider trading involves UPSI about the company, which is a different offence.

Question 2

A group of connected entities repeatedly buy and sell the same shares among themselves to create an impression of heavy trading interest. This is:

  1. A legitimate block deal executed through the separate trading window
  2. Hedging of an existing portfolio position
  3. Circular trading to create a false appearance of volume
  4. Arbitrage between two stock exchanges
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Circular trading to create a false appearance of volume

Trades among a closed group that create artificial volumes or prices are circular trades, a manipulative practice. Arbitrage and hedging are legitimate strategies, and block deals follow a separate disclosed mechanism.

Question 3

Trades in which the buyer and seller are the same person, so there is no real change in beneficial ownership, are known as:

  1. Odd-lot trades
  2. Wash trades
  3. Spot trades
  4. Bulk deals
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Wash trades

Wash trades do not transfer beneficial ownership and only create a misleading appearance of trading. PFUTP prohibits any act that creates a false or misleading appearance of trading in the securities market.

Question 4

A person posts fabricated 'insider tips' about a small company on social media and sells his holdings after the price jumps. This is best described as:

  1. An offer for sale through the stock exchange
  2. A permitted expression of personal investment opinion
  3. A buy-back of shares by the company
  4. A 'pump and dump' scheme using false information
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A 'pump and dump' scheme using false information

Spreading false or misleading information to inflate a price and then selling is a fraudulent and manipulative practice under PFUTP. Genuine, honest opinion is different from knowingly circulating false tips.

Question 5

A distributor sells a high-risk credit risk debt scheme to an 80-year-old retired investor seeking capital safety, without checking suitability or explaining risks. Under PFUTP, this is an example of:

  1. Legitimate portfolio rebalancing
  2. A permitted execution-only transaction by the distributor
  3. Mis-selling of units of a mutual fund scheme
  4. Market making in mutual fund units
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Mis-selling of units of a mutual fund scheme

PFUTP treats mis-selling to include selling without reasonable regard to the investor's suitability and without disclosing risk factors. Ignoring the investor's risk profile and needs amounts to mis-selling.

Question 6

To whom do the prohibitions under the PFUTP Regulations apply?

  1. Only listed companies, their promoters and the directors on their boards
  2. Only foreign portfolio investors
  3. Only stock brokers registered with SEBI
  4. Any person dealing in securities, whether or not a SEBI intermediary
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Any person dealing in securities, whether or not a SEBI intermediary

PFUTP prohibitions are framed as 'no person shall', so they cover any person dealing in securities, including investors, companies and intermediaries. Restricting them to brokers or listed companies is incorrect.

Question 7

Under Section 15HA of the SEBI Act, the penalty for fraudulent and unfair trade practices can extend to:

  1. ₹1 lakh or the amount of profit made by the person, whichever is lower
  2. ₹25 crore or three times the profits made, whichever is higher
  3. ₹10 lakh only, irrespective of profit made
  4. Twice the brokerage earned on the trades
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹25 crore or three times the profits made, whichever is higher

Section 15HA provides a penalty of not less than ₹5 lakh, which may extend to ₹25 crore or three times the profits made, whichever is higher. The fixed lower amounts in the other options are not correct.

Question 8

A broker uses securities lying in Client A's account to meet the pay-in obligation of Client B without authorisation. Under PFUTP, this is:

  1. Misuse of client securities, a prohibited fraudulent practice
  2. An approved securities lending transaction
  3. Permitted if Client B repays within a week
  4. A normal netting practice allowed for all brokers under exchange bye-laws
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Misuse of client securities, a prohibited fraudulent practice

Using one client's funds or securities for another client or for the broker's own purpose without authority is a fraudulent practice. Securities lending is permitted only through the approved SLB mechanism with consent.
