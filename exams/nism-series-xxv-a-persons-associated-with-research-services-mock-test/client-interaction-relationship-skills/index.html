Client Interaction & Relationship Skills: NISM Series XXV-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Client Interaction & Relationship Skills, from the NISM Series XXV-A (Persons Associated with Research Services (Sales and Other Non-Core Services)) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter focuses on the people skills needed by persons associated with research services: understanding client needs, communicating research clearly and honestly, handling objections and complaints, managing client expectations during volatile markets, recognising behavioural biases, maintaining confidentiality and treating clients fairly. It also covers prohibited practices such as mis-selling, pressure selling and sharing unpublished research selectively. Expect scenario questions asking for the most appropriate response in a client situation.

Client Interaction & Relationship Skills questions

Question 1

A prospective client asks whether subscribing to research will guarantee profits. The best response is to:

  1. Assure him that most subscribers double their money within a year
  2. Tell him profits are guaranteed if he follows every call strictly
  3. Avoid answering and ask him to pay the fee first
  4. Explain that research improves decisions but profits are not guaranteed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Explain that research improves decisions but profits are not guaranteed

Honest communication is essential when selling research. Research can inform decisions, but markets are uncertain and no outcome or profit can be guaranteed to any client.

Question 2

A client calls angrily after a recommended stock falls. A good first step for the sales staff is to:

  1. Blame the analyst who wrote the report in front of the client
  2. Listen calmly and acknowledge the client's concern
  3. Promise the client that the firm will compensate all losses
  4. End the call quickly and avoid further contact
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Listen calmly and acknowledge the client's concern

Active listening and empathy defuse tension. Staff can then share the analyst's updated view and the reasons, without blaming colleagues or making promises.

Question 3

A client wants to put all savings into one small-cap stock from a research report. The sales executive should:

  1. Encourage it, since the report is positive on the stock
  2. Point out concentration risk and the report's risk disclosures
  3. Offer the client a discount on fees for doing so
  4. Tell the client that small caps are risk-free
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Point out concentration risk and the report's risk disclosures

While sales staff do not give personal advice, they should communicate the risks highlighted in research and avoid encouraging behaviour that exposes the client to excessive risk.

Question 4

Which practice by sales staff amounts to pressure selling?

  1. Sending the prospect a clear fee schedule and terms to review
  2. Telling a prospect the 'offer ends tonight' to force a quick decision
  3. Answering the prospect's questions about the research process
  4. Giving the prospect time to read the client agreement
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Telling a prospect the 'offer ends tonight' to force a quick decision

Pressure selling uses artificial urgency or fear to push decisions. Clients should be given time and full information to decide.

Question 5

A client tells the sales executive confidential details about his business. The executive should:

  1. Share it with colleagues for gossip
  2. Post it on social media as a success story
  3. Pass it on to other clients as a tip
  4. Keep it confidential and use it only for servicing
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Keep it confidential and use it only for servicing

Client information must be kept confidential and used only for legitimate purposes. Misuse breaches ethics, data protection norms and possibly insider trading rules.

Question 6

A client repeatedly checks prices and wants to sell after every small dip. This behaviour mostly reflects:

  1. Loss aversion and short-term focus
  2. Disciplined long-term investing
  3. Efficient use of a research report
  4. The disposition effect of holding losers
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Loss aversion and short-term focus

Over-reacting to small losses reflects loss aversion and myopic focus. Staff can remind the client of the research horizon and the report's risk disclosures.

Question 7

When a client complains about a delayed report delivery, the sales staff should:

  1. Log the complaint and update the client on resolution
  2. Tell the client to file a police complaint
  3. Ignore the complaint if the client is a small subscriber
  4. Delete the complaint from the system
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Log the complaint and update the client on resolution

All complaints should be recorded and tracked to resolution, with the client kept informed. Records also help the firm meet regulatory grievance reporting obligations.

Question 8

A long-standing client asks for early access to a research report before it is released to other clients. The staff should:

  1. Agree, since loyal clients deserve special treatment
  2. Share a summary of the report in a personal message
  3. Agree if the client pays an extra fee
  4. Decline, as reports must be shared fairly with clients
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Decline, as reports must be shared fairly with clients

Research must be distributed fairly to all eligible clients at the same time. Selective early sharing disadvantages other clients and may enable trading on unpublished information.
