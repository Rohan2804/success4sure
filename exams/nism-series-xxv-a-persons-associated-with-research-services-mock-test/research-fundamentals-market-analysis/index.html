Research Fundamentals & Market Analysis: NISM Series XXV-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Research Fundamentals & Market Analysis, from the NISM Series XXV-A (Persons Associated with Research Services (Sales and Other Non-Core Services)) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the basics of how research is done, so that non-core staff can explain research to clients accurately. It includes macroeconomic indicators (GDP, inflation, interest rates, fiscal and current account deficits), industry analysis, company analysis using financial statements and ratios, valuation methods, technical analysis concepts, and data sources such as exchange filings and company disclosures. Focus on interpreting common ratios and indicators and on explaining how economic events affect sectors and companies.

Research Fundamentals & Market Analysis questions

Question 1

A company's operating profit margin is calculated as:

  1. Net profit divided by total shareholders' equity
  2. Total debt divided by shareholders' equity
  3. Dividends divided by net profit
  4. Operating profit divided by revenue
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Operating profit divided by revenue

Operating margin = operating profit (EBIT) ÷ revenue, showing how much of each rupee of sales remains after operating costs. The other options are ROE, debt-equity and payout ratio.

Question 2

A sharp depreciation of the rupee is most likely to benefit which sector?

  1. Oil marketing companies that import most of their crude oil
  2. Airlines that pay for fuel and aircraft leases in dollars
  3. Companies with large unhedged foreign currency loans
  4. IT services companies that earn mainly in US dollars
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. IT services companies that earn mainly in US dollars

Exporters earning in foreign currency get more rupees per dollar when the rupee weakens. Importers and firms with unhedged foreign debt see costs rise.

Question 3

A company has current assets of ₹240 crore and current liabilities of ₹160 crore. Its current ratio is:

  1. 1.5
  2. 0.67
  3. 2.4
  4. 4.0
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 1.5

Current ratio = current assets ÷ current liabilities = 240 ÷ 160 = 1.5. It indicates the firm's ability to meet short-term obligations from short-term assets.

Question 4

An 'inverted yield curve' is often watched by analysts because it:

  1. Always signals that inflation will fall to zero within a year
  2. Has historically been associated with economic slowdowns
  3. Means that long-term bonds have no interest rate risk
  4. Shows that share prices will rise in the next quarter
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Has historically been associated with economic slowdowns

When short-term yields exceed long-term yields, it often reflects expectations of rate cuts due to weaker growth. It is a signal, not a certainty.

Question 5

Which source should research staff rely on for official corporate announcements of listed companies?

  1. Messages forwarded in social media investment groups
  2. Comments posted on unverified discussion forums
  3. Rumours heard from other market participants
  4. Filings on stock exchange websites
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Filings on stock exchange websites

Listed companies must disclose material information through stock exchanges, which makes exchange filings the authoritative source. Unverified social media content can be inaccurate or even deliberately manipulative.

Question 6

In technical analysis, a 'support level' is a price level where:

  1. The company's management has promised to buy back shares
  2. SEBI has fixed a minimum price for the stock
  3. Buying interest has tended to stop the price falling
  4. The stock's book value per share is exactly equal to price
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Buying interest has tended to stop the price falling

Support is an area where demand has historically been strong enough to halt declines. It can break, and it is not a guaranteed floor.

Question 7

A rise in the government's fiscal deficit financed by heavy borrowing may lead to:

  1. An automatic fall in inflation to below 2% in every case
  2. Lower corporate taxes for all listed companies
  3. An immediate rise in all share prices
  4. Higher bond yields as government borrowing grows
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Higher bond yields as government borrowing grows

Heavy government borrowing increases the supply of bonds and can push up yields, raising borrowing costs. The effect on equities and inflation depends on many factors.

Question 8

Price-to-earnings (P/E) ratios are usually not meaningful for a company that:

  1. Has negative earnings
  2. Pays regular dividends every year
  3. Has a market capitalisation above ₹1 lakh crore
  4. Is part of the Nifty 50 index
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Has negative earnings

If earnings are negative, the P/E ratio is negative or undefined and cannot be compared. Analysts may use price-to-sales or EV/EBITDA instead for loss-making firms.
