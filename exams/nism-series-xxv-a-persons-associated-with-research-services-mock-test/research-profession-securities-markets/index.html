Research Profession & Securities Markets: NISM Series XXV-A practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Research Profession & Securities Markets, from the NISM Series XXV-A (Persons Associated with Research Services (Sales and Other Non-Core Services)) syllabus.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces the research profession and the securities market in which it operates. It covers the role of research analysts and research entities, the difference between core research functions and the sales and other non-core functions performed by persons associated with research services, the structure of Indian markets (primary and secondary, equity, debt and derivatives), key intermediaries and regulators, and basic market concepts such as indices and market capitalisation. Focus on what a non-core staff member may and may not do, and on basic market vocabulary used with clients.

Research Profession & Securities Markets questions

Question 1

A sales executive at a SEBI-registered research analyst firm wants to tell a client which stock to buy based on her own view. She should:

  1. Give her own buy call, since she understands the client better than the analyst does
  2. Give the call only on WhatsApp so that no record of the advice is created
  3. Share only the firm's published research, not personal recommendations
  4. Give personal calls only to clients whose fees have been paid in advance
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Share only the firm's published research, not personal recommendations

Recommendations must come from qualified research analysts through approved research. Non-core staff can distribute and explain published research but must not issue their own calls.

Question 2

Which activity is a core research function rather than a sales or support function?

  1. Onboarding new clients and completing their KYC formalities
  2. Explaining the subscription plans and fees to prospects
  3. Handling client queries about login and report delivery
  4. Preparing a valuation and target price for a stock
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Preparing a valuation and target price for a stock

Analysis, valuation and recommendations are core research functions performed by research analysts. Onboarding, fee explanation and service queries are non-core activities.

Question 3

A research analyst registered with SEBI can be:

  1. An individual, partnership firm or body corporate
  2. Only a listed company with net worth above ₹100 crore
  3. Only an individual working from home
  4. Only a stock exchange or depository
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. An individual, partnership firm or body corporate

SEBI's Research Analysts Regulations allow registration of individuals, partnership firms, LLPs and bodies corporate, each meeting qualification, certification and net worth or deposit requirements.

Question 4

A client asks what the 'free float' of a company means. A correct explanation is:

  1. The total number of shares the company is legally permitted to issue in future
  2. Shares that the company gives away free to employees each year
  3. The cash balance held by the company in its bank accounts
  4. Shares readily available for trading, excluding promoter holdings
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Shares readily available for trading, excluding promoter holdings

Free float excludes shares held by promoters, government and other locked-in holders. Many indices, including the Nifty 50, use free-float market capitalisation for weighting.

Question 5

Which body administers and supervises research analysts on behalf of SEBI?

  1. The Association of Mutual Funds in India acting on its own
  2. The Reserve Bank of India's financial markets department
  3. A recognised Research Analyst Administration and Supervisory Body
  4. The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A recognised Research Analyst Administration and Supervisory Body

SEBI has recognised a RAASB (BSE) to administer and supervise research analysts, including enlistment, monitoring and grievance handling. AMFI, RBI and IRDAI have no role here.

Question 6

The secondary market's main function for investors is to:

  1. Provide liquidity by letting investors trade existing securities
  2. Let companies raise fresh capital directly from the public through IPOs
  3. Fix the interest rates on bank deposits each month
  4. Approve new research analyst registrations
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Provide liquidity by letting investors trade existing securities

The secondary market lets investors buy and sell already-issued securities, providing liquidity and price discovery. Fresh capital is raised in the primary market.

Question 7

A client asks why derivatives volumes in India are very high compared with cash market volumes. A balanced response is:

  1. Derivatives are completely risk-free and therefore extremely popular
  2. Cash market trades are banned for retail investors
  3. Derivatives need only margin, so notional turnover is large
  4. SEBI guarantees profits in derivatives trades
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Derivatives need only margin, so notional turnover is large

Futures and options are traded on margin, so the notional value traded is many times the capital deployed. SEBI studies show most individual F&O traders lose money.

Question 8

Persons associated with research services who handle sales and other non-core functions are required to hold:

  1. A licence from the stock exchange as a sub-broker
  2. The NISM Series V-A mutual fund distributor certificate only
  3. A chartered financial analyst charter in all cases
  4. The NISM Series XXV-A certification
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The NISM Series XXV-A certification

SEBI requires persons associated with research services in sales and other non-core roles to obtain the NISM Series XXV-A certification. Research analysts themselves need NISM Series XV.
