English Language: SBI PO practice questions

15 free questions with answers and explanations on English Language, from the SBI PO (SBI Probationary Officer (Prelims)) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this section covers

The English section of SBI PO prelims and mains tests reading comprehension, vocabulary in context, grammar and sentence structure. Expect passages on banking, economy and social issues followed by inference and tone questions, plus error spotting, fillers, para jumbles, sentence improvement and word usage. Focus on subject–verb agreement, conditionals, fixed conjunction pairs and connectors, and build vocabulary through regular reading of editorials. In comprehension, answer only from what the passage states or clearly implies, and eliminate options that are too extreme or bring in outside information.

English Language questions

Question 1

Read the passage and answer the question that follows. Many first-time borrowers in small towns now take loans through mobile apps. The speed is welcome, but some apps hide charges in fine print and use aggressive recovery methods. Regulators have responded by requiring lenders to disclose the full annual cost of a loan upfront. Experts say disclosure helps, yet borrowers also need basic financial literacy to compare offers sensibly. What is the central concern raised in the passage?

  1. Fast digital loans can harm borrowers who do not understand their true cost
  2. Mobile apps have made banks in small towns completely unnecessary
  3. Regulators have banned digital lenders from operating in small towns
  4. Borrowers in small towns prefer slow loans from moneylenders
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Fast digital loans can harm borrowers who do not understand their true cost

The passage welcomes speed but warns of hidden charges and harsh recovery, then stresses disclosure and literacy. Nothing says banks are unnecessary or that lending is banned, so only the first statement captures its central concern.

Question 2

Read the passage and answer the question that follows. Many first-time borrowers in small towns now take loans through mobile apps. The speed is welcome, but some apps hide charges in fine print and use aggressive recovery methods. Regulators have responded by requiring lenders to disclose the full annual cost of a loan upfront. Experts say disclosure helps, yet borrowers also need basic financial literacy to compare offers sensibly. According to the passage, why is disclosure alone considered insufficient?

  1. Lenders can easily ignore the disclosure requirement
  2. The annual cost of a loan cannot be calculated in advance
  3. Mobile apps do not allow lenders to display any charges
  4. Borrowers must also be able to compare loan offers sensibly
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Borrowers must also be able to compare loan offers sensibly

The last sentence says disclosure helps, yet borrowers also need financial literacy to compare offers. The passage does not claim lenders ignore the rule or that costs cannot be calculated.

Question 3

Read the passage and answer the question that follows. Many first-time borrowers in small towns now take loans through mobile apps. The speed is welcome, but some apps hide charges in fine print and use aggressive recovery methods. Regulators have responded by requiring lenders to disclose the full annual cost of a loan upfront. Experts say disclosure helps, yet borrowers also need basic financial literacy to compare offers sensibly. Which word is closest in meaning to 'aggressive' as used in the passage?

  1. Generous
  2. Delayed
  3. Careless
  4. Forceful
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Forceful

'Aggressive recovery methods' means pushy, forceful ways of collecting dues. 'Generous' is nearly opposite, while 'delayed' and 'careless' do not describe the pressure the passage is criticising.

Question 4

Fill in the blank with the most suitable word. Despite several reminders from the branch, the borrower remained ________ about repaying the overdue amount.

  1. punctual
  2. indifferent
  3. diligent
  4. eager
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. indifferent

'Despite several reminders' signals that the borrower did not care, so 'indifferent' fits. 'Punctual', 'diligent' and 'eager' would all suggest willingness to repay, which contradicts the contrast set up by 'despite'.

Question 5

Select the pair of words that fits both blanks correctly. The committee's report was ________ by the board, which promptly ________ most of its recommendations.

  1. endorsed, adopted
  2. rejected, adopted
  3. ignored, implemented
  4. endorsed, discarded
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. endorsed, adopted

A board that approves a report would logically adopt its recommendations, so 'endorsed, adopted' is consistent. The other pairs contradict each other, for example rejecting a report and then adopting its recommendations.

Question 6

Find the part of the sentence that has a grammatical error. Each of the applicants (A) / have been asked (B) / to submit (C) / the documents by Friday. (D)

  1. (A)
  2. (B)
  3. (C)
  4. (D)
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. (B)

The subject is 'Each', which is singular, so the verb must be 'has been asked', not 'have been asked'. The phrase 'of the applicants' does not change the number of the subject.

Question 7

Find the part of the sentence that has a grammatical error. Hardly had the auditors (A) / entered the branch (B) / than the manager (C) / produced all the ledgers. (D)

  1. (A)
  2. (B)
  3. (C)
  4. (D)
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. (C)

'Hardly' and 'scarcely' are followed by 'when', not 'than'. The correct form is 'Hardly had the auditors entered the branch when the manager produced all the ledgers'. 'No sooner' is the phrase that takes 'than'.

Question 8

Find the part of the sentence that has a grammatical error. The volume of digital payments (A) / in rural districts (B) / have risen steadily (C) / over the last five years. (D)

  1. (A)
  2. (B)
  3. (C)
  4. (D)
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. (C)

The head noun of the subject is 'volume', which is singular, so the verb should be 'has risen'. The plural 'payments' and 'districts' are inside prepositional phrases and do not decide the verb.

Question 9

Improve the part in capitals, if needed. If I WOULD HAVE KNOWN about the deadline, I would have applied much earlier.

  1. had known
  2. would know
  3. have known
  4. No improvement required
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. had known

In a third conditional the if-clause takes the past perfect: 'If I had known…, I would have applied'. 'Would have' should appear only in the main clause, so the sentence needs correction.

Question 10

Arrange the sentences P, Q, R and S in a logical order. P: As a result, the branch decided to open on Saturdays as well. Q: Footfall at the new branch was far higher than expected. R: The extra day reduced waiting time considerably. S: Long queues began forming on weekdays.

  1. SQPR
  2. QPSR
  3. QSRP
  4. QSPR
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. QSPR

Q introduces high footfall, S states its effect (long queues), P gives the response ('As a result', Saturday opening) and R reports the outcome of that extra day. Other orders place the result before its cause.

Question 11

In the sentence 'A prudent lender checks the repayment capacity of every borrower', which word can replace PRUDENT without changing the meaning?

  1. Reckless
  2. Generous
  3. Judicious
  4. Hasty
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Judicious

'Prudent' means acting with care and good judgement, which matches 'judicious'. 'Reckless' and 'hasty' are near opposites, and 'generous' refers to willingness to give rather than careful judgement.

Question 12

Pick the word that is the opposite of FRUGAL.

  1. Economical
  2. Extravagant
  3. Thrifty
  4. Careful
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Extravagant

'Frugal' describes someone who spends sparingly. Its opposite is 'extravagant', meaning spending lavishly. 'Economical' and 'thrifty' are synonyms of frugal, and 'careful' is close in meaning, not opposite.

Question 13

What does the idiom 'a red herring' mean in the sentence: 'The rumour about a merger was a red herring during the audit'?

  1. Something that distracts attention from the real issue
  2. A financial loss that is hidden in the accounts
  3. A warning sign that predicts an approaching crisis
  4. A rare opportunity that must be grabbed at once
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Something that distracts attention from the real issue

A red herring is a misleading clue that draws attention away from the actual matter. It does not refer to a hidden loss, a warning sign or an opportunity, though these may sound related in a banking context.

Question 14

Choose the single word for: 'A form of government in which power rests with a small group of people'.

  1. Monarchy
  2. Democracy
  3. Anarchy
  4. Oligarchy
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Oligarchy

Oligarchy means rule by a few. Monarchy is rule by one sovereign, democracy is rule by the people through elected representatives, and anarchy is the absence of any government authority.

Question 15

Choose the connector that best fits the blank. The pilot project earned steady profits for two years; ________, the board approved its expansion to fifty more branches.

  1. nevertheless
  2. otherwise
  3. consequently
  4. meanwhile
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. consequently

Profits leading to approval of expansion is a cause-and-effect link, so 'consequently' fits. 'Nevertheless' signals contrast, 'otherwise' signals an alternative condition and 'meanwhile' signals simultaneous events, none of which suit this logic.
