Reasoning Ability: SBI PO practice questions

15 free questions with answers and explanations on Reasoning Ability, from the SBI PO (SBI Probationary Officer (Prelims)) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this section covers

Reasoning Ability for SBI PO tests puzzles and seating arrangements (linear, circular, floor-based), syllogisms including possibility cases, coded and direct inequalities, blood relations, direction sense, coding-decoding, ranking and alphanumeric series. Mains adds input-output and logical reasoning such as assumptions and courses of action. Draw neat diagrams, fix definite clues first and test each remaining case. In syllogisms use Venn diagrams and treat 'possibility' conclusions separately. In inequalities, a conclusion follows only when it is definitely true.

Reasoning Ability questions

Question 1

Six persons A, B, C, D, E and F live on the six floors of a building, one on each floor. The lowest floor is numbered 1 and the top floor 6. • C lives on an even-numbered floor above D. • Exactly two persons live between C and F. • A lives immediately above F. • D lives on an even-numbered floor, immediately above E. • B does not live on the top floor. Who lives on the floor immediately below B?

  1. C
  2. F
  3. A
  4. D
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A

C must be on 6 (an even floor above an even floor D with E below it). Then F is on 3, A on 4, D on 2 and E on 1, leaving B on 5. A is directly below B.

Question 2

Six persons A, B, C, D, E and F live on the six floors of a building, one on each floor. The lowest floor is numbered 1 and the top floor 6. • C lives on an even-numbered floor above D. • Exactly two persons live between C and F. • A lives immediately above F. • D lives on an even-numbered floor, immediately above E. • B does not live on the top floor. How many persons live between E and A?

  1. One
  2. Two
  3. Three
  4. None
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Two

The arrangement from bottom is E(1), D(2), F(3), A(4), B(5), C(6). Between E on floor 1 and A on floor 4 are D and F, so two persons live between them.

Question 3

Five persons P, Q, R, S and T sit in a row facing north. • R sits exactly in the middle. • Q sits at one of the extreme ends. • S sits second to the left of R. • T does not sit next to Q. Who sits immediately to the left of Q?

  1. P
  2. T
  3. S
  4. R
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. P

R is 3rd, so S is 1st. Q must then be at the right end (5th). T cannot be next to Q, so T is 2nd and P is 4th, immediately to the left of Q.

Question 4

Statements: All banks are buildings. Some buildings are offices. No office is a shop. Conclusions: I. Some banks are offices. II. Some buildings are not shops.

  1. Only conclusion I follows
  2. Both I and II follow
  3. Neither I nor II follows
  4. Only conclusion II follows
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Only conclusion II follows

The buildings that are offices cannot be shops, so some buildings are definitely not shops (II follows). Banks may or may not overlap with offices, so I is only a possibility and does not follow.

Question 5

Statements: Some pens are pencils. No pencil is an eraser. Conclusions: I. Some pens are not erasers. II. All erasers being pens is a possibility.

  1. Only conclusion I follows
  2. Only conclusion II follows
  3. Both I and II follow
  4. Neither I nor II follows
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Both I and II follow

Pens that are pencils can never be erasers, so I follows. Erasers can lie wholly inside the pen circle as long as they avoid the pencil region, so II is a valid possibility. Both follow.

Question 6

Statements: P ≥ Q > R = S; T < S ≤ U Conclusions: I. P > T II. U ≥ R

  1. Only conclusion I follows
  2. Both I and II follow
  3. Only conclusion II follows
  4. Neither I nor II follows
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Both I and II follow

Combining, P ≥ Q > R = S > T gives P > T, so I is true. Also U ≥ S = R gives U ≥ R, so II is true. Both conclusions follow.

Question 7

Statements: A > B ≥ C; D ≤ C < E; F = E Conclusions: I. F > D II. B > D

  1. Only conclusion I follows
  2. Only conclusion II follows
  3. Both I and II follow
  4. Neither I nor II follows
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Only conclusion I follows

F = E > C ≥ D gives F > D, so I follows. For II, B ≥ C ≥ D only gives B ≥ D; B could equal D, so B > D is not certain. Only I follows.

Question 8

Pointing to a young man, Kavya said, 'His mother is the only daughter of my mother.' How is the young man related to Kavya?

  1. Nephew
  2. Brother
  3. Cousin
  4. Son
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Son

Kavya is a woman, so the only daughter of her mother is Kavya herself. The young man's mother is therefore Kavya, making him her son. 'Nephew' would require a sister, which the clue rules out.

Question 9

A + B means A is the father of B; A − B means A is the wife of B; A × B means A is the brother of B; A ÷ B means A is the daughter of B. If P ÷ Q − R × S, how is R related to P?

  1. Uncle
  2. Brother
  3. Father
  4. Grandfather
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Father

P is the daughter of Q, and Q is the wife of R, so R is P's father. R × S only tells us R is S's brother, making S P's uncle or aunt, not changing R's relation.

Question 10

Manoj walks 10 m north, turns right and walks 6 m, turns right again and walks 18 m, then turns left and walks 9 m. How far and in which direction is he from his starting point?

  1. 15 m, South-East
  2. 17 m, South-East
  3. 17 m, North-East
  4. 13 m, South-West
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 17 m, South-East

Net movement: east 6 + 9 = 15 m and south 18 − 10 = 8 m. Distance = √(15² + 8²) = 17 m. Being east and south of the start, he is to the South-East.

Question 11

In a certain code, PLANT is written as QNDRY. How is SAVER written in that code?

  1. TCYIW
  2. TBYIV
  3. TCXIW
  4. UCYIW
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. TCYIW

The letters are shifted forward by 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 places in order: P+1=Q, L+2=N, A+3=D, N+4=R, T+5=Y. Applying the same to SAVER gives T, C, Y, I, W.

Question 12

In a secret language, 'loan is approved' is coded 'ka zo ri', 'approved by manager' is coded 'zo lu pe' and 'manager is busy' is coded 'pe ka mi'. Which code stands for 'by'?

  1. zo
  2. pe
  3. ri
  4. lu
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. lu

'approved' = zo (common to first two), 'manager' = pe (common to last two) and 'is' = ka. In 'approved by manager', the remaining code lu must mean 'by'. 'ri' stands for 'loan'.

Question 13

In a row of students, Asha is 12th from the left and Bina is 18th from the right. When they exchange places, Asha becomes 20th from the left. How many students are in the row?

  1. 38
  2. 36
  3. 37
  4. 30
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 37

After exchanging, Asha occupies Bina's old seat, which is 20th from the left and 18th from the right. Total = 20 + 18 − 1 = 37. Asha's original position is not needed.

Question 14

Study the sequence: M 3 # K 8 R @ 5 T % 2 Q 9 & L How many symbols are immediately preceded by a number and immediately followed by a letter?

  1. One
  2. Two
  3. Three
  4. Four
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Two

Check each symbol: '#' has 3 before and K after (counts); '@' has R before; '%' has T before; '&' has 9 before and L after (counts). So exactly two symbols meet the condition.

Question 15

If the letters of the word BRANDS are arranged in alphabetical order, how many letters will remain in the same position?

  1. Two
  2. One
  3. Three
  4. None
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Two

Alphabetical order gives A B D N R S. Comparing with B R A N D S position by position, only N (4th) and S (6th) stay unchanged, so two letters remain in place.
