Portfolio Management Process: NISM Series XXI-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Portfolio Management Process, from the NISM Series XXI-B (Portfolio Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the full process of managing client portfolios: gathering client information, assessing risk tolerance and capacity, setting return objectives, identifying constraints (liquidity, horizon, tax, legal, unique), drafting an investment policy statement, strategic and tactical asset allocation, security selection, execution, monitoring and feedback. It also discusses goal-based investing and the role of behavioural factors in client communication. Expect case-based questions applying the process to different client profiles.

Portfolio Management Process questions

Question 1

A client requires a 9% annual return to meet her retirement goal but has low risk tolerance. The portfolio manager should first:

  1. Invest aggressively to reach 9% regardless of her low tolerance
  2. Promise her 9% so she remains comfortable with the plan
  3. Discuss trade-offs and possibly revise the goal
  4. Ignore her risk tolerance because the goal is more important
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Discuss trade-offs and possibly revise the goal

When required return and risk tolerance conflict, the manager must explain the trade-offs: save more, extend the horizon or lower the target. Ignoring tolerance or promising returns is unsuitable.

Question 2

An investment policy statement (IPS) is most useful for:

  1. Replacing the need for any client agreement with the manager
  2. Guaranteeing that the portfolio will meet its target return
  3. Allowing the manager to change the strategy without client input
  4. Guiding decisions and evaluating the manager
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Guiding decisions and evaluating the manager

The IPS documents objectives, constraints and allocation, giving a reference for decisions and reviews. It does not guarantee returns or replace legal agreements.

Question 3

A 45-year-old client in the 30% tax bracket asks how taxes affect asset allocation. The most relevant consideration is:

  1. Post-tax returns of each asset class
  2. Only the pre-tax returns shown in fund factsheets
  3. Only the asset class that gave the highest return last year
  4. The number of securities in each asset class
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Post-tax returns of each asset class

Taxes reduce realised returns differently across assets and holding periods. Comparing post-tax expected returns helps choose an efficient allocation for the client.

Question 4

Strategic asset allocation for a client is primarily determined by:

  1. Next month's market outlook given by a television analyst
  2. The asset class with the highest return over the past six months
  3. Long-term objectives, risk profile and constraints
  4. The portfolio manager's personal investment preferences
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Long-term objectives, risk profile and constraints

Strategic allocation is the long-term policy mix based on the client's profile. Short-term market views belong to tactical allocation within permitted ranges.

Question 5

Goal-based investing differs from a single-portfolio approach because it:

  1. Puts all goals into one portfolio with one common risk level
  2. Ignores the time horizon of individual goals
  3. Focuses only on beating a benchmark index every year
  4. Builds separate allocations for each goal
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Builds separate allocations for each goal

Goal-based investing segments wealth by goals such as education, home or retirement, each with its own horizon and risk. This can make allocations and progress clearer to clients.

Question 6

A client inherits ₹2 crore and has no immediate need for the money. Before investing, the portfolio manager should:

  1. Update the client's profile and IPS
  2. Invest the full amount immediately in the existing strategy without review
  3. Advise the client to keep the money in a savings account permanently
  4. Invest all of it in a single high-conviction stock
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Update the client's profile and IPS

A major change in wealth can change risk capacity, goals and constraints. The manager should reassess the profile and update the IPS before deploying the new money.

Question 7

During the feedback stage of portfolio management, the manager mainly:

  1. Opens the client's demat and bank accounts for the first time
  2. Collects KYC documents from the client
  3. Reviews results against objectives and adjusts
  4. Signs the PMS agreement with the client
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Reviews results against objectives and adjusts

Feedback involves measuring performance, comparing it with objectives and benchmarks, and adjusting the portfolio or IPS. Account opening and KYC happen at the start.

Question 8

Which factor most increases a client's ability to take risk?

  1. A large loan EMI due every month with uncertain income
  2. A planned large withdrawal within the next year
  3. Total dependence on the portfolio for daily living expenses
  4. A long horizon with stable income
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A long horizon with stable income

Risk capacity rises with longer horizons, stable income and lower liabilities. Heavy obligations, near-term withdrawals and dependence on the portfolio reduce capacity.
