Introduction to Indices: NISM Series XXI-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Introduction to Indices, from the NISM Series XXI-B (Portfolio Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains what market indices are, why they matter and how they are built. It covers index uses (benchmarking, passive products, derivatives, market sentiment), construction methods (price-weighted, equal-weighted, free-float market-cap weighted, factor and smart-beta indices), base value and divisor, rebalancing and reconstitution, and the main Indian indices across equity and debt. For portfolio managers, the key focus is choosing appropriate benchmarks and understanding how index methodology affects measured performance.

Introduction to Indices questions

Question 1

An index starts with a base value of 1,000. If the total free-float market value of its constituents rises 12% from the base date, the index value is:

  1. 1,120
  2. 1,012
  3. 1,200
  4. 112
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 1,120

A market-cap weighted index moves in proportion to the aggregate free-float value: 1,000 × 1.12 = 1,120. The base value simply sets the starting level.

Question 2

Smart-beta or factor indices differ from traditional cap-weighted indices because they:

  1. Always contain exactly the same stocks and weights as the Nifty 50
  2. Weight stocks by factors such as value or quality
  3. Are calculated only once every five years by the stock exchange
  4. Include only government securities and no equities at all
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Weight stocks by factors such as value or quality

Factor indices use rules based on characteristics like value, momentum, low volatility or quality to select or weight stocks, aiming to capture specific return drivers.

Question 3

Why does an index divisor get adjusted after events like a stock split or constituent change?

  1. To keep the index level continuous
  2. To increase the index value artificially after every corporate action
  3. To reduce the number of stocks in the index each time
  4. To reflect the dividend paid by each company in cash
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. To keep the index level continuous

Corporate actions and constituent changes alter the aggregate value without any market movement. Adjusting the divisor ensures the index value changes only because of price moves.

Question 4

A debt fund manager wants a benchmark for a portfolio of short-term corporate bonds. A suitable choice is:

  1. The Nifty 50 Total Return Index of large-cap equities
  2. A gold price index tracking international bullion prices
  3. A short-term corporate bond index
  4. A small-cap equity index of recently listed companies
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A short-term corporate bond index

Benchmarks should reflect the portfolio's asset class, maturity and credit profile. An equity or gold index would not measure a bond manager's skill.

Question 5

Which feature makes an index 'investable'?

  1. It contains only unlisted companies that rarely trade
  2. Its weights are changed secretly without any announcement
  3. Its constituents are liquid and replicable
  4. It is computed from the fund manager's own estimates of value
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Its constituents are liquid and replicable

An investable index uses liquid securities and transparent rules so that a fund can actually replicate it. This makes it a fair benchmark and a basis for passive products.

Question 6

In a price-weighted index of three stocks priced ₹100, ₹200 and ₹700, a 10% rise in the ₹700 stock (others unchanged) raises the index by about:

  1. 10%
  2. 7%
  3. 3.3%
  4. 0.7%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 7%

In a price-weighted index, the ₹700 stock is 700/1,000 = 70% of the total. A 10% rise adds ₹70 to the ₹1,000 total, raising the index by 7%.

Question 7

A total return index for equities assumes that:

  1. Dividends are ignored and only price movement counts
  2. Dividends are reinvested in the index
  3. All constituents have exactly the same weight
  4. Prices are adjusted for inflation every month
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Dividends are reinvested in the index

TRI includes dividends reinvested, so it reflects the full return an investor would earn. SEBI requires mutual funds and PMS to benchmark against total return variants.

Question 8

Index rebalancing at regular intervals mainly helps to:

  1. Guarantee that the index will outperform active managers
  2. Keep the index aligned with its methodology
  3. Freeze constituents so they never change over time
  4. Remove all price volatility from the index
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Keep the index aligned with its methodology

Over time, price changes and corporate events cause drift. Periodic rebalancing restores weights and eligibility per the rules so the index keeps representing its target segment.
