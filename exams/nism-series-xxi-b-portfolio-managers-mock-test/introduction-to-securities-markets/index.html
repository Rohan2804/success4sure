Introduction to Securities Markets: NISM Series XXI-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Introduction to Securities Markets, from the NISM Series XXI-B (Portfolio Managers) syllabus.

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how Indian securities markets work from a portfolio manager's perspective: primary and secondary markets, market participants and intermediaries, trading mechanisms, order types, clearing and settlement, depositories, and the regulatory role of SEBI, RBI and the Ministry of Finance. It also covers market indices, market capitalisation segments and investor protection measures. Understand how trades are executed and settled for client portfolios and what each intermediary is responsible for.

Introduction to Securities Markets questions

Question 1

A 'stop-loss' sell order is generally used to:

  1. Buy more shares automatically whenever the price rises by a set amount
  2. Guarantee that shares are sold at exactly the trigger price in all conditions
  3. Limit losses if the price falls to a trigger level
  4. Cancel all pending orders at the end of the trading day
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Limit losses if the price falls to a trigger level

A stop-loss order becomes active when the trigger price is hit and then sells, limiting further losses. In fast markets it may execute below the trigger, so the price is not guaranteed.

Question 2

Which market segment is used by the Reserve Bank of India to conduct auctions of Government securities?

  1. The primary market for G-secs
  2. The equity cash segment of a stock exchange
  3. The commodity derivatives segment of an exchange
  4. The SME platform of a stock exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The primary market for G-secs

The RBI issues G-secs on behalf of the government through primary auctions. Secondary trading happens on platforms like NDS-OM and exchange debt segments.

Question 3

Novation by a clearing corporation means it:

  1. Becomes the counterparty to both buyer and seller
  2. Chooses which brokers are allowed to trade on a particular day
  3. Transfers shares directly from the company to the buyer
  4. Sets the issue price for initial public offerings
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Becomes the counterparty to both buyer and seller

Novation replaces the original contract between buyer and seller with two contracts with the clearing corporation, which removes counterparty risk between trading members.

Question 4

Shares of a listed company that are pledged by promoters are a concern to portfolio managers mainly because:

  1. Pledged shares cannot be counted in the company's market capitalisation at all
  2. SEBI bans the company from paying dividends while any pledge exists
  3. Pledged shares automatically convert into debt of the company
  4. Forced selling by lenders can hit the share price
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Forced selling by lenders can hit the share price

If the share price falls, lenders may invoke the pledge and sell, adding to downward pressure. High promoter pledging is a governance and risk signal analysts watch.

Question 5

Which of the following is regulated primarily by the RBI rather than SEBI?

  1. Stock brokers in the equity cash segment
  2. The government securities and money markets
  3. Mutual funds and their asset management companies
  4. Portfolio managers registered under the 2020 Regulations
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The government securities and money markets

RBI regulates the G-sec, money and foreign exchange markets, along with banks and NBFCs. Brokers, mutual funds and portfolio managers are regulated by SEBI.

Question 6

An investor sees a stock's 'circuit filter' hit on the upper side. This means:

  1. The stock has been permanently delisted from the stock exchange
  2. The company has announced a bonus issue for all shareholders
  3. Trading is restricted beyond a set price band for the day
  4. The stock's price is guaranteed to rise further the next day
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Trading is restricted beyond a set price band for the day

Price bands limit how far a stock can move in a day to curb volatility. Once the band is hit, trades cannot happen beyond it until the next session or band revision.

Question 7

The market capitalisation category of a stock for Indian mutual funds and many portfolio managers is based on:

  1. The number of shareholders recorded in the company's register
  2. The face value of its shares and the size of its dividend
  3. Its ranking by full market capitalisation in AMFI's list
  4. The number of employees it has across all its offices
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Its ranking by full market capitalisation in AMFI's list

AMFI publishes a half-yearly list ranking companies by full market cap: 1–100 large cap, 101–250 mid cap and the rest small cap. The industry widely uses this classification.

Question 8

Which document must a company file for an IPO that gives investors detailed information about the issue?

  1. The offer document or prospectus
  2. The portfolio manager's disclosure document for its PMS clients
  3. The company's annual GST return filed with the tax department
  4. The depository's statement of holdings for promoters
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The offer document or prospectus

Issuers disclose business, financials, risks and issue terms through the draft and final offer documents filed with SEBI and the exchanges. Investors should read them before applying.
