Performance Measurement and Evaluation of Portfolio Managers: NISM Series XXI-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Performance Measurement and Evaluation of Portfolio Managers, from the NISM Series XXI-B (Portfolio Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how portfolio managers' performance is measured and evaluated. It covers return calculations (holding period, annualised, time-weighted, money-weighted), SEBI's requirements for TWRR and standardised benchmarking, risk-adjusted measures (Sharpe, Treynor, Jensen's alpha, information ratio, M-squared, Sortino), performance attribution (allocation, selection, interaction), and the qualities of fair performance presentation. Practise calculations and understand which measure suits which situation.

Performance Measurement and Evaluation of Portfolio Managers questions

Question 1

A portfolio earned 16% with standard deviation 20%; the market earned 12% with standard deviation 15%; the risk-free rate is 6%. The portfolio's M-squared (M²) is:

  1. 16.0%
  2. 12.0%
  3. 13.5%
  4. 10.0%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 13.5%

Sharpe = (16 − 6)/20 = 0.5. M² = Rf + Sharpe × σm = 6 + 0.5 × 15 = 13.5%. It beats the market's 12% on a risk-adjusted basis, despite higher volatility.

Question 2

In performance attribution, the 'allocation effect' measures the value added by:

  1. Picking better stocks within each sector of the benchmark
  2. Over- or under-weighting sectors versus the benchmark
  3. Charging lower fees than competing managers
  4. Reducing the number of client accounts managed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Over- or under-weighting sectors versus the benchmark

Allocation effect captures returns from sector weighting decisions. Selection effect captures returns from choosing stocks within sectors that beat the sector benchmark.

Question 3

A portfolio returned 1.5% per month for 12 months. Its annualised return (compounded) is approximately:

  1. 18.0%
  2. 12.0%
  3. 19.6%
  4. 21.0%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 19.6%

Annual return = (1.015)^12 − 1 ≈ 19.56%. Simply multiplying 1.5% by 12 gives 18%, which ignores the effect of compounding monthly returns over the year.

Question 4

Which measure would best show whether a manager's outperformance is consistent rather than due to a few big bets?

  1. Absolute return over one month
  2. Total value of assets under management
  3. Number of stocks in the portfolio
  4. Information ratio
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Information ratio

Information ratio divides active return by tracking error, rewarding steady outperformance per unit of active risk. A high return from a few bets would come with high tracking error.

Question 5

A client invests ₹10 lakh, which grows to ₹11 lakh after 6 months; he adds ₹9 lakh, and the portfolio ends the year at ₹21 lakh. The time-weighted return for the year is:

  1. 10.0%
  2. 5.0%
  3. 15.5%
  4. 11.0%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 15.5%

Sub-period 1: 11/10 = 1.10. Sub-period 2: start 11 + 9 = 20, end 21, so 1.05. TWR = 1.10 × 1.05 − 1 = 15.5%. TWR removes the effect of the client's added money.

Question 6

Why should portfolio performance generally be shown net of fees to clients?

  1. Gross returns are always lower than net returns
  2. SEBI does not allow fees to be disclosed
  3. Clients actually receive net returns
  4. Fees have no effect on long-term returns
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Clients actually receive net returns

Fees and expenses directly reduce investor outcomes, so net-of-fee performance reflects the client's experience. Gross figures can overstate the value delivered.

Question 7

A positive Jensen's alpha indicates that the portfolio:

  1. Had lower standard deviation than the market portfolio
  2. Had a beta of exactly 1.0 during the period
  3. Charged no fees to its investors
  4. Earned more than CAPM predicts for its beta
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Earned more than CAPM predicts for its beta

Jensen's alpha = actual return − CAPM required return. A positive value suggests the manager added value beyond what systematic risk would explain.

Question 8

Composite reporting of a PMS strategy's performance means:

  1. Combining all accounts managed under that strategy
  2. Showing only the single best-performing account
  3. Reporting the average of all strategies of every manager in India
  4. Using only accounts opened in the last month
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Combining all accounts managed under that strategy

Composites group all fee-paying discretionary accounts in a strategy, preventing cherry-picking. They give clients a representative picture of the strategy's results.
