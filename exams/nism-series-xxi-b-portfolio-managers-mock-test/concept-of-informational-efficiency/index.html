Concept of Informational Efficiency: NISM Series XXI-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Concept of Informational Efficiency, from the NISM Series XXI-B (Portfolio Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter introduces the Efficient Market Hypothesis and its implications for portfolio management. It explains weak, semi-strong and strong forms of efficiency, tests of efficiency (event studies, random walk, anomalies), market anomalies such as momentum, size, value and calendar effects, and the debate between active and passive management. Focus on what each form of efficiency implies about technical analysis, fundamental analysis and insider information, and how anomalies are interpreted.

Concept of Informational Efficiency questions

Question 1

If markets are semi-strong form efficient, which approach should NOT earn consistent excess returns?

  1. Fundamental analysis of public information
  2. Trading on genuine non-public inside information
  3. Holding a low-cost index fund to match market returns
  4. Diversifying across many securities to reduce risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Fundamental analysis of public information

Semi-strong efficiency means prices reflect all public information, so analysing published data cannot reliably beat the market. Only private information could, and trading on it is illegal.

Question 2

An event study examining share prices around earnings announcements is used to test:

  1. How quickly prices absorb new information
  2. Whether a company's auditors are independent
  3. How many shares the promoters have pledged
  4. Whether a company pays tax on time
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. How quickly prices absorb new information

Event studies measure abnormal returns around events. In an efficient market, prices adjust quickly and fully, leaving no predictable abnormal returns afterwards.

Question 3

The 'momentum anomaly' refers to the tendency for:

  1. Stocks with low trading volume to always rise in price
  2. Recent winners to keep outperforming for a while
  3. All stocks to fall on the first trading day of the month
  4. Large companies to always outperform small companies
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Recent winners to keep outperforming for a while

Studies show stocks with strong recent returns tend to keep outperforming over the following months. This challenges weak-form efficiency, though risk or behaviour may explain it.

Question 4

Strong-form efficiency implies that:

  1. Only technical analysis can earn excess returns in the market
  2. Even insider information cannot earn excess returns
  3. Public information is not reflected in prices at all
  4. Fund managers can always beat the index with research
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Even insider information cannot earn excess returns

Strong-form efficiency means prices reflect all information, public and private. Evidence of insider profits suggests markets are not strong-form efficient.

Question 5

If a market is weak-form efficient, which statement is correct?

  1. Chart patterns reliably forecast price movements
  2. Fundamental analysis is useless in all circumstances
  3. Past prices cannot predict future returns
  4. Prices already reflect all private information
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Past prices cannot predict future returns

Weak-form efficiency means prices already reflect past trading data, so technical analysis cannot consistently earn excess returns. Fundamental analysis may still add value.

Question 6

The growth of passive investing is often supported by the argument that:

  1. Index funds guarantee higher returns than all active funds every year
  2. SEBI has banned active management for large investors
  3. Passive funds do not carry any market risk
  4. Most active funds fail to beat benchmarks after costs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Most active funds fail to beat benchmarks after costs

If markets are reasonably efficient, active managers on average match the market before costs and lag after costs. Passive funds still carry full market risk.

Question 7

A stock rises sharply over three days after positive results are announced, rather than adjusting immediately. This pattern suggests:

  1. Perfect strong-form efficiency in the market
  2. Slow reaction, inconsistent with semi-strong efficiency
  3. That insider trading must have occurred before the results
  4. That the company's results were not public information
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Slow reaction, inconsistent with semi-strong efficiency

Post-announcement drift shows that prices take time to absorb public news, which semi-strong efficiency rules out. It is a well-documented anomaly.

Question 8

Why might apparent market anomalies disappear after they are published?

  1. SEBI bans trading in stocks linked to the anomaly
  2. Stock exchanges stop publishing prices of those stocks
  3. Companies change their accounting year to remove them
  4. Investors trade on them and arbitrage them away
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Investors trade on them and arbitrage them away

Once an anomaly is widely known, investors exploit it, which moves prices and removes the excess return. Some anomalies may also have been data-mining results.
