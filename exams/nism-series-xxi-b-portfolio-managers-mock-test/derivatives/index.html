Derivatives: NISM Series XXI-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Derivatives, from the NISM Series XXI-B (Portfolio Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how portfolio managers use derivatives: futures and options on indices and stocks, pricing through cost of carry, option pay-offs and basic strategies (protective put, covered call, collar, spreads), the option Greeks at an introductory level, and hedging with beta-adjusted futures. It also covers margins, mark-to-market and SEBI's limits on derivatives use in PMS. Practise hedge-ratio and pay-off calculations and understand when derivatives reduce versus add risk.

Derivatives questions

Question 1

A portfolio worth ₹5 crore has a beta of 0.8. The Nifty futures contract value is ₹20 lakh. The number of contracts to sell for a full hedge is:

  1. 25
  2. 16
  3. 20
  4. 40
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 20

Hedge value = portfolio × beta = ₹5 crore × 0.8 = ₹4 crore. Contracts = ₹4 crore ÷ ₹20 lakh = 20. Ignoring beta would suggest 25 contracts, over-hedging the portfolio.

Question 2

A covered call strategy involves:

  1. Buying a call option without owning the underlying shares
  2. Holding a stock and selling a call on it
  3. Selling a put option and holding cash to cover assignment
  4. Buying both a call and a put at the same strike price
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Holding a stock and selling a call on it

The investor owns the shares and writes calls against them to earn premium, accepting a cap on upside. Buying a call and put together is a straddle.

Question 3

An option's delta of 0.6 means that, for a small ₹1 rise in the underlying price, the option premium will change by about:

  1. ₹6.00
  2. ₹1.60
  3. ₹0.60
  4. ₹0.06
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹0.60

Delta measures the sensitivity of the option price to the underlying. A delta of 0.6 implies about ₹0.60 change for a ₹1 move in the underlying, for small changes.

Question 4

A 'collar' on a stock holding is created by:

  1. Selling a put and buying a call on the stock at the same strike
  2. Buying futures and simultaneously buying a call option
  3. Selling the stock and buying a government bond with the proceeds
  4. Buying a put and selling a call on the stock
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Buying a put and selling a call on the stock

A collar buys downside protection (put) and funds it partly by selling upside (call). The result is a range of outcomes bounded on both sides.

Question 5

Option theta measures:

  1. Loss of option value as time passes
  2. The change in option price for a change in interest rates
  3. The change in delta for a change in the underlying price
  4. The change in option price for a change in volatility
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Loss of option value as time passes

Theta captures time decay — options lose value as expiry nears, all else equal. Rho relates to interest rates, gamma to changes in delta and vega to volatility.

Question 6

A portfolio manager expects a stock to stay in a narrow range until expiry. Which strategy best fits this view?

  1. Selling a strangle
  2. Buying a straddle at the current market price
  3. Buying out-of-the-money call options only
  4. Buying the stock's futures with full leverage
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Selling a strangle

Selling out-of-the-money call and put options earns premium if the stock stays within the range. Buying a straddle profits from big moves, the opposite view. Short strangles carry large risk if the range breaks.

Question 7

Initial margin on a futures position is meant to:

  1. Pay the full contract value upfront to the seller
  2. Cover potential one-day losses on the position
  3. Act as brokerage paid to the trading member
  4. Provide a guaranteed return on the investor's capital
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Cover potential one-day losses on the position

Initial margin is collateral sized to cover likely adverse moves, typically based on risk models. Futures require only a fraction of the contract value, which creates leverage.

Question 8

Vega of an option is highest for options that are:

  1. At the money with longer time to expiry
  2. Deep in the money and expiring the same day
  3. Deep out of the money and expiring the same day
  4. Already exercised by the buyer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. At the money with longer time to expiry

Vega measures sensitivity to volatility. At-the-money options with more time remaining have the most time value and therefore respond most to changes in volatility.
