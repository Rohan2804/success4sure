Fixed Income Portfolio Management Strategies: NISM Series XXI-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Fixed Income Portfolio Management Strategies, from the NISM Series XXI-B (Portfolio Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers strategies used to manage bond portfolios: passive approaches (buy-and-hold, indexing), structured approaches (immunisation, cash-flow matching, dedication), and active approaches (duration management based on rate views, yield curve strategies such as bullet, barbell and ladder, sector and credit rotation, riding the yield curve). It also covers liability-driven investing. Understand how each strategy behaves when rates change and how duration, convexity and credit risk are managed.

Fixed Income Portfolio Management Strategies questions

Question 1

A manager expects interest rates to fall sharply. An appropriate active strategy is to:

  1. Shorten portfolio duration to near zero
  2. Sell all bonds and hold only cash
  3. Buy only floating-rate bonds
  4. Lengthen portfolio duration
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Lengthen portfolio duration

Bond prices rise when rates fall, and longer-duration bonds gain more. Shortening duration or holding floaters would reduce the benefit of falling rates.

Question 2

A barbell portfolio holds bonds concentrated at:

  1. One single intermediate maturity
  2. Short and long maturities, with little in between
  3. Evenly spread maturities every year
  4. Only perpetual bonds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Short and long maturities, with little in between

A barbell combines short-term and long-term bonds. A bullet concentrates around one maturity, while a ladder spreads maturities evenly over time.

Question 3

Cash-flow matching for a pension fund involves:

  1. Holding equities that are expected to grow faster than liabilities
  2. Buying bonds whose cash flows match each liability
  3. Keeping the entire portfolio in a savings account
  4. Trading bonds daily to capture price movements
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Buying bonds whose cash flows match each liability

Cash-flow matching dedicates bond coupons and maturities to meet specific future payments, minimising reinvestment and interest rate risk on those liabilities.

Question 4

'Riding the yield curve' is a strategy that:

  1. Buys only the shortest maturity T-bills and rolls them weekly
  2. Holds bonds exactly to maturity in all cases
  3. Invests only in bonds with a flat yield curve
  4. Buys bonds longer than the horizon and sells as they roll down an upward curve
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Buys bonds longer than the horizon and sells as they roll down an upward curve

With an upward-sloping curve, as a bond ages its yield falls along the curve and its price rises. Selling before maturity captures this extra roll-down gain, if the curve stays stable.

Question 5

A laddered bond portfolio is useful for investors who want to:

  1. Make a large bet on a single maturity point
  2. Avoid any interest rate risk completely
  3. Spread reinvestment risk with regular maturities
  4. Hold only long-term bonds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Spread reinvestment risk with regular maturities

A ladder holds bonds maturing at regular intervals, so some money is reinvested each period at prevailing rates, smoothing reinvestment and rate risk.

Question 6

A manager buys AA corporate bonds expecting the spread over G-secs to narrow as the economy improves. This is a:

  1. Credit spread strategy
  2. Pure duration strategy based on rate falls
  3. Cash-flow matching strategy for pensions
  4. Passive indexing strategy
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Credit spread strategy

Credit strategies take positions on changes in credit spreads. If the economy improves and default risk falls, spreads narrow and the bonds outperform G-secs.

Question 7

Immunisation of a bond portfolio is achieved by setting:

  1. Portfolio duration to twice the liability horizon
  2. Portfolio duration equal to the liability horizon
  3. Portfolio duration to zero by holding cash
  4. Portfolio maturity to the longest bond available
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Portfolio duration equal to the liability horizon

Matching duration to the horizon balances price risk against reinvestment risk, so the portfolio value at the horizon is protected against small parallel rate changes.

Question 8

An investor who expects a flattening of the yield curve (long yields falling relative to short yields) would prefer a:

  1. Barbell over a bullet of the same duration
  2. Bullet over a barbell of the same duration
  3. Portfolio of only T-bills
  4. Portfolio of only floating-rate notes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Barbell over a bullet of the same duration

Barbells have more convexity and more exposure to long maturities. When the curve flattens, the long bonds in the barbell gain, so the barbell tends to outperform a bullet.
