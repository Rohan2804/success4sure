Operational Aspects of Portfolio Managers: NISM Series XXI-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Operational Aspects of Portfolio Managers, from the NISM Series XXI-B (Portfolio Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers day-to-day operations of PMS: client onboarding, KYC and agreement, the disclosure document, custodian and bank account arrangements, trade execution and allocation, fee and expense computation, high-water mark mechanics, periodic statements and annual audit, handling of corporate actions, and closure or transfer of accounts. Practise fee computations with high-water marks and hurdle rates, and know what information clients must receive and how often.

Operational Aspects of Portfolio Managers questions

Question 1

A client's portfolio is ₹1.20 crore at the end of year 1 (high-water mark) and ₹1.10 crore at the end of year 2. A 20% performance fee over the high-water mark in year 2 is:

  1. ₹2 lakh
  2. ₹4 lakh
  3. Nil
  4. ₹22 lakh
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Nil

The year-2 value is below the high-water mark of ₹1.20 crore, so no new gains exist and no performance fee is payable. Fees resume only after the portfolio exceeds ₹1.20 crore.

Question 2

Portfolio managers must get each client's portfolio and accounts audited:

  1. Only when the client asks for an audit in writing
  2. Annually by an independent chartered accountant
  3. Once every five years by the stock exchange
  4. Daily by the client's distributor
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Annually by an independent chartered accountant

SEBI requires annual audit of client accounts by an independent chartered accountant, with a copy of the certificate given to the client. This independently verifies holdings and fees.

Question 3

Operating expenses charged to a PMS client, excluding brokerage, are:

  1. Unlimited, provided they are mentioned somewhere in the agreement
  2. Capped by SEBI as a percentage of average AUM
  3. Paid fully by the distributor, never by the client
  4. Charged only when the client's portfolio makes a loss
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Capped by SEBI as a percentage of average AUM

SEBI caps operating expenses (excluding brokerage) as a percentage of the client's average daily AUM. This prevents managers from loading clients with unlimited costs.

Question 4

A PMS client wants to close the account and receive her securities instead of cash. The portfolio manager should:

  1. Insist on selling all securities and paying only cash
  2. Transfer the securities to her own demat account
  3. Hold the securities until the next financial year
  4. Transfer the securities to the distributor's account
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Transfer the securities to her own demat account

Clients may choose to receive securities in specie on closure. The manager must arrange transfer to the client's own demat account after settling dues as per the agreement.

Question 5

When the PM buys a stock for many discretionary clients in one block order, the shares must be allocated:

  1. First to clients who pay the highest management fees
  2. Only to the client accounts of the PM's own employees
  3. Fairly as per a pre-defined allocation policy
  4. Randomly, without keeping any records of the allocation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Fairly as per a pre-defined allocation policy

Block trades must be allocated according to a documented fair-allocation policy with records. Favouring higher-fee or related accounts is a conflict of interest.

Question 6

A portfolio of ₹80 lakh grows to ₹96 lakh. The hurdle rate is 10% and the performance fee is 20% of returns above the hurdle (no catch-up). The performance fee is:

  1. ₹1.6 lakh
  2. ₹3.2 lakh
  3. ₹0.8 lakh
  4. ₹1.92 lakh
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹1.6 lakh

Hurdle value = 80 × 1.10 = ₹88 lakh. Excess = 96 − 88 = ₹8 lakh. Fee = 20% × 8 = ₹1.6 lakh. Charging 20% on the full ₹16 lakh gain would give ₹3.2 lakh.

Question 7

Corporate actions such as dividends and bonus shares on PMS holdings are:

  1. Credited to the respective client's account
  2. Kept by the portfolio manager as part of its fee income
  3. Shared equally among all clients regardless of holdings
  4. Ignored because PMS accounts do not receive corporate actions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Credited to the respective client's account

Since securities are held in each client's name, dividends, bonuses and other benefits accrue to that client. The manager must reflect them in client statements.

Question 8

Before a portfolio manager changes the fee structure for an existing client, it should:

  1. Simply deduct the new fees from the next quarter onwards
  2. Inform only the distributor, who will tell the client later
  3. Publish the change on its website and assume consent
  4. Obtain the client's consent and amend the agreement
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Obtain the client's consent and amend the agreement

Fees are part of the client agreement, so changes require the client's informed consent. Deducting revised fees without agreement breaches the contract and SEBI norms.
