Free NISM Series XXI-B practice questions

Question 1 · Investment Landscape

Gold held in physical form is generally considered a:

  1. Financial asset with fixed coupons
  2. Short-term debt instrument
  3. Real asset often used as an inflation and crisis hedge
  4. Equity-like claim on profits
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Real asset often used as an inflation and crisis hedge

Gold is a tangible asset with no cash flows, often held for diversification and hedging.

Question 2 · Introduction to Securities Markets

A market order on the exchange:

  1. Executes immediately at the best available price
  2. Executes only at a specified limit price
  3. Is valid only after market close
  4. Is used only for bonds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Executes immediately at the best available price

Market orders prioritise execution over price.

Question 3 · Investing in Stocks

A company with return on equity of 20% and retention ratio of 60% has a sustainable growth rate of:

  1. 8%
  2. 12%
  3. 20%
  4. 32%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 12%

g = ROE × retention = 20% × 0.6 = 12%.

Question 4 · Investing in Fixed Income Securities

For two bonds with the same maturity, the one with the lower coupon will have:

  1. Lower duration
  2. No interest rate risk
  3. Identical duration
  4. Higher duration and greater price sensitivity to yield changes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Higher duration and greater price sensitivity to yield changes

Lower coupons push more cash flow to maturity, increasing duration.

Question 5 · Derivatives

A protective put strategy involves:

  1. Selling a stock and selling a call
  2. Holding a stock and buying a put option on it
  3. Buying a call and selling the stock short
  4. Holding cash and selling puts
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Holding a stock and buying a put option on it

The put sets a floor on losses while preserving upside, like insurance.

Question 6 · Mutual Fund

Mutual fund schemes are required to benchmark against:

  1. Price return index only
  2. Any index the AMC chooses in a given month
  3. Fixed deposit rates
  4. Total return variant of the index
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Total return variant of the index

SEBI requires TRI benchmarks so dividends are reflected, as they are in scheme NAVs.

Question 7 · Role of Portfolio Managers

A portfolio manager is obliged to act in a fiduciary capacity towards clients. This means the manager must:

  1. Place clients' interests ahead of its own and avoid or disclose conflicts
  2. Guarantee returns to clients
  3. Invest only in fixed deposits
  4. Follow the distributor's instructions
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Place clients' interests ahead of its own and avoid or disclose conflicts

Fiduciary duty requires loyalty, care and transparent handling of conflicts.

Question 8 · Operational Aspects of Portfolio Managers

Accounts of each PMS client must be audited annually by:

  1. The distributor
  2. The client's employer
  3. An independent chartered accountant, with a copy of the certificate given to the client
  4. The stock exchange
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. An independent chartered accountant, with a copy of the certificate given to the client

Annual independent audit of client accounts provides assurance to clients.

Question 9 · Portfolio Management Process

An investment policy statement (IPS) primarily serves to:

  1. Document the client's objectives, constraints and the agreed investment approach
  2. Report monthly NAV
  3. Register the client with SEBI
  4. Replace the KYC form
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Document the client's objectives, constraints and the agreed investment approach

The IPS guides decisions and evaluation and reduces misunderstandings.

Question 10 · Taxation

In PMS, the tax on gains from each sale in the portfolio is determined by:

  1. The portfolio manager's tax rate
  2. The client's own holding period and tax status, as if the client invested directly
  3. A flat rate set by SEBI
  4. The fund's NAV
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The client's own holding period and tax status, as if the client invested directly

PMS portfolios are owned by the client, so each transaction is taxed in the client's hands.

Question 11 · Regulatory, Governance and Ethical Aspects of Portfolio Managers

Under the SEBI (Portfolio Managers) Regulations, 2020, a portfolio manager must maintain a minimum net worth of:

  1. ₹2 crore
  2. ₹50 lakh
  3. ₹5 crore
  4. ₹10 crore
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹5 crore

The 2020 Regulations raised the net worth requirement to ₹5 crore.

Question 12 · Introduction to Indices

A price-weighted index gives more weight to:

  1. Companies with larger market capitalisation
  2. Stocks with higher dividend yields
  3. All stocks equally
  4. Stocks with higher share prices
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Stocks with higher share prices

In a price-weighted index, higher-priced shares have greater influence on movements.

Question 13 · Concept of Informational Efficiency

If historical prices cannot be used to earn consistent excess returns, the market is at least:

  1. Strong-form efficient
  2. Semi-strong form efficient
  3. Inefficient
  4. Weak-form efficient
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Weak-form efficient

Weak-form efficiency implies past price data is fully reflected in current prices.

Question 14 · Behavioral Finance

An investor refuses to sell a stock that has fallen sharply, hoping to 'get back to break-even', while quickly selling winners. This is:

  1. Herding
  2. Home bias
  3. The disposition effect
  4. Overconfidence in forecasting
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The disposition effect

The disposition effect is the tendency to hold losers too long and sell winners too early.

Question 15 · Introduction to Modern Portfolio Theory

Combining two assets that are less than perfectly correlated results in:

  1. A portfolio risk higher than both assets
  2. A portfolio risk lower than the weighted average of individual risks
  3. No change in risk
  4. Zero risk always
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A portfolio risk lower than the weighted average of individual risks

Diversification benefit arises when correlation is below +1.
