Taxation: NISM Series XXI-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Taxation, from the NISM Series XXI-B (Portfolio Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains the taxation of investments relevant to portfolio managers and their clients: capital gains on equity, debt and mutual fund units, holding period rules, short-term and long-term rates, indexation (where applicable), set-off and carry-forward of losses, dividend and interest income, STT, GST on fees, TDS for non-residents and tax treatment of PMS expenses. Rates and rules change frequently, especially through Union Budgets and the new Income-tax Act, so focus on principles and confirm current rates.

Taxation questions

Question 1

For capital gains purposes, the holding period of shares bought through PMS is counted from:

  1. The date the client signed the PMS agreement with the manager
  2. The start of the financial year in which the shares were bought
  3. The date the portfolio manager obtained SEBI registration
  4. The date the client acquired each share
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The date the client acquired each share

Each security in the client's name has its own acquisition date, which determines whether a gain is short-term or long-term. The agreement date or financial year start is irrelevant.

Question 2

When a client transfers existing shares into a PMS account (no sale), the transfer generally:

  1. Is not a taxable sale
  2. Triggers long-term capital gains tax on the full value
  3. Is treated as a gift taxable in the manager's hands
  4. Requires payment of STT at the time of transfer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Is not a taxable sale

Moving securities into a PMS account held in the client's own name does not change ownership, so there is no sale and no capital gain. Original cost and dates carry over.

Question 3

GST on portfolio management fees is borne by:

  1. The custodian, from its own revenue
  2. The client, as part of fees charged
  3. SEBI, as part of investor protection
  4. The companies whose shares are held in the portfolio
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The client, as part of fees charged

Portfolio management is a taxable service, so GST is levied on management and performance fees and collected from the client along with the fee.

Question 4

A client has a short-term capital loss from listed shares and a long-term capital gain from listed shares in the same year. The loss:

  1. Can be set off only against salary income
  2. Can be set off against the long-term gain
  3. Must be carried forward and cannot be used this year
  4. Is permanently lost if it is not used against short-term gains
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Can be set off against the long-term gain

Short-term capital losses can be set off against both short-term and long-term capital gains in the same year. Long-term losses, however, can only be set off against long-term gains.

Question 5

A client sells shares and immediately buys them back mainly to book a loss for tax purposes. A portfolio manager should note that:

  1. Tax and anti-avoidance rules may apply
  2. The loss can always be claimed with no conditions whatsoever
  3. Such trades are banned by SEBI in all circumstances
  4. The client will automatically receive a refund of all past taxes
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Tax and anti-avoidance rules may apply

Tax-loss harvesting can be legitimate, but transactions mainly designed to avoid tax may be examined under tax rules. Clients should seek professional tax advice.

Question 6

Interest received on corporate bonds held in a PMS account is taxed as:

  1. Long-term capital gains at a concessional rate
  2. Exempt income because bonds are listed
  3. Income at the client's slab rate
  4. Business income of the portfolio manager
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Income at the client's slab rate

Interest is taxed as income from other sources at the client's applicable slab rate, regardless of whether the bond is listed. Gains on sale of the bond are taxed separately as capital gains.

Question 7

For a non-resident PMS client, which is generally the portfolio manager's responsibility in relation to capital gains?

  1. Paying the client's tax from its own net worth
  2. Deciding the client's country of tax residence
  3. Claiming treaty benefits without any documents
  4. Facilitating TDS as required by law
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Facilitating TDS as required by law

For NRIs, tax must be deducted at source on capital gains, and the manager or its custodian facilitates this. Treaty benefits need documents such as a tax residency certificate.

Question 8

Why do portfolio managers often track 'tax lots' in client portfolios?

  1. To decide the number of shares a company may issue
  2. To compute gains using correct cost and dates
  3. To set the price band for a stock on the exchange
  4. To calculate the market capitalisation of an index
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. To compute gains using correct cost and dates

Each purchase creates a tax lot with its own cost and date. Tracking lots lets the manager compute gains accurately and choose sales that are more tax-efficient.
