Investment Landscape: NISM Series XXI-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Investment Landscape, from the NISM Series XXI-B (Portfolio Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter gives the portfolio manager's view of the Indian investment landscape: household savings and their shift towards financial assets, the main asset classes (equity, fixed income, real estate, gold, cash and alternatives), the role of financial intermediaries and the range of investment products available. It also introduces the concepts of risk, return, liquidity, inflation and the investor life cycle. Focus on how different investor segments use different products and why portfolio management services fit high-net-worth investors with specific needs.

Investment Landscape questions

Question 1

Over the long run, the main reason an investor accepts equity volatility is:

  1. Equity prices are protected by SEBI against falls of more than 10% a year
  2. Equity dividends are fixed in advance like bank deposit interest
  3. The potential to earn returns above inflation
  4. Equity markets in India never fall over any three-year period
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The potential to earn returns above inflation

Equity offers ownership in growing businesses, giving potential returns above inflation over long periods. Prices are not protected, dividends are not fixed and multi-year losses can occur.

Question 2

An investor in the 'accumulation' phase of the life cycle typically:

  1. Draws down savings to meet everyday living expenses after retirement
  2. Transfers wealth to heirs through wills and trusts as the main priority
  3. Builds wealth with a long horizon and steady income
  4. Holds almost all wealth in cash because no goals remain
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Builds wealth with a long horizon and steady income

The accumulation phase covers working years, when income exceeds spending and the horizon is long, allowing more growth assets. Drawing down and wealth transfer belong to later stages.

Question 3

Which asset class generally offers the highest liquidity with the lowest price risk?

  1. Cash and short-term money-market instruments
  2. Unlisted shares of a private company in a growing sector
  3. A commercial office property in a metro city
  4. Listed small-cap equity shares with low trading volume
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Cash and short-term money-market instruments

Cash and money-market instruments can be converted quickly with little change in value. Unlisted shares, property and thinly traded small caps are slow or costly to sell.

Question 4

Financialisation of household savings in India refers to:

  1. A shift from mutual funds back to gold jewellery and land
  2. The government taking control of all household bank deposits
  3. A shift from physical assets to financial assets
  4. Banks converting savings accounts into fixed deposits automatically
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A shift from physical assets to financial assets

Indian households have traditionally held much wealth in gold and real estate. Financialisation describes the growing share of savings going into deposits, mutual funds, equities, insurance and pensions.

Question 5

An investor's nominal portfolio return is 10% and inflation is 6.5%. The approximate real return is:

  1. 16.5%
  2. 6.5%
  3. 1.5%
  4. 3.5%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 3.5%

Approximate real return = nominal return − inflation = 10 − 6.5 = 3.5%. The exact figure is 1.10 / 1.065 − 1 ≈ 3.3%, slightly lower than the approximation.

Question 6

Which product would generally NOT be considered a market-linked investment?

  1. A bank fixed deposit
  2. An equity-oriented mutual fund scheme bought through SIP
  3. Units of a listed REIT traded on the exchange
  4. A discretionary PMS equity strategy
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A bank fixed deposit

A fixed deposit pays a contracted rate regardless of market movement. Mutual fund units, REIT units and PMS portfolios fluctuate with market prices.

Question 7

A high-net-worth investor chooses PMS over a mutual fund mainly to obtain:

  1. A guaranteed return that is higher than any mutual fund scheme can offer
  2. A customised portfolio held in her own name
  3. Exemption from all taxes on capital gains made in the portfolio
  4. The ability to invest with a ticket size of just ₹5,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A customised portfolio held in her own name

PMS offers individual portfolios, direct ownership and tailoring to the client's needs. It carries no guarantee, no tax exemption and requires a minimum of ₹50 lakh.

Question 8

Liquidity risk for an investor means the risk that:

  1. The company issuing the asset will stop all business operations
  2. An asset cannot be sold quickly without a price concession
  3. Inflation will be higher than the return on the asset
  4. Interest rates will fall after the asset is bought
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. An asset cannot be sold quickly without a price concession

Liquidity risk concerns the ability to convert an asset to cash at a fair price when needed. Business failure is credit or business risk; inflation and rate changes are separate risks.
