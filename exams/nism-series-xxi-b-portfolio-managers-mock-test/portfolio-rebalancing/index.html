Portfolio Rebalancing: NISM Series XXI-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Portfolio Rebalancing, from the NISM Series XXI-B (Portfolio Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains why and how portfolios are rebalanced. Topics include drift from target allocations, calendar and threshold (percentage-range) rebalancing, constant-mix, buy-and-hold and CPPI strategies and how each performs in trending and oscillating markets, transaction costs and taxes in rebalancing, and the use of cash flows and derivatives to rebalance efficiently. Understand the trade-off between staying close to target risk and incurring costs, and be able to compute the trades needed to rebalance.

Portfolio Rebalancing questions

Question 1

A ₹1 crore portfolio targets 50% equity and 50% debt. After a rally, equity is ₹62 lakh and debt ₹48 lakh. To rebalance, the manager should:

  1. Sell ₹7 lakh of equity and buy ₹7 lakh of debt
  2. Sell ₹12 lakh of equity and buy ₹12 lakh of debt
  3. Buy ₹7 lakh of equity and sell ₹7 lakh of debt
  4. Sell ₹14 lakh of equity and hold it in cash
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Sell ₹7 lakh of equity and buy ₹7 lakh of debt

Total = ₹110 lakh, so the target is ₹55 lakh each. Equity must fall by 62 − 55 = ₹7 lakh and debt must rise by 55 − 48 = ₹7 lakh.

Question 2

Threshold (range-based) rebalancing means rebalancing:

  1. On a fixed date every year regardless of the portfolio's drift
  2. Only when the client requests it in writing
  3. When weights drift beyond set bands
  4. Every day to keep exact target weights
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. When weights drift beyond set bands

Range-based rebalancing acts when an asset's weight moves beyond limits, such as ±5%. It ties trading to actual drift rather than the calendar.

Question 3

A constant-mix strategy tends to perform best in markets that:

  1. Trend strongly upwards for many years without reversal
  2. Fluctuate up and down without a clear trend
  3. Trend strongly downwards for many years without reversal
  4. Have zero volatility at all times
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Fluctuate up and down without a clear trend

Constant-mix sells after rises and buys after falls, which pays off when prices oscillate. In strong trends it underperforms buy-and-hold, as it keeps selling winners.

Question 4

A CPPI strategy with a floor of ₹80 lakh, portfolio value ₹1 crore and multiplier 3 would invest in equity:

  1. ₹20 lakh
  2. ₹60 lakh
  3. ₹80 lakh
  4. ₹100 lakh
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹60 lakh

Cushion = 100 − 80 = ₹20 lakh. Equity = multiplier × cushion = 3 × 20 = ₹60 lakh. The rest, ₹40 lakh, goes into safe assets.

Question 5

Using fresh client inflows to buy underweight assets instead of selling overweight ones helps to:

  1. Increase the portfolio's turnover and short-term gains
  2. Rebalance with fewer costs and taxes
  3. Avoid ever reviewing the portfolio's allocation
  4. Guarantee higher returns than the benchmark
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Rebalance with fewer costs and taxes

Directing new money to underweight assets moves the portfolio towards target without selling, reducing brokerage and avoiding capital gains taxes.

Question 6

Why might a manager decide not to rebalance a small drift of 1% from target?

  1. Rebalancing is not permitted under SEBI rules
  2. Small drifts always correct themselves the next day
  3. The client's IPS never mentions target weights
  4. Costs and taxes may exceed the benefit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Costs and taxes may exceed the benefit

Each rebalance incurs transaction costs and possibly taxes. If drift is small, the risk reduction gained may not justify those costs; bands help manage this trade-off.

Question 7

A buy-and-hold strategy, compared with constant-mix, has a pay-off that is:

  1. Concave, gaining in oscillating markets
  2. Convex, protecting a fixed floor value
  3. Always identical to CPPI's pay-off
  4. Linear in the risky asset's return
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Linear in the risky asset's return

Buy-and-hold never trades, so its value moves linearly with the risky asset. Constant-mix is concave and CPPI convex, offering protection in falling markets.

Question 8

Using index futures to rebalance a large portfolio temporarily is attractive because it:

  1. Removes all market risk from the portfolio permanently
  2. Avoids the need for any margin payments
  3. Changes exposure quickly at low cost
  4. Is the only rebalancing method permitted by SEBI
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Changes exposure quickly at low cost

Futures let managers adjust equity exposure rapidly without selling many individual stocks. They require margins and must be managed within regulatory limits.
