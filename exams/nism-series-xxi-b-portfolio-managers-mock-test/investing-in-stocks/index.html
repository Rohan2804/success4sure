Investing in Stocks: NISM Series XXI-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Investing in Stocks, from the NISM Series XXI-B (Portfolio Managers) syllabus.

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers equity research and valuation for portfolio managers. It explains top-down and bottom-up approaches, economy and industry analysis, company analysis using financial statements, key ratios (ROE, ROCE, margins, leverage, turnover), valuation through discounted cash flow, dividend discount and relative multiples (P/E, P/B, EV/EBITDA), and an introduction to technical analysis. Practise calculations such as the Gordon growth model, sustainable growth and EV/EBITDA, and learn which method suits which kind of company.

Investing in Stocks questions

Question 1

A company is expected to pay a dividend of ₹6 next year, growing at 8% forever. If the required return is 14%, its value per share under the Gordon growth model is:

  1. ₹75
  2. ₹100
  3. ₹42.86
  4. ₹108
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. ₹100

Value = D1 / (r − g) = 6 / (0.14 − 0.08) = 6 / 0.06 = ₹100. The model works only when the required return exceeds the growth rate.

Question 2

Enterprise value (EV) of a company is generally calculated as:

  1. Market cap − debt + cash and cash equivalents held
  2. Market cap + debt − cash
  3. Book value of equity + reserves − all current liabilities
  4. Net profit × dividend payout ratio × number of shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Market cap + debt − cash

EV measures the value of the whole business to all capital providers. Debt is added because an acquirer must pay it off, and cash is subtracted because it reduces the net cost.

Question 3

A company has EV of ₹1,200 crore and EBITDA of ₹150 crore. Its EV/EBITDA multiple is:

  1. 12×
  2. 8×
  3. 6×
  4. 10×
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 8×

EV/EBITDA = 1,200 ÷ 150 = 8. This multiple is useful for comparing companies with different capital structures, because EV includes debt and EBITDA is before interest.

Question 4

Return on capital employed (ROCE) differs from ROE mainly because ROCE:

  1. Uses net profit after tax divided only by shareholders' equity
  2. Ignores debt and measures only the dividend paid per share
  3. Is calculated only for banks and insurance companies
  4. Uses operating profit on equity plus debt
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Uses operating profit on equity plus debt

ROCE = EBIT ÷ (equity + debt), showing returns on all capital in the business. ROE uses net profit on equity only and can be inflated by leverage.

Question 5

A bottom-up stock selection approach begins with:

  1. A forecast of GDP growth, inflation and interest rates for the economy
  2. Choosing the best-performing sectors for the next two years first
  3. Selecting a benchmark index and copying its weights exactly
  4. Analysis of individual companies
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Analysis of individual companies

Bottom-up investors focus on company fundamentals first, regardless of economic or sector views. Top-down investing starts from the economy and then narrows to sectors and stocks.

Question 6

A firm's ROE is 18% and it pays out 40% of earnings as dividends. Its sustainable growth rate is approximately:

  1. 7.2%
  2. 10.8%
  3. 18.0%
  4. 22.0%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 10.8%

Sustainable growth = ROE × retention ratio = 18% × (1 − 0.40) = 18% × 0.60 = 10.8%. Retained earnings reinvested at the ROE drive growth without new equity.

Question 7

A discounted cash flow (DCF) valuation is most sensitive to:

  1. The discount rate and terminal growth assumptions
  2. The company's face value per share and its listing date
  3. The number of shareholders who attended the last AGM
  4. The colour and design of the company's annual report
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The discount rate and terminal growth assumptions

Most DCF value often lies in the terminal value, which depends heavily on long-term growth and the discount rate. Small changes in these inputs can change the valuation substantially.

Question 8

A 'moving average crossover' signal is a tool of:

  1. Technical analysis
  2. Fundamental analysis of the company's balance sheet and cash flows
  3. Credit analysis of the company's bonds by rating agencies
  4. Macroeconomic analysis of the country's current account
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Technical analysis

Technical analysts use price-based indicators such as moving averages; a short-term average crossing above a long-term one is read as bullish. Fundamental analysis studies business value.
