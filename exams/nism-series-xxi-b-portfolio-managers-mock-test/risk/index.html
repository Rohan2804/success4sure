Risk: NISM Series XXI-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Risk, from the NISM Series XXI-B (Portfolio Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter discusses the types and measurement of risk in portfolio management. It covers systematic and unsystematic risk, market, interest rate, credit, liquidity, currency, inflation, reinvestment, concentration and operational risks, and measures such as standard deviation, variance, beta, downside deviation, tracking error, Value at Risk, drawdown and stress tests. Focus on matching each risk to its measure and on understanding what each statistic does and does not reveal about a portfolio.

Risk questions

Question 1

Which risk would most affect an Indian investor holding US technology shares when the rupee strengthens against the dollar?

  1. Reinvestment risk on bond coupons received during the year
  2. Currency risk
  3. Credit risk of the Indian government on its rupee bonds
  4. Liquidity risk in Indian small-cap stocks
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Currency risk

When the rupee strengthens, dollar assets are worth fewer rupees, reducing returns for an Indian investor. This is currency or exchange rate risk.

Question 2

A portfolio has a 95% one-day VaR of ₹8 lakh. Which statement is correct?

  1. The portfolio will never lose more than ₹8 lakh on any single day
  2. The portfolio is expected to lose ₹8 lakh on 95% of days
  3. There is a 5% chance of losing more than ₹8 lakh in a day
  4. The portfolio's expected daily gain is ₹8 lakh
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. There is a 5% chance of losing more than ₹8 lakh in a day

VaR at 95% gives the loss level exceeded on about 5% of days. It does not cap losses, and it says nothing about how large losses are beyond that level.

Question 3

Concentration risk in a portfolio arises when:

  1. A large share is in one stock, sector or issuer
  2. The portfolio holds a broad mix across many sectors and asset classes
  3. The client invests a small amount through monthly SIPs
  4. The portfolio is benchmarked against a total return index
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A large share is in one stock, sector or issuer

Heavy exposure to a single security, issuer or sector means one adverse event can cause large losses. Diversification and limits help manage this risk.

Question 4

Operational risk for a portfolio manager includes:

  1. Losses from failed processes, systems or people
  2. Losses from a general fall in equity markets across the country
  3. Changes in interest rates that affect bond prices
  4. Changes in the exchange rate of the rupee
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Losses from failed processes, systems or people

Operational risk covers errors, fraud, system failures and process breakdowns, such as wrong trade entries. Market, rate and currency risks are separate categories.

Question 5

A portfolio's returns over four years were 10%, −5%, 15% and 0%. The arithmetic mean return is:

  1. 5.0%
  2. 4.5%
  3. 7.5%
  4. 20.0%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 5.0%

Arithmetic mean = (10 − 5 + 15 + 0) ÷ 4 = 20 ÷ 4 = 5%. The geometric mean, which reflects compounding, would be slightly lower.

Question 6

Downside deviation is preferred to standard deviation by some investors because it:

  1. Treats upside and downside surprises as equally bad
  2. Ignores all negative returns in the period
  3. Is always larger than standard deviation
  4. Counts only returns below a target
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Counts only returns below a target

Investors usually worry about falls, not gains. Downside deviation measures volatility below a minimum acceptable return, and is used in the Sortino ratio.

Question 7

Which risk measure is most relevant for an index fund manager?

  1. Absolute VaR of the client's bank deposits
  2. Tracking error
  3. Credit rating of the custodian
  4. Number of trades per day
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Tracking error

An index fund aims to replicate its benchmark, so the key risk is deviation from the index return, measured by tracking error.

Question 8

Inflation risk is greatest for which investor?

  1. A young investor holding a diversified equity portfolio for 20 years
  2. A retiree with long-term fixed-income bonds
  3. An investor holding inflation-indexed government bonds
  4. A business owner whose prices rise with inflation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A retiree with long-term fixed-income bonds

Fixed nominal payments lose purchasing power when inflation rises. Equity and inflation-indexed bonds offer some protection, as do businesses able to raise prices.
