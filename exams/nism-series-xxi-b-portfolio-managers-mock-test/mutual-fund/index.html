Mutual Fund: NISM Series XXI-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Mutual Fund, from the NISM Series XXI-B (Portfolio Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers mutual funds as a comparison and complement to PMS. Topics include the three-tier structure (sponsor, trustee, AMC), types of schemes under SEBI categorisation, NAV computation, expense ratio and loads, direct and regular plans, systematic plans (SIP, SWP, STP), riskometer, scheme documents, and taxation of mutual fund units. Portfolio managers should understand how mutual fund costs, liquidity and regulation compare with PMS when advising clients.

Mutual Fund questions

Question 1

In India, the entity that holds mutual fund assets in trust for unitholders is the:

  1. Board of trustees or trustee company
  2. Asset management company that runs the investment portfolio
  3. Registrar and transfer agent that maintains investor records
  4. Stock exchange where the fund's trades are executed
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Board of trustees or trustee company

Mutual funds are set up as trusts. Trustees hold assets for unitholders and oversee the AMC, which manages the money. RTAs and exchanges provide services but do not own assets.

Question 2

A scheme's total expense ratio (TER) is charged:

  1. Daily, through a reduction in NAV
  2. Once a year as a separate invoice sent to each investor
  3. Only at the time of redemption as a lump sum
  4. Only on investors who invest through distributors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Daily, through a reduction in NAV

TER is accrued daily and deducted from scheme assets, so the NAV reflects it. It applies to all investors in a plan; direct plans have a lower TER than regular plans.

Question 3

A Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) is most useful for an investor who wants to:

  1. Invest a fixed amount every month from salary into equity schemes
  2. Shift money from a debt scheme to an equity scheme in instalments
  3. Receive regular cash flows from investments
  4. Lock in money for three years for tax benefits under the scheme
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Receive regular cash flows from investments

SWP redeems units periodically to provide regular income. SIP invests regularly, STP transfers between schemes and ELSS carries a lock-in.

Question 4

Under SEBI's scheme categorisation, a flexi-cap fund must invest at least:

  1. 25% each in large, mid and small-cap stocks at all times
  2. 80% in large-cap stocks and the rest in debt
  3. 65% in equity across market caps
  4. 100% in mid-cap and small-cap stocks only
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 65% in equity across market caps

Flexi-cap funds must keep at least 65% in equity with freedom across market caps. Multi-cap funds, in contrast, must hold at least 25% each in large, mid and small caps.

Question 5

A key difference between a mutual fund and a PMS for the investor is that, in a mutual fund:

  1. Each trade made by the fund manager is taxed in the investor's hands
  2. Tax arises only when the investor redeems units
  3. The investor's minimum investment is ₹50 lakh in all cases
  4. The investor can instruct the fund manager on which stocks to buy
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Tax arises only when the investor redeems units

Mutual fund trades inside the scheme do not create tax for unitholders; tax arises on redemption or transfer of units. In PMS, each sale is taxed in the client's hands.

Question 6

An investor puts ₹12,000 into a scheme at NAV ₹40 and redeems all units when NAV is ₹46, with no loads. The absolute return is:

  1. 6%
  2. 13%
  3. 20%
  4. 15%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 15%

Units = 12,000 ÷ 40 = 300. Redemption value = 300 × 46 = ₹13,800. Absolute return = 1,800 ÷ 12,000 = 15%, the same as (46 − 40) ÷ 40.

Question 7

The riskometer of a mutual fund scheme is:

  1. Fixed at launch and never changed for the life of the scheme
  2. Decided each year by the investors through a vote
  3. Reviewed monthly based on the scheme's portfolio
  4. Shown only to institutional investors and not to retail investors
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Reviewed monthly based on the scheme's portfolio

SEBI requires AMCs to evaluate the riskometer monthly using portfolio-based parameters and disclose it, so investors can see changes in scheme risk.

Question 8

Which mutual fund category is designed to track an index with minimal deviation?

  1. Focused funds holding up to 30 high-conviction stocks
  2. Contra funds that buy out-of-favour sectors
  3. Dynamic asset allocation funds changing equity exposure
  4. Index funds and ETFs
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Index funds and ETFs

Passive funds replicate an index, aiming for low tracking error and low costs. Focused, contra and dynamic funds are actively managed.
