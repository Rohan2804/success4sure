Role of Portfolio Managers: NISM Series XXI-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Role of Portfolio Managers, from the NISM Series XXI-B (Portfolio Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains the role and responsibilities of portfolio managers under the SEBI (Portfolio Managers) Regulations, 2020. It covers the types of PMS services, the minimum investment, client eligibility including accredited investors, the qualifications and certification of the principal officer and key staff, the manager's fiduciary and suitability obligations, and how PMS strategies are designed and offered. Know clearly the distinctions between discretionary, non-discretionary and advisory mandates and what each implies for decision-making and liability.

Role of Portfolio Managers questions

Question 1

A portfolio manager registered with SEBI can accept as a client:

  1. Only resident individuals, never companies, trusts or NRIs
  2. Only investors who already hold a mutual fund account
  3. Only investors recommended by a stock exchange
  4. Any eligible investor meeting PMS norms
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Any eligible investor meeting PMS norms

PMS is open to individuals, HUFs, companies, trusts, NRIs and others who meet KYC and minimum investment norms. There is no requirement to hold mutual funds or be referred by an exchange.

Question 2

Accredited investors in PMS may be offered:

  1. Lower minimums under the large value framework
  2. Guaranteed returns linked to the repo rate
  3. Exemption from KYC and anti-money laundering checks
  4. Permission to use unlimited leverage on their accounts
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Lower minimums under the large value framework

SEBI allows portfolio managers to offer accredited investors flexibilities such as relaxation of the ₹50 lakh minimum under the large value accredited investor framework. KYC and leverage rules still apply.

Question 3

In a discretionary PMS, who decides which securities to buy and sell?

  1. The client, who must approve every single trade in advance
  2. The portfolio manager, within the agreement
  3. The custodian, based on its own market view
  4. The distributor who introduced the client to the manager
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The portfolio manager, within the agreement

Under a discretionary mandate, the client gives the manager authority to take investment decisions within the agreed objectives and restrictions. The custodian and distributor do not decide trades.

Question 4

Under the 2020 Regulations, the principal officer of a portfolio manager must have:

  1. Only a degree in any discipline without any work experience
  2. Required qualification, experience and NISM certification
  3. At least 25 years of experience as a stock broker
  4. A licence from the Reserve Bank of India
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Required qualification, experience and NISM certification

SEBI specifies minimum professional qualifications, relevant experience and NISM certification for the principal officer, who is responsible for investment decisions and operations.

Question 5

A portfolio manager's 'suitability' obligation requires it to:

  1. Offer every client the strategy with the highest past return
  2. Accept any client who meets the minimum investment, whatever their needs
  3. Ensure that no client portfolio ever shows a loss
  4. Match strategies to the client's risk profile and goals
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Match strategies to the client's risk profile and goals

Suitability means recommending only what fits the client's financial situation, risk tolerance and objectives. Past returns or minimum eligibility alone do not make a product suitable.

Question 6

Which activity falls outside the scope of a portfolio manager's services?

  1. Managing a client's equity portfolio on a discretionary basis
  2. Advising a client on securities under an advisory mandate
  3. Executing trades for a client after approval under non-discretionary PMS
  4. Accepting deposits from clients with a fixed interest rate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Accepting deposits from clients with a fixed interest rate

Portfolio managers manage or advise on securities portfolios. Accepting deposits at fixed interest is a banking or NBFC activity and is not permitted for portfolio managers.

Question 7

Employees of a portfolio manager involved in investment decision-making are required to:

  1. Hold a separate SEBI registration as research analysts in all cases
  2. Also register with IRDAI as insurance agents
  3. Obtain the specified NISM certification
  4. Have at least 15 years of experience in banking
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Obtain the specified NISM certification

SEBI requires the principal officer and employees in decision-making roles to hold the relevant NISM certification (Series XXI-B) to ensure minimum knowledge standards.

Question 8

A client asks a discretionary portfolio manager to avoid all stocks in the alcohol sector. The manager should:

  1. Ignore it, because discretionary managers decide everything freely
  2. Refuse to accept the client because restrictions are not allowed
  3. Move the client to a mutual fund without explaining why
  4. Record the restriction and follow it
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Record the restriction and follow it

Discretionary authority operates within the client agreement. Client-specific restrictions should be documented and followed, as customisation is a key feature of PMS.
