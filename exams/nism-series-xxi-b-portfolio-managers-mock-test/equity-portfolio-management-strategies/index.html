Equity Portfolio Management Strategies: NISM Series XXI-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Equity Portfolio Management Strategies, from the NISM Series XXI-B (Portfolio Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers how equity portfolios are managed: passive strategies (full replication, sampling, optimisation), active strategies (top-down, bottom-up, growth, value, GARP, momentum, quality, contrarian, sector rotation), enhanced indexing, core-satellite, long-short and market-neutral approaches, and portfolio construction issues such as concentration, position sizing, turnover and liquidity. Know the features, sources of return and risks of each style, and how a style should be benchmarked.

Equity Portfolio Management Strategies questions

Question 1

An index fund that holds only a representative subset of index stocks to reduce costs is using:

  1. Full replication of every constituent in exact index weights
  2. Sector rotation based on an economic outlook
  3. Stratified sampling
  4. A concentrated high-conviction approach
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Stratified sampling

Sampling selects stocks so that the portfolio matches key index characteristics (sector, size, factor exposure) without holding every constituent. It may add some tracking error.

Question 2

A GARP (growth at a reasonable price) investor typically looks for stocks with:

  1. The highest P/E ratios in the market regardless of growth
  2. Falling earnings and very high dividend payouts
  3. Good growth at moderate valuations
  4. Prices that have fallen the most in the last month
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Good growth at moderate valuations

GARP blends growth and value, seeking firms with solid earnings growth that are not overpriced. A common metric is the PEG ratio (P/E divided by growth).

Question 3

A stock has a P/E of 24 and expected earnings growth of 16%. Its PEG ratio is:

  1. 0.67
  2. 3.84
  3. 8.0
  4. 1.5
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. 1.5

PEG = P/E ÷ growth rate (in percentage points) = 24 ÷ 16 = 1.5. Many GARP investors see a PEG near or below 1 as attractive.

Question 4

Sector rotation as an active strategy involves:

  1. Holding every sector at exactly its index weight at all times
  2. Investing only in a single sector for the life of the portfolio
  3. Buying stocks with the lowest trading volume in each sector
  4. Overweighting sectors expected to do well in the next phase of the cycle
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Overweighting sectors expected to do well in the next phase of the cycle

Sector rotation shifts weights across sectors based on economic or market views, for example favouring defensives in slowdowns and cyclicals in recoveries.

Question 5

A contrarian equity manager is most likely to:

  1. Buy the stocks with the strongest recent price momentum
  2. Buy out-of-favour stocks that others are selling
  3. Follow the consensus of analysts on every stock
  4. Hold only the largest stocks in the benchmark
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Buy out-of-favour stocks that others are selling

Contrarians believe markets overreact, so they buy unpopular, beaten-down stocks expecting a rebound as sentiment normalises. This approach is the opposite of momentum investing.

Question 6

An enhanced indexing strategy aims to:

  1. Earn returns several times higher than the index with high leverage
  2. Track the index exactly with zero difference in returns
  3. Hold only cash and short-term bonds
  4. Beat the index modestly with low tracking error
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Beat the index modestly with low tracking error

Enhanced indexing makes small, controlled deviations from the benchmark — such as factor tilts — to add modest excess return while keeping tracking error low.

Question 7

A market-neutral equity strategy aims to:

  1. Earn returns independent of market direction
  2. Deliver returns exactly equal to the market index
  3. Hold only long positions in high-beta stocks
  4. Maximise exposure to the overall market
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Earn returns independent of market direction

Market-neutral strategies balance long and short positions so that net beta is near zero. Returns come from stock selection rather than market movement.

Question 8

Why does high portfolio turnover often hurt net returns in PMS?

  1. It guarantees that the manager will beat the benchmark
  2. It reduces the management fee charged to clients
  3. It raises transaction costs and short-term taxes
  4. It lowers the portfolio's beta automatically
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. It raises transaction costs and short-term taxes

Frequent trading adds brokerage, impact cost and STT, and creates short-term capital gains taxed in the client's hands. Gains from active trading must exceed these costs.
