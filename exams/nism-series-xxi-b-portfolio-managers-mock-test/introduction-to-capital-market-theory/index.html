Introduction to Capital Market Theory: NISM Series XXI-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Introduction to Capital Market Theory, from the NISM Series XXI-B (Portfolio Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter extends MPT by adding a risk-free asset. It covers the capital allocation line, the Capital Market Line and the market portfolio, separation theorem, the Capital Asset Pricing Model and the Security Market Line, beta estimation, and alternatives such as Arbitrage Pricing Theory and multi-factor models (Fama-French). Practise CAPM, CML and beta calculations and be clear on when to use total risk (CML) versus systematic risk (SML).

Introduction to Capital Market Theory questions

Question 1

The Capital Market Line uses which measure of risk on its horizontal axis?

  1. Beta of the security relative to the market
  2. Duration of the bond portfolio
  3. Standard deviation of portfolio returns
  4. Dividend yield of the market index
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Standard deviation of portfolio returns

The CML plots efficient portfolios combining the risk-free asset and the market portfolio, so it uses total risk (standard deviation). The SML uses beta.

Question 2

The separation theorem states that:

  1. Each investor must hold a completely different risky portfolio
  2. Investors should never combine risky assets with the risk-free asset
  3. Portfolio decisions should ignore the risk-free rate entirely
  4. All investors hold the same risky portfolio, varying only the risk-free share
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. All investors hold the same risky portfolio, varying only the risk-free share

With a risk-free asset, the optimal risky portfolio is the same market portfolio for everyone. Risk preferences only decide how much to lend or borrow at the risk-free rate.

Question 3

A stock's covariance with the market is 0.03 and the market's variance is 0.025. The stock's beta is:

  1. 1.2
  2. 0.83
  3. 0.75
  4. 0.03
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 1.2

Beta = covariance(stock, market) ÷ variance(market) = 0.03 ÷ 0.025 = 1.2. A beta above 1 means the stock tends to move more than the market.

Question 4

The Fama-French three-factor model adds which two factors to the market factor?

  1. Inflation and GDP growth across countries
  2. Dividend yield and the number of shareholders
  3. Interest rates and currency exchange rates
  4. Size and value
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Size and value

Fama and French found that small companies and high book-to-market (value) stocks earned returns not explained by beta. Their model adds size (SMB) and value (HML) factors.

Question 5

The risk-free rate is 7%, the market's expected return is 13% and its standard deviation is 15%. A portfolio on the CML with 10% standard deviation has an expected return of:

  1. 13%
  2. 11%
  3. 9%
  4. 15%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. 11%

CML: E(R) = Rf + [(Rm − Rf) / σm] × σp = 7 + (6 / 15) × 10 = 7 + 4 = 11%. The slope 0.4 is the market's Sharpe ratio.

Question 6

Under CAPM, a stock with a beta of zero should earn:

  1. The risk-free rate
  2. The market return
  3. Zero return
  4. Twice the risk-free rate
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The risk-free rate

With beta zero, the stock has no systematic risk, so CAPM gives Rf + 0 × (Rm − Rf) = Rf. Its own volatility does not earn a premium if it is diversifiable.

Question 7

An investor who wants a portfolio riskier than the market portfolio on the CML must:

  1. Sell the market portfolio and hold only government bonds
  2. Hold only a single high-beta stock
  3. Borrow at the risk-free rate to buy more of the market
  4. Avoid the risk-free asset and pick stocks at random
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Borrow at the risk-free rate to buy more of the market

Points beyond the market portfolio on the CML are reached by leverage: borrowing at the risk-free rate to invest more than 100% in the market portfolio.

Question 8

A key assumption of CAPM that is often unrealistic is that:

  1. Investors care about both risk and return
  2. Securities markets have many participants
  3. Investors can borrow and lend at the risk-free rate
  4. Diversification reduces unsystematic risk
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Investors can borrow and lend at the risk-free rate

CAPM assumes unlimited borrowing and lending at a single risk-free rate, no taxes or transaction costs and homogeneous expectations. In practice borrowing rates exceed lending rates.
