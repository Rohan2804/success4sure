Regulatory, Governance and Ethical Aspects of Portfolio Managers: NISM Series XXI-B practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Regulatory, Governance and Ethical Aspects of Portfolio Managers, from the NISM Series XXI-B (Portfolio Managers) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers SEBI (Portfolio Managers) Regulations, 2020 and allied rules: registration and eligibility, net worth, code of conduct, restrictions on investments (associates, unlisted securities, derivatives, leverage), segregation of client funds and securities, conflict of interest policy, prevention of insider trading and front-running, KYC and AML obligations, grievance redressal (SCORES, ODR), inspection and penalties, and ethical standards in client dealing. Questions often test what a portfolio manager may or may not do in practical situations.

Regulatory, Governance and Ethical Aspects of Portfolio Managers questions

Question 1

A discretionary portfolio manager can invest a client's funds in unlisted securities only:

  1. Without any limit, if the manager believes in the company
  2. Only if the unlisted company is owned by the manager's group
  3. Never, because unlisted securities are banned for all PMS
  4. Up to the SEBI-specified limit, with disclosure
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Up to the SEBI-specified limit, with disclosure

SEBI caps discretionary PMS investment in unlisted securities as a share of client AUM. Non-discretionary and advisory mandates may follow client instructions within norms.

Question 2

Employees of a portfolio manager who learn of large client orders and trade ahead of them personally are guilty of:

  1. Legitimate personal investment based on their own research
  2. Portfolio rebalancing as per the client's IPS
  3. Front-running
  4. Tax-loss harvesting for their own benefit
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Front-running

Front-running exploits knowledge of pending client orders for personal gain, harming clients. SEBI treats it as a fraudulent practice punishable under the PFUTP regulations.

Question 3

Under SEBI rules, can a portfolio manager borrow funds on behalf of a client to buy securities?

  1. Yes, up to two times the client's portfolio value
  2. Yes, if the distributor approves the borrowing
  3. No, PMS client portfolios cannot be leveraged
  4. Yes, but only for buying unlisted shares
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. No, PMS client portfolios cannot be leveraged

SEBI prohibits portfolio managers from leveraging client portfolios for investment, protecting clients from magnified losses. Derivatives use is also restricted to permitted purposes.

Question 4

A portfolio manager's conflict of interest policy should cover situations such as:

  1. The colour scheme used in the company's office
  2. The number of holidays given to employees
  3. The location of the registered office
  4. Investing client money in associate companies
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Investing client money in associate companies

Conflicts arise where the manager or associates benefit at clients' expense, such as investing in associates or allocating trades unfairly. The policy must identify, disclose and manage them.

Question 5

Which of the following must a portfolio manager do under KYC and AML norms?

  1. Skip KYC for clients investing above ₹1 crore
  2. Verify identity and monitor for suspicious transactions
  3. Rely only on the distributor's oral confirmation of identity
  4. Report clients' personal details publicly on its website
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Verify identity and monitor for suspicious transactions

Portfolio managers are reporting entities under PMLA and must carry out KYC, risk categorisation and transaction monitoring, and report suspicious transactions to FIU-IND.

Question 6

A portfolio manager's marketing brochure shows only the best five years of performance. This is a problem because:

  1. Selective performance can mislead investors
  2. SEBI requires brochures to show only the worst years
  3. Performance can never be shown in marketing material
  4. Five years is always too short a period to be legal
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Selective performance can mislead investors

Advertisements must be fair and not misleading. Showing cherry-picked periods hides the full record; standardised, complete performance reporting is expected.

Question 7

If a portfolio manager fails to comply with SEBI regulations, SEBI can:

  1. Only send an advisory letter with no further action possible
  2. Transfer the manager's clients to a mutual fund automatically
  3. Ask the clients to pay the penalty instead
  4. Impose penalties or suspend or cancel registration
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Impose penalties or suspend or cancel registration

SEBI has wide powers of inspection, adjudication and enforcement, including monetary penalties, directions to clients' benefit, and suspension or cancellation of the portfolio manager's registration.

Question 8

A portfolio manager receives a complaint from a client. Ideally it should:

  1. Ignore it unless SEBI also receives the complaint
  2. Ask the client to approach the stock exchange first
  3. Close the client's account to avoid the complaint
  4. Resolve it promptly and record it
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Resolve it promptly and record it

Intermediaries must address complaints quickly, maintain records and report status. Unresolved complaints may be escalated by the client through SCORES and ODR.
