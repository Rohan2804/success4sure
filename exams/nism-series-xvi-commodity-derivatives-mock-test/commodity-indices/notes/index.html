Commodity Indices: NISM Series XVI revision notes

Commodity indices summarise price movements across a basket of commodities and can be traded through index futures. This short chapter covers why indices exist, how they are constructed, the effect of rolling futures, and examples from Indian exchanges. Questions test construction choices, the difference between spot and futures-based indices, and features of index futures such as cash settlement.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Purpose

Indices show broad commodity price trends, serve as benchmarks for funds, help measure inflation pressures and provide an underlying for index derivatives. A rising composite commodity index can signal input cost pressure for manufacturers before it shows in retail inflation.

2. Futures-based construction

Most investable commodity indices are built from futures prices rather than spot, because futures are traded, transparent and easy to replicate. The index rolls from the near-month contract to the next before expiry, according to a published schedule.

3. Weighting methods

Weights can be based on production value, trading liquidity, or a combination, with caps to prevent one commodity or sector from dominating. For example, a composite index may cap energy so that a crude oil spike does not swamp agricultural signals.

4. Roll yield

When the index rolls, it sells the expiring contract and buys the next one. In backwardation (next contract cheaper), rolling adds return; in contango (next contract dearer), rolling reduces return. This is why index returns can differ from spot price changes.

5. Indian examples

MCX publishes the iCOMDEX family, including a composite index and sectoral indices for bullion, base metals and energy. NCDEX has had agricultural indices. MCX launched bullion index futures (BULLDEX) and later other index futures, which are cash settled.

6. Index futures features

Commodity index futures are cash settled because delivering a basket is impractical. They allow a trader to take a view on a whole sector, such as bullion, with one contract. Margins and position limits are set by the exchange and clearing corporation.

7. Uses for investors and hedgers

A jewellery company can hedge broad bullion exposure with a bullion index contract; a fund can gain diversified commodity exposure. Since indices may not match a specific hedger's input exactly, basis risk remains.

8. Rebalancing and governance

Index providers periodically review constituents and weights, publish methodology documents and have index committees. Changes are announced in advance so that users can adjust positions.

Common traps in the exam

  • Roll yield is positive in backwardation and negative in contango.
  • Commodity index futures are cash settled, not delivery based.
  • Most investable commodity indices use futures prices, not mandi spot prices.
  • Capping weights is a design choice to avoid concentration, not a regulatory requirement for all indices.

Quick recap

Commodity indices track baskets for benchmarking, inflation signals and trading. Investable indices use futures, roll according to a schedule and are weighted by production or liquidity with caps. Roll yield helps in backwardation, hurts in contango. MCX iCOMDEX includes composite and sector indices; index futures like BULLDEX are cash settled. Basis risk remains for hedgers.
