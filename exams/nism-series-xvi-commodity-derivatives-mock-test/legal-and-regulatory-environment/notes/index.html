Legal and Regulatory Environment: NISM Series XVI revision notes

Commodity derivatives moved from a separate regulatory world into the securities framework in 2015. This chapter covers the legal foundation, SEBI's powers, related regulators such as WDRA, and rules on who may participate. Exam questions test dates, the legal status of commodity derivatives, institutional participation and the role of exchanges in surveillance.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. From FCRA to SCRA

The Forward Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1952 and the Forward Markets Commission regulated commodity futures for decades. The Finance Act, 2015 repealed FCRA and merged FMC with SEBI from 28 September 2015. Commodity derivatives were added to the definition of securities under SCRA.

2. SEBI's powers

SEBI now recognises commodity exchanges, registers members as stock brokers, approves new contracts, sets margin and position limit frameworks, and enforces rules against manipulation using the SEBI Act and PFUTP Regulations. Its commodity derivatives department handles policy.

3. Product approval

Exchanges must obtain SEBI approval to launch a commodity contract. SEBI looks at the commodity's market size, spot price transparency, deliverability and susceptibility to manipulation. Some sensitive agri commodities have had trading suspended in the interest of controlling food inflation.

4. WDRA and warehouses

The Warehousing (Development and Regulation) Act, 2007 created WDRA, which registers warehouses and regulates negotiable warehouse receipts. SEBI requires exchanges to use WDRA-registered or accredited warehouses for delivery of agri commodities.

5. Institutional participation

Since 2018-19 SEBI has allowed category III AIFs, portfolio managers and mutual funds (through specified schemes) to participate, with limits. Eligible foreign entities with actual exposure to Indian commodities can participate in specified non-agri contracts. Banks have limited participation subject to RBI rules.

6. Position limits and hedge exemption

SEBI prescribes client-level and member-level position limits for agri and non-agri commodities. Hedgers can obtain hedge limits from exchanges based on underlying exposure, verified by documents such as stock statements or contracts.

7. Universal exchange

In 2018 SEBI allowed exchanges to offer equity and commodity derivatives under one roof. So NSE and BSE introduced commodities and MCX introduced other products, increasing competition.

8. Investor protection

Commodity brokers are subject to the same KYC, client fund segregation, grievance redressal, SCORES and ODR framework as equity brokers. Exchanges maintain investor protection funds for commodity segments.

Common traps in the exam

  • The FMC-SEBI merger took effect in September 2015, not 2003 (when national commodity exchanges began).
  • Commodity derivatives are now securities under SCRA; FCRA no longer applies.
  • WDRA regulates warehouses and receipts, not the exchanges or brokers.
  • SEBI approval is required before an exchange launches a new commodity contract.

Quick recap

FCRA was repealed and FMC merged with SEBI on 28 September 2015; commodity derivatives became securities under SCRA. SEBI recognises exchanges, registers brokers, approves contracts and enforces anti-manipulation rules. WDRA regulates warehouses under the 2007 Act. Category III AIFs, PMS, MFs and eligible foreign entities may participate within limits. Universal exchanges allowed from 2018.
