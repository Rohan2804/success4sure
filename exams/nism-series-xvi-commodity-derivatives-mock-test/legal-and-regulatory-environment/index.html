What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the legal framework for commodity derivatives in India after the merger of the Forward Markets Commission with SEBI. It explains the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act and the inclusion of commodity derivatives as securities, the SEBI Act, regulations on stock brokers and intermediaries, the notification of commodities by the Central Government, the role of exchanges and clearing corporations, and the regulation of warehousing. Revise who regulates what, the powers of SEBI and exchanges, the reasons for position limits and surveillance, and why the old Forward Contracts (Regulation) Act was repealed.

Legal and Regulatory Environment questions

Question 1

After the FMC–SEBI merger, the Forward Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1952 was:

  1. Retained as the main law with SEBI as its administrator
  2. Repealed, with commodity derivatives brought under the SCRA framework
  3. Amended to apply only to agricultural commodities
  4. Merged into the Warehousing (Development and Regulation) Act
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Repealed, with commodity derivatives brought under the SCRA framework

The Finance Act, 2015 repealed the FCRA and brought commodity derivatives within the definition of securities under the SCRA, with SEBI as regulator. The warehousing law is separate.

Question 2

Which body regulates the warehouses that issue negotiable warehouse receipts in India?

  1. Securities and Exchange Board of India
  2. Warehousing Development and Regulatory Authority
  3. Reserve Bank of India
  4. Food Corporation of India
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Warehousing Development and Regulatory Authority

The WDRA regulates warehouses under the Warehousing (Development and Regulation) Act, 2007, including registration and e-NWRs. SEBI regulates exchanges and intermediaries, while FCI is a procurement agency.

Question 3

A stock broker wishes to offer commodity derivatives trading to clients. Under current SEBI rules it must:

  1. Obtain a separate licence from the Ministry of Consumer Affairs for each commodity it wishes to offer
  2. Register with the WDRA as a warehouse service provider
  3. Be a SEBI-registered stock broker with membership of the exchange's commodity segment
  4. Only obtain approval from its bank for handling client funds
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Be a SEBI-registered stock broker with membership of the exchange's commodity segment

Commodity derivatives brokers are SEBI-registered stock brokers with membership of the relevant segment of a recognised exchange. No separate ministry or WDRA licence is required for broking.

Question 4

Why are exchanges required to run surveillance on commodity derivative contracts?

  1. To detect manipulation, abnormal price moves and position concentration
  2. To ensure that prices of agricultural commodities never fall below the minimum support price
  3. To fix daily closing prices for each contract
  4. To approve each client's trading strategy in advance
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. To detect manipulation, abnormal price moves and position concentration

Surveillance identifies unusual trading, manipulation and excessive concentration so corrective action can be taken. It does not target any particular price level or pre-approve strategies.

Question 5

SEBI may suspend trading in a particular agricultural commodity derivative. The most likely reason is:

  1. The commodity having too many registered warehouses in the producing states
  2. A fall in trading volumes on the exchange for one day
  3. A request from a single large trader holding a losing position in that contract
  4. Concerns about excessive volatility or its effect on food inflation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Concerns about excessive volatility or its effect on food inflation

Regulators have occasionally suspended trading in sensitive agricultural contracts amid inflation or volatility concerns. A single trader's losses or warehouse numbers are not grounds for suspension.

Question 6

Recognised commodity derivatives exchanges in India must have a separate clearing corporation mainly to:

  1. Allow the exchange to avoid SEBI regulation of its trading platform
  2. Enable the exchange to own and operate warehouses directly
  3. Separate trading from clearing and strengthen risk management
  4. Provide loans to clients for margin payments when prices move sharply
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Separate trading from clearing and strengthen risk management

Separating clearing into a distinct entity improves risk management and governance. Clearing corporations are themselves regulated by SEBI and do not provide margin loans.

Question 7

Under SEBI's regulations, which entity's rules, bye-laws and regulations govern day-to-day trading conduct of members in commodity derivatives?

  1. The recognised stock exchange on which the member trades
  2. The Commodity Board of the relevant crop
  3. The Central Warehousing Corporation
  4. The state agricultural produce market committee
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The recognised stock exchange on which the member trades

Members are bound by the rules, bye-laws and regulations of the recognised exchange, approved by SEBI. Commodity boards and APMCs regulate physical markets, not exchange trading.

Question 8

A person repeatedly places and cancels large orders without the intention of trading, to create a false impression of demand. This practice is:

  1. Legitimate hedging
  2. Spoofing, a prohibited manipulative practice
  3. Permitted arbitrage
  4. Normal market making
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Spoofing, a prohibited manipulative practice

Placing orders without intent to execute, to mislead other participants, is spoofing, which is prohibited under SEBI's rules on fraudulent and unfair trade practices.
