About the NISM Series XVI exam

NISM Series XVI covers commodity derivatives — commodity markets and indices, futures and options on commodities, pricing, hedging strategies for producers and users, trading, clearing and the regulatory framework.

Who should take it: Associated persons of brokers in the commodity derivatives segment, and hedgers in agriculture, metals and energy businesses.

Sample 1 · Introduction to Commodity Markets

Regulation of commodity derivatives in India was transferred from the Forward Markets Commission to SEBI in:

  1. 2008
  2. 2003
  3. 2015
  4. 2020
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. 2015

The FMC merged with SEBI in September 2015, making SEBI the regulator of commodity derivatives.

Sample 2 · Commodity Indices

A commodity index is mainly used to:

  1. Fix government procurement prices
  2. Track the overall price movement of a basket of commodities
  3. Measure stock market liquidity
  4. Calculate import duties
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Track the overall price movement of a basket of commodities

Indices summarise commodity price trends and can serve as benchmarks or underlyings.

Sample 3 · Commodity Futures

Spot price of a commodity is ₹5,000 per quintal. Interest is 10% p.a. (simple) and storage cost for 3 months is ₹30 per quintal. The fair price of a 3-month futures contract is:

  1. ₹5,155
  2. ₹5,125
  3. ₹5,530
  4. ₹5,030
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹5,155

F = 5,000 × (1 + 0.10 × 3/12) + 30 = 5,125 + 30 = ₹5,155.

Sample 4 · Commodity Options

On Indian commodity exchanges, an exercised option on commodity futures devolves into:

  1. Physical delivery of the commodity immediately
  2. A cash payment equal to the strike price
  3. A futures position in the underlying contract
  4. A new option with a later expiry
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. A futures position in the underlying contract

Options on futures convert into the corresponding long or short futures position on exercise.

Sample 5 · Strategies using Commodity Futures and Options

A jeweller holds gold inventory and fears prices will fall before it is sold. The appropriate hedge is to:

  1. Sell gold futures
  2. Buy gold futures
  3. Buy gold call options
  4. Buy more physical gold
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Sell gold futures

A short hedge on the inventory offsets losses if gold prices decline.
