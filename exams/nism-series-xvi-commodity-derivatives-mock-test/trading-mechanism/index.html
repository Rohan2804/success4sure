Trading Mechanism: NISM Series XVI practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Trading Mechanism, from the NISM Series XVI (Commodity Derivatives) syllabus.

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter explains how commodity derivatives are traded on Indian exchanges. It covers the electronic order-matching system, order types (limit, market, stop-loss, immediate-or-cancel and good-till types), price-time priority, trading sessions for agricultural and non-agricultural contracts, daily price limits, position limits, contract launch calendars and the rules on client registration such as KYC and unique client codes. Revise how orders are matched, what each order type does, why price bands and position limits exist, and the responsibilities of trading members towards clients when accepting and executing orders.

Trading Mechanism questions

Question 1

On an electronic commodity exchange, two buy orders are at the same price. Which order is executed first?

  1. The order for the larger quantity
  2. The order placed by a hedger rather than a speculator
  3. The order placed by an institutional client
  4. The order that was entered earlier in time
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. The order that was entered earlier in time

Exchanges follow price-time priority: at the same price, the order entered first is matched first. Size, client type or purpose of the trade do not change priority.

Question 2

A trader wants to buy only if the price rises to ₹9,150 so as to limit losses on an existing short position. Which order suits this need?

  1. A sell limit order at ₹9,150
  2. A buy stop-loss order with a trigger price of ₹9,150
  3. A buy limit order at ₹8,800
  4. An immediate-or-cancel sell order at the market price
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. A buy stop-loss order with a trigger price of ₹9,150

A stop-loss buy order becomes active only when the trigger price is reached, so it caps losses on a short position. A buy limit order at a lower price would not protect against a rise.

Question 3

An immediate-or-cancel (IOC) order on a commodity exchange:

  1. Remains in the order book until the end of the trading day
  2. Is executed only at the closing session price
  3. Must be fully executed or else it is converted to a market order
  4. Executes whatever quantity is available at once and cancels the rest
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Executes whatever quantity is available at once and cancels the rest

An IOC order is matched immediately to the extent possible and any unexecuted quantity is cancelled. A day order would remain in the book until the session ends.

Question 4

The unique client code (UCC) used in commodity derivatives trading mainly helps to:

  1. Fix the brokerage rate payable by each client to the member
  2. Decide which accredited warehouse a client must use for any delivery
  3. Exempt small clients from margin requirements
  4. Track each client's trades and positions for surveillance
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Track each client's trades and positions for surveillance

The UCC links every order to a specific client, allowing exchanges to monitor client-level positions, enforce limits and detect manipulation. It has no role in brokerage or warehouse allocation.

Question 5

When a commodity futures contract hits its daily price limit, exchanges typically:

  1. Cancel all trades executed during the day and refund all margins collected to the members
  2. Close the contract permanently and settle at the previous day's price
  3. Permit trades only within the band, relaxing it in phases after a cooling-off
  4. Allow unlimited price movement for the rest of the day
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Permit trades only within the band, relaxing it in phases after a cooling-off

Daily price limits restrict trades beyond the band. Exchanges usually provide for a cooling-off period and phased relaxation of the limit, rather than cancelling trades or delisting the contract.

Question 6

Client-level position limits in an agricultural commodity are usually linked to:

  1. The estimated deliverable supply or production of that commodity
  2. The client's annual income as declared in the KYC form
  3. The broker's net worth only
  4. The number of trading days left in the contract
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. The estimated deliverable supply or production of that commodity

For agricultural commodities, position limits are generally set with reference to the deliverable supply so that no single participant can corner the market. The client's income is relevant for suitability, not exchange limits.

Question 7

A client calls his broker to place an order but does not specify the price. The broker places a market order. A market order:

  1. Has execution certainty but no price certainty
  2. Has price certainty but no execution certainty
  3. Is executed only at the previous day's settlement price
  4. Can be modified by the exchange to the best bid of the next day
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Has execution certainty but no price certainty

A market order is executed immediately against the best available counter-orders, so the trade is almost certain but the price is not known in advance. A limit order provides price certainty instead.

Question 8

Before trading in commodity derivatives, a client receives the 'Risk Disclosure Document'. Its main purpose is to:

  1. Guarantee a minimum return to clients who follow the broker's research advice
  2. Record the client's bank account details for margin payments
  3. Exempt the broker from any liability for errors in execution
  4. Explain the risks of leveraged trading so the client decides knowingly
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Explain the risks of leveraged trading so the client decides knowingly

The risk disclosure document informs clients about leverage, volatility, liquidity and other risks of derivatives trading. It gives no guarantee and does not exempt brokers from their obligations.
