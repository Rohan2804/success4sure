Clearing, Settlement and Risk Management: NISM Series XVI revision notes

Clearing and settlement in commodities includes everything seen in equity derivatives plus physical delivery through warehouses. This chapter covers the clearing corporation's role, margin types peculiar to commodities, mark-to-market, the delivery process with accredited warehouses and assayers, and default protection. Questions often test margin names, delivery steps and who certifies quality.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Clearing corporation

Each commodity exchange settles through a recognised clearing corporation that becomes the counterparty to every trade by novation. It computes obligations, collects margins and settles funds and goods. Clearing members are responsible for the trades of their clients and trading members.

2. Initial and extreme loss margin

Initial margin covers the likely one-day loss, computed through a portfolio-based risk model at a high confidence level. Extreme loss margin covers losses beyond that. Both must be collected upfront from clients. Margins rise when volatility rises.

3. Additional and special margins

Exchanges can levy additional or special margins when prices move sharply or when a contract shows concentration, for example during a sudden surge in guar or cotton prices. These are temporary measures to curb excessive speculation.

4. Pre-expiry and delivery margins

As expiry approaches, delivery contracts carry staggered pre-expiry margins that rise each day. During the tender or delivery period, delivery period margins apply to positions that will result in delivery, so that a defaulting buyer or seller does not disrupt settlement.

5. Mark to market

Futures positions are marked to market daily at the settlement price. Profits are paid and losses collected in cash on the next working day. Failure to pay MTM leads to the member being restricted from trading and positions being closed.

6. Delivery process

Sellers deposit goods in exchange-accredited warehouses, where assayers check quality against contract specifications. Electronic negotiable warehouse receipts are created in a repository. On settlement, the buyer pays funds and receives the warehouse receipt, then can lift the goods or keep them stored.

7. Quality and penalties

Goods within an allowed range of quality may be accepted with premiums or discounts. Goods below the minimum grade are rejected. Sellers who fail to deliver face penalties, and the buyer receives compensation according to exchange rules.

8. Settlement guarantee fund

The clearing corporation maintains a core settlement guarantee fund to complete settlement if a member defaults. The default waterfall uses the defaulter's margins and contributions first, then the clearing corporation's own resources and other contributions in order.

Common traps in the exam

  • Assayers certify quality; warehouses store goods; the clearing corporation settles. Each role is separate.
  • Delivery period margins are charged on positions headed for delivery, in addition to normal margins.
  • MTM is settled in cash daily, even in delivery-based contracts.
  • Goods slightly off-grade may be accepted at a discount; only below minimum grade are rejected.

Quick recap

Clearing corporation novates every trade and settles funds and goods. Margins: initial, extreme loss, additional or special, pre-expiry staggered and delivery period margins, all upfront. Daily cash MTM. Delivery through accredited warehouses with assayers and electronic warehouse receipts; quality premiums and discounts apply. Core SGF and default waterfall protect settlement.
