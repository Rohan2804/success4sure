Clearing, Settlement and Risk Management: NISM Series XVI practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Clearing, Settlement and Risk Management, from the NISM Series XVI (Commodity Derivatives) syllabus.

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers what happens after a trade: novation by the clearing corporation, the margining system (initial or SPAN-type margin, extreme loss margin, mark-to-market, additional and delivery margins), daily settlement, final settlement by cash or delivery, the delivery process through accredited warehouses and vaults, and the Settlement Guarantee Fund. It also explains risk controls such as position limits and capital adequacy of clearing members. Focus on how margins are computed and collected, the sequence of the delivery process, and how the clearing corporation manages defaults to protect the market.

Clearing, Settlement and Risk Management questions

Question 1

Through novation, the clearing corporation in a commodity futures trade:

  1. Acts only as a record keeper without taking any counterparty role
  2. Transfers the trade to a warehouse for delivery on the same day
  3. Guarantees a profit to both the buyer and the seller
  4. Becomes the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Becomes the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer

Novation substitutes the clearing corporation as counterparty to both sides, which is the basis of its settlement guarantee. It does not guarantee profits and is not merely a record keeper.

Question 2

A trader buys one gold mini contract of 100 grams at ₹72,000 per 10 grams. If the initial margin is 8%, the initial margin required is:

  1. ₹5,760
  2. ₹72,000
  3. ₹57,600
  4. ₹7,20,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. ₹57,600

Contract value = (100 ÷ 10) × 72,000 = ₹7,20,000. Initial margin at 8% = ₹57,600. ₹7,20,000 is the full contract value, not the margin.

Question 3

The Settlement Guarantee Fund (Core SGF) maintained by a clearing corporation is used to:

  1. Pay daily mark-to-market profits to all clients
  2. Fund the exchange's technology and staff costs
  3. Meet settlement obligations if a clearing member defaults
  4. Compensate traders for losses due to price movements
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Meet settlement obligations if a clearing member defaults

The core SGF is a default fund that allows the clearing corporation to complete settlement when a member fails. Normal MTM pay-outs come from losing members, and market losses are not compensated.

Question 4

Mark-to-market settlement in commodity futures means that:

  1. Profits are paid only when the position is finally closed or the contract expires
  2. Margins are fixed once at the start of the contract and are never revised by the exchange afterwards
  3. Only losses are collected while profits are held back until expiry
  4. Open positions are revalued at the daily settlement price and gains or losses settled daily
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Open positions are revalued at the daily settlement price and gains or losses settled daily

MTM settles notional gains and losses daily against the settlement price, preventing losses from accumulating. Profits are credited and losses debited each day.

Question 5

Which margin is designed to cover losses in situations beyond those captured by the normal VaR-based initial margin?

  1. Mark-to-market margin
  2. Pay-in margin
  3. Extreme loss margin
  4. Delivery period margin
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Extreme loss margin

The extreme loss margin is an additional buffer for price moves larger than those assumed in the VaR or SPAN-based initial margin. MTM covers realised daily losses, not tail risk.

Question 6

In a compulsory-delivery agricultural contract, a seller wishing to deliver must ensure that the commodity:

  1. Is transported directly to the buyer's factory by the seller at the seller's own cost and risk
  2. Is sold first in the local mandi and the sale proceeds deposited with the exchange
  3. Is in an accredited warehouse and meets the contract's quality specifications
  4. Is certified only by the seller's own quality inspector
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Is in an accredited warehouse and meets the contract's quality specifications

Exchange deliveries take place through accredited warehouses, and the goods must be tested and certified by approved assayers as meeting contract specifications. Self-certification is not accepted.

Question 7

A clearing member fails to pay MTM losses on time. The clearing corporation is most likely to first:

  1. Cancel the trades of the member's clients from the very start of the contract
  2. Ask the opposite counterparties to accept lower payments for that day's settlement
  3. Block the member's trading and close out positions using its collateral
  4. Suspend trading on the entire exchange for the day
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Block the member's trading and close out positions using its collateral

The clearing corporation acts against the defaulting member by blocking trading, closing out positions and using the member's margins and collateral, then other default resources if needed. Counterparties are still paid in full.

Question 8

A clearing corporation increases margins on a contract when it enters the delivery period mainly because:

  1. Exchanges earn more fees from higher margins
  2. The risk of delivery default and price manipulation rises close to expiry
  3. Delivery contracts are not covered by the Settlement Guarantee Fund
  4. Speculators are legally barred from holding positions in the delivery period
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. The risk of delivery default and price manipulation rises close to expiry

Near expiry, open positions turn into delivery obligations, increasing the risk of default and squeeze. Higher delivery-period margins protect the system. Margins are not a fee income.
