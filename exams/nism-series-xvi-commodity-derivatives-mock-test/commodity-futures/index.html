What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the pricing and features of commodity futures. Key topics are the cost-of-carry model (spot price plus interest, storage and insurance, less convenience yield), contango and backwardation, basis and convergence, contract specifications (lot size, tick size, quotation unit, delivery centre, quality grade) and the different settlement types: compulsory delivery, both-option delivery and cash settlement against a reference price. Practise numerical questions on fair value, basis and profit or loss per lot, and understand how delivery logistics and grade differentials affect the price at which a contract finally settles.

Commodity Futures questions

Question 1

A commodity trades at ₹4,800 per quintal in the spot market. With a 9% annual (simple) financing rate and storage charges of ₹20 per quintal for two months, what should a two-month futures contract be worth?

  1. ₹4,892
  2. ₹4,872
  3. ₹4,820
  4. ₹4,932
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹4,892

Interest for two months is 4,800 × 9% × 2/12 = ₹72. Fair price = 4,800 + 72 + 20 = ₹4,892. ₹4,872 ignores storage cost, and ₹4,820 ignores interest.

Question 2

As a commodity futures contract approaches expiry, its price should move towards the spot price at the delivery centre. This process is called:

  1. Contango
  2. Rolling
  3. Convergence
  4. Backwardation
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Convergence

Near expiry the carrying period shrinks to zero, so futures and spot prices come together; this is convergence. Contango and backwardation describe the shape of the futures curve, not the expiry process.

Question 3

A trader buys 3 lots of a commodity future, each of 1,000 units, at ₹215.50. The day's settlement price is ₹212.80. His mark-to-market result is:

  1. Loss of ₹8,100
  2. Profit of ₹8,100
  3. Loss of ₹2,700
  4. Loss of ₹81,000
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Loss of ₹8,100

Change per unit = 212.80 − 215.50 = −₹2.70. For a long position of 3 × 1,000 units, loss = 2.70 × 3,000 = ₹8,100. ₹2,700 would be the loss on a single lot.

Question 4

Crude oil futures on Indian exchanges are commonly settled in cash rather than by delivery. Their final settlement price is generally linked to:

  1. The average retail price of petrol in the four metro cities
  2. The price fixed by the oil ministry for refiners
  3. The settlement price of a benchmark international crude oil futures contract
  4. The closing price of the near-month Indian contract on the first trading day
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. The settlement price of a benchmark international crude oil futures contract

Cash-settled energy contracts in India typically settle against a reference price derived from an international benchmark contract, converted to rupees. Retail fuel prices and ministry prices are not used.

Question 5

Which factor would cause the futures price of a storable commodity to trade BELOW its spot price?

  1. An increase in interest rates
  2. A rise in warehousing charges
  3. An increase in insurance costs on stored goods
  4. A high convenience yield because current stocks are scarce
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. A high convenience yield because current stocks are scarce

When inventories are tight, holders value physical stock highly and the convenience yield can outweigh carrying costs, pushing futures below spot (backwardation). Higher interest, storage or insurance raise the futures price.

Question 6

In a 'both-option' delivery contract, which statement is correct about positions open at expiry?

  1. Every open position must be settled by physical delivery at the delivery centre without any exception
  2. All open positions are closed out at the international reference price
  3. Delivery occurs only where the seller's and buyer's delivery intentions are matched
  4. Only the buyer can choose whether delivery will take place
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Delivery occurs only where the seller's and buyer's delivery intentions are matched

In both-option contracts, delivery happens when the seller's intention to deliver is matched with a buyer's intention to take delivery; unmatched positions are cash settled. Compulsory delivery contracts require delivery of all open positions.

Question 7

A spot price of ₹60,000 and a three-month futures price of ₹60,900 imply an annualised cost of carry (simple) of about:

  1. 6%
  2. 1.5%
  3. 9%
  4. 4.5%
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. 6%

The three-month premium is 900 ÷ 60,000 = 1.5%. Annualised, 1.5% × 4 = 6%. 1.5% is the unannualised figure, a common mistake.

Question 8

A commodity's contract specification says 'tick size ₹1 per quintal, trading unit 10 MT'. The value of a one-tick move per lot is:

  1. ₹100
  2. ₹10
  3. ₹1,000
  4. ₹1
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. ₹100

10 MT equals 100 quintals. A ₹1 per quintal move therefore changes the lot value by 100 × ₹1 = ₹100. Using 10 or 1,000 confuses the units.
