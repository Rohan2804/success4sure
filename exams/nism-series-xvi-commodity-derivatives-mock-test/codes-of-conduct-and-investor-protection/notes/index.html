Codes of Conduct and Investor Protection: NISM Series XVI revision notes

The final chapter of NISM XVI sets out how commodity derivatives brokers and their staff must treat clients. It covers client onboarding, risk disclosure, the broker code of conduct, handling of client funds, grievance redressal and investor protection funds. Questions are mostly scenario-based ethics questions: identify the correct conduct or the right escalation route.

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Client onboarding

Brokers must complete KYC, collect the account opening form and financial information, provide rights and obligations, the risk disclosure document and the tariff sheet, and allot a UCC. Financial proof is required before activating derivatives trading.

2. Hedger versus speculator profile

Understanding whether a client is a hedger (farmer, trader, processor) or a speculator helps the broker explain products and margin needs properly. A farmer producer organisation hedging soybean needs explanation of delivery rules, not leveraged trading tips.

3. Risk disclosure

Clients must be told that leverage can cause losses beyond the initial margin, that prices can hit limits and become illiquid, that delivery obligations may arise at expiry, and that international events can move prices overnight.

4. Broker code of conduct

Brokers must act with integrity, due skill and care, avoid misleading statements, not promise returns, avoid churning, execute orders at the best available price, keep client information confidential and maintain adequate infrastructure and qualified staff.

5. Client funds and margins

Client funds must be kept separate from the broker's own and used only for that client's obligations. Margins must be collected upfront and reported accurately. Running account settlement returns idle funds periodically.

6. Contract notes and statements

Contract notes must be sent within 24 hours of trades, and periodic statements of funds and positions must be provided, so clients can verify every transaction.

7. Grievance redressal

Complaints go first to the broker, then to the exchange's investor services, then to SEBI's SCORES portal, and disputes can be taken to the online dispute resolution platform for conciliation and arbitration.

8. Unregistered tips and advice

Staff should not forward commodity tips from unregistered sources or run paid advisory groups. Giving recommendations for a fee needs SEBI registration as an investment adviser or research analyst. Exchanges also warn investors against dummy or 'dabba' trading platforms that operate outside the exchange system and offer no protection.

9. Investor protection fund

Commodity exchanges maintain investor protection funds to compensate clients of defaulting members for genuine claims, within prescribed limits, and to finance investor education such as farmer awareness programmes.

Common traps in the exam

  • IPF compensation covers member default, not trading losses or price movements.
  • Promising assured returns on commodity trading violates the code of conduct.
  • Delivery obligations must be explained to clients holding positions in compulsory-delivery contracts near expiry.
  • Complaints must normally go to the broker first before SCORES.

Quick recap

Onboard with KYC, financial proof, UCC, rights and obligations and risk disclosure. Understand whether the client hedges or speculates. Never promise returns or churn. Segregate client funds, collect margins upfront, send contract notes within 24 hours. Escalate complaints from broker to exchange to SCORES to ODR. IPFs compensate for broker default only.
