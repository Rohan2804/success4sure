Codes of Conduct and Investor Protection: NISM Series XVI practice questions

8 free questions with answers and explanations on Codes of Conduct and Investor Protection, from the NISM Series XVI (Commodity Derivatives) syllabus.

Last updated 11 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · How we create questions

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the conduct expected of brokers, authorised persons and their employees in commodity derivatives, and the protections available to clients. Topics include the SEBI code of conduct for stock brokers, segregation of client funds and securities, KYC, contract notes and statements of accounts, prohibition of fraudulent and unfair trade practices, investor grievance redressal through SCORES and online dispute resolution, the Investor Protection Fund, and arbitration. Learners should be able to spot mis-selling, unauthorised trading and misuse of client funds, and know the escalation path available to an aggrieved client.

Codes of Conduct and Investor Protection questions

Question 1

A client discovers trades in his account that he never authorised. His first step should be to:

  1. Complain to the broker at once and escalate to the exchange or SEBI if unresolved
  2. Wait for the year-end statement of accounts before taking any action against the broker
  3. Settle the losses directly with the opposite counterparty
  4. Close his bank account to stop margin payments
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Complain to the broker at once and escalate to the exchange or SEBI if unresolved

Unauthorised trades should be disputed promptly with the broker in writing; unresolved complaints can then be escalated to the exchange, SEBI's SCORES and online dispute resolution. Delay weakens the client's case.

Question 2

Segregation of client funds by a commodity broker means that:

  1. Each client's trades are executed on different exchanges
  2. Client money is kept separately and cannot be used for the broker's own trading
  3. Clients must keep their funds in a separate bank chosen by the exchange
  4. Clients' profits are held by the broker until the year-end
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Client money is kept separately and cannot be used for the broker's own trading

Client funds must be maintained separately from the broker's own funds and used only for that client's obligations. Using client money for proprietary trading is a serious violation.

Question 3

A broker's dealer tells a new client that a particular commodity strategy 'can never make a loss'. This is:

  1. Misrepresentation, which the code of conduct prohibits
  2. Acceptable if the client signs the risk disclosure document
  3. Permitted for hedging strategies only
  4. Acceptable marketing as long as no fee is charged
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Misrepresentation, which the code of conduct prohibits

Guaranteeing returns or claiming a derivatives strategy is loss-free is misleading. The code of conduct requires fair and accurate representation of risks, which a signed risk disclosure does not override.

Question 4

Disputes between a client and a trading member that are not settled through grievance redressal can be referred under SEBI's current framework to:

  1. The local civil court only, as arbitration is not available
  2. Online conciliation and arbitration through the ODR platform
  3. The Warehousing Development and Regulatory Authority
  4. The commodity board for the relevant crop
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Online conciliation and arbitration through the ODR platform

SEBI's online dispute resolution framework allows unresolved disputes with market intermediaries to go for online conciliation and arbitration. WDRA and commodity boards do not resolve broker disputes.

Question 5

The Investor Protection Fund of an exchange is primarily used to:

  1. Pay compensation for trading losses due to adverse price moves
  2. Compensate eligible clients when a member is declared a defaulter
  3. Fund the exchange's advertising and marketing
  4. Pay CTT on behalf of small clients
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Compensate eligible clients when a member is declared a defaulter

The IPF compensates genuine claims of clients against defaulting members, subject to limits, and supports investor awareness. Market losses from price moves are not compensated.

Question 6

Which of the following must a trading member provide to clients periodically under the regulatory framework?

  1. A statement of accounts for funds and positions
  2. A guarantee of minimum annual return
  3. A copy of the broker's internal audit report
  4. The names of other clients trading the same commodity
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. A statement of accounts for funds and positions

Brokers must send periodic statements of funds and securities or positions, along with contract notes. Guaranteeing returns or sharing other clients' details would be improper.

Question 7

An authorised person (AP) of a broker collects margin money from a client in cash in his own name. This is:

  1. Permitted if the AP issues a handwritten receipt
  2. Permitted for amounts below the daily margin requirement of that particular client account
  3. Required to speed up margin payments
  4. Not permitted, as client funds must reach the broker through banking channels
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Not permitted, as client funds must reach the broker through banking channels

Authorised persons cannot accept client funds in their own name; payments must go to the broker's designated client bank account. Cash dealings in an AP's name create risk of misuse.

Question 8

A dealer uses his knowledge of a client's large pending sell order to sell for his own account first. This is a breach mainly because it:

  1. Breaches only the exchange's dress code for dealers
  2. Puts the dealer's interest ahead of the client's: front running
  3. Is permitted if the dealer later informs the client about the trade and its price
  4. Is acceptable when the market is volatile
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Puts the dealer's interest ahead of the client's: front running

Trading ahead of a client's order using knowledge of that order is front running, a breach of the duty to act in the client's interest. Later disclosure does not cure it.
