Free NISM Series XVI practice questions

Question 1 · Introduction to Commodity Markets

Which of the following is an agricultural ('soft') commodity?

  1. Crude oil
  2. Copper
  3. Gold
  4. Cotton
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Cotton

Cotton is a farm product; crude oil is energy, copper is a base metal and gold is a precious metal.

Question 2 · Commodity Indices

A commodity index constructed from futures prices must deal with contract expiry by:

  1. Periodically rolling from the expiring contract into a later one
  2. Holding the expiring contract until delivery
  3. Switching to spot prices at expiry
  4. Stopping the index at each expiry
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Periodically rolling from the expiring contract into a later one

Futures-based indices roll positions forward, and roll yield can affect index returns.

Question 3 · Commodity Futures

Commodity futures may trade below spot (backwardation) when users value holding the physical stock highly. This benefit of holding the physical commodity is called:

  1. Storage cost
  2. Basis risk
  3. Convenience yield
  4. Roll yield
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Convenience yield

A high convenience yield, often during shortages, can outweigh carry costs and push futures below spot.

Question 4 · Commodity Options

Unlike options on commodity futures, 'options on goods' result on exercise in:

  1. A position in commodity futures
  2. Cash settlement on a commodity index
  3. Delivery of the underlying physical commodity
  4. Automatic rollover to the next month
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Delivery of the underlying physical commodity

Options on goods have the physical commodity as the underlying, so exercise leads to delivery.

Question 5 · Strategies using Commodity Futures and Options

A biscuit manufacturer must buy wheat in three months and fears prices will rise. The appropriate hedge is to:

  1. Buy wheat futures
  2. Sell wheat futures
  3. Sell wheat call options
  4. Sell wheat inventory
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Buy wheat futures

A long hedge gains if prices rise, offsetting the higher cost of buying wheat.

Question 6 · Trading Mechanism

Trading in non-agricultural commodity derivatives in India extends into the late evening mainly because:

  1. Indian farmers trade only in the evening
  2. These commodities track international markets that trade in other time zones
  3. Warehouses operate only at night
  4. Agricultural prices are polled in the evening
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. These commodities track international markets that trade in other time zones

Bullion, energy and base metals are priced globally, so longer hours reduce gap risk.

Question 7 · Clearing, Settlement and Risk Management

Physical delivery in exchange-traded commodity futures is effected through:

  1. Any private godown chosen by the buyer
  2. Exchange-accredited warehouses or vaults
  3. The RBI's currency chests
  4. Retail shops of the seller
Show answer & explanation

Answer: B. Exchange-accredited warehouses or vaults

Accredited warehouses and vaults ensure quality, quantity and standardised delivery.

Question 8 · Legal and Regulatory Environment

After the FMC merger, commodity derivatives in India are governed by the:

  1. Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956
  2. Forward Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1952
  3. Essential Commodities Act, 1955 alone
  4. Warehousing Act, 2007 alone
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956

The FCRA was repealed and commodity derivatives were brought under the SCRA and SEBI.

Question 9 · Accounting and Taxation

Commodity Transaction Tax (CTT) is levied on:

  1. All physical spot purchases of commodities
  2. Only imports of agricultural commodities
  3. Sale of non-agricultural commodity derivatives on recognised exchanges
  4. Purchase of gold coins from banks
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. Sale of non-agricultural commodity derivatives on recognised exchanges

CTT applies to exchange-traded non-agri commodity derivatives; specified agricultural commodities are exempt.

Question 10 · Codes of Conduct and Investor Protection

A broker who places his own order ahead of a large client order to profit from the expected price impact is engaging in:

  1. Arbitrage
  2. Hedging
  3. Portfolio rebalancing
  4. Front running
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. Front running

Front running misuses client order information and is prohibited.

Question 11 · Introduction to Commodity Markets

A feature that distinguishes physical commodities from financial underlyings like shares is that commodities:

  1. Vary in quality and grade, and incur storage and warehousing costs
  2. Cannot be priced
  3. Always trade at a fixed price
  4. Never require delivery logistics
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Vary in quality and grade, and incur storage and warehousing costs

Grade standards, storage and transport are central to commodity markets, unlike dematerialised shares.

Question 12 · Commodity Indices

A bullion sub-index would typically comprise:

  1. Gold and silver
  2. Crude oil and natural gas
  3. Copper and zinc
  4. Wheat and maize
Show answer & explanation

Answer: A. Gold and silver

Bullion refers to precious metals, primarily gold and silver.

Question 13 · Commodity Futures

The spot price of a commodity is ₹6,200 and the futures price is ₹6,350. The basis (spot minus futures) is:

  1. +₹150
  2. ₹12,550
  3. −₹150
  4. ₹0
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. −₹150

Basis = Spot − Futures = 6,200 − 6,350 = −150.

Question 14 · Commodity Options

Commodity options traded on Indian exchanges are of which exercise style?

  1. American
  2. Bermudan
  3. European
  4. Asian
Show answer & explanation

Answer: C. European

They can be exercised only on the expiry day.

Question 15 · Strategies using Commodity Futures and Options

A farmer sells futures at ₹5,400 when spot is ₹5,300. At harvest, spot is ₹5,000 and futures ₹5,150; he sells his crop spot and closes the futures. His effective selling price is:

  1. ₹5,300
  2. ₹5,400
  3. ₹5,150
  4. ₹5,250
Show answer & explanation

Answer: D. ₹5,250

Futures gain = 5,400 − 5,150 = 250; effective price = 5,000 + 250 = ₹5,250 (the weaker basis cost ₹50).
