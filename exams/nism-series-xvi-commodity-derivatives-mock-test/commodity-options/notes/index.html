Commodity Options: NISM Series XVI revision notes

SEBI allowed commodity options in 2017, starting with options on gold futures on MCX, and later permitted options on goods that settle directly by delivery. This chapter covers how these options work, how they settle at expiry, pricing factors and Greeks. Questions often test devolvement, the difference between options on futures and options on goods, and payoff basics.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key points

1. Options on futures

In an option on futures, the underlying is a futures contract of the same commodity. On exercise, the option devolves into a futures position: a long call becomes a long futures position at the strike, and the counterparty gets the short futures position. The futures then follow their own settlement rules.

2. Options on goods

In options on goods (introduced later), the underlying is the physical commodity itself. ITM options at expiry result in delivery of the goods, giving hedgers a direct route to physical settlement without first going through futures.

3. Style and exercise

Commodity options in India are European style: they can be exercised only on expiry day. In-the-money options are exercised automatically unless the holder gives a contrary instruction within the window allowed by the exchange, which is useful for near-the-money strikes where costs may exceed value.

4. Moneyness and payoff

A gold call option with strike ₹72,000 per 10 g is ITM when gold futures trade at ₹72,800. Intrinsic value is ₹800 per 10 g. Buyer's maximum loss is the premium; seller's loss can be large. Payoff logic is the same as in equity options.

5. Pricing

Options on futures are typically priced using the Black-76 model, a variant of Black-Scholes using the futures price instead of spot and no separate carry term. Inputs: futures price, strike, time, volatility and interest rate. Higher volatility raises both call and put premiums.

6. Greeks

Delta shows change in premium per unit change in the futures price; gamma shows how fast delta changes; theta shows time decay; vega shows sensitivity to volatility. A farmer who buys puts is long vega: if volatility rises during a monsoon scare, the puts gain value.

7. Uses for hedgers

A producer can buy puts to set a floor price while keeping upside; a processor can buy calls to cap input costs. Options cost a premium upfront but avoid margin calls that futures hedgers face, which suits small hedgers with limited working capital.

8. Margins and settlement

Buyers pay the full premium upfront; sellers pay initial and exposure margins. Near expiry, sellers and ITM buyers may face additional margins because positions may devolve into futures or delivery. Premium is settled on T+1.

Common traps in the exam

  • Options on futures devolve into futures positions; they do not directly deliver the commodity.
  • Indian commodity options are European, exercisable only on expiry.
  • Option buyers pay premium, not margin; sellers pay margin.
  • Black-76 uses the futures price as the underlying, not the spot price.

Quick recap

Commodity options began in 2017 with options on gold futures. Options on futures devolve into futures; options on goods settle in the physical commodity. European style with auto-exercise of ITM options unless contrary instruction. Priced with Black-76; Greeks as in equity. Hedgers buy puts for a floor or calls for a cap. Buyers pay premium; sellers pay margins.
