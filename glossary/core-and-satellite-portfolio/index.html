What is Core and satellite portfolio?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Core and satellite portfolio: A core and satellite portfolio puts most of the money in a stable, diversified, low-cost core and a smaller portion in satellite holdings that seek extra return.

The core, often 70–80% of the portfolio, aims for market returns with low cost and low maintenance. Typical core holdings are broad index funds, large cap or flexi cap funds, and high-quality debt funds. The core is held for the long term and rebalanced periodically.

Satellites, the remaining 20–30%, are where the investor or adviser takes specific views: a mid or small cap fund, a sectoral or thematic fund, international equity, a specialised strategy or individual stocks. Satellites can be changed more often and are expected to be more volatile.

The structure limits damage from wrong calls. If a thematic fund in the satellite falls 40%, the whole portfolio drops much less. It also controls cost, since the expensive active or niche products sit only in the satellite. Many advisers find it helps clients who want 'some excitement' without risking the main plan.

Mistakes include letting satellites grow until they dominate, choosing satellites that duplicate the core (a large cap fund as satellite to a Nifty 50 core), and treating the core as boring and neglecting its rebalancing.

Example

Kunal builds a ₹15 lakh equity portfolio: core of ₹11.25 lakh (75%) split between a Nifty 50 index fund and a Nifty Next 50 index fund, and satellites of ₹3.75 lakh (25%) in a small cap fund and a banking sector fund. If both satellites fall 30%, the loss is about ₹1.13 lakh, or 7.5% of the portfolio, while the core continues tracking the broad market.

FAQs

What should be in the core of a portfolio?

Usually broad, diversified, low-cost holdings that suit the investor's long-term allocation: index funds or diversified equity funds and high-quality debt. The core should be something the investor is comfortable holding through market cycles with minimal changes.

How large should the satellite portion be?

Commonly 10–30% of the portfolio, depending on risk profile and experience. The more volatile or concentrated the satellite holdings, the smaller this share should be. The key is that a satellite failure should not derail the investor's goals.
