What is Cheque?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Cheque: A cheque is a written, signed instruction by an account holder ordering a specified bank to pay a certain sum of money on demand to a named person or to the bearer.

Section 6 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 defines a cheque as a bill of exchange drawn on a specified banker and not expressed to be payable otherwise than on demand. The Act's 2002 amendment also recognised electronic cheques and truncated cheques. There are three parties: the drawer (who writes it), the drawee (the bank on which it is drawn) and the payee (who receives the money).

Types by payee: a bearer cheque is payable to whoever presents it, so it is risky if lost; an order cheque (where 'or bearer' is struck off) is payable only to the named payee or someone to whom he endorses it. A crossed cheque is payable only through a bank account, not across the counter. A self cheque lets the drawer withdraw cash. A post-dated cheque carries a future date and cannot be paid before it; a stale cheque is one presented after its validity.

RBI reduced cheque validity to three months from the date written on it, effective 1 April 2012. Banks follow CTS-2010 standards for cheque format, with features like a pantograph 'VOID' mark and UV logo to prevent fraud. Under the Positive Pay System, customers re-confirm key details of cheques above a threshold (₹50,000 onwards, mandatory at many banks for ₹5 lakh and above) before payment.

Common confusions: the drawee is always a bank, never a person. A cheque is not legal tender; the payee may refuse it. Alterations on the cheque, other than the date, are not allowed under CTS rules; a fresh cheque must be issued.

Example

On 10 January Suresh writes a cheque for ₹38,500 to 'Neha Traders or bearer'. Suresh is the drawer, his bank (say Bank of Baroda, Pune branch) is the drawee and Neha Traders is the payee. If Suresh strikes off 'or bearer', it becomes an order cheque. The cheque is valid until 9 April, three months from its date. Presented on 15 April, it would be returned as stale and Suresh would need to issue a new one.

FAQs

How long is a cheque valid in India?

A cheque is valid for three months from the date written on it, as directed by RBI from 1 April 2012. Earlier the validity was six months. After three months the cheque becomes stale and the bank will not pay it. The drawer must revalidate or issue a fresh cheque.

Can a bank pay a post-dated cheque before its date?

No. A post-dated cheque becomes payable only on or after the date written on it. If a bank pays it earlier and the drawer's other cheques bounce meanwhile, the bank may be liable. Also, the drawer can stop payment or close the account before that date.
