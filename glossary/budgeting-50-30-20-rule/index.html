What is 50-30-20 budgeting rule?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

50-30-20 budgeting rule: The 50-30-20 rule is a simple budgeting guideline that splits take-home income into 50% for needs, 30% for wants and 20% for savings and debt repayment.

Needs are expenses you must pay to live and work: rent or home loan EMI, groceries, utilities, basic transport, insurance premiums, school fees and minimum loan payments. Wants are discretionary: eating out, entertainment, travel, gadgets, shopping beyond basics, subscriptions. Savings include SIPs, PPF, emergency fund contributions and extra loan prepayments.

The rule became popular through US personal finance writing, and it works as a starting framework rather than a law. In high-rent Indian cities, needs may take 60% of a young professional's income. Those with family responsibilities may need a different split. Many planners suggest raising savings to 25–30% when possible, especially once salaries rise.

Its value is in simplicity. It gives a quick check on whether lifestyle spending is crowding out savings, without tracking every rupee. Automating the savings portion on salary day, the 'pay yourself first' habit, makes the rule work.

Mistakes include classifying wants as needs (a premium phone plan or a car upgrade), applying percentages to gross salary instead of take-home pay, and not adjusting as income grows. Note that EPF deducted from salary is already a form of saving.

Example

Ankit, 26, takes home ₹80,000 a month in Pune. Under 50-30-20: needs ₹40,000 (rent ₹18,000, food and utilities ₹12,000, transport ₹4,000, insurance and family support ₹6,000), wants ₹24,000, savings ₹16,000. He sets up SIPs of ₹12,000 and an RD of ₹4,000 for his emergency fund on the 2nd of each month, so the savings happen before spending begins.

FAQs

Is 20% savings enough?

It is a reasonable minimum for many people, but those starting late, with ambitious goals or high incomes may need to save more, often 25–40%. Goal-based calculations show the actual savings needed. The 50-30-20 rule is a starting point, not a target for everyone.

Where do EMIs fit in the 50-30-20 rule?

Minimum EMI payments on essential loans like a home loan are generally treated as needs. Extra prepayments count toward the savings portion. EMIs for lifestyle purchases, such as a gadget on a no-cost EMI, are better treated as wants.
