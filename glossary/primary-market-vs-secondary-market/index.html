What is Primary Market vs Secondary Market?

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Primary Market vs Secondary Market: The primary market is where companies issue new securities to raise money; the secondary market is where investors trade existing securities among themselves on stock exchanges.

When a company sells shares or bonds for the first time — through an IPO, a follow-on offer, a rights issue, a private placement or a QIP — the money goes to the company (or to selling shareholders in an offer for sale). That is the primary market, sometimes called the new issue market. Merchant bankers, registrars, underwriters and bankers to the issue are the key intermediaries here.

Once those securities are listed, they trade on exchanges such as NSE and BSE. That is the secondary market. Here the company receives nothing: if you buy 100 shares of a listed bank, your money goes to another investor who was selling. Stock brokers, clearing corporations and depositories are the main intermediaries.

The two markets depend on each other. Investors are willing to subscribe in the primary market because they know they can later exit in the secondary market. And secondary market prices tell companies how much investors value them, which affects the price at which they can raise fresh capital.

A frequent exam trap: an offer for sale (OFS) through the exchange window happens on the exchange platform, yet the shares are existing ones sold by promoters, and the company does not get the money. Similarly, a bonus issue creates new shares but raises no money, so it is not a fund-raising primary issue at all.

Example

A company raises ₹500 crore through an IPO by issuing 2 crore new shares at ₹250 each — that ₹500 crore reaches the company's bank account (minus issue expenses). A month after listing, the share trades at ₹310 on NSE. An investor who sells 1,000 shares at ₹310 receives ₹3,10,000 from another investor; the company's cash balance does not change by a single rupee.

FAQs

Is an IPO part of the primary or secondary market?

An IPO is a primary market transaction because the shares are being issued for the first time. However, many Indian IPOs combine a fresh issue (money goes to the company) with an offer for sale (money goes to existing shareholders). Both parts happen in the primary market, but only the fresh issue portion raises capital for the business.

Why is the secondary market important if the company gets no money?

It gives investors liquidity and continuous price discovery. Without a place to sell, few people would invest in new issues. A healthy secondary market also lets companies judge investor appetite and raise money later through follow-on offers, QIPs or rights issues at prices linked to the market value.
