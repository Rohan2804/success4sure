What is Retirement corpus?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Retirement corpus: A retirement corpus is the total sum of money a person needs to have accumulated by retirement to fund living expenses for the rest of their life.

Estimating it starts with today's annual expenses that will continue after retirement, excluding costs like children's education or home loan EMIs that will end, and adding new ones like higher medical spending. These expenses are inflated to the retirement date. Then the planner estimates how much money is needed at retirement to fund those expenses, rising with inflation, for 25–30 years or more.

A quick rule of thumb is 25 to 33 times first-year retirement expenses, linked to the idea of a sustainable withdrawal rate of 3–4%. A more precise method uses the present value of a growing annuity, with an assumed post-retirement return and inflation. Pensions, EPF, NPS and rental income reduce the corpus required from personal savings.

The biggest risks are underestimating inflation, especially medical inflation, underestimating life expectancy, and assuming high returns after retirement. Sequence-of-returns risk, where markets fall just after retirement, can also damage a corpus.

Many people underestimate the corpus because they think in today's rupees. Planners show figures in both future and today's terms to make them meaningful.

Example

Suresh, 35, spends ₹50,000 a month (₹6 lakh a year) and plans to retire at 60. At 6% inflation, his annual expenses at retirement would be about ₹6 lakh × 1.06^25 ≈ ₹25.75 lakh. Using a 25-times rule, his target corpus is about ₹6.4 crore. In today's rupees that is about ₹1.5 crore, which helps him grasp the scale. His EPF and NPS balances will count toward this.

FAQs

How much money is enough to retire in India?

It depends on your expenses, lifestyle, inflation, life expectancy, pension and other income. A rough approach is 25–33 times your first-year retirement expenses. A registered adviser or retirement adviser can build a detailed projection with realistic assumptions.

Should my retirement corpus be fully in debt after retirement?

Usually not. Retirement may last 25–30 years, during which inflation erodes fixed-income returns. Many planners keep a portion in equity for growth, with several years of expenses in safer assets to avoid selling equity during downturns.
