What is Fund of Funds (FoF)?

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Fund of Funds (FoF): A fund of funds (FoF) is a mutual fund scheme that invests mainly in units of other mutual fund schemes or ETFs rather than directly in shares or bonds.

An FoF builds its portfolio out of other funds. A domestic FoF might hold a mix of equity, debt and gold ETFs to create a multi-asset portfolio. An overseas FoF invests in a foreign mutual fund or ETF, giving Indian investors access to US or global markets in rupees through an ordinary folio. Gold and silver FoFs invest in the corresponding ETFs so that investors without a demat account can participate.

Costs come in two layers: the FoF's own expense ratio and the expenses of the underlying schemes. SEBI caps the combined TER for FoFs, but total cost can still be higher than buying the underlying fund directly. Investors should look at the total, which AMCs disclose.

Overseas FoFs are subject to industry-wide limits on foreign investment set by SEBI and RBI. When the limits were reached in 2022, several AMCs stopped accepting fresh money, which surprised investors who had running SIPs.

Tax treatment of an FoF depends on how much it invests in domestic equity; most FoFs do not qualify as equity-oriented and are taxed under non-equity rules that have changed several times recently. Check current income tax rules as of 2026 before assuming a rate.

Example

An asset allocation FoF invests ₹100 crore as 50% in a Nifty 50 ETF, 30% in a short duration fund and 20% in a gold ETF. Its own TER is 0.15% and the weighted TER of its holdings is about 0.35%, so the total annual cost is roughly 0.50%. An investor putting ₹2 lakh into this FoF gets a three-asset portfolio, rebalanced by the fund manager, through one scheme and one NAV.

FAQs

Why do fund of funds have higher costs?

Investors bear the FoF's own expenses plus the expenses charged inside each underlying scheme. Even though SEBI caps the total, the combination is often higher than investing in a single fund directly. The convenience of one-stop allocation or overseas access is what the extra cost buys.

Do I need a demat account for a gold FoF?

No. A gold FoF is a regular mutual fund scheme that invests in gold ETFs. You buy it from the AMC at NAV, like any other fund, and can use SIPs. To buy a gold ETF directly you would need a demat and broking account.
