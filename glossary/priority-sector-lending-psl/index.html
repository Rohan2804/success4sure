What is Priority Sector Lending (PSL)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Priority Sector Lending (PSL): Priority sector lending is RBI's requirement that banks direct a minimum share of their credit to sectors considered important for development, like agriculture, MSMEs and weaker sections.

The idea dates back to 1972, after bank nationalisation, when the government wanted credit to reach farmers and small businesses. Today RBI's Master Directions list eight categories: agriculture, micro small and medium enterprises, export credit, education, housing, social infrastructure, renewable energy, and others (including loans to weaker sections and small personal loans to distressed persons).

For domestic scheduled commercial banks and foreign banks with 20 or more branches, the overall target is 40% of Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC) or Credit Equivalent of Off-Balance Sheet Exposure (CEOBE), whichever is higher. Sub-targets include 18% for agriculture (with a part reserved for small and marginal farmers), 7.5% for micro enterprises, and a share for weaker sections. RRBs and small finance banks have higher overall targets. These percentages have been revised several times, so check the latest RBI Master Direction (as of 2026).

Banks that fall short must deposit the shortfall in funds like the Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF) with NABARD or similar funds with SIDBI and NHB, at below-market interest rates. Since 2016, banks can also trade Priority Sector Lending Certificates (PSLCs) on RBI's e-Kuber platform: a bank with surplus PSL loans sells certificates to a bank with a shortfall, without transferring the loans.

Common mistakes: not every agricultural loan qualifies (limits apply to some categories), and housing loans qualify only up to set amount limits based on city population.

Example

A bank's ANBC is ₹2,00,000 crore. PSL target = 40% = ₹80,000 crore; agriculture sub-target = 18% = ₹36,000 crore; micro enterprises = 7.5% = ₹15,000 crore. If the bank's agri lending is only ₹33,000 crore, it has a ₹3,000 crore shortfall. It can buy agriculture PSLCs worth ₹3,000 crore from a bank with surplus, paying a fee set in the auction, say 1.2%, which is ₹36 crore.

FAQs

What is ANBC in priority sector lending?

Adjusted Net Bank Credit is broadly the bank's net bank credit plus certain investments in non-SLR bonds held to maturity, minus specified exclusions like certain refinance and eligible bills rediscounted. PSL targets are calculated on the higher of ANBC or the credit equivalent of off-balance sheet exposures, as of the corresponding date of the previous year.

Do PSLCs transfer the actual loans?

No. Priority Sector Lending Certificates transfer only the PSL credit, not the loans or their credit risk. The selling bank keeps the loans on its books, while the buying bank can count the certificate towards its target. Certificates are traded on RBI's e-Kuber platform and expire at the end of the financial year.
