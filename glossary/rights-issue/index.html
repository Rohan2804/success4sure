What is Rights Issue?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Rights Issue: A rights issue is an offer of new shares by a listed company to its existing shareholders, in proportion to their current holding, usually at a discount to the market price.

Rights issues let a company raise capital while giving existing shareholders the first chance to maintain their percentage ownership. The offer is made in a ratio such as 1:5, meaning one new share for every five held on the record date. Under the Companies Act, 2013, the offer must remain open for a minimum period, and shareholders may accept, decline or renounce their rights in favour of others.

SEBI has made the process largely electronic. Rights entitlements (REs) are credited to shareholders' demat accounts under a separate ISIN and can be traded on the exchange during the renunciation period. A shareholder who does not want to invest can sell the REs instead of letting them lapse. Applications are made through ASBA.

Because the new shares come at a discount, the market price usually adjusts after the record date. The theoretical ex-rights price (TERP) is the weighted average of the old price and the rights price across old and new shares. A shareholder who neither subscribes nor sells the REs suffers dilution and an effective loss in value.

Many investors think a rights issue at a discount is 'free money'. It is not: you must pay to subscribe, and the discount is offset by the fall in price after the issue. Rights issues also differ from bonus issues, which involve no payment, and from preferential allotments, which go only to selected investors.

Example

A company's share trades at ₹300. It announces a 1:4 rights issue at ₹200. A shareholder with 400 shares is entitled to 100 new shares. TERP = (4 × ₹300 + 1 × ₹200) ÷ 5 = ₹280. If she subscribes, she pays ₹20,000 and holds 500 shares worth ₹1,40,000 — the same as her earlier ₹1,20,000 plus ₹20,000 paid. If she ignores the offer, her 400 shares fall to about ₹1,12,000.

FAQs

What happens if I don't apply in a rights issue?

Your rights entitlements lapse at the end of the issue, and you do not get the new shares. Because the market price typically adjusts downward toward the theoretical ex-rights price, your holding loses some value relative to before. Selling the REs during the renunciation period lets you capture their market value instead.

Can I apply for more shares than my entitlement?

In many rights issues, shareholders may apply for additional shares beyond their entitlement. Additional shares are allotted only if some shareholders do not subscribe, and the basis of allotment is given in the letter of offer. Promoters often also commit to subscribe to unsubscribed portions.
