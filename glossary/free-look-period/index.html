What is Free-Look Period?

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Free-Look Period: The free-look period is a window after receiving a new insurance policy during which the policyholder can review it and return it for a refund if not satisfied with the terms.

IRDAI introduced the free-look provision to protect buyers from mis-selling. Once you receive the policy document, you can read the terms, exclusions and charges, and if they are not what you were told, cancel without penalty, stating your reasons. Under the IRDAI (Protection of Policyholders' Interests) Regulations and the 2024 master circular, the free-look period for life and health policies is 30 days from the receipt of the policy document, whether physical or electronic. Earlier it was 15 days, with 30 days for distance marketing. Check IRDAI for current rules (as of 2026).

The refund is not always the full premium. For traditional life policies, the insurer may deduct proportionate risk premium for the days of cover, the cost of medical examination and stamp duty. For ULIPs, the insurer also adjusts for the change in unit value, so the refund equals the fund value on cancellation plus non-allocated premium and charges levied, minus the permitted deductions.

The free-look option is not available for policies with tenure of less than one year in some categories, such as short-term travel or motor insurance, and normally applies to new policies, not renewals. In health insurance, it also does not apply to portability renewals in the same way.

Common confusion: free-look is different from the grace period. Free-look is at the start of a policy to cancel it; grace period is the time allowed to pay a renewal premium after the due date.

Example

Deepa buys a traditional life policy with an annual premium of ₹48,000. She receives the policy document on 1 June and finds charges she wasn't told about. On 20 June she submits a free-look cancellation. The insurer deducts proportionate risk premium for 20 days (say ₹180), medical tests of ₹1,500 and stamp duty of ₹200. Refund = 48,000 − 180 − 1,500 − 200 = ₹46,120.

FAQs

Will I get my full premium back in the free-look period?

Not always. The insurer may deduct the proportionate risk premium for the days you were covered, stamp duty, and medical examination costs. For ULIPs, the refund is also adjusted for any change in the value of the units. Still, the deductions are small compared with surrendering a policy later.

When does the free-look period start?

It starts from the date you receive the policy document, not from the date of payment or issuance. For electronic policies, it is the date of receipt of the electronic document. Keeping proof of the delivery date, such as a courier receipt or email timestamp, helps if there is a dispute.
