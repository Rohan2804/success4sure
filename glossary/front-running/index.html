What is Front Running?

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Front Running: Front running is trading in a security ahead of a known large pending order from a client or fund, to profit from the price movement that order is likely to cause.

Large orders move prices. A dealer who knows that a mutual fund will buy 20 lakh shares of a stock in the afternoon can buy the same stock earlier — in their own name or through a relative — and sell it to the fund or in the market after the price rises. The profit comes directly at the cost of the client, who pays a higher price.

SEBI treats front running as a fraudulent and unfair trade practice under the PFUTP Regulations. It applies to anyone who has access to non-public information about large orders, including employees of brokers, fund houses, insurance companies and custodians, and anyone they share the information with. Intermediaries must have policies to prevent it, such as restricting personal trading and recording dealer calls.

Detection relies on trade surveillance. Regulators look for accounts that repeatedly buy just before a large order and sell during or just after it, often in matching quantities, and link them through phone records, IP addresses and family relationships. Several SEBI orders in recent years have involved dealers of fund houses and their relatives.

Front running is different from insider trading, though both misuse confidential information. Insider trading concerns company-specific UPSI, while front running concerns knowledge of pending orders. It also differs from legitimate trading on public news, which anyone may do.

Example

A fund house dealer receives an order to buy 10 lakh shares of a mid-cap at around ₹300. Before placing it, he tips a friend, who buys 50,000 shares at ₹300. The fund's large order pushes the price to ₹309, and the friend sells at an average ₹308. Profit: ₹8 × 50,000 = ₹4 lakh, which effectively came out of the fund investors' pockets through the higher purchase price.

FAQs

Is front running the same as insider trading?

No, though both are prohibited. Insider trading uses unpublished price sensitive information about a company. Front running uses knowledge of an upcoming large order. Both are forms of misuse of confidential information, but they are covered by different regulations: PIT Regulations and PFUTP Regulations respectively.

How do intermediaries prevent front running?

They restrict and monitor personal trading by employees, require pre-approval of personal trades, record dealer phone lines, use surveillance to flag trades ahead of client orders and separate dealing teams from others. SEBI also requires fund houses to have an institutional mechanism to deter and detect such misconduct.
