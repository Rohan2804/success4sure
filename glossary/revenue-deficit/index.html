What is Revenue Deficit?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Revenue Deficit: Revenue deficit is the excess of the government's revenue expenditure over its revenue receipts, showing that it is borrowing to meet day-to-day running costs.

Revenue receipts are tax revenue (income tax, corporation tax, GST, customs, excise, net of states' share) and non-tax revenue (dividends from RBI and PSUs, fees, interest received). Revenue expenditure covers spending that creates no asset: salaries, pensions, interest payments, subsidies and grants. Formula: Revenue deficit = Revenue expenditure − Revenue receipts.

A revenue deficit is considered worse than a fiscal deficit of the same size because the borrowed money is used for consumption rather than investment. It adds to debt without creating assets that could generate future income. That is why the FRBM framework originally aimed to eliminate the revenue deficit altogether.

Effective revenue deficit (ERD) refines this. A large part of the Centre's revenue expenditure is grants to states for creating capital assets, such as roads under centrally sponsored schemes. These are revenue spending in the Centre's books but build assets in the economy. ERD = Revenue deficit − Grants for creation of capital assets. The concept was introduced in the 2011-12 Budget.

Exam pointers: interest payments are revenue expenditure, so they push up the revenue deficit; loans given by the government to states or PSUs are capital expenditure, not revenue. Disinvestment proceeds are capital receipts and do not reduce the revenue deficit. India's revenue deficit has been positive for most years since the 1980s.

Example

Suppose revenue receipts are ₹34 lakh crore and revenue expenditure is ₹39 lakh crore. Revenue deficit = 39 − 34 = ₹5 lakh crore. If ₹3.2 lakh crore of that spending is grants to states for creating capital assets, the effective revenue deficit = 5 − 3.2 = ₹1.8 lakh crore. With a fiscal deficit of ₹15.2 lakh crore, about 5/15.2 = 33% of borrowing finances revenue spending.

FAQs

Are interest payments revenue or capital expenditure?

Interest payments are revenue expenditure because they do not create any asset; they are a cost of past borrowing. They are the single largest item of the Centre's revenue spending. Repayment of the principal of a loan, by contrast, is a capital expenditure item.

Can disinvestment reduce the revenue deficit?

No. Selling government shares in PSUs brings non-debt capital receipts, which reduce the fiscal deficit but not the revenue deficit. Using one-time asset sales to fund recurring spending is criticised because the government loses the asset while the spending continues next year.
