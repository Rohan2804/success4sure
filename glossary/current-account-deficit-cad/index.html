What is Current Account Deficit (CAD)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Current Account Deficit (CAD): Current account deficit arises when a country's payments to the rest of the world for goods, services, income and transfers exceed what it receives from abroad in a period.

The current account is one half of the balance of payments (BoP); the other is the capital and financial account. The current account has four parts: merchandise trade (goods exports minus imports), services trade (IT, travel, transport), primary income (interest, dividends, wages earned or paid abroad) and secondary income (mostly remittances). Services, primary income and transfers together are called invisibles.

India runs a large merchandise trade deficit because of crude oil, gold and electronics imports. This is offset partly by a services surplus led by software exports and by large remittances from Indians abroad. The net result is usually a modest CAD, commonly between about 1% and 3% of GDP. RBI publishes quarterly BoP data.

A CAD must be financed by capital inflows: foreign direct investment, foreign portfolio investment, external commercial borrowings or NRI deposits. If inflows fall short, RBI draws down forex reserves. A widening CAD with weak inflows puts pressure on the rupee, as in 2013, when India was grouped with the 'Fragile Five' economies.

Watch the twin deficits idea: a high fiscal deficit can widen the CAD because government borrowing raises demand, including for imports. Also note that a current account surplus is not automatically good; India had one in 2020-21 mainly because imports collapsed during the pandemic.

Example

In a quarter, suppose goods exports are $110 billion and imports $175 billion, a trade deficit of $65 billion. Net services bring in $45 billion, net remittances $30 billion, and net primary income is −$12 billion. CAD = −65 + 45 + 30 − 12 = −$2 billion, a small deficit. If quarterly GDP is $1,000 billion, that is 0.2% of GDP. A $15 billion jump in oil imports would raise it to $17 billion, or 1.7%.

FAQs

What is the difference between CAD and trade deficit?

Trade deficit usually refers only to goods: merchandise imports minus exports. CAD is broader and also includes services, income flows and transfers like remittances. India's trade deficit is much larger than its CAD because services exports and remittances cover a big part of the goods gap.

How does a high CAD affect the rupee?

A CAD means more foreign currency is needed for payments than earned. If capital inflows do not fill the gap, demand for dollars exceeds supply and the rupee weakens. RBI may sell dollars from reserves to smooth the fall, but a persistent large CAD is a source of currency vulnerability.
