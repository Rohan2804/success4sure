What is Time value of money?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Time value of money: Time value of money is the principle that a rupee today is worth more than a rupee in the future, because today's rupee can be invested to earn a return.

If you can earn 8% a year, ₹1,00,000 today becomes ₹1,08,000 in a year. So receiving ₹1,00,000 next year is worth less than receiving it today. Inflation reinforces the idea: the same amount buys less in future.

Two formulas capture the principle. Future value: FV = PV × (1 + r)^n, where r is the rate per period and n the number of periods. Present value: PV = FV ÷ (1 + r)^n, which discounts a future amount back to today. Annuity formulas extend this to regular payments such as SIPs, EMIs and pensions.

Time value of money is the foundation of financial planning. It is used to calculate how much to save for a goal, to compare a lump sum with a series of payments, to value bonds, and to decide whether to take a pension or commutation. NISM adviser exams test FV, PV and annuity calculations.

A common error is ignoring the time value when comparing offers. A policy that 'returns ₹10 lakh after 20 years for ₹5 lakh paid' sounds like doubling money, but in time-value terms that is only about 3.5% a year.

Example

A company offers Ramesh either ₹1,00,000 in five years or ₹70,000 today. If he can earn 8% a year, the present value of ₹1,00,000 in five years is ₹1,00,000 ÷ 1.08^5 ≈ ₹68,058. Since ₹70,000 today is worth more than that, taking the money now is better at that rate. If his expected return were 6%, the present value would be about ₹74,726, and waiting would be better.

FAQs

What is the difference between present value and future value?

Future value tells you what an amount invested today will grow to at a given rate. Present value tells you what a future amount is worth today, by discounting it at a given rate. They are the same formula rearranged.

Which discount rate should be used for present value?

Use the return you could reasonably earn on an alternative investment of similar risk, often called the opportunity cost. For a safe future payment, a government bond or bank deposit rate is common. For risky cash flows, a higher rate is used.
