What is Bonus Issue (Bonus Shares)?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Bonus Issue (Bonus Shares): A bonus issue is a free distribution of additional shares to existing shareholders by converting part of the company's reserves into share capital.

When a company capitalises its free reserves, securities premium or capital redemption reserve, it issues new fully paid shares to shareholders in a stated ratio. A 1:1 bonus gives one new share for every share held; 1:2 gives one new share for every two held. No money is paid by shareholders and none is raised by the company. Bonus shares cannot be issued from revaluation reserves or in lieu of dividend.

Since the company's total value does not change, the share price adjusts on the ex-date roughly in proportion to the ratio. If you held 100 shares at ₹900 before a 1:2 bonus, you hold 150 shares worth about ₹600 each afterward — still about ₹90,000. Exchanges also adjust derivative contracts so that futures and options positions remain economically the same.

Why issue bonus shares then? It brings the share price into a more affordable range, increases the number of shares available for trading, signals management confidence that reserves are healthy, and moves reserves into paid-up capital. Dividend per share may fall but total dividend paid may stay the same or rise.

Bonus vs stock split is a classic exam question. A bonus changes the balance sheet — reserves fall and share capital rises — while the face value per share stays the same. A split changes the face value (for example ₹10 to ₹2) and leaves reserves and total share capital unchanged.

Example

A company has 10 crore shares of face value ₹10 (share capital ₹100 crore) and reserves of ₹800 crore. It announces a 1:1 bonus. After the issue it has 20 crore shares, share capital of ₹200 crore and reserves of ₹700 crore — total net worth unchanged at ₹900 crore. An investor's 60 shares at ₹1,480 become 120 shares at roughly ₹740.

FAQs

Are bonus shares really free?

In the sense that you pay nothing, yes. But your wealth does not increase at the moment of issue because the share price adjusts downward in proportion. The value of your holding before and after the ex-date is about the same; any gain or loss afterwards comes from normal market movement.

How long does it take to receive bonus shares?

Bonus shares are credited to demat accounts by the depositories after the record date, and SEBI has shortened the timeline so that bonus shares become available for trading soon after the record date. Check the company's exchange announcement for the exact credit and trading dates for any specific bonus issue.
