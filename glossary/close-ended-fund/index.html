What is Close-ended fund?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Close-ended fund: A close-ended fund is a mutual fund scheme with a fixed maturity whose units are sold only during the new fund offer and are not redeemed by the AMC before maturity.

A close-ended scheme raises money once, through its NFO, and then invests for a stated tenure such as three or five years. The unit capital is fixed after the offer. Investors cannot buy more units from the AMC later and cannot ask the AMC to buy units back before maturity.

To give investors an exit, SEBI requires close-ended schemes to list their units on a recognised stock exchange. In practice trading volumes are often thin, and units may change hands at a discount to NAV. On maturity, the scheme is wound up or converted and investors receive the NAV of that date.

Fixed maturity plans (FMPs) are the best-known close-ended debt schemes; they buy bonds maturing close to the scheme's own maturity, which reduces interest rate risk if held to the end. Some close-ended equity funds use the fixed tenure to invest in smaller, less liquid companies without worrying about redemptions.

A frequent confusion: a close-ended fund is not a guaranteed-return product. FMP brochures show 'indicative' yields, but returns depend on the actual portfolio and defaults can occur. NAV is still disclosed regularly even though you cannot redeem at it daily.

Example

An FMP with a 1,100-day tenure raises ₹400 crore at ₹10 per unit and buys bonds maturing just before the scheme. After a year its NAV is ₹10.80, but on the exchange units trade at ₹10.50 because few buyers exist. An investor who must exit now loses about 2.8% versus NAV. Another who holds to maturity receives the final NAV, say ₹12.30, directly from the fund.

FAQs

Can I sell close-ended fund units before maturity?

Yes, by selling them on the stock exchange where they are listed, through your demat account and broker. The AMC itself will not redeem them early. Liquidity can be thin, so you may have to accept a price below NAV.

Why do close-ended funds trade at a discount?

Low trading volumes, few buyers and the lock-in until maturity make investors demand a lower price. The discount usually narrows as maturity approaches because the payout at NAV becomes near and certain.
