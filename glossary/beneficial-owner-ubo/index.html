What is Beneficial Owner (UBO)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Beneficial Owner (UBO): A beneficial owner is the natural person who ultimately owns or controls a client account, or on whose behalf a transaction is conducted, even if the account is in another entity's name.

When a client is a company, partnership, trust or other entity, intermediaries cannot stop at the entity's name. Under PMLA rules and SEBI circulars they must identify the individuals behind it — the ultimate beneficial owners (UBOs). This prevents criminals from hiding behind layers of shell companies.

For a company, a beneficial owner is a natural person who, alone or together with others, owns more than a specified percentage of shares or capital, or exercises control through other means such as voting agreements or the right to appoint directors. The thresholds were tightened in 2023 to more than 10% for companies and partnership firms (as of 2026 — check the current PML Rules and SEBI circulars). For trusts, the settlor, trustees, protector, beneficiaries with a defined stake and anyone with ultimate control are identified.

If no individual crosses the threshold and no one exercises control by other means, the senior managing official is treated as the beneficial owner. Intermediaries must verify the identity of UBOs, keep records and update them when ownership changes.

In depository terms, 'beneficial owner' also has a different, narrower meaning: the person whose name appears in the demat account as entitled to the securities, as opposed to the depository, which is the registered owner. Exam questions can test either meaning, so read the context carefully.

Example

A private company opens a trading account. Its shareholding: Holding Co. A (60%), Mr. Rao directly (8%), others (32%). Holding Co. A is owned 50% by Ms. Mehta and 50% by Mr. Rao. Indirect stakes: Ms. Mehta 30% and Mr. Rao 30% + 8% = 38%. Both exceed 10%, so both are beneficial owners whose KYC the broker must collect and verify.

FAQs

Why do intermediaries need to identify beneficial owners?

Without identifying the real individuals behind an entity, it would be easy to use shell companies or trusts to hide illegal money or evade sanctions. Identifying UBOs lets intermediaries assess risk properly, screen against sanctions lists and report suspicious activity linked to the true controllers.

Is the beneficial owner the same as the account holder?

Not always. For an individual, the account holder is usually the beneficial owner. For a company or trust, the account is in the entity's name, but the beneficial owners are the natural persons who ultimately own or control it. In depository law, the demat account holder is called the beneficial owner.
