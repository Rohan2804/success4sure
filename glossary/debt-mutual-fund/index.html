What is Debt mutual fund?

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Debt mutual fund: A debt mutual fund invests in fixed-income securities such as government securities, treasury bills, corporate bonds and money market instruments, earning interest and price changes.

Debt funds pool money to buy bonds and money market paper. Returns come from two sources: interest accruing on the holdings and changes in their market prices. When interest rates fall, existing bond prices rise and NAVs go up; when rates rise, NAVs fall. The longer the portfolio's duration, the bigger this effect.

SEBI's 2017 categorisation created 16 debt categories, defined by duration (overnight, liquid, ultra short, low, money market, short, medium, medium to long, long), by credit quality (corporate bond, credit risk, banking and PSU) or by issuer (gilt, gilt with 10-year constant duration), plus dynamic bond and floater funds. Each scheme must also disclose a potential risk class matrix showing its maximum interest rate and credit risk.

The two main risks are interest rate risk and credit risk, the chance that an issuer delays or defaults. Liquidity risk matters too: some bonds are hard to sell in a hurry. The 2020 closure of six debt schemes by one AMC showed what can happen when all three combine.

Many investors treat debt funds as fixed deposits. They are not guaranteed, NAV can fall, and as of 2026 gains on debt funds bought after 1 April 2023 are taxed at slab rate regardless of holding period. Check current tax rules before comparing with FDs.

Example

A medium duration fund has a portfolio Macaulay duration of about 4 years and modified duration of about 3.7. If market yields on similar bonds rise by 0.50 percentage point, the NAV would fall by roughly 3.7 × 0.5% ≈ 1.85%. An investor with ₹4 lakh in the fund would see the value dip by about ₹7,400, partly offset over time by higher accrual at the new yields.

FAQs

Can a debt mutual fund give negative returns?

Yes. If interest rates rise sharply, longer duration funds can show losses over short periods. If an issuer defaults, the fund marks the bond down and NAV falls immediately. Over longer holding periods, accrual usually outweighs price changes in good-quality portfolios, but nothing is assured.

How are debt funds taxed in India?

As of 2026, for units of specified debt-oriented funds bought on or after 1 April 2023, all gains are taxed at the investor's slab rate with no indexation, whatever the holding period. Older units may follow earlier rules. Tax law changes frequently, so confirm with the income tax department's current guidance.
