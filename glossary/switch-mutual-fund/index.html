What is Switch (mutual funds)?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Switch (mutual funds): A switch is a single instruction to redeem units of one scheme or plan and invest the proceeds in another scheme or plan of the same mutual fund house.

Switching saves the trouble of redeeming, waiting for money in the bank and reinvesting. The AMC processes both legs, often on the same day, so the money is never out of the market. Investors use switches to change asset allocation, move from a regular plan to a direct plan, or move from growth to IDCW option.

Legally, a switch is a redemption followed by a purchase. The switch-out leg is valued at the source scheme's applicable NAV and the switch-in leg at the target's. Because of this, capital gains tax applies on the switch-out, and exit load applies if the units are still within the load period.

Switching between plans of the same scheme, for example regular to direct, is also a redemption for tax purposes. There is no special tax exemption for switching within the same fund house.

Investors sometimes switch frequently in response to market news, generating tax and load costs while gaining little. A planned switch as part of rebalancing is sensible; reactive switching usually is not. A systematic version, the STP, spreads switches over time.

Example

Harish holds 4,000 units of an equity fund's regular plan bought 18 months ago at NAV ₹50 (₹2,00,000). NAV is now ₹65. He switches all units to the direct plan. Switch-out value is ₹2,60,000, so he has a long-term capital gain of ₹60,000 for this year. There is no exit load because the 1-year period has passed. The ₹2,60,000 buys units in the direct plan at its own NAV, which is slightly higher.

FAQs

Is a switch between two schemes of the same AMC taxable?

Yes. Tax law treats the switch-out as a sale of units, so any capital gain is taxable in that financial year. Holding period for the new scheme starts afresh from the switch-in date.

Can I switch between mutual funds of different AMCs?

No. A switch works only within one fund house. To move between AMCs, redeem from one and invest in the other separately. The tax and load consequences are the same as for a switch.
