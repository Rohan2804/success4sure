What is Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)?

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR): Statutory Liquidity Ratio is the minimum share of net demand and time liabilities that a bank must hold in liquid assets: cash, gold, or unencumbered approved government securities.

SLR is prescribed under Section 24 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949. Unlike CRR, the assets stay with the bank. In practice almost all SLR holdings are government securities: central government bonds, treasury bills, and state development loans. The ceiling under the Act is 40% of NDTL; RBI has kept it at 18% since 2020 (check the current figure as of 2026).

SLR serves two purposes. First, it ensures solvency and liquidity: if depositors suddenly withdraw, the bank has assets that can be sold or pledged quickly. Second, it creates a captive demand for government bonds, which helps the government borrow. Critics call this financial repression because it pushes banks to hold bonds even when private lending would earn more.

Changes in SLR are now rare and are not used for routine monetary policy. However, SLR securities interact with other tools: under the Marginal Standing Facility, banks may dip into SLR holdings up to a limit. Securities in excess of the SLR requirement are what banks pledge for repo borrowing.

Remember the differences from CRR: SLR assets earn interest, are held by the bank itself, and can be in three forms, while CRR is only cash parked with RBI. The 'unencumbered' condition matters: securities already pledged elsewhere cannot be counted.

Example

A bank with NDTL of ₹1,50,000 crore and an 18% SLR must hold ₹27,000 crore in eligible assets. If it holds ₹30,000 crore of government securities, ₹3,000 crore is excess SLR, which it can pledge for repo. If these bonds yield 6.8%, the SLR portfolio earns about 27,000 × 6.8% = ₹1,836 crore a year, unlike CRR money which earns nothing.

FAQs

Is SLR maintained with RBI?

No. SLR assets are maintained by the bank itself, in its own vaults or in its securities account (SGL account with RBI for government bonds). The bank owns and earns interest on them. Only CRR is a cash balance with RBI. This is one of the most frequently tested differences.

Can SLR and LCR overlap?

Yes. Government securities held for SLR can also count as high-quality liquid assets for the liquidity coverage ratio, within limits RBI allows, such as the MSF dip-down portion and a further 'FALLCR' carve-out. So the same bond can partly serve both requirements.
