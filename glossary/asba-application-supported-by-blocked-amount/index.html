What is ASBA (Application Supported by Blocked Amount)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

ASBA (Application Supported by Blocked Amount): ASBA is the mandatory process for public issue applications in which the application money stays in the investor's bank account but is blocked until shares are allotted.

Before ASBA, investors paid by cheque and waited weeks for refunds if they got nothing. Under ASBA, a self-certified syndicate bank (SCSB) blocks the bid amount in the investor's account. The money keeps earning savings interest and cannot be used for anything else while blocked. On allotment, only the amount for allotted shares is debited, and the balance is unblocked.

Individual investors can apply in two main ways. They can use net banking ASBA directly with their bank, or apply through a broker, DP, registrar or app using the UPI mechanism, where the investor approves a UPI mandate on their phone to block funds. UPI-based applications have a value limit for individuals — ₹5 lakh as of 2026; check current SEBI rules.

ASBA is compulsory for all public issues: IPOs, FPOs, rights issues and public issues of debt. One PAN can submit only one application per category in an IPO; multiple applications with the same PAN are rejected, which is why using different apps does not increase your chances.

Common confusion: a mandate request that is not approved before the cut-off time means no application at all. Also, the blocked amount is visible in your bank as a 'lien' or reduced available balance — it is not a payment, and you do not need to request a refund if nothing is allotted.

Example

Priya applies for 2 lots of 60 shares in an IPO with a band of ₹235–₹248, bidding at cut-off. Her bank blocks 2 × 60 × ₹248 = ₹29,760 via UPI mandate. Final price is ₹248 and she gets 1 lot. On allotment day ₹14,880 is debited and ₹14,880 is unblocked. Had she received nothing, the full ₹29,760 would simply have been unblocked.

FAQs

What is an SCSB?

A self-certified syndicate bank is a SEBI-registered banker to an issue that offers ASBA facility — accepting applications, blocking funds and transferring money on allotment. Most large commercial banks are SCSBs. The list, along with branches that accept physical ASBA forms and banks that support UPI, is published on SEBI's website.

Does ASBA apply to rights issues?

Yes. Shareholders applying in rights issues also use ASBA, and SEBI has moved rights issues to a largely electronic process where rights entitlements are credited to demat accounts and can be traded. Applying through ASBA means the money stays in your account until shares are allotted.
