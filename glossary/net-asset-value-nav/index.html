What is Net Asset Value (NAV)?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Net Asset Value (NAV): Net Asset Value (NAV) is the per-unit market value of a mutual fund scheme: total assets minus liabilities, divided by the number of units outstanding.

NAV is the price at which investors buy and sell units of an open-ended mutual fund scheme. The AMC values every security in the portfolio at the day's closing market price, adds cash and accrued income, subtracts liabilities such as expenses payable and redemption money owed, and divides the result by the number of units in issue.

Most schemes declare NAV once every business day, after markets close, and AMFI and the AMC websites publish it the same evening. Your purchase or redemption is processed at the NAV of the day your application and money reach the fund within the cut-off time, so you never know the exact price at the moment you place an order. This is called forward pricing and it protects existing investors from people trading on stale prices.

Each plan and option of a scheme has its own NAV. A direct plan carries a lower expense ratio than the regular plan of the same scheme, so its NAV grows slightly faster and the gap widens over the years.

The most common confusion is treating NAV like a share price. A fund with a NAV of ₹12 is not cheaper than one at ₹300. If both portfolios rise 10%, both NAVs rise 10% and your ₹10,000 grows to ₹11,000 either way. What matters is the change in NAV over time, the costs and the quality of the portfolio, not the NAV level itself. New fund offers launched at ₹10 are not bargains for the same reason.

Example

An equity scheme holds shares worth ₹515 crore and cash of ₹5 crore, so total assets are ₹520 crore. It owes ₹20 crore (expenses payable and pending redemptions). Net assets are ₹500 crore. With 25 crore units outstanding, NAV = ₹500 crore ÷ 25 crore = ₹20 per unit. An investor putting in ₹50,000 that day receives 50,000 ÷ 20 = 2,500 units.

FAQs

Is a mutual fund with a lower NAV better to buy?

No. NAV only reflects the value of the portfolio divided by units. A ₹15 NAV fund and a ₹500 NAV fund give the same percentage return if their portfolios move the same way. Compare funds on portfolio, consistency of returns, risk, costs and fit with your goal, not on the NAV level.

When is mutual fund NAV declared?

For most schemes NAV is computed and published every business day after market close, on the AMC website and AMFI's site. Some schemes such as fund of funds investing overseas may publish a little later because underlying prices arrive later. Check the scheme documents for exact timelines.
