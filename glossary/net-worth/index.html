What is Net worth?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Net worth: Net worth is the total value of everything a person owns minus everything they owe, giving a snapshot of their overall financial position.

Assets include bank balances, fixed deposits, mutual funds, shares, EPF and PPF balances, NPS, gold, real estate at realistic market value, and the surrender value (not sum assured) of insurance policies. Liabilities include outstanding home loan, car loan, personal loans, education loans and credit card balances.

Planners often separate financial assets from lifestyle assets. A self-occupied house and a car add to net worth on paper but do not generate income or fund goals unless sold. Calculating 'investable net worth' excluding them gives a clearer view of what can support retirement.

Tracking net worth once or twice a year shows whether you are actually getting wealthier. A rising salary with a flat net worth means spending or debt is absorbing the gains. A falling net worth during a market dip is less worrying than one falling because of new borrowings.

Common mistakes: counting a term insurance sum assured as an asset; using the purchase price of property instead of a realistic sale value; ignoring small loans and card dues; and comparing net worth with friends rather than with your own goals.

Example

Rajiv, 36, lists assets: savings and FDs ₹4 lakh, mutual funds ₹11 lakh, EPF ₹9 lakh, PPF ₹3 lakh, gold ₹2 lakh and a flat worth ₹70 lakh, totalling ₹99 lakh. Liabilities: home loan ₹48 lakh, car loan ₹3 lakh and card dues ₹50,000, totalling ₹51.5 lakh. Net worth = ₹99 lakh − ₹51.5 lakh = ₹47.5 lakh. Excluding the flat and its loan, his investable net worth is ₹29 lakh − ₹3.5 lakh = ₹25.5 lakh.

FAQs

Should my house be included in net worth?

Include it for total net worth, at a realistic sale value, along with the home loan as a liability. For retirement and goal planning, many advisers also compute investable net worth excluding the home you live in, since it will not generate cash unless sold or rented.

How often should I calculate my net worth?

Once or twice a year is enough for most people. Tracking it monthly can lead to over-reacting to market movements. Compare against your own past figures and goals rather than with others.
