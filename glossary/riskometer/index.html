What is Riskometer?

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Riskometer: The riskometer is a SEBI-mandated dial shown on every mutual fund scheme's documents that rates the scheme's risk on a six-level scale from Low to Very High.

SEBI introduced the riskometer to give investors a quick visual sense of risk. Since January 2021 the scale has six levels: Low, Low to Moderate, Moderate, Moderately High, High and Very High. The level is calculated using a prescribed method that considers factors such as market capitalisation, volatility and liquidity for equity, and interest rate, credit and liquidity risk for debt.

The riskometer is reviewed every month based on the actual portfolio. If the level changes, the AMC must disclose it and inform unit holders. AMCs also show the riskometer of the scheme's benchmark, and must publish how many times the scheme's risk level changed during the year.

It appears on the SID, KIM, factsheet, advertisements and application forms. Distributors and advisers use it as one input when matching products with investor risk profiles.

Its limitation is coarseness. Almost all pure equity funds, from large cap to sectoral, show 'Very High', so the riskometer cannot tell you which of two equity funds is riskier. For debt funds, the separate potential risk class matrix gives a more precise view of maximum interest rate and credit risk.

Example

A short duration debt fund's riskometer shows 'Moderate' in January. In March, the fund manager adds lower-rated corporate bonds and lengthens duration. The monthly calculation moves the scheme to 'Moderately High'. The AMC updates its website and sends a communication to unit holders. Priya, whose risk profile is conservative, now sees that the scheme may no longer match her comfort level and reviews her holding.

FAQs

How many levels does the mutual fund riskometer have?

Six: Low, Low to Moderate, Moderate, Moderately High, High and Very High. The 'Very High' level was added in 2020–21 when SEBI revised the framework, so older documents may show only five levels.

Can a scheme's riskometer change?

Yes. The level is recalculated monthly based on the portfolio, so it can move up or down as holdings change. AMCs must disclose changes on their website and notify existing investors. Year-end disclosures show how often the level changed.
