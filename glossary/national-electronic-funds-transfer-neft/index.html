What is National Electronic Funds Transfer (NEFT)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

National Electronic Funds Transfer (NEFT): NEFT is a nationwide electronic payment system run by RBI that transfers funds between bank accounts in batches settled every half hour, round the clock.

NEFT was launched by RBI in November 2005. Since 16 December 2019 it operates 24x7, all 365 days, including holidays. Transactions are settled in half-hourly batches, 48 batches a day. The sender needs the beneficiary's name, account number and the branch IFSC (Indian Financial System Code), an 11-character code identifying the bank branch.

RBI has not set any minimum or maximum amount for NEFT, though banks may impose their own limits for online transfers. RBI has asked banks not to charge savings account customers for online NEFT transactions; charges for branch-initiated transfers are capped. Even people without a bank account can deposit cash at a branch to send up to ₹50,000 per transaction through NEFT, subject to KYC details.

If a beneficiary bank cannot credit the account, it must return the funds within two hours of the batch settlement. If credit is delayed, the bank must pay penal interest at the current RBI LAF repo rate plus 2% for the delay. Customers get an SMS or email once the credit is confirmed (positive confirmation).

NEFT is the go-to choice for most routine payments like rent or vendor payments. It differs from RTGS, which settles each transaction individually and in real time, and from IMPS and UPI, which are run by NPCI and credit almost instantly.

Example

Ravi sends ₹45,000 rent through NEFT at 10:12 pm on a Sunday. The transaction joins the 10:30 pm batch; the landlord's account is credited soon after settlement, typically within an hour. Had the beneficiary account number been wrong, the money would be returned within two hours of the batch. If credit were delayed by two days without reason, the bank would owe interest at repo + 2%; at 7.5%, that is 45,000 × 7.5% × 2/365 = about ₹18.

FAQs

Is there a minimum amount for NEFT?

No. RBI has not prescribed any minimum or maximum amount for NEFT transactions. You can send ₹1 or ₹1 crore. Banks may set their own per-day or per-transaction limits for internet and mobile banking based on risk, and cash-based remittances by walk-in customers are capped at ₹50,000 per transaction.

Does NEFT work on bank holidays?

Yes. Since December 2019 NEFT is available 24x7, including Sundays and bank holidays. Batches run every half hour around the clock. Earlier it worked only on working days during set hours, which is why some older textbooks mention fixed timings.
