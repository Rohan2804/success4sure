What is Initial Public Offering (IPO)?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Initial Public Offering (IPO): An initial public offering (IPO) is the first sale of a company's shares to the public, after which the shares are listed and traded on stock exchanges.

A company goes public to raise capital for expansion or debt repayment, to let early investors and promoters sell part of their stake, and to gain the visibility and liquidity of a listed share. An IPO can have a fresh issue (new shares, money to the company), an offer for sale (existing shares, money to sellers), or both.

The process is governed by the SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018. The company appoints merchant bankers (book running lead managers), files a draft red herring prospectus with SEBI, receives observations, and then opens the issue — typically for three working days. Most IPOs use book building, where investors bid within a price band.

Bids are divided into categories: qualified institutional buyers (QIBs), non-institutional investors (NIIs) and retail individual investors, plus sometimes employee and shareholder quotas. Retail investors bid up to ₹2 lakh. Payment is through ASBA, so money is only blocked, not debited, until allotment. Listing now happens on T+3 — three working days after the issue closes (as of 2026; check SEBI circulars).

Two misconceptions are common. First, oversubscription does not mean you will get shares; in retail, allotment is by lottery to give as many applicants as possible one minimum lot. Second, the IPO price is not a 'fair' value certified by SEBI — SEBI reviews disclosures, not the price, and listing-day prices can be above or below the issue price.

Example

A company offers shares in a price band of ₹270–₹285 with a lot size of 52 shares. A retail investor bids for 3 lots at the cut-off price, so the ASBA block is 3 × 52 × ₹285 = ₹44,460. The retail portion is subscribed 20 times. The investor is allotted one lot in the lottery at the final price of ₹285, so ₹14,820 is debited and the remaining ₹29,640 block is released.

FAQs

What is the minimum amount to apply in an IPO?

The minimum is one lot, and SEBI requires the minimum application value to fall within a prescribed range, so it is usually around ₹14,000–₹15,000 for mainboard IPOs. The exact amount depends on the lot size and price band printed in the RHP and issue advertisement. SME IPOs have much larger minimum lots.

What is the cut-off price in an IPO?

Retail and employee applicants can tick 'cut-off', meaning they agree to buy at whatever final price is fixed within the band. Their block is calculated at the upper end of the band. If the final price is lower, the extra amount is unblocked. Institutions and NIIs cannot bid at cut-off; they must specify a price.
