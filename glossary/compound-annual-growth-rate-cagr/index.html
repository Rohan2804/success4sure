What is Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR)?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR): Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) is the constant yearly rate at which an investment would have to grow to go from its starting value to its ending value over a period.

The formula is CAGR = (ending value ÷ beginning value)^(1 ÷ number of years) − 1. It smooths out year-to-year fluctuations into one annual figure, which makes it easy to compare investments held for different lengths of time. Mutual fund returns for periods over one year are typically shown as CAGR.

CAGR is different from the simple average of annual returns. If an investment rises 50% one year and falls 50% the next, the average return is 0%, but you have actually lost 25% (₹100 becomes ₹150, then ₹75). The CAGR is about −13.4% a year, which reflects reality.

CAGR suits a single lump sum with no further investments or withdrawals. For SIPs or irregular cash flows, CAGR is misleading because different amounts were invested for different periods; XIRR is the right tool there.

A CAGR figure also hides the path. Two funds with 12% CAGR over five years may have had very different volatility along the way. And CAGR for a short period, such as 1.5 years, can look extreme; be careful annualising returns for periods under a year.

Example

Shalini invested ₹1,00,000 in a fund in April 2019. In April 2025 it was worth ₹2,00,000. CAGR = (2,00,000 ÷ 1,00,000)^(1/6) − 1 = 2^(0.1667) − 1 ≈ 12.25% a year. The absolute return is 100%, but spreading it over six years gives the more comparable annual figure.

FAQs

What is the difference between CAGR and absolute return?

Absolute return is the total percentage gain over the whole period, regardless of length. CAGR converts it into an annual compounded rate. A 50% absolute return over 2 years is about 22.5% CAGR; over 5 years it is about 8.4% CAGR.

Can CAGR be used for SIP returns?

Not accurately. In a SIP, each instalment is invested for a different length of time, so a single start and end value does not represent the investment. XIRR accounts for the timing of each cash flow and is the standard measure for SIP returns.
