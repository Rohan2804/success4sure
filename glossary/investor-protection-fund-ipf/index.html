What is Investor Protection Fund (IPF)?

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Investor Protection Fund (IPF): An investor protection fund is a fund maintained by each stock exchange and depository to compensate investors for eligible claims when a member defaults.

Stock exchanges are required by SEBI to set up an investor protection fund (IPF), usually managed through a trust. Its main purpose is to pay valid claims of investors against a trading member who is declared a defaulter or expelled. Depositories maintain a similar fund for claims arising from DP defaults. The funds are financed by contributions from exchanges, fees and penalties.

When a broker is declared a defaulter, the exchange publishes notices inviting claims within a specified period. A committee examines each claim to verify that it relates to genuine trades on the exchange, settled or unsettled obligations, or securities and money held with the broker in the normal course of business. Approved claims are paid up to a per-investor limit set by the exchange in line with SEBI guidelines; the limit is revised from time to time, so check the exchange website.

The IPF does not cover losses from market movements, from trading on bad advice, or from transactions that were not on the exchange, such as private deals, assured return schemes or loans to the broker. Claims also need documentary support such as contract notes, ledger statements and bank records.

Students often confuse the IPF with the settlement guarantee fund. The Core SGF guarantees settlement between clearing members, protecting the system. The IPF compensates individual investors for losses caused by their broker's default. SEBI also maintains its own Investor Protection and Education Fund, which supports investor education and can be used for refunds in certain cases.

Example

A broker is declared a defaulter. Meera had a credit balance of ₹3.4 lakh in her ledger and 200 unpaid-for shares worth ₹1.1 lakh that the broker had not delivered after she paid. She files a claim with contract notes and bank statements within the claim period. The committee verifies both items, and since the ₹4.5 lakh total is within the exchange's per-investor limit, she is paid in full from the IPF.

FAQs

Does the investor protection fund cover trading losses?

No. The IPF covers genuine claims arising from a member's default, such as money or securities the broker owed the investor. It does not compensate for losses due to price movements, poor investment decisions, or dealings outside the exchange system such as assured-return schemes.

How long do I have to file a claim after a broker default?

Exchanges publish a notice inviting claims and specify a period for filing. SEBI's guidelines allow claims to be filed within a prescribed period from the date of the notice, with some flexibility for delayed claims in genuine cases. Check the exchange's defaulter notice for the exact deadline.
