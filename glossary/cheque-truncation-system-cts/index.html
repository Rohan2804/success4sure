What is Cheque Truncation System (CTS)?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Cheque Truncation System (CTS): Cheque Truncation System is an image-based cheque clearing system where the physical cheque stops at the collecting bank and only its electronic image and data travel to the paying bank.

'Truncation' means stopping the movement of the paper cheque somewhere along the clearing chain. Under CTS, the presenting bank scans the cheque and sends the image along with MICR data (the magnetic code at the bottom, including cheque number, bank and branch codes) to the clearing house, which forwards it to the drawee bank for payment. The 2002 amendment to the NI Act gave legal recognition to truncated cheques.

RBI piloted CTS in New Delhi in 2008, then rolled it out to Chennai and Mumbai, and by September 2021 brought all bank branches in the country under three grids. CTS is operated by NPCI. It removed the cost and delay of physically moving cheques, cut frauds involving tampering in transit and allowed faster credit.

To work with CTS, cheques must follow the CTS-2010 standard: specific paper with a watermark, a 'VOID' pantograph that appears on photocopies, a UV-visible bank logo, and fixed field positions. Changes on the cheque (except date) are not permitted. RBI's Positive Pay System, launched in 2021, lets the drawer pre-share details like date, amount and payee name, which the paying bank matches before paying high-value cheques.

RBI has moved CTS from batch clearing to continuous clearing with settlement on realisation, in phases from October 2025, aiming for same-day credit within hours. Check RBI for the latest timelines (as of 2026).

Example

Sneha deposits a ₹1,20,000 cheque drawn on an HDFC Bank branch into her SBI account. SBI scans it and sends the image plus MICR data to the CTS grid. HDFC Bank checks the image, signature and Positive Pay details that the drawer had entered earlier (amount ₹1,20,000, payee Sneha), finds a match, and confirms payment. Sneha's account is credited without the paper cheque ever leaving SBI.

FAQs

Why are corrections on a cheque not allowed under CTS?

In image-based clearing, the paying bank sees only a scan, so it cannot easily verify whether a change was made by the drawer or a fraudster. To reduce risk, RBI directed that no changes or corrections be made on CTS cheques, except to the date with the drawer's authentication. Any other correction requires a fresh cheque.

What is the Positive Pay System?

Positive Pay is a fraud-prevention mechanism for cheques. The drawer shares key details such as cheque number, date, amount and payee name with their bank through SMS, app or net banking. When the cheque is presented in CTS, these details are matched. Banks can make it mandatory for cheques of ₹5 lakh and above.
