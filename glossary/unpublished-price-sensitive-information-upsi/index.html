What is UPSI (Unpublished Price Sensitive Information)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

UPSI (Unpublished Price Sensitive Information): UPSI is information about a company or its securities that is not generally available and that, if made public, is likely to materially affect the share price.

The SEBI PIT Regulations give an illustrative list of UPSI: financial results, dividends, changes in capital structure, mergers, demergers, acquisitions, delistings, disposals and expansion of business, and changes in key managerial personnel. Later amendments added items such as fraud or defaults by the company, resolution plans and significant orders. Companies also judge other information on its likely price impact.

Information becomes 'generally available' when it is published in a way that gives investors non-discriminatory access — typically through disclosure to the stock exchanges. Media speculation does not make information public; a leak in a news report is still UPSI until the company formally discloses it.

Companies must control the flow of UPSI. They share it only on a 'need-to-know' basis, maintain a structured digital database recording who received UPSI and when, and use non-disclosure agreements with advisers. Chinese walls separate teams that handle UPSI from teams that trade or advise clients, especially in financial firms.

A common confusion is to treat all non-public information as UPSI. Routine operational details are not price sensitive. The test is whether the information would be likely to materially affect the price. Another misconception is that analysts' estimates are UPSI; analyses built from public information are not, but selective disclosure of non-public data to analysts can be.

Example

A listed cement company's board is negotiating to acquire a competitor for ₹4,500 crore. The deal team, legal advisers and two bankers know about it; each is entered in the structured digital database. A news portal reports rumours of 'a large acquisition'. Until the company informs the exchanges after board approval, details of the deal remain UPSI, and anyone in the database is barred from trading the shares.

FAQs

When does UPSI stop being unpublished?

When it becomes generally available, which usually means the company has disclosed it to the stock exchanges or through another method that gives the public equal access. Insiders should wait until the market has had a reasonable opportunity to absorb the information before trading, consistent with the company's code of conduct.

Can UPSI be shared with lenders or investors during a deal?

Yes, for legitimate purposes, such as due diligence for a transaction, provided confidentiality agreements are signed and the recipients are recorded in the structured digital database. Recipients then become insiders and cannot trade in the company's securities until the information becomes public.
