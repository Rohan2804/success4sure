What is External Benchmark Lending Rate (EBLR)?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

External Benchmark Lending Rate (EBLR): External Benchmark Lending Rate is a floating loan rate linked to an outside benchmark, such as RBI's repo rate or a treasury bill yield, plus a spread, instead of the bank's internal rate.

From 1 October 2019, RBI made it mandatory for banks to link all new floating-rate personal or retail loans (housing, auto, education, personal) and floating-rate loans to micro and small enterprises to an external benchmark. Banks may choose one of four: the RBI policy repo rate, the Government of India 3-month treasury bill yield, the 6-month treasury bill yield published by FBIL, or any other benchmark yield published by FBIL. Most banks chose the repo rate, so these loans are popularly called repo-linked lending rate (RLLR) loans.

Loan rate = External benchmark + Spread. The spread has two parts: the bank's operating cost and credit risk premium. The credit risk premium can change only if the borrower's credit profile changes substantially, as agreed in the loan contract. The interest rate must be reset at least once every three months.

The main benefit is transparency and faster transmission. When the MPC changes repo, every repo-linked loan reprices at its next reset, in both directions. The borrower can see exactly why the rate changed. In 2023 RBI also required banks to give borrowers the choice of increasing EMI, extending tenure, or both when rates rise, and to share clear information on the reset.

Exam point: EBLR is mandatory for new floating loans in the specified categories; MCLR continues for other loans and older accounts. NBFCs are not covered by the EBLR mandate.

Example

A bank links its home loans to repo. With repo at 5.50% and a spread of 2.60%, the loan rate is 8.10%. For a ₹50 lakh, 20-year loan, the EMI is about ₹42,134. If MPC cuts repo by 0.25%, the rate becomes 7.85% at the next quarterly reset, and the EMI on the same balance and remaining tenure falls to about ₹41,356, roughly ₹778 lower each month.

FAQs

Which external benchmarks can banks use?

RBI allows four: the policy repo rate, the 3-month Government of India treasury bill yield published by FBIL, the 6-month treasury bill yield published by FBIL, and any other benchmark market interest rate published by FBIL. A bank must use a uniform benchmark within a loan category for transparency.

How often does a repo-linked loan rate change?

The rate must be reset at least once every three months under RBI rules. Banks usually reset on the first day of the month or quarter after an MPC decision. Between resets the rate stays the same even if the repo rate changes, so the full effect appears only at the next reset date.
