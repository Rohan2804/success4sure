What is Auction and Short Delivery?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Auction and Short Delivery: Short delivery occurs when a seller fails to deliver shares on settlement day; the clearing corporation then buys them in an auction or closes out the trade in cash at the seller's cost.

Short delivery usually happens when someone sells shares they do not have — for example, selling in delivery mode by mistake, selling BTST shares whose purchase itself failed, or not authorising the debit of shares from their demat account in time. In India, retail investors are not allowed to short sell in the cash market overnight, so any undelivered quantity is treated as a delivery default.

The clearing corporation conducts an auction to buy the shortfall from other sellers in the market. The buyer receives the auctioned shares a day later than normal. The defaulting seller pays the difference if the auction price is higher than the original sale price, plus penalties prescribed by the exchange.

If no shares are offered in the auction, the trade is closed out in cash. The close-out price, under exchange rules, is generally the higher of the highest price traded from trade day to auction day and a set percentage (commonly 20%) above the closing price on the auction day — check the exchange's current circular. This can be very costly for the seller.

Buyers sometimes think short delivery means they have lost money. In fact, the buyer either receives the shares through the auction or receives cash at the close-out price, which is usually above the buy price. The buyer does lose the shares they intended to hold, which matters if a corporate action such as a dividend or bonus record date was close.

Example

Rahul sells 100 shares at ₹500 on Monday but delivers only 60 because 40 were pledged. On Tuesday, the clearing corporation auctions the 40 shares and buys them at ₹520. Rahul bears the ₹20 × 40 = ₹800 difference plus penalty. Had nobody offered shares and the close-out price been ₹612 (20% above a ₹510 closing price), Rahul's loss would be (₹612 − ₹500) × 40 = ₹4,480 plus penalty.

FAQs

Can a retail investor short sell shares in India?

Retail investors can short sell in the equity cash segment only intraday, closing the position the same day. Overnight short positions in the cash market lead to short delivery and auction. To hold a short position longer, investors use stock futures, put options or the securities lending and borrowing (SLB) mechanism.

Who receives the money if a trade is closed out?

The buyer whose shares were short-delivered receives the close-out amount, credited by the clearing corporation through their broker. This amount is usually higher than the original purchase price because of the close-out price formula, which is meant to compensate the buyer and discourage sellers from defaulting.
