What is Fiscal Policy?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Fiscal Policy: Fiscal policy is the government's use of taxation, public expenditure and borrowing to influence demand, growth, employment, prices and income distribution in the economy.

In India, the Union Government sets fiscal policy through the annual Union Budget presented by the Finance Minister under Article 112 of the Constitution, and state governments do the same through state budgets. Its tools are tax rates and structure (income tax, GST, customs), public spending (on infrastructure, subsidies, welfare schemes) and the size and form of borrowing.

Expansionary fiscal policy means cutting taxes or raising spending to boost demand, usually during a slowdown. It widens the fiscal deficit. Contractionary policy, raising taxes or cutting spending, cools an overheated economy and reduces the deficit. Automatic stabilisers, such as tax collections that fall in a recession, work even without new decisions.

Fiscal policy differs from monetary policy in who runs it and how it acts. Monetary policy is run by RBI through interest rates and liquidity and works broadly across the economy. Fiscal policy is run by the government and can target specific sectors, regions or groups, such as a capital expenditure push or a fertiliser subsidy. The two should ideally complement each other; a very loose fiscal policy can force RBI to keep rates higher to control inflation.

Fiscal discipline in India is guided by the FRBM Act, 2003. The multiplier effect explains why ₹1 of public spending can raise GDP by more than ₹1: the money becomes someone's income and is spent again.

Example

Suppose the government spends an extra ₹1 lakh crore on highways, and households spend 75% of any extra income (marginal propensity to consume 0.75). The simple multiplier is 1/(1 − 0.75) = 4, so total demand could rise by up to ₹4 lakh crore. In reality leakages such as taxes and imports lower the multiplier, so studies for India often find capital spending multipliers well below this textbook figure, though higher than for revenue spending.

FAQs

Who controls fiscal policy in India?

The Union Government through the Ministry of Finance controls central fiscal policy, approved by Parliament through the Budget and Finance Bill. State governments run their own fiscal policies through state legislatures. The GST Council, with both Centre and states, decides GST rates. RBI has no role in setting fiscal policy.

What is crowding out?

When the government borrows heavily, it competes with private firms for the same pool of savings. This can push up interest rates and reduce private investment. The effect is called crowding out and is one argument for keeping fiscal deficits under control, especially when the economy is already near full capacity.
