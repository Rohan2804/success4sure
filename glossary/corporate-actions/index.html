What is Corporate Actions?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Corporate Actions: Corporate actions are events initiated by a listed company that change its securities or deliver benefits to shareholders, such as dividends, bonus issues, splits, rights and buybacks.

Corporate actions fall into three broad groups. Mandatory actions apply to all shareholders automatically — cash dividends, bonus issues, stock splits, consolidations and mergers or demergers that allot new shares. Voluntary actions need the shareholder to choose — buyback tenders, open offers, rights issues. Mandatory-with-choice actions give a default with an option, such as some dividend reinvestment choices.

Each action goes through board approval, shareholder approval where required, an announcement to the exchanges and fixation of a record date. The registrar and transfer agent (RTA) prepares the entitlement list from depository data, and the depositories credit new securities to demat accounts. Cash benefits go to the bank account linked with the demat.

Exchanges and clearing corporations adjust prices, indices and derivative contracts after corporate actions so that positions remain fair. For example, after a 1:1 bonus, futures prices and option strikes are halved and lot sizes doubled. Index divisors are changed after mergers or spin-offs.

Investors often miss voluntary actions because they need active participation. Ignoring a rights issue or a buyback tender is a choice too, with consequences for value and ownership percentage. Keeping your demat contact details, bank account and email updated is the simplest way to avoid missing notices.

Example

In one year, an investor holding 200 shares of a company sees: a ₹12 dividend (₹2,400 credited to her bank), a 1:1 bonus (holding becomes 400 shares), and a demerger in which one share of a new company is given for every four held (100 shares of the new company credited). She also receives a buyback offer, which she must actively tender if she wants to participate — the other three happened automatically.

FAQs

Do corporate actions affect futures and options positions?

Yes. For bonus, split, rights and large special dividends, exchanges adjust strike prices, lot sizes or both so that the value of open positions remains the same before and after the event. Ordinary dividends usually do not lead to adjustment, which is why they are priced into futures.

Who handles corporate actions for a company?

The company's board decides on the action, and its registrar and transfer agent (RTA) handles the processing, coordinating with depositories, exchanges and banks. Investors resolve issues such as unpaid dividends or missing credits by contacting the RTA or the company's investor relations team.
