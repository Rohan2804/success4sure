What is New Fund Offer (NFO)?

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

New Fund Offer (NFO): A new fund offer (NFO) is the first subscription window of a newly launched mutual fund scheme, when units are usually issued at a face value of ₹10.

When an AMC launches a scheme, it files the scheme information document with SEBI and then opens an NFO. Under SEBI rules an NFO generally cannot stay open for more than 15 days. Units are allotted after the offer closes, and an open-ended scheme then reopens for continuous purchase and redemption at NAV within a few business days, as SEBI prescribes.

During the NFO, the price is usually ₹10 per unit because the scheme has no portfolio yet. This leads to the biggest myth: that an NFO at ₹10 is cheaper than an existing fund at ₹200. It is not. Your ₹1 lakh buys 10,000 units in the NFO or 500 units of the older fund, and both rise or fall with their portfolios. The face value tells you nothing about value.

Before investing in an NFO, ask whether it offers something your existing funds do not: a new index, a new category or a genuinely different strategy. An NFO has no track record for that scheme, so you rely on the AMC's process and the fund manager's record elsewhere. SEBI has also tightened rules on how quickly NFO money must be deployed; check current circulars.

Close-ended schemes, FMPs and ELSS launches also use NFOs. For close-ended funds the NFO is effectively the only chance to buy from the AMC.

Example

A new Nifty Midcap 150 index fund opens its NFO from 1 to 14 March at ₹10. Ananya invests ₹50,000 and receives 5,000 units. An existing midcap index fund tracking the same index has NAV ₹18.40, so ₹50,000 would buy 2,717.39 units. If the index rises 12%, both holdings become about ₹56,000 before costs, showing that the unit price at entry made no difference.

FAQs

Is investing in an NFO better than an existing fund?

Not inherently. NFOs often get heavy marketing, but the ₹10 price is not a discount. Prefer an NFO only if it offers exposure you genuinely cannot get through existing schemes. Otherwise an established fund with a visible track record and portfolio lets you judge what you are buying.

How long does an NFO stay open?

SEBI rules generally limit an NFO to a maximum of 15 days. Allotment follows within a set number of business days of closure, and open-ended schemes reopen for regular transactions soon after. Check the scheme documents for the exact dates of a particular NFO.
