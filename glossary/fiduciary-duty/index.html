What is Fiduciary duty?

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Fiduciary duty: Fiduciary duty is the legal and ethical obligation of an adviser to act in the client's best interest, placing the client's interests above their own.

A fiduciary relationship arises when one party trusts another to act on its behalf. SEBI's Investment Advisers Regulations require registered advisers to act in a fiduciary capacity towards clients. This means recommendations must be driven by the client's needs, not by what earns the adviser more.

In practice, fiduciary duty includes: giving suitable advice based on proper risk profiling; disclosing all conflicts of interest, including any relationship with product providers; not receiving commissions or other benefits from products recommended; maintaining confidentiality; charging fees transparently and within limits; and keeping records of advice and its rationale.

The duty is stronger than the 'suitability' standard sometimes applied to distributors. A distributor must sell suitable products, but may legitimately earn more from one product than another. A fiduciary must choose the better option for the client even if it earns nothing extra, such as recommending a direct plan or advising a client to prepay a loan rather than invest.

Fiduciary duty does not mean guaranteeing outcomes. An adviser who follows a sound process can still see a recommended portfolio fall in a bad market without breaching duty.

Example

A client asks her RIA whether to invest ₹10 lakh or prepay part of a home loan at 9.5%. The RIA could charge a higher fee on a larger portfolio under advice, but analysis shows prepayment gives a certain post-tax saving close to 9.5%, while a conservative portfolio might earn less after tax. Acting as a fiduciary, the RIA recommends prepaying ₹6 lakh and investing ₹4 lakh for liquidity and goals, and documents the reasoning.

FAQs

Are mutual fund distributors fiduciaries?

Distributors are expected to follow AMFI's code of conduct and recommend suitable products, but they are paid by AMCs through commissions. SEBI's Investment Advisers Regulations, which impose a fiduciary obligation, apply to registered advisers who are paid by clients. The standards are different.

What happens if an adviser breaches fiduciary duty?

Clients can complain to the adviser, then through SEBI's SCORES platform and the online dispute resolution mechanism. SEBI can take regulatory action, including penalties or cancellation of registration. Documentation of advice is important for both sides.
