What is Special Mention Account (SMA)?

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Special Mention Account (SMA): A Special Mention Account is a loan that is still standard but shows early signs of stress, classified by how many days payments are overdue before it becomes an NPA.

RBI introduced SMA categories in 2014 under its framework for early recognition of financial distress, and refined them in the 2019 Prudential Framework for Resolution of Stressed Assets. For loans other than revolving facilities, the buckets are: SMA-0 when principal or interest is overdue for 1 to 30 days; SMA-1 for more than 30 and up to 60 days; and SMA-2 for more than 60 and up to 90 days. Beyond 90 days the account becomes an NPA.

For cash credit and overdraft accounts, the SMA-1 and SMA-2 buckets are based on how long the account has been continuously out of order: more than 30 up to 60 days for SMA-1, and more than 60 up to 90 days for SMA-2.

Banks report SMA status of borrowers with aggregate exposure of ₹5 crore and above to RBI's Central Repository of Information on Large Credits (CRILC). Under the 2019 framework, lenders must review a borrower's account within 30 days of default and, for large exposures, may sign an inter-creditor agreement and implement a resolution plan within 180 days, failing which additional provisions apply.

SMA status also matters to borrowers: it is reported to credit bureaus and can affect credit scores. A common error is treating SMA as an NPA category; it is still a standard asset, so interest is booked normally and only the standard-asset provision applies.

Example

A borrower's EMI due on 5 January is unpaid. On 6 January the account is SMA-0. On 5 February it is still overdue (31 days), so it moves to SMA-1. On 7 March, overdue 61 days, it becomes SMA-2. If nothing is paid and the overdue period reaches 91 days on 6 April, it slips into NPA. Paying the January EMI on 10 February but missing February's EMI keeps the oldest overdue at the February date, so the account stays in SMA.

FAQs

Is an SMA account an NPA?

No. An SMA is still a standard asset. The classification is an early warning so lenders can act before the account turns bad. Interest continues to be recognised as income, and only standard-asset provisioning applies, although banks may watch the account more closely or start a resolution process.

Does SMA status affect my credit score?

Yes. Banks report overdue days to credit information companies like CIBIL, and repeated SMA status signals payment stress. Even short delays of a few days show up as days past due. Paying dues before they are overdue is the simplest way to keep the record clean.
