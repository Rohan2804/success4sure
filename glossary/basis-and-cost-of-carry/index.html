What is Basis and Cost of Carry?

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Basis and Cost of Carry: Cost of carry is the net cost of holding an asset until a futures contract's expiry; basis is the difference between the futures price and the spot price.

The cost-of-carry model says a fair futures price equals the spot price plus the cost of financing it until expiry, minus any income the asset generates. For a stock or index, the main cost is interest and the main income is dividends: F = S × (1 + r × t) − D, in simple-interest form, where r is the annual rate and t the fraction of a year. For commodities, storage and insurance costs are added.

Basis is commonly defined in equity derivatives texts as futures price minus spot price; some commodity texts use spot minus futures. Whichever convention is used, basis is not constant: it shrinks as expiry nears because the financing period gets shorter. On expiry, the futures price converges to the spot price and basis becomes zero.

If futures trade well above the fair value, arbitrageurs buy in the cash market and sell futures (cash-and-carry arbitrage). If futures trade below fair value, they do the reverse. This activity keeps futures close to the cost-of-carry price in liquid contracts.

Many traders assume futures above spot means the market expects a rise. Usually it simply reflects interest cost. Conversely, a futures price below spot can occur when a large dividend is due before expiry, not necessarily because of bearish sentiment.

Example

A stock is at ₹2,000 and the risk-free rate is 7% a year. For a 30-day future with no dividend, fair price ≈ 2,000 × (1 + 0.07 × 30/365) = ₹2,011.51, so basis is about ₹11.51. If a ₹15 dividend is due before expiry, fair price ≈ ₹1,996.51 — below spot. With 5 days left (no dividend), fair price ≈ ₹2,001.92; on expiry, futures equals spot.

FAQs

Why do futures usually trade above spot?

Because buying the asset today requires money that could otherwise earn interest. A futures buyer avoids this financing cost, so the futures price includes it. When the interest cost exceeds income from the asset, such as dividends, futures trade above spot. This is called a positive cost of carry.

What is convergence in futures?

Convergence means the futures price moves towards the spot price as expiry approaches, because the cost of carry shrinks with time. On the final settlement day, the futures price equals the spot price. Arbitrageurs rely on convergence to lock in profits when the basis is mispriced.
