What is Assets Under Management (AUM)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Assets Under Management (AUM): Assets under management (AUM) is the total market value of all investments a mutual fund scheme or an AMC manages on behalf of investors at a point in time.

A scheme's AUM equals its NAV multiplied by the number of units outstanding. It changes for two reasons: net inflows (purchases minus redemptions) and market movement of the holdings. A fund whose AUM grew 30% in a year may have done so mainly through a rising market rather than investor interest.

AMCs report AUM monthly, and AMFI publishes industry-wide figures. Quarterly average AUM (AAUM) is also disclosed and is often used for ranking AMCs because month-end figures can be distorted by large temporary flows, especially in liquid funds.

AUM matters for costs. SEBI's TER slabs fall as AUM rises, so very large schemes have lower maximum expense ratios. Size can also bring operational efficiency.

But bigger is not automatically better. A very large small cap fund may struggle to deploy money into illiquid stocks without moving prices, and may have to restrict inflows. A very small fund may face concentration of investors, where a few large redemptions disturb the portfolio. Check AUM alongside category, strategy and investor concentration rather than treating it as a quality score.

Example

A flexi cap scheme starts the year with 50 crore units at NAV ₹40, so AUM is ₹2,000 crore. During the year investors add net 5 crore units, and NAV rises to ₹46. Year-end AUM = 55 crore × ₹46 = ₹2,530 crore. Of the ₹530 crore increase, roughly ₹300 crore came from market gains (50 crore units × ₹6) and the rest from net inflows plus gains on new money.

FAQs

Does a higher AUM mean a better mutual fund?

Not necessarily. High AUM shows popularity and can lower costs, but it does not guarantee good returns. In mid and small cap categories, very large size can make it harder to buy and sell stocks efficiently. Evaluate strategy, consistency, risk and cost too.

What is the difference between AUM and AAUM?

AUM is the value on a specific date, usually month-end. AAUM is the average of daily AUM over a period, typically a quarter. AAUM smooths out temporary spikes, such as corporate money parked in liquid funds for a few days, so it gives a fairer picture of an AMC's size.
