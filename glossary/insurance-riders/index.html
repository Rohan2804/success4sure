What is Insurance Riders?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Insurance Riders: A rider is an optional add-on benefit attached to a base insurance policy, for an extra premium, that provides cover beyond what the main policy offers.

Riders cannot be bought alone; they ride on a base policy. Common life insurance riders include: Accidental Death Benefit (ADB), paying an extra sum if death is due to an accident; Accidental Total and Permanent Disability, paying if an accident leaves the insured unable to work; Critical Illness, paying a lump sum on diagnosis of listed illnesses like cancer, heart attack or stroke; Waiver of Premium, under which future premiums are waived if the policyholder dies or becomes disabled; Term rider, adding extra life cover; and Hospital Cash.

Riders are cheaper than buying separate policies because the insurer saves on administration and underwriting. But they end when the base policy ends or lapses, and their benefits are usually more limited than standalone covers. IRDAI rules cap rider benefits and premiums: the rider sum assured generally cannot exceed the base sum assured, and there are limits on rider premium as a share of the base premium. Check IRDAI product regulations for current limits (as of 2026).

Under IRDAI rules, rider terms must be disclosed clearly and the policyholder can generally add or remove certain riders at policy anniversaries, depending on product terms. Rider claims are assessed separately: an accidental death claim requires proof that death was caused by an accident, not illness.

A frequent misunderstanding: a waiver of premium rider does not pay money to the family; it keeps the policy going by paying future premiums. Another is assuming an ADB rider covers death from any cause; it covers only accidental death within a time window after the accident.

Example

Sanjay buys a ₹50 lakh term plan with a ₹25 lakh ADB rider and a ₹10 lakh critical illness rider. If he is diagnosed with a listed cancer in year 5, the CI rider pays ₹10 lakh and the term cover continues. If he later dies in a road accident, the nominee gets ₹50 lakh + ₹25 lakh = ₹75 lakh. If he died of an illness instead, the nominee would get only the base ₹50 lakh.

FAQs

Can a rider be bought without a base policy?

No. A rider is an add-on that can only be attached to a base life or health insurance policy. If you want standalone protection, you can buy a separate personal accident or critical illness policy instead. When the base policy lapses, matures or is surrendered, the attached riders also end.

What is a waiver of premium rider?

It is a rider under which the insurer pays all future premiums of the base policy if the policyholder dies or suffers a specified disability or critical illness, depending on the variant. It is common in child plans where the parent is the proposer, so the child's policy continues even if the parent is no longer there.
