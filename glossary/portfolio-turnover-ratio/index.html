What is Portfolio turnover ratio?

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Portfolio turnover ratio: Portfolio turnover ratio measures how much of a mutual fund's portfolio is bought and sold during a period, usually a year, as a percentage of average assets.

The common formula takes the lower of total purchases or total sales during the year and divides it by average net assets. Using the lower figure excludes trades that merely invest new money or meet redemptions. AMCs disclose turnover in factsheets, often for equity and debt portions separately.

A turnover of 25% means roughly a quarter of the portfolio was replaced during the year, implying an average holding period of about four years. A turnover of 200% implies the manager replaces holdings about every six months.

High turnover means higher trading costs: brokerage, taxes such as STT on sales, and market impact. These costs are borne by the scheme and reduce NAV, though not all of them show up in the expense ratio. Low turnover usually indicates a buy-and-hold style.

Turnover is not good or bad by itself. Arbitrage funds have very high turnover by design, and index funds have low turnover except during index rebalancing. Compare a fund's turnover with others in its category and with what its stated strategy implies. A 'long-term quality' fund with 150% turnover is worth questioning.

Example

A mid cap fund has average AUM of ₹1,000 crore during a year. It bought securities worth ₹300 crore and sold ₹250 crore. Portfolio turnover = lower of ₹300 crore and ₹250 crore ÷ ₹1,000 crore = 25%. The implied average holding period is about 1 ÷ 0.25 = 4 years. A peer fund with ₹800 crore of sales on similar AUM would have 80% turnover.

FAQs

Is high portfolio turnover bad for investors?

It raises transaction costs, which reduce returns, so high turnover needs to be justified by better stock selection. It is expected in arbitrage and some tactical funds. For long-term diversified equity funds, persistently high turnover can be a warning sign.

Where can I find a fund's portfolio turnover ratio?

AMCs publish it in the monthly factsheet for each scheme, usually near the portfolio statistics such as standard deviation and beta. Some research platforms also show it. Compare it over several periods rather than a single month.
