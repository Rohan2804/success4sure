What is Flexi cap fund?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Flexi cap fund: A flexi cap fund is an equity mutual fund that must invest at least 65% in equities but can allocate freely across large, mid and small cap companies.

SEBI introduced the flexi cap category in November 2020, after it tightened the rules for multi cap funds. Many funds that had been running as multi cap with mostly large cap holdings shifted to flexi cap to retain their freedom. The fund manager decides how much to put in each market cap segment and can change the mix as opportunities change.

This flexibility is the category's strength and its risk. A skilled manager can tilt toward mid and small caps when they are cheap and back to large caps when they are expensive. A poor call, or style drift, can hurt returns. Investors should check the factsheet periodically to see actual allocation.

Flexi cap funds are often used as a core holding because a single fund gives diversified exposure across the market. They are benchmarked against broad indices such as Nifty 500 TRI or BSE 500 TRI.

Do not confuse flexi cap with multi cap. Multi cap funds must hold at least 25% each in large, mid and small caps; flexi cap funds have no such minimums. Two flexi cap funds can therefore have very different risk profiles.

Example

On ₹10,000 crore of AUM, Flexi Cap Fund A holds 75% large cap, 18% mid cap, 4% small cap and 3% cash. Flexi Cap Fund B, same category, holds 45% large cap, 30% mid cap and 23% small cap. In a year when small caps fall 25% and large caps fall 8%, Fund B's mix would typically fall noticeably more than Fund A's, even though both carry the same category label.

FAQs

What is the difference between flexi cap and multi cap funds?

A multi cap fund must invest at least 25% each in large, mid and small cap stocks, so it always has meaningful small cap exposure. A flexi cap fund only needs 65% in equity overall and can choose any mix across market caps. Flexi cap gives the manager more discretion.

Is a flexi cap fund good as a single core fund?

Many investors use one as a core equity holding because it covers the whole market and lets the manager adjust allocation. Whether it suits you depends on your goals, risk profile and other holdings. Check the fund's actual cap allocation and consistency rather than relying on the category name.
