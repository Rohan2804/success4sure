What is Suitability?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Suitability: Suitability is the obligation to recommend only those investment products that match a client's risk profile, financial situation, goals and understanding.

Under SEBI's Investment Advisers Regulations, an adviser must ensure that every recommendation is suitable for the client based on their risk profile, investment objectives, time horizon and financial position, and must document the reasons. The principle also runs through distributor codes of conduct and NISM curricula.

Suitability is product-level matching. A conservative 65-year-old needing monthly income from her savings should not be advised into a sectoral equity fund or a complex structured product, however attractive the projected return. A 25-year-old saving for retirement may be poorly served by keeping everything in fixed deposits.

The process has three parts: know the client (profile, goals, constraints), know the product (risk, liquidity, costs, complexity), and match the two with written reasoning. Where a client insists on an unsuitable product, advisers should record the warning and the client's decision.

Mis-selling is the opposite of suitability. Typical examples include selling long-term insurance-linked savings to someone needing liquidity, recommending a credit risk fund as a 'safe FD alternative', or pushing a new fund offer for commission. Suitability is judged against the client's needs, not the product's popularity or past returns.

Example

A 70-year-old widow with ₹30 lakh in fixed deposits and no other income asks a bank relationship manager about higher returns. The RM suggests a small cap fund that returned 40% last year. That is unsuitable: she needs income and capital stability, has low capacity to absorb a 40% fall, and limited time to recover. A suitable approach might include senior citizen savings options, a short duration debt fund and a modest conservative hybrid allocation, documented with reasons.

FAQs

What is the difference between suitability and appropriateness?

Suitability considers the client's full financial situation and goals before a recommendation. Appropriateness, a narrower test used in some markets, checks only whether the client understands the product's risks. In Indian advisory regulation, suitability is the broader standard expected of registered advisers.

What should an adviser do if a client wants an unsuitable product?

Explain clearly why the product does not fit, in writing, and record the client's decision if they still insist. The adviser should not present it as their recommendation. SEBI rules focus on what the adviser recommends and documents.
