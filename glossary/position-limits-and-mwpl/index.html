What is Position Limits and MWPL (F&O Ban)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Position Limits and MWPL (F&O Ban): Position limits cap how many derivative contracts a client, broker or the whole market can hold in an underlying; MWPL is the market-wide limit for a stock's derivatives.

Position limits prevent any single participant from controlling an outsized share of a contract and reduce the risk of manipulation and settlement stress. Exchanges set limits at three levels: the market as a whole (market-wide position limit, MWPL), trading or clearing members, and individual clients. Index derivatives have separate limits based on gross open positions.

For individual stocks, the MWPL is based on the stock's free float and trading activity. When aggregate open interest across all participants exceeds 95% of the MWPL, the stock enters a ban period. During the ban, traders can only reduce existing positions; any increase attracts penalties. The ban lifts when open interest falls below a lower threshold, traditionally 80% of MWPL.

SEBI revised the methodology in 2025, including the way open interest is measured for options (moving towards delta-based or future-equivalent measures) and the way MWPL is computed. Exact formulas and thresholds should be checked in the latest SEBI and exchange circulars.

Traders sometimes believe a ban means no trading in the stock at all. The cash market continues normally, and existing F&O positions can be closed. The ban only stops fresh positions that would increase open interest.

Example

A stock's MWPL is 20 crore shares. Its open interest across futures and options reaches 19.1 crore — 95.5% of MWPL — so it enters the ban from the next trading day. A trader who is short 3 lots may buy them back, but cannot add a fourth. Over a few sessions, positions are unwound and OI drops to 15.8 crore (79%), so the stock exits the ban.

FAQs

What happens if I increase my position during an F&O ban?

Exchanges levy a penalty on clients and brokers who increase open positions in a banned stock, and brokers usually block such orders. The penalty is calculated on the increase in position value. Closing or reducing existing positions is permitted throughout the ban period.

Do position limits apply to index derivatives?

Yes, but differently. Index derivatives are not subject to stock-style ban periods. Instead, exchanges set position limits for trading members, foreign portfolio investors, mutual funds and clients, often linked to total open interest or fixed rupee amounts. SEBI also introduced intraday monitoring of index derivatives positions.
