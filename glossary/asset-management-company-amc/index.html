What is Asset Management Company (AMC)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Asset Management Company (AMC): An asset management company (AMC) is the SEBI-approved company that launches mutual fund schemes and manages their money as investment manager, under supervision of the fund's trustees.

In India a mutual fund is set up as a trust. The sponsor creates the trust, the trustees hold the assets for unit holders, and the trustees appoint an asset management company to run the schemes day to day. The AMC designs products, files scheme documents with SEBI, employs fund managers and research analysts, buys and sells securities, calculates NAV and handles investor service through registrars.

The AMC is paid out of the scheme's total expense ratio. That fee is charged as a percentage of assets under management, so an AMC earns more as AUM grows, whether from new money or market gains. It does not own the investors' money: securities sit with a separate custodian in the scheme's name, which is why a problem with an AMC's own finances does not directly put scheme assets at risk.

Every AMC must be approved by SEBI, maintain a minimum net worth, have independent directors on its board, and follow the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations. Well-known Indian AMCs include those run by SBI, HDFC, ICICI Prudential and Nippon India.

Learners often mix up the AMC with the mutual fund itself. 'SBI Mutual Fund' is the trust; 'SBI Funds Management Ltd' is the AMC. Exam questions on the three-tier structure test exactly this distinction.

Example

Suppose an AMC manages a flexi cap scheme with AUM of ₹8,000 crore and the scheme's expense ratio is 1.6% a year. Total expenses charged to the scheme are about ₹128 crore annually (₹8,000 crore × 1.6%), accrued daily into the NAV. Part of this covers distributor commission, registrar and custody costs; the remainder is the AMC's management fee. If AUM rises to ₹10,000 crore through market gains, the same percentage brings in more fee income.

FAQs

Can an AMC run other businesses besides mutual funds?

SEBI allows AMCs to undertake certain other activities, such as portfolio management or advisory services to some categories of clients, only if there is no conflict of interest and they keep proper segregation. The exact list of permitted activities is set by SEBI regulations, so check the current rules before relying on a specific case.

What happens to my investment if an AMC shuts down?

Scheme assets are held by a custodian in the name of the scheme, not on the AMC's balance sheet. If an AMC exits, the trustees can transfer management of the schemes to another AMC or wind them up and return money at NAV, as per SEBI rules. Market losses in the portfolio, however, remain the investor's risk.
