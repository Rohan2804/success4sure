What is ISIN (International Securities Identification Number)?

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

ISIN (International Securities Identification Number): An ISIN is a unique 12-character alphanumeric code that identifies a specific security, such as a share, bond or mutual fund unit, across markets and depositories.

ISIN follows the international standard ISO 6166. In India, NSDL allots ISINs for equity, debt, government securities and mutual fund units (with CDSL also using them), so every holding in your demat account carries one. Exchanges, depositories, registrars and custodians use the ISIN rather than the company name, because names change and similar names can cause errors.

The structure is fixed: two letters for the country (IN for India), nine characters that identify the issuer and the type of security, and a final check digit calculated from the other characters to catch typing mistakes. For Indian equities, the third character is usually 'E' and many equity ISINs end with a segment showing the share type, while debt, government securities and mutual fund units follow their own patterns.

The important point is that an ISIN identifies a particular security, not a company. One company can have many ISINs: its equity shares, partly paid shares, each bond series and each type of debenture get separate codes. Similarly, each plan and option of a mutual fund scheme has its own ISIN.

ISINs can change after certain corporate actions, for example when partly paid shares become fully paid, or after a face-value change in some cases. Investors usually notice this when a holding temporarily appears twice or under a new code in their statement.

Example

A company has equity shares with ISIN beginning INE and also two non-convertible debenture series. In the investor's demat statement, the equity holding of 100 shares and the 50 debentures show under three different ISINs, even though all carry the same company name. When the investor gives an off-market transfer instruction for 20 equity shares, the DP asks for the equity ISIN — quoting a debenture ISIN would move the wrong security.

FAQs

Is ISIN the same as the stock symbol?

No. A symbol like a short ticker is an exchange-specific trading code that can differ between NSE and BSE (BSE also uses numeric scrip codes). The ISIN is the same everywhere and is used for settlement and depository records. In exams, remember: symbol for trading screens, ISIN for depository identification.

Where can I find the ISIN of a security?

Your demat holding statement and contract notes show it. You can also search the NSDL ISIN database, exchange websites, the company's investor relations page or a mutual fund's factsheet. Always match the ISIN when transferring securities or filling corporate action forms.
