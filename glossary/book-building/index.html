What is Book Building?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Book Building: Book building is a price-discovery method in public issues where investors bid within a price band and the final issue price is set based on the demand recorded at each price.

In a fixed price issue, the company announces one price in advance and investors simply apply. In book building, the company announces a price band — the upper end can be at most 120% of the lower end (floor), and in practice bands are much narrower. Investors bid the quantity and price they are willing to pay, and all bids are recorded in an electronic 'book' maintained by the book running lead managers.

When bidding closes, the lead managers and the company study the demand curve: how many shares are demanded at each price. The final issue price is usually set at the highest price at which the entire issue can be sold, often the top of the band when demand is strong. Bids below the final price are rejected; bids at or above it are eligible for allotment at the final price.

SEBI rules reserve portions for different categories. In a typical book-built IPO by a company meeting the profitability track-record test, not more than 50% goes to QIBs, at least 35% to retail investors and at least 15% to non-institutional investors. Companies that do not meet the profitability test must allot at least 75% to QIBs, and the retail share falls to at most 10%.

Book building is preferred because it reflects actual demand rather than a guess by the issuer. Its weakness is that institutional demand can strongly influence the price, and a high subscription number may simply reflect leveraged or speculative applications.

Example

An issue of 1,00,00,000 shares has a band of ₹100–₹108. Bids received: 40 lakh shares at ₹108, 50 lakh more at ₹106 and above, and 30 lakh more at ₹100–₹105. Cumulative demand at ₹106 or higher is 90 lakh — short of 1 crore. At ₹105 and above, demand might reach 1.05 crore, so the issue can be fully sold at ₹105. Bids below ₹105 are rejected and everyone allotted pays ₹105.

FAQs

What is the difference between a fixed price issue and book building?

In a fixed price issue the price is known when you apply, and demand is disclosed only after closing. In book building the price band is known, the final price is decided after bidding, and demand is visible during the bidding period. Most large Indian IPOs today use book building.

Who maintains the book in book building?

The book running lead managers (merchant bankers) appointed by the issuer manage the process. Bids are entered by syndicate members, brokers, DPs, registrars and banks into the exchanges' electronic bidding platforms, which provide a consolidated demand picture. The final price is decided by the issuer in consultation with the lead managers.
