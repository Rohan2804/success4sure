What is Standard deduction?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Standard deduction: Standard deduction is a flat amount that salaried employees and pensioners can subtract from their salary or pension income before tax, without showing any proof of expenses.

Standard deduction was reintroduced in 2018 in place of separate transport and medical allowances. It is automatic: employers factor it into TDS, and taxpayers claim it in their return without bills or investments. Family pension recipients have a separate, smaller deduction.

As of 2026, the standard deduction is ₹75,000 under the new tax regime and ₹50,000 under the old regime. The new-regime figure was raised in the July 2024 Budget. Under the new regime, combined with the rebate, salaried individuals with income up to about ₹12.75 lakh pay no income tax for FY 2025-26, subject to conditions.

Standard deduction applies only to income under the head 'salaries' (including pension). Freelancers and business owners do not get it; they deduct actual business expenses instead. The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026 and renumbers sections, so the familiar section name may now map to a new reference; check the income tax department's current guidance.

A common error is thinking standard deduction is a tax saving of ₹75,000. It reduces taxable income; the actual tax saved depends on your slab. At a 10% marginal rate, ₹75,000 of deduction saves about ₹7,500 plus cess.

Example

Priya earns a salary of ₹12,75,000 in FY 2025-26 under the new regime with no other income. After the ₹75,000 standard deduction, taxable income is ₹12,00,000. As of 2026 the tax on this, after the rebate available up to ₹12 lakh, is nil. Her colleague earning ₹13,00,000 has taxable income of ₹12,25,000, above the rebate threshold, and pays some tax subject to marginal relief. (Check current rules.)

FAQs

Do I need to submit proof for standard deduction?

No. It is a flat deduction given automatically to salaried employees and pensioners. Your employer applies it while calculating TDS, and you claim it in the return without documents.

Is standard deduction available in both tax regimes?

Yes. As of 2026 it is ₹75,000 under the new regime and ₹50,000 under the old regime for salaried individuals and pensioners. Limits are revised in budgets from time to time, so confirm the current figures.
