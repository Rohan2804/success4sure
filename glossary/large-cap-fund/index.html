What is Large cap fund?

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Large cap fund: A large cap fund is an equity mutual fund that must invest at least 80% of its assets in large cap companies, defined as the 100 largest listed companies by full market capitalisation.

SEBI's 2017 categorisation fixed precise definitions of large, mid and small cap. AMFI publishes a list every six months ranking all listed companies by average full market capitalisation. Ranks 1 to 100 are large cap, 101 to 250 mid cap, and 251 onward small cap. Large cap funds can keep up to 20% in other stocks or debt.

Large companies are typically market leaders with diversified businesses and better access to capital, such as major banks, IT firms and consumer goods companies. Their share prices tend to be less volatile than smaller companies, and the stocks are highly liquid, so funds can buy and sell large amounts without moving prices much.

The universe is narrow and widely researched, so it is harder for active managers to beat the benchmark after costs. That is why many investors compare a large cap fund with a Nifty 50 or Nifty 100 index fund before choosing.

'Large cap' does not mean safe. In 2008 and early 2020, large cap funds fell 30–50% from peak to trough. They are less volatile than mid and small cap funds, but still carry full equity market risk.

Example

A large cap fund with AUM of ₹20,000 crore must hold at least ₹16,000 crore in stocks ranked 1–100 on AMFI's list. Suppose a stock it owns slips from rank 98 to rank 104 in the next half-yearly list. The fund does not need to sell immediately; SEBI allows a rebalancing window after the list is published so that the portfolio returns within limits.

FAQs

How is a large cap company defined in India?

SEBI defines large cap as the 1st to 100th company by full market capitalisation. AMFI publishes the ranked list twice a year, in January and July, based on average market cap over the preceding six months. Funds use this list to classify holdings.

Is a large cap fund suitable for beginners?

Large cap funds are often seen as a starting point for equity investing because they are less volatile than mid and small cap funds. But they can still fall sharply in market downturns. Suitability depends on time horizon and risk profile, so a holding period of five years or more is commonly suggested.
