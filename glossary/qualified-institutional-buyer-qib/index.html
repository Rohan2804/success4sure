What is Qualified Institutional Buyer (QIB)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Qualified Institutional Buyer (QIB): A qualified institutional buyer (QIB) is a large, sophisticated institutional investor recognised by SEBI's ICDR Regulations as eligible for the QIB category in issues.

The QIB definition lists categories of institutions presumed to have the expertise and resources to evaluate issues. They include mutual funds, foreign portfolio investors (excluding individuals, corporate bodies and family offices), scheduled commercial banks, insurance companies, public financial institutions, multilateral and bilateral development institutions, alternative investment funds and venture capital funds, and pension and provident funds above a minimum corpus size.

QIBs get a reserved portion in book-built IPOs — up to 50% for companies with a profitability track record, and at least 75% for those without. Within the QIB portion, 5% (excluding the anchor part) is set aside for mutual funds. QIBs must bid with a price, cannot bid at cut-off, and cannot withdraw or lower their bids once placed.

QIB status also matters for other routes. A qualified institutions placement (QIP) can be made only to QIBs, and certain debt issues and institutional trading platforms are restricted to them. Allotment to QIBs in an IPO is proportionate, unlike the lottery used for retail investors.

The QIB subscription figure is closely tracked because institutions do detailed research. But it is not a quality certificate; high QIB demand can reflect index inclusion needs or allocation strategy rather than a judgement on value. And an individual, however wealthy, cannot be a QIB — high-net-worth individuals fall in the non-institutional category.

Example

An IPO of ₹1,000 crore by a profitable company reserves ₹500 crore for QIBs, ₹150 crore for NIIs and ₹350 crore for retail. If ₹300 crore of the QIB portion goes to anchors, ₹200 crore remains, of which 5% (₹10 crore) is reserved for mutual funds. If QIBs bid for ₹9,000 crore against ₹200 crore, the QIB portion is subscribed 45 times and each QIB gets roughly 1/45th of its bid.

FAQs

Is an HNI a QIB?

No. High-net-worth individuals and most companies bidding above ₹2 lakh fall in the non-institutional investor (NII) category. QIB status depends on the type of institution as listed in SEBI's ICDR Regulations, not on wealth. Some family offices and corporate bodies registered as FPIs are also specifically excluded.

Why can't QIBs withdraw bids in an IPO?

SEBI barred QIB and NII withdrawals to stop large investors from creating an artificial impression of demand early in the issue and then pulling out. Because others may rely on the subscription data, institutional bids must stay firm. Retail investors, by contrast, can modify or cancel bids during the bidding period.
