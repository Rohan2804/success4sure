What is Circuit Breaker (Market-wide)?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Circuit Breaker (Market-wide): A market-wide circuit breaker is an automatic trading halt across all equity and equity-derivative markets when the Sensex or Nifty moves by 10%, 15% or 20% in a day.

SEBI's index-based circuit breaker applies to all exchanges together. It is triggered by the movement of either the BSE Sensex or the NSE Nifty 50, whichever breaches the threshold first, and applies to both upward and downward moves. The point levels are calculated from the index's previous closing value.

The halt duration depends on the threshold and the time of the day. For a 10% move before 1:00 p.m., trading halts for 45 minutes; between 1:00 and 2:30 p.m., for 15 minutes; after 2:30 p.m., there is no halt. For 15%, the halt is 1 hour 45 minutes before 1:00 p.m., 45 minutes between 1:00 and 2:00 p.m., and for the rest of the day after 2:00 p.m. A 20% move at any time halts trading for the rest of the day. After a halt, trading resumes with a pre-open call auction session.

The purpose is to give investors time to absorb information, check margins and avoid panic selling. It is different from the price band on individual stocks: a stock can hit its upper or lower band ('upper circuit' or 'lower circuit') while the overall market trades normally.

India's most-cited example is March 2020, when the market-wide circuit breaker was triggered twice in a single month during the pandemic sell-off. Many investors use 'circuit' loosely for both concepts; in exams, be precise about whether the question is about index-based halts or scrip-level bands.

Example

The Nifty closed at 24,000 yesterday. A 10% fall means 21,600 (2,400 points). If the Nifty touches 21,600 at 11:20 a.m., all equity and equity-derivative trading on NSE and BSE halts for 45 minutes, followed by a 15-minute pre-open session. Had the Sensex crossed its 10% level first, the same halt would apply. A further fall to 20,400 (15%) after trading resumes, but still before 1:00 p.m., would trigger a 1-hour-45-minute halt.

FAQs

Is a circuit breaker the same as upper circuit or lower circuit?

Not quite. 'Upper circuit' and 'lower circuit' describe a single stock hitting its daily price band, after which trades can occur only within the band. The market-wide circuit breaker is index-based and halts all trading on all exchanges. The two mechanisms work independently.

Do circuit breakers apply to currency and commodity markets?

The index-based market-wide circuit breaker described here applies to equity and equity derivative segments. Commodity and currency derivatives have their own daily price limits and margin rules set by the exchanges, which can include temporary cooling-off periods when limits are hit.
