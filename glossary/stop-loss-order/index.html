What is Stop-Loss Order?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Stop-Loss Order: A stop-loss order is an instruction to buy or sell once the price reaches a specified trigger price, used to limit losses or protect profits.

A stop-loss order has a trigger price. Until the market reaches the trigger, the order is held without being active in the order book. Once the last traded price touches the trigger, the order is released to the exchange. For a long position, a sell stop-loss trigger is placed below the current price; for a short position, a buy stop-loss is placed above it.

There are two common variants. A stop-loss limit (SL) order has both a trigger price and a limit price: once triggered, it becomes a limit order at the limit price. A stop-loss market (SL-M) order becomes a market order when triggered. Some brokers and segments restrict SL-M orders, so check what your platform allows.

The trade-off is execution certainty versus price certainty. An SL order protects you from selling far below your limit, but in a fast fall the price can skip past your limit and leave the order unexecuted. An SL-M order will execute, but possibly at a much worse price, especially when a stock gaps down at the open after bad news.

Stop-losses are risk management tools, not guarantees. They do not protect against gap openings, and placing them too close to the current price can lead to being stopped out by normal fluctuations. In exams, remember: trigger price activates; limit price controls the execution price.

Example

Sneha bought 200 shares at ₹600. She places a sell SL order with trigger ₹570 and limit ₹568. If the price drifts down to ₹570, the order activates and sells at ₹568 or better, limiting her loss to about ₹32 × 200 = ₹6,400. If results disappoint and the stock opens at ₹540, the limit of ₹568 is above the market, so the order stays unexecuted and her paper loss is ₹12,000.

FAQs

What is the difference between trigger price and limit price?

The trigger price is the level that activates the stop-loss order. The limit price is the worst price at which you are willing to trade once activated. For a sell stop-loss, the limit is usually set slightly below the trigger to allow some room for execution in a falling market.

Does a stop-loss order guarantee my maximum loss?

No. A stop-loss limit order may remain unexecuted if the price falls past your limit, and a stop-loss market order may execute well below the trigger in a gap-down or fast-moving market. It helps manage risk in normal conditions but cannot remove gap or liquidity risk.
