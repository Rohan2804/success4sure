What is Strategic asset allocation?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Strategic asset allocation: Strategic asset allocation is a long-term target mix of asset classes, set according to an investor's goals, time horizon and risk profile, and maintained through periodic rebalancing.

Under a strategic approach, the adviser and client agree on a 'policy' portfolio, say 60% equity, 30% debt and 10% gold, and stick to it regardless of short-term market views. The mix is reviewed only when the client's circumstances or goals change, such as a new child, a job change or approaching retirement.

Because markets move, the actual weights drift from the targets. The discipline lies in rebalancing back to the policy mix at set intervals or when a weight drifts beyond a band, for example more than 5 percentage points. This automatically sells assets that have risen and buys those that have fallen.

A strategic allocation can change predictably over time. A glide path reduces equity as a goal approaches, as in target-date funds or retirement planning. This is still strategic because the changes follow a pre-agreed plan, not market forecasts.

Strategic allocation is the foundation of most professional financial plans and is a core topic in NISM investment adviser exams. Its weakness is that it ignores valuations; investors who want to respond to market conditions add a tactical layer on top.

Example

Rohit sets a strategic allocation of 70% equity and 30% debt for his ₹10 lakh retirement portfolio, with a rebalancing band of ±5 points. After a strong year equity grows to ₹8.4 lakh and debt to ₹3.15 lakh, a total of ₹11.55 lakh with equity at 72.7%, inside the band, so he does nothing. Next year equity reaches 77%, outside the band, and he moves money back to 70:30.

FAQs

How often should a strategic asset allocation be changed?

Only when the investor's goals, time horizon or risk profile change, or as part of a pre-planned glide path. It should not change because of market news. Rebalancing to the existing targets, however, happens regularly, such as once a year or when weights drift beyond set bands.

What is the difference between strategic and tactical asset allocation?

Strategic allocation is the long-term target mix based on the investor's profile. Tactical allocation is a short-term deviation from that mix to exploit perceived market opportunities, such as raising equity when valuations look cheap. Tactical moves return to the strategic mix later.
