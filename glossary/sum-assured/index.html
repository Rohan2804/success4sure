What is Sum Assured?

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Sum Assured: Sum assured is the fixed amount of money that a life insurance company promises to pay on the occurrence of the insured event, such as death of the life assured or maturity of the policy.

In a term plan, sum assured is the death benefit paid to the nominee. In an endowment plan, it is typically payable on death during the term or on survival to maturity, along with any bonuses declared. Some plans pay a higher 'sum assured on death', defined by IRDAI rules as the highest of a multiple of annual premium, the basic sum assured, or a percentage of premiums paid.

Sum assured differs from sum insured. Life insurance is not a contract of indemnity because a human life cannot be valued exactly, so the insurer pays a fixed, agreed amount. General insurance like health, motor and fire is indemnity-based: the 'sum insured' is the maximum the insurer will pay, and the actual claim is limited to the real loss.

How much to choose? A common method is Human Life Value (HLV), which estimates the present value of the income a person's family would lose. A simpler rule of thumb is a multiple of annual income, often 10 to 15 times, adjusted for loans and goals. Insurers also apply financial underwriting: they will not issue a sum assured far beyond what the person's income justifies, to avoid over-insurance and moral hazard.

Riders have their own sum assured, usually not more than the base policy. Exam pointer: premium is directly proportional to sum assured for the same age and term, so a ₹50 lakh cover costs roughly half of a ₹1 crore cover, though rebates for larger covers can make the larger one slightly cheaper per lakh.

Example

Amit, 32, earns ₹9 lakh a year; about ₹2.5 lakh goes on his own expenses and taxes, so his family relies on ₹6.5 lakh a year. Assuming 28 working years left and a 6% discount rate, the present value is about ₹6.5 lakh × 13.41 = ₹87 lakh. He also has a ₹25 lakh home loan, so he might consider a term cover of around ₹1.1 to 1.2 crore. If he dies during the term, his nominee receives the sum assured.

FAQs

What is the difference between sum assured and sum insured?

Sum assured is used in life insurance and is the fixed amount paid on death or maturity, regardless of actual financial loss. Sum insured is used in general insurance such as health, motor and property, and is the maximum limit; the claim paid is the actual loss up to that limit, following the principle of indemnity.

Can I get a sum assured of any amount I want?

No. Insurers use financial underwriting to check that the sum assured is reasonable compared with your income, age and existing cover. They may ask for income proof for larger amounts. Very high cover relative to income may be declined or reduced because it could create a motive for fraud.
