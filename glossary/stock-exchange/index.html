What is Stock Exchange?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Stock Exchange: A stock exchange is a regulated marketplace where buy and sell orders for listed securities are matched electronically under rules approved by the market regulator.

In India the two national stock exchanges for equities are the National Stock Exchange (NSE) and BSE (formerly the Bombay Stock Exchange). Commodity derivatives trade mainly on MCX and NCDEX, while NSE and BSE also run segments for equity derivatives, currency derivatives and debt. Every exchange is recognised by SEBI under the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956.

An exchange does not buy or sell shares itself. It provides an anonymous order-matching system: orders from thousands of brokers flow into an order book, and the system matches them on price-time priority — the best price first, and at the same price, the earliest order first. The exchange also sets trading hours, price bands, surveillance rules and listing requirements for companies.

Ordinary investors cannot place orders directly on the exchange. They must go through a SEBI-registered stock broker who is a trading member of that exchange. After a trade is matched, the exchange's clearing corporation takes over to settle it, so the buyer gets shares and the seller gets money even if the counterparty fails.

A common confusion is to treat 'the market' and 'the exchange' as the same thing. The exchange is the infrastructure; prices are set by the buyers and sellers using it. Another is assuming a share listed on BSE cannot be sold on NSE — many large companies are listed on both, and a share bought on one exchange can usually be sold on the other because the holding sits in your demat account, not with the exchange.

Example

Suppose at 10:15 a.m. the NSE order book for a stock shows the best buy order at ₹501.50 for 200 shares and the best sell order at ₹502.00 for 150 shares. No trade happens until someone crosses the gap. If you place a market buy order for 100 shares, the system matches you instantly against the ₹502.00 sell order, leaving 50 shares at that price for the next buyer. Your broker receives the trade confirmation within seconds.

FAQs

How many stock exchanges are there in India?

There are a few SEBI-recognised exchanges, but almost all equity trading happens on NSE and BSE. MCX and NCDEX dominate commodity derivatives, and the Metropolitan Stock Exchange (MSE) is a smaller national exchange. Most older regional exchanges have exited. For the current list, check SEBI's website under recognised stock exchanges.

Can I buy shares without a stock exchange?

Off-market transfers between two people are possible through depository instructions, for example gifting shares to a family member. But for buying from the general public at market prices, you need a broker and an exchange. Unlisted shares can be traded privately, but without the price discovery, liquidity and settlement guarantee an exchange provides.
