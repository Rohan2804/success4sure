What is Index Futures?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Index Futures: Index futures are futures contracts whose underlying is a stock market index such as the Nifty 50 or Sensex, settled in cash at expiry.

Index futures let investors take a view on the whole market, or hedge a portfolio, with a single contract. In India they trade on NSE (Nifty 50, Nifty Bank and others) and BSE (Sensex, Bankex). Contracts for the near, next and far months trade simultaneously, and each has a lot size fixed by the exchange to meet SEBI's minimum contract value.

Because an index cannot be delivered, settlement is in cash. Daily mark-to-market uses the futures closing price, and final settlement uses the closing value of the index on expiry day. Since 2025, SEBI has allowed each exchange to choose either Tuesday or Thursday as its expiry day for equity derivatives, so check the exchange calendar for current expiry dates.

Index futures are priced using the cost-of-carry model: spot index plus interest minus expected dividends from the constituent stocks. They are useful for hedging diversified portfolios — a fund manager expecting short-term volatility can sell index futures instead of selling dozens of stocks, avoiding transaction costs and taxes on the portfolio.

Index futures carry less single-stock risk than stock futures, but they are still leveraged. A move of a few hundred points can produce gains or losses equal to the full margin. Another misconception is that index futures are physically settled in the constituent shares; they are not.

Example

The Nifty is at 24,000 and the one-month future at 24,140. With an illustrative lot size of 75, contract value is ₹18,10,500. A trader buys 1 lot. If the index future rises to 24,600 when he exits, the gain is 460 × 75 = ₹34,500. If it falls to 23,800, the loss is 340 × 75 = ₹25,500 — collected through daily MTM along the way.

FAQs

Why are index futures cash settled?

An index is a calculated number representing a basket of stocks. Delivering every constituent in the right proportions would be impractical, so contracts are settled by paying the difference between the contract price and the final settlement index value. Cash settlement keeps index derivatives simple and liquid.

Can index futures hedge any portfolio?

They work best for diversified portfolios that move broadly with the index. Portfolios concentrated in mid-caps, small-caps or a single sector face basis risk, because their returns may diverge from the index. Using beta to size the hedge helps, but does not remove this mismatch fully.
