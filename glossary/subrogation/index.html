What is Subrogation?

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Subrogation: Subrogation is the right of an insurer, after paying a claim, to step into the shoes of the insured and recover the amount from the third party responsible for the loss.

Subrogation is a corollary of the principle of indemnity. If an insured could collect from the insurer and also sue the person who caused the damage, they would recover twice and profit from the loss. Subrogation prevents this by transferring the insured's legal rights against the wrongdoer to the insurer, up to the amount the insurer has paid.

The insurer's right arises only after it has paid the claim. It can then sue in the insured's name or its own, and the insured must cooperate, for example by providing documents and evidence. If the insurer recovers more than it paid, the excess goes to the insured. Policies usually contain a subrogation clause, and the insured signs a letter of subrogation when receiving the claim.

The insured must not do anything that harms the insurer's subrogation rights, such as releasing the wrongdoer from liability or settling privately for a small sum. Doing so can reduce or void the claim. Insurers may also acquire salvage, the damaged property itself, which is a related form of recovery.

Subrogation applies to indemnity contracts such as motor, fire, marine and property insurance. It does not apply to life insurance or personal accident death benefits, which pay a fixed amount; the family can both receive the sum assured and sue the person responsible for the death.

Example

Neeta's car, insured for an IDV of ₹6 lakh, is hit by a truck whose driver is at fault. Repairs cost ₹1,20,000. Her insurer pays ₹1,15,000 after the ₹5,000 compulsory deductible. Through subrogation, the insurer files a recovery claim against the truck owner's insurer under third-party liability and recovers ₹1,20,000. It keeps ₹1,15,000 and passes ₹5,000 to Neeta, the deductible she had borne.

FAQs

Does subrogation apply to life insurance?

No. Life insurance is not a contract of indemnity; the insurer pays a fixed sum assured regardless of actual loss. So the insurer gets no right to recover from a third party responsible for the death. The family can receive the policy money and still claim compensation from the wrongdoer, for example through a Motor Accident Claims Tribunal.

When does the insurer get subrogation rights?

Subrogation rights arise only after the insurer has paid the claim, and only up to the amount paid. Before payment, the insured holds the rights. Many policies require the insured to sign a subrogation letter at the time of settlement and to avoid any action that could weaken the insurer's recovery.
