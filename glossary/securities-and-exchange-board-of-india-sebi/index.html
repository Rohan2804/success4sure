What is SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India)?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India): SEBI is India's statutory regulator for the securities market, set up to protect investors' interests and to develop and regulate the market.

SEBI was formed in 1988 as a non-statutory body and received statutory powers through the SEBI Act, 1992. Its headquarters is in Mumbai. The board has a chairperson, two members from the Union Finance Ministry, one from the RBI and five other members, of whom at least three are whole-time members. Before SEBI, capital issues were controlled by the Controller of Capital Issues.

The SEBI Act's preamble sums up its three aims: protecting the interests of investors, promoting the development of the securities market, and regulating it. SEBI registers and supervises intermediaries — stock brokers, depositories, DPs, mutual funds, portfolio managers, investment advisers, research analysts, merchant bankers, RTAs and others — and regulates listed companies' disclosures, takeovers and insider trading.

SEBI has three types of powers. Quasi-legislative: it frames regulations. Executive: it inspects, investigates and enforces rules. Quasi-judicial: it passes orders, imposes penalties and can bar entities from the market. Appeals against SEBI orders go to the Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT), and then to the Supreme Court.

SEBI does not guarantee investment returns or approve the merit of any security. It also does not regulate everything financial: banks fall under the RBI, insurance under IRDAI, pensions under PFRDA. Since 2015, commodity derivatives have also been under SEBI.

Example

A listed company fails to disclose a material default to the exchanges on time. SEBI examines the case, issues a show-cause notice and, after a hearing, an adjudicating officer imposes a penalty on the company and its compliance officer. The company appeals to SAT, which may uphold, modify or set aside the order; a further appeal can go to the Supreme Court. This flow shows SEBI's executive and quasi-judicial roles in action.

FAQs

When was SEBI established?

SEBI was established on 12 April 1988 as a non-statutory body through a government resolution. It became a statutory body with the SEBI Act, 1992, which gave it legal powers to regulate the securities market. This distinction between formation and statutory status is a common exam question.

Where can I appeal against a SEBI order?

Appeals against SEBI orders lie with the Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT), generally within 45 days of receiving the order, subject to condonation for delay. Orders of SAT can be appealed before the Supreme Court on questions of law. SAT also hears appeals against orders of IRDAI and PFRDA.
