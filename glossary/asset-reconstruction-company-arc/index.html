What is Asset Reconstruction Company (ARC)?

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Asset Reconstruction Company (ARC): An asset reconstruction company is an RBI-registered specialised financial institution that buys non-performing loans from banks at a discount and tries to recover or restructure them.

ARCs are set up and registered with RBI under Section 3 of the SARFAESI Act, 2002. India's first, ARCIL, began in 2003. Banks sell NPAs to ARCs to clean their balance sheets and get cash or tradable instruments in return, while ARCs specialise in recovery: restructuring the borrower's business, changing management, rescheduling debt, enforcing security or selling the assets.

In a typical deal, the ARC sets up a trust that buys the NPA from the bank at an agreed price, often well below the outstanding amount. The trust pays the bank partly in cash and partly through security receipts (SRs), which entitle the holder to a share of future recoveries. RBI requires the ARC to invest at least 15% of the SRs issued in each deal, so it has skin in the game; the rest (up to 85%) may be held by the selling bank or other investors. If recoveries exceed the price, SR holders gain; if not, they lose.

RBI raised the minimum net owned fund for ARCs to ₹300 crore in 2022 and has tightened rules on governance and on banks investing in SRs of their own sold loans. These limits may change, so check RBI's master directions (as of 2026).

The National Asset Reconstruction Company Ltd (NARCL), sometimes called a 'bad bank', was set up in 2021 with government-guaranteed security receipts to take over large legacy NPAs. Exam traps: ARCs are regulated by RBI, not SEBI; and selling an NPA to an ARC does not waive the borrower's liability, which simply passes to the ARC.

Example

A bank holds an NPA with ₹500 crore outstanding, fully provided except ₹100 crore. An ARC agrees to buy it for ₹200 crore. It pays 15% (₹30 crore) by investing in SRs itself and issues ₹170 crore of SRs to the bank (in practice deals often include upfront cash). Over five years the ARC recovers ₹260 crore by selling the borrower's factory. After its fees and expenses of ₹20 crore, ₹240 crore is distributed to SR holders, a gain over the ₹200 crore price.

FAQs

Who regulates asset reconstruction companies in India?

ARCs are registered and regulated by the Reserve Bank of India under the SARFAESI Act, 2002. RBI sets norms for their capital, investment in security receipts, governance and acquisition of assets. SEBI's role is limited to listed security receipts, where applicable, but the primary regulator is RBI.

Does a borrower's loan get waived when sold to an ARC?

No. When a bank sells an NPA to an ARC, the debt is transferred with all its rights. The borrower still owes the full contractual amount, now to the ARC, which can use SARFAESI, DRT or the IBC to recover it. The discounted price paid by the ARC does not reduce what the borrower owes.
