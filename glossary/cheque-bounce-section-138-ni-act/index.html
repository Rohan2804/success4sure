What is Cheque Bounce (Section 138, NI Act)?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Cheque Bounce (Section 138, NI Act): Cheque bounce under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act is the criminal offence of a cheque, issued to discharge a legally enforceable debt, being returned unpaid for insufficient funds.

The offence applies when a cheque is dishonoured because the amount in the account is insufficient or exceeds the arrangement with the bank. Courts have extended it to dishonour due to 'account closed' and 'payment stopped by drawer' where the intention was to avoid payment. It does not apply to cheques given as gifts, donations or security where no debt exists at the time of presentation, though courts examine this case by case.

Conditions and timeline: the cheque must be presented within its validity (three months). After dishonour, the payee must send a written demand notice to the drawer within 30 days of receiving information from the bank. The drawer gets 15 days from receipt of notice to pay. If he does not, the payee must file a complaint before a Judicial Magistrate within one month after the 15-day period ends. Courts may condone delay for sufficient cause.

Punishment: imprisonment up to two years, or a fine up to twice the cheque amount, or both. The court may order interim compensation of up to 20% of the cheque amount under Section 143A (added in 2018). The offence is compoundable, meaning parties can settle at any stage. The law presumes the cheque was issued for a debt (Section 139), so the drawer must prove otherwise.

Banks also act: if cheques of ₹1 crore or more bounce four times in a financial year due to insufficient funds, banks are required to stop issuing a new cheque book and may close the account at their discretion.

Example

Ramesh gives a supplier a ₹2,00,000 cheque dated 1 March. It bounces on 10 March; the supplier gets the bank's return memo on 12 March. The demand notice must be sent by 11 April (30 days). Ramesh receives it on 15 April and has until 30 April to pay. If he doesn't, the complaint must be filed by 30 May. If convicted, he could face up to two years in prison or a fine up to ₹4,00,000, or both.

FAQs

Is a cheque bounce a criminal case or a civil case?

It can be both. Section 138 makes it a criminal offence with possible imprisonment and fine. The payee can also file a civil recovery suit, including a summary suit under Order XXXVII of the Code of Civil Procedure, to recover the money. Both remedies can be pursued at the same time.

What if the demand notice is not sent within 30 days?

The complaint under Section 138 will fail because notice within 30 days of receiving the bank's information is a mandatory condition. However, the payee can present the cheque again within its validity period and, on fresh dishonour, send a new notice in time. Civil recovery of the debt also remains available.
