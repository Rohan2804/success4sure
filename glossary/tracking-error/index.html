What is Tracking error?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Tracking error: Tracking error is the standard deviation of the difference between a fund's returns and its benchmark index's returns, showing how consistently a passive fund follows its index.

An index fund or ETF should, ideally, move exactly with its index. In practice, expenses, cash held for redemptions, delays in reinvesting dividends, and rebalancing costs cause small gaps. Tracking error measures how much these gaps fluctuate. It is usually calculated from daily return differences and annualised.

A related but different measure is tracking difference, the simple gap between fund return and index return over a period, such as −0.25% over one year. Tracking difference tells you how much you lost to costs; tracking error tells you how predictable that loss was. A fund can have a large but steady tracking difference (low tracking error) or a small average gap with wild swings (high tracking error).

SEBI requires AMCs to disclose tracking error and tracking difference for passive funds on their websites and caps tracking error for index funds and ETFs; check current limits as of 2026. Investors comparing two Nifty 50 index funds should look at both numbers alongside TER.

For active funds, the same calculation is sometimes called active risk, showing how far the manager deviates from the benchmark. There, a high figure is a choice, not a flaw.

Example

Over six days an index fund's daily returns were 0.80%, −0.35%, 1.12%, 0.40%, −0.90% and 0.65%, while the index returned 0.85%, −0.30%, 1.20%, 0.38%, −0.82% and 0.70%. The daily differences are −0.05, −0.05, −0.08, +0.02, −0.08 and −0.05 percentage points. Their standard deviation is about 0.037 percentage point per day. Annualised using roughly 250 trading days (× √250 ≈ 15.8), tracking error is about 0.58%.

FAQs

Is a lower tracking error always better?

For an index fund or ETF, yes, generally a lower tracking error means the fund follows its index more faithfully. But also check tracking difference, which shows the actual return lost. A fund with slightly higher tracking error but much lower cost can still deliver better returns.

What causes tracking error in index funds?

Main causes are the expense ratio, cash kept for redemptions, timing delays in investing inflows or reinvesting dividends, transaction costs when the index is rebalanced, and corporate actions. Larger funds with efficient operations usually have lower tracking error.
