What is Tax-loss harvesting?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Tax-loss harvesting: Tax-loss harvesting is selling investments that are below their purchase cost to realise a capital loss, which can then be used to reduce taxable capital gains.

Capital losses are only recognised for tax when realised by selling. An investor who has booked gains during the year, and also holds investments showing losses, can sell the loss-making holdings before 31 March so that the realised losses offset the gains.

Indian set-off rules, as of 2026: short-term capital losses can be set off against both short-term and long-term gains; long-term capital losses only against long-term gains. Unabsorbed losses can be carried forward for eight assessment years, but only if the income tax return is filed by the due date.

A related strategy, sometimes called tax-gain harvesting, involves booking long-term equity gains up to the annual exemption limit each year and reinvesting, to reset the cost base higher at no tax cost.

Cautions: selling and immediately buying back the same asset may be questioned by tax authorities under anti-avoidance principles, even though India has no specific wash-sale rule like the US; exit loads and transaction costs reduce the benefit; and the main purpose of an investment decision should not be tax alone. Check current law or consult a tax professional.

Example

In FY 2025-26, Sameer has booked ₹1,00,000 of short-term gains on equity funds. Another equity fund he bought six months ago shows a ₹40,000 unrealised loss. He sells it in March, realising a ₹40,000 short-term loss, which reduces his net STCG to ₹60,000. At 20% plus cess, his tax falls from ₹20,800 to ₹12,480, a saving of ₹8,320. He reinvests in a different fund with similar exposure to stay invested.

FAQs

Can capital losses be set off against salary income?

No. Capital losses can only be set off against capital gains, following the short-term and long-term rules. They cannot reduce salary or business income. Unused losses can be carried forward for eight years if the return is filed on time.

Is tax-loss harvesting legal in India?

Realising genuine losses to offset gains is permitted. However, artificial transactions done only to create losses may attract scrutiny under general anti-avoidance principles. Keep transactions genuine and documented, and consult a tax professional for complex cases.
