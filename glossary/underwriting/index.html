What is Underwriting (Insurance)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Underwriting (Insurance): Underwriting is the process by which an insurer evaluates the risk in a proposal and decides whether to accept it, on what terms and at what premium.

Underwriting protects insurers from adverse selection, the tendency of people with higher risk to seek more insurance. Without it, premiums would have to rise for everyone. The underwriter classifies each proposer into a risk class so that people with similar risks pay similar premiums.

In life insurance, underwriters consider medical factors (age, height and weight, blood pressure, diabetes, past illnesses, family history), lifestyle (smoking, alcohol, hazardous hobbies), occupation, and financial factors (income versus sum assured, existing cover). Sources include the proposal form, the agent's confidential report, medical examination and reports, and financial documents. Non-medical underwriting is allowed for smaller covers and younger ages, relying on the declarations alone.

Hazards are of two kinds: physical hazard, arising from the subject itself (a chronic illness, or a factory storing chemicals), and moral hazard, arising from the attitude or behaviour of the insured (carelessness, dishonesty). Underwriting decisions include: accept at standard rates; accept with extra premium (rating up); accept with exclusions, lien or reduced cover; postpone (for example, until recovery from a recent surgery); or decline.

In general insurance, underwriters look at the property's construction, location, safety measures and claims history. Underwriting at the field level begins with the agent, who is often called the primary underwriter because he first sees the proposer.

Example

Two 40-year-olds apply for a ₹1 crore term plan. Asha is healthy and a non-smoker, so she is accepted at the standard premium of ₹18,000. Vinod has controlled diabetes and a BMI of 33. The underwriter accepts him with a 50% extra premium, so he pays ₹27,000. A third applicant, who had heart surgery three months ago, is postponed for a year so that his recovery can be assessed before cover is offered.

FAQs

Why is the agent called the primary underwriter?

The agent meets the proposer face to face and sees their lifestyle, health signs, occupation and financial standing before anyone at the insurer does. The agent's confidential report and the information collected at this stage form the base of the insurer's risk assessment, which is why IC-38 calls the agent the primary underwriter.

What is the difference between physical and moral hazard?

Physical hazard relates to tangible features of the risk, such as a person's medical condition or a building's construction material. Moral hazard relates to the insured's behaviour or intentions, such as carelessness after being insured or a temptation to make false claims. Underwriters assess both before accepting a proposal.
