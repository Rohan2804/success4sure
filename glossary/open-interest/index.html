What is Open Interest (OI)?

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Open Interest (OI): Open interest is the total number of outstanding derivative contracts that have been opened and not yet closed, exercised or expired at a given time.

Each contract has a buyer and a seller, but open interest counts it only once. OI rises when a new buyer and a new seller create a fresh position. It falls when an existing buyer and existing seller both close positions. If one side closes and the other is newly opened (an existing position transferred to a new trader), OI stays unchanged.

Volume and OI measure different things. Volume counts all contracts traded during the day, including intraday trades that are opened and closed. OI is a snapshot of positions still open at the end of the day (or at a point in time). A contract can have high volume and low OI if most trading is intraday.

Traders combine price and OI changes for clues. Price up with OI up is read as long build-up; price down with OI up as short build-up; price up with OI down as short covering; price down with OI down as long unwinding. In options, strikes with very large call OI or put OI are sometimes viewed as resistance or support levels.

These are interpretations, not rules. Large OI can come from hedgers and arbitrageurs, not just directional traders. Exchanges also set market-wide position limits in terms of OI, and stocks crossing 95% of their limit enter a ban period in which new F&O positions are not allowed.

Example

On Monday, A buys 1 futures contract from B: OI = 1. On Tuesday, C buys 2 contracts from D (both new): OI = 3. On Wednesday, A sells his 1 contract to E, a new trader: OI stays 3 — the position simply changed hands. On Thursday, C sells 2 contracts to D, closing both positions: OI falls to 1. Total volume over four days was 6 contracts.

FAQs

What is the difference between volume and open interest?

Volume counts every contract traded in a period, while open interest counts the contracts still open at a point in time. If a trader buys and sells the same contract in a day, volume rises by two transactions while open interest does not change. OI shows positions carried; volume shows activity.

Does high open interest mean the price will go up?

No. High open interest only means many positions are open. Whether those positions are long or short, hedges or speculative bets, cannot be determined from OI alone. Traders look at the combination of price movement and change in OI, but these signals are probabilistic, not certain.
