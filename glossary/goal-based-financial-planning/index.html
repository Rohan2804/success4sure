What is Goal-based financial planning?

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Goal-based financial planning: Goal-based financial planning organises a person's investments around specific future needs, such as a child's education or retirement, each with its own amount, date and strategy.

Instead of asking 'which fund gives the best return?', goal-based planning asks 'what do I need, by when?' Each goal gets a target amount in today's money, a time horizon and a priority. The adviser then inflates the cost to the future date, decides an asset mix suitable for that horizon, estimates a reasonable return for that mix and calculates how much must be invested each month or as a lump sum.

Different horizons lead to different mixes. A goal three years away is mostly in debt because equity could be down when the money is needed. A goal fifteen years away can take more equity. As a goal approaches, money is gradually shifted to safer assets, a glide path, so a market fall in the final year does not wreck it.

Priorities matter when money is limited. Essential goals like an emergency fund, insurance and retirement usually come before aspirational ones like a foreign holiday.

Goal-based planning makes progress measurable and helps behaviour: investors are less tempted to stop a SIP that is clearly tied to their daughter's college fees. Its main pitfalls are unrealistic return assumptions and underestimating inflation for items like education and healthcare.

Example

Ritu wants ₹15 lakh in today's money for her son's college in 12 years. Assuming 8% education inflation, the future cost is about ₹15 lakh × 1.08^12 ≈ ₹37.8 lakh. With an equity-heavy mix assumed to earn 11% a year, she would need a monthly SIP of roughly ₹12,700 for 12 years. In the last three years she will shift gradually to debt to protect the amount.

FAQs

Why should each goal have a separate plan?

Because goals differ in amount, timing and priority, and therefore need different asset mixes. Mixing all money in one pot makes it hard to tell whether each goal is on track and tempts investors to use long-term money for short-term wants.

What return should I assume for goal planning?

Use conservative, realistic assumptions for each asset mix and time horizon rather than recent high returns. Many planners assume returns a few points above inflation for equity-heavy portfolios and lower for debt. Reviewing progress yearly lets you correct course if actual returns differ.
