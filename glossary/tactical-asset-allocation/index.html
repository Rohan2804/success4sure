What is Tactical asset allocation?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Tactical asset allocation: Tactical asset allocation is a short-term, deliberate deviation from a portfolio's long-term strategic mix to take advantage of perceived market opportunities or reduce risk.

A tactical decision might be: equity valuations look stretched, so reduce equity from a 60% target to 50% for some months; or long-term bond yields are unusually high, so tilt debt toward longer duration. The tilt is temporary and within pre-set ranges, such as ±10 points of the strategic target.

Tactical calls can be based on valuation measures (price-to-earnings ratios, earnings yield versus bond yield), economic indicators, or momentum. Balanced advantage funds are a packaged form of tactical allocation driven by a model.

The approach has costs and risks. Each shift may trigger capital gains tax and exit loads for individual investors. Forecasts are often wrong; markets can remain 'expensive' for years. That is why advisers keep tactical bands narrow and document the reasoning.

Tactical allocation is sometimes confused with market timing. The difference is degree and discipline: market timing tries to move fully in or out based on predictions, while tactical allocation makes limited, rule-bound tilts around a long-term anchor.

Example

An adviser's strategic mix for a client is 60% equity, 40% debt on a ₹50 lakh portfolio, with tactical bands of ±10 points. With Nifty valuations well above their long-term average, the adviser moves to 52% equity (₹26 lakh) and 48% debt (₹24 lakh). Eighteen months later, after a correction, valuations normalise and the portfolio returns to 60:40. If the correction never came, the client would have lagged the strategic mix during the rally.

FAQs

Is tactical asset allocation suitable for retail investors?

It can be, in modest doses and with discipline, but it adds complexity, tax costs and the risk of wrong calls. Many retail investors get similar behaviour more cheaply through balanced advantage funds or simply by rebalancing a strategic mix. Suitability depends on the individual.

What limits should tactical allocation have?

Advisers usually set bands around the strategic weights, such as ±5 or ±10 percentage points, so that a wrong call cannot derail the plan. Decisions should be based on documented criteria rather than news headlines and should be reviewed for tax impact.
