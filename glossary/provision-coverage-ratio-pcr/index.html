What is Provision Coverage Ratio (PCR)?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Provision Coverage Ratio (PCR): Provision coverage ratio is the percentage of a bank's gross non-performing assets that it has already covered by setting aside provisions from its profits.

Basic formula: PCR = Total provisions held for NPAs ÷ Gross NPAs × 100. A higher PCR means the bank has already absorbed more of the expected loss in its accounts, so future earnings and capital are better protected. Analysts treat PCR as a measure of balance-sheet conservatism.

Banks often report two versions. PCR excluding technical write-offs uses only provisions against NPAs still on the books. PCR including technical write-offs adds loans that have been fully provided and removed from the balance sheet but are still being pursued for recovery; it adds these amounts to both the provisions and the gross NPA figures, which pushes the ratio higher. When comparing banks, check which version is quoted.

RBI required banks to reach a PCR of 70% by September 2011, a requirement later withdrawn in favour of building countercyclical buffers. There is no standalone mandatory PCR now, but RBI and investors monitor it closely; Indian banks improved PCR significantly after the post-2015 asset quality clean-up.

Link with net NPA: Net NPA = Gross NPA − Provisions. So a high PCR directly lowers net NPA. A bank with a 4% gross NPA ratio and 75% PCR will have net NPA of about 1% of advances.

Example

A bank has gross NPAs of ₹8,000 crore and specific provisions of ₹6,000 crore. PCR = 6,000/8,000 = 75%. Net NPA = ₹2,000 crore. It has also technically written off ₹4,000 crore (fully provided). Including write-offs, PCR = (6,000 + 4,000)/(8,000 + 4,000) = 10,000/12,000 = 83.3%. The same bank can quote either figure.

FAQs

Is a higher PCR always better?

A higher PCR means the bank is better cushioned against bad loans and less likely to face earnings shocks later. However, very high provisioning reduces current profits. Investors generally prefer a high PCR, especially when gross NPAs are elevated, but they also look at recoveries, slippages and capital adequacy.

What is a technical write-off?

A technical write-off removes a fully provided NPA from the bank's balance sheet for accounting purposes, while the bank continues its legal recovery efforts. The borrower's liability is not waived. Any later recovery is booked as income. It differs from a compromise settlement, where the bank actually agrees to accept less.
