What is Scheme Information Document (SID)?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Scheme Information Document (SID): A scheme information document (SID) is the offer document of a specific mutual fund scheme, setting out its objective, asset allocation, risks, costs and terms.

Every mutual fund scheme has its own SID, filed with SEBI before launch and kept updated afterwards. It is the legal basis of the scheme: the fund manager must invest within the asset allocation it describes, and a change to fundamental attributes (such as the type of scheme or investment objective) requires investors to be given an exit option without exit load.

A SID covers the investment objective and strategy, the asset allocation table with minimum and maximum ranges, the benchmark, risk factors, the fund manager, expense ratio structure, exit loads, minimum investment amounts, and procedures for buying and redeeming units. Riskometer and, for debt schemes, the potential risk class are also shown.

Information common to all schemes of a mutual fund, such as the sponsor's background, details of trustees and the AMC, and general tax and legal matters, is in a separate statement of additional information (SAI). The SID and SAI together form the complete offer document. A shorter summary, the KIM, goes with the application form.

Many investors never read the SID, then are surprised when a 'flexi cap' fund holds 30% in small caps or when an exit load applies. The asset allocation table, risk factors and load structure take ten minutes to read and answer most questions.

Example

Before investing ₹2 lakh in a 'balanced advantage' scheme, Farhan opens its SID. The asset allocation table shows equity can range from 0% to 100%, with derivatives up to 100% for hedging. The load section says 1% if more than 10% of units are redeemed within one year. He now knows the fund may behave very differently from an aggressive hybrid fund and that an early exit could cost ₹2,000 or more.

FAQs

What is the difference between SID and SAI?

The SID is specific to one scheme and covers its objective, allocation, risks and costs. The SAI contains information common to all schemes of that mutual fund, such as the sponsor, trustees, AMC and legal aspects. Together they make up the full offer document.

What happens if a scheme changes a fundamental attribute in its SID?

The AMC must get trustee approval, inform SEBI, notify investors and give them a window to exit at prevailing NAV without exit load before the change takes effect. This protects investors who invested on the basis of the original terms.
