What is IDCW option (Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

IDCW option (Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal): IDCW, or Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal, is the mutual fund option (earlier called the dividend option) under which the scheme pays out part of its distributable surplus to unit holders.

Every mutual fund plan offers a growth option and usually an IDCW option. Under growth, all gains stay in the scheme and show up as a rising NAV. Under IDCW, the trustees may declare a distribution from time to time; the NAV falls by the amount paid out, and investors receive cash (payout) or extra units (reinvestment).

SEBI renamed the 'dividend' option to IDCW from April 2021 because the old name misled investors. A mutual fund 'dividend' is not extra profit like a company dividend; it is often your own capital being handed back. Part of each distribution can come from the equalisation reserve built out of money that new investors paid in.

Taxation tilts the choice for most people. Since April 2020, IDCW is taxable in the investor's hands at slab rate, and the fund deducts TDS above a threshold. Under the growth option, tax arises only on redemption and only on the gain, at capital gains rates. For an investor in a higher slab, growth plus a planned SWP is often more efficient than IDCW payouts.

IDCW is neither fixed nor assured. The amount and frequency depend on available surplus and the trustees' decision, and payouts can stop entirely.

Example

Kavita holds 10,000 units of a hybrid fund's IDCW option at NAV ₹25 (value ₹2,50,000). The fund declares IDCW of ₹1 per unit. She receives ₹10,000, and the NAV falls to about ₹24, so her holding is now worth ₹2,40,000. Her total wealth is unchanged at ₹2,50,000, but the ₹10,000 is added to her income and taxed at her slab rate. Had she chosen growth, nothing would be taxed until she redeemed.

FAQs

Is IDCW better than growth for regular income?

Not necessarily. IDCW payouts are irregular and taxed fully at slab rate. A growth option with a systematic withdrawal plan gives a predictable amount and taxes only the gain portion of each withdrawal. Investors in low tax brackets may find the difference small; those in higher slabs usually find SWP more efficient.

Does NAV fall after IDCW is declared?

Yes. On the record date the NAV of the IDCW option falls by the amount distributed plus any statutory levy. This is why an IDCW does not add to your wealth; it only moves money from the fund to your bank account.
