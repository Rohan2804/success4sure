What is Liquid fund?

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Liquid fund: A liquid fund is an open-ended debt mutual fund that invests only in money market and debt securities with maturity of up to 91 days.

Liquid funds hold treasury bills, commercial paper, certificates of deposit, repos and short-term government securities. Because maturities are so short, interest rate movements hardly affect the NAV, and the NAV generally rises a little almost every day. SEBI also requires liquid funds to keep a part of assets in cash-like instruments and to value holdings on a mark-to-market basis.

NAV is declared for every calendar day, including weekends and holidays. Applications for liquid funds have an earlier cut-off (1:30 pm) than other schemes, and you get the previous day's NAV only if money is credited to the fund before the cut-off. Redemptions are usually paid the next business day, and many AMCs offer instant redemption up to a small limit.

SEBI mandates a graded exit load for the first six days to discourage very short-term arbitrage by large investors. From day seven there is no load.

Liquid funds are low-risk, not risk-free. In 2019–2020, defaults in some commercial paper caused sharp one-day NAV drops in a few schemes. Check the credit quality of the portfolio and the scheme's risk-o-meter. Gains are taxed at slab rate as of 2026 under the rules for debt schemes bought after April 2023.

Example

Meera parks ₹3 lakh of her emergency fund in a liquid fund instead of a savings account. On a day her NAV is ₹4,000, she gets 75 units. She needs ₹40,000 on day 4. The graded exit load for day 4 is 0.0055%, so she pays about ₹2.20. If she redeemed on day 7 or later, she would pay nothing.

FAQs

Is a liquid fund safer than a savings account?

They carry different risks. A bank savings deposit is covered by DICGC deposit insurance up to a limit and has no NAV fluctuation. A liquid fund has no such insurance and carries small credit and liquidity risk, though it may earn more. Many people split emergency money between the two.

What is the exit load on liquid funds?

SEBI prescribes a graded load for redemptions within six days: 0.0070% on day 1 falling by 0.0005% each day to 0.0045% on day 6, and nil from day 7. The amounts are tiny for small investors. Check the scheme documents for any changes.
