What is DDPI (Demat Debit and Pledge Instruction)?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

DDPI (Demat Debit and Pledge Instruction): DDPI is a limited authorisation a client gives to a broker-DP to debit or pledge securities from the client's demat account only for specified purposes.

Earlier, brokers took a general power of attorney (POA) from clients to move shares for settlement. Misuse of POAs — including pledging client shares for the broker's own borrowing — led SEBI to introduce the DDPI framework in 2022. The DDPI restricts what the broker can do with your holdings.

A DDPI covers four purposes: transfer of securities for delivery against your sale on the exchange; pledging or re-pledging in favour of the broker or clearing corporation for margin; mutual fund transactions executed on stock exchange order-entry platforms; and tendering shares in open offers or buybacks through the exchange. Anything else, such as an off-market transfer, still needs your separate authorisation.

Signing a DDPI is optional. Without it, you can still sell shares by authorising each sale electronically through the depository's e-DIS/TPIN or OTP process. The DDPI simply saves you this step every time you sell, which active traders find convenient.

A common misunderstanding is that DDPI lets the broker sell your shares. It does not authorise trades — only the delivery of shares you have already sold. Another point: shares pledged for margin under the DDPI remain in your demat account with a pledge marked, and you continue to get corporate benefits.

Example

Lata has signed a DDPI with her broker. She sells 50 shares on Monday; on Tuesday the broker-DP debits the 50 shares and delivers them for pay-in without asking her for a TPIN. Her friend Raj has not signed a DDPI; when he sells, he gets a prompt to authorise the debit through CDSL or NSDL's e-DIS with an OTP. If Raj misses it, his sale may be short-delivered and go into auction.

FAQs

Is DDPI mandatory to trade?

No. SEBI made DDPI optional. Clients can trade without it and authorise each delivery through the depository's electronic DIS process using a TPIN or OTP. Some brokers may require DDPI for certain products, such as pledging shares for margin, so check your broker's terms.

Can a broker transfer my shares off-market using DDPI?

No. A DDPI is limited to delivery against exchange sales, margin pledges, exchange-platform mutual fund transactions and tendering in buybacks or open offers. Off-market transfers or transfers to another person's account require separate instructions from you.
