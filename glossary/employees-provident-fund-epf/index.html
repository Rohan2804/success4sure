What is Employees' Provident Fund (EPF)?

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Employees' Provident Fund (EPF): The Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) is a mandatory retirement savings scheme for salaried employees, managed by EPFO, with contributions from both employee and employer.

Establishments with 20 or more employees must register with EPFO. Employees whose basic salary plus dearness allowance is up to ₹15,000 a month must join; others may join voluntarily. The employee contributes 12% of basic plus DA. The employer also contributes 12%, of which 8.33% (on wages up to ₹15,000) goes to the Employees' Pension Scheme (EPS) and the rest to EPF.

EPFO's central board declares the interest rate each year, approved by the government; it was 8.25% for recent years, but check the current rate. Each member has a Universal Account Number (UAN) that stays with them across jobs, making transfers easier.

Partial withdrawals are allowed for purposes such as housing, medical treatment, education and marriage, subject to service conditions, and EPFO has been simplifying these rules. Full withdrawal is allowed on retirement or after a period of unemployment. Withdrawals before five years of continuous service are generally taxable.

Tax: employee contributions qualify for 80C under the old regime. Interest on employee contributions above ₹2.5 lakh a year (₹5 lakh where there is no employer contribution) is taxable. Many employees overlook EPF as their largest retirement asset.

Example

Rahul's basic plus DA is ₹30,000 a month. He contributes 12% = ₹3,600. His employer contributes ₹3,600: 8.33% of the ₹15,000 wage ceiling, about ₹1,250, goes to EPS, and the remaining ₹2,350 to EPF. So ₹5,950 goes into his EPF account monthly, about ₹71,400 a year, plus EPS credit. His own ₹43,200 annual contribution counts under 80C in the old regime.

FAQs

Is EPF withdrawal taxable?

Withdrawal after five years of continuous service, including service with previous employers if the account was transferred, is generally tax-free. Withdrawal before five years is usually taxable, with TDS if above a threshold and PAN conditions. Rules can change, so check EPFO and income tax guidance.

What is the difference between EPF and PPF?

EPF is linked to employment, with mandatory contributions from employee and employer and an interest rate declared annually by EPFO. PPF is a voluntary scheme anyone can open, with a 15-year tenure and a rate set quarterly by the government. Both are long-term, low-risk retirement tools.
