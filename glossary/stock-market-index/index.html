What is Stock Market Index (Sensex and Nifty)?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Stock Market Index (Sensex and Nifty): A stock market index is a number that tracks the combined price movement of a selected group of stocks, representing the market or a segment of it.

India's two headline indices are the BSE Sensex, with 30 large companies and a base value of 100 for 1978–79, and the NSE Nifty 50, with 50 companies and a base value of 1,000 as of November 1995. Both are free-float market capitalisation weighted, so bigger companies with more publicly held shares move the index more.

The index value is calculated as the current free-float market capitalisation of the constituents divided by a divisor, which is adjusted for corporate actions and constituent changes so that the index level is continuous. A bonus issue or stock split therefore does not cause the index to jump. Index committees review constituents periodically, based on criteria such as free-float market cap, liquidity (often measured by impact cost) and listing history.

Beyond headline indices there are broad indices (Nifty 500, BSE 500), size-based indices (Midcap, Smallcap), sectoral indices (Bank Nifty, Nifty IT) and thematic or strategy indices. Indices serve as benchmarks for mutual funds, underlying assets for index futures and options, and a basis for ETFs and index funds.

Points versus percentage is a classic confusion. A 500-point fall in the Sensex sounds dramatic, but when the index is around 80,000 it is only about 0.6%. Also, the index going up does not mean every stock went up — a handful of heavyweights can move it while most stocks fall.

Example

An index has a base market cap of ₹5,00,000 crore set equal to 1,000 points, so the divisor is 500. Today, the free-float market cap of constituents is ₹12,40,000 crore, giving an index value of 2,480. If a constituent issues bonus shares, its price falls but the share count rises, so the market cap — and the index — stay unchanged without any divisor adjustment.

FAQs

What is the difference between Sensex and Nifty?

The Sensex belongs to BSE and has 30 stocks with a 1978–79 base; the Nifty 50 belongs to NSE Indices and has 50 stocks with a 1995 base. Both use free-float methodology and move broadly together. The Nifty is more widely used as the underlying for derivatives and index funds.

Can I invest directly in an index?

No, an index is just a number. You can get index exposure through index funds and ETFs that hold the constituent stocks in proportion to their weights, or through index futures and options, which are derivatives. Each route carries its own costs, tracking differences and risks.
