What is Exchange Traded Fund (ETF)?

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Exchange Traded Fund (ETF): An exchange traded fund (ETF) is a mutual fund scheme, usually passive, whose units are listed and traded on a stock exchange at market prices throughout the trading day.

An ETF holds a basket of securities that tracks an index, gold, silver or a bond index. Retail investors buy and sell ETF units on NSE or BSE through a broker and a demat account, at the live market price, just like shares. Large participants called authorised participants and market makers can create or redeem units directly with the AMC in big blocks.

Because units trade on the market, the price can differ slightly from the underlying value. Exchanges and AMCs publish an indicative NAV (iNAV) during market hours so investors can judge whether the market price is fair. In liquid ETFs the gap is tiny; in thinly traded ETFs it can be meaningful.

ETFs generally have very low expense ratios, but investors pay brokerage, demat charges and the bid-ask spread. SIPs are possible only through brokers that offer them, since the AMC does not run regular-sized ETF SIPs directly.

Popular Indian ETFs include Nifty 50, Bank Nifty, gold and Bharat Bond ETFs. A common confusion is to think every ETF is equity; gold and debt ETFs exist too. Another is to ignore liquidity: always check traded volume and spread before buying.

Example

A Nifty 50 ETF has a NAV of ₹245.10 and an iNAV of ₹245.30 at 11 a.m. On the exchange the best bid is ₹245.20 and best offer ₹245.40. Anil buys 400 units at ₹245.40, paying ₹98,160 plus brokerage. If the ETF were illiquid with an offer at ₹249, he would be overpaying by about 1.5% versus iNAV, which is why he checks the spread first.

FAQs

What is the difference between an ETF and an index fund?

Both can track the same index. An index fund is bought from the AMC at end-of-day NAV, needs no demat account and suits SIPs. An ETF trades on the exchange at live prices, needs a demat and broking account, and involves brokerage and bid-ask spreads but often has a lower expense ratio.

What is iNAV in an ETF?

iNAV, or indicative NAV, is an estimate of the ETF's per-unit value computed through the trading day from live prices of its holdings. It helps investors check whether the market price is close to fair value. AMCs and exchanges publish it at regular intervals.
