What is Protective Put?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Protective Put: A protective put is a strategy in which an investor who owns shares buys a put option on them to limit losses if the price falls.

The put works like an insurance policy. The premium is the insurance cost, the strike price is like the level of cover and expiry is the policy period. If the share falls below the strike, the put gains value and offsets the loss on the shares. If the share rises, the put expires worthless but the investor enjoys the full upside minus the premium.

The maximum loss is fixed: (purchase price − strike price + premium) × quantity. The breakeven at expiry is the purchase price plus the premium. Choosing the strike is a trade-off: a strike closer to the current price gives better protection but costs more; a lower strike is cheaper but leaves a bigger gap before protection starts.

Investors use protective puts before uncertain events, such as elections or major results, or when they want to keep a long-term holding but fear a short-term fall. Portfolio managers often buy index puts as a protective put on a diversified portfolio rather than buying puts on each stock.

The common mistake is buying protection continuously; repeated premium costs can significantly reduce long-term returns. Another is assuming the put protects beyond expiry — once it expires, the shares are unprotected. The payoff of a protective put resembles that of a long call, a relationship captured by put-call parity.

Example

Vikram holds 400 shares bought at ₹2,500 (₹10,00,000). He buys a ₹2,400 put at ₹45 (₹18,000). If the share falls to ₹2,100, the shares lose ₹1,60,000, the put pays ₹1,20,000 and the net loss is ₹58,000 — his maximum, since (2,500 − 2,400 + 45) × 400 = ₹58,000. If the share rises to ₹2,800, he gains ₹1,20,000 less the ₹18,000 premium, i.e., ₹1,02,000.

FAQs

Is a protective put the same as a stop-loss?

Both aim to limit losses, but they work differently. A stop-loss is free but may execute at a worse price in a gap-down and closes the position. A protective put costs premium but guarantees the right to sell at the strike until expiry, regardless of gaps, and lets you keep the shares if the price recovers.

How do I choose the strike for a protective put?

It depends on how much loss you can tolerate and how much premium you are willing to pay. An at-the-money put gives the most protection at the highest cost. An out-of-the-money put is cheaper but only protects after a larger fall. Compare the premium against the size of the protection gap.
