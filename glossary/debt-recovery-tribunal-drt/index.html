What is Debt Recovery Tribunal (DRT)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Debt Recovery Tribunal (DRT): Debt Recovery Tribunals are special quasi-judicial bodies set up to quickly decide cases filed by banks and financial institutions for recovery of debts above a set threshold.

DRTs were created under the Recovery of Debts Due to Banks and Financial Institutions Act, 1993, now renamed the Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act, 1993 (RDB Act). Before them, banks filed ordinary civil suits that could take decades. The minimum claim for a DRT is ₹20 lakh, raised from ₹10 lakh in 2018; smaller debts go to civil courts or other forums.

A DRT is headed by a Presiding Officer, usually a person qualified to be a District Judge. Appeals from DRT orders go to the Debt Recovery Appellate Tribunal (DRAT) within 30 days; to file an appeal, the borrower must usually deposit 50% of the amount determined by DRT, which the DRAT can reduce to 25%. Further challenges go to the High Court under its writ jurisdiction.

The process: the bank files an Original Application; the tribunal summons the defendant; after hearing, it issues a Recovery Certificate. A Recovery Officer then executes it by attaching and selling property, appointing receivers or even ordering arrest. The Act aims for disposal within 180 days, though actual timelines are often longer due to pending cases.

DRTs have additional roles. Borrowers appeal against SARFAESI actions to the DRT under Section 17 of SARFAESI. Under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, DRTs act as the adjudicating authority for insolvency of individuals and partnership firms, while the NCLT handles companies.

Example

A bank has an unsecured business loan claim of ₹65 lakh against a partnership firm. It files an Original Application in the DRT with jurisdiction. After hearing, the DRT issues a Recovery Certificate for ₹65 lakh plus interest. The firm appeals to DRAT and is told to deposit 50%, or ₹32.5 lakh; the DRAT can reduce this to 25%, ₹16.25 lakh. A claim of only ₹12 lakh could not go to DRT, being below the ₹20 lakh threshold.

FAQs

What is the minimum amount for filing a case in DRT?

Banks and financial institutions can approach the DRT for debts of ₹20 lakh and above. The central government raised this limit from ₹10 lakh in 2018 to reduce the load on tribunals. For smaller amounts, banks use civil courts, Lok Adalats or SARFAESI if the loan is secured and eligible.

Is DRT the same as NCLT?

No. DRT handles recovery suits by banks and financial institutions and insolvency of individuals and partnership firms under the IBC. The National Company Law Tribunal handles insolvency of companies and LLPs, plus company law matters. Appeals from DRT go to DRAT, and from NCLT to NCLAT.
