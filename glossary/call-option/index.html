What is Call Option?

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Call Option: A call option gives its buyer the right, but not the obligation, to buy the underlying asset at the strike price on or before expiry, in exchange for a premium.

A call buyer benefits when the underlying price rises above the strike price. At expiry, the call's value is the greater of zero and (spot price − strike price). The buyer's profit is that value minus the premium paid, so the breakeven point is strike price plus premium. Below the strike, the call expires worthless and the buyer loses only the premium.

The call seller (writer) receives the premium and must deliver the underlying (or pay the difference in cash-settled contracts) if the option is exercised. The seller profits if the price stays below the breakeven, with maximum gain equal to the premium. If the price rises sharply, losses for a naked call writer have no upper limit.

Calls are used by traders expecting a rise, by investors who want upside with limited downside, and by those who hold shares and write calls to earn extra income (covered calls). Fund managers sometimes buy calls when they want market exposure but have cash yet to be deployed.

A common confusion: a call buyer does not need the price to just rise — it must rise enough to cover the premium before expiry. Time decay works against the buyer every day. Also, 'call' has nothing to do with phoning a broker or with callable bonds; it only means the right to call away (buy) the asset.

Example

An index stands at 24,000. A trader buys a 24,200 call at a premium of 120 points, with an illustrative lot size of 75 (cost ₹9,000). Breakeven at expiry is 24,320. If the index settles at 24,500, the call is worth 300 points, giving a profit of (300 − 120) × 75 = ₹13,500. At 24,250 the call is worth 50, a loss of ₹5,250. Anywhere at or below 24,200, the full ₹9,000 is lost.

FAQs

What is the maximum loss for a call option buyer?

The premium paid. If the underlying stays at or below the strike price at expiry, the call expires worthless and the buyer loses the entire premium, but nothing more. This defined risk is why many beginners buy calls, though the probability of losing the premium can be high.

What is the breakeven point of a call option?

For the buyer, the breakeven at expiry is strike price plus premium paid. Only above this level does the buyer make a net profit. For example, a ₹500 strike call bought at ₹15 breaks even at ₹515, ignoring brokerage and taxes.
