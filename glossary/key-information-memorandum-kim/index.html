What is Key Information Memorandum (KIM)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Key Information Memorandum (KIM): A key information memorandum (KIM) is a short summary of a mutual fund scheme's offer document, which must accompany every application form.

Most investors will not read a hundred-page SID. SEBI therefore requires AMCs to provide a KIM, a condensed version with the essentials: investment objective, asset allocation, risk profile and riskometer, benchmark, plans and options, minimum amounts, loads, expense ratio, fund manager, and past performance compared with the benchmark.

The KIM is attached to physical application forms and is available online with digital applications. It is updated periodically so that performance and expense information stays current. Investor service details, such as the registrar's contact, are also included.

Because it is short, the KIM is the most practical document for a first look. Distributors are expected to give it to investors and ensure they understand the scheme's risk before investing.

A misunderstanding to avoid: the KIM summarises, but the SID and SAI prevail. If something in the KIM seems unclear, especially about loads, lock-ins or fundamental attributes, check the SID. Another mistake is reading the past performance table as an indication of future returns; mutual fund documents must say that past performance may or may not be sustained.

Example

Lakshmi is comparing two short duration funds. From their KIMs she notes Fund P has an expense ratio of 0.35% (direct), no exit load, and a potential risk class of moderate interest rate and moderate credit risk. Fund Q has 0.30% expense but allows up to 35% in lower-rated bonds and charges 0.5% exit load within 30 days. Ten minutes with the two KIMs shows the cheaper fund carries more credit risk.

FAQs

Is the KIM a legal document?

Yes. It is part of the scheme's offer documents required by SEBI and must be accurate and consistent with the SID. But it is a summary; in case of any conflict, the full SID and SAI prevail. It is designed to help investors make informed decisions quickly.

Where can I find the KIM of a mutual fund?

It is available on the AMC's website, usually alongside the SID and factsheet, and is attached to application forms. Distributors and online platforms also link to it. AMCs must keep the latest version accessible to investors.
