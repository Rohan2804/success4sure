What is Bank Guarantee?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Bank Guarantee: A bank guarantee is a written promise by a bank to pay a specified amount to a beneficiary if the bank's customer fails to meet a contractual obligation or payment.

Legally, a guarantee is a 'contract of guarantee' under Section 126 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872. There are three parties: the applicant or principal debtor (the bank's customer), the beneficiary (who receives the guarantee) and the guarantor (the bank). The bank charges a commission and usually takes security or margin money from its customer, because paying out under the guarantee creates a loan to the customer.

Types: a financial guarantee covers a payment obligation, for example that a buyer will pay a supplier. A performance guarantee covers completion of work or delivery, common in government contracts and construction. A bid bond (earnest money guarantee) assures that a bidder will sign the contract if selected. An advance payment guarantee protects a buyer who pays upfront. A deferred payment guarantee covers instalments for machinery bought on credit.

Most bank guarantees in India are unconditional: the bank must pay on the beneficiary's written demand within the validity and claim period, without investigating the underlying dispute. Courts intervene only in rare cases of egregious fraud or irretrievable injustice. Guarantees have a validity period plus a claim period, and Section 28 of the Contract Act allows a claim period of not less than one year.

Since a guarantee is not a funded loan, it is an off-balance sheet (non-fund based) exposure, but banks still hold capital against it. Exam trap: in a letter of credit the bank is the primary payer; in a bank guarantee it pays only if the customer defaults.

Example

A road contractor wins a ₹50 crore government project that needs a 5% performance guarantee. Its bank issues a guarantee of ₹2.5 crore in favour of the state PWD, valid for three years plus a one-year claim period. The bank charges 1.5% a year, about ₹3.75 lakh annually, and keeps a 20% margin (₹50 lakh) as fixed deposit. If the contractor abandons work, the PWD invokes the guarantee, the bank pays ₹2.5 crore, and the contractor now owes the bank.

FAQs

What is the difference between a bank guarantee and a letter of credit?

Under a letter of credit, the bank pays the seller when the seller presents documents showing it has fulfilled the terms, so the bank is the primary paymaster. Under a bank guarantee, the bank pays only if its customer fails to perform or pay. An LC facilitates a transaction; a guarantee protects against default.

Can a bank refuse to pay an invoked guarantee because the customer disputes it?

Generally no. An unconditional bank guarantee is an independent contract, and the bank must honour a valid demand made within the terms, regardless of disputes between the customer and beneficiary. Courts stop payment only in exceptional cases of established fraud known to the bank or irretrievable harm.
