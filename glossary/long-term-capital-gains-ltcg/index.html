What is Long-term capital gains (LTCG)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Long-term capital gains (LTCG): Long-term capital gain (LTCG) is the profit from selling a capital asset, such as shares, mutual fund units or property, after holding it longer than the period specified by tax law.

Whether a gain is long or short term depends on the holding period. As of 2026, listed equity shares and equity-oriented mutual funds become long term after 12 months; most other assets, including property and unlisted shares, after 24 months. Debt mutual fund units bought on or after 1 April 2023 are taxed at slab rate regardless of holding period, so the long-term label gives no benefit there.

For listed equity and equity-oriented funds, LTCG above an annual exemption (₹1.25 lakh per financial year as of 2026) is taxed at 12.5% plus cess, without indexation. Gains on equity bought before 1 February 2018 are 'grandfathered': the cost is taken as the higher of actual cost and the market value on 31 January 2018, subject to conditions. For most other long-term assets the rate is 12.5% without indexation, with transitional options for property bought before July 2024.

These rates changed in the July 2024 Budget, and the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act from April 2026 with renumbered sections. Always check the income tax department's current guidance.

Common confusion: the exemption applies to total equity LTCG across all holdings in a year, not per fund. Each SIP instalment has its own holding period.

Example

Meera sells equity fund units in FY 2025-26 bought three years earlier, making a gain of ₹3,00,000. She has no other equity LTCG that year. Taxable LTCG = ₹3,00,000 − ₹1,25,000 = ₹1,75,000. Tax at 12.5% = ₹21,875, plus 4% cess = ₹22,750. Had she spread redemptions over two financial years, she could have used the exemption twice. (Rates as of 2026; check current law.)

FAQs

Is LTCG on mutual funds tax-free up to ₹1.25 lakh?

For equity-oriented funds and listed shares, as of 2026, long-term gains up to ₹1.25 lakh in aggregate per financial year are exempt; tax at 12.5% applies above that. The exemption is per person per year across all such assets. Debt fund gains do not get this exemption. Check current law.

How is the holding period counted for SIPs?

Each SIP instalment is a separate purchase with its own date. When you redeem, units are taken on a first-in-first-out basis. Some units may be long term and others short term, so the gain is split accordingly.
