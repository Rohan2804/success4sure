What is Implied Volatility (IV) and India VIX?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Implied Volatility (IV) and India VIX: Implied volatility is the market's expectation of future volatility of an underlying, backed out from current option prices using an option pricing model.

An option pricing model takes the spot price, strike, time, interest rate, dividends and volatility to produce a price. All inputs except volatility are known. If we take the actual market price of an option and solve for the volatility that produces it, we get implied volatility (IV). It is expressed as an annualised percentage.

Historical volatility looks backward, measuring how much prices actually moved. IV looks forward, reflecting what option buyers and sellers collectively expect, including fear and event risk. Different strikes often show different IVs — the 'volatility smile' or 'skew' — with out-of-the-money puts often carrying higher IV because of demand for downside protection.

India VIX, published by NSE, is a volatility index computed from Nifty option prices. It represents the expected annualised volatility of the Nifty over the next 30 days. A VIX of 15 suggests the market expects the Nifty to move within roughly ±15% over a year with about 68% probability, under normal-distribution assumptions. VIX tends to rise when markets fall sharply.

IV is not a forecast of direction; it only measures the expected size of moves. Another misunderstanding is treating IV as a fixed property of an option — it changes continuously with demand and supply. High IV makes options expensive for buyers and attractive for sellers, but it is high for a reason.

Example

India VIX is at 15 and the Nifty is at 24,000. To estimate the one-month expected move, divide by √12: 15% ÷ 3.464 ≈ 4.33%. So one standard deviation over a month is about 24,000 × 4.33% ≈ 1,039 points — the market is pricing roughly a 68% chance that the Nifty stays between about 22,960 and 25,040, assuming a normal distribution.

FAQs

Is India VIX the same as implied volatility?

India VIX is an index derived from the implied volatilities of near- and next-month Nifty option prices across several strikes. It summarises expected Nifty volatility for the next 30 days. Individual stock or strike IVs can be higher or lower than VIX.

Does high implied volatility mean the market will fall?

Not necessarily. High IV means a large move is expected but says nothing about direction. That said, IV and VIX often rise during market falls because demand for protective puts increases. A rising VIX is a sign of uncertainty, not a prediction of direction.
