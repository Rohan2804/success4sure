What is Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF): Liquidity Adjustment Facility is RBI's framework of operations, mainly repo and reverse repo-type windows, used to inject or absorb liquidity in the banking system day to day.

RBI introduced the LAF in June 2000 on the recommendation of the Narasimham Committee (1998). Before that, liquidity was managed mainly through CRR changes and refinance. LAF made the repo rate the main policy rate and allowed RBI to fine-tune liquidity every day.

Today LAF has standing facilities and market operations. Standing facilities are available on demand: the Standing Deposit Facility (floor) and the Marginal Standing Facility (ceiling). Market operations include fixed-rate repo, variable rate repo (VRR) auctions to inject funds and variable rate reverse repo (VRRR) auctions to absorb funds. The repo rate sits in the middle of the corridor.

RBI's operating target is the weighted average call rate (WACR), the rate at which banks lend to each other overnight. RBI tries to keep WACR close to the repo rate. If liquidity is in surplus, WACR falls towards the SDF floor, and RBI runs VRRR auctions. If liquidity is in deficit, WACR rises towards the MSF ceiling, and RBI injects funds through VRR auctions or open market purchases.

Liquidity has two kinds of drivers. Frictional changes, such as tax payments or festival cash withdrawals, are handled through LAF. Durable changes, such as large foreign exchange inflows, are handled through open market operations, CRR changes or forex swaps. Exams often ask which tool fits which situation.

Example

Suppose repo is 5.50%, SDF 5.25% and MSF 5.75%, so the corridor is 50 basis points wide. In mid-March, advance tax payments move about ₹1.5 lakh crore from bank accounts to the government's account with RBI. Banks run short, and WACR climbs to 5.70%. RBI announces a 7-day VRR auction of ₹1 lakh crore; banks borrow, and WACR settles back near 5.50%.

FAQs

What is the LAF corridor?

It is the band between the SDF rate (floor) and the MSF rate (ceiling), with the repo rate in the middle. Overnight market rates are expected to stay inside it because banks will not lend below what RBI pays them, nor borrow above what RBI charges. The width is set by RBI and has typically been 50 basis points.

Who can participate in LAF?

Scheduled commercial banks (except RRBs), and certain other eligible entities such as scheduled urban co-operative and state co-operative banks, standalone primary dealers for some windows, as permitted by RBI. Individuals and ordinary NBFCs cannot directly access LAF.
