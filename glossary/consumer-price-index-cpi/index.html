What is Consumer Price Index (CPI)?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Consumer Price Index (CPI): The Consumer Price Index measures the change over time in retail prices of a fixed basket of goods and services bought by households, and is India's main inflation gauge.

The all-India CPI (Rural, Urban and Combined) is compiled by the National Statistics Office (NSO) under the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) and released monthly, around the 12th. Older series still exist for specific groups: CPI for Industrial Workers (CPI-IW) by the Labour Bureau, used for dearness allowance, and CPI for Agricultural and Rural Labourers (CPI-AL and CPI-RL).

The index compares the cost of a basket to its cost in a base year, which is set to 100. Items carry weights based on household consumption surveys. In the 2012-base series, food and beverages had the largest weight, close to half of the combined index, which is why vegetable and pulse prices move CPI so sharply. MoSPI has been revising the base year and weights using newer consumption survey data; check MoSPI for the current base and weights (as of 2026).

CPI Combined inflation is the official target for monetary policy since 2016. Unlike WPI, CPI includes services such as housing, education, health and transport, and uses retail prices, including taxes. That makes it closer to what people actually feel.

A frequent exam mix-up: CPI-IW is published by the Labour Bureau (Ministry of Labour), while CPI Combined is published by NSO. Another: CPI inflation is the year-on-year change in the index, not the index value itself.

Example

Suppose the CPI Combined index was 192.0 in October last year and 202.8 in October this year. Year-on-year inflation = (202.8 − 192.0)/192.0 × 100 = 5.625%, reported as 5.6%. If the index rose from 201.5 in September to 202.8 in October, the month-on-month change is about 0.65%, which is a different number and should not be confused with annual inflation.

FAQs

Why does RBI target CPI and not WPI?

CPI reflects the prices consumers actually pay, including services, so it better captures the cost of living and inflation expectations. WPI covers only goods at the wholesale stage and is heavily influenced by global commodity prices. The Urjit Patel committee in 2014 recommended CPI Combined as the nominal anchor, and the 2016 framework adopted it.

What is CPI-IW used for?

CPI for Industrial Workers, compiled by the Labour Bureau, is used to revise dearness allowance for central government employees and pensioners and for wage indexation in many industries and some public sector banks. It is not the measure used by RBI for its inflation target.
