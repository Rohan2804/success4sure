What is Anchor Investor?

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Anchor Investor: An anchor investor is a qualified institutional buyer who commits a large amount to a book-built IPO one working day before the issue opens to the public.

Anchor investors were introduced by SEBI to give an IPO a stable base of committed institutional money and to signal demand to other investors. Under the ICDR Regulations, an anchor investor must be a QIB and must apply for at least ₹10 crore. Up to 60% of the QIB portion can be allotted to anchors, and one-third of the anchor portion is reserved for domestic mutual funds.

The anchor book is opened a day before the public issue. The anchor price is fixed within the price band, and allotment is announced through an exchange filing listing each anchor and the number of shares. If the final issue price later turns out higher than the anchor price, anchors must pay the difference; if it is lower, they do not get a refund.

To discourage quick flipping, anchor shares carry a lock-in: 50% of the shares cannot be sold for 30 days from allotment and the remaining 50% for 90 days (as of 2026; confirm in current ICDR rules). These lock-in expiry dates are often followed by heavy selling and price moves, which traders watch.

A misconception is that strong anchor participation guarantees listing gains. Anchors take a view based on their own horizon and allocation needs; anchors in many issues have seen listing prices below the issue price. Retail investors should still read the RHP rather than rely only on anchor names.

Example

An IPO's QIB portion is ₹600 crore. Up to 60%, or ₹360 crore, can go to anchors, of which one-third (₹120 crore) is reserved for domestic mutual funds. Say 18 anchors are allotted at ₹410 per share. Each must invest at least ₹10 crore. One fund receiving 3,65,853 shares (≈₹15 crore) can sell 1,82,926 shares after 30 days and the rest only after 90 days.

FAQs

Is the anchor portion part of the QIB quota?

Yes. The anchor allocation comes out of the QIB portion, not in addition to it. Any shares not taken by anchors are added back to the QIB category for the public bidding period. So when you see the QIB subscription number, remember it often refers to the non-anchor part of the QIB book.

Can an anchor investor withdraw its bid?

No. Anchor investors cannot withdraw or lower their bids after the anchor bidding day, which is what makes their commitment meaningful. Like other QIBs and non-institutional investors in an IPO, they are also not allowed to revise bids downward during the issue.
