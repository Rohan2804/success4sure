What is Payments Bank?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Payments Bank: A payments bank is a differentiated bank licensed by RBI to accept small deposits and provide payments and remittance services, but which is not allowed to give loans.

Payments banks were also an idea of the Nachiket Mor Committee, meant to bring basic banking to migrant workers, low-income households and small businesses using technology and agent networks. RBI issued guidelines in November 2014 and gave in-principle approval to 11 applicants in 2015. Operational examples include Airtel Payments Bank, India Post Payments Bank, Fino Payments Bank, Jio Payments Bank and NSDL Payments Bank.

Permitted activities: savings and current accounts with a maximum end-of-day balance of ₹2 lakh per customer (raised from ₹1 lakh in 2021); debit cards and ATM cards; payments and remittances through UPI, IMPS, NEFT and AePS; bill payments; and distribution of mutual funds, insurance and pension products. They can act as business correspondents of other banks. Not permitted: any lending, credit cards, or accepting NRI deposits.

Because they cannot lend, payments banks must hold at least 75% of demand deposit balances in SLR-eligible government securities and treasury bills of up to one year maturity, and can keep at most 25% in current and time deposits with other scheduled banks. Their minimum paid-up capital is ₹100 crore, and capital adequacy is set at 15%. Earnings come from fees, float income and distribution.

Payments banks are regulated like other banks on KYC and compliance. In 2024 RBI barred Paytm Payments Bank from accepting fresh deposits over persistent non-compliance, a reminder that regulatory obligations apply in full.

Example

A worker in Surat keeps his wages in a payments bank account linked to UPI. At the end of a day his balance reaches ₹2.3 lakh after a bonus. Since the limit is ₹2 lakh, the excess ₹30,000 is swept automatically into a partner bank's account if he has opted for that facility. Of the payments bank's ₹1,000 crore demand deposits, at least ₹750 crore must be in government securities or T-bills of up to one year.

FAQs

Can a payments bank give loans?

No. Payments banks are prohibited from lending in any form, including credit cards. They can, however, distribute loan products of other banks or NBFCs as an agent, with the partner taking the credit risk. This restriction is the defining feature that separates them from small finance banks and universal banks.

Are deposits in a payments bank insured?

Yes. Payments banks are banks under the Banking Regulation Act, and their deposits are insured by DICGC up to ₹5 lakh per depositor. Since the deposit limit itself is ₹2 lakh per customer, a depositor's balance in a payments bank will normally be fully covered.
