What is Insurable Interest?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Insurable Interest: Insurable interest is the legally recognised financial interest a person has in the life or property being insured, such that its loss or damage would cause that person a financial loss.

Insurable interest is what separates insurance from a bet. Without it, anyone could insure a stranger's house or life and profit from its loss, which would be gambling and create a motive for harm. Every valid insurance contract needs it, along with the subject matter of insurance and premium as consideration.

In life insurance, a person has unlimited insurable interest in their own life. A husband and wife have insurable interest in each other's lives. For other relatives, such as parent and child or siblings, the law looks for a financial dependence or pecuniary interest rather than mere relationship, though insurers routinely allow a parent to propose a policy on a minor child's life. A creditor has insurable interest in a debtor's life up to the debt, an employer in a key employee's life (keyman insurance), and partners in each other's lives.

Timing differs by type. In life insurance, insurable interest must exist at the time of taking the policy; it need not exist at the time of claim. So a divorced spouse's policy remains valid. In fire and most property insurance, it must exist both at inception and at the time of loss. In marine insurance, it must exist at the time of loss, because cargo changes hands during the voyage.

Common confusion: insurable interest is about financial loss, not emotional attachment. A friend may be very close, but without a financial stake he cannot insure your life.

Example

Arjun, a trader, sells goods worth ₹8 lakh on credit to Bimal and takes a policy on Bimal's life for ₹8 lakh, since Bimal's death could mean losing the debt. This is valid. He cannot insure Bimal for ₹50 lakh, as his interest is only ₹8 lakh. Separately, Arjun's warehouse with stock worth ₹30 lakh is insured for fire; if he sells the warehouse in June and a fire occurs in July, he cannot claim because he no longer has insurable interest at the time of loss.

FAQs

When must insurable interest exist in life insurance?

In life insurance, insurable interest must exist at the time the policy is taken. It does not have to exist at the time of the claim. For example, if a wife insures her husband and they later divorce, the policy remains valid and the claim is payable if the insured event occurs.

Does a person have insurable interest in a friend's life?

Generally no, because a friend's death does not cause a recognised financial loss. Emotional closeness is not enough. However, if the friend owes money or is a business partner, there can be insurable interest limited to the financial stake, such as the debt amount or partnership interest.
