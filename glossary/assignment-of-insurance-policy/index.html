What is Assignment of Insurance Policy (Section 38)?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Assignment of Insurance Policy (Section 38): Assignment is the legal transfer of the rights, title and interest in a life insurance policy from the policyholder (assignor) to another person or institution (assignee), under Section 38 of the Insurance Act.

Assignment is usually done when a policy is used as security for a loan, or to gift the benefits to someone. It can be done by an endorsement on the policy or by a separate instrument, signed by the assignor and attested by at least one witness, and it must state the fact of transfer and reasons. The assignment takes effect against the insurer only when written notice is given to the insurer, which then registers it.

Types: an absolute assignment transfers all rights permanently to the assignee, who can then surrender the policy, take a loan or receive claims. A conditional assignment transfers rights subject to a condition, typically that the rights revert to the assignor if he survives to maturity, or that the policy is reassigned when a loan is repaid. Banks commonly take conditional assignment of policies as collateral for home loans.

Key points from the 2015 amendment: the insurer may decline to act on an assignment if it believes it is not bona fide, not in the policyholder's interest, or for trafficking in policies, giving reasons in writing; the decision can be appealed to IRDAI within 30 days. An assignment automatically cancels an existing nomination, except an assignment to the insurer itself for a policy loan.

Difference from nomination: nomination only decides who receives money on death and gives no rights during the policyholder's lifetime; assignment transfers ownership of the rights immediately.

Example

Neha takes a ₹40 lakh home loan and conditionally assigns her ₹50 lakh term policy to the bank. The bank registers the assignment with the insurer, and her earlier nomination of her husband is cancelled. If she dies with ₹32 lakh of loan outstanding, the insurer pays ₹50 lakh: ₹32 lakh to the bank and, after reassignment, the remaining ₹18 lakh as per her estate or fresh nomination. Once she repays the loan, the bank reassigns the policy and she can nominate again.

FAQs

What happens to nomination when a policy is assigned?

Under Section 39(7) of the Insurance Act, an assignment automatically cancels any existing nomination. The exception is an assignment to the insurer itself as security for a policy loan, in which case the nomination is affected only to the extent of the insurer's interest. After reassignment, the policyholder should make a fresh nomination.

Can an insurer refuse to register an assignment?

Yes, since the 2015 amendment the insurer may refuse if it has sufficient reason to believe the assignment is not bona fide, not in the policyholder's interest, against public policy, or meant for trading in policies. It must record its reasons in writing, and the policyholder can appeal to IRDAI within 30 days.
