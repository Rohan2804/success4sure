What is Asset allocation?

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Asset allocation: Asset allocation is the decision of how to divide a portfolio among asset classes such as equity, debt, gold, real estate and cash, based on goals and risk profile.

The mix of assets has a much bigger effect on how much a portfolio swings than the choice of individual funds within each class. A portfolio with 80% equity will behave very differently from one with 30% equity, whichever specific funds are chosen. That is why advisers decide asset allocation first and pick products second.

Different asset classes respond differently to the same events. Equity tends to do well when growth is strong, high-quality bonds often hold up when equity falls, and gold has historically helped during currency weakness or crises. Combining them can reduce overall volatility without proportionately reducing returns.

There are many methods. Rules of thumb such as '100 minus age in equity' are simple starting points. Goal-based allocation assigns each goal its own mix depending on time horizon: a goal three years away needs mostly debt, a goal twenty years away can carry more equity. Strategic allocation sets a long-term mix; tactical allocation makes temporary tilts.

Two mistakes are common: thinking that owning ten equity funds is diversification when they all hold similar stocks, and letting allocation drift without rebalancing after a strong rally.

Example

Priya, 35, has ₹20 lakh. Her adviser suggests a strategic allocation of 60% equity, 30% debt and 10% gold: ₹12 lakh in a mix of index and flexi cap funds, ₹6 lakh in short duration and PPF-type debt, and ₹2 lakh in a gold fund. If equity falls 25%, debt gains 6% and gold rises 10% in a bad year, the portfolio falls about 15% − 1.8% − 1% = roughly 12.2% (₹2.44 lakh), less than the 25% an all-equity portfolio would lose.

FAQs

What is the ideal asset allocation by age?

There is no single ideal. Rules like '100 minus age in equity' give a rough start, but goals, income stability, existing assets and risk tolerance matter more. A 50-year-old with a large pension may hold more equity than a 30-year-old with an unstable income.

Is asset allocation more important than fund selection?

For overall risk, generally yes. The proportion in equity versus debt largely determines how much the portfolio moves. Fund selection affects returns within each asset class and still matters, especially on costs, but it rarely offsets a poorly chosen allocation.
