What is Standing Deposit Facility (SDF)?

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Standing Deposit Facility (SDF): The Standing Deposit Facility is an RBI window where banks deposit surplus funds, usually overnight, and earn interest without receiving any collateral from RBI.

RBI introduced the SDF in April 2022 using powers under Section 17 of the RBI Act, as amended in 2018. The idea had been recommended by the Urjit Patel committee in 2014. Unlike reverse repo, RBI does not have to give the bank government securities in return, so RBI can absorb any amount of surplus money even when its own stock of securities is limited.

The SDF rate is set at 25 basis points below the repo rate and acts as the floor of the liquidity adjustment facility (LAF) corridor. The MSF rate, 25 basis points above repo, is the ceiling. Because the floor moves automatically with the repo rate, every policy rate change shifts the whole corridor. The width and the spreads can be revised by RBI, so confirm current figures (as of 2026).

Banks use SDF at the end of the day when they have cash left after meeting their cash reserve ratio and other needs. Balances kept under SDF are not counted towards CRR, but RBI has allowed them to be counted as an eligible asset for the liquidity coverage ratio. The window is available every day, including weekends and holidays, and banks can access it at their discretion, which is what 'standing' means.

The key exam point is the purpose: SDF is a liquidity absorption tool, not a lending tool. It also helps keep the overnight call money rate (the weighted average call rate, RBI's operating target) from falling too far below the repo rate when liquidity is in surplus.

Example

Assume repo is 5.50%, so SDF is 5.25% and MSF is 5.75%. A bank with ₹2,000 crore surplus on a Friday parks it under SDF until Monday (3 days). Interest = 2,000 × 5.25% × 3/365 = about ₹0.863 crore, or roughly ₹86.3 lakh. RBI gives no securities in return; the bank's deposit is simply credited back with interest on Monday.

FAQs

Why did RBI need SDF when reverse repo already existed?

Reverse repo needs RBI to hand over government securities as collateral. During the large liquidity surplus after 2020, absorbing huge sums that way strained RBI's stock of securities. SDF removes the collateral requirement, so RBI can absorb surplus money without limit and without disturbing its balance sheet holdings of bonds.

Is SDF the same as a fixed deposit with RBI?

Loosely, yes, but only banks and other eligible institutions can use it, and it is mainly overnight. Individuals cannot deposit with RBI under SDF. The rate is tied to the repo rate rather than negotiated, and the balance is not counted towards the bank's cash reserve ratio.
