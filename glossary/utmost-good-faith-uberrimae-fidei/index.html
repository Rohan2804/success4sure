What is Utmost Good Faith (Uberrimae Fidei)?

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Utmost Good Faith (Uberrimae Fidei): Utmost good faith, or uberrimae fidei, is the principle that both parties to an insurance contract must voluntarily disclose all material facts fully and accurately, whether asked or not.

Ordinary commercial contracts follow 'caveat emptor' (let the buyer beware), where each side looks after itself. Insurance is different because the proposer knows far more about the risk than the insurer: their health, habits, past illnesses, or the condition of their property. So the law imposes a higher duty of disclosure on both sides.

A material fact is any fact that would influence a prudent insurer's decision to accept the risk or fix the premium. Examples: age, existing diseases, past surgeries, smoking and alcohol habits, hazardous occupation, existing insurance policies, and for property, the nature of construction and goods stored. Facts that reduce risk, facts the insurer already knows, and matters of common knowledge need not be disclosed.

Breach can be non-disclosure (silence about a material fact), misrepresentation (an inaccurate statement), or concealment (deliberate hiding). The insurer may avoid the contract if the breach is material. However, under Section 45 of the Insurance Act, a life policy cannot be questioned on any ground after three years from issue or revival, which limits how long this defence is available.

The duty applies to the insurer too: it must explain policy terms, exclusions and charges clearly and not hide important conditions. The duty is strongest at proposal stage and continues at renewal and revival.

Example

Ramesh, 42, applies for a ₹1 crore term plan and does not disclose that he has been on diabetes medication for four years. The insurer issues the policy at a standard premium of ₹22,000. He dies of a cardiac event 18 months later. During claim investigation, hospital records show the diabetes history. Because this was a material non-disclosure within three years, the insurer can repudiate the claim under Section 45, giving written reasons. Disclosure might have meant a loaded premium, perhaps ₹30,000, but a valid policy.

FAQs

What is a material fact in insurance?

A material fact is any information that would influence an insurer's decision on whether to accept a risk, on what terms, or at what premium. Examples include pre-existing illnesses, smoking, risky occupations and previous claim history. If in doubt, the safer course is to disclose, because non-disclosure can lead to claim rejection.

Does utmost good faith apply to the insurer as well?

Yes. The insurer must also act in utmost good faith by disclosing policy terms, exclusions, charges and benefits clearly, and must not mislead the proposer. IRDAI's policyholder protection regulations require insurers to provide key information documents and explain features before sale.
