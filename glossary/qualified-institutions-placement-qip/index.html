What is Qualified Institutions Placement (QIP)?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Qualified Institutions Placement (QIP): A qualified institutions placement is a fund-raising route in which a listed company issues shares or convertible securities only to qualified institutional buyers.

SEBI introduced the QIP route in 2006 so Indian listed companies could raise capital from domestic institutions quickly, instead of going abroad through depository receipts. A QIP needs shareholder approval by special resolution, but unlike an FPO it does not require a full offer document to be reviewed by SEBI — a placement document is prepared and filed with the exchanges.

Pricing follows a formula in the ICDR Regulations. The floor price is the average of the weekly high and low closing prices over the two weeks before the 'relevant date' (the date the board or committee decides to open the issue). The issuer may offer a discount of up to 5% on this floor price with shareholder approval.

Allotment rules ensure the issue is genuinely spread among institutions: at least 10% must go to mutual funds where available, a minimum number of allottees is required depending on issue size, and no single allottee can receive more than 50% of the issue. Promoters and their related parties cannot participate.

QIPs are popular with banks, NBFCs and growth companies because they can be launched and closed within days. The downside for existing retail shareholders is dilution without the chance to participate, unlike a rights issue.

Example

On the relevant date, a bank's two-week average of weekly high and low closing prices is ₹520. The floor price is ₹520; with a 5% discount, the issue price can be as low as ₹494. Raising ₹3,000 crore at ₹494 means issuing about 6.07 crore new shares. If the bank had 150 crore shares, the dilution is about 3.9%.

FAQs

Can retail investors take part in a QIP?

No. A QIP is restricted to qualified institutional buyers such as mutual funds, insurers, banks and eligible foreign portfolio investors. Retail investors can buy the shares afterwards in the secondary market once they are listed, but they cannot apply in the placement itself.

What is the difference between a QIP and an FPO?

An FPO is a public offer open to all categories of investors and requires a full offer document. A QIP is a private placement to QIBs only, uses a shorter placement document and is priced using SEBI's formula. QIPs are faster and cheaper, which is why listed companies prefer them.
