What is Insurance Premium?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Insurance Premium: An insurance premium is the amount a policyholder pays the insurer, once or at regular intervals, in exchange for the insurer's promise to pay benefits on the insured event.

Premium is the consideration that makes an insurance contract valid. In life insurance, it is built from three elements. The risk premium covers the expected cost of claims, based on mortality tables (Indian insurers use the Indian Assured Lives Mortality table). Interest is credited because premiums are collected in advance and invested. Loading is added for the insurer's expenses, commissions, contingencies and profit. The result is the office premium, the amount the customer actually pays.

Factors that raise a life premium: higher age at entry, larger sum assured, longer term, smoking or tobacco use, health conditions, hazardous occupations and adverse family history. In general insurance, premium depends on the value and risk of the subject matter: for motor, the vehicle's insured declared value, cubic capacity and claim history; for health, age, city, sum insured and plan features.

Payment modes include single premium, yearly, half-yearly, quarterly and monthly. Yearly payment is cheapest overall because insurers charge a modal loading for more frequent payments, and some give a rebate for large sum assured. Level premium means the same premium every year even though risk rises with age; the excess paid in early years builds a reserve that funds later years.

Exam pointers: premium in advance is a basic principle under Section 64VB of the Insurance Act for general insurance, where cover starts only after premium is received. Indirect tax on premium depends on product type; individual life and health policies were exempted from GST from September 2025. Premium paid is not refundable once risk has run, except as policy terms allow.

Example

Two 30-year-olds buy a ₹1 crore, 30-year term plan. The non-smoker pays an annual premium of, say, ₹12,000; the smoker pays ₹19,500, about 62% more, for the same cover. If the non-smoker chooses monthly payments of ₹1,050, the yearly total becomes ₹12,600, which is ₹600 (5%) more than paying annually, because of the modal loading. Over 30 years, that small difference adds up to ₹18,000.

FAQs

Why is the premium for smokers higher?

Smokers and tobacco users have statistically higher mortality and illness rates, so the expected cost of claims is higher. Insurers price this extra risk through higher rates. Concealing tobacco use to get a lower premium is a breach of utmost good faith and can lead to claim rejection within the period allowed under Section 45.

Is GST charged on life insurance premium?

From 22 September 2025 the GST Council exempted premiums on individual life and individual health insurance policies, including term plans. Group policies and most general insurance, such as motor and fire, still attract GST. Rates and exemptions are set by the GST Council and can change, so check the current position (as of 2026).
