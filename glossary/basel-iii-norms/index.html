What is Basel III Norms?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Basel III Norms: Basel III is a set of international banking standards from the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision that requires banks to hold more and better-quality capital and liquidity to absorb losses.

The Basel Committee, hosted by the Bank for International Settlements in Basel, Switzerland, issued Basel I (1988) on credit risk capital, Basel II (2004) with three pillars, and Basel III (2010) after the global financial crisis exposed thin capital and weak liquidity. India began implementing Basel III in April 2013 in phases.

The three pillars are: Pillar 1, minimum capital requirements for credit, market and operational risk; Pillar 2, supervisory review, where banks assess their overall risks (ICAAP) and supervisors can require more capital; and Pillar 3, market discipline through public disclosures.

India's Basel III requirements are stricter than the global minimum. Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) of 5.5% of risk-weighted assets, Tier 1 capital of 7%, and total capital (CRAR) of 9%, plus a Capital Conservation Buffer (CCB) of 2.5% in CET1, making 11.5% overall. Domestic systemically important banks (D-SIBs) like SBI, HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank carry an extra CET1 surcharge. A countercyclical buffer can be activated in boom times. These figures are as of 2026; check RBI's Master Circular.

Basel III also added liquidity standards, the Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) and Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR), both at 100%, and a leverage ratio (4% for D-SIBs and 3.5% for other banks in India) to cap borrowing irrespective of risk weights.

Example

A bank has risk-weighted assets of ₹1,00,000 crore. It needs CET1 of 5.5% + 2.5% CCB = ₹8,000 crore, Tier 1 of 7% + 2.5% = ₹9,500 crore, and total capital of 11.5% = ₹11,500 crore. If it holds CET1 of ₹9,000 crore, AT1 bonds of ₹1,000 crore and Tier 2 of ₹2,500 crore, total capital is ₹12,500 crore, or 12.5%, comfortably above the minimum.

FAQs

Why are India's Basel III norms stricter than global ones?

RBI set total capital at 9% against the Basel minimum of 8%, and CET1 at 5.5% against 4.5%. The higher bar reflects RBI's conservative approach given India's history of bad loans and the importance of public confidence in banks. Extra buffers on top provide further protection.

What is the Capital Conservation Buffer?

It is an extra 2.5% of risk-weighted assets that banks must hold in common equity above the minimum. In good times it builds a cushion. If a bank dips into the buffer, it faces restrictions on dividends and bonus payouts until it rebuilds capital, which conserves capital during stress.
