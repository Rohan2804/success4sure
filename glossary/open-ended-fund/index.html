What is Open-ended fund?

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Open-ended fund: An open-ended fund is a mutual fund scheme that issues and redeems units on every business day at NAV, with no fixed maturity or limit on unit capital.

Most mutual fund schemes in India are open-ended. After the new fund offer closes, the scheme reopens within a short period for continuous sale and repurchase. When an investor buys, new units are created; when an investor redeems, units are cancelled. The size of the fund therefore expands and shrinks with investor flows.

Liquidity comes directly from the AMC. You do not need a buyer on an exchange; the fund itself pays you at the applicable NAV, usually within a few working days for equity schemes (SEBI sets maximum timelines) and faster for liquid funds. There is no maturity date, so you can hold for decades.

The flip side is that the fund manager must keep some cash and liquid securities to meet redemptions. Heavy outflows during a market fall can force selling at poor prices, which hurts remaining investors. Exit loads and, in extreme cases, SEBI-permitted restrictions on redemption help manage this.

Open-ended does not mean free of lock-in: ELSS is open-ended yet each unit is locked for three years. Nor does it mean units trade on an exchange; that is the feature of ETFs and close-ended funds.

Example

A large cap fund has 10 crore units and NAV ₹30, so AUM is ₹300 crore. On Monday investors buy ₹6 crore and redeem ₹3 crore at that day's NAV of ₹30. The fund issues 20 lakh new units and cancels 10 lakh units, ending with 10.1 crore units and AUM of ₹303 crore. Nothing had to trade on a stock exchange.

FAQs

Can an open-ended fund stop redemptions?

Only in exceptional situations and as permitted by SEBI, for example severe market disruption or liquidity crisis, and subject to conditions such as trustee approval and time limits. In normal conditions an open-ended fund must honour redemptions at NAV within the prescribed time.

Is an open-ended fund better than a close-ended fund?

Open-ended funds offer daily liquidity and the option to invest through SIPs, which suits most retail investors. Close-ended funds lock money for a fixed term, which can help managers invest in less liquid securities. Which suits you depends on your need for liquidity and the strategy.
