What is Diversification?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Diversification: Diversification means spreading investments across different securities, sectors and asset classes so that poor performance of one holding has a limited effect on the whole portfolio.

The idea is old: don't put all your eggs in one basket. If you own one stock and the company faces fraud or a bad product, you can lose most of your money. If you own fifty stocks across sectors, one failure hurts much less. Risk specific to individual companies or sectors, called unsystematic or diversifiable risk, falls quickly as holdings increase.

What diversification cannot remove is systematic or market risk, the risk that affects all stocks together, such as a recession or a global sell-off. To reduce that, investors diversify across asset classes that behave differently, such as equity, high-quality debt and gold.

Mutual funds provide diversification at low cost; a single index fund holds dozens of companies. But many investors 'over-diversify' by holding a dozen equity funds that own the same large caps, adding complexity without reducing risk. Checking portfolio overlap across funds helps.

In severe crises, correlations between risky assets often rise and many assets fall together, so diversification helps less than in normal times. That is a reason to include genuinely different assets, not just more of the same.

Example

Ajay holds ₹5 lakh in one bank stock. A governance problem hits the bank and the share falls 50%, a ₹2.5 lakh loss. His friend Sanjay holds ₹5 lakh spread across a Nifty 50 index fund, in which that bank is about 4% of the index. The same event costs Sanjay roughly 4% × 50% × ₹5 lakh = ₹10,000, a 2% dent, assuming the rest of the index is unchanged.

FAQs

Can diversification eliminate all risk?

No. It reduces company-specific and sector-specific risk but not market risk, which affects almost all investments in a downturn. Diversifying across asset classes like debt and gold helps further, but no portfolio is free of risk.

How many mutual funds are enough for diversification?

There is no fixed number, but a few well-chosen funds across categories and asset classes are often enough. Several funds in the same category usually overlap heavily. Check what the funds hold rather than how many funds you own.
