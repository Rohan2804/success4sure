What is Section 80C deduction?

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Section 80C deduction: Section 80C is the income tax provision under which individuals and HUFs can reduce taxable income by up to ₹1.5 lakh a year through specified investments and expenses, under the old tax regime.

Eligible items include employee contribution to EPF, PPF deposits, ELSS mutual funds, life insurance premiums (within limits relative to sum assured), the principal portion of home loan EMIs, tuition fees for up to two children, five-year tax-saver bank fixed deposits, National Savings Certificates, Sukanya Samriddhi deposits and the Senior Citizens' Savings Scheme. The combined deduction is capped at ₹1.5 lakh a year as of 2026; NPS contributions under related sections share part of this cap.

The deduction is available only under the old tax regime. The new regime, which is the default, does not allow it. Whether 80C matters to you depends on which regime gives lower tax after considering all deductions.

Lock-ins vary widely: three years for ELSS, five years for tax-saver FDs, fifteen years for PPF, and until retirement or job change for EPF. Many salaried people find EPF and home loan principal already use most of the limit, so further 80C purchases add nothing. The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026 and renumbers sections, so the familiar section name may now map to a new reference; check the income tax department's current guidance.

The most common mistake is buying insurance-linked savings products in March just to save tax, locking money into low-return plans that do not suit the goal.

Example

Neha, in the old regime with taxable income in the 30% slab, has EPF deductions of ₹72,000 and home loan principal of ₹40,000, totalling ₹1,12,000. She can claim only ₹38,000 more under 80C. Investing ₹38,000 in ELSS or PPF saves about ₹38,000 × 31.2% = ₹11,856 in tax. Investing ₹1 lakh more would not save any additional tax because the cap is reached.

FAQs

Can I claim 80C under the new tax regime?

No. As of 2026, the new regime does not allow Section 80C deductions; it offers lower slab rates and a higher standard deduction instead. If your 80C and other deductions are large, the old regime may still be better. Compare both each year.

What is the maximum limit under Section 80C?

The limit is ₹1.5 lakh per financial year for individuals and HUFs as of 2026, combined across all eligible investments and expenses. Additional deductions such as 80CCD(1B) for NPS and 80D for health insurance are separate. Check current law, as limits and section numbers can change.
