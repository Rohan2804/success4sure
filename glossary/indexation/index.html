What is Indexation?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Indexation: Indexation is the adjustment of an asset's purchase cost for inflation using the government's Cost Inflation Index (CII), which reduces the taxable long-term capital gain.

Inflation means part of any long-term gain is not a real gain at all. Indexation addresses this by raising the purchase cost in line with inflation. Indexed cost = original cost × (CII of the year of sale ÷ CII of the year of purchase). The CII is notified by the Central Board of Direct Taxes each year, with 2001-02 as the base year at 100.

Until July 2024, indexation was available for many long-term assets, including debt mutual funds bought before April 2023, gold, and property, usually with a 20% tax rate on the indexed gain. The July 2024 Budget removed indexation for most assets and moved to a flat 12.5% rate without indexation.

A transitional choice remains for land or buildings acquired before 23 July 2024 by resident individuals and HUFs: they can pay the lower of 12.5% without indexation or 20% with indexation. Indexation still matters in exams, in older cases and in this property option. The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026 and renumbers sections, so the familiar section name may now map to a new reference; check the income tax department's current guidance.

A common confusion: indexation never applied to listed equity shares or equity-oriented mutual funds, which have their own LTCG regime.

Example

Kiran bought a gold investment for ₹1,00,000 in FY 2015-16 (CII 254) and sold it for ₹1,60,000 in FY 2023-24 (CII 348), under the old rules. Indexed cost = ₹1,00,000 × 348 ÷ 254 ≈ ₹1,37,008. Indexed LTCG = ₹1,60,000 − ₹1,37,008 = ₹22,992. At 20%, tax was about ₹4,598 before cess. Without indexation at the current 12.5% on the full ₹60,000 gain, tax would be ₹7,500, illustrating why the change matters.

FAQs

Is indexation still available on debt mutual funds?

For debt fund units bought on or after 1 April 2023, gains are taxed at slab rate without indexation. For older units, the July 2024 changes removed indexation and set a flat 12.5% on long-term gains from that date. Transitional rules can be complex, so check current law.

What is the Cost Inflation Index?

The CII is a number notified by the CBDT each financial year to reflect inflation. With 2001-02 as base 100, it is used to adjust the purchase cost of assets when indexation is allowed. For example, the CII for FY 2023-24 is 348 and for FY 2024-25 is 363.
