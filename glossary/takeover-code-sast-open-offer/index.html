What is Takeover Code (SAST) and Open Offer?

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Takeover Code (SAST) and Open Offer: The takeover code, SEBI's Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers (SAST) Regulations, 2011, requires an acquirer crossing set thresholds to make an open offer to public shareholders.

The idea is fairness: when control of a listed company changes or someone builds a large stake, public shareholders should get a chance to exit at a fair price. An acquirer who, alone or with persons acting in concert, acquires 25% or more of voting rights must make an open offer. Acquiring control, even without crossing 25%, also triggers an offer.

Holders already between 25% and the maximum permissible non-public shareholding (75%) can acquire up to 5% more voting rights in a financial year without an open offer — the creeping acquisition limit. Beyond that, an open offer is required. The open offer must be for at least 26% of the total shares of the company.

The offer price must be at least the highest of several benchmarks, including the negotiated price under the agreement that triggered the offer, the volume-weighted average price paid by the acquirer in the preceding 52 weeks, the highest price paid in the preceding 26 weeks, and the 60-trading-day volume-weighted average market price for frequently traded shares. A merchant banker manages the offer and an escrow account secures payment.

The code also requires disclosures: anyone crossing 5% must disclose, and further changes of 2% or more must be disclosed. Promoters must disclose their holdings and encumbrances. A misconception is that an open offer means the acquirer must buy all public shares — the minimum is 26%, and acceptance can be proportionate if more is tendered.

Example

Company X has 10 crore shares. An acquirer buys 26% (2.6 crore shares) from the promoter at ₹180 per share, crossing 25%. It must make an open offer for at least another 26% — 2.6 crore shares — at no less than the highest benchmark price, say ₹192 (the 60-day VWAP). If public shareholders tender 4 crore shares, acceptance is proportionate at 65%; if all goes through, the acquirer ends at 52%.

FAQs

What is creeping acquisition?

Creeping acquisition lets a shareholder who already holds 25% or more, but less than the maximum permissible non-public shareholding, acquire up to 5% additional voting rights in a financial year without making an open offer. It allows gradual consolidation while still requiring an open offer for faster or larger acquisitions.

Do I have to tender my shares in an open offer?

No, participation is voluntary. Shareholders can tender all, some or none of their shares. If more shares are tendered than the offer size, the acquirer accepts them proportionately. Shareholders who do not tender continue to hold their shares in a company with a new or stronger controlling shareholder.
