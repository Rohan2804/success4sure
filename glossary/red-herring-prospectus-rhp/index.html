What is Red Herring Prospectus (RHP)?

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Red Herring Prospectus (RHP): A red herring prospectus is the offer document for a book-built issue that contains full disclosures about the company but not the final issue price or exact number of shares.

The name comes from the practice of printing a warning on the cover in red, signalling that some details are incomplete. In a book-built IPO the price is discovered only after bidding, so the document filed before the issue opens cannot state it. It does state the price band (announced separately before opening), the issue size in rupees or shares, and all other disclosures required under SEBI's ICDR Regulations.

The sequence is: Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP) filed with SEBI for observations and made public for comments → Red Herring Prospectus (RHP) filed with the Registrar of Companies before the issue opens → final Prospectus filed after the price is fixed, adding the issue price and the number of shares.

For investors, the RHP is the single most useful document. Key sections are risk factors, objects of the issue (how the money will be used), the offer structure, financial statements, outstanding litigation, promoter and group company details, and the basis for issue price, which compares valuation ratios with listed peers.

A common mistake is to read only media summaries or the abridged prospectus attached to forms. The abridged version is useful but short; contingent liabilities, related-party transactions and restated financial notes are often only in the full RHP, available on SEBI, exchange and lead-manager websites.

Example

A company files its DRHP in January proposing a ₹1,200 crore IPO: ₹800 crore fresh issue and ₹400 crore OFS. After SEBI observations, it files the RHP in May, and two days before opening announces a band of ₹440–₹463. The issue is subscribed and priced at ₹463, so the final prospectus records the fresh issue as about 1.73 crore shares (₹800 crore ÷ ₹463 ≈ 1,72,78,618 shares).

FAQs

Is a DRHP approved by SEBI?

SEBI does not 'approve' an offer document or vouch for the company. It issues observations on whether disclosures are adequate, and the issuer must address them before filing the RHP. Every offer document carries a disclaimer saying SEBI does not take responsibility for the financial soundness of the project or correctness of statements.

Why is it called a red herring prospectus?

The term comes from US practice, where the preliminary prospectus carried a cautionary statement printed in red ink on the cover. It warned that the document was not final and that pricing and some details would be added later. The name stuck, and Indian law uses it for book-built issues.
