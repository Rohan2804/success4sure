What is Section 80CCD(1B) NPS deduction?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Section 80CCD(1B) NPS deduction: Section 80CCD(1B) allows an additional deduction of up to ₹50,000 a year for own contributions to the National Pension System, over and above the Section 80C limit, under the old regime.

NPS contributions can be claimed under three provisions. Section 80CCD(1) covers the subscriber's own contribution within the overall ₹1.5 lakh 80C limit. Section 80CCD(1B) gives an exclusive extra deduction of up to ₹50,000 for own NPS Tier I contributions. Section 80CCD(2) covers the employer's contribution to the employee's NPS account, up to a percentage of salary.

80CCD(1B) is available only under the old tax regime as of 2026. The employer contribution deduction under 80CCD(2) is available in both regimes, with a higher percentage limit allowed under the new regime for private sector employees; check current limits.

The deduction makes NPS attractive for taxpayers in higher slabs who have already exhausted 80C. But the money is locked until 60, apart from limited partial withdrawals, and a portion at exit must be used to buy an annuity under PFRDA rules, which is taxable as income. The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026 and renumbers sections, so the familiar section name may now map to a new reference; check the income tax department's current guidance.

A frequent mistake is contributing to NPS Tier II and expecting the deduction; Tier II is a voluntary account and generally does not qualify.

Example

Anjali, in the old regime and the 30% slab, has already used ₹1.5 lakh under 80C through EPF and PPF. She contributes ₹50,000 to her NPS Tier I account. Under 80CCD(1B) her taxable income falls by ₹50,000, saving ₹50,000 × 31.2% = ₹15,600 in tax. Her employer also contributes 10% of basic salary to NPS, which she claims separately under 80CCD(2).

FAQs

Is 80CCD(1B) part of the ₹1.5 lakh 80C limit?

No. 80CCD(1B) is an additional deduction of up to ₹50,000 over and above the ₹1.5 lakh limit, available only for own contributions to NPS Tier I and only under the old regime as of 2026. Check current law for any changes.

Can I claim NPS deduction in the new tax regime?

Only the employer's contribution under 80CCD(2) is allowed in the new regime, as of 2026. Your own contributions under 80CCD(1) and 80CCD(1B) are not deductible there. Salaried employees can ask employers about NPS through salary restructuring.
