What is Market Manipulation (PFUTP)?

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Market Manipulation (PFUTP): Market manipulation is any deliberate act that distorts the price or volume of a security to mislead other investors, prohibited under SEBI's PFUTP Regulations.

The SEBI (Prohibition of Fraudulent and Unfair Trade Practices relating to Securities Market) Regulations, 2003 — usually called PFUTP — prohibit any manipulative, fraudulent or deceptive device in connection with dealing in securities. The rules list examples but are deliberately broad so that new schemes can be caught.

Common forms include circular trading, where a group trades among itself to create artificial volume; synthetic or reversal trades with no change in beneficial ownership; pump and dump schemes, where operators buy a cheap stock, spread rumours or 'tips' through social media or messaging apps, and sell at inflated prices; spoofing, where large orders are placed with no intention to execute; and marking the close to influence closing prices.

SEBI uses exchange surveillance, data analytics and social media monitoring to detect manipulation, and can impose penalties, disgorgement, trading bans and refer cases for prosecution. SEBI has also restricted associations between regulated entities and unregistered 'finfluencers' who give stock recommendations, as these have been used in pump and dump schemes.

Investors often think a sharp rise in volume and price signals good news. In small and illiquid stocks, such moves — especially with aggressive promotions on messaging groups — can be manipulation. Exchange surveillance measures such as ASM/GSM lists and tighter price bands are useful warning signs.

Example

Operators accumulate 20 lakh shares of a small company at around ₹12. They then trade among their own accounts, pushing volume from 50,000 shares a day to 15 lakh, while promoting the stock on messaging channels with claims of a 'big order win'. The price rises to ₹38. They sell 18 lakh shares to new investors at an average ₹35 — a gain of about ₹4.1 crore — before the price collapses back towards ₹14.

FAQs

What is circular trading?

Circular trading is when a group of connected people repeatedly buy and sell a security among themselves to create an impression of active trading and price movement, without any real change in ownership. It misleads other investors about liquidity and demand. SEBI treats it as a manipulative practice under the PFUTP Regulations.

How can I protect myself from pump and dump schemes?

Be wary of unsolicited stock tips on social media or messaging groups, especially for small, illiquid companies. Check company disclosures on exchange websites, look at surveillance lists, and avoid buying because of sudden volume spikes alone. Remember that SEBI-registered advisers and analysts must disclose their registration numbers.
