What is Systematic Investment Plan (SIP)?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Systematic Investment Plan (SIP): A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is a facility to invest a fixed amount in a mutual fund scheme at regular intervals, usually monthly, through an automatic bank debit.

A SIP is not a product; it is a way of investing in an ordinary mutual fund scheme. You choose the scheme, amount, date and frequency, register a bank mandate (NACH or UPI AutoPay), and on each date the amount is debited and units are allotted at that day's applicable NAV.

Because the amount is fixed and the NAV varies, you buy more units when prices are low and fewer when they are high. Over time this gives an average purchase cost below the simple average of NAVs, a result called rupee cost averaging. More importantly, a SIP turns investing into a habit that does not depend on reading the market.

SIPs can be paused, modified or stopped, generally with notice of a few days to the AMC. Missing an instalment because of low bank balance usually carries no penalty from the fund, though your bank may charge a bounce fee and repeated failures can lead to cancellation. A step-up or top-up SIP raises the instalment every year, often in line with salary growth.

Common misconceptions: a SIP does not guarantee positive returns, does not by itself reduce risk in a falling market that never recovers, and is not tied to a lock-in unless the scheme is ELSS, where each instalment is locked for three years from its own date. Each instalment is also a separate purchase for capital gains tax.

Example

Riya starts a ₹5,000 monthly SIP in an equity index fund. In month 1 NAV is ₹100 and she gets 50 units. In month 2 NAV falls to ₹80 and she gets 62.5 units. In month 3 NAV is ₹125, giving 40 units. After three months she has invested ₹15,000 and holds 152.5 units, an average cost of about ₹98.36 per unit, below the simple average NAV of ₹101.67.

FAQs

What is the minimum amount for a SIP?

It depends on the scheme. Many funds accept monthly SIPs from ₹500, and several allow ₹100 or similar small amounts. The minimum is stated in the scheme information document and on the AMC website, and SEBI has also encouraged small-ticket SIPs, so check the current scheme terms.

Is SIP better than a lump sum?

Neither is always better. A lump sum invested before a sustained rise does better; a SIP smooths entry when markets are volatile and suits people investing from monthly income. For money already in hand, many investors use an STP to spread entry. The right choice depends on cash flows and risk comfort.
