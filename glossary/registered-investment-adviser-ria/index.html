What is Registered Investment Adviser (RIA)?

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Registered Investment Adviser (RIA): A Registered Investment Adviser (RIA) is a person or firm registered with SEBI under the Investment Advisers Regulations to give investment advice to clients for a fee.

Since 2013 SEBI has required anyone giving investment advice for consideration to be registered as an investment adviser, subject to some exemptions. Individual RIAs must meet educational qualifications and pass the NISM Series X-A and X-B certifications, and firms need qualified principal officers and staff. RIAs must enrol with SEBI's administration body for advisers and renew certifications periodically.

The defining feature is that an RIA is paid by the client, not by product manufacturers. RIAs cannot earn commissions on the products they recommend, and SEBI requires separation of advisory and distribution activities at the client level, so the same family cannot receive both advice and commission-based distribution from the same entity. This is meant to remove the conflict of interest present when an adviser earns more by selling one product over another.

SEBI caps the fees an RIA may charge individual and HUF clients, either as a fixed fee or a percentage of assets under advice, and limits how much fee can be taken in advance. These limits have been revised; check the current regulations as of 2026.

RIAs must carry out risk profiling and suitability assessment, maintain records of advice, have a written agreement with each client and disclose conflicts. A distributor with an ARN is not an RIA, and calling oneself an 'adviser' without registration is not permitted.

Example

Nisha, 34, pays an RIA a fixed annual fee of ₹40,000. The RIA profiles her risk, maps her goals (home down payment in 5 years, retirement at 58) and recommends direct plans of an index fund, a short duration debt fund and her existing EPF. Because the RIA earns nothing from fund houses, there is no extra cost hidden in the products. Nisha executes through an online platform and reviews the plan with the adviser every year.

FAQs

How can I check if an investment adviser is SEBI-registered?

SEBI's website lists all registered investment advisers with registration numbers and validity. The adviser must also display the registration number on its website, agreements and communications. Avoid anyone giving paid tips or advice without a valid registration.

Can an RIA also sell mutual funds?

An RIA cannot earn commission on products it recommends to advisory clients. SEBI requires client-level segregation between advisory and distribution, so an entity cannot provide both to the same family. RIAs can help clients implement through direct plans without commission.
