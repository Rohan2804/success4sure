What is SPAN Margin and Exposure Margin?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

SPAN Margin and Exposure Margin: SPAN margin is the initial margin for derivatives positions, computed by a risk model that estimates the worst likely one-day loss on a portfolio.

SPAN stands for Standard Portfolio Analysis of Risk, a system developed by the Chicago Mercantile Exchange and used by Indian clearing corporations. It simulates a set of scenarios — combinations of price moves and volatility changes — for each position and works out the largest likely loss across the portfolio. That figure becomes the SPAN, or initial, margin.

Because SPAN looks at the whole portfolio, offsetting positions get credit. A long futures position combined with a long put on the same underlying needs less margin than the futures alone, since the put limits the downside. This is why hedged option strategies often need far less margin than naked option selling.

On top of SPAN, the clearing corporation collects an exposure margin (also called extreme loss margin, ELM) as a cushion for moves beyond the SPAN scenarios. SEBI has added further requirements over time, such as extra margins on short options on expiry day and upfront collection of the full margin, under the peak margin framework. Specific percentages are revised often — check the clearing corporation's latest circulars.

A frequent misconception is that margin is a fee. It is collateral, returned when the position is closed, adjusted for profits and losses. Another is that margin requirements stay fixed; they change daily with volatility, and can jump during market stress, forcing traders to add funds or cut positions.

Example

A trader sells 1 lot of index futures worth ₹18,00,000. SPAN margin is, say, ₹1,80,000 and exposure margin ₹36,000, a total of ₹2,16,000. If she also buys an out-of-the-money call that caps her loss on a sharp rise, SPAN recalculates the worst-case loss for the combined position, and the requirement might fall to around ₹1,10,000 in this illustration. Actual amounts come from the exchange's margin calculator.

FAQs

What is the difference between SPAN margin and exposure margin?

SPAN margin covers the likely worst one-day loss estimated through risk scenarios. Exposure margin is an additional buffer for extreme moves not captured by SPAN, set as a percentage of contract value. Together they form the initial margin a trader must have before taking a futures or short options position.

Why is margin lower for hedged positions?

SPAN evaluates risk at the portfolio level. When positions offset each other, the worst-case loss is smaller than the sum of individual worst cases, so the required margin falls. This encourages hedging and explains why spreads often need much less margin than single naked option sales.
