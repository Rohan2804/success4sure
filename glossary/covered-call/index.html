What is Covered Call?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Covered Call: A covered call is an options strategy in which an investor holds the underlying shares and sells call options on them to earn premium income.

The strategy is called 'covered' because the shares owned cover the obligation if the call is exercised. The investor collects premium upfront. If the share price stays below the strike at expiry, the call expires worthless and the investor keeps both the shares and the premium. If the price rises above the strike, the shares are effectively sold at the strike price.

Maximum profit is capped at (strike − purchase price) + premium received. Downside is reduced only by the premium; if the share falls sharply, the investor still bears most of the loss. The breakeven at expiry is the purchase price minus the premium. The strategy suits a mildly bullish or neutral view.

Investors often sell out-of-the-money calls month after month to generate extra income from long-term holdings. Some mutual fund schemes in India are allowed to write covered calls on their equity holdings within SEBI's limits. Physical settlement of stock options means an in-the-money short call at expiry results in delivery of shares.

The main misconception is that covered calls are risk-free income. The real risks are giving up big upside rallies and still facing almost full downside in a crash. Taxes and transaction costs on frequent option writing also reduce net income.

Example

Divya holds 500 shares bought at ₹1,200. She sells a one-month ₹1,260 call at ₹22, receiving ₹11,000. At expiry: at ₹1,230, the call expires worthless — share gain ₹15,000 plus ₹11,000 premium = ₹26,000. At ₹1,300, the call is exercised; her gain is capped at (₹60 × 500) + ₹11,000 = ₹41,000. At ₹1,150, the shares lose ₹25,000, partly offset by premium, so the net loss is ₹14,000.

FAQs

When is a covered call a good strategy?

It suits investors with a neutral to mildly bullish view who are willing to sell their shares at the strike price. It works best when the stock moves sideways, letting the investor collect premium repeatedly. It is less suitable if a strong rally is expected, since gains above the strike are given up.

What is the maximum loss in a covered call?

The maximum loss occurs if the share price falls to zero: the loss on the shares minus the premium received. In formula terms, it is the purchase price minus the premium, multiplied by the quantity. The premium cushions only a small part of a big decline.
