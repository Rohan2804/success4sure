What is Demat Account?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Demat Account: A demat account is an account with a depository participant that holds shares, bonds, ETFs and other securities in electronic form instead of paper certificates.

'Demat' is short for dematerialised. Since SEBI made delivery in demat form compulsory for listed shares, and transfers of physical shares were stopped in 2019 (except transmission and transposition), anyone who buys listed securities in India needs a demat account. You open it with a DP after completing KYC, and it is linked to your PAN and bank account.

A demat account is not where you place orders — that is the trading account with your broker. Think of it this way: the trading account is the shop counter, the demat account is your locker. When you buy, shares land in the demat; when you sell, they leave it. Many brokers open both together, which is why people mix them up.

Main types include the regular demat account for resident Indians, the Basic Services Demat Account (BSDA) for small investors with lower charges, and repatriable and non-repatriable demat accounts for NRIs. A demat can be held singly or jointly (up to three holders), and nomination can be registered or explicitly opted out of.

Besides equity shares, a demat account can hold ETFs, government securities, sovereign gold bonds, corporate bonds, debentures and mutual fund units. Corporate benefits such as bonus shares, split shares and rights entitlements are credited directly, while dividends go to the linked bank account.

Example

Anil buys 40 shares of a company at ₹1,250 on Wednesday through his trading account, paying ₹50,000 plus charges. On Thursday (T+1) the 40 shares appear in his demat account. Two months later the company announces a 1:1 bonus; on the credit date his demat shows 80 shares with no action needed from him. When he later sells 30 shares, the demat balance falls to 50 after the sale settles.

FAQs

Is a demat account the same as a trading account?

No. A trading account with a broker is used to place buy and sell orders on the exchange. A demat account with a DP stores the securities. You need both to trade in the stock market, but you need only a demat account to receive IPO allotments, gifts or transfers, or to hold securities you already own.

Can I hold physical share certificates now?

You can still own them, but transfers of physical shares of listed companies are no longer allowed except in limited cases such as transmission on death. To sell, you must first dematerialise the certificates through your DP, which sends them to the company's registrar for verification and then credits your demat account.
