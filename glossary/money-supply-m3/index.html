What is Money Supply (M0, M1, M3)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Money Supply (M0, M1, M3): Money supply is the total stock of money available in an economy at a point of time, measured by RBI through aggregates such as M0, M1 and M3.

RBI publishes money aggregates fortnightly. Reserve money (M0), also called base money or high-powered money, is currency in circulation plus bankers' deposits with RBI plus other deposits with RBI. It is the money RBI directly creates.

M1 (narrow money) = currency with the public + demand deposits with banks (current accounts and the demand portion of savings accounts) + other deposits with RBI. M2 adds savings deposits with post office savings banks. M3 (broad money) = M1 + time deposits with banks (fixed and recurring deposits). M4 adds total post office deposits. M3 is the most watched measure of money growth in India. RBI also compiles new liquidity aggregates (L1, L2, L3) suggested by a 1998 working group.

The money multiplier links them: M3 = m × M0. When banks receive deposits, they keep a fraction as reserves (CRR) and lend the rest, which is redeposited and lent again. A lower CRR or more lending raises the multiplier. That is why RBI's actions on reserve money can expand broad money several times.

Common exam trap: liquidity decreases as you move from M1 to M4 because time deposits are less readily spendable than cash. Also, 'other deposits with RBI' are deposits of quasi-government bodies, foreign central banks and similar entities, not of commercial banks.

Example

Suppose currency with the public is ₹35 lakh crore, demand deposits ₹30 lakh crore, other deposits with RBI ₹1 lakh crore, and bank time deposits ₹200 lakh crore. M1 = 35 + 30 + 1 = ₹66 lakh crore; M3 = 66 + 200 = ₹266 lakh crore. If reserve money is ₹48 lakh crore, the money multiplier = 266/48 = about 5.5.

FAQs

Which measure of money supply is called broad money?

M3 is called broad money. It includes currency with the public, demand deposits, other deposits with RBI and time deposits with banks. M1 is called narrow money. RBI gives most attention to M3 when analysing growth of money and its link with inflation and credit.

Why is M0 called high-powered money?

M0 is the base on which banks create deposits through lending. Every rupee of reserve money can support several rupees of broad money through the multiplier process, so changes in M0 have a magnified effect on the overall money supply. Only RBI can create M0.
