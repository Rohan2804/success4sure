What is Co-payment (Health Insurance)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Co-payment (Health Insurance): Co-payment is a cost-sharing clause in health insurance under which the policyholder pays a fixed percentage of every admissible claim, and the insurer pays the rest.

With a 20% co-pay, the insured bears 20% of each eligible claim amount and the insurer pays 80%, up to the sum insured. Insurers use co-payment to reduce premiums and discourage unnecessary or inflated claims, since the insured has a stake in every rupee spent.

Co-pay is common in policies for senior citizens, in group policies, for treatment in higher-cost cities (zone-based co-pay), for treatment at non-network hospitals, and sometimes for specific illnesses. Some policies allow buyers to opt for a voluntary co-pay to lower the premium. IRDAI requires co-payment terms to be clearly disclosed in the policy schedule and customer information sheet.

Co-payment differs from a deductible. A deductible is a fixed amount, say the first ₹25,000 per year, that the insured pays before the insurer pays anything. Co-pay is a percentage that applies to every claim regardless of size. A policy can have both. Co-pay is also different from sub-limits, such as caps on room rent or specific procedures, which limit what the insurer pays for particular items.

Exam and buyer tip: co-pay usually applies after other deductions. First, non-payable items (like some consumables) and sub-limit excesses are removed to arrive at the admissible amount; then the co-pay percentage is applied. So the insured's actual share is often larger than the co-pay percentage of the total bill.

Example

Mr Iyer, 66, has a ₹5 lakh senior citizen policy with 20% co-pay. His hospital bill is ₹2,50,000, of which ₹10,000 is non-payable consumables. Admissible amount = ₹2,40,000. Co-pay = 20% × 2,40,000 = ₹48,000. The insurer pays ₹1,92,000. Mr Iyer pays ₹48,000 + ₹10,000 = ₹58,000, which is 23.2% of the total bill, more than the 20% headline co-pay.

FAQs

What is the difference between co-payment and deductible?

A deductible is a fixed amount the insured pays first, before the insurer starts paying, often annually. Co-payment is a percentage of every admissible claim that the insured shares. With a ₹20,000 deductible, small claims may not be paid at all; with 10% co-pay, the insurer pays 90% of every admissible claim.

Why do senior citizen health policies usually have co-payment?

Older people have a higher probability and cost of hospitalisation, which would make premiums very high. Co-payment lowers the insurer's cost per claim, keeping premiums more affordable, and encourages careful use of healthcare. Some insurers offer senior plans without co-pay at a higher premium.
