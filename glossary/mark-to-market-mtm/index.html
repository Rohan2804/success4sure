What is Mark-to-Market (MTM)?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Mark-to-Market (MTM): Mark-to-market is the daily revaluation of open futures positions at the settlement price, with gains credited and losses debited to traders' accounts every day.

In a forward contract, gains and losses build up until maturity, which increases the chance that the losing side cannot pay. Futures avoid this by settling the change in value every trading day. The clearing corporation calculates a daily settlement price, usually based on the closing price, and each open position is revalued against it.

On the first day, MTM is calculated as (settlement price − trade price) × quantity for a long position. On later days, it is (today's settlement price − yesterday's settlement price) × quantity. Losses must be paid in cash by the next morning, and profits are released to the trader. After each day's settlement, the position effectively carries the new settlement price.

MTM losses are separate from the initial margin. If losses shrink the account below the maintenance level, the broker asks for more funds (a margin call). If the client does not pay, the broker can close the position. This daily discipline keeps the clearing corporation's exposure to a single day's price change, backed by margins.

The term also appears in accounting and in bond valuations, where 'marking to market' means valuing assets at current market prices instead of cost. In derivatives exams, focus on the daily cash flow mechanism and remember that options buyers do not face daily MTM on their long positions — they pay the premium upfront.

Example

Priya buys 1 lot of 400 shares in stock futures at ₹950 on Monday. Settlement prices: Monday ₹958, Tuesday ₹944, Wednesday ₹961. Her MTM: Monday (958 − 950) × 400 = +₹3,200; Tuesday (944 − 958) × 400 = −₹5,600; Wednesday (961 − 944) × 400 = +₹6,800. Net so far +₹4,400, which equals (961 − 950) × 400 — the daily flows add up to her total gain.

FAQs

Is MTM loss a real loss if I haven't closed my position?

Yes, in cash terms. MTM losses are actually debited from your account daily, even if you keep the position open. If the price later recovers, you get MTM credits back. The final profit or loss equals the sum of all daily MTM flows plus the closing difference.

Do options have mark-to-market settlement?

Option buyers pay the full premium upfront, so their positions are not subject to daily MTM cash settlement. Option sellers' positions are revalued daily for margin purposes, and margin requirements can rise sharply if the market moves against them, but premium is settled upfront rather than through daily MTM.
