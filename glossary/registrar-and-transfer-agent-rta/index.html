What is Registrar and Transfer Agent (RTA)?

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Registrar and Transfer Agent (RTA): A registrar and share transfer agent (RTA) is a SEBI-registered intermediary that maintains shareholder or unitholder records and processes investor service requests for issuers.

RTAs are registered under the SEBI (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents) Regulations, 1993. Companies and mutual funds appoint them to manage registers of holders, process transmission of securities on death, record nominations, distribute dividends and interest, handle demat and remat requests and respond to investor complaints.

In public issues, the registrar to an issue plays a central role. It collects application data from the bidding platform and SCSBs, rejects invalid applications (such as multiple applications with one PAN), finalises the basis of allotment with the exchange, credits shares to demat accounts and arranges unblocking of ASBA funds. IPO allotment status is checked on the registrar's website.

For mutual funds, RTAs such as CAMS and KFin Technologies maintain folio records, process purchases and redemptions, and generate account statements. For listed companies, RTAs coordinate with depositories for corporate action credits.

Investors often contact the company for issues that the RTA actually handles — such as unclaimed dividends, KYC updates for physical folios or transmission. Knowing that the RTA is the record-keeper speeds up resolution. RTAs must also follow SEBI's investor grievance timelines and are covered under the SCORES and ODR frameworks.

Example

After an IPO closes on Wednesday, the registrar validates 25 lakh applications and finds 40,000 duplicates by PAN, which are rejected. On Thursday it finalises allotment with the designated exchange: the retail portion of 10 lakh lots is allotted by lottery among 18 lakh valid applicants. On Friday it instructs depositories to credit shares and SCSBs to debit allotted amounts and unblock the rest, so listing can happen on T+3.

FAQs

Who should I contact for transmission of shares after a death?

For physical folios, contact the company's RTA, which processes transmission documents such as the death certificate and claim forms. For demat holdings, the surviving joint holder or nominee approaches the DP. Simplified documentation limits apply for smaller values under SEBI's norms.

How do I check IPO allotment status?

You can check on the registrar's website using your PAN, application number or DP ID, and also on the stock exchange websites. Your bank or UPI app will show whether the blocked amount was debited or released, which also indicates the allotment outcome.
