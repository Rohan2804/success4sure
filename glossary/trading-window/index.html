What is Trading Window?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Trading Window: The trading window is the period during which a company's designated persons and their relatives are permitted to trade in its securities; it is closed when they may have access to UPSI.

Under the SEBI PIT Regulations, every listed company's compliance officer manages the trading window. For financial results, the window closes from the end of every quarter until 48 hours after the results are declared. The company can also close the window at other times when designated persons are expected to have UPSI, such as during a major acquisition.

The rule applies to designated persons — directors, key managerial personnel, employees in finance, legal and other sensitive functions identified by the board — and to their immediate relatives. While the window is closed, they cannot trade in the company's securities, even if they personally have no UPSI.

When the window is open, designated persons still need pre-clearance from the compliance officer for trades above the threshold in the company's code, must complete pre-cleared trades within a set number of trading days, and must avoid contra trades within six months. Trades under an approved trading plan can be carried out despite window closure, subject to conditions.

Many employees think that only those who actually know the results are barred. The window closure is a blanket rule meant to remove any doubt. Another misconception is that buying ESOP shares through exercise is always restricted; exercising options is generally allowed during closure, but selling the shares is not.

Example

A company's quarter ends on 30 June and its board declares results on 8 August at 5 p.m. The trading window for designated persons is closed from 1 July until 48 hours after the announcement, i.e., until 10 August at 5 p.m., so trades can resume from the next trading session after that. A finance manager who buys 500 shares on 15 July violates the code, even if he did not see the numbers.

FAQs

Who has to follow trading window restrictions?

Designated persons identified by the company's board — such as directors, key managerial personnel and employees with access to sensitive information — and their immediate relatives. The company's code of conduct lists the categories. Others who come to possess UPSI are still covered by the general prohibition on insider trading.

Can a designated person trade during closure under a trading plan?

Yes. SEBI's regulations allow trades under a trading plan that was approved and publicly disclosed in advance, because the trades were decided before the insider had the UPSI. Trading plans have conditions about cool-off periods and duration, so they must be set up carefully.
