What is Immediate Payment Service (IMPS)?

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Immediate Payment Service (IMPS): IMPS is an instant, round-the-clock interbank fund transfer service operated by NPCI, available through mobile banking, internet banking, ATMs, SMS and bank branches.

IMPS was launched by the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) in November 2010, initially as a mobile payment system. It credits the beneficiary almost immediately, 24x7, including holidays, which was a big advance when NEFT worked only in batches on working days.

Funds can be sent in several ways: using the beneficiary's account number and IFSC; using their mobile number plus MMID (Mobile Money Identifier, a 7-digit code linked to their account); or, in newer versions, using the mobile number and bank name alone. The per-transaction limit was raised by RBI to ₹5 lakh in 2021, though banks set their own limits within this (check as of 2026).

Unlike NEFT and RTGS, which RBI runs and settles in central bank money, IMPS is a retail system run by NPCI, with transactions cleared instantly and settled between banks in multiple cycles. Banks can levy small charges for IMPS; many offer it free on digital channels.

IMPS is also the backbone that UPI was built on. UPI added the virtual payment address (like name@bank) and a common app interface. Exam questions often ask which organisation runs IMPS (NPCI), its launch year (2010) and its current limit.

Example

At 11:45 pm on a Saturday, Ankit transfers ₹25,000 to his sister for a hostel fee due the next morning. Using IMPS with her account number and IFSC, the money reaches her account within seconds. If he used NEFT, it would go in the 12:00 midnight batch and usually still arrive quickly. But for ₹6 lakh, IMPS would not work (above the ₹5 lakh cap), so he would use NEFT or RTGS.

FAQs

Who operates IMPS in India?

IMPS is operated by the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI), an umbrella organisation for retail payments set up by RBI and the Indian Banks' Association in 2008. NPCI also runs UPI, RuPay, NACH, AePS and the cheque truncation system. NEFT and RTGS are run directly by RBI.

What is MMID in IMPS?

MMID or Mobile Money Identifier is a seven-digit number issued by a bank and linked to a customer's mobile number and account. A sender can transfer money using the recipient's mobile number and MMID instead of account number and IFSC. With UPI's spread, MMID use has declined but it remains a valid IMPS method.
