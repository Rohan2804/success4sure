What is Open Market Operations (OMO)?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Open Market Operations (OMO): Open market operations are outright purchases or sales of government securities by RBI in the market, used to inject or absorb durable liquidity in the banking system.

When RBI buys government securities from banks and other market participants (an OMO purchase), it pays them money, which increases bank reserves and liquidity. When RBI sells securities (an OMO sale), buyers pay RBI, and money leaves the system. Unlike repo, these are outright transactions with no promise to reverse, so the effect on liquidity is durable.

RBI conducts OMOs through auctions on its E-Kuber platform or by trading on the NDS-OM screen. OMOs also affect bond yields. An OMO purchase raises demand for bonds, pushing prices up and yields down; a sale does the opposite. In a 'twist' operation, RBI buys long-term and sells short-term securities together, aiming to lower long-term yields without changing total liquidity.

RBI uses OMOs to offset durable liquidity shifts. For example, when RBI buys foreign currency to stop the rupee from rising, it releases rupees; it may then sell bonds through OMOs to absorb the excess, a process called sterilisation. During a deficit, as in heavy forex sales by RBI, OMO purchases add rupees back.

Exam trap: an OMO purchase is expansionary (adds money), an OMO sale is contractionary. Also, OMO is a quantitative tool of monetary policy, along with CRR, SLR and LAF, whereas moral suasion and margin requirements are qualitative tools.

Example

RBI announces an OMO purchase auction of ₹20,000 crore. A bank sells a 10-year bond with face value ₹1,000 crore at a price of ₹102 per ₹100, so it receives ₹1,020 crore in its RBI current account. Across all sellers, ₹20,000 crore of fresh reserves enters the system. If banks keep 3% as CRR on new deposits created later, the rest can support further lending.

FAQs

Is OMO the same as repo?

No. Repo is a temporary loan against securities that is reversed after a fixed period, so liquidity returns to RBI. OMO is an outright sale or purchase, so the change in liquidity is permanent until RBI reverses it with another trade. RBI uses repo for short-term needs and OMO for lasting shifts.

What is sterilisation in OMO?

When RBI buys dollars to manage the exchange rate, it injects rupees into the system. To prevent this from adding to inflation, RBI may sell government securities through OMOs to absorb the same amount of rupees. This offsetting operation is called sterilisation.
