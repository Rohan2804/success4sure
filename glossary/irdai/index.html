What is IRDAI (Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India)?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

IRDAI (Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India): IRDAI is the statutory regulator of India's insurance sector, set up under the IRDA Act, 1999, to protect policyholders and to regulate, promote and ensure orderly growth of insurance.

The Malhotra Committee (1993-94) recommended opening insurance to private players and creating an independent regulator. The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority Act, 1999 established the authority, which began work in April 2000. It was later renamed IRDAI. Its head office is in Hyderabad.

The authority has a chairperson, up to five whole-time members and up to four part-time members, appointed by the Central Government. Its functions under Section 14 of the IRDA Act include: registering insurers and renewing, modifying or cancelling registrations; protecting policyholder interests in matters like nomination, assignment, surrender value and claim settlement; specifying qualifications, code of conduct and training for agents and intermediaries; regulating investment of insurance funds; supervising solvency margins; and adjudicating disputes between insurers and intermediaries.

IRDAI regulates life insurers, general and health insurers, reinsurers, insurance brokers, corporate agents, web aggregators, surveyors and third-party administrators (TPAs). It approves products through its product regulations, sets the IC-38 examination standard for agents through the insurance institute, and runs the Bima Bharosa grievance portal.

In recent years, IRDAI has pursued 'Insurance for All by 2047', replacing many rules with principle-based regulations and master circulars in 2024, raising the FDI cap in insurance, and launching initiatives such as Bima Sugam, a digital insurance marketplace.

Example

A policyholder's death claim of ₹25 lakh is pending for 45 days after all documents were submitted. Her husband first complains to the insurer's grievance officer. Receiving no response in the time allowed, he registers the complaint on IRDAI's Bima Bharosa portal, which tracks it with the insurer. If the matter is still unresolved, he can approach the Insurance Ombudsman for claims up to ₹50 lakh. IRDAI itself does not decide individual claim amounts.

FAQs

Where is IRDAI headquartered and who appoints its members?

IRDAI's head office is in Hyderabad, Telangana. The chairperson and members, up to five whole-time and four part-time, are appointed by the Central Government from persons with expertise in life insurance, general insurance, actuarial science, finance, economics, law, accountancy or administration.

Does IRDAI settle individual insurance claims?

No. IRDAI regulates insurers and intermediaries and monitors grievances through its Bima Bharosa system, but it does not adjudicate individual claim disputes. For a claim dispute, a policyholder can approach the insurer's grievance cell, then the Insurance Ombudsman for amounts within its limit, or a consumer commission or court.
