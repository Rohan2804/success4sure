What is Principle of Indemnity?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Principle of Indemnity: The principle of indemnity says an insurance claim should restore the insured to the same financial position they were in just before the loss, no better and no worse.

Indemnity prevents people from profiting from insurance. If you could earn more from a claim than your actual loss, you might be tempted to cause or exaggerate losses. So general insurance such as fire, marine, motor, property and most health policies (hospitalisation reimbursement) pays only the actual loss, subject to the sum insured, deductibles and policy terms.

Indemnity can be provided in four ways: cash payment, repair, replacement, or reinstatement (restoring a building to its earlier condition). Actual loss in property insurance is usually the market value at the time of loss, which accounts for depreciation, unless the policy is on a reinstatement value or 'new for old' basis.

Two related principles flow from indemnity. Subrogation transfers the insured's rights against a third party to the insurer after it pays the claim, so the insured cannot recover twice. Contribution applies when the same property is insured with more than one insurer: each insurer shares the loss in proportion, and the insured cannot collect the full loss from each. Under-insurance triggers the 'average' clause: if the sum insured is less than the value, the claim is reduced proportionately.

Indemnity does not apply to life insurance or personal accident benefits (for death or disability), because a human life has no market value. These are 'benefit' or 'valued' policies that pay a fixed amount.

Example

A shop's stock is worth ₹20 lakh but insured for only ₹15 lakh. A fire destroys stock worth ₹8 lakh. Under the average clause, the claim = 8 lakh × 15/20 = ₹6 lakh. If the stock had been fully insured for ₹20 lakh, the claim would be ₹8 lakh, the actual loss, not the ₹20 lakh sum insured. And if the owner also had a ₹10 lakh policy with another insurer, contribution would split the payout between them.

FAQs

Why does indemnity not apply to life insurance?

Indemnity requires measuring the actual financial loss, but a human life cannot be valued precisely in money. So life insurance pays the agreed sum assured on death or maturity, regardless of the actual loss. That is why life policies are called benefit policies, while general insurance policies are contracts of indemnity.

What is the average clause in fire insurance?

If property is under-insured, meaning the sum insured is less than its actual value, the average clause reduces the claim in the same proportion. Claim = Loss × Sum insured ÷ Value of property. It encourages people to insure for full value and is a direct application of indemnity and fairness among policyholders.
