What is Rule of 72?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Rule of 72: The rule of 72 is a quick mental shortcut: divide 72 by the annual rate of return to estimate how many years it takes for money to double.

At 8% a year, money doubles in about 72 ÷ 8 = 9 years. At 12%, in about 6 years. The rule works because of the mathematics of compounding; the precise doubling time is ln(2) ÷ ln(1 + r), and 72 gives a close approximation for typical interest rates between about 6% and 10%.

It can be used in reverse: to double money in 5 years, you need roughly 72 ÷ 5 = 14.4% a year. It also applies to anything that compounds, including inflation and debt. At 6% inflation, the purchasing power of money halves in about 12 years.

The rule is popular in financial education and competitive exams because it gives an instant sense of scale without a calculator. Variants exist: the rule of 114 estimates tripling time and the rule of 144 estimates quadrupling.

Remember that the rule assumes a constant compounded return. Equity returns vary year to year, so for equity it gives only a rough idea. At very high rates, such as 30%, the rule becomes less accurate; 72 ÷ 30 = 2.4 years versus the true 2.64 years.

Example

Neha has ₹5 lakh in a fixed deposit at 7%. By the rule of 72 it doubles in about 72 ÷ 7 ≈ 10.3 years (the exact figure is about 10.2 years). If inflation runs at 6%, prices also double in about 12 years. So in real terms her FD barely grows. An equity fund averaging 12% would double in about 6 years, though with much more year-to-year variation.

FAQs

How accurate is the rule of 72?

Very close for returns between about 6% and 10%, within a few months. At low rates (2–3%) or high rates (above 20%) the error grows. Some people use 69 or 70 for continuous compounding. For exact figures, use a calculator with the compounding formula.

Can the rule of 72 be used for inflation?

Yes. Divide 72 by the inflation rate to estimate how long it takes for prices to double, or equivalently for your money's purchasing power to halve. At 6% inflation, ₹1 lakh will buy only what about ₹50,000 buys today in roughly 12 years.
