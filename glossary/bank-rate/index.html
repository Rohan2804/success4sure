What is Bank Rate?

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Bank Rate: Bank rate is the standard rate at which the Reserve Bank of India is prepared to buy or rediscount bills of exchange and other commercial paper, as defined in Section 49 of the RBI Act.

Bank rate is one of the oldest instruments of monetary policy. In earlier decades, before repo operations, RBI used it as the main signal: a higher bank rate made long-term refinance costlier and tightened credit. The rate is published under Section 49 of the RBI Act, 1934.

With the introduction of the Liquidity Adjustment Facility in 2000 and the repo rate's rise as the policy rate, bank rate lost its signalling role. Today RBI keeps it aligned with the Marginal Standing Facility rate, so when MPC changes the repo rate, MSF and bank rate move automatically by the same amount. It is no longer an independent policy decision.

Why learn it then? Bank rate is still used as a reference in several rules. Penal interest that RBI charges banks for shortfalls in maintaining CRR or SLR is linked to the bank rate, for example bank rate plus 3% at first, rising to bank rate plus 5% if the shortfall continues. Some statutes and contracts also cite 'bank rate' when fixing interest on delayed payments.

The classic difference: repo is a short-term loan against securities (a repurchase deal), while bank rate refers to rediscounting of bills or long-term lending without a repurchase agreement. In current exams, if asked 'which rate is equal to MSF', the answer is bank rate.

Example

Say repo is 5.50%, so MSF and bank rate are 5.75%. A bank falls short of its required CRR by ₹100 crore for one day. Penal interest at bank rate + 3% = 8.75%. Charge = 100 × 8.75%/365 = about ₹0.024 crore, roughly ₹2.40 lakh for that day. If the shortfall continues and the rate rises to bank rate + 5% (10.75%), the daily cost goes up to about ₹2.95 lakh.

FAQs

Is bank rate the same as the rate banks charge customers?

No. Despite the name, bank rate is the rate RBI uses, not a rate banks charge borrowers. Your loan rate depends on the bank's benchmark, such as the repo-linked rate or MCLR, plus a spread. Bank rate today mostly matters as a reference for penal charges and in some legal provisions.

Under which section of the RBI Act is bank rate defined?

Section 49 of the RBI Act, 1934 requires RBI to make public the standard rate at which it is prepared to buy or rediscount bills of exchange or other commercial paper eligible for purchase under the Act. This published standard rate is the bank rate.
