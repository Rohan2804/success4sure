What is Alpha?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Alpha: Alpha is the return a portfolio earns above what would be expected given its level of market risk, often seen as the value added by the fund manager.

In its simplest form, alpha is fund return minus benchmark return. If a fund returns 15% and its benchmark 12%, alpha is 3 percentage points. This simple version ignores risk: a fund may have beaten the index just by taking more market exposure.

Jensen's alpha corrects for that using the capital asset pricing model. Expected return = risk-free rate + beta × (market return − risk-free rate). Alpha = actual return − expected return. A fund with beta of 1.2 is expected to beat a rising market simply because it is more aggressive, and Jensen's alpha strips this out.

Factsheets often report alpha over three years. Advisers use it to judge whether an active fund justifies its higher expense ratio compared with an index fund. Persistent positive alpha after costs is rare, which is a key argument for passive investing in efficient segments.

Watch for three traps. Alpha calculated against the wrong benchmark, such as a price index instead of TRI, is inflated. Short-period alpha is often luck. And alpha in a regular plan is lower than in the direct plan of the same scheme because of the commission in the expense ratio.

Example

Over a year, a mid cap fund returned 15%. The benchmark returned 12%, the risk-free rate was 7% and the fund's beta was 1.1. Expected return = 7% + 1.1 × (12% − 7%) = 12.5%. Jensen's alpha = 15% − 12.5% = 2.5%. The simple alpha of 3% overstated the manager's contribution by 0.5 point because the fund carried more market risk than the index.

FAQs

Is a positive alpha always good?

It indicates outperformance after adjusting for market risk in that period. But it may be due to luck, especially over short periods, or to style tilts the benchmark does not capture. Check whether alpha is consistent across rolling periods and remains positive after costs.

What is the difference between alpha and beta?

Beta measures how sensitive a portfolio is to market movements; a beta of 1.2 means it tends to move 1.2% for each 1% market move. Alpha measures the return earned beyond what that beta would predict. Beta is about risk exposure, alpha about excess performance.
