What is Contract Note?

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Contract Note: A contract note is the legal confirmation a stock broker issues to a client for trades executed on an exchange, showing prices, quantities and all charges.

SEBI and the exchanges require brokers to issue a contract note for every trading day on which the client trades, generally within 24 hours of execution. Most brokers send it electronically, digitally signed, to the client's registered email. It is the primary evidence of your trades if there is ever a dispute.

A contract note shows the order number, trade number, time, exchange and segment, the security, quantity, price, and whether it was a buy or sell. It also shows each charge separately: brokerage, securities transaction tax (STT), exchange transaction charges, SEBI turnover fees, stamp duty, and GST on brokerage and charges. It carries the client's unique client code (UCC) and PAN.

Checking contract notes helps you spot unauthorised trades, wrong quantities and excessive charges. If you see a trade you did not place, you should raise it with the broker immediately and escalate to the exchange if unresolved. Exchanges also send trade alerts by SMS and email directly to clients, which you can cross-check with the contract note.

Contract notes are important for tax as well. They support capital gains calculations and show STT paid. Investors sometimes rely only on the broker's P&L report; keeping contract notes (or downloading them periodically) is the safer practice.

Example

Mohan buys 100 shares at ₹1,000 (value ₹1,00,000). His contract note shows illustrative charges: brokerage ₹20, STT ₹100 (0.1% on delivery buy), exchange charges ₹3, stamp duty ₹15, SEBI fee ₹0.10 and GST of 18% on brokerage, exchange charges and SEBI fee (about ₹4.16). Total cost ≈ ₹1,00,142. Rates change — check current exchange and tax notifications rather than these figures.

FAQs

What should I do if my contract note shows a trade I did not place?

Write to the broker immediately with details, ask for the order log and call recording, and keep records. If the broker does not resolve it, file a complaint with the exchange or through SEBI SCORES, and if needed use the online dispute resolution (ODR) mechanism. Prompt action strengthens your case.

Is a contract note required for mutual fund purchases?

Contract notes are issued for trades executed on stock exchanges, including ETFs and mutual fund units bought through exchange platforms. Direct mutual fund purchases with an AMC generate an account statement instead, such as the consolidated account statement, rather than a contract note.
