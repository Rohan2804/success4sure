What is Theta (Time Decay)?

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Theta (Time Decay): Theta measures how much an option's premium is expected to fall with the passage of one day, assuming other factors stay the same.

Options lose time value as expiry approaches because there is less time for a favourable move. Theta is usually expressed as a negative number per day for a long option: a theta of −0.48 means the option loses about ₹0.48 per unit each day. For the seller, the same theta is a daily gain.

Time decay is not linear. It is slow when expiry is months away and accelerates in the last few weeks, especially in the final days. At-the-money options have the largest theta in rupee terms because they carry the most time value. Deep in-the-money and far out-of-the-money options have smaller theta because they have less time value to lose.

Theta and gamma are two sides of the same trade-off. Buyers get positive gamma — the chance of profiting from sharp moves — and pay for it through negative theta. Sellers earn theta but take on negative gamma. Strategies such as covered calls and short strangles aim to collect theta, while long straddles pay theta in the hope of a big move.

A widespread misconception is that time decay happens only on trading days. Option pricing models treat time as continuous, so weekends and holidays also reduce time value, which markets typically price in before the break. Another is that selling options is 'free money' from theta; one large move can wipe out months of decay income.

Example

A ₹1,000 strike at-the-money call with 30 days left, 20% volatility and 7% interest is worth about ₹25.79 by the Black-Scholes model. With 29 days left and nothing else changed, it is worth about ₹25.31 — a theta of roughly −₹0.48 per day. On a lot of 600, a buyer loses about ₹290 a day to time decay, and a seller gains the same, if the price stays put.

FAQs

Which options lose value fastest due to time decay?

At-the-money options close to expiry lose time value the fastest in rupee terms, because their premium is entirely time value and the remaining time is short. Far out-of-the-money options lose a larger percentage of their small premium, often becoming nearly worthless in the last few days.

Is theta good for option sellers?

Theta works in favour of sellers because they gain as time value decays. But sellers take on negative gamma and face potentially large losses if the price moves sharply. Many experienced sellers use spreads or hedges to limit this risk rather than relying only on theta.
