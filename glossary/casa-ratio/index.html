What is CASA Ratio?

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

CASA Ratio: CASA ratio is the share of a bank's total deposits that comes from current accounts and savings accounts, which are its cheapest sources of funds.

Formula: CASA ratio = (Current account deposits + Savings account deposits) ÷ Total deposits × 100. Current accounts, used by businesses for daily transactions, pay no interest. Savings accounts pay a low rate, deregulated by RBI in 2011 and typically much lower than fixed deposit rates. Term deposits (fixed and recurring) cost more.

A high CASA ratio means a lower average cost of funds, which lets the bank either lend more cheaply or earn a higher net interest margin. That is why banks invest heavily in branch networks, salary account tie-ups, digital banking and transaction services: CASA deposits come from customers who use the bank daily, not those shopping for the best rate.

CASA is also considered a stable, sticky funding source in normal times, though digital banking has made savings balances more mobile. Private banks with strong retail franchises often report CASA ratios around 40% or more, while some newer banks rely more on term deposits. When interest rates rise, customers tend to shift money from savings to fixed deposits, and CASA ratios fall across the industry.

Common mistake: CASA is a ratio of deposits, not of total liabilities or loans. Also, a high CASA ratio alone does not mean a bank is healthy; asset quality and capital also matter.

Example

A bank has current deposits of ₹40,000 crore (0% interest), savings deposits of ₹60,000 crore at 2.75%, and term deposits of ₹1,50,000 crore at 7%. Total deposits = ₹2,50,000 crore. CASA ratio = 1,00,000/2,50,000 = 40%. Interest cost = 0 + 1,650 + 10,500 = ₹12,150 crore, an average cost of 4.86%. If CASA were 30% (₹75,000 crore, split in the same 40:60 ratio) the average cost would rise to about 5.395%.

FAQs

Why do banks want a high CASA ratio?

Current accounts pay no interest and savings accounts pay little, so a higher CASA share lowers the bank's average cost of deposits. Cheaper funds mean better margins or more competitive loan rates. CASA customers also bring fee income from transactions and cross-selling opportunities, making them valuable relationships.

Does a fixed deposit count in CASA?

No. Fixed deposits and recurring deposits are term deposits and are excluded from CASA. However, some banks offer sweep-in accounts where surplus savings balance is moved into a fixed deposit automatically; the swept portion is generally reported as a term deposit, not CASA.
