What is Net Interest Margin (NIM)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Net Interest Margin (NIM): Net interest margin is a bank's interest earned minus interest paid, expressed as a percentage of its average interest-earning assets, showing how profitably it lends.

Formula: NIM = (Interest income − Interest expense) ÷ Average interest-earning assets × 100. The numerator is net interest income (NII). Interest-earning assets include loans, investments in bonds and inter-bank placements. Some analysts use total assets instead, so check the definition when comparing.

NIM is the core profitability measure for banks because lending is their main business. In India, NIMs typically fall roughly in a band of about 2.5% to 4.5% depending on the bank's business mix. Drivers include: the CASA ratio (cheaper deposits lift NIM); the loan mix (unsecured retail and microfinance loans earn more than corporate loans); asset quality (interest on NPAs is not recognised, so high NPAs reduce NIM); and the rate cycle.

With most retail loans now linked to the external repo benchmark, loan rates reprice quickly after an RBI rate change, while fixed deposit rates change only when deposits renew. So when RBI cuts rates, NIMs often shrink in the short term; when it hikes, NIMs often widen first.

NIM differs from spread. Spread is the yield on advances minus the cost of deposits, two rates subtracted from each other. NIM uses income and expense amounts over the asset base, so it also reflects how much of the balance sheet earns interest and how it is funded.

Example

A bank's average interest-earning assets are ₹3,00,000 crore. It earns interest of ₹25,500 crore and pays ₹15,300 crore on deposits and borrowings. NII = ₹10,200 crore. NIM = 10,200/3,00,000 = 3.4%. If a repo cut lowers loan yields by 0.25% on half the assets (₹1,50,000 crore) before deposit costs fall, income drops by ₹375 crore and NIM falls to 9,825/3,00,000 = 3.275%.

FAQs

Why do bank NIMs fall when RBI cuts the repo rate?

Most retail and MSE loans are linked to the repo rate and reprice within three months, while fixed deposits keep their old rates until maturity. So interest income falls faster than interest expense, compressing NIM temporarily. As deposits renew at lower rates, margins usually recover partly.

Is a higher NIM always better?

A higher NIM generally means more profitable lending, but it may come from riskier loans, such as unsecured personal loans or microfinance, that can produce higher credit losses later. Analysts look at NIM together with credit costs, asset quality and return on assets to judge real profitability.
