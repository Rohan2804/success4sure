What is Delisting of Shares?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Delisting of Shares: Delisting is the permanent removal of a company's shares from a stock exchange, after which they can no longer be traded on that exchange.

Delisting in India is governed by the SEBI (Delisting of Equity Shares) Regulations, 2021. There are two types. Voluntary delisting happens when the promoters choose to take the company private and buy out public shareholders. Compulsory delisting happens when an exchange removes a company as a penalty, for example for prolonged non-compliance, suspension or failure to pay listing fees.

In voluntary delisting, a floor price is calculated under SEBI's formula, and the traditional method is reverse book building: public shareholders offer their shares at prices they choose, and the discovered price is the one at which the acquirer's holding reaches 90% of total shares. The acquirer may accept that price or make a counter-offer. SEBI amendments in 2024 also allow a fixed-price route for certain companies at a premium over the floor price — check the current regulations for conditions.

If delisting succeeds, shareholders who did not tender can still sell to the promoters at the exit price for at least one year through an exit window. If it fails, shares tendered are returned and the company stays listed.

Compulsory delisting is harsher for investors: there is no buyer at a premium. Promoters must buy shares at a fair value fixed by an independent valuer, and the company, its whole-time directors and promoters are barred from accessing the securities market for ten years. Shareholders of delisted companies still own their shares but may find them very hard to sell.

Example

A promoter holds 80% of a company with 10 crore shares and wants to delist. To cross 90%, at least 1 crore more shares (10% of total) must be acquired. In reverse book building, public shareholders bid between the floor price of ₹200 and higher prices. If the cumulative offer reaches 1 crore shares at ₹265, the discovered price is ₹265, and the acquirer must pay about ₹265 crore to complete the purchase at that price.

FAQs

What happens to my shares if a company is delisted?

You still own them in your demat account. In voluntary delisting, you can tender during the offer or sell to the promoters at the exit price during the exit window. In compulsory delisting, promoters must buy at the fair value fixed by an independent valuer. After that, selling usually requires finding a private buyer.

Can a delisted company list again?

Yes, but after a waiting period. Under SEBI rules, a company that delisted voluntarily cannot reapply for listing for a specified number of years, and a compulsorily delisted company faces a longer bar. It must then follow the normal listing process, including a fresh offer document.
