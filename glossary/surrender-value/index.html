What is Surrender Value?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Surrender Value: Surrender value is the amount a life insurer pays the policyholder who voluntarily ends the policy before maturity, after it has acquired a surrender value under its terms.

When you surrender a policy, the contract ends and all future benefits, including life cover, stop. You receive the surrender value, which is usually well below the premiums paid in the early years because the insurer has already spent on commissions, underwriting and the risk cover provided.

Traditional policies quote two values. The Guaranteed Surrender Value (GSV) is a minimum stated in the policy, calculated as a percentage of total premiums paid (excluding extra premiums and rider premiums), with the percentage rising with the number of years the policy has run. The Special Surrender Value (SSV) is a non-guaranteed amount the insurer calculates using the paid-up value, accrued bonuses and a surrender factor; it is often higher than the GSV. The insurer pays the higher of the two.

When does a policy acquire surrender value? Older rules required at least two or three full years' premiums. IRDAI's 2024 product regulations allow surrender value after one full year's premium in many cases and require insurers to make early-exit values fairer. Check the product brochure and current IRDAI rules (as of 2026). For ULIPs, surrender within the five-year lock-in moves the money to a discontinued policy fund, paid out only after the lock-in ends, minus a capped discontinuance charge.

A common mistake is surrendering a long-term policy in a hurry for cash. Alternatives include a policy loan (up to a percentage of surrender value), making it paid-up, or assigning it. Surrender can also have tax consequences, so check before acting.

Example

Kavita paid ₹30,000 a year for 5 years on a 20-year endowment plan, total ₹1,50,000. The GSV factor for year 5 is, say, 50%, so GSV = ₹75,000. Her paid-up sum assured is ₹6 lakh × 5/20 = ₹1,50,000; with ₹40,000 of bonuses, total paid-up value = ₹1,90,000. With an SSV factor of 0.48, SSV = ₹91,200. She receives the higher value, ₹91,200, and her ₹6 lakh life cover ends.

FAQs

Why is surrender value so low in the early years?

In the first few years, a large part of the premium goes towards agent commission, policy issue costs, underwriting and the cost of life cover already provided. Only the remaining part is invested for the policyholder. Surrender values are therefore low early on and improve as the policy runs longer.

Is surrendering better than letting a policy lapse?

If the policy has acquired a surrender value, surrendering returns some money, while lapsing may convert it to a reduced paid-up policy that still provides some cover and pays at maturity. The better choice depends on your need for cover and cash. A policy loan is another option if the need for money is temporary.
