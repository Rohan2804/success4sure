What is Endowment Policy?

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Endowment Policy: An endowment policy is a life insurance plan that pays the sum assured, along with any bonuses, either on the death of the life assured during the term or on survival to the end of the term.

Endowment plans combine protection and savings. Part of the premium covers the life risk and the rest builds a fund, invested mainly in government securities and bonds by the insurer. Because the insurer pays in both situations, death or survival, premiums are much higher than for term insurance with the same sum assured.

With-profit (participating) endowment plans share in the insurer's surplus through bonuses. Reversionary bonuses, declared yearly as a rate per ₹1,000 of sum assured, are added to the policy and paid with the sum assured at maturity or on death. A final or terminal bonus may also be paid at the end. Once declared, reversionary bonuses are guaranteed. Without-profit (non-participating) plans pay only guaranteed amounts, sometimes as guaranteed additions.

Variants include money-back plans (which pay a part of the sum assured at intervals during the term), double endowment and joint life endowment. Policies acquire surrender and paid-up values after the minimum period, and policy loans are available.

For IC-38, remember: endowment = death benefit + survival benefit. The trade-off is lower life cover for the premium and modest returns, so they suit people who value guaranteed, disciplined savings with some cover. Compare the cover needed with a term plan before choosing.

Example

Sunita buys a 20-year with-profit endowment policy with a sum assured of ₹5 lakh at an annual premium of ₹24,000. If the insurer declares an average reversionary bonus of ₹45 per ₹1,000 each year, annual bonus = 5,00,000/1,000 × 45 = ₹22,500, totalling ₹4,50,000 over 20 years. At maturity she could receive ₹5,00,000 + ₹4,50,000 plus any final bonus, against premiums of ₹4,80,000. If she dies in year 8, her nominee gets ₹5,00,000 + bonuses accrued to date (₹1,80,000).

FAQs

What is the difference between an endowment plan and a term plan?

A term plan pays only on death within the term and nothing on survival, so its premium is low. An endowment plan pays on death or on survival to maturity, so it costs much more for the same cover. Term plans focus on protection; endowment plans combine protection with savings.

Are bonuses in an endowment policy guaranteed?

Bonuses are not guaranteed in advance; the insurer declares them each year based on its surplus and investment experience. However, once a reversionary bonus is declared and attached to the policy, it cannot be taken away and becomes payable along with the sum assured at maturity or on death.
