What is Total Return Index (TRI)?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Total Return Index (TRI): A total return index (TRI) measures an index's performance assuming all dividends paid by its constituent companies are reinvested, unlike a price return index which tracks only price changes.

The Nifty 50 figure you see on news channels is a price return index. When a company pays a dividend, its share price drops, and the price index records a fall even though shareholders received cash. A TRI adds the dividend back as if reinvested in the index, giving a true picture of what an investor would have earned.

Mutual funds receive dividends from the companies they hold and those dividends become part of NAV. Comparing a fund with a price index therefore made funds look better than they really were. SEBI required mutual funds to benchmark against TRI from February 2018, closing this gap.

SEBI later introduced tiered benchmarking: a first-tier benchmark common to the category, such as Nifty 500 TRI for flexi cap funds, and an optional second-tier benchmark reflecting the fund's own style.

The gap between TRI and price index is roughly the dividend yield of the index each year, often around 1–1.5% for Indian large cap indices. Learners often see '15% Nifty return' in the news and wonder why an index fund returned 16%; the answer is usually TRI.

Example

Over a year, the Nifty 50 price index rises from 22,000 to 24,200, a 10% price return. Its constituent companies pay dividends equal to about 1.3% of the index value. The Nifty 50 TRI therefore rises about 11.3%. A large cap fund that returned 10.8% looked like an outperformer against the price index but actually lagged the TRI by about 0.5 percentage point.

FAQs

Why do mutual funds use TRI as benchmark?

Because funds collect dividends from their holdings and include them in NAV. A price index ignores dividends, so comparing against it overstates a fund's relative performance. SEBI made TRI benchmarking mandatory in 2018 to ensure a fair comparison.

Is Nifty 50 TRI the same as Nifty 50?

No. Nifty 50 is the price return index tracking only prices. Nifty 50 TRI also includes reinvested dividends, so it grows faster over time. The difference each year is roughly the index's dividend yield.
