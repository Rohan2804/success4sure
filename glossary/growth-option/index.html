What is Growth option?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Growth option: The growth option is a mutual fund choice under which no income is paid out; all earnings remain invested and are reflected in a rising NAV.

When you pick the growth option, interest, dividends received from companies and realised gains stay inside the scheme. Your number of units stays the same and the NAV rises over time, so the value of your holding is simply units × current NAV. The compounding happens quietly inside the fund.

Tax is deferred until you redeem. Only then is the gain (sale value minus purchase cost) taxed as short or long term capital gain, depending on the scheme type and holding period. Nothing is taxed while you hold. This makes growth the default choice for long-term goals such as retirement or a child's education.

The growth option of a scheme and its IDCW option share the same portfolio and expense ratio within a plan, but have different NAVs because the IDCW NAV drops each time a payout is made. Comparing returns across these options without adjusting for payouts gives misleading results.

Investors who need cash flow can still choose growth and set up a systematic withdrawal plan. One mistake is assuming growth means a 'growth stock' strategy; it says nothing about how the fund invests, only what happens to its earnings.

Example

Sameer invests ₹1,00,000 in the growth option of an equity fund at NAV ₹50, getting 2,000 units. Five years later the NAV is ₹88. His holding is worth 2,000 × ₹88 = ₹1,76,000. He paid no tax during these years. If he redeems now, his long-term gain is ₹76,000, taxed under the equity LTCG rules for that year after any available exemption.

FAQs

Can I switch from IDCW to growth in the same scheme?

Yes, but it is treated as a redemption from the IDCW option and a fresh purchase in the growth option. That may trigger exit load if within the load period, and capital gains tax on the units redeemed. Check costs before switching.

Why do growth and IDCW options have different NAVs?

Both start at the same NAV, but every IDCW payout reduces the IDCW option's NAV. The growth option keeps all earnings, so its NAV ends up higher. The underlying portfolio and returns before payouts are the same.
