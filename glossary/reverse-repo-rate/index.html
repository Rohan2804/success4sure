What is Reverse Repo Rate?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Reverse Repo Rate: Reverse repo rate is the interest rate RBI pays banks when they lend surplus money to RBI and receive government securities as collateral in return.

Reverse repo is the mirror image of repo. When banks have more cash than they need, they can lend it to RBI. RBI gives them government securities as collateral and pays interest at the reverse repo rate. The tool absorbs excess liquidity from the banking system, so it is called a liquidity-absorption window.

For many years the reverse repo rate was the floor of RBI's interest-rate corridor, with a fixed gap below the repo rate. A limitation was that RBI needed enough government securities to hand over as collateral. In April 2022 RBI introduced the Standing Deposit Facility (SDF), which absorbs funds without collateral, and made SDF the new floor. The fixed-rate reverse repo still exists on paper but is now a secondary tool used at RBI's discretion; RBI also runs variable rate reverse repo (VRRR) auctions to drain surplus liquidity.

Why does it matter for exams? Questions often ask which rate absorbs liquidity (reverse repo, SDF) and which injects it (repo, MSF). Older study material may say 'reverse repo is the floor of the LAF corridor'. That was true before April 2022, but for current-affairs papers like RBI Grade B, the SDF rate is the floor. Since the fixed reverse repo rate has not been revised alongside the policy rate for a long time, verify the figure in the latest RBI statement (as of 2026).

A common mistake is to think a high reverse repo rate is good for borrowers. It is the opposite: an attractive rate for parking money with RBI makes banks less eager to lend to customers at low rates.

Example

Say a bank has ₹500 crore idle at the end of the day. It parks the money with RBI under a 14-day VRRR auction at a cut-off of 5.20%. Interest earned = 500 × 5.20% × 14/365 = about ₹0.997 crore, roughly ₹99.7 lakh. If instead it uses the overnight SDF window at 5.25%, it earns 500 × 5.25%/365 = about ₹7.19 lakh for one night, without RBI needing to give it securities.

FAQs

Is reverse repo rate always lower than repo rate?

Yes. RBI pays banks less for parking money than it charges them for borrowing, otherwise banks could borrow and re-lend to RBI for a riskless profit. The spread between the two has changed over time, and since 2022 the SDF rate, not the fixed reverse repo rate, sits just below repo as the corridor floor.

What is variable rate reverse repo (VRRR)?

VRRR is an auction in which banks bid the interest rate at which they will park surplus funds with RBI for a fixed term, such as 7 or 14 days. RBI decides the amount to absorb, and the cut-off rate comes from the bids. It lets RBI drain large, lasting surpluses more flexibly than the overnight windows.
