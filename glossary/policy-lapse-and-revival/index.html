What is Policy Lapse and Revival?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Policy Lapse and Revival: A policy lapses when the premium is not paid by the end of the grace period, ending the insurance cover; revival is the process of restoring it by paying arrears and meeting conditions.

When a policy lapses, the insurer is no longer liable to pay the death benefit. For traditional policies, if premiums were paid for the minimum period needed to acquire a paid-up value (usually set in the product terms), the policy does not fully lapse but becomes 'paid-up' with a reduced sum assured. For ULIPs within the five-year lock-in, the fund moves to a discontinued policy fund.

Revival lets the policyholder restore full benefits within a revival period. IRDAI product regulations allow five years from the date of the first unpaid premium for non-linked policies and three years for linked policies (ULIPs). Check the latest IRDAI rules (as of 2026). To revive, the policyholder must pay all overdue premiums with interest at the rate set by the insurer, and submit a declaration of good health; for longer lapses or large covers, the insurer may require a fresh medical examination.

The insurer can accept, reject or accept the revival with modified terms, because the risk may have changed since the policy was issued. Once revived, the contestability period under Section 45 restarts from the date of revival for statements made at revival. Some insurers run special revival campaigns with reduced late fees.

Common misunderstanding: reviving an old policy is often cheaper than buying a new one at an older age, but it is not automatic. Also, any claim during the lapsed period is not covered, even if the policy is later revived.

Example

Ramesh's annual premium of ₹24,000 fell due on 1 June 2024 and he did not pay it then, nor on 1 June 2025. In August 2025 he wants to revive. Arrears = ₹48,000. If the insurer charges 9% simple interest, interest ≈ 24,000 × 9% × 14.5/12 + 24,000 × 9% × 2.5/12 = ₹2,610 + ₹450 = ₹3,060. He pays about ₹51,060 and submits a good-health declaration. He is within five years from the first unpaid premium, so revival is allowed.

FAQs

Can a lapsed policy always be revived?

Only within the revival period, which is five years for traditional and three years for unit-linked policies under IRDAI rules, and subject to the insurer's acceptance of risk. The insurer can ask for medical tests and may refuse or impose conditions if health has worsened. After the revival period, the policy cannot be revived.

What happens to the money paid in a lapsed policy?

It depends on how long premiums were paid. If the policy acquired a surrender or paid-up value, you can receive the surrender value or keep a reduced paid-up cover. If it lapsed very early, before acquiring any value, the premiums paid may be lost, which is why early lapse is costly.
