What is Standard deviation (volatility)?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Standard deviation (volatility): Standard deviation measures how widely a fund's returns are spread around their average; a higher figure means more volatile, less predictable returns.

To calculate it, take a series of returns (say monthly returns over three years), find the average, measure how far each return is from the average, square these differences, average them and take the square root. Factsheets usually report annualised standard deviation, which for monthly data is the monthly figure multiplied by the square root of 12.

If returns are roughly normally distributed, about two-thirds of outcomes fall within one standard deviation of the average and about 95% within two. A fund with average return 12% and standard deviation 15% could reasonably see years anywhere from −18% to +42%.

Standard deviation is the risk input in the Sharpe ratio and in modern portfolio theory. Advisers use it to compare funds within a category and to explain to clients what range of outcomes to expect.

Its limitations: it treats upside and downside surprises the same, it understates the chance of extreme events, and it is backward-looking. A debt fund can show very low standard deviation for years and then fall sharply after a credit default. Use it alongside measures like maximum drawdown.

Example

Over five years, Fund A returned 18%, −6%, 24%, 9% and −2%; average 8.6%, sample standard deviation about 12.8%. Fund B returned 10%, 8%, 12%, 9% and 11%; average 10%, standard deviation about 1.6%. Fund A's investors lived through a 6% loss year and a 24% gain year, while Fund B's had a much smoother ride and a higher average too.

FAQs

What is a good standard deviation for a mutual fund?

There is no absolute good figure; it depends on the asset class. Liquid funds show very low numbers, equity funds much higher. Compare a fund with its category average and benchmark over the same period, and consider it with returns using ratios like Sharpe.

Is standard deviation the same as risk?

It is one measure of risk, specifically volatility. It does not capture the risk of permanent loss, credit defaults, liquidity problems or the risk of not meeting a goal. Investors should look at several measures and at what the fund actually holds.
