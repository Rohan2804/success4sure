What is IRAC Norms (Income Recognition and Asset Classification)?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

IRAC Norms (Income Recognition and Asset Classification): IRAC norms are RBI's prudential rules on Income Recognition, Asset Classification and provisioning, which decide when a loan is bad and how much a bank must set aside for it.

Introduced in 1992-93 following the Narasimham Committee, IRAC norms brought Indian bank accounting close to international practice. Income recognition: interest on an NPA cannot be booked on accrual basis; it is recognised only when actually received. Interest already booked but not collected is reversed when the account turns NPA.

Asset classification puts loans into four categories. Standard assets are performing loans. Substandard assets have remained NPA for up to 12 months. Doubtful assets have remained in substandard category for 12 months; they are further split into D1 (doubtful up to 1 year), D2 (1 to 3 years) and D3 (more than 3 years). Loss assets are those identified by the bank, auditors or RBI as uncollectible, even if not written off.

Provisioning rises with age. Broad current rates: standard assets generally 0.40% (higher for some sectors, lower for farm and SME loans); substandard 15% (25% if unsecured); doubtful: 100% of the unsecured portion plus, on the secured portion, 25% for D1, 40% for D2 and 100% for D3; loss assets 100%. RBI has proposed shifting to an expected credit loss approach, so check current RBI circulars (as of 2026).

Classic trap: the 12-month clocks run from the date of becoming NPA, not from the date of default. Also, erosion in security value can make RBI treat an account as doubtful or loss directly, without waiting.

Example

A ₹50 lakh loan with security realisable at ₹30 lakh turned NPA on 1 April 2023. It is substandard till 31 March 2024, needing 15% × 50 = ₹7.5 lakh. From 1 April 2024 it is D1: unsecured ₹20 lakh × 100% + secured ₹30 lakh × 25% = ₹27.5 lakh. In D2 (from April 2025): 20 + 30 × 40% = ₹32 lakh. In D3: the full ₹50 lakh.

FAQs

How long does an account remain substandard?

An account remains substandard for a maximum of 12 months from the date it became an NPA. After that, if not upgraded, it moves to doubtful category. If the security is seriously eroded or fraud is involved, it may be downgraded faster, directly to doubtful or loss.

Why is provision higher for unsecured loans?

If a secured loan fails, the bank can sell the security to recover part of the dues. An unsecured loan has no such backstop, so the expected loss is higher. RBI therefore requires extra provisioning on unsecured exposures, such as 25% instead of 15% in substandard and 100% on the unsecured part of doubtful assets.
