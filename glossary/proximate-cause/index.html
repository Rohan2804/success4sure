What is Proximate Cause?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Proximate Cause: Proximate cause is the active and efficient cause that sets in motion a chain of events resulting in a loss, without the intervention of any new independent force.

Many losses result from several causes acting together or one after another. To decide whether a claim is covered, insurers and courts identify the dominant, effective cause, not necessarily the one closest in time. If that proximate cause is an insured peril, the claim is payable; if it is an excluded peril, it is not.

When there is an unbroken chain, the first cause is the proximate cause. For example, if an earthquake (excluded under a standard fire policy unless added) causes a fire, the loss is due to the earthquake. When a new, independent cause breaks the chain, the new cause becomes proximate. If insured and excluded perils act concurrently and their effects cannot be separated, the exclusion usually prevails; if they can be separated, the insured part is paid.

In life insurance, proximate cause matters mainly for riders. A basic life policy pays on death from any cause (except specific exclusions like suicide in the first year). But an Accidental Death Benefit rider pays only if an accident was the proximate cause. If a person suffers a heart attack while driving and then crashes, the proximate cause is the heart attack, so the ADB rider may not pay even though the base policy will.

The principle comes from English law (Pawsey v Scottish Union, 1907) and is part of IC-38's core principles alongside utmost good faith, insurable interest and indemnity.

Example

A godown with stock worth ₹25 lakh is insured under a fire policy that excludes riot. A riot breaks out and rioters set the godown on fire, causing ₹18 lakh of damage. The proximate cause is the riot, an excluded peril, so the claim is rejected. If instead an electrical short circuit had started the fire, an insured cause, the ₹18 lakh loss would be payable, subject to policy terms and average clause.

FAQs

Is proximate cause the cause nearest in time to the loss?

Not necessarily. Proximate cause is the dominant, effective cause that started the chain of events, even if other events occurred in between. The last event before the loss may be just a link in the chain. Courts and insurers look at what was truly responsible for the loss.

How does proximate cause apply to accident riders?

An accident rider pays only if an accident is the proximate cause of death or disability. If illness caused the event, such as a stroke causing a fall, the rider may not pay. The basic life cover still pays on death from any cause, subject to standard exclusions like suicide within the first year.
