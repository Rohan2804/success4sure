What is Pay-in and Pay-out?

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Pay-in and Pay-out: Pay-in is when clearing members deliver funds and securities to the clearing corporation; pay-out is when the clearing corporation releases them to the receiving members and clients.

Settlement has two legs. During pay-in, selling members deliver securities from clients' demat accounts to the clearing corporation's account at the depository, and buying members' clearing banks transfer the required funds. During pay-out, the clearing corporation credits the securities to buyers and the funds to sellers' members. Under T+1, both happen on the next working day after the trade.

SEBI has tightened how pay-out reaches clients. Securities are now paid out directly from the clearing corporation to the client's demat account rather than to a broker pool account, which reduced the risk of brokers misusing client shares. For funds, the broker must credit the client or keep the money in a running account settlement as per the client's choice and SEBI rules.

Sellers' shares are taken for pay-in using early pay-in or a DDPI or electronic authorisation given by the client. Buyers must have paid funds to their broker by the pay-in deadline; if not, the broker may square off or the shares may be held as unpaid securities and sold after a few days, as per SEBI rules on unpaid securities.

A misconception is that pay-out reaching your broker means it has reached you. In the funds leg, there can be a short gap while the broker processes payouts to your bank, and many brokers settle client funds periodically under the running account system unless you choose otherwise.

Example

On Monday, Arjun sells 200 shares at ₹350 and Neha buys 200 shares at ₹350 through different brokers. On Tuesday morning, Arjun's broker delivers the 200 shares (pay-in of securities) and Neha's broker's clearing bank pays ₹70,000 (pay-in of funds). After processing, the clearing corporation credits the 200 shares directly into Neha's demat account and releases ₹70,000 to Arjun's broker (pay-out), which credits Arjun's ledger.

FAQs

What is early pay-in?

Early pay-in means the seller's shares are delivered to the clearing corporation before the normal pay-in deadline, often on trade day itself when the client sells holdings. It helps brokers avoid collecting margins on the sale, because the delivery obligation is already met, and reduces the risk of short delivery.

What is a running account settlement?

It is an arrangement where a broker keeps client funds in a running account and settles them periodically — monthly or quarterly as the client chooses — rather than after every trade. SEBI requires brokers to actually settle these accounts on the scheduled dates and to return excess funds as per prescribed norms.
