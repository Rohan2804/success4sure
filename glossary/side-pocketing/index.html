What is Side pocketing (segregated portfolio)?

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Side pocketing (segregated portfolio): Side pocketing is the separation of a defaulted or sharply downgraded debt security into a segregated portfolio, so it does not affect the main portfolio's NAV and liquidity.

SEBI allowed mutual funds to create segregated portfolios from December 2018, after defaults by some large non-banking companies. When a debt or money market instrument suffers a credit event, typically a downgrade below investment grade or a default, the AMC may, with trustee approval, move that security into a separate portfolio.

On that day, every existing unit holder receives units in the segregated portfolio equal to their units in the main portfolio. The main portfolio continues as normal, with the bad paper removed and a lower NAV. The segregated portfolio cannot accept new investment or redemptions; its units are listed on an exchange for exit, and any money recovered from the issuer is paid out to holders of those units.

The benefit is fairness. Without side pocketing, quick-moving investors could redeem at a NAV that did not fully reflect the problem, leaving those who stayed to bear the loss. Segregation ensures recoveries go to investors who held units at the time of the event, and stops new investors from buying into it.

Side pocketing is not a rescue. The loss on the bad security is still marked down; it is merely isolated. SEBI also links fund manager performance assessment and AMC fees to these events to discourage risky credit calls.

Example

Anita holds 10,000 units of a credit risk fund at NAV ₹15 (₹1,50,000). A bond forming 5% of the portfolio is downgraded to default. The AMC side-pockets it. Anita now holds 10,000 main-portfolio units at about ₹14.25 (₹1,42,500) and 10,000 segregated units valued by the AMC at, say, ₹0.20 (₹2,000) after the markdown. If the issuer later repays 40% of what it owes, she receives her share of the recovery from the segregated portfolio.

FAQs

Can I redeem units of a segregated portfolio?

Not from the AMC. The segregated portfolio is closed for subscriptions and redemptions. Its units are listed on a stock exchange so investors can sell them, though trading is thin. Otherwise you wait for recoveries, which are paid out as and when the AMC receives money from the issuer.

Is side pocketing allowed for equity funds?

The SEBI framework applies to debt and money market instruments affected by credit events, and to certain unrated or unlisted securities in specific situations. Equity shares are not side-pocketed under this mechanism. Check current SEBI circulars for exact conditions.
