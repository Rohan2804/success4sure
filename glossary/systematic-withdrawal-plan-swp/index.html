What is Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP)?

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP): A Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) lets an investor redeem a fixed amount or the appreciation from a mutual fund scheme at regular intervals, creating a steady cash flow.

An SWP is the mirror image of a SIP. You hold units in a scheme and instruct the AMC to redeem enough units each month or quarter to pay a fixed sum into your bank account. Retirees use it to draw a 'salary' from a corpus, and parents use it to pay college fees over several years.

Each withdrawal is a redemption. The AMC sells units at that day's NAV, so the number of units sold varies: more units go when NAV is low. Only the gain portion of each redemption is taxable as capital gain, which is why an SWP is often more tax-efficient than an IDCW payout where the full distribution is taxed at slab rate.

The key risk is sequence of returns. If markets fall sharply in the early years while you keep withdrawing, you sell many units cheaply and the corpus may not recover. Retirement advisers therefore keep two or three years of withdrawals in debt or liquid funds and run the SWP from there.

An SWP is not a guaranteed pension. If the withdrawal rate is higher than the portfolio's return, the corpus shrinks and eventually runs out. Exit loads may apply to units redeemed within the load period.

Example

Mr. Iyer, 62, has ₹20 lakh in a conservative hybrid fund and sets up an SWP of ₹15,000 a month (₹1.8 lakh a year, 9% of the corpus). If the fund earns 8% a year, compounded monthly, the corpus would still be about ₹16.95 lakh after 10 years and would last roughly 331 months, about 27.5 years. If returns were lower or he withdrew more, it would run out much sooner. These figures are illustrations, not projections.

FAQs

Is SWP income taxable?

Only the capital gain inside each withdrawal is taxed, not the full amount. Part of every redemption is your own original investment returning to you. Whether the gain is short or long term depends on how long those specific units were held, using first-in-first-out. Tax rules change, so check current income tax department guidance.

How much can I withdraw through an SWP safely?

There is no single safe figure. It depends on the corpus, expected return, inflation and how many years the money must last. Many planners test withdrawal rates of around 3–5% a year for long retirements. A registered investment adviser can model your situation; the fund house itself will not set the rate for you.
