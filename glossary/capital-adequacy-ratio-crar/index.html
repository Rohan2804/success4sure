What is Capital Adequacy Ratio (CRAR)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Capital Adequacy Ratio (CRAR): Capital adequacy ratio, or capital to risk-weighted assets ratio (CRAR), is a bank's capital expressed as a percentage of its risk-weighted assets, showing its ability to absorb losses.

Formula: CRAR = (Tier 1 capital + Tier 2 capital) ÷ Risk-weighted assets × 100. Tier 1 is core capital: equity share capital, reserves and retained profits (Common Equity Tier 1), plus Additional Tier 1 instruments such as perpetual bonds. Tier 2 is supplementary capital: certain subordinated debt, general provisions within limits and revaluation reserves at a discount.

Risk-weighted assets (RWA) recognise that not every loan is equally risky. Each asset is multiplied by a risk weight. Cash and central government securities carry 0%; a home loan with a low loan-to-value ratio might carry a lower weight than an unsecured personal loan; a corporate loan's weight depends on its credit rating. RWA also includes charges for market risk and operational risk.

In India, banks must maintain a minimum CRAR of 9% under Basel III, plus a 2.5% capital conservation buffer, effectively 11.5%. Small finance banks and payments banks have a higher minimum of 15%. NBFCs have their own CRAR norms of 15%. These are as of 2026; verify with RBI.

Why it matters: a bank with low CRAR cannot grow its loan book without raising capital, and RBI may place it under Prompt Corrective Action (PCA), which restricts lending, branch expansion and dividends. Investors and depositors watch CRAR as a basic health indicator.

Example

A bank has: cash and government securities ₹40,000 crore (0% weight), home loans ₹30,000 crore (35% weight), corporate loans ₹50,000 crore (100%), and unsecured retail ₹10,000 crore (125%). Credit RWA = 0 + 10,500 + 50,000 + 12,500 = ₹73,000 crore. Add ₹7,000 crore for market and operational risk: RWA = ₹80,000 crore. With Tier 1 of ₹8,800 crore and Tier 2 of ₹1,600 crore, CRAR = 10,400/80,000 = 13%.

FAQs

What is the minimum CRAR for Indian banks?

Scheduled commercial banks must maintain total capital of at least 9% of risk-weighted assets, plus a 2.5% capital conservation buffer in common equity, making 11.5% overall. D-SIBs need an additional surcharge. Small finance banks and payments banks must maintain 15%. These figures are as of 2026; check RBI's latest master circular.

Is CRAR the same as leverage ratio?

No. CRAR divides capital by risk-weighted assets, so risky assets need more capital. The leverage ratio divides Tier 1 capital by total exposure without risk weights, acting as a simple backstop to stop banks from building huge balance sheets of assets with low risk weights.
