What is Overnight fund?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Overnight fund: An overnight fund is a debt mutual fund that invests only in securities maturing in one business day, mainly tri-party repos and reverse repos.

Overnight funds sit at the lowest end of the risk spectrum among SEBI's debt fund categories. Every day the fund lends its money for one night, mostly through TREPS (tri-party repo on government securities) on the Clearing Corporation of India platform, and gets it back the next morning. Because the loans are collateralised by government securities and last one day, both credit risk and interest rate risk are minimal.

Returns closely follow the overnight money market rate, which in turn tracks the RBI's policy corridor. When the RBI raises or cuts rates, overnight fund yields change almost immediately. Over a year, returns are usually a little lower than a liquid fund's because the fund gives up the small extra yield on 30–90 day paper.

Overnight funds have no exit load and, like liquid funds, publish NAV for every calendar day. Corporates and businesses use them to park cash for a few days, for example between receiving payments and paying salaries. Individuals sometimes use them for the portion of an emergency fund they may need very quickly.

Do not mistake low risk for no risk. Operational and counterparty issues are extremely unlikely but not impossible, and returns can fall below inflation.

Example

A small trading firm receives ₹50 lakh on Thursday and must pay suppliers the next Tuesday. It invests the money in an overnight fund at an annualised yield of about 6%. Over five calendar days the fund earns roughly ₹50,00,000 × 6% × 5 ÷ 365 ≈ ₹4,110 before tax. The money would otherwise have sat idle in a current account earning nothing.

FAQs

What is the difference between an overnight fund and a liquid fund?

An overnight fund holds only one-day securities, so it has almost no interest rate or credit risk. A liquid fund can hold instruments up to 91 days, including commercial paper, giving slightly higher returns with slightly more risk. Liquid funds also carry a small graded exit load for six days; overnight funds usually have none.

Are overnight funds suitable for long-term investing?

Generally no. Their returns track short-term interest rates and are likely to be lower than inflation plus taxes over long periods. They are designed for parking money for days or weeks, not for goals several years away.
