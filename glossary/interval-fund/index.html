What is Interval fund?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Interval fund: An interval fund is a mutual fund scheme that combines close-ended and open-ended features, allowing purchases and redemptions only during pre-specified transaction intervals.

Between transaction windows an interval fund behaves like a close-ended scheme: no fresh subscriptions and no redemptions with the AMC. During the specified transaction period, which SEBI requires to be at least a few business days, investors can buy or redeem units at NAV. The windows recur at intervals stated in the scheme information document, such as every quarter or year.

Like close-ended schemes, interval fund units must be listed on a stock exchange so investors have some exit route outside the windows, although trading is usually limited.

The structure helps a fund manager match the portfolio to known liquidity dates. A debt interval fund with quarterly windows can buy instruments maturing around those dates, reducing the need for distress selling. SEBI also limits how far the portfolio's maturity can extend beyond the next interval.

Interval funds are relatively rare in India today, but they are a standard exam topic. Do not confuse them with schemes that simply have lock-ins (ELSS) or with SIP intervals.

Example

A debt interval fund has a transaction window from 1 to 5 March, June, September and December. Ravi invests ₹2 lakh on 3 June at NAV ₹20, getting 10,000 units. In August he needs cash; the AMC will not redeem, so he can sell on the exchange or wait. On 2 September he redeems directly at that day's NAV of ₹20.35, receiving ₹2,03,500.

FAQs

Is an interval fund open-ended or close-ended?

SEBI's classification treats it as a hybrid of the two. It is close-ended between transaction periods and allows entry and exit like an open-ended fund during specified windows. Exam questions often describe it as a fund with features of both.

Why must interval funds be listed?

Listing gives investors a way to exit between transaction windows by selling to another investor on the exchange. Without it, investors could be stuck until the next interval. Actual liquidity on the exchange may be limited.
