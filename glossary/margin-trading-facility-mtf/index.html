What is Margin Trading Facility (MTF)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Margin Trading Facility (MTF): A margin trading facility lets an investor buy eligible shares by paying only part of the value upfront, with the stock broker funding the rest and charging interest.

MTF is offered by SEBI-registered brokers under exchange and SEBI rules. Only stocks in an eligible list (largely liquid Group I securities and certain ETFs) can be bought under MTF. The client pays the initial margin — at least the VaR and extreme loss margin required for that stock — and the broker lends the balance from its own funds or approved borrowings.

The purchased shares are pledged in favour of the broker as collateral, using a specific MTF pledge. The client pays interest on the funded amount for each day the position is held, at the rate set by the broker. If the share price falls and the client's margin falls below the required level, the broker issues a margin call; if it is not met, the broker can sell the shares.

Leverage cuts both ways. A small price rise produces a larger percentage gain on the client's own money, and a small fall produces a larger percentage loss — plus interest costs every day. Brokers must report MTF exposure to exchanges, and SEBI caps the broker's total MTF book relative to its net worth.

Many beginners confuse MTF with intraday leverage. Intraday positions must be closed the same day, while MTF lets you hold delivery positions for weeks or months. Another misconception is that MTF is free leverage; interest can quietly erode returns, especially if the position is held for long.

Example

Sunil buys shares worth ₹2,00,000 under MTF, paying ₹50,000 margin; the broker funds ₹1,50,000 at an illustrative 15% per annum. After 60 days, interest ≈ ₹1,50,000 × 15% × 60/365 = ₹3,699. If the stock rises 10%, the gain of ₹20,000 minus interest leaves ₹16,301 — a 32.6% return on his ₹50,000. If it falls 10%, the loss of ₹20,000 plus interest is ₹23,699 — a 47.4% loss.

FAQs

Which shares can be bought under MTF?

Only securities that appear in the exchanges' eligible list for margin trading, which is largely based on liquidity and trading frequency, plus some eligible ETFs. Brokers may restrict the list further. Shares outside this list must be bought with full payment.

What happens if I cannot meet a margin call in MTF?

If you do not add funds or collateral within the time given, the broker can sell some or all of the pledged shares to bring the account back within margin requirements. You bear any resulting loss and continue to owe any shortfall plus interest.
