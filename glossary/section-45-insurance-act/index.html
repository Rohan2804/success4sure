What is Section 45 of the Insurance Act?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Section 45 of the Insurance Act: Section 45 of the Insurance Act, 1938 says no life insurance policy can be called in question on any ground after three years from the date of issue, risk commencement, revival or rider addition, whichever is later.

Section 45 protects policyholders and their families from claims being rejected many years later over old statements in the proposal form. It was substantially rewritten by the Insurance Laws (Amendment) Act, 2015. The three years are counted from the date of the policy, the date of commencement of risk, the date of revival, or the date of the rider, whichever is latest.

Within three years, the insurer may repudiate a policy on the ground of fraud, or on the ground of misstatement or suppression of a material fact. It must communicate the grounds and materials for its decision in writing to the insured, nominee or legal representatives. If the repudiation is for fraud, the insurer need not refund the premium. If it is for innocent non-disclosure without fraud, the premiums collected until the date of repudiation must be refunded within 90 days.

Defences against a fraud allegation are built in: the insurer cannot repudiate for fraud if the policyholder proves that the misstatement was true to the best of their knowledge and belief with no deliberate intention to suppress, or that it was not within their knowledge. Silence is not fraud unless there was a duty to speak.

Two practical points: Section 45 does not prevent the insurer from asking for proof of age at any time and adjusting terms if age was misstated. And revival restarts the three-year clock only for statements made at revival.

Example

Meera buys a ₹50 lakh term plan on 1 July 2022 without disclosing a thyroid condition. Case 1: she dies on 1 March 2025, within three years; the insurer finds the non-disclosure, judges it material but not fraudulent, and repudiates, refunding premiums of ₹31,500 paid so far within 90 days. Case 2: she dies on 15 August 2025, more than three years after issue; the insurer cannot question the policy on any ground and must pay ₹50 lakh.

FAQs

Can an insurer reject a claim after three years for non-disclosure?

No. Under Section 45 as amended in 2015, a life insurance policy cannot be called in question on any ground, including fraud or non-disclosure, after three years from the latest of the policy issue, risk commencement, revival or rider date. The insurer can, however, adjust benefits if age was misstated.

Does reviving a lapsed policy restart the three-year period?

Yes, for statements made at the time of revival. If the policyholder makes a false or incomplete health declaration while reviving, the insurer can question the policy on that basis within three years from the revival date. Statements in the original proposal remain protected if more than three years have passed since issue.
