What is Specialised Investment Fund (SIF)?

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Specialised Investment Fund (SIF): A Specialised Investment Fund (SIF) is a SEBI-regulated product offered by eligible mutual funds that allows strategies such as long-short equity, with a high minimum investment.

SEBI created the SIF framework in 2025 to fill the gap between mutual funds, which have a ₹100–₹500 entry point and strict restrictions, and portfolio management services or alternative investment funds, which need ₹50 lakh or ₹1 crore. As of 2026 the minimum investment is ₹10 lakh per investor across all SIF strategies of an AMC, with exemptions for accredited investors; check SEBI's current rules.

SIFs are run by AMCs that meet SEBI's eligibility conditions on size or track record, under distinct branding from their mutual fund schemes. Permitted strategies include equity long-short, sector rotation, debt long-short and hybrid long-short approaches, offered as open-ended, close-ended or interval structures. A SIF may take limited unhedged short exposure through derivatives, within a cap on net assets set by SEBI.

Because strategies are more complex and riskier than ordinary mutual funds, distributors selling SIFs must hold the NISM Series V-D certification in addition to their ARN. SIFs carry their own risk band disclosures.

Learners should not assume a SIF is just a 'premium mutual fund'. Short positions can lose money when markets rise, liquidity terms may be restricted, and the strategies have limited track record in India.

Example

An investor puts ₹12 lakh into an equity long-short SIF. The fund holds ₹11 lakh of shares it likes and, through stock futures, takes a short position worth ₹2 lakh in stocks it considers overvalued, a short of about 17% of net assets. If the market rises 10% and both legs move with it, the long side gains ₹1.1 lakh and the short loses ₹20,000, a net gain of about ₹90,000 before costs. If the shorted stocks fall while the market rises, both legs gain.

FAQs

Who can invest in a Specialised Investment Fund?

Any eligible investor who meets the minimum investment threshold, ₹10 lakh across SIF strategies of an AMC as of 2026, can invest. Accredited investors are exempt from the threshold. The product is intended for investors who understand higher-risk strategies; check SEBI's latest circulars for current limits.

Which NISM exam is required to distribute SIFs?

NISM Series V-D, the Specialised Investment Fund Distributors certification, is required for individuals distributing SIF strategies, along with valid AMFI registration. Requirements may change, so verify with NISM and AMFI before starting distribution.
