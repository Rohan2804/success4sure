What is Put Option?

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Put Option: A put option gives its buyer the right, but not the obligation, to sell the underlying asset at the strike price on or before expiry, in exchange for a premium.

A put buyer benefits when the underlying price falls below the strike price. At expiry, the put's value is the greater of zero and (strike price − spot price). Breakeven for the buyer is strike price minus premium. If the price stays above the strike, the put expires worthless and the buyer loses only the premium.

The put seller receives the premium and must buy the underlying at the strike (or pay the difference in cash) if exercised. The seller's maximum gain is the premium, while the maximum loss is large — up to the strike price minus the premium if the underlying falls to zero. Exchanges therefore require margins from put writers.

Puts are the classic insurance tool. An investor holding shares can buy puts to cap the downside; this is called a protective put. Traders expecting a fall can buy puts instead of short selling, with known maximum loss. Fund managers buy index puts to hedge portfolios during uncertain periods such as elections or budget announcements.

Beginners sometimes think buying a put means they must own the shares. You do not need to hold the underlying to buy a put; you profit from the fall in price. Another misunderstanding: a put writer is bullish or neutral, not bearish, since the writer wants the price to stay above the strike.

Example

A share trades at ₹800. An investor buys a ₹780 put at ₹18 for a lot of 600 shares (premium ₹10,800). Breakeven is ₹762. If the share falls to ₹700 at expiry, the put is worth ₹80, a profit of (₹80 − ₹18) × 600 = ₹37,200. At ₹770 the put is worth ₹10, a net loss of ₹4,800. At ₹780 or above, the entire ₹10,800 is lost.

FAQs

Is buying a put the same as short selling?

Both profit from falling prices, but the risk profiles differ. A short seller faces unlimited loss if prices rise, while a put buyer's loss is limited to the premium. Puts also have a fixed expiry, so the fall must happen within that time to be profitable.

Why would someone sell a put option?

Put writers collect premium and profit if the price stays above the strike. Some investors write puts on stocks they would be happy to buy at the strike price, effectively getting paid while waiting. The risk is a sharp fall, which forces them to buy at a price well above the market.
