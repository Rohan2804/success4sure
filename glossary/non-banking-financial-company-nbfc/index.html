What is Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC): A non-banking financial company is a company registered under the Companies Act whose principal business is lending, investing, leasing or hire purchase, but which is not a bank.

NBFCs must register with RBI under Section 45-IA of the RBI Act, 1934, and hold minimum net owned funds as prescribed. The 'principal business' test applies: if financial assets are more than 50% of total assets and income from financial assets is more than 50% of gross income, the company is treated as an NBFC. Companies doing agriculture, industrial activity, trading in goods or buying property as their main business are not NBFCs.

Three key differences from banks: NBFCs cannot accept demand deposits (current and savings accounts); they are not part of the payment and settlement system and cannot issue cheques drawn on themselves; and deposits with deposit-taking NBFCs are not insured by DICGC. Only a small number of NBFCs are allowed to accept public fixed deposits, with limits on tenure and interest rates.

RBI introduced scale-based regulation in October 2022, dividing NBFCs into Base Layer, Middle Layer, Upper Layer and a Top Layer (kept empty unless needed). The bigger and more interconnected an NBFC, the stricter the rules, with Upper Layer NBFCs facing bank-like capital and governance requirements and mandatory listing. Types include investment and credit companies, housing finance companies, microfinance institutions, infrastructure finance companies and core investment companies.

Some financial firms are not regulated by RBI despite being finance companies: insurance companies (IRDAI), stock brokers and mutual funds (SEBI) and chit funds (state governments).

Example

Rahul takes a ₹6 lakh two-wheeler and personal loan bundle from an NBFC at 14% while his bank offered 11.5% but needed more paperwork. His father considers a 3-year NBFC fixed deposit at 8.5% against 7% at a bank. On ₹5 lakh, the NBFC FD would earn about ₹1.39 lakh over three years (compounded annually) versus about ₹1.13 lakh at the bank. But the NBFC deposit has no DICGC cover, while the bank deposit is insured up to ₹5 lakh.

FAQs

Can an NBFC open a savings account for customers?

No. NBFCs cannot accept demand deposits, which include savings and current accounts. Only some RBI-approved deposit-taking NBFCs can accept fixed deposits, for limited tenures. NBFCs also cannot issue cheque books on themselves because they are not part of the payment and settlement system.

Who regulates NBFCs in India?

Most NBFCs are registered and regulated by RBI under the RBI Act. Housing finance companies were moved from NHB to RBI regulation in 2019, though NHB still supervises them. Some finance-related entities are regulated by others: insurers by IRDAI, merchant bankers and mutual funds by SEBI, and chit funds by state governments.
