What is Gross Value Added (GVA)?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Gross Value Added (GVA): Gross Value Added is the value of output of a sector or the whole economy minus the value of intermediate goods and services used up in producing it.

GVA looks at the economy from the producer's side. For each firm, GVA = value of output − intermediate consumption. A bakery that sells bread worth ₹10 lakh and spends ₹6 lakh on flour, fuel and packaging adds ₹4 lakh of value. Adding this across all producers gives the economy's GVA.

India reports GVA at basic prices, which includes production taxes and excludes production subsidies, but not product taxes like GST. The link is: GDP at market prices = GVA at basic prices + product taxes − product subsidies. So if tax collections rise sharply, GDP can grow faster than GVA, and when subsidies jump, GDP growth can lag GVA.

GVA is split by sector: agriculture, forestry and fishing; industry (mining, manufacturing, electricity, construction); and services (trade, hotels, transport, financial, real estate, public administration and others). This makes it the preferred measure to see which parts of the economy are driving or dragging growth. Services account for more than half of India's GVA.

Exam questions often ask for the formula or why GVA and GDP growth differ. Remember that GVA tells you about supply, while GDP's expenditure side tells you about demand. India adopted GVA reporting with the 2011-12 base revision in 2015, following the UN System of National Accounts.

Example

Assume GVA at basic prices is ₹270 lakh crore, product taxes (GST, customs, excise) are ₹32 lakh crore and product subsidies (food, fertiliser, fuel) are ₹6 lakh crore. GDP = 270 + 32 − 6 = ₹296 lakh crore. If next year GVA rises 6% to ₹286.2 lakh crore, taxes rise 12% to ₹35.84 lakh crore and subsidies stay at ₹6 lakh crore, GDP becomes ₹316.04 lakh crore, about 6.8% growth, faster than GVA.

FAQs

Why does India report both GDP and GVA growth?

GVA shows sector-wise production and avoids distortions from changes in tax rates or subsidies, so it is better for judging economic activity. GDP is the internationally standard headline figure used for comparisons and for ratios like fiscal deficit to GDP. Reporting both gives a fuller picture.

What is the difference between GVA at basic prices and at factor cost?

GVA at factor cost excludes all taxes and includes all subsidies. GVA at basic prices adds production taxes (like land revenue or stamp duty) and subtracts production subsidies, but still excludes product taxes. India switched from factor cost to basic prices in the 2015 revision.
