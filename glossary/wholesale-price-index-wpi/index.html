What is Wholesale Price Index (WPI)?

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Wholesale Price Index (WPI): The Wholesale Price Index measures the average change in prices of goods traded in bulk, at the first point of sale, before retail margins and most taxes are added.

WPI is compiled and released monthly by the Office of the Economic Adviser in the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), Ministry of Commerce and Industry, usually around the 14th. The current series uses 2011-12 as base; a revision has been under discussion, so check for a newer series (as of 2026).

The basket is divided into three major groups: Primary Articles (food grains, vegetables, minerals, crude petroleum), Fuel and Power (petrol, diesel, electricity, LPG), and Manufactured Products (textiles, chemicals, metals, food products and more). Manufactured products carry the largest weight, around two-thirds of the index. WPI does not cover services at all.

Until 2014 WPI was India's headline inflation number. Its importance for policy fell when RBI moved to CPI-based targeting, but WPI still matters. It signals input-cost pressure on producers, is used to deflate parts of GDP to arrive at real values, and appears in price escalation clauses in contracts. Since 2017 WPI excludes indirect taxes, aligning it with the GST framework.

Students often think WPI and CPI should move together. They can diverge sharply: when crude oil and metal prices crash globally, WPI can turn negative while CPI stays positive due to food and services. The gap itself is studied as a sign of margin pressure on producers.

Example

Say the WPI All Commodities index was 150.0 in August last year and 153.3 in August this year. WPI inflation = (153.3 − 150.0)/150.0 × 100 = 2.2%. In the same month CPI inflation might be 5.5% because vegetable prices, rents and school fees rose, none of which (except vegetables) feature in WPI. The 3.3 percentage-point gap shows retail prices running ahead of wholesale prices.

FAQs

Why does WPI not include services?

WPI is designed to track prices of goods at the point of bulk transaction, and services are not traded in wholesale markets in the same way. Proposals exist for a separate Producer Price Index (PPI) that would include services and measure prices at each production stage, but WPI itself remains goods-only.

Can WPI inflation be negative while CPI is positive?

Yes. India saw negative WPI inflation in parts of 2015, 2020 and 2023, mostly because of falling global commodity and fuel prices. CPI stayed positive because food, housing and services prices kept rising. Negative WPI is sometimes loosely called wholesale deflation.
