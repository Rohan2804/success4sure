What is Total Expense Ratio (TER)?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Total Expense Ratio (TER): Total expense ratio (TER) is the annual cost of running a mutual fund scheme, shown as a percentage of its average assets and deducted daily from the NAV.

TER covers the AMC's management fee, distributor commission (in regular plans), registrar and custodian fees, audit, marketing and other operating costs, along with GST on management fees. You never receive a bill. Instead, one-365th of the annual rate is accrued every day, so the NAV you see is already net of expenses.

SEBI caps TER in slabs that fall as a scheme's AUM rises, with different ceilings for equity, debt, index funds and fund of funds. SEBI has revised the TER framework more than once, so check the current Mutual Fund Regulations and AMFI disclosures for exact limits as of 2026. AMCs must publish each scheme's TER on their website and give advance notice to investors before increasing it.

Brokerage paid on trades within the portfolio is partly outside the headline figure, and exit loads are separate. A direct plan has a lower TER than the regular plan of the same scheme because it pays no distributor commission.

The misconception is that small percentages do not matter. Costs are certain while returns are not, and the difference compounds on a growing base every year. For passive funds, where portfolios are almost identical across AMCs, TER is one of the main differences between choices.

Example

Two investors each put ₹10 lakh into funds whose portfolios earn 12% a year before costs. Fund A charges 1.5% TER, so the net return is about 10.5%; Fund B charges 0.5%, so the net return is about 11.5%. After 20 years Fund A grows to roughly ₹73.7 lakh and Fund B to roughly ₹88.2 lakh. The 1 percentage point difference in cost costs about ₹14.5 lakh, more than the original investment.

FAQs

Is expense ratio deducted from my investment amount?

No. You are not charged separately. The expense is accrued daily within the scheme and reduces the NAV slightly every day. That is why returns reported by funds are already after expenses. Your statement shows units and NAV, not a separate fee line.

Is a low expense ratio fund always better?

Not automatically. For index funds and ETFs tracking the same index, lower TER and lower tracking error usually make one fund better. For actively managed funds, quality of management also matters. But a higher fee must be earned back through better performance every year, which is a high bar.
