What is Emergency fund?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Emergency fund: An emergency fund is money set aside in safe, easily accessible form to cover unexpected expenses or loss of income without breaking long-term investments or borrowing.

Job loss, medical emergencies not fully covered by insurance, urgent home repairs or a family crisis can all demand cash quickly. Without a buffer, people swipe credit cards, take personal loans at high rates or redeem equity funds at the worst time. An emergency fund is the first building block of any financial plan, before investing for goals.

A common guideline is three to six months of essential expenses, including EMIs and insurance premiums. Self-employed people, those with irregular income, single-income families and people in volatile industries often keep 9–12 months. The figure is based on expenses, not income.

Safety and liquidity matter more than return. Typical places are a savings account, a sweep-in fixed deposit, and liquid or overnight mutual funds. Many people split it: one month's expenses in the savings account for instant access, the rest in a sweep FD or liquid fund that can be accessed within a day.

Mistakes include investing the emergency fund in equity, counting a credit card limit as an emergency fund, and failing to rebuild it after use. Health insurance is separate; an emergency fund is not a substitute for adequate cover.

Example

Farida's monthly essentials are ₹40,000: rent ₹15,000, groceries and utilities ₹12,000, EMI ₹8,000, insurance and transport ₹5,000. Six months' cover means ₹2.4 lakh. She keeps ₹40,000 in her savings account, ₹1 lakh in a sweep-in FD and ₹1 lakh in a liquid fund. When her father needs a ₹60,000 medical procedure, she uses the savings balance and the FD without touching her equity SIPs.

FAQs

Should an emergency fund be invested in mutual funds?

Part of it can be, in liquid or overnight funds, which are low-risk and usually redeemable within a business day. Equity funds are unsuitable because they can be down sharply when you need the money. Keep some in a bank account for immediate access.

How do I build an emergency fund quickly?

Set a target, automate a monthly transfer into a separate account, and direct windfalls like bonuses or tax refunds to it. Cutting discretionary spending for a few months helps. Pause new long-term investments only if needed, and continue term and health insurance.
