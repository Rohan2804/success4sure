What is Depository Participant (DP)?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Depository Participant (DP): A depository participant (DP) is a SEBI-registered agent of NSDL or CDSL through which investors open and operate demat accounts.

The depository keeps the master records, but it does not deal with millions of individual investors. That front-office role belongs to depository participants. Banks, stock brokers, custodians and some financial institutions register with SEBI as DPs and connect to either NSDL, CDSL or both. Each DP is identified by a DP ID, and your demat account number combines the DP ID with your client ID.

A DP's services include opening accounts after KYC, processing delivery instruction slips (DIS) or electronic instructions to transfer shares, handling pledge and unpledge requests, dematerialising physical certificates, issuing transaction and holding statements, updating nominations and bank details, and closing or transferring accounts.

DPs charge fees such as account maintenance charges and per-transaction debit charges. These are set by each DP within the framework of the depository, so they vary. Many brokers offer a Basic Services Demat Account (BSDA) for small holders with reduced or nil maintenance charges, subject to holding-value limits that SEBI revises from time to time — check the current limit on SEBI's or your DP's website.

A common misconception is that if your DP or broker shuts down, your shares are lost. Your holdings are recorded at the depository in your name; if a DP exits, accounts are shifted to another DP. Equally, a DP cannot move shares out of your account without a valid instruction — either a signed slip, an online authorisation or a DDPI you have given for specific purposes.

Example

Meena's demat account number is IN30xxxx12345678 on NSDL: the first eight characters (IN30xxxx) are the DP ID of her bank, and the last eight digits are her client ID. She buys shares through a discount broker but wants delivery in her existing bank demat account. She gives the broker her full NSDL account number; after T+1 settlement, the bank DP credits the shares and her monthly holding statement shows them.

FAQs

Can I have demat accounts with more than one DP?

Yes. An investor can hold several demat accounts with different DPs, even across NSDL and CDSL, using the same PAN. Each account carries its own charges and statements. Many people consolidate to reduce maintenance fees and simplify tracking, which can be done by transferring holdings to one account and closing the others.

What is a delivery instruction slip (DIS)?

A DIS is the instruction an account holder gives the DP to debit securities from their demat account, for example for an off-market transfer or delivery against a sale. It used to be a pre-numbered paper booklet; today most instructions are given electronically with OTP or TPIN-based authentication, or through a DDPI for broker-linked sales.
