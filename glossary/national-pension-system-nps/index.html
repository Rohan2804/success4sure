What is National Pension System (NPS)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

National Pension System (NPS): The National Pension System (NPS) is a voluntary, defined-contribution retirement savings scheme regulated by PFRDA, investing in equity, corporate bonds and government securities.

Any Indian citizen aged roughly 18 to 70 can open an NPS account, and it is mandatory for most central and state government employees who joined after 2004. The Tier I account is the pension account, with restrictions on withdrawal and tax benefits. Tier II is a voluntary savings account with easy withdrawals and generally no tax benefit.

Subscribers choose a pension fund manager and either an active choice, setting their own split between equity (E), corporate debt (C), government securities (G) and a small alternative assets class, or an auto choice that reduces equity with age. Equity is capped at a percentage that falls with age. Costs are among the lowest of any retirement product.

Historically, at exit at 60, at least 40% of the corpus had to buy an annuity and up to 60% could be taken as a tax-free lump sum. PFRDA has revised exit and withdrawal rules for some subscriber categories, and government employees now have other options such as the Unified Pension Scheme, so check current PFRDA rules as of 2026. Annuity income is taxable.

Tax benefits under the old regime include deduction within 80C, the extra ₹50,000 under 80CCD(1B), and employer contribution under 80CCD(2), which is also allowed in the new regime.

Example

Deepak, 30, contributes ₹5,000 a month to NPS Tier I with an auto-choice moderate life cycle fund. Suppose his corpus at 60 is ₹1 crore. Under the historical rule, he could take up to ₹60 lakh as a tax-free lump sum and use ₹40 lakh to buy an annuity; at an illustrative 6.5% annuity rate, that would pay about ₹2.6 lakh a year, taxable as income. Current exit rules should be checked with PFRDA before planning.

FAQs

What is the difference between NPS Tier I and Tier II?

Tier I is the main pension account with withdrawal restrictions until retirement and tax benefits. Tier II is an optional voluntary account linked to Tier I, offering flexible withdrawals but generally no tax deductions, except a special case for government employees. You need Tier I to open Tier II.

Can I withdraw NPS money before 60?

Partial withdrawals from Tier I are allowed after a minimum period for specified purposes such as children's education, marriage, house purchase or critical illness, within limits. Premature exit is also possible with a higher annuity requirement. PFRDA revises these rules, so check the latest before planning.
