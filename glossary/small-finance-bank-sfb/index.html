What is Small Finance Bank (SFB)?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Small Finance Bank (SFB): A small finance bank is a specialised bank licensed by RBI to accept deposits and lend, with a mandate to serve small businesses, small farmers, low-income households and the unorganised sector.

The concept came from the Nachiket Mor Committee (2014) on financial inclusion, and RBI issued SFB guidelines in November 2014. Ten entities got in-principle approval in 2015, many of them microfinance institutions such as Ujjivan, Equitas and Jana, along with AU Financiers, a vehicle finance NBFC. Since 2019 licences are 'on tap'.

SFBs are licensed under Section 22 of the Banking Regulation Act and can do almost everything a commercial bank does: savings, current and fixed deposits, loans, debit and credit cards, and distribution of mutual funds and insurance. They must maintain CRR and SLR, and their deposits are insured by DICGC up to ₹5 lakh.

Special conditions reflect their purpose. The minimum paid-up capital for new SFBs is ₹200 crore. At least 50% of their loan portfolio must be loans of up to ₹25 lakh. They have a much higher priority sector target than universal banks (75% of ANBC originally, later revised by RBI). Their minimum capital adequacy ratio is 15%. Promoters must hold at least 40% initially and reduce in phases. Check RBI's current guidelines for exact figures (as of 2026).

RBI allows well-run, listed SFBs meeting conditions on net worth, profitability and asset quality to apply to become universal banks, and AU Small Finance Bank received in-principle approval in 2025. A common confusion: an SFB is a full bank, unlike a payments bank, which cannot lend.

Example

An SFB has a loan book of ₹20,000 crore. At least 50%, or ₹10,000 crore, must be in loans up to ₹25 lakh each, like microfinance loans of ₹50,000 and MSE loans of ₹10 lakh. With risk-weighted assets of ₹16,000 crore, it needs capital of at least 15% = ₹2,400 crore, compared with ₹1,840 crore at the 11.5% that applies to a universal bank. Its depositors enjoy the same ₹5 lakh DICGC cover as at any bank.

FAQs

Is it safe to keep money in a small finance bank?

SFBs are scheduled banks regulated by RBI with the same CRR, SLR and supervision as other banks, and their deposits are insured by DICGC up to ₹5 lakh per depositor. Like any bank, each SFB's financial health differs, so depositors can check capital adequacy and asset quality in published results.

What is the main difference between a small finance bank and a payments bank?

A small finance bank can both accept deposits and lend money, including to small businesses and farmers. A payments bank can accept deposits only up to ₹2 lakh per customer and cannot lend at all. SFBs are full-service niche banks; payments banks focus on payments and remittances.
