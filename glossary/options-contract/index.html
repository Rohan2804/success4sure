What is Options Contract?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Options Contract: An option is a contract that gives the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell an underlying asset at a fixed price on or before a specified date.

Every option has a buyer (holder) and a seller (writer). The buyer pays a premium upfront for the right. The seller receives the premium and takes on the obligation to perform if the buyer exercises. A call option gives the right to buy; a put option gives the right to sell. The fixed price is the strike price, and the last date is the expiry.

This asymmetry is what makes options different from futures. The buyer's maximum loss is the premium paid, while the potential gain can be large. The seller's maximum gain is the premium received, while the potential loss can be large — unlimited for a naked call writer. Because of this, sellers must post margin, while buyers pay only the premium.

In India, exchange-traded equity options on indices and stocks are European style: they can be exercised only at expiry, though they can be sold anytime before. Index options are cash settled; in-the-money stock options are physically settled. Option prices are influenced by the underlying price, strike, time to expiry, volatility, interest rates and dividends.

Options are used for hedging (buying puts to protect a portfolio), income (writing covered calls), and speculation. A big misconception is that buying options is low risk because loss is limited. The loss is limited per trade, but the probability of losing the whole premium is high, especially for out-of-the-money options close to expiry.

Example

A share trades at ₹1,000. Ravi buys a call with strike ₹1,050, paying a premium of ₹20 per share for a lot of 500 (₹10,000). At expiry: if the share is at ₹1,120, the call is worth ₹70, giving a profit of (₹70 − ₹20) × 500 = ₹25,000. If the share is at ₹1,040, the call expires worthless and Ravi loses ₹10,000. The seller's result is the exact opposite.

FAQs

What is the difference between an option buyer and an option seller?

The buyer pays a premium and has a right but no obligation; maximum loss is the premium. The seller receives the premium and has an obligation if the buyer exercises; losses can be large. That is why exchanges collect margins from sellers but not from buyers.

Can I exit an option before expiry?

Yes. Although Indian equity options are European and can be exercised only at expiry, they trade on the exchange until expiry. You can square off by selling a bought option or buying back a sold one at the current premium, booking the profit or loss.
