What is Impact Cost?

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Impact Cost: Impact cost is the percentage by which the actual execution price of an order of a given size differs from the ideal price, which is the mid-point of the best bid and ask.

Impact cost measures liquidity more precisely than the spread because it accounts for order size. A small order may fill at the best ask, but a large order eats into deeper levels of the order book at worse prices. The more the price is 'impacted', the less liquid the stock is for that order size.

The formula is: impact cost (%) = (actual execution price − ideal price) ÷ ideal price × 100, where ideal price = (best bid + best ask) ÷ 2. For a sell order, the difference is calculated with the ideal price above the execution price, and the result is still expressed as a positive cost.

NSE Indices uses impact cost as a liquidity screen when selecting stocks for indices such as the Nifty 50; a stock must show low impact cost for a specified basket size over a review period. Exact thresholds and basket sizes are set in the index methodology and are revised from time to time — check the latest NSE Indices document.

Impact cost is a snapshot that changes through the day. Institutional investors care about it the most, which is why they split large orders into smaller pieces or use block deal windows. For retail investors, it explains why the same stock can be cheap to trade in small sizes but costly for big orders.

Example

Order book (sell side): 1,000 shares at ₹100.10, 2,000 at ₹100.20, 3,000 at ₹100.40. Best bid is ₹99.90, so the ideal price is (99.90 + 100.10) ÷ 2 = ₹100.00. Buying 3,000 shares fills 1,000 at ₹100.10 and 2,000 at ₹100.20, an average of ₹100.1667. Impact cost = (100.1667 − 100) ÷ 100 × 100 ≈ 0.17%. Buying 6,000 shares would average ₹100.2833, an impact cost of about 0.28%.

FAQs

What is the ideal price in impact cost?

The ideal price is the average of the best bid and best ask at that moment. It represents the theoretical price at which a very small order could trade without moving the market. Impact cost measures how far a real order of a given size moves away from this price.

Is low impact cost good for investors?

Yes. A low impact cost means the stock is liquid enough that large orders can be executed without moving the price much. Index providers prefer such stocks because index funds and ETFs need to buy and sell them in bulk, and traders face lower hidden costs.
