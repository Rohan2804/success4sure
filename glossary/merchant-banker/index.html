What is Merchant Banker?

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Merchant Banker: A merchant banker is a SEBI-registered intermediary that manages public issues and related corporate finance activities such as IPOs, rights issues, buybacks and open offers.

Merchant bankers are regulated under the SEBI (Merchant Bankers) Regulations. In an IPO they act as book running lead managers (BRLMs). They advise the company on issue structure and timing, carry out due diligence on the business and its disclosures, draft the offer document, coordinate with SEBI and exchanges, market the issue to investors and help discover the price.

Due diligence is their most important legal responsibility. The lead manager signs a due diligence certificate stating that the offer document is fair and adequate and complies with SEBI rules. SEBI can penalise merchant bankers for poor disclosure even after an issue is completed.

Beyond IPOs, merchant bankers manage rights issues, QIPs, offers for sale, buybacks, delisting offers and open offers under the takeover code, and may also underwrite issues. Many are divisions of large banks or brokerage groups.

People sometimes mix up merchant bankers with investment banks in the US or with commercial banks. In India, the 'banking' in merchant banking does not mean accepting deposits. A registered merchant banker cannot take deposits from the public; its business is fee-based advisory and issue management.

Example

A company planning a ₹2,000 crore IPO appoints three BRLMs. One handles due diligence and the DRHP, another leads institutional marketing roadshows, the third coordinates retail distribution. They recommend a price band of ₹540–₹570 after studying peer valuations and investor feedback. After the issue, one BRLM acts as the coordinating lead manager and files post-issue reports with SEBI.

FAQs

Is a merchant banker the same as a stock broker?

No. A stock broker executes buy and sell orders on exchanges for clients. A merchant banker manages capital raising and corporate actions for issuers. Some financial groups have both licences in different entities, but the roles, regulations and responsibilities are separate.

What is a due diligence certificate?

It is a certificate filed with SEBI by the lead merchant banker confirming that it has examined the issuer's documents and that the offer document gives true, fair and adequate disclosure for investors to make an informed decision. It creates legal accountability for the merchant banker.
