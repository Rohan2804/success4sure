What is Commodity Futures?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Commodity Futures: Commodity futures are exchange-traded contracts to buy or sell a standard quantity and quality of a commodity at a fixed price on a future date.

India's main commodity derivatives exchanges are MCX, which leads in bullion, energy and base metals, and NCDEX, which focuses on agricultural commodities. Since the Forward Markets Commission merged with SEBI in September 2015, commodity derivatives have been regulated by SEBI under the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act.

Each contract specifies the commodity, quantity, quality grade, delivery centre and settlement method. For example, a gold contract may cover 1 kg, while smaller 'mini' and 'petal' variants exist for smaller traders. Many agricultural contracts are compulsory delivery, settled through exchange-accredited warehouses, while energy contracts such as crude oil are generally cash settled against international benchmark prices.

Hedgers include farmers and farmer producer organisations who sell futures before harvest, processors and millers who buy futures to lock in raw material costs, and jewellers who hedge gold inventory. Options on commodity futures and options on goods have also been introduced, giving hedgers insurance-like tools.

Commodity prices are influenced by global factors — weather, OPEC decisions, the rupee-dollar rate and international prices. Traders sometimes forget that domestic gold and crude futures embed both international prices and the exchange rate. Also, commodity contracts have their own daily price limits and margins, separate from equity rules.

Example

A cotton ginner expects to buy 100 bales in three months and fears a price rise. Cotton futures trade at ₹57,000 per bale (illustrative). She buys futures for 100 bales. At expiry, cotton is ₹60,500. She pays ₹3,500 more per bale in the physical market (₹3,50,000 extra) but gains about ₹3,50,000 on futures, keeping her effective cost near ₹57,000 per bale, apart from basis differences between her local market and the exchange delivery centre.

FAQs

Who regulates commodity derivatives in India?

SEBI, since the Forward Markets Commission was merged into it in September 2015. Commodity exchanges such as MCX and NCDEX and their clearing corporations are recognised under the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act. Physical warehouse standards are also overseen as part of the exchange and clearing corporation framework.

Do I have to take delivery in commodity futures?

It depends on the contract. Many agricultural contracts are compulsory delivery, so open positions at expiry lead to delivery through accredited warehouses. Others are cash settled. Some contracts give options to deliver. Always read the contract specifications before trading close to expiry.
