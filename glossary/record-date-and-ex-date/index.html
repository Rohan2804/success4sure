What is Record Date and Ex-Date?

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Record Date and Ex-Date: The record date is the date on which a company checks its shareholder list for a corporate benefit; the ex-date is the first day the share trades without that benefit.

When a company declares a dividend, bonus, split or rights issue, it fixes a record date. Everyone whose name appears as a beneficial owner in the depository records at the end of that date is entitled. Because buying a share does not make you the owner until settlement, the exchange sets an ex-date — the date from which a buyer will not get the benefit.

With India's T+1 settlement, the ex-date and record date are now the same day. To be entitled, you must buy no later than the trading day before the record date, so that your purchase settles on or before the record date. A buyer on the ex-date (record date) itself does not qualify, because the purchase settles a day later.

On the ex-date, the share price adjusts for the value of the benefit. For a dividend, the price typically opens lower by about the dividend amount; for a bonus or split, it adjusts in proportion to the ratio. Exchanges also adjust strike prices and lot sizes of derivatives for large special dividends and for bonus, split and rights.

A frequent mistake is 'dividend stripping' — buying just before the record date to collect a dividend, assuming the price will not fall. In practice the price adjusts, and taxes on the dividend can make the trade a loss. Another error is using the pre-T+1 rule that the ex-date is one day before the record date; that rule is outdated.

Example

A company declares a ₹20 dividend with record date Friday, 14 March. Since ex-date equals record date under T+1, buying on Thursday the 13th (settles Friday) qualifies; buying on Friday does not. If the share closes at ₹860 on Thursday, it is likely to open near ₹840 on Friday. An investor with 150 shares receives ₹3,000 in dividend, subject to tax deducted at source if applicable.

FAQs

If I sell on the ex-date, do I still get the dividend?

Yes. If you held the shares before the ex-date and sell on the ex-date or later, your sale settles after the record date, so you are still on the register on the record date and receive the benefit. The buyer of your shares on the ex-date does not get it.

What is book closure?

Book closure is an older method where the company closes its register of members for a period to determine entitlements. With depositories maintaining electronic records, listed companies mostly use a single record date instead. Some still mention book closure periods, especially for annual general meeting dividends.
