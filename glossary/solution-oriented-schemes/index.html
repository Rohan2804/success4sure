What is Solution oriented schemes?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Solution oriented schemes: Solution oriented schemes are a SEBI mutual fund category made up of retirement funds and children's funds, each carrying a lock-in linked to the goal.

SEBI's 2017 categorisation created a separate group for schemes designed around specific life goals. A retirement fund has a lock-in of at least five years or until retirement age, whichever is earlier. A children's fund has a lock-in of at least five years or until the child attains majority, whichever is earlier.

Within these limits, the AMC may run the fund as equity-heavy, hybrid or debt-heavy, and some offer several plans with different asset mixes. Some retirement funds automatically shift from equity to debt as the investor ages.

The lock-in is meant to stop investors from using goal money for other purposes and from exiting during market falls. It also means liquidity is restricted; investors need separate emergency savings.

Two misconceptions are common. First, the label does not make the fund safer or more suitable than an ordinary diversified fund; the portfolio and costs still matter. Second, these schemes are not insurance: unlike a child plan from an insurer, there is no life cover. Taxation follows the fund's equity share, not its name.

Example

Rakesh, 32, invests ₹2,00,000 in an equity-oriented retirement fund. The lock-in is five years or until he turns 60, whichever is earlier, so the units unlock when he is 37. Separately he invests ₹1,00,000 for his 14-year-old daughter in a children's fund; since she turns 18 in four years, those units unlock at her majority, before the five-year mark.

FAQs

What is the lock-in for children's mutual funds?

SEBI's rule is at least five years or until the child attains majority, whichever is earlier. So for a 16-year-old child, units unlock at 18; for a 5-year-old, after five years. Each AMC may set the exact terms within this rule, so read the SID.

Is a retirement mutual fund better than NPS?

They are different. NPS offers specific tax benefits, very low costs and a regulated annuity component at exit. Retirement mutual funds offer more flexibility after the lock-in and a wider choice of strategies. The right mix depends on individual needs; it is not a one-line answer.
