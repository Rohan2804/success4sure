What is Wilful Defaulter?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Wilful Defaulter: A wilful defaulter is a borrower who defaults on repayment to a lender despite having the capacity to pay, or who diverts, siphons off or misuses the borrowed funds.

RBI's framework on wilful defaulters, consolidated in its 2024 Master Direction on Treatment of Wilful Defaulters and Large Defaulters, lists situations that count as wilful default: not paying despite having the ability; using funds for purposes other than those sanctioned (diversion); siphoning off funds so that they do not exist in the form of assets; and disposing of or removing secured assets without the lender's knowledge. The current threshold for examination is an outstanding amount of ₹25 lakh and above (check RBI's direction as of 2026).

Identification follows a set process. An Identification Committee of senior officials examines the evidence and issues a show-cause notice. The borrower gets 15 days to reply and may be given a personal hearing. A Review Committee headed by the bank's chief executive confirms the decision, after which the name is reported to credit information companies and RBI.

Consequences are severe: no additional credit facilities from any bank or NBFC; a declared wilful defaulter and the company's promoters and whole-time directors are barred from institutional finance for new ventures for a period (five years from removal of the name from the list, under RBI rules) and cannot get restructuring of their loans; lenders may start criminal proceedings and the IBC bars such persons from bidding for stressed assets under Section 29A.

Exam traps: a borrower who cannot pay because the business genuinely failed is a defaulter, not a wilful defaulter. Non-executive and nominee directors are generally not tagged unless shown to have been involved.

Example

A company borrowed ₹10 crore for a new plant. Audits show it transferred ₹6 crore to a promoter's real-estate firm and stopped paying EMIs, though its main business earned ₹2 crore a year in profit. This is diversion plus default despite capacity. After a show-cause notice and hearing, the bank's committees declare the company and its promoter wilful defaulters. Neither can get fresh loans from any bank or NBFC, and the promoter cannot float a new company for the barred period.

FAQs

What is the difference between a defaulter and a wilful defaulter?

A defaulter simply has not repaid on time, often due to genuine business losses or personal hardship. A wilful defaulter has the capacity to pay but refuses, or has diverted or siphoned the loan money, or disposed of security without permission. Only the latter faces the special bans and reporting under RBI's framework.

Can a borrower challenge a wilful defaulter tag?

Yes. RBI's process requires a show-cause notice, a chance to reply within 15 days and a personal hearing where appropriate, followed by a reasoned order reviewed by a senior committee. A borrower who believes the process was flawed can approach a High Court through a writ petition.
