What is Contango and Backwardation?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Contango and Backwardation: Contango is when futures prices are above the spot price (and later expiries higher still); backwardation is when futures trade below the spot price.

In a normal market for financial assets and storable commodities, futures trade at a premium to spot because of interest and storage costs. This upward-sloping structure — near month above spot, next month higher, far month highest — is called contango. Most Indian index futures and gold futures are in contango most of the time.

Backwardation occurs when futures prices are below spot. It happens when holding the physical asset gives a benefit, called the convenience yield — for example, a shortage of a commodity in the current season, or when a big dividend is expected before expiry in an equity future. Agricultural commodities often show backwardation just before a new harvest arrives, since prices are expected to fall.

The structure matters for rolling positions. A long futures holder in contango pays more for each new contract when rolling over, which eats into returns over time. In backwardation, rolling a long position earns a gain. Commodity ETFs and long-term hedgers watch this closely.

Students often confuse contango with a bullish view and backwardation with a bearish view. The curve mainly reflects carrying costs, supply conditions and convenience yield, not simply market sentiment.

Example

Gold spot ₹72,000 per 10 g. MCX futures: 2-month ₹72,950, 4-month ₹73,900 — contango, since each later month is higher (roughly ₹475 per month reflecting carrying costs). A pulse futures market just before harvest shows spot ₹7,800 per quintal, near month ₹7,650 and next month ₹7,400 — backwardation, because the market expects a large new crop to push prices down.

FAQs

Is contango good or bad for investors?

It depends on the position. Long futures holders who roll repeatedly lose some return in contango because each new contract costs more. Short futures holders gain from the roll. For someone simply hedging a single future purchase, contango is just the cost of locking in a price.

What is convenience yield?

Convenience yield is the benefit of holding the physical commodity rather than a futures contract, such as being able to keep a factory running during a shortage. When this benefit is high, spot prices can rise above futures prices, producing backwardation.
