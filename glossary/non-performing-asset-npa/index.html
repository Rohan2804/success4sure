What is Non-Performing Asset (NPA)?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Non-Performing Asset (NPA): A non-performing asset is a loan or advance on which interest or principal instalment remains overdue for more than 90 days, so it stops generating income for the bank.

Under RBI's prudential norms, a term loan becomes an NPA if interest or instalment is overdue for more than 90 days. A cash credit or overdraft account becomes NPA if it remains 'out of order' for more than 90 days, meaning the balance stays above the sanctioned limit or drawing power, or no credits come in enough to cover interest. Bills purchased and discounted become NPA if overdue for more than 90 days. For agricultural loans, the period is linked to crop seasons: two crop seasons for short-duration crops and one for long-duration crops.

Once an account is an NPA, the bank cannot book interest as income until it is actually received, and it must set aside provisions from profits. Classification is done borrower-wise, not facility-wise: if one loan of a borrower is NPA, all facilities to that borrower with the same bank become NPA.

Gross NPA is the total of all NPA loans. Net NPA = Gross NPA − Provisions held. Both are expressed as a percentage of total advances. Rising NPAs hurt bank profits, capital and lending capacity, which is why the 2015 Asset Quality Review and later recovery laws like the IBC were major events in Indian banking.

A common misunderstanding is that a loan becomes NPA on the 90th day. It becomes NPA when overdue for more than 90 days, so the 91st day of continuous overdue status. Also, NPA status is now updated daily as part of the day-end process, not just at the quarter-end.

Example

An EMI of ₹25,000 on a business loan was due on 1 April and remains unpaid. On 30 June it has been overdue for 90 days; on 1 July it becomes 91 days overdue and the account turns NPA. If the bank's gross advances are ₹1,00,000 crore, gross NPAs ₹4,000 crore and provisions ₹2,800 crore, gross NPA ratio = 4%, net NPA = ₹1,200 crore, about 1.25% of net advances (1,200/97,200).

FAQs

What is the difference between gross NPA and net NPA?

Gross NPA is the total outstanding of all loans classified as non-performing. Net NPA deducts the provisions the bank has already made against those loans. Net NPA shows the bank's real exposure to bad loans after cushioning. A bank with high gross NPA but strong provisions may have low net NPA.

Can an NPA account become standard again?

Yes. If the borrower pays all overdue interest and principal, the account can be upgraded to standard. For restructured accounts, upgrade happens only after satisfactory performance during a specified period. Partial payments that leave dues overdue beyond 90 days do not upgrade the account.
