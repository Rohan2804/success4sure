What is Risk profiling?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Risk profiling: Risk profiling is the process an adviser or distributor uses to assess how much investment risk a client can afford and is willing to take, before recommending products.

A risk profile combines two things. Risk capacity is the financial ability to absorb losses, based on age, income stability, dependants, existing assets, liabilities and time horizon. Risk tolerance is the psychological willingness to see the portfolio fall without panicking. A proper profile looks at both, and when they conflict, the lower of the two usually guides the recommendation.

Advisers typically use a written questionnaire with questions such as 'If your portfolio fell 20% in a year, what would you do?' along with factual data on finances and goals. The answers produce a category such as conservative, moderate or aggressive, which maps to a suggested asset allocation.

SEBI requires registered investment advisers to carry out risk profiling, document it, explain it to the client, and recommend only products whose risk matches the profile. Distributors are also expected to consider suitability. Profiles should be refreshed when life circumstances change.

Common mistakes: treating risk profiling as a tick-box form; letting a client's enthusiasm after a bull market drive an aggressive profile; and confusing a long time horizon with high tolerance. A 30-year-old who sold everything in the 2020 crash may have high capacity but low tolerance.

Example

Two clients aged 40 each have ₹25 lakh to invest. Anil has a secure government job, no loans and 18 years to retirement; his questionnaire answers show he held on during the 2020 fall. Bharat runs a seasonal business, has a home loan and admits he sold equity funds when markets dropped. Anil is profiled as moderately aggressive, with perhaps 65–70% equity suggested; Bharat as moderate-conservative, nearer 35–40% equity, plus a larger emergency fund.

FAQs

What is the difference between risk tolerance and risk capacity?

Risk capacity is your financial ability to bear losses, based on income, assets, liabilities and time horizon. Risk tolerance is your emotional comfort with ups and downs. Someone can have high capacity but low tolerance, or the reverse. Good advice considers both.

How often should a risk profile be updated?

Whenever there is a major life change, such as marriage, a child, job loss, inheritance or nearing retirement, and in any case periodically, often once a year during a review. Risk profiles based on outdated circumstances can lead to unsuitable recommendations.
