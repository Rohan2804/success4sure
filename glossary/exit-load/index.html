What is Exit load?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Exit load: Exit load is a fee, expressed as a percentage of redemption value, that a mutual fund deducts when units are redeemed or switched out before a specified holding period.

AMCs use exit loads to discourage short-term trading that can disturb the portfolio and hurt long-term investors. A common equity fund structure is 1% if units are redeemed within 12 months of allotment and nil afterwards. Debt funds often have shorter periods or none, and liquid funds carry a graded load only for the first six days.

The load is applied to the redemption value, not the gain. The fund pays you NAV minus the load, and under SEBI rules the exit load collected is credited back to the scheme, which benefits remaining investors. GST on the load is handled as per tax rules.

Each purchase, including each SIP instalment, has its own allotment date. If you redeem, units are treated on a first-in-first-out basis, so the oldest units go first. Some may be beyond the load period and others within it.

There is no entry load in India; SEBI abolished it in 2009. Switches between schemes and between plans of the same scheme are redemptions and can attract exit load. Exit load structures can be changed by the AMC, but the load applicable at the time you invested generally applies to those units. Check the scheme information document and KIM before investing.

Example

Arjun invested ₹2 lakh in an equity fund at NAV ₹40, getting 5,000 units. The exit load is 1% within one year. After nine months the NAV is ₹44, so redemption value is 5,000 × ₹44 = ₹2,20,000. Exit load = 1% × ₹2,20,000 = ₹2,200. He receives ₹2,17,800 (before any tax). Had he waited three more months, there would have been no load.

FAQs

Is exit load charged on SIPs?

Yes, but separately for each instalment. Each SIP instalment has its own allotment date and the load period runs from that date. If you redeem everything after 13 months on a one-year load, the first instalment is load-free while the last 12 instalments still attract the load.

Who keeps the exit load money?

Under current SEBI rules, the exit load collected is credited to the scheme itself, net of applicable GST, rather than kept by the AMC. This compensates the remaining investors for transaction costs caused by early exits. Check current SEBI circulars for any updates.
