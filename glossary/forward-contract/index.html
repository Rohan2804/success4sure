What is Forward Contract?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Forward Contract: A forward contract is a private agreement between two parties to buy or sell an asset at a fixed price on a specified future date.

Forwards are customised: the two parties decide the quantity, quality, price, delivery date and place. Nothing is paid upfront except, sometimes, collateral. On the settlement date, the asset is delivered against payment, or the difference is settled in cash. Because they are negotiated privately, forwards are over-the-counter (OTC) instruments.

In India, the most common forwards are currency forwards between banks and their customers. An exporter expecting dollars in three months can sell them forward to a bank at a rate fixed today. The forward rate reflects the spot rate adjusted for the interest rate difference between India and the US, called the forward premium.

The main drawbacks are counterparty risk — the other side may default — and lack of liquidity, since you cannot easily exit a customised contract before maturity. There is also limited price transparency. Futures were developed to address these issues by standardising contracts and placing a clearing corporation in the middle.

Exam questions often test the contrast: forwards are OTC, customised, settled at maturity and carry counterparty risk; futures are exchange-traded, standardised, marked to market daily and guaranteed by a clearing corporation. Remember that both create an obligation for both parties, unlike options.

Example

An exporter will receive USD 1,00,000 in 90 days. Spot USD/INR is 84.00 and the bank quotes a 90-day forward rate of 84.45. The exporter sells USD forward at 84.45 and is assured ₹84,45,000. If the spot rate on the due date is 83.20, the exporter is protected — the open market would have given only ₹83,20,000. If it is 85.50, the exporter misses ₹1,05,000 of extra gain but has the certainty sought.

FAQs

Can a forward contract be cancelled?

Yes, but only by agreement with the counterparty, usually at a cost. In bank currency forwards, early cancellation means the bank closes the deal at the current forward rate and the customer pays or receives the difference. There is no exchange where you can simply sell the contract to someone else.

Why are forward rates different from spot rates?

Forward rates reflect the interest rate difference between the two currencies. If Indian interest rates are higher than US rates, the dollar trades at a forward premium against the rupee. This is called interest rate parity and prevents risk-free profit from borrowing in one currency and investing in another.
