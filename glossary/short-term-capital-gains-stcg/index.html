What is Short-term capital gains (STCG)?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Short-term capital gains (STCG): Short-term capital gain (STCG) is the profit from selling a capital asset held for a period shorter than the threshold set by tax law for long-term treatment.

For listed equity shares and equity-oriented mutual funds, units sold within 12 months produce STCG. As of 2026, such gains, where securities transaction tax is paid, are taxed at a flat 20% plus cess, regardless of the investor's slab. This rate rose from 15% in July 2024.

For other assets, such as property held under 24 months, gold funds, or unlisted shares, STCG is added to income and taxed at the investor's slab rate. Debt fund units bought after 1 April 2023 are taxed at slab rate whatever the holding period.

Short-term capital losses can be set off against both short-term and long-term capital gains in the same year, and unabsorbed losses can be carried forward for eight years if the return is filed on time. Long-term losses can be set off only against long-term gains.

Two practical points: the basic exemption limit can absorb STCG for residents with low income, and there is no annual exemption for equity STCG like the one for LTCG. Tax rules change frequently, and the Income-tax Act, 2025 renumbers sections from April 2026, so verify current provisions.

Example

Arjun buys equity fund units for ₹2,00,000 in June 2025 and sells them in February 2026 for ₹2,80,000, an STCG of ₹80,000. Tax at 20% = ₹16,000, plus 4% cess = ₹16,640. If he had waited until after June 2026, the gain would have been long term and, if within the ₹1.25 lakh exemption with no other equity gains, possibly tax-free. (Rates as of 2026; check current rules.)

FAQs

What is the STCG tax rate on mutual funds?

As of 2026, equity-oriented funds sold within 12 months are taxed at 20% plus cess. Debt funds bought after 1 April 2023 are taxed at slab rate regardless of holding period. Hybrid and other funds depend on their equity share. Check current income tax rules for your case.

Can short-term capital losses reduce long-term gains?

Yes. A short-term capital loss can be set off against both short-term and long-term capital gains in the same year. A long-term loss, however, can only be set off against long-term gains. Unused losses can be carried forward for up to eight years if returns are filed on time.
