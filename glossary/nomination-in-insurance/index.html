What is Nomination in Insurance (Section 39)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Nomination in Insurance (Section 39): Nomination is the policyholder's choice of the person or persons who should receive the policy money if the life assured dies, governed by Section 39 of the Insurance Act, 1938.

Only the holder of a policy on his own life can nominate. Nomination can be made at the time of buying the policy or later, by an endorsement on the policy or a written notice to the insurer, and it can be changed any number of times during the policy term. Multiple nominees can be named, with shares specified.

Before 2015, a nominee was only a trustee who received the money on behalf of the legal heirs. The Insurance Laws (Amendment) Act, 2015 introduced 'beneficial nominees': if the nominee is the policyholder's parent, spouse, children, or spouse and children, the nominee is entitled to the money in their own right. For other nominees, such as a brother or friend, the old position still applies and the legal heirs can claim the money.

If the nominee is a minor, the policyholder must appoint an appointee, an adult who receives the money on the minor's behalf until the minor turns 18. If the nominee dies before the policyholder, the nomination fails, and without a new nomination the money goes to the policyholder's legal heirs, which can mean succession certificates and delay.

Nomination and assignment interact: an assignment (other than to the insurer for a policy loan) cancels the nomination automatically. Under the Married Women's Property Act policies (Section 6), nomination is not permitted because the policy already creates a trust for the wife and children.

Example

Vikas takes a ₹75 lakh term plan and nominates his wife 60% and his 12-year-old son 40%, naming his mother as appointee. If he dies, his wife receives ₹45 lakh as a beneficial nominee. His son's share of ₹30 lakh is paid to the appointee, his mother, who holds it for the son until he turns 18. If Vikas had nominated his brother instead, the brother would receive ₹75 lakh only as a trustee for the legal heirs.

FAQs

What is a beneficial nominee?

Introduced by the 2015 amendment to Section 39, a beneficial nominee is a parent, spouse or child of the policyholder named as nominee. Such a nominee receives the policy money as their own, and legal heirs cannot claim it. Any other nominee only receives the money as a trustee for the legal heirs.

Can I change my nominee after buying a policy?

Yes. You can change or cancel a nomination any number of times during the policy term by submitting a written request or endorsement to the insurer. The insurer records the change and acknowledges it. Payment made to the last registered nominee in good faith discharges the insurer's liability.
