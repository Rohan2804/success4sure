What is Unit Linked Insurance Plan (ULIP)?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Unit Linked Insurance Plan (ULIP): A Unit Linked Insurance Plan is a life insurance policy in which part of the premium buys life cover and the rest is invested in market-linked funds chosen by the policyholder, in the form of units.

ULIPs are regulated by IRDAI, not SEBI. After deducting charges, the premium buys units in funds such as equity, debt, balanced or liquid funds, at the fund's Net Asset Value (NAV). The fund value at any time = number of units × current NAV. The investment risk is borne by the policyholder, which IRDAI requires insurers to state clearly.

Main charges: premium allocation charge (deducted upfront from each premium), policy administration charge, mortality charge (the cost of life cover, deducted by cancelling units monthly), fund management charge (capped by IRDAI at 1.35% a year), and switching, partial withdrawal and discontinuance charges. Since 2010, IRDAI caps the total reduction in yield, making modern ULIPs cheaper than earlier versions.

ULIPs have a lock-in of five years. If premiums stop within the lock-in, the fund moves to a discontinued policy fund, earning a minimum guaranteed rate, and is paid out only after the lock-in ends, minus a capped discontinuance charge. After lock-in, partial withdrawals are allowed. Policyholders can switch between funds, often a few times a year free.

Death benefit is typically the higher of the sum assured (subject to a minimum multiple of annual premium under IRDAI rules) or the fund value. Common mistake: comparing ULIP returns directly with a mutual fund without accounting for mortality and other charges.

Example

Asha pays a ₹1,00,000 annual ULIP premium. The allocation charge is 4%, so ₹96,000 is invested. With the equity fund NAV at ₹24, she gets 96,000/24 = 4,000 units. Monthly mortality and admin charges cancel, say, 40 units over the year, leaving 3,960 units. If NAV rises to ₹26.40 by year-end, fund value = 3,960 × 26.40 = ₹1,04,544. If she had stopped paying in year 3, her fund would move to the discontinued fund until year 5.

FAQs

Who regulates ULIPs in India?

ULIPs are life insurance products and are regulated by IRDAI under the Insurance Act and IRDAI product regulations. A 2010 government ordinance, later an Act, settled a dispute between SEBI and IRDAI by confirming that ULIPs fall under IRDAI's jurisdiction, even though they invest in market-linked funds.

Can I withdraw money from a ULIP before five years?

No. ULIPs have a mandatory five-year lock-in. If you surrender or stop paying within this period, your fund value, minus a capped discontinuance charge, moves to a discontinued policy fund and is paid only after the fifth policy year. Partial withdrawals are allowed only after the lock-in.
