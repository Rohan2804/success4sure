What is Insider Trading?

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Insider Trading: Insider trading is dealing in a company's securities while in possession of unpublished price sensitive information (UPSI), or communicating such information without a legitimate purpose.

Insider trading in India is governed by the SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015, often called the PIT Regulations. An 'insider' is any connected person — directors, employees, auditors, advisers and others with access to the company — or anyone in possession of or with access to UPSI. A person does not need to be an employee to be an insider; a friend who receives a tip can be one.

The regulations prohibit two things: trading when in possession of UPSI, and communicating or procuring UPSI except for legitimate purposes, performance of duties or legal obligations. Companies must maintain a structured digital database of people with whom UPSI is shared and a code of conduct for designated persons.

Designated persons face additional rules. They cannot trade when the trading window is closed, need pre-clearance for trades above a threshold, must disclose trades above a value threshold within a short time, and cannot do a contra trade (opposite trade) within six months of a previous trade. Trading plans approved in advance provide a defence for insiders who need to trade regularly.

Penalties are severe: under Section 15G of the SEBI Act, the penalty can be up to ₹25 crore or three times the profit made, whichever is higher, besides disgorgement of gains, market bans and possible criminal prosecution. A misconception is that trading through a relative's account avoids detection; SEBI routinely analyses trading patterns, call records and relationships.

Example

A finance manager learns on 5 April that the quarterly profit will be 40% below estimates, to be announced on 20 April. On 8 April, his brother-in-law sells 10,000 shares at ₹620. After results, the share falls to ₹520. The loss avoided is ₹10 lakh. If SEBI establishes that UPSI was shared, both can be penalised — up to ₹25 crore or three times the ₹10 lakh gain, whichever is higher — and the gain disgorged.

FAQs

Is it illegal to trade on a tip from a friend who works at a company?

Yes, if the tip contains unpublished price sensitive information. Under SEBI's PIT Regulations, anyone in possession of UPSI is treated as an insider, whether or not they work for the company. Trading on such information, or passing it on, can attract penalties for both the tipper and the person who trades.

What is a contra trade under insider trading rules?

A contra trade is a trade in the opposite direction — for example, selling shares within six months of buying them. Designated persons are prohibited from contra trading within six months. If they do, any profit must be disgorged and transferred to SEBI's Investor Protection and Education Fund, unless relaxation is granted.
