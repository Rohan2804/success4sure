What is Offer for Sale (OFS)?

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Offer for Sale (OFS): An offer for sale is a sale of existing shares by promoters or other shareholders to the public, where the selling shareholders, not the company, receive the money.

The term appears in two settings. In an IPO, the OFS component lets existing shareholders such as private equity funds and promoters sell part of their holding alongside or instead of a fresh issue. The proceeds go to the sellers, so the company's capital does not change. Investors read the RHP to see how much of an IPO is OFS versus fresh issue.

The second setting is the OFS through stock exchange mechanism for already listed companies. SEBI introduced it so promoters of listed companies can sell shares quickly — for example to meet the 25% minimum public shareholding requirement, or for government disinvestment. A separate exchange window is used, and the seller announces a floor price at least one trading day in advance.

In the exchange route, at least 10% of the offer is reserved for retail investors (bids up to ₹2 lakh), who may bid at the cut-off price, and at least 25% is reserved for mutual funds and insurance companies. Non-retail bids typically happen on day T and retail bids on day T+1. Allotment is by price priority for non-retail investors, and settlement follows normal exchange settlement.

Common confusion: an OFS uses the exchange platform, which makes it look like secondary market trading, but the floor price is often at a discount to the market price to attract bidders. Also, the government's disinvestment sales of PSU shares often use this window, which is why OFS is frequent in news.

Example

The government plans to sell 3% of a PSU with 100 crore shares, i.e. 3 crore shares, at a floor price of ₹420 when the market price is ₹445 — a discount of about 5.6%. Retail investors get at least 10%, or 30 lakh shares. A retail investor bidding for 470 shares at cut-off uses ₹1,97,400 (₹420 × 470), within the ₹2 lakh retail limit.

FAQs

Does the company get money in an offer for sale?

No. In an OFS, existing shares change hands, so the proceeds go to the selling shareholders. The company only receives money through a fresh issue of shares. In an IPO that combines both, check the 'objects of the issue' section of the RHP, which explains the use of the fresh issue proceeds only.

Who can sell through the OFS window?

Promoters and promoter group entities of eligible listed companies, and in some cases non-promoter shareholders holding a significant stake, can use the OFS mechanism, subject to SEBI's eligibility conditions such as minimum offer size and market capitalisation thresholds. These conditions are revised from time to time, so check the latest SEBI circular.
