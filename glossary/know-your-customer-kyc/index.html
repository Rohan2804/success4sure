What is KYC (Know Your Customer)?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

KYC (Know Your Customer): KYC is the process by which a regulated entity verifies the identity, address and other basic details of a customer before opening an account or providing services.

KYC is required under the Prevention of Money-laundering Act, 2002 and its rules, and by SEBI's master circulars for intermediaries. Its purpose is to make sure that financial services are not misused by people hiding their identity, and that intermediaries understand their clients well enough to monitor suspicious activity.

For securities markets, the investor provides PAN (compulsory), proof of identity and address — for example Aadhaar, passport or voter ID — a photograph and contact details. Verification is done in person, by video-based in-person verification or through Aadhaar-based e-KYC. Brokers also collect income range, occupation and, for derivatives trading, proof of financial capacity.

SEBI created KYC Registration Agencies (KRAs) so investors need to complete KYC only once across intermediaries. After one intermediary uploads the KYC, the KRA validates key attributes such as PAN, name, address, mobile number, email and income range. Others can then fetch the record. A KYC can show statuses like 'registered', 'validated' or 'on hold', and an on-hold KYC blocks new investments until fixed.

KYC is not a one-time event. Intermediaries must periodically update KYC, more often for high-risk clients, and update records when details change. A common misconception is that a bank KYC automatically covers the securities market — it usually needs to be validated with a KRA or CKYC, even if details match.

Example

Sana opens a trading account with a broker using Aadhaar e-KYC and PAN. The broker uploads her KYC to a KRA, which validates her PAN with the income tax database and her email and mobile through OTPs, marking the KYC 'validated'. A year later, she invests through a mutual fund platform; it fetches her KRA record and lets her invest without repeating the full process.

FAQs

What is a KRA?

A KYC Registration Agency is a SEBI-registered entity that stores and validates KYC records of investors in the securities market, so they don't have to repeat KYC with each intermediary. CVL (CDSL Ventures), NDML (NSDL), CAMS, KFintech and NSE's KRA are examples. Intermediaries upload and fetch records from KRAs.

Why is my KYC on hold?

Common reasons are mismatch between PAN and Aadhaar details, unverified email or mobile number, PAN not linked with Aadhaar, or incomplete documents. You can check status on any KRA website using PAN and correct the issue through your broker, DP or mutual fund platform.
