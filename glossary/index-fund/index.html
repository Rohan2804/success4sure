What is Index fund?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Index fund: An index fund is a passively managed mutual fund that holds the same securities as a chosen market index, in the same proportions, aiming to match the index's return.

Instead of a fund manager picking stocks, an index fund simply replicates an index such as the Nifty 50, Sensex or Nifty Next 50. When the index provider changes constituents or weights, the fund adjusts. SEBI requires index funds to invest at least 95% of assets in the securities of the underlying index.

Because no research team is needed to pick securities, expense ratios are low. The fund will slightly underperform its index because of expenses, cash held for redemptions and timing of trades. This shortfall is measured by tracking difference, and its variability by tracking error; AMCs disclose both.

Index funds are bought and sold with the AMC at NAV, just like any open-ended fund, so SIPs are easy and no demat account is required. That is the main practical difference from an ETF, which trades on the exchange throughout the day.

Investors often assume an index fund is low-risk. It carries full market risk of its index: a Nifty 50 index fund falls as much as the Nifty in a crash. Its promise is market return at low cost, not protection. Different index funds on the same index can still differ in returns because of cost and tracking efficiency.

Example

Two Nifty 50 index funds track the same index. Over one year the Nifty 50 Total Return Index rises 14.0%. Fund X, with 0.20% TER and tight tracking, returns 13.75%. Fund Y, with 0.50% TER and more cash drag, returns 13.35%. On ₹5 lakh, the gap is about ₹2,000 in a single year, purely from implementation.

FAQs

Is an index fund better than an actively managed fund?

It depends on the category. In large caps, many active funds have found it hard to beat the index after costs, so index funds are popular there. In less efficient segments some active funds have done better. An index fund offers predictable relative performance; an active fund offers a chance of outperformance with the risk of underperformance.

Do I need a demat account for an index fund?

No. An index fund is a regular mutual fund scheme bought from the AMC or through a distributor or platform at NAV. A demat account is needed only for ETFs, which trade on stock exchanges.
