What is Depository (NSDL and CDSL)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Depository (NSDL and CDSL): A depository is a SEBI-registered institution that holds securities in electronic (dematerialised) form for investors and records every transfer of ownership by book entry.

India has two depositories: National Securities Depository Ltd (NSDL), set up in 1996, and Central Depository Services (India) Ltd (CDSL), set up in 1999. They are governed by the Depositories Act, 1996 and SEBI (Depositories and Participants) Regulations. Before them, shares existed as paper certificates that had to be physically transferred, signed and sent to the company — a process prone to forgery, loss and delays.

A depository works much like a bank for securities. Just as a bank holds your money and moves it between accounts by entries in its books, a depository holds your shares, bonds, mutual fund units and government securities and transfers them by debiting one demat account and crediting another. No paper changes hands.

Investors do not deal with the depository directly. They open a demat account with a depository participant (DP) — a bank, broker or financial institution registered as an agent of NSDL or CDSL. The DP provides the account, statements and service; the depository maintains the master record. In company records, the depository is shown as the registered owner, while you are the beneficial owner with all economic and voting rights.

Depositories also support pledges, corporate actions such as bonus and dividend credits, freezing of accounts, nomination and electronic voting. Their role in settlement is critical: on settlement day the clearing corporation instructs the depositories to move shares from sellers to buyers.

Example

Ravi holds 50 shares of a company in his CDSL demat account through his broker-DP. He sells them on NSE on Monday. Under T+1 settlement, the shares are debited from his account and delivered to the clearing corporation, which passes them to the buyer's DP on Tuesday. The buyer may hold an NSDL account — the two depositories are interconnected, so the inter-depository transfer happens automatically without either investor doing anything extra.

FAQs

What is the difference between NSDL and CDSL?

Functionally they do the same job and are interoperable. The differences are in ownership and history: NSDL was promoted mainly by IDBI, UTI and NSE, while CDSL was promoted by BSE along with banks. Your choice usually depends on which depository your broker or bank DP is linked with, not on any difference in safety.

Does the depository hold my money too?

No. A depository holds only securities. Money from sales goes through your broker and the clearing corporation to your bank account. Keeping this distinction clear helps in exams: depositories deal with securities transfer, clearing corporations handle funds and securities settlement obligations, and banks handle cash.
