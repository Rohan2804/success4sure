What is Green Shoe Option (Over-allotment)?

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Green Shoe Option (Over-allotment): A green shoe option lets an issuer allot shares beyond the issue size and use the extra money to support the share price after listing through a stabilising agent.

The name comes from the Green Shoe Manufacturing Company, the first US issuer to use the mechanism. Under SEBI's ICDR Regulations, an issuer can over-allot up to 15% of the issue size. The extra shares are borrowed from the promoters, and one of the merchant bankers is appointed as the stabilising agent (SA).

The money received from the over-allotted shares goes into a separate account. During the stabilisation period — up to 30 days from listing — if the market price falls below the issue price, the SA uses this money to buy shares from the market. This buying creates support and limits the fall. The shares bought are returned to the promoters to close the borrowing.

If the price stays above the issue price, the SA has no reason to buy. Instead, the company issues fresh shares to the promoters equal to the over-allotted quantity, effectively completing a larger issue. Any combination is possible: the SA may buy back some shares and the company issues the rest.

The mechanism is rare in Indian IPOs but frequently appears in exam questions. It is often confused with an oversubscription retention, where an issuer keeps excess money from a debt issue. A green shoe is about post-listing price stabilisation, not simply keeping excess subscription.

Example

A company issues 1 crore shares at ₹200 with a green shoe of 15 lakh shares borrowed from the promoter, so investors receive 1.15 crore shares and ₹30 crore sits with the stabilising agent. After listing the price drops to ₹188. The SA buys 10 lakh shares at an average ₹190 (₹19 crore) and returns them to the promoter. The company then issues the remaining 5 lakh shares to the promoter and receives ₹10 crore from the account; the ₹1 crore surplus goes to the exchange's investor protection and education fund.

FAQs

Who decides whether to use the green shoe option?

The issuer decides to include a green shoe in the offer document, and the stabilising agent — normally one of the lead managers — decides when and how much to buy after listing, within the rules. All stabilisation activity must be reported to the exchanges and disclosed after the period ends.

What happens to the profits from stabilisation?

Any surplus in the stabilisation account after closing, net of expenses, is not kept by the issuer or the stabilising agent. Under SEBI rules it must be transferred to the investor protection and education fund of the exchanges. This removes any incentive to profit from stabilising.
