What is Currency Futures (USD-INR)?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Currency Futures (USD-INR): Currency futures are exchange-traded contracts to buy or sell a fixed amount of foreign currency at a set exchange rate on a future date, settled in rupees.

Exchange-traded currency futures started in India in 2008 with USD-INR contracts on NSE, followed by BSE and MSE. Contracts are available on USD-INR, EUR-INR, GBP-INR and JPY-INR, and cross-currency pairs such as EUR-USD, GBP-USD and USD-JPY. The market is jointly regulated: the RBI sets the policy framework for currency derivatives, while SEBI regulates exchanges and brokers.

A standard USD-INR futures contract is for USD 1,000, quoted in rupees per dollar up to four decimal places. Contracts are cash settled in rupees — no dollars change hands. Final settlement uses the RBI reference rate on the last trading day, which is typically two working days before the last business day of the month. Daily mark-to-market applies like other futures.

Importers buy USD-INR futures to protect against a rising dollar; exporters sell them to protect against a falling dollar. Exchange-traded contracts are transparent and accessible to smaller businesses that may not get good forward rates from banks. RBI rules on who can take positions and whether an underlying exposure must be shown have changed over time, so check the current RBI directions on exchange-traded currency derivatives.

A confusion to avoid: a rise in the USD-INR rate means the rupee has weakened (more rupees per dollar). Another is assuming currency futures let you hold foreign currency; they are cash-settled contracts used for price risk, not a way to move money abroad.

Example

An importer must pay USD 50,000 in two months. USD-INR futures for that month trade at 84.20. She buys 50 contracts (50 × USD 1,000). At expiry, the RBI reference rate is 85.10. Her futures gain (85.10 − 84.20) × 50,000 = ₹45,000, offsetting the higher cost of buying dollars in the spot market. Her effective rate stays close to 84.20, apart from basis and costs.

FAQs

Are currency futures settled in dollars?

No. Currency futures on Indian exchanges are cash settled in Indian rupees. Only the difference between the contract rate and the final settlement rate is paid or received. To actually buy dollars, a business must still go through its bank, but the futures gain or loss offsets the rate change.

What is the RBI reference rate?

It is the official rupee exchange rate published by the RBI (through FBIL, the benchmark administrator) each business day for major currencies. Exchanges use it to settle currency futures and options at expiry, ensuring an independent and transparent final settlement price.
