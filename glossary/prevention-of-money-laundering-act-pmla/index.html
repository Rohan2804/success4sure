What is PMLA (Prevention of Money-laundering Act)?

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

PMLA (Prevention of Money-laundering Act): The Prevention of Money-laundering Act, 2002 is India's main law to prevent money laundering and to confiscate property derived from crime.

PMLA came into force on 1 July 2005. It applies to banks, financial institutions and intermediaries such as stock brokers, depositories, DPs, mutual funds, portfolio managers and RTAs, which are called 'reporting entities'. They must verify client identity, maintain records of transactions, and report certain transactions to the Financial Intelligence Unit–India (FIU-IND).

Money laundering is usually described in three stages: placement (introducing illegal cash into the financial system), layering (moving it through many transactions to hide the source), and integration (bringing it back as apparently legitimate wealth). Securities markets are vulnerable mainly at the layering and integration stages, for example through rapid trades, off-market transfers or using third-party accounts.

Key obligations include client due diligence, identifying beneficial owners, risk categorisation of clients, ongoing monitoring, filing cash transaction reports (CTRs) and suspicious transaction reports (STRs), and maintaining records for at least five years after the business relationship ends. Each entity must appoint a Principal Officer for reporting and a Designated Director responsible for compliance.

A frequent misconception is that PMLA only concerns cash. Most securities market transactions are cashless, yet the law still applies, and suspicious patterns such as unusual third-party payments, sudden large trades inconsistent with income, or links to sanctioned persons must be reported.

Example

A broker's monitoring system flags a new client who declared annual income of ₹4 lakh but deposits ₹65 lakh from three different bank accounts over two weeks, buys a thinly traded stock and requests withdrawal to a fourth account. The Principal Officer reviews the alerts, finds no satisfactory explanation, and files an STR with FIU-IND, without informing the client — tipping off is prohibited.

FAQs

What is FIU-IND?

The Financial Intelligence Unit–India is the central national agency that receives, analyses and disseminates information on suspicious financial transactions. It works under the Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance. Reporting entities file CTRs and STRs with FIU-IND through its online system.

How long must records be kept under PMLA?

Reporting entities must keep records of transactions and of client identity for at least five years — transaction records from the date of the transaction and identity records from the end of the business relationship. SEBI's guidelines may require longer retention in some cases, so check the current master circular.
