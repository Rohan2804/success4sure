What is T+1 Settlement?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

T+1 Settlement: T+1 settlement means a stock trade done on trading day T is settled — shares delivered to the buyer and funds to the seller — on the next working day.

India moved in phases from T+2 to T+1 for all listed equity shares, completing the shift in January 2023, making it one of the first major markets on a T+1 cycle. 'T' is the trade date and '+1' counts settlement days, which exclude weekends and settlement holidays, not calendar days.

What happens in between: on T, the exchange sends trade details to the clearing corporation, which computes each broker's net obligation of funds and securities. On T+1 morning, brokers complete pay-in — sellers' shares are moved from their demat accounts and buyers' funds are paid to the clearing corporation. Pay-out follows the same day: shares go directly to buyers' demat accounts and funds to brokers, who credit sellers.

Shorter settlement reduces counterparty risk because fewer days of price movement are exposed, frees up capital faster for investors and reduces margin needs. SEBI also introduced an optional same-day (T+0) settlement cycle for a limited set of shares from 2024, running alongside T+1; its scope may expand, so check current SEBI circulars.

A common confusion is about selling shares bought the previous day (BTST). You can sell shares on T+1 before they are credited, but if your purchase fails because the seller short-delivers, your own sale can go into auction. Another confusion: intraday trades squared off the same day have no delivery obligation and only result in a money settlement.

Example

Kiran buys 25 shares at ₹1,820 on Thursday. Funds of ₹45,500 plus charges are taken by her broker for pay-in on Friday (T+1), and the shares reach her demat account the same Friday. If she had instead traded on Friday, settlement would happen on Monday — Saturday and Sunday are not settlement days. If Monday is an exchange holiday, settlement moves to Tuesday.

FAQs

Can I sell shares before they reach my demat account?

Yes. Shares bought on T can be sold on T+1 before they are credited (often called BTST). The risk is that if the original seller fails to deliver, your purchase goes to auction while you still owe delivery on your sale, which can lead to auction or close-out charges on your sale.

Is T+1 the same for derivatives?

No. Equity futures and options are settled daily through mark-to-market and at expiry in cash or, for stock derivatives, by physical delivery. Cash flows for daily mark-to-market settle on the next working day. The T+1 cycle described here applies to the equity cash (delivery) segment.
