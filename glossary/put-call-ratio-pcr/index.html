What is Put-Call Ratio (PCR)?

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Put-Call Ratio (PCR): The put-call ratio is the ratio of put options to call options traded or outstanding, used as an indicator of market sentiment.

PCR can be calculated in two ways. The open interest PCR divides total put open interest by total call open interest for an underlying, usually across all strikes of an expiry. The volume PCR divides the number of put contracts traded by the number of call contracts traded in a day. Both are widely published for the Nifty and Bank Nifty.

Interpretation depends on who is assumed to be active. If buyers dominate, a high PCR means many people are buying protection, suggesting bearish sentiment. But much of the open interest in Indian index options comes from sellers. Heavy put writing by sellers, who expect the market to stay above certain levels, also raises PCR — which is why some traders read a rising PCR as supportive.

Many traders use PCR as a contrarian indicator at extremes. A very high PCR may signal excessive pessimism and a possible rebound; a very low PCR may signal over-optimism. There is no universally correct threshold, and readings around 1 are often called neutral.

PCR should not be used in isolation. It does not distinguish hedgers from speculators, buyers from sellers, or near-the-money from far strikes. Combining it with price trend, open interest changes at specific strikes and implied volatility gives a more complete picture.

Example

For the current weekly expiry, total put open interest is 1.2 crore contracts and total call open interest is 1.0 crore, so OI PCR = 1.2. Today's put volume is 45 lakh contracts and call volume 60 lakh, so volume PCR = 0.75. The two readings tell different stories: positions held overnight lean towards puts, while today's trading leaned towards calls.

FAQs

What is a good put-call ratio?

There is no universally good value. Readings near 1 are often considered neutral. Some traders treat values well above 1 as signs of pessimism (and possible contrarian buying) and values well below 1 as signs of complacency. Thresholds vary by underlying and market conditions, so PCR is best compared with its own history.

Is PCR based on open interest more useful than volume PCR?

They answer different questions. Open interest PCR shows positions carried over, reflecting medium-term positioning. Volume PCR shows the day's activity, which can be noisy and include many intraday trades. Traders often track both and look for changes rather than absolute levels.
