What is Free-Float Market Capitalisation?

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Free-Float Market Capitalisation: Free-float market capitalisation is the market value of only those shares readily available for trading, excluding promoter, strategic and locked-in holdings.

Full market capitalisation equals the share price multiplied by all outstanding shares. But in Indian companies, promoters often hold 50–75% of shares, and governments hold large stakes in PSUs. These shares rarely trade. Free-float market capitalisation multiplies the price by only the shares held by the public that can actually be bought and sold.

Index providers calculate an investable weight factor (IWF), or free-float factor, for each company. Shares held by promoters and promoter group, government in a strategic capacity, associate companies, employee welfare trusts and certain locked-in holdings are excluded. A company with 60% promoter holding has a free-float factor of about 0.40.

The Nifty 50, Sensex and most other Indian indices use free-float market capitalisation weighting. This means a company's influence on the index depends on how much of its stock is in public hands, not just its size. Index funds and ETFs tracking these indices therefore buy shares in proportion to free-float, matching what is actually available.

A common confusion is between free-float and minimum public shareholding. SEBI requires listed companies to maintain at least 25% public shareholding (with time allowed for new listings), but free-float for index purposes may differ because some 'public' holders, such as strategic investors, are also excluded.

Example

Company A has 200 crore shares at ₹500, giving a full market cap of ₹1,00,000 crore. Promoters hold 72%, so the free-float factor is 0.28 and free-float market cap is ₹28,000 crore. Company B has a full market cap of ₹60,000 crore with only 20% promoter holding, so its free-float cap is ₹48,000 crore. Despite being smaller, B gets the larger weight in a free-float weighted index.

FAQs

Why do indices use free-float instead of full market cap?

Free-float weighting reflects what investors can actually buy. A company with a large promoter stake would otherwise get an oversized weight, and index funds would struggle to buy enough shares, pushing the price up artificially. Free-float weighting also makes the index a fairer measure of public investors' experience.

Does free-float change over time?

Yes. When promoters sell shares through an OFS, a company issues new shares to the public, or locked-in shares become free, the free-float factor changes. Index providers update these factors periodically, usually during scheduled index reviews, and the change can lead to buying or selling by index funds.
