What is Suspicious Transaction Report (STR)?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Suspicious Transaction Report (STR): A suspicious transaction report is a report filed by a regulated entity with FIU-IND about a transaction or attempted transaction that appears linked to money laundering or terrorist financing.

Under PMLA rules, a transaction is suspicious if, to a person acting in good faith, it gives reasonable grounds to suspect proceeds of crime, appears unusually complex, has no apparent economic rationale, or may involve terrorist financing. There is no minimum amount — even a small transaction can be suspicious, and attempted transactions that were not completed must also be reported.

The Principal Officer of the intermediary must file the STR with FIU-IND within seven working days of being satisfied that the transaction is suspicious. The clock starts from the date of that conclusion, not from the date of the trade, so alerts must be reviewed promptly. Reasons for the conclusion should be documented.

Typical red flags in securities markets include clients whose trading volumes are far beyond declared income, frequent payments from unrelated third parties, trades in illiquid stocks at prices far from the market, sudden activation of dormant accounts, off-market transfers without clear reasons, and clients reluctant to provide KYC information.

Two rules are critical. The intermediary must not tip off the client that an STR has been filed, and it should continue the relationship normally unless there are other reasons to stop. Filing an STR is not an accusation — it is information for FIU-IND to analyse along with other data.

Example

On 3 June, a DP's surveillance system flags a client who received shares worth ₹48 lakh by off-market transfer from five unrelated accounts and sold them within two days. The compliance team investigates and, on 10 June, concludes the pattern is suspicious. The Principal Officer must file the STR within seven working days of 10 June — the date of that conclusion, not the 3 June alert or the original transfers.

FAQs

Is there a minimum amount for filing an STR?

No. Unlike cash transaction reports, which have thresholds, an STR has no minimum value. Any transaction, of any size, that gives reasonable grounds for suspicion must be reported, including attempted transactions that were abandoned or rejected by the intermediary.

Can the client be told that an STR was filed?

No. Disclosing that an STR has been or will be filed is called tipping off and is prohibited. Staff must keep the information confidential. The intermediary may continue dealing with the client normally while FIU-IND and other agencies examine the report.
