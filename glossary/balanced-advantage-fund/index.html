What is Balanced Advantage Fund (BAF)?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Balanced Advantage Fund (BAF): A balanced advantage fund, also called a dynamic asset allocation fund, is a hybrid scheme that changes its mix of equity and debt based on a pre-defined model or market valuations.

SEBI's category allows a dynamic asset allocation fund to hold anywhere from 0% to 100% in equity or debt. In practice most Indian balanced advantage funds use a valuation-based model, often price-to-earnings or price-to-book ratios of the Nifty, sometimes combined with trend indicators. When markets look expensive, net equity is reduced; when they look cheap, it is raised.

Many BAFs keep gross equity above 65% so the fund qualifies for equity taxation, but use derivatives (arbitrage or hedges) to bring net, unhedged equity much lower. The factsheet shows both gross and net equity; net equity is what drives the fund's market sensitivity.

The appeal is behavioural: the fund buys low and sells high systematically, without the investor needing to time markets. It suits investors who want equity participation with smaller drawdowns.

The risk is model risk. Valuation signals can stay 'expensive' for years, keeping the fund underweight equity during a strong rally, so it may lag pure equity funds for long periods. Different AMCs' BAFs behave very differently, so comparing them only by name is a mistake.

Example

A BAF holds ₹1,000 crore. When Nifty valuations are high, its model sets net equity at 35%. The fund holds ₹700 crore in stocks (70% gross equity) but sells index futures worth ₹350 crore as a hedge, so unhedged exposure is ₹350 crore. If the market falls 10%, the portfolio loses roughly 3.5% from equity, not 7%. When valuations later drop, the model lifts net equity, say to 70%.

FAQs

What is net equity in a balanced advantage fund?

Net equity is the portion of the portfolio fully exposed to stock market movements after subtracting hedged positions. A fund may show 70% gross equity but only 35% net equity if half is offset by derivatives. Net equity tells you how the fund will move if the market rises or falls.

Can balanced advantage funds lose money?

Yes. They aim to reduce volatility, not eliminate it. With net equity above zero they will fall in market declines, and a debt portion can lose value if rates rise. They may also underperform equity funds during long rallies if the model keeps equity low.
