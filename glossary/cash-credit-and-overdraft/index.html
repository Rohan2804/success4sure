What is Cash Credit and Overdraft?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Cash Credit and Overdraft: Cash credit and overdraft are running loan accounts that let a borrower withdraw up to a sanctioned limit, paying interest only on the amount actually used and only for the days used.

Cash credit (CC) is the main working capital loan for traders and manufacturers. It is secured by hypothecation or pledge of current assets such as stock and receivables. The amount the borrower can actually draw at any time is the drawing power (DP), calculated from the latest stock statement: value of eligible stock and debtors minus the bank's margin. The borrower can draw the lower of the sanctioned limit and DP.

An overdraft (OD) lets a customer draw beyond the balance in a current or savings account. It may be secured against fixed deposits, property, shares or insurance policies, or be unsecured for salaried customers. OD limits are often fixed or reducing, and they are commonly used for short-term or personal needs rather than inventory financing.

Both accounts are reviewed periodically, usually once a year. For NPA classification, they are treated as 'out of order' if the outstanding balance remains continuously above the sanctioned limit or DP for 90 days, or if there are no credits for 90 days or credits are not enough to cover the interest debited. Drawing power based on stock statements older than three months is treated as irregular.

Key differences: CC is mainly for businesses and linked to current assets, while OD can be given to individuals and is linked to other security. Banks have also shifted large working capital borrowers partly to a loan component under RBI's 'loan system for delivery of bank credit' to improve fund planning.

Example

A trader has a CC limit of ₹50 lakh. His stock is ₹40 lakh and eligible debtors ₹20 lakh. With a 25% margin on both, DP = (40 + 20) × 75% = ₹45 lakh, so he can draw at most ₹45 lakh, not ₹50 lakh. If he uses ₹30 lakh for 20 days at 10% a year, interest = 30,00,000 × 10% × 20/365 = about ₹16,438. Separately, his salaried brother takes an OD of ₹2 lakh against a ₹2.5 lakh FD.

FAQs

What is drawing power in a cash credit account?

Drawing power is the maximum amount a borrower can actually withdraw at a given time, based on current assets like stock and receivables after deducting the bank's margin. It is updated from monthly or quarterly stock statements. Even if the sanctioned limit is higher, the borrower cannot draw beyond the drawing power.

When does a cash credit account become an NPA?

A CC or OD account becomes NPA when it stays 'out of order' for more than 90 days. That happens if the outstanding balance stays continuously above the sanctioned limit or drawing power, or if credits are absent or not enough to cover interest debited over the period. Old stock statements can also make DP irregular.
