What is Primary Deficit?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Primary Deficit: Primary deficit is the fiscal deficit minus interest payments on past borrowing, showing how much the government is borrowing for its current-year spending alone.

Formula: Primary deficit = Fiscal deficit − Interest payments. The fiscal deficit includes interest on debt taken in earlier years, which the present government cannot avoid. Removing it isolates the gap caused by this year's policy choices on spending and taxes.

If the primary deficit is zero, the government is borrowing only to pay interest on old debt. A primary surplus means current revenues more than cover non-interest spending, and some of the interest is also being paid from revenue. Countries trying to bring down debt often aim for a primary surplus.

Primary deficit is central to debt sustainability. The debt-to-GDP ratio stays stable or falls when the primary balance is close to zero and nominal GDP growth exceeds the interest rate on debt. India's high interest burden, which takes up a large share of the Centre's revenue receipts, means that even a modest primary deficit can keep debt high.

Common confusion: some students subtract interest receipts too. The standard definition subtracts only interest payments. Also, primary deficit is not the same as revenue deficit; the latter compares revenue expenditure with revenue receipts and ignores capital spending.

Example

Take a fiscal deficit of ₹15.7 lakh crore and interest payments of ₹12.8 lakh crore. Primary deficit = 15.7 − 12.8 = ₹2.9 lakh crore. With GDP of ₹357 lakh crore, it is 2.9/357 = 0.81% of GDP. If next year interest payments rise to ₹13.6 lakh crore while the fiscal deficit stays at ₹15.7 lakh crore, the primary deficit falls to ₹2.1 lakh crore, meaning current policy got tighter even though the headline deficit didn't change.

FAQs

What does a primary surplus mean?

A primary surplus means revenue and non-debt receipts exceed all spending except interest payments. The government is then using part of its own income to service old debt rather than borrowing for it. Sustained primary surpluses are one way countries reduce their debt-to-GDP ratio.

Why is primary deficit called a measure of current fiscal stance?

Interest payments depend on past borrowing and past interest rates, which the current government cannot change. By removing them, primary deficit shows whether today's decisions on taxes and spending are expansionary or contractionary, making it a cleaner indicator of present policy.
