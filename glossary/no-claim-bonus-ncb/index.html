What is No Claim Bonus (NCB)?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

No Claim Bonus (NCB): No claim bonus is a reward given by an insurer for a claim-free policy year, either as a discount on the next renewal premium in motor insurance or as an increase in sum insured in health insurance.

In motor insurance, NCB is a discount on the own-damage part of the premium, not on third-party liability premium. It builds up over consecutive claim-free years in standard slabs: 20% after one year, 25% after two, 35% after three, 45% after four and 50% after five or more claim-free years. A single own-damage claim usually resets the NCB to zero at the next renewal, unless an NCB protection add-on is bought.

NCB belongs to the insured person, not the vehicle. When you sell a car, you can transfer the NCB to your new vehicle by obtaining an NCB certificate or retention letter, and the policy on the old car is transferred to the buyer without NCB. If the policy is not renewed within 90 days of expiry, the accumulated NCB is lost.

In health insurance, NCB is usually called cumulative bonus. For each claim-free year, the sum insured increases by a percentage, such as 10% to 50% a year, up to a cap, at no extra premium. Under IRDAI health regulations, if a claim is made, the bonus can be reduced in the next year at the same rate it was added, but the base sum insured is protected.

Common mistakes: assuming NCB applies on the full motor premium, or that changing insurers resets NCB; it does not, as long as you renew on time.

Example

Pooja's car has an own-damage premium of ₹12,000 and third-party premium of ₹3,400. After four claim-free years her NCB is 45%, so the OD premium becomes 12,000 × 55% = ₹6,600, total ₹10,000 (before taxes). If she makes a ₹15,000 repair claim this year, next year's NCB drops to 0% and the OD premium returns to about ₹12,000, raising her premium by ₹5,400, more than a third of the claim value. She may choose to pay a small repair herself.

FAQs

Can I transfer my car's NCB to a new car?

Yes. NCB is attached to the owner, not the vehicle. When you sell your old car, ask the insurer for an NCB certificate or retention letter and present it when insuring your new vehicle. The new policy will then carry the same NCB percentage, usually valid if used within three years of the old policy's expiry.

What happens to NCB if I renew my policy late?

In motor insurance, if you renew within 90 days after the policy expires, the NCB is retained, though the vehicle may need inspection and is not covered during the gap. Beyond 90 days, the accumulated NCB lapses and you start again from zero. Timely renewal is the simplest way to keep it.
