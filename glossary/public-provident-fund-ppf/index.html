What is Public Provident Fund (PPF)?

Last updated 26 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Public Provident Fund (PPF): The Public Provident Fund (PPF) is a government-backed long-term savings scheme with a 15-year tenure, offering tax-free interest and qualifying for Section 80C under the old regime.

A PPF account can be opened at a post office or authorised bank by any resident Indian individual, including on behalf of a minor. The minimum deposit is ₹500 a year and the maximum ₹1.5 lakh per financial year, across your own and minor's accounts. The government sets the interest rate every quarter; it has been 7.1% a year for an extended period, but check the current rate.

Interest is calculated on the lowest balance between the 5th and the last day of each month and credited annually, so depositing before the 5th of a month, ideally before 5 April, maximises interest. The account matures after 15 full financial years and can be extended in blocks of five years, with or without fresh deposits.

Liquidity is limited: loans are allowed from the third to the sixth year and partial withdrawals from the seventh year, within limits. Premature closure is permitted after five years only for specified reasons such as serious illness or higher education, with an interest penalty.

PPF has 'EEE' status: deposits qualify for deduction under the old regime, interest is tax-free and maturity is tax-free. The sovereign backing makes it very safe, but the long lock-in makes it unsuitable for short-term goals.

Example

Kavya deposits ₹1,50,000 every April for 15 years. At an assumed constant 7.1%, compounded annually, her balance at maturity would be about ₹40.7 lakh, of which ₹22.5 lakh is her contribution and about ₹18.2 lakh is tax-free interest. Under the old regime in the 30% slab, each year's deposit also reduces tax by about ₹46,800. Actual rates vary quarterly, so this is illustrative.

FAQs

Can I withdraw from PPF before 15 years?

Partial withdrawals are allowed from the seventh financial year, up to a limit linked to earlier balances. Premature closure is allowed after five years only for specific reasons such as serious illness or higher education, with an interest reduction. Loans are available from the third to the sixth year.

Is PPF interest taxable?

No. PPF enjoys EEE status as of 2026: contributions qualify for deduction under the old regime, interest earned is tax-free and the maturity amount is tax-free. Even under the new regime, interest and maturity remain tax-free, though the deposit is not deductible.
