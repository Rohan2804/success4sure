What is SARFAESI Act, 2002?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

SARFAESI Act, 2002: The SARFAESI Act lets banks and financial institutions take possession of and sell the security of a defaulting borrower, without going to court, once the loan has become an NPA.

The Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act, 2002 has three pillars: securitisation of financial assets, reconstruction of assets through asset reconstruction companies (ARCs), and enforcement of security interest by secured creditors. It was passed because civil court recovery took years, and even the DRT system was slow.

Process: once the account is classified as NPA, the secured creditor issues a demand notice under Section 13(2), giving the borrower 60 days to repay the full dues. The borrower may make a representation, which the bank must answer within 15 days. If the borrower fails to pay, under Section 13(4) the bank can take possession of the secured asset, take over management of the business, appoint a manager, or ask anyone who owes money to the borrower to pay the bank instead. The bank can then sell the asset through a public auction after valuation and a 30-day sale notice. Section 14 allows the bank to seek help from the District Magistrate or Chief Metropolitan Magistrate to get physical possession.

Exemptions: SARFAESI does not apply to agricultural land, loans where the amount due is less than 20% of principal and interest, or security interest for amounts below ₹1 lakh. For consortium loans, creditors holding at least 60% of the outstanding amount must agree.

Borrower's remedy: appeal to the Debt Recovery Tribunal under Section 17 within 45 days of the possession measure, and further to the DRAT.

Example

A borrower's ₹40 lakh home loan, secured by his flat, turns NPA on 1 July. The bank issues a Section 13(2) notice on 5 July demanding ₹38.5 lakh outstanding. The 60-day period ends on 3 September. With no payment, the bank takes symbolic possession under Section 13(4), then physical possession through the District Magistrate. The flat is auctioned for ₹45 lakh; after dues and costs of ₹39.2 lakh, the bank must return about ₹5.8 lakh to the borrower.

FAQs

Can a bank use SARFAESI for any loan?

No. It applies only to secured loans that have been classified as NPA, where the outstanding is at least ₹1 lakh and at least 20% of principal and interest. It cannot be used on agricultural land. Unsecured loans like most personal loans and credit cards cannot be recovered through SARFAESI.

What can a borrower do against a SARFAESI action?

The borrower can make a representation against the Section 13(2) notice, which the bank must answer within 15 days. If the bank takes possession, the borrower or any aggrieved person can appeal to the Debt Recovery Tribunal under Section 17 within 45 days. Paying the full dues before the sale also stops the process.
