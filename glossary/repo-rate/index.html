What is Repo Rate?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Repo Rate: Repo rate is the interest rate at which the Reserve Bank of India lends short-term money to banks, which pledge government securities as collateral and buy them back later.

'Repo' is short for repurchase agreement. A bank that needs funds sells eligible government securities to RBI and agrees to buy them back the next day (or after a few days) at a slightly higher price. The difference between the two prices, expressed as an annual percentage, is the repo rate.

The repo rate is RBI's policy rate, the single number that signals the stance of monetary policy. It is fixed by the six-member Monetary Policy Committee (MPC), which meets at least four times a year (usually six). When the MPC raises the repo rate, borrowing from RBI becomes costlier, banks raise their own lending and deposit rates, credit growth slows and demand cools, which helps control inflation. A cut does the opposite and supports growth.

Since October 2019, banks must link floating-rate retail and MSE loans to an external benchmark, and most banks chose the repo rate. So a repo change now passes to home-loan EMIs within one reset period instead of months later. The repo rate also anchors the liquidity corridor: the Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) sits below it and the Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) above it.

A common confusion in bank exams is the direction of money. Under repo, RBI gives money to banks; under reverse repo, banks park money with RBI. Another trap: the repo rate is not the rate you pay on a loan. Your bank adds a spread (for credit risk and costs) on top of it. The current figure changes with each policy review, so always check the latest RBI monetary policy statement (as of 2026).

Example

Suppose the repo rate is 5.50% and a bank sells securities worth ₹1,000 crore to RBI for one day. Interest = 1,000 × 5.50% × 1/365 = about ₹0.15 crore (₹15.07 lakh). The bank buys the securities back next day for roughly ₹1,000.15 crore. If a home loan is priced at repo + 2.75%, it would carry 8.25%. If the MPC cuts repo by 0.25%, the loan rate falls to 8.00% at the next reset.

FAQs

Who decides the repo rate in India?

The Monetary Policy Committee constituted under Section 45ZB of the RBI Act decides the repo rate by majority vote. It has three RBI members including the Governor and three external members appointed by the Central Government. If votes are tied, the Governor has a casting vote. RBI then implements the decision through its liquidity operations.

Does a repo rate cut immediately reduce my loan EMI?

Only for loans linked to the repo rate, and only at the next reset date, which must be at least once every three months under external benchmark rules. Loans on older MCLR or base rate systems adjust more slowly. Many banks also keep the EMI unchanged and shorten the tenure instead, so check your loan statement.
