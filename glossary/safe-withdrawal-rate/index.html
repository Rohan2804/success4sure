What is Safe withdrawal rate?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Safe withdrawal rate: A safe withdrawal rate is the percentage of a retirement corpus that can be withdrawn in the first year, then increased with inflation, without the money running out over retirement.

The best-known version is the 4% rule, based on research by William Bengen in the 1990s using US market history. It found that withdrawing 4% of the starting portfolio, adjusted for inflation each year, would have lasted at least 30 years in almost all historical periods for a balanced stock-bond portfolio.

The rule cannot be copied blindly to India. Indian inflation has historically been higher than US inflation, and retirements may last longer than 30 years for someone retiring at 55. Many Indian planners therefore use 3–3.5% as a more conservative starting point.

The safe rate depends on asset allocation, fees, taxes, flexibility in spending and the sequence of returns early in retirement. Retirees who can cut spending in bad years can start at a higher rate. Those with pensions covering basic needs can afford more risk.

The rule is a planning tool, not a guarantee. It is also often misunderstood: 4% is the first-year amount, which then rises with inflation, not 4% of whatever the corpus is each year. Annual reviews help adjust withdrawals to actual results.

Example

Lata retires at 60 with ₹2 crore. Using a 4% rule, she withdraws ₹8 lakh in year one. If inflation is 6%, she withdraws ₹8.48 lakh in year two, ₹8.99 lakh in year three, and so on, regardless of market performance. At a more conservative 3.5%, she would start at ₹7 lakh. She keeps about three years of withdrawals in debt funds and runs an SWP from there.

FAQs

Is the 4% rule safe in India?

It is a rough guide derived from US data. Higher inflation and potentially longer retirements in India mean many planners suggest starting lower, around 3–3.5%, and reviewing every year. A retirement adviser can test withdrawal rates against your specific situation.

What if markets fall early in retirement?

This is sequence-of-returns risk. Withdrawals from a falling portfolio deplete it faster. Holding several years of expenses in safer assets, reducing withdrawals temporarily and avoiding selling equity during falls help protect the corpus.
