What is Demand Draft (DD)?

Last updated 23 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Demand Draft (DD): A demand draft is a prepaid negotiable instrument issued by a bank, ordering its own branch or correspondent to pay a specified sum to a named payee on demand.

The customer who wants to pay (the purchaser) gives the bank cash or a debit to their account plus a commission, and the bank issues the draft. Because the bank has already collected the money, a DD cannot bounce for lack of funds, which is why colleges, government offices and examination bodies have traditionally asked for DDs.

Parties: the drawer is the issuing bank branch, the drawee is the paying branch (often of the same bank), and the payee is the person named. Unlike a cheque, the purchaser is not a party to the instrument. A DD is always an order instrument and is usually crossed 'account payee', so it is credited to the payee's account.

Rules to remember: like cheques, DDs are valid for three months from the date of issue; after that they must be revalidated. Under RBI's KYC directions, a DD of ₹50,000 or more must be issued only by debit to the customer's account or against a cheque, not against cash. Since September 2018, RBI requires the purchaser's name to be printed on the face of the draft to improve traceability. If a DD is lost, the bank issues a duplicate against an indemnity, after checking the original has not been paid.

DD vs pay order (banker's cheque): a pay order is payable only in the same city where it is issued, while a DD is typically payable at another city. With NEFT, RTGS and UPI available 24x7, DDs are now used mainly where an institution insists on them.

Example

Kiran needs to pay ₹75,000 as admission fee to a college in Bengaluru that accepts only a DD. She asks her bank in Indore for a draft payable at Bengaluru. Since the amount exceeds ₹50,000, the bank debits her savings account instead of taking cash. It charges a commission, say ₹3 per ₹1,000 with a minimum of ₹50, which is ₹225. The DD, crossed 'A/c payee', is valid for three months from issue.

FAQs

Can a demand draft bounce like a cheque?

Not for insufficient funds, because the bank collects the money upfront before issuing the draft. A DD may still be returned for technical reasons such as being stale (over three months old), mutilated, or having a mismatch in the payee name. In such cases it is revalidated or reissued by the bank.

What is the difference between a DD and a cheque?

A cheque is drawn by an account holder on his bank and can bounce if funds are short. A DD is drawn by a bank on itself or its branch, prepaid by the purchaser, so payment is assured. A cheque can be bearer or order; a DD is always payable to order.
