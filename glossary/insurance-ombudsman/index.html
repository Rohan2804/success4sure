What is Insurance Ombudsman?

Last updated 19 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Insurance Ombudsman: The Insurance Ombudsman is an independent, cost-free forum where individual policyholders can resolve complaints against insurers, brokers and agents without going to court.

The scheme was created by the Redressal of Public Grievances Rules, 1998, and is now governed by the Insurance Ombudsman Rules, 2017, amended in 2021. The Council for Insurance Ombudsmen administers ombudsman offices across India, each covering a territory. Ombudsmen are typically retired senior officials from insurance, civil services or judicial backgrounds.

Who can complain and about what: individuals, including sole proprietors and micro enterprises in some cases, can complain about delays in claim settlement, partial or total repudiation, disputes over premium, policy terms, mis-selling, non-issue of policy and other service deficiencies. The 2021 amendment extended jurisdiction to insurance brokers and agents, and allowed online complaints and video hearings.

Conditions: the complainant must first complain to the insurer. If the insurer rejects it, does not reply within 30 days, or the reply is unsatisfactory, the policyholder may approach the Ombudsman within one year of the insurer's reply. The claim must be within ₹50 lakh including expenses, and the matter must not be pending before a court or consumer forum.

The Ombudsman first tries mediation; if that fails, it passes an award, ideally within three months of receiving all requirements. The award is binding on the insurer, which must comply within 30 days, but not on the policyholder, who can still go to a consumer commission or court if unhappy. There is no fee. Limits are as of 2026; check the CIO website.

Example

Rakesh's health insurer pays only ₹1.4 lakh of his ₹3.2 lakh hospitalisation bill, citing a room rent limit. He complains to the insurer's grievance officer on 2 May; the insurer upholds its decision on 25 May. Rakesh files with the Insurance Ombudsman in July, well within one year. After a video hearing, the Ombudsman finds the room-rent clause wrongly applied and awards an additional ₹1.35 lakh. The insurer must pay within 30 days.

FAQs

Is the Insurance Ombudsman's award binding?

The award is binding on the insurer, which must comply within 30 days of receiving the policyholder's acceptance. It is not binding on the policyholder. If the complainant is not satisfied, they can reject the award and approach a consumer commission, civil court or other forum.

What is the maximum claim the Insurance Ombudsman can handle?

Under the Insurance Ombudsman Rules, the Ombudsman can decide complaints where the amount claimed, including expenses, does not exceed ₹50 lakh. Larger disputes must go to consumer commissions or civil courts. Check the Council for Insurance Ombudsmen website for any revision of the limit.
