What is Sectoral and thematic fund?

Last updated 20 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Sectoral and thematic fund: A sectoral or thematic fund is an equity mutual fund that invests at least 80% of its assets in one sector, such as banking or pharma, or one theme, such as infrastructure or consumption.

A sectoral fund focuses on a single industry: banking and financial services, IT, pharma, FMCG. A thematic fund takes a broader idea that cuts across sectors, such as manufacturing, ESG, PSU companies, consumption or international technology. Both fall in the same SEBI category and must keep at least 80% in their chosen area.

Concentration is the defining feature. Returns depend heavily on whether that sector does well. IT funds shone in 2020–21 and lagged afterwards; infrastructure funds struggled for much of the 2010s. The fund manager cannot move out of the sector even if its outlook worsens.

Because of this, many advisers treat sectoral and thematic funds as satellite holdings, a small share of the portfolio added to a diversified core, and only for investors who understand and can monitor the sector cycle. Riskometers for these funds are usually 'Very High'.

A common trap is buying a thematic NFO because the theme is in the news. By the time a theme is widely talked about, much of the good news may already be in prices. Another is unknowingly doubling up: a banking fund on top of a large cap fund that is already heavy in banks.

Example

Rahul has ₹10 lakh in equity funds: ₹8 lakh in a flexi cap fund and ₹2 lakh in a pharma fund. His flexi cap fund already holds about 6% in pharma stocks, so his total pharma exposure is roughly (8 × 6%) + 2 = ₹2.48 lakh, about 25% of his equity. If pharma stocks fall 30%, he loses about ₹74,000 from that sector alone.

FAQs

What is the difference between a sectoral and a thematic fund?

A sectoral fund invests in one industry, such as banking. A thematic fund invests in companies linked by an idea that can span sectors, such as consumption, which includes FMCG, autos and retail. SEBI places both in one category with the same 80% minimum.

Should sectoral funds be a large part of a portfolio?

Most advisers keep them small because of concentration risk and the difficulty of timing sector cycles. They can complement a diversified portfolio for informed investors. Suitability depends on the individual's risk profile, so treat this as general information, not advice.
