What is Regional Rural Bank (RRB)?

Last updated 25 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Regional Rural Bank (RRB): Regional Rural Banks are scheduled commercial banks set up to provide credit and banking services in rural areas, especially to small farmers, labourers, artisans and small entrepreneurs.

RRBs were created following the Narasimham Working Group (1975). The first five RRBs began on 2 October 1975 under an ordinance, the earliest being Prathama Bank in Moradabad, sponsored by Syndicate Bank. The Regional Rural Banks Act, 1976 then gave them a legal framework. The aim was to combine the local feel of co-operatives with the professional management of commercial banks.

Ownership is shared: the Central Government holds 50%, the sponsor bank (a public or private sector bank) 35%, and the state government 15%. A 2015 amendment allowed RRBs to raise capital from the public, reducing the combined Centre and sponsor stake to not less than 51%. The sponsor bank provides management support, staff and refinance.

RRBs are regulated by RBI and supervised by NABARD. Their priority sector lending target is 75% of ANBC, much higher than commercial banks. They are scheduled commercial banks but are not allowed to access some RBI facilities like LAF in the same way.

RRB numbers have fallen sharply through amalgamation, from 196 at their peak to 43 by 2021 and fewer after the 'One State, One RRB' consolidation in 2025. Check NABARD or the Finance Ministry for the current number (as of 2026). IBPS conducts a separate RRB recruitment exam for Officer Scale and Office Assistant posts.

Example

A new RRB is set up with authorised capital raised in the standard ratio. If its issued capital is ₹200 crore, the Centre contributes 50% = ₹100 crore, the sponsor bank 35% = ₹70 crore, and the state government 15% = ₹30 crore. If its ANBC is ₹6,000 crore, its PSL requirement at 75% is ₹4,500 crore, compared with ₹2,400 crore at 40% for a commercial bank of the same size.

FAQs

Who owns Regional Rural Banks?

RRBs are jointly owned by the Central Government (50%), the sponsor bank (35%) and the concerned state government (15%). The 2015 amendment to the RRB Act allows them to raise capital from other sources, provided the Centre and sponsor bank together keep at least 51%. Most sponsor banks are public sector banks.

Which body supervises RRBs?

RBI is the regulator for RRBs, but on-site supervision and inspection are carried out by NABARD under powers delegated by RBI. NABARD also provides refinance to RRBs for agriculture and rural lending. This dual arrangement is a frequent question in banking exams.
