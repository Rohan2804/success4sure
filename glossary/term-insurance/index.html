What is Term Insurance?

Last updated 17 November 2026 · By the Success4Sure editorial team · Free

Term Insurance: Term insurance is a pure protection life insurance plan that pays the sum assured to the nominee if the life assured dies during the policy term, with no payout on survival.

A term plan covers a fixed period, such as 20, 30 or 40 years, or up to an age like 70 or 85. Because there is no savings or maturity component, almost the entire premium goes towards the cost of risk cover. This makes term insurance the cheapest way to buy a large life cover, often several times cheaper than an endowment plan for the same sum assured.

Variants include: level term (sum assured stays the same); increasing term (cover rises each year to offset inflation); decreasing term, also called mortgage redemption insurance, where cover falls with an outstanding loan; and term with return of premium (TROP), which refunds premiums on survival but costs much more. Death benefit can be paid as a lump sum, monthly income, or a mix.

Underwriting is stricter for large covers: medical tests, income proof and disclosure of habits. Premiums are fixed at entry for the whole term, so buying younger locks in a lower rate. Riders like accidental death, critical illness and waiver of premium can be added.

Common confusion: people often see 'no return on survival' as money wasted. The premium buys protection, much like motor or fire insurance. Another misunderstanding is that term plans don't pay for death by illness; they do, subject to standard exclusions like suicide within the first 12 months, where only a limited refund applies.

Example

Rohit, 28, a non-smoker earning ₹10 lakh a year, buys a ₹1 crore term plan for 35 years at about ₹11,500 a year. For the same premium, an endowment plan might give a sum assured of only around ₹4 to 5 lakh. If Rohit dies in year 12, his nominee receives ₹1 crore. If he survives to age 63, the policy ends with no payout, and the total premium paid of ₹4,02,500 bought 35 years of protection.

FAQs

What happens if I outlive my term insurance policy?

In a pure term plan, nothing is paid if you survive the term; the policy simply ends. In a term with return of premium plan, the premiums paid (excluding taxes and certain charges) are refunded at maturity. Return of premium plans cost significantly more for the same sum assured.

Does term insurance cover death due to illness?

Yes. A term plan covers death due to illness, accident or natural causes during the policy term, subject to exclusions mentioned in the policy, such as suicide within the first 12 months. Accurate disclosure of health conditions at the proposal stage is important to avoid problems at claim time.
