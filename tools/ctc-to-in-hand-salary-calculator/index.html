How the CTC to in-hand salary works

Your gross fixed salary is your CTC minus the employer's PF contribution, gratuity and variable pay, because these are part of the CTC but are not paid to you every month.

From the gross salary we deduct your own PF contribution (12% of basic), professional tax (set by your state — up to ₹2,500 a year) and income tax.

Income tax uses the new tax regime: a ₹75,000 standard deduction, slabs of 0% up to ₹4 lakh, 5% to ₹8 lakh, 10% to ₹12 lakh, 15% to ₹16 lakh, 20% to ₹20 lakh, 25% to ₹24 lakh and 30% above, the section 87A rebate that makes taxable income up to ₹12 lakh tax-free, and 4% cess.

This is an estimate. Your payslip can differ because of allowances, HRA, meal cards, the old tax regime, or how your employer structures PF. Check the exact rules on the income tax department's website.

FAQs

Why is my in-hand salary lower than my CTC?

CTC includes costs your employer pays on your behalf, such as their PF contribution and gratuity, plus variable pay that is paid later. Your own PF, professional tax and income tax are then deducted from the monthly salary, so in-hand pay is usually 70–90% of CTC divided by 12.

Is a salary of ₹12 lakh tax-free under the new regime?

Under the new regime, taxable income up to ₹12 lakh gets a full rebate under section 87A. Salaried people also get a ₹75,000 standard deduction, so gross salary of about ₹12.75 lakh can work out to zero tax. Confirm the current year's rules before you file.

Should I choose the old or new tax regime?

The new regime has lower rates but almost no deductions. The old regime can work out cheaper if you claim large deductions such as HRA, 80C investments and home-loan interest. Compare both on the income tax department's calculator or with a tax adviser — this tool shows the new regime only.
